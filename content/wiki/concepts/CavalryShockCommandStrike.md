# Cavalry Shock Command Strike / 骑兵奇袭指挥中枢

Cavalry shock command strike / 骑兵奇袭指挥中枢 is the tactical pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-151-huanyuan-pengcheng-zhizhan-xiangyu-zai-fengshen-2-lqy1uu29xbfipo9e0kq6obkl3oay|Hanji 151 part 2]] extracts from [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s counterattack at the [[PengchengBattle|彭城之战]]. A smaller elite mounted and chariot force does not try to crush every allied unit evenly. It preserves surprise, cuts the retreat line at [[XiaoCounty|萧县]], then drives directly at [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s command center so the larger coalition cannot coordinate its numerical advantage.

The concept depends on several linked conditions. [[BattlefieldInformationControl|战场信息控制]] keeps the enemy uncertain about the main force's movement; local terrain and night movement keep the elite force mobile; [[BattlefieldEncirclementLogistics|战场围困与断粮]] turns route control into panic; and pressure toward rivers such as [[SuiRiver|睢水]] converts disorder into mass casualties.

The source presents this as more than personal bravery. Xiang Yu's armor, forward presence, and shock matter, but they work because the attack sequence has already created the wrong enemy orientation, fragile allied cohesion, and a vulnerable command node.

## Key Claims
- A smaller mobile force can neutralize a larger army when it attacks command, cohesion, and retreat conditions instead of evenly matching all units.
- Surprise is sustained by information control before it becomes battlefield impact.
- Cutting a retreat or supply route can make the enemy's size work against it by creating congestion and panic.
- A coalition with weak internal discipline is more vulnerable to command-center shock than a unified army.
- Cavalry mobility becomes decisive when paired with timing, terrain, and a clear target, not when treated as speed alone.
