# Health Exposome

## Definition
The health exposome is the accumulated external environment a person encounters, including particulate matter, pollen, fungi, pesticides, chemicals, and microplastics, considered alongside internal physiological responses.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats external exposure as another longitudinal data stream rather than a static background variable. Portable monitoring and collected samples may help connect time and place with inflammation, metabolism, symptoms, or other internal markers. The central challenge is causal attribution: exposures co-occur, sensors measure only selected signals, dose and timing matter, and correlations across dense datasets can multiply faster than validated health conclusions.

## Key Claims
- Environmental exposure varies across person, place, and time and can therefore contribute to individual health differences.
- Portable sensors can add temporal context to personal physiological data.
- PM2.5 and collected biological or chemical material represent different measurement pathways and should not be collapsed into one exposure score.
- Linking external and internal measurements generates hypotheses; it does not by itself establish causality or treatment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source summary does not establish exposure-response thresholds, causal links, clinical utility, or which signals were validated. Portable devices may have calibration, sampling, and coverage limits. Broad statements about particles reaching blood or brain require compound-, size-, dose-, and evidence-specific interpretation.
