# Career Exit Friction / 职业退出摩擦

Career exit friction is the force that makes a person stay in a role after fit has already weakened. In [[154-sishisui-ganyan-bu-zuo-nazhi-wenshuili-de-qingwa-lg-qj3p2lq-8sk-rn2vk209tq9x]], [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] uses the image of the warm-water frog to describe how high income, institutional prestige, title, platform resources, and social recognition can delay exit from a job that no longer has internal momentum.

The concept extends [[CareerSelfRescue]] by explaining why rescue is hard before the decisive move. It also extends [[FinancialCareerRisk]] because financial careers can make the water warmer: compensation, network value, status, and bonus cycles create real opportunity cost, so leaving cannot be reduced to courage or cowardice.

## Key Claims
- The more a job supplies pay, status, and external admiration, the harder it can be to admit that it no longer fits.
- Exit friction can rise even when the person is materially successful; prestige can hide a [[TelosCrisis|telos crisis]].
- Large institutions create switching costs through deferred benefits, identity, colleagues, career narratives, and family expectations.
- Fast exit is not always rational, but never testing fit can turn stability into self-loss.
- The source frames exit as condition-dependent: savings, family support, and a concrete next desire made the move possible for [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]].
