# Returning Army Interdiction Risk / 归师勿遏式追击风险

## Definition
Returning Army Interdiction Risk / 归师勿遏式追击风险 is the risk that blocking or pursuing a force trying to return home, rescue its rear, or escape a route crisis can harden morale and create counter-attack danger unless the command situation shows that resolve is missing.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1023 frames the danger side through Cao Cao's Anzhong withdrawal and Jia Xu's two pursuit judgments. Zhang Xiu and Liu Biao try to exploit Cao Cao's retreat by blocking dangerous terrain, but the pressure gives Cao Cao a death-ground opportunity: he opens a narrow path at night, signals escape, draws the allied force into pursuit, and strikes from front and rear. Jia Xu's advice prevents the rule from becoming simplistic. The first pursuit fails because Cao Cao personally commands the rear guard; the second pursuit succeeds because Cao has moved faster with light cavalry and left a weaker rear guard under other generals.

Hanji 1063 adds the inverse test at Ye. Cao-side generals fear that Yuan Shang's rescue force is a returning army and should not be blocked, but Cao Cao asks what Yuan Shang's route reveals. A main-road approach would suggest a willingness to stake everything on saving Ye; the western-mountain route suggests a preserved exit. Yuan Shang's later surrender request, night flight, and loss of supplies confirm that the maxim is a diagnostic problem, not an automatic ban on attack.

## Key Claims
- A retreating or returning army can become more dangerous when its route, rear security, and survival objective all point in the same direction.
- Blocking a return path can convert an enemy's logistical problem into forced resolve rather than automatic collapse.
- Pursuit judgment depends on commander location, rear-guard quality, terrain, timing, and whether the enemy's conduct shows resolve or a fallback path.
- Conditions can reverse after first contact: a failed pursuit may be followed by a successful one if the main commander leaves and the rear guard changes.
- False escape can make the blocking side abandon advantageous terrain and enter a prepared ambush.
- A rescue army can be attacked when its route choice reveals caution, exit preservation, or weak death-fight commitment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Sunzi maxim is not a universal ban on pursuit or interdiction. Hanji 1023 says Zhang Xiu's second pursuit succeeds once Cao Cao is no longer personally holding the rear and the remaining guard is weaker. Hanji 1063 says Yuan Shang's rescue force can be attacked because his route and later conduct do not show true no-retreat resolve. The concept should therefore be used as a condition test, not as a slogan that retreating or returning armies are always untouchable.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1063's Ye relief-force test, where Cao Cao uses Yuan Shang's route choice to decide whether "归师勿遏" applies.
- Reframed the concept from a pursuit warning into a broader diagnostic rule about resolve, fallback paths, and commander conduct.
