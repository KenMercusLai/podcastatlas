# Hemorrhagic Fever With Renal Syndrome Escalation

## Definition
Hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome escalation is the source's China-relevant hantavirus warning frame: fever plus rodent-exposure history, "three reds and three pains," bleeding points, shock signs, oliguria, or later fluid/electrolyte problems should lower the threshold for prompt medical care.

## Current Synthesis
In VOL.205, [[ZihanDoctor|子涵医生]] distinguishes China's common public-health concern from Americas-associated pulmonary syndrome. The episode uses the mnemonic "三红三痛" to make early danger visible: facial, neck, and upper-chest flushing plus headache, orbital pain, and low-back pain. It then situates those signs inside a possible five-stage severe course: fever, hypotensive shock, oliguria, polyuria, and recovery. The public-facing synthesis is not self-diagnosis; it is rapid escalation when symptoms, exposure history, and bleeding or kidney-related signs align.

## Key Claims
- In China and Eurasia, hantavirus concern often centers on hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome rather than pulmonary syndrome.
- Exposure history matters: sudden high fever after possible rodent-contaminated environmental exposure changes the risk interpretation.
- "Three reds and three pains" are practical public warning signs, especially when accompanied by bleeding points or low-back pain.
- Severe untreated courses can involve shock, acute kidney injury, reduced urine, later excessive urine, dehydration, and electrolyte disturbance.
- Early diagnosis and supportive care can interrupt the severe disease chain, so delayed care is the avoidable risk.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a public warning-sign concept, not a diagnostic checklist. Similar symptoms can come from other conditions, and suspected infection or severe symptoms require qualified medical evaluation rather than home triage.
