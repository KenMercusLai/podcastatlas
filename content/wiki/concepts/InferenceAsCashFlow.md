# Inference as Cash Flow

## Definition
Inference as cash flow is the idea that production model use creates recurring compute demand, unlike frontier training runs that are closer to large episodic investments.

## Current Synthesis
The sources describe an emerging inference economy in which users, agents, applications, vehicles, and robots generate repeated model calls. This can make inference a durable capacity and revenue driver, especially when each workflow involves planning, tools, context, verification, retries, or continuous physical operation. The thesis remains conditional: recurring tokens become economic value only when utilization, pricing, reliability, power, memory, and software efficiency support sustainable service.

## Key Claims
- Production inference can become a more persistent demand source than episodic training runs.
- Agentic workflows multiply calls through planning, tool use, context refresh, verification, and retries.
- Physical-AI systems extend inference from user sessions into continuously operating vehicles and robots.
- Lower inference cost can expand total consumption by making more workflows economically viable.
- Recurring token volume does not automatically imply profit, useful intelligence, or justified infrastructure orders.

## Evidence
### Recurring software demand
- [[e230-1-wan-yi-shouru-yuqi-beihou-yingweida-de-dianfeng-yu-ruanlei-d97446f1-d6e3-4894-89d1-dca0a362b10b]] distinguishes training as a large one-time investment from inference as repeated use across agents, long context, coding, and production applications.

### Infrastructure and physical-AI extension
- [[ep-36-nvidia-gtc-2026-everything-that-matters-recapped]] makes the inference economy its main builder takeaway and connects recurring token output to Vera Rubin, agent stacks, autonomous vehicles, and robotics.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The sources do not establish future token demand, customer willingness to pay, utilization, margins, or the share captured by any one vendor.
- Efficiency can lower unit cost but may not increase useful demand enough to absorb all planned capacity.
- Token volume is an incomplete outcome metric unless quality, latency, reliability, task success, and energy cost are also measured.
- Vehicle and robot deployments face safety, regulation, operations, and physical-world reliability gates beyond inference hardware.
