# Bed-Based Sleep Sensing / 床面睡眠传感

## Definition
Bed-based sleep sensing measures sleep-related movement and physiological signals from the mattress or bed surface, reducing dependence on a worn and charged device and potentially feeding automatic changes to the sleep environment.

## Current Synthesis
The evidence uses [[EightSleep|Eight Sleep]] to describe a closed loop rather than a standalone tracker. Bed sensors estimate sleep state, heart rate, HRV, respiration, and personal patterns; software then changes temperature and, in the newer source, may also adjust head elevation, sound, and waking cues. The bed is attractive because people remain there for hours, it has power and network access, and sensing can happen without an extra nightly habit.

The sources distinguish bed sensing from optical watches, bands, and rings and from clinical polysomnography. Home sensing can establish a longitudinal personal baseline and make deviations easier to notice, but consumer estimates are not automatically diagnoses. The newer source's illness-warning, disease-correlation, pregnancy, and sleep-apnea claims raise the evidence burden because false positives, false reassurance, representativeness, clinical follow-up, and regulatory status matter once a convenience product implies medical action.

## Key Claims
- Embedding sensors in the bed can reduce wear, charging, and manual-start friction.
- The strongest product claim is measurement linked to automatic intervention rather than another dashboard.
- Longitudinal personal baselines may reveal deviations that isolated measurements miss.
- Proprietary sensors and paired intervention-outcome data can support personalization but do not by themselves prove causality or clinical validity.
- Bed-based signals differ from optical wearables and do not automatically include every measurement, such as blood oxygen.
- Consumer sensing can support screening or escalation but remains distinct from clinician-interpreted sleep testing.
- Medical uses require explicit performance evidence, regulatory status, false-alert handling, and a route to qualified care.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source independently validates consumer sleep stages, anomaly alerts, disease detection, pregnancy comparisons, sleep-apnea performance, or outcome improvement. The earlier source says the bed-based approach discussed does not measure blood oxygen. Company anecdotes and correlations do not establish diagnosis or causality, and serious symptoms or impairment should move to clinical assessment rather than device-led self-treatment.

## What Changed
- Extended the concept from stage-aware temperature control to multiple automatic interventions.
- Added low-friction placement and longitudinal personal-baseline value.
- Added proprietary intervention-outcome data as a possible learning asset, not clinical proof.
- Added false-positive, clinical-escalation, and regulatory boundaries for preventive-health claims.
