# Alarm Fatigue

## Definition

Alarm fatigue is the loss of attention, discrimination, or response that can occur when warnings are too frequent or insufficiently selective.

## Current Synthesis

[[running-hot|Running Hot]] applies alarm fatigue to public sirens: when routine and genuinely life-critical calls use the same intense signal, listeners may become desensitized and the signal's informational value may decline. [[JeffJarvisEMS|Jeff Jarvis]] and [[BenNaddaffHafrey]] hypothesize that reserving sirens for true urgency could restore stronger public reaction.

The source supports the diagnosis more strongly than the proposed behavioral benefit. It documents extensive use relative to potentially lifesaving intervention and recurring neighborhood exposure, but explicitly says that improved public reaction from selective use still requires testing.

## Key Claims

- Warning frequency and selectivity affect the information carried by an alarm.
- Universal urgency can make genuinely exceptional events harder to distinguish.
- Reducing false or low-value alarms may improve attention, but this is an empirical question.
- Alarm policy must preserve sensitivity to rare high-consequence events while improving specificity.
- Cultural familiarity and inherited practice can sustain alarms even when their present value is unclear.

## Evidence

### Siren overuse context

- [[running-hot]] reports high warning-device use relative to potentially lifesaving intervention and repeated neighborhood exposure.

### Attention hypothesis

- [[running-hot]] attributes the alarm-fatigue frame to Jarvis and explicitly marks restored public responsiveness as untested.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supplies no behavioral experiment showing that residents ignore sirens, respond differently after a policy change, or can reliably infer clinical urgency from sound. A reduction that is too aggressive could also weaken collision avoidance or delay a time-critical response. Alarm-fatigue mechanisms and acceptable error tradeoffs vary across medical, industrial, consumer, and public-warning settings.
