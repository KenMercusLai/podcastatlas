# Multi-Perspective Murder Narration

Multi-perspective murder narration is the way [[MyNameIsRed|《我的名字叫红》]] lets the murder mystery unfold through many first-person voices, including lovers, suspects, objects, images, colors, and the murderer. [[153-wode-mingzi-jiao-hong-yong-yiqian-lingyi-ye-de-xintiao-wancheng-zhechang-mousha-shang-917670296]] introduces the method through the corpse's opening chapter, the dog performance in the coffeehouse, the murderer's confession, Enishte, Black, and young Orhan's household view. In [[154-wode-mingzi-jiao-hong-yong-yiqian-lingyi-ye-de-xintiao-wancheng-zhechang-mousha-xia-919262400]], [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] treats this structure as central to the book's meaning: who speaks, who sees, and who controls the frame matter as much as who killed whom.

The form mirrors the art debate. Western portraiture pulls toward centered individual likeness, while [[OttomanMiniaturePainting]] holds a more distributed relation to scene, memory, and meaning. The novel's many voices make the reader compare perspectives rather than settle into one authoritative eye.

[[17-diyubian-zhongsheng-jie-ku-yi-nian-ru-mo-561780565]] adds a compact Akutagawa case through [[InAGrove|《竹林中》]]. The murder's bandit, wife, and dead husband produce incompatible but internally persuasive accounts, and [[RashomonFilm|黑泽明《罗生门》]] gives that structure its better-known film afterlife. Unlike the Pamuk case, this branch emphasizes truth fracture more than distributed worldbuilding.
