# Museum Interpretation Legibility / 博物馆阐释可读性

## Definition
Museum interpretation legibility is the degree to which an exhibition helps non-specialist visitors understand what objects are, why they matter, how to read them, and what context makes them meaningful.

## Current Synthesis
The [[BowuzhiPodcast|博物志]] episode on [[YiweiHangzhiExhibition|“意味杭之”]] treats legibility as a curatorial obligation. Labels, visual annotation, translation, comparison, short videos, and repeated orientation cues are not decorative supplements; they are the mechanism by which rare books become public knowledge rather than private expertise behind glass.

The [[LucasMuseumOfNarrativeArt|Lucas Museum]] supplies a negative comparison. A visually spectacular building and unusually broad collection do not explain why cave imagery, large reproductions, illustration, manga, film props, and conventional artworks belong together. Optional website lookup can extend interpretation, but it does not fully replace accessible orientation inside the gallery, especially for the general audience the institution seeks.

## Key Claims
- Exhibition quality depends on explanation as well as object rarity.
- Ordinary visitor confusion should prompt curatorial reflection before blaming the audience.
- Legibility improves when labels explain object identity, technical terms, historical period, physical form, and why a displayed detail matters.
- Multimedia is useful when it supplies missing knowledge or comparison, not when it only animates images.
- Repeated orientation can be good design when visitors need to compare stages, forms, or unfamiliar categories.
- A broad or unconventional curatorial category requires more explicit explanation, not less, if visitors are to understand its organizing argument.

## Evidence
- Explanation over rarity - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] says the exhibition would remain valuable even with facsimiles because it performs education and interpretation well.
- Visitor responsibility - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] criticizes ancient-book exhibitions that simply open books in cases without making them understandable.
- Design devices - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] describes red-line highlights, white-language explanation boxes, glass labels, short phrases, term screens, and folding/binding videos.
- Missing-context comparison - [[the-mob-rule-criminality-consumes-south-africa-6ab24a5a1f5b87e5353dbc86]] reports that sparse wall text and website-dependent explanation leave the Lucas Museum's broad narrative-art juxtapositions difficult to follow.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are podcast evaluations of two exhibitions or institutions, not a comparative museum-studies survey. They support the visitor-legibility criterion but do not establish one universal label density or delivery method. Minimal text can be intentional, and the Lucas Museum may evolve through programs and post-opening interpretation.

## What Changed
- Created a museum-interpretation concept grounded in the Hangzhou ancient-books exhibition.
- Added the Lucas Museum as a contrasting case where spectacle, collection breadth, and digital lookup do not yet produce a clear public argument.
