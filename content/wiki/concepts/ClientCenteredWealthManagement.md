# Client-Centered Wealth Management / 以客户为中心的财富管理

Client-centered wealth management is [[162-caifu-de-benzhi-yiji-ziyou-de-zhenzheng-hanyi-chuantai-shifen-xiyin-lofyxfhiprud7zvekufexsjhtust]]'s distinction between managing assets and managing wealth. The source argues that true wealth management has to understand the client's life, cognition, risk tolerance, anxiety, career, relationships, family needs, and desired freedom, not only recommend a product mix or maximize return appetite.

The concept extends [[PortfolioSuitability]] and [[GoalBasedClientProfiling]]. Asset management can be one component, but wealth management asks what the money is for, which risks the household can actually live with, which capital forms matter beyond financial assets, and whether the client needs better holding experience, education, or life support more than a higher expected-return product.

## Key Claims
- Wealth management should start from life goals, constraints, and [[WealthDesireGap]], not from products.
- A client's human capital, relationships, business direction, reputation, and family obligations are part of the wealth picture.
- Good service may reduce anxiety and improve holdability even when it does not maximize short-term return.
- Product recommendation without client understanding can inflame desire and comparison rather than increase freedom.
- The source's family-office examples make wealth management a bridge between [[BourdieuCapitalForms]], [[CapitalConversionLoop]], and investment implementation.
