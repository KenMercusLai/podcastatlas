# Multimodal Function-Centered Pain Care

## Definition
Multimodal function-centered pain care is an individualized treatment model that combines appropriate biological, psychological, behavioral, rehabilitative, procedural, complementary, and self-management tools while judging success by quality of life and meaningful function as well as symptom relief.

## Current Synthesis
Because pain emerges through interacting body, brain, behavior, and social systems, chronic-pain treatment rarely reduces to one drug, procedure, exercise, or mindset. The source groups care into medication, procedures, psychological and behavioral therapies, physical and occupational rehabilitation, complementary approaches, and education or self-empowerment. Selection depends on diagnosis, risk, access, response, and the person's goals.

Function-centered does not mean minimizing suffering or withholding relief. Sleep, work, relationships, mobility, mood, and daily activity can justify treatment, while durable benefit matters more than a brief change that blocks rehabilitation. Medication and nonmedication tools remain subject to contraindications, interactions, evidence quality, practitioner competence, and reassessment.

The kratom evidence sharpens the intervention-specific rule. [[scim3176341770-scim3176341770]] distinguishes leaf-like products from extracts, concentrates, isolates, and chemically produced 7-hydroxymitragynine, so “kratom” cannot function as one stable item in a pain-care menu. Analgesic reports, opioid-substitution use, tolerance, dependence, withdrawal, interaction, and respiratory risk must be evaluated by product and person rather than inferred from plant origin.

## Key Claims
- Chronic-pain care usually benefits from combining treatment categories rather than seeking one universal remedy.
- Quality of life, sleep, relationships, work, mobility, and valued activity are core outcomes.
- Education, pacing, CBT-related skills, mindfulness, relaxation, and rehabilitation can increase agency without implying that pain is imaginary.
- Medication, procedures, TENS, heat, cold, acupuncture, manual care, nutrition changes, and supplements require distinct evidence and safety judgments; kratom especially requires product-form specificity because leaf, extracts, concentrates, isolates, and modified derivatives can produce materially different exposures.
- Temporary relief is most useful when it supports durable function rather than dependency on repeated passive treatment.
- Opioid care requires patient-specific benefit-risk judgment, monitoring, and compassionate voluntary change rather than ideological extremes or abandonment.
- Access to skilled interdisciplinary care remains a systems constraint, not merely a patient motivation problem.

## Evidence
- Treatment architecture - [[tools-to-reduce-manage-pain-dr-sean-mackey-scim1698075556]] names six broad categories and argues that combinations usually work best for chronic pain.
- Functional threshold - [[tools-to-reduce-manage-pain-dr-sean-mackey-scim1698075556]] ties treatment decisions to interference with quality of life, sleep, activity, work, and relationships.
- Rehabilitation and psychology - [[tools-to-reduce-manage-pain-dr-sean-mackey-scim1698075556]] presents physical therapy, pacing, CBT, mindfulness, acceptance-based skills, relaxation, breathing, biofeedback, and brief pain education as complementary tools.
- Intervention boundaries - [[tools-to-reduce-manage-pain-dr-sean-mackey-scim1698075556]] qualifies NSAIDs, acetaminophen, opioids, cannabis, kratom, supplements, acupuncture, chiropractic care, heat, cold, TENS, nerve blocks, stimulators, and pumps by risk and evidence.
- Kratom product specificity - [[scim3176341770-scim3176341770]] separates traditional leaf, powder, extracts, concentrates, isolates, and 7-hydroxymitragynine products while qualifying analgesia, tolerance, dependence, interactions, withdrawal treatment, and respiratory claims.
- Access and policy - [[tools-to-reduce-manage-pain-dr-sean-mackey-scim1698075556]] describes digital delivery ambitions and the National Pain Strategy as responses to uneven access and public-health burden.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
“Multimodal” does not mean using every modality or stacking treatments without coordination. The supplied episode notes do not provide comparative effect sizes, full evidence grades, dosing, contraindications, or patient-selection rules. Kratom evidence includes anecdotes, surveys, animal work, chemical analysis, and a single interaction case; none establishes a general pain protocol, and 7-hydroxymitragynine animal findings do not quantify human risk. Individual diagnosis and clinician or pharmacist guidance remain necessary for medication, procedures, restrictive diets, supplements, dependence, or severe and changing symptoms.

## What Changed
- Created a whole-person care framework organized around function, combined modalities, durability, and individualized risk.
- Added product-form specificity for kratom and separated leaf-like products from concentrated or modified derivatives.
