## Definition

盐铁会议 / Salt and Iron Conference is the 81 BCE Han Zhao-era policy debate in which court authorities summoned worthy and literary scholars to review Han Wudi-era policies, especially salt and iron monopoly, junshu, and liquor monopoly, under [[HuoGuangWesternHan|Huo Guang]]'s regency.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 397-2 presents the conference as both fiscal-policy review and court-political struggle. On the policy surface, [[SangHongyangWesternHan|Sang Hongyang]] defends the Han Wudi-era state-operation system, while the worthy and literary scholars attack salt, iron, junshu, and liquor monopoly as government competition with the people for profit. On the political surface, Huo Guang can use that criticism to weaken Sang without accepting a total dismantling of the institutions Sang helped build.

The outcome is therefore a limited correction. The conference supports the early Han Zhao "with the people at rest" turn after the [[LateHanWudiDisplacementCrisis|late Han Wudi displacement crisis]], but it does not abolish the main salt and iron system. The episode says the concrete rollback is liquor monopoly and some Guannei iron offices, while the later Yantielun preserves the debate's intensity as a polished record.

## Key Claims

- The conference reviews the political economy of Han Wudi's reign in the shadow of displacement, rebellion, and fiscal exhaustion.
- The worthy and literary scholars treat state operation as a popular-burden problem because officials compete with commoners for profit.
- Sang Hongyang's defense of Han Wudi-era fiscal institutions makes the debate a personal and factional contest as well as a policy discussion.
- Huo Guang uses criticism of the old fiscal program to set a post-Wudi direction while avoiding fiscal disruption from total abolition.
- The settlement is limited: liquor monopoly and some local iron offices are abolished, but salt, iron, and junshu-style state capacity mostly remain.
- The later Yantielun turns the meeting record into a durable text of Western Han fiscal and moral-political argument.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current wiki evidence is limited to a short podcast summary and does not reconstruct the full Yantielun text, the biographies of all participants, or the fiscal effects of the conference's limited rollback. The page should not be read as saying Huo Guang was anti-monopoly in principle; the episode's point is that he used criticism selectively while preserving much of the fiscal machinery.
