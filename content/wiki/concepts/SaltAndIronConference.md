## Definition

盐铁会议 / Salt and Iron Conference is the 81 BCE Han Zhao-era policy debate in which court authorities summoned worthy and literary scholars to review Han Wudi-era policies, especially salt and iron monopoly, junshu, liquor monopoly, and the social costs of direct state economic operation, under [[HuoGuangWesternHan|Huo Guang]]'s regency.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 397-1 supplies the burden background for the conference. The state monopoly system emerges from [[HanWudi|Han Wudi]]'s war-finance and social-control needs, but the episode stresses how official salt and iron administration reaches ordinary households: bad and expensive iron tools, inconvenient official shops, forced sales, higher salt prices, cash-only distribution, hospitality costs shifted onto villages, headcount charges, arrests, and flight.

Hanji 397-2 then presents the conference as both fiscal-policy review and court-political struggle. On the policy surface, [[SangHongyangWesternHan|Sang Hongyang]] defends the Han Wudi-era state-operation system, while the worthy and literary scholars attack salt, iron, junshu, and liquor monopoly as government competition with the people for profit. On the political surface, Huo Guang can use that criticism to weaken Sang without accepting a total dismantling of the institutions Sang helped build.

The outcome is therefore a limited correction. The conference supports the early Han Zhao "with the people at rest" turn after the [[LateHanWudiDisplacementCrisis|late Han Wudi displacement crisis]], but it does not abolish the main salt and iron system. Hanji 397-2 says the concrete rollback is liquor monopoly and some Guannei iron offices, while Hanji 398 links the same post-Wudi recovery direction to stable Han-Xiongnu relations, lighter taxes and corvee, and an edict removing officials tied to several monopoly categories. The later Yantielun preserves the debate's intensity as a polished record.

## Key Claims

- The conference reviews Han Wudi's political economy in the shadow of displacement, rebellion, fiscal exhaustion, and concrete monopoly burdens such as poor goods, high prices, cash friction, quota coercion, and official harassment.
- The worthy and literary scholars treat state operation as a popular-burden problem because officials compete with commoners for profit.
- Sang Hongyang's defense of Han Wudi-era fiscal institutions makes the debate a personal and factional contest as well as a policy discussion.
- Huo Guang uses criticism of the old fiscal program to set a post-Wudi direction while avoiding fiscal disruption from total abolition.
- The settlement is limited: liquor monopoly and some local iron offices are abolished, but salt, iron, and junshu-style state capacity mostly remain.
- The next narrative step links the settlement to a broader rest-and-recovery program: frontier stability, lighter burdens, and partial removal of monopoly offices are presented together.
- The later Yantielun turns the meeting record into a durable text of Western Han fiscal and moral-political argument.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current wiki evidence is limited to short podcast summaries and does not reconstruct the full Yantielun text, the biographies of all participants, or the fiscal effects of the conference's limited rollback. Hanji 397-1 gives vivid burden examples but not regional enforcement variation or aggregate fiscal data. The page should not be read as saying Huo Guang was anti-monopoly in principle; Hanji 397-2's point is that he used criticism selectively while preserving much of the fiscal machinery. Hanji 398's "nine monopoly officials" wording remains source-scoped until checked against the primary text and institutional terminology.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 397-1's concrete monopoly-burden background: bad official goods, higher prices, cash-only distribution, coercive quotas, and flight risk.
- Added Hanji 398's immediate rest-and-recovery extension: partial monopoly-office removal is tied to lighter taxes, corvee reduction, and a stabilized Han-Xiongnu environment.
