# Reproductive-Centered Sex Education / 生殖中心性教育

## Definition
Reproductive-centered sex education teaches sexuality mainly through menstruation, pregnancy, hygiene, and risk while omitting complete genital anatomy, pleasure, consent, and practical bodily self-knowledge.

## Current Synthesis
[[af3491ca3aca3a270aadb44a7f572e93-af3491ca3aca3a270aadb44a7f572e93]] shows how a curriculum can transmit correct reproductive facts yet leave students unable to name external anatomy or locate the clitoris. That gap is not merely missing vocabulary: it defines the body through what it can reproduce rather than how a person experiences, understands, protects, and communicates about it. A broader curriculum would connect accurate anatomy with consent, health, variation, pleasure, and clinical help-seeking while remaining age-appropriate and avoiding the opposite mistake of reducing sexual education to pleasure alone.

## Key Claims
- Reproductive accuracy is necessary but insufficient for complete sexual-health literacy.
- Anatomical naming supports communication, self-observation, consent, and clinical care as well as pleasure.
- Shame can persist even when a term is mentioned if students receive no usable map or permission to ask questions.
- Omitting pleasure can reproduce a value judgment that only fertility-related anatomy deserves formal knowledge.
- Comprehensive education should integrate reproduction, anatomy, consent, safety, variation, and pleasure rather than treating them as competing curricula.

## Evidence
- **Knowledge without a map:** [[af3491ca3aca3a270aadb44a7f572e93-af3491ca3aca3a270aadb44a7f572e93]] recounts speakers who knew the word “clitoris” or its symbolic association with pleasure but did not know its location, shape, or surrounding vulvar anatomy.
- **Curricular boundary:** [[af3491ca3aca3a270aadb44a7f572e93-af3491ca3aca3a270aadb44a7f572e93]] says their school instruction emphasized menstruation, care, and pregnancy rather than a complete account of female genital structure.
- **Alternative practice:** [[af3491ca3aca3a270aadb44a7f572e93-af3491ca3aca3a270aadb44a7f572e93]] places mirror-based vulvar self-examination within 1970s feminist bodily-autonomy work, where seeing and naming the body was treated as a starting point for self-knowledge.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Two speakers' experiences do not establish the content or effect of every school curriculum. Age, culture, family context, teacher preparation, safeguarding, disability access, and misinformation exposure all affect what comprehensive education requires. The source does not evaluate a specific curriculum or measured educational outcome.

## What Changed
- Established the distinction between reproductive instruction and complete, usable sexual-health literacy.
- Connected anatomical naming to consent and care as well as autonomous pleasure.
