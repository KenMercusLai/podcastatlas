## Definition

谗言疑心式反馈失灵 / slander-suspicion feedback collapse is a court-politics pattern in which a ruler's suspicion and susceptibility to slander make truthful advice unstable: worthy people are withdrawn quickly, petty people gain room to coordinate accusations, and the feedback environment deteriorates even before formal policy collapses.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-477-xihan-shouwei-jianchen-da-huanguan-shi-ruhe-raoluan-chaozheng-de-lq_tjdwttakvkciban4sptyyhuhb|Hanji 477]] grounds the concept in [[LiuXiang|刘向 / 刘更生]]'s warning to [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] after [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] repeatedly slanders [[ZhouKanWesternHan|周堪]] and [[ZhangMengWesternHan|张猛 / 张萌]]. Liu Xiang does not treat slander as a private quarrel alone. He links it to court disharmony, disasters, the ruler's unstable judgment, and the wider danger that petty people can turn rumor and factional language into governing signals.

The key mechanism is speed and asymmetry. Worthy people and good measures are abandoned quickly after denunciation, while removing petty men is described as difficult. That makes the ruler's feedback channel progressively worse: loyal recommendation can be misread as faction, slander can be read as vigilance, and ministers who see the danger must use disaster, classical precedent, and indirect warning to speak.

## Key Claims

- Feedback collapse can begin when the ruler treats denunciation as more actionable than sustained evidence of worth.
- Slander becomes structurally dangerous when it pairs with ruler suspicion, not only when the slanderer is powerful.
- Disaster language can serve as an indirect warning that the court's human feedback system has already become disordered.
- Loyal mutual recommendation and petty factional collusion must be distinguished; collapsing them benefits slanderers.
- The pattern drives upright ministers toward retreat, indirect remonstrance, or self-protective speech.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in one source note and should not be used as a complete theory of Western Han factional politics. The episode presents Liu Xiang's memorial through a podcast summary, not the full classical text. Yuan's inner motives, Shi Xian's exact accusation record, and the legal status of Zhou Kan and Zhang Meng remain source-scoped.
