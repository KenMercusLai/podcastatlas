# Anchoring Effect / 锚定效应

Anchoring effect is the pull that an initial number or frame has on later judgment. In [[106-bie-zai-haipa-chongtu-kuai-lai-xuexi-tanpan-shu-742539645]], the first-offer question is treated as conditional: making the first offer can set the anchor when one has good information, but letting the other side go first can reveal their expectations.

The episode uses a real-estate valuation experiment to show that even professionals can be influenced by nearby listed prices while denying the influence. It also suggests extreme counteroffers as a way to offset an extreme anchor, while warning that the counter must be managed carefully so the negotiation does not collapse.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-116-shishang-zuican-taizi-bei-fuwang-qinshou-zhansha-lthruqxtd4iqofdxzvim58ph8rlk]] adds a historical command-selection case. When [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] asks how many troops are needed to conquer [[ChuState|楚国]], [[LiXinQin|李信]] first says 200,000 will suffice; [[WangJianQin|王翦]] later says 600,000 are necessary. The source treats Li Xin's lower first answer as the anchor that makes Wang Jian's larger estimate look aged and timid rather than appropriately cautious.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-2-xuezhedian-qinchao-wujiang-jingran-ruci-gaoqingshang-lsyw3eitcnzfexl1l6vfh2gbtaoy]] gives the outcome of that command anchor. Li Xin and [[MengTian|蒙恬]] initially win cities, but [[XiangYan|项燕]] defeats the expedition near [[NanCommandery|南郡]], forcing Ying Zheng to return to Wang Jian's 600,000-troop requirement. The episode therefore treats anchoring not as a small judgment quirk but as a strategic-scale planning error that must be corrected after battlefield loss.

## Key Claims
- Anchors work partly because later judgments adjust from the first salient number.
- Expertise does not eliminate anchoring pressure.
- First offers are useful only when they are backed by enough information about [[ZoneOfPossibleAgreement]].
- Counter-anchors need explanation and emotional buffering to avoid damaging the relationship.
- Anchors can distort military and managerial judgment as well as bargaining: a lower first estimate may make a larger realistic estimate look like fear or incompetence.
- Later evidence can break an anchor, but the correction may come only after the underestimated cost has already been paid.
