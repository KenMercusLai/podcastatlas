# Cold War Spycraft / 冷战间谍技艺

## Definition

Cold War spycraft is the practical system of identifying, recruiting, handling, protecting, interpreting, and, when necessary, extracting human sources under persistent counterintelligence pressure.

## Current Synthesis

The Gordievsky sources make spycraft a counterimage to glamorous fiction. [[80-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-shang-691866649]] emphasizes file reading, surveillance awareness, patient social contact, motive testing, cautious recruitment, and restraint in using intelligence. [[81-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-xia-693711209]] extends the system into sanitized ally briefings, source-career management, coded signals, diplomatic cover, border improvisation, and rehearsed extraction.

[[37-spies-with-ben-macintyre-glt4382644548]] broadens the framework beyond one operation. [[KimPhilby|Philby]] and [[OlegGordievsky|Gordievsky]] show that ideology can initiate betrayal but need not remain the only motive; [[UrsulaKaczynski|Agent Sonya]] shows that domestic normality and institutional gender bias can serve as cover; and sleeper networks show why undetected cases distort the historical sample. Technical collection expands the system rather than eliminating its dependence on human credibility and interpretation.

## Key Claims

- Recruitment often depends on slow inference, motive testing, and trust rather than direct persuasion.
- Source handling must balance information use against the risk that action reveals the source.
- Ordinary social, domestic, and bureaucratic details can be operationally decisive.
- Escape planning and extraction credibility are part of recruitment and protection, not final-act improvisations.
- Ideology, ambition, secrecy, loneliness, resentment, and fear can combine or change across a career.
- Signals and cyber intelligence remain dependent on human interpretation, access, and believable identities.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

These sources center British and Soviet cases and cannot establish a universal intelligence doctrine. Dramatic successful or exposed agents are vulnerable to selection bias; unknown failures and undetected operations are harder to count. Claims about motive often rely on retrospective testimony, while continuing file closure prevents full verification of handling and policy impact.
