# Cold War Spycraft / 冷战间谍技艺

Cold War spycraft / 冷战间谍技艺 is the episode's counterimage to glamorous spy fiction. [[80-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-shang-691866649]] makes real intelligence work look like file reading, surveillance awareness, social pacing, dead-letter caution, source handling, plausible deniability, and bureaucratic patience.

The concept is built through [[MI6]]'s cultivation of [[OlegGordievsky|Oleg Gordievsky]] and the [[KGB]] world around him. Officers do not simply "flip" a target. They infer vulnerability from patterns, watch reactions, test trust through intermediaries, and delay full exploitation because a valuable source can be lost through one careless use of information.

[[81-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-xia-693711209]] extends the concept from recruitment into extraction and strategic use. Spycraft includes sanitized ally briefings, feeding a source usable low-level intelligence, emergency clothing and bag signals, diplomatic-car theater, paper notes under sheets, anti-tracking shoes, border-dog distractions, and preplanned success phrases.

## Key Claims
- Recruitment often depends on slow inference rather than direct persuasion.
- Spycraft is relational: dignity, fear, resentment, ideology, and personal loneliness affect operational choices.
- Bureaucratic and domestic details can be strategically important because they reveal pressure points.
- Escape planning, meeting signals, and source protection are core parts of intelligence work, not final-act drama.
- The most effective operational tools can be mundane: chocolate, bags, diapers, music, family complaints, and car-handling discipline.
