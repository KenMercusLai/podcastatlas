# Cross-Species Translation Limits

## Definition
Cross-species translation limits are the persistent gaps between decoding another species' signals and sharing its sensory world, concepts, interests, embodiment, or lived experience.

## Current Synthesis
Sensors, behavioral data, and language models may reveal structure in whale sounds, bat calls, bird communication, dolphin behavior, or bodily movement. Yet translation is constrained by each species' Umwelt: polarized light, magnetic fields, echolocation, vibration, smell, and other channels organize realities humans may not inhabit. Better communication can therefore expose incompatibility as well as common ground.

## Key Claims
- Signal regularity is not the same as semantic or experiential equivalence.
- Translation may need movement, environment, and physiology as well as sound.
- Sensory overlap constrains what can be rendered into human concepts.
- Communication does not automatically align interests or social norms.
- Persistent asymmetry can remain even within mutual understanding.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Limits are not proof that translation is futile. Partial, task-specific communication may improve welfare and coordination without yielding complete phenomenological access. The source provides scenarios and forecasts rather than validated general-purpose animal translation.
