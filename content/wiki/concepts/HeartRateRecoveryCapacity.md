# Heart Rate Recovery Capacity / 心率恢复能力

## Definition
Heart rate recovery capacity is the episode's preferred practical field metric for anaerobic and cardiovascular capacity: reach a maximum heart rate with hard work, then track how quickly the heart rate falls, at roughly half a beat per second.

## Current Synthesis
Galpin treats anaerobic capacity as a total physiological limitation reached with roughly 30 to 45 seconds of all-out work, possibly up to 120 seconds, and he uses lab methods such as a 30-second Wingate test or a 60-second Bosco jump protocol as gold standards. In the field he keeps the effort maximal, typically 30 seconds to a minute of sprinting, air bike, or rowing, and avoids kettlebell swings because too many other variables enter the test.

Because no universal standards exist across modalities, the preferred practical metric is the recovery curve. A rough maximum-heart-rate guide is 220 minus age, and recovery markers are roughly 30 beats within the first minute, about 60 by two minutes, with the three-minute value halving again. A curve worse than those markers is treated as evidence of a problem in anaerobic or cardiovascular capacity, while the 220-minus-age figure is explicitly called extremely generic and not an indicator of fitness by itself.

## Key Claims
- Anaerobic capacity is a total physiological limitation reached in roughly 30 to 45 seconds of all-out work, possibly up to 120 seconds.
- Field tests should use a modality without technical constraints, which is why kettlebell swings are avoided for this measurement.
- No universal standards exist because the modality changes absolute results, so completing the test and the recovery response matter more than a score.
- Heart rate recovery is the preferred practical metric, with roughly 30 beats in the first minute and 60 by two minutes as the markers he looks for.
- The 220-minus-age maximum-heart-rate estimate is a generic guide and is explicitly not an indicator of fitness on its own.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Heart-rate recovery is influenced by age, training status, hydration, heat, caffeine, sleep, medication such as beta blockers, and the modality used, so a single curve cannot cleanly separate anaerobic from cardiovascular limitation. The episode presents this as a practical screen rather than a diagnostic test, and maximal-effort protocols are inappropriate for people with cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled blood pressure, or symptoms during exertion without medical clearance.
