# Narrative Creativity Training

## Definition
Narrative creativity training is a structured practice that generates novelty by changing a world's rules, adopting another actor's motivations, or forcing new actions and collaborations inside a problem space.

## Current Synthesis
[[the-science-of-creativity-how-to-enhance-creative-innovation-scim5096680097]] presents three narrative tools as an alternative to manipulating mood or arousal. World building changes the governing constraints; perspective shifting asks what another person or character wants and perceives; and action generation creates interactions or “creative collisions” that the original setup would not produce. Their shared value is bounded imagination: a changed narrative frame makes unfamiliar combinations possible while preserving enough structure for later evaluation.

## Key Claims
- World building generates alternatives by changing the rules and affordances of a setting.
- Perspective shifting expands the option set by modeling another actor's motives and viewpoint.
- Action generation creates new material by forcing interaction, collaboration, or consequence.
- Narrative constraints can support imagination more reliably than novelty without structure.
- The tools can be applied beyond fiction to art, design, teaching, scientific framing, and problem solving.

## Evidence
- Three-tool framework - [[the-science-of-creativity-how-to-enhance-creative-innovation-scim5096680097]] identifies world building, perspective shifting, and action generation as trainable routes into creativity.
- Constraint change - [[the-science-of-creativity-how-to-enhance-creative-innovation-scim5096680097]] describes world building as changing the rules of the creative environment.
- Motivation and collision - [[the-science-of-creativity-how-to-enhance-creative-innovation-scim5096680097]] uses perspective and forced interaction to generate material that does not arise from an unchanged viewpoint.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode summary does not establish comparative training effects, transfer across domains, or superiority to ordinary craft practice. Perspective shifting remains an inference about another mind rather than direct access to it, and forced collaboration can create noise or conflict without good facilitation and later evaluation.

## What Changed
- Established a narrative route to creative training alongside attention- and state-based methods.
