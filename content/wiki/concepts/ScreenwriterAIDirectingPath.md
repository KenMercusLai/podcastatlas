## Definition
The screenwriter-to-director AI path is the hypothesis that sufficiently controllable generative-video tools could let screenwriters translate scripts into audiovisual works with less dependence on traditional production hierarchies.

## Current Synthesis
The episode frames AI as both uncertainty and possible leverage. Song sees routine coordination, continuity, editing, and parts of directing as exposed to automation, but imagines that lower execution barriers could return competitive weight to story, theme, and script quality. In that scenario, a screenwriter gains some of the expressive reach now concentrated in directors and production systems.

The hypothesis does not mean prompting automatically becomes directing. Tool control, performance, continuity, visual judgment, rights, labor, financing, and audience trust remain unresolved. AI can reduce the cost of realization while leaving authorship and production competence demanding.

## Key Claims
- Generative video can narrow the gap between written intention and audiovisual execution.
- Lower execution cost may increase the relative importance of story judgment.
- Routine production roles may be affected earlier than high-level creative integration.
- Screenwriters need new technical and visual literacy to benefit from the shift.
- Direct realization can increase authorial control while also increasing responsibility for the whole work.

## Evidence
- Role forecast: [[lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu-lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu]] records Song's expectation that AI will affect continuity, coordination, postproduction editing, and directing work.
- Writer opportunity: [[lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu-lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu]] calls sufficiently capable text-to-video a possible “黎明” for screenwriters because scripts and ideas could move closer to finished films.
- Capability qualification: [[lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu-lt24pcobvqnyxjeven2dwhuhvmfu]] also says current AI creative work remains early and that writers must update their own methods.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a forecast, not an observed mature production model. Lower technical barriers may shift rather than remove bottlenecks, and could strengthen platforms that control models, compute, rights, distribution, or audience access. The source does not establish job-loss timing, feature-length coherence, production economics, or whether viewers will value AI-realized films similarly.
