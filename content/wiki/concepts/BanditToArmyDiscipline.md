# 盗匪成军纪律化 / Bandit-to-Army Discipline

盗匪成军纪律化 / bandit-to-army discipline is the organization pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-135-mingyun-tai-shenqi-cong-yufu-zuodao-wang-lrh1lhuxxikx4m6prpi6qm0jcafb|Qinji 135]] draws from [[PengYue|彭越]]. The source distinguishes a loose outlaw gathering from a usable military force by whether commands are credible, deadlines matter, and punishment is believed.

Peng Yue's group begins as local young men and bandits who want a leader during the [[ChenSheng|陈胜]] and [[XiangLiang|项梁]] uprising wave. Peng Yue accepts only after setting a sunrise assembly deadline and warning that latecomers will be killed. When many arrive late, he executes the last arrival rather than killing everyone.

The execution is brutal, but the episode's organizational point is narrower: the group stops laughing at the rule and begins obeying. Peng Yue then sets up a ritual platform, declares the uprising, takes surrounding land, and absorbs scattered soldiers until he can meet [[LiuBang|刘邦]] with a real following instead of a casual band.

This differs from [[CharismaticPoliticalPerformance|造势型政治表演]]. Liu Bang's early performance creates status before resources exist; Peng Yue's act creates obedience before the group has mature institutions. Both depend on audience belief, but Peng Yue's mechanism is discipline through credible enforcement.

## Key Claims
- A rebel group is not automatically an army; it needs credible rules, accepted command, and consequences for noncompliance.
- Selective punishment can signal that a leader's words are real without destroying the entire group.
- In a collapse environment, organizational discipline can matter more than noble status or prior office.
- Peng Yue's later ability to attach to Liu Bang depends on the prior transformation from loose followers into a controllable force.
