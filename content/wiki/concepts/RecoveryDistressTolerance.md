# Recovery Distress Tolerance

## Definition
Recovery distress tolerance is the learned ability to experience discomfort, craving, shame, fear, anger, loneliness, or arousal without automatically escaping through the substance or behavior that previously provided rapid relief.

## Current Synthesis
In [[RyanSoave]]'s account, recovery is not a promise that emotional storms disappear. It is the construction of a “better boat”: earlier recognition of dysregulation, greater ability to distinguish discomfort from actual threat, and more choice between feeling and action. That capacity depends on both preparation and response—scheduled practices build familiarity with state change, while brief tools and human contact help during acute moments.

Distress tolerance is not a demand to endure medical danger alone. Physical stabilization and professional care come first where withdrawal, psychiatric risk, or severe impairment makes self-directed exposure unsafe. The concept instead describes what becomes possible after safety is established: staying present long enough to respond rather than react.

## Key Claims
- Recovery requires learning that discomfort is not always equivalent to threat.
- The aim is flexible response under stress, not permanent calm or avoidance of all emotional storms.
- Proactive practice increases access to skills before crisis narrows attention and judgment.
- Reactive tools can create enough space to interrupt an automatic addictive response.
- Connection and disclosure distribute the burden that solitary willpower cannot reliably carry.
- Medical danger and ordinary emotional discomfort must not be conflated.

- Storm metaphor - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] frames peace as navigating storms rather than preventing them.
- Preparation and response - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] distinguishes proactive practices from tools used in the moment.
- Safety ordering - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] places assessment, detoxification when needed, and stabilization before deeper recovery work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Cold exposure, breathwork, exercise, meditation, and yoga nidra or NSDR are discussed as possible practices, not universal prescriptions or replacements for medical treatment. Distress tolerance does not mean ignoring dangerous withdrawal, suicidality, psychosis, severe psychiatric symptoms, or other emergencies.
