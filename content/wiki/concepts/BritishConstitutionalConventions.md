# British Constitutional Conventions

## Definition

British constitutional conventions are unwritten but politically enforceable expectations governing how officeholders use formal power, retain confidence, accept limits, and leave office within the [[ParliamentarySystem|parliamentary system]].

## Current Synthesis

[[205-the-last-days-of-boris-johnson-glt2329301983]] uses [[BorisJohnson|Boris Johnson]]'s July 2022 fall to distinguish legal capacity from sustainable authority. A prime minister may invoke a large election victory and retain formal powers for a time, but the mandate belongs within a parliamentary party and confidence structure rather than to a separately elected presidency. The episode compares these conventions with Roman *mos maiorum*: repeated civic practice can constrain conduct even without one codified text. Their resilience depends on political actors enforcing them, as ministers and MPs eventually did by withdrawing support.

## Key Claims

- Unwritten rules can constrain officeholders when parties, ministers, Parliament, and public judgment enforce them.
- A general-election victory does not create a separate personal presidential mandate for a British prime minister.
- Formal ability to remain in office can outlast the political authority needed to govern.
- Resignation and orderly transfer are constitutional practices, not merely questions of personal dignity.
- A severe breach can demonstrate institutional resilience if other actors impose consequences rather than normalize it.

## Evidence

### Parliamentary confidence over personal mandate

- [[205-the-last-days-of-boris-johnson-glt2329301983]] contrasts Johnson's appeal to the 2019 Conservative vote with the parliamentary reality that MPs, ministers, and party support sustain a prime minister.

### Enforcement through withdrawal

- [[205-the-last-days-of-boris-johnson-glt2329301983]] points to resignations, refusal to defend unsustainable claims, and senior Conservatives turning against Johnson as evidence that conventions retained force.

### Exit conduct

- [[205-the-last-days-of-boris-johnson-glt2329301983]] contrasts Johnson's resistance with faster or more dignified departures and invokes *mos maiorum* to frame restraint as a civic obligation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode is an immediate interpretation recorded before the transition was settled, not a legal treatise or complete test of constitutional damage. Eventual removal does not show that every convention worked promptly, that no institutional harm occurred, or that informal enforcement will succeed in future cases. Historical analogies to Rome illuminate the hosts' moral argument but do not establish institutional equivalence.

## What Changed

- Added a distinction between formal tenure, personal electoral rhetoric, and parliamentary authority.
- Treated enforcement by ministers and MPs as the mechanism that gives unwritten rules practical force.
