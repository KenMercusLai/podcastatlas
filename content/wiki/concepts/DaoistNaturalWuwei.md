# Daoist Natural Wuwei / 道家自然无为

## Definition
Daoist natural wuwei / 道家自然无为 is [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-3-rang-ni-chushi-gengshang-yige-jingjie-laozi-jiao-ni-qiangu-zhihui-llaynmqfl8wfwo7ckkacdxommu9|Hanji 456-3]]'s Laozi-centered claim that human conduct should follow natural arising, decline, work, rest, and self-organization rather than be overdriven by moral display or rigid correction.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses Laozi's reply to [[Confucius|孔子]] to distinguish living nature from proclaimed virtue. Renyi is not rejected as a word, but the source warns that a self-advertised pursuit of renyi can become artificial when it ignores war, self-interest, embodied need, and the way things already move. Wuwei here means less passivity than non-forcing: do not keep calling the child back so insistently that the child runs farther away.

## Key Claims
- Natural wuwei is a critique of forced moralization rather than a license for indifference.
- The source treats human nature as something easily disturbed by over-insistent renyi language.
- Wuwei works through letting people and things follow their own patterns of action, rest, life, and death.
- The episode's child-running-away analogy makes coercive correction itself part of the problem.
- The concept belongs to philosophical self-cultivation, not to the wiki's ritual-technology or alchemical Daoist practice cluster.

## Evidence
Naturalness over moral display:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-3-rang-ni-chushi-gengshang-yige-jingjie-laozi-jiao-ni-qiangu-zhihui-llaynmqfl8wfwo7ckkacdxommu9|Hanji 456-3]] has Laozi ask whether renyi is human nature, then criticize universal-love and selflessness claims when they detach from violent social reality and hidden self-interest.

Non-forcing as practical psychology:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-3-rang-ni-chushi-gengshang-yige-jingjie-laozi-jiao-ni-qiangu-zhihui-llaynmqfl8wfwo7ckkacdxommu9|Hanji 456-3]] uses the child-running-away example to show that excessive calling-back can intensify the very movement one wants to stop.

Daoist practice boundary:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-3-rang-ni-chushi-gengshang-yige-jingjie-laozi-jiao-ni-qiangu-zhihui-llaynmqfl8wfwo7ckkacdxommu9|Hanji 456-3]] keeps this branch philosophical: it does not discuss talismans, alchemy, daoyin, or temple labor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is an interpretive retelling of Laozi instructing Confucius, not a full historical or philological argument about early Daoism. It should not be generalized into a rule that all moral language is harmful, nor into a denial of Confucian ethics elsewhere in the wiki.
