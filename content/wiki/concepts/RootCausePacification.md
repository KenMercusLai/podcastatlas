# 查因安抚式治理 / Root-Cause Pacification

## Definition

Root-cause pacification / 查因安抚式治理 is the governance pattern where officials ask why people rebelled before deciding how much relief, personnel replacement, reassurance, resettlement, and targeted force to use.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence treats rebellion as a governance signal, not only a security event. Hanji 922 explains the Bandun Man revolt through illegal taxes, blocked complaints, and local abuse before repair through amnesty and a clean administrator. Hanji 927 generalizes the same logic through Jia Cong's Jiaozhi appointment: investigate causes, reduce burdens, recall refugees, punish violent leaders, and install capable local officials.

Hanji 892 extends the pattern backward into the Eastern Qiang case. Even after [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] completes a successful frontier campaign, [[SimaGuang|司马光]] and the host reject exterminatory interpretation: Qiang rebellion is linked to long-running commandery exploitation and harsh administration. The concept therefore allows force, but only when force is bounded by diagnosis and repair rather than by treating frontier populations as inherently killable enemies.

## Key Claims

- Rebellion diagnosis starts by asking what local conditions made disorder socially rational.
- Illegal extraction, harsh treatment, and blocked complaint channels convert ordinary subjects or frontier peoples into rebels.
- Durable pacification requires administrative repair: clean officials, burden relief, reassurance, refugee recall, and sometimes resettlement.
- Force can be part of repair, but it should be targeted at violent leadership or immediate military danger rather than generalized revenge.
- Hanji 892 adds an ethical boundary: military success does not erase the ruler's duty to treat non-Han peoples as life-valuing humans.
- Root-cause pacification is narrower than conquest pacification and broader than any one local leniency case.

## Evidence

- Bandun Man diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-922-ruhe-zai-fanchen-zhong-xiulian-qingshang-lsxiqneyyeod6aeeqxuhs517pn1x|Hanji 922]] says Chen Bao attributes the Bandun Man revolt to illegal taxes, forced household sales, ignored complaints, and blocked routes to the capital.
- Bandun repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-922-ruhe-zai-fanchen-zhong-xiulian-qingshang-lsxiqneyyeod6aeeqxuhs517pn1x|Hanji 922]] says Emperor Ling appoints Cao Qian to Ba Commandery and issues amnesty, after which the Bandun people submit quickly.
- Jiaozhi diagnosis and repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-927-zuo-shengyi-de-zuigao-jingjie-shi-shenme-lngfbjn6xui9k-9lp-ihdqkhpbpr|Hanji 927]] says Jia Cong investigates extraction and blocked complaints, reassures people, recalls refugees, exempts corvee labor, kills major violent leaders, and appoints clean county officials.
- Eastern Qiang ethical boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] uses Sima Guang's critique of Duan Jiong's campaign to argue that Qiang and other frontier peoples cannot be treated as animals or grasses to be killed without distinction.
- Eastern Qiang root cause: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] accepts that Qiang rebellion arose from commandery-level exploitation and harshness, so better officials and generals could have reduced unrest.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Root-cause pacification does not claim that every rebellion can be solved without coercion. The current sources include military defeat, execution of violent leaders, and forced settlement. The concept's boundary is that coercion should follow diagnosis and repair needs; it should not become an excuse for extraction, blanket extermination, or denial that abused populations have intelligible grievances.
