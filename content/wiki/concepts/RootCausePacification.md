## Definition

Root-cause pacification / 查因安抚式治理 is the governance pattern where officials ask why people rebelled, became thieves, or fled authority before deciding how much personnel replacement, clean administration, burden relief, reassurance, pardon, resettlement, surrender policy, and targeted force to use.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence treats rebellion and theft as governance signals, not only security events. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] now supplies an earlier local-bandit case through [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]: as Jingzhou inspector he sends officials to comfort and pardon thieves, keeps the surrender promise when Xia Mi and more than six hundred leaders submit, and restores order by making state grace credible. The same source extends the pattern at Taishan, where Li Gu dismisses most troops, keeps a small capable force, and uses reputation plus pardon rather than generalized pursuit.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] supplies the earliest explicit Qiang-front diagnosis: [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|皇甫规]] argues that clear politics and lawful, clean commandery governors matter more than merely finding fierce generals or studying military classics. The same episode gives negative personnel controls through [[GuoHongLateHan|郭闳]], whose merit-seeking obstruction damages [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]]'s campaign, and [[HuHongLateHan|胡闳]], whose weak replacement command lets Qiang attacks worsen.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-856-lishi-gaosu-ni-ren-ruo-xinluan-zhenjiao-zi-luan-lo7m-9mz-pvstxtfgavrne4bszks|Hanji 856]] then supplies the operational continuation: Huangfu Gui stabilizes his own army, then impeaches frontier officials tied to extraction, killing surrenderers, and incompetence before wider Qiang surrender follows. Liu Yu's Huan-era warning supplies the court-level layer, connecting eunuch privilege, palace spending, mansion construction, harsh labor, judicial bribery, and popular desperation to banditry and revolt if the ruler treats symptoms as isolated disturbances.

Hanji 922 explains the Bandun Man revolt through illegal taxes, blocked complaints, and local abuse before repair through amnesty and a clean administrator. Hanji 927 generalizes the same logic through Jia Cong's Jiaozhi appointment: investigate causes, reduce burdens, recall refugees, punish violent leaders, and install capable local officials.

Hanji 883 and Hanji 892 extend the pattern into the Eastern Qiang case from opposite sides of the campaign. Hanji 883 records the policy dispute before later pacification: [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]] favors surrender and peace because residual Qiang groups are hard to eliminate, while [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] argues for hard suppression and rejects inward resettlement. Hanji 892 gives the ethical and causal qualification after Duan's victory: [[SimaGuang|司马光]] and the host reject treating Qiang people as inherently killable enemies and connect rebellion to long-running commandery exploitation. The concept therefore allows force, but only when force is bounded by diagnosis and repair rather than by treating frontier populations as permanent weeds to uproot.

## Key Claims

- Disorder diagnosis starts by asking what local conditions made rebellion, theft, or flight socially rational.
- Illegal extraction, harsh treatment, judicial corruption, palace waste, and blocked complaint channels convert ordinary subjects or frontier peoples into rebels.
- Personnel accountability can itself be pacification when abusive, obstructive, or incompetent officials are part of the reason people refuse trust.
- Clean political administration can matter more than heroic commanders when frontier disorder is fed by the governing side's conduct.
- Durable pacification requires administrative repair: clean officials, burden relief, reassurance, refugee recall, and sometimes resettlement.
- Force can be part of repair, but it should be targeted at violent leadership or immediate military danger rather than generalized revenge.
- Hanji 883 adds that surrender policy itself needs diagnosis: conciliation can reduce killing, but unintegrated surrender may also create provisioning, settlement, or rebound risks.

## Evidence

- Jingzhou pardon and surrender: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] says Li Gu comforts and pardons thieves, honors the promise after Xia Mi and more than six hundred heads submit, and restores order within about half a year.
- Taishan reduced-force pacification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] says Li Gu dismisses most troops, keeps a small selected force, and pacifies long-running thieves through grace and authority.
- Governance-before-heroics diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] has Huangfu Gui argue that clear politics and lawful, clean commandery administrators matter more for Qiang disorder than simply seeking brave generals or rereading war books.
- Negative personnel cases: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] shows Guo Hong's merit-seeking obstruction and Hu Hong's weak replacement command as internal defects that worsen the Qiang-front field.
- Surrender and renewed-risk field: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] says Huangfu Gui wins surrender from Xianling and other Qiang groups through prestige and trustworthiness, but renewed Shendi Qiang attacks still create a later crisis.
- Qiang-front personnel repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-856-lishi-gaosu-ni-ren-ruo-xinluan-zhenjiao-zi-luan-lo7m-9mz-pvstxtfgavrne4bszks|Hanji 856]] says Huangfu Gui impeaches frontier officials tied to corruption, killing surrenderers, and incompetence, after which wider Qiang submission and road reopening follow.
- Internal stabilization before pacification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-856-lishi-gaosu-ni-ren-ruo-xinluan-zhenjiao-zi-luan-lo7m-9mz-pvstxtfgavrne4bszks|Hanji 856]] says Huangfu Gui first reassures an isolated, epidemic-hit army, showing that the governing side's morale and credibility must be repaired before surrender policy can work.
- Court-level cause chain: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-866-zhenzheng-huomingbai-de-ren-dongde-sanbuzhan-lhhmmyjfo6mkf-qs6bhhzu8hhln6|Hanji 866]] has Liu Yu connect eunuch enfeoffment, palace women, fiscal waste, mansion construction, harsh labor, and judicial bribery to public distress.
- Rebellion-warning logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-866-zhenzheng-huomingbai-de-ren-dongde-sanbuzhan-lhhmmyjfo6mkf-qs6bhhzu8hhln6|Hanji 866]] says grievances can become banditry and wider disorder when the ruler ignores small disturbances.
- Bandun Man diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-922-ruhe-zai-fanchen-zhong-xiulian-qingshang-lsxiqneyyeod6aeeqxuhs517pn1x|Hanji 922]] says Chen Bao attributes the Bandun Man revolt to illegal taxes, forced household sales, ignored complaints, and blocked routes to the capital.
- Bandun repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-922-ruhe-zai-fanchen-zhong-xiulian-qingshang-lsxiqneyyeod6aeeqxuhs517pn1x|Hanji 922]] says Emperor Ling appoints Cao Qian to Ba Commandery and issues amnesty, after which the Bandun people submit quickly.
- Jiaozhi diagnosis and repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-927-zuo-shengyi-de-zuigao-jingjie-shi-shenme-lngfbjn6xui9k-9lp-ihdqkhpbpr|Hanji 927]] says Jia Cong investigates extraction and blocked complaints, reassures people, recalls refugees, exempts corvee labor, kills major violent leaders, and appoints clean county officials.
- Eastern Qiang policy dispute: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] contrasts Zhang Huan's surrender-and-peace recommendation with Duan Jiong's argument for continued suppression and opposition to inward resettlement.
- Eastern Qiang ethical boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] uses Sima Guang's critique of Duan Jiong's campaign to argue that Qiang and other frontier peoples cannot be treated as animals or grasses to be killed without distinction.
- Eastern Qiang root cause: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] accepts that Qiang rebellion arose from commandery-level exploitation and harshness, so better officials and generals could have reduced unrest.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Root-cause pacification does not claim that every rebellion can be solved without coercion. The current sources include military defeat, execution of violent leaders, forced settlement, and suspicion that surrender can be temporary. Hanji 822 itself has Li Gu keep a small capable force in Taishan, so trust-backed amnesty is paired with selective coercive capacity rather than the absence of force. Hanji 855 has Huangfu Gui defeat Qiang forces and then face renewed Shendi Qiang pressure, so even trust-based surrender does not close the frontier problem permanently. The concept's boundary is that coercion should follow diagnosis and repair needs; it should not become an excuse for extraction, blanket extermination, judicial sale, or denial that abused populations have intelligible grievances.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 822 as an earlier local-bandit pacification case through Li Gu's Jingzhou pardon and Taishan reduced-force method.
- Reframed the concept to include theft and flight from authority as diagnostic disorder signals alongside open rebellion.
- Kept Hanji 855 as the earliest explicit Qiang-front diagnosis and Hanji 856 as its operational repair layer.
- Kept the Bandun, Jiaozhi, Eastern Qiang, and court-level disorder cases inside the same bounded pattern.
