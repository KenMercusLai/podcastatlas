## Definition

Root-cause pacification / 查因安抚式治理 is the governance pattern where officials ask why people rebelled, became thieves, or fled authority before deciding how much personnel replacement, clean administration, burden relief, reassurance, pardon, resettlement, surrender policy, and targeted force to use.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence treats rebellion and theft as governance signals, not only security events. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-823-bingbuxueren-shou-zeikou-ta-ruhe-zuodao-de-luai1f0nhb-ddajli99dw4bxj9m2|Hanji 823]] now gives the clearest domestic-rebel version through [[ZhangGangLateHan|张纲]] and [[ZhangYingGuanglingLateHan|张婴]]: Zhang Gang names official greed and violence as the grievance behind Zhang Ying's long rebellion, separates that grievance from the legitimacy of rebellion, pairs pardon with a credible warning of imperial force, and then makes surrender livable through dispersal, residence, farmland, and office pathways.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] supplies an adjacent local-bandit case through [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]: as Jingzhou inspector he sends officials to comfort and pardon thieves, keeps the surrender promise when Xia Mi and more than six hundred leaders submit, and restores order by making state grace credible. The same source extends the pattern at Taishan, where Li Gu dismisses most troops, keeps a small capable force, and uses reputation plus pardon rather than generalized pursuit.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] supplies the earliest explicit Qiang-front diagnosis: [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|皇甫规]] argues that clear politics and lawful, clean commandery governors matter more than merely finding fierce generals or studying military classics. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-856-lishi-gaosu-ni-ren-ruo-xinluan-zhenjiao-zi-luan-lo7m-9mz-pvstxtfgavrne4bszks|Hanji 856]] then gives the operational continuation: Huangfu Gui stabilizes his army, impeaches frontier officials tied to extraction, killing surrenderers, and incompetence, and sees wider surrender follow.

Hanji 922 explains the Bandun Man revolt through illegal taxes, blocked complaints, and local abuse before repair through amnesty and a clean administrator. Hanji 927 generalizes the same logic through Jia Cong's Jiaozhi appointment: investigate causes, reduce burdens, recall refugees, punish violent leaders, and install capable local officials.

Hanji 883 and Hanji 892 extend the pattern into the Eastern Qiang case from opposite sides of the campaign. Hanji 883 records the policy dispute before later pacification: [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]] favors surrender and peace because residual Qiang groups are hard to eliminate, while [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] argues for hard suppression and rejects inward resettlement. Hanji 892 gives the ethical and causal qualification after Duan's victory: [[SimaGuang|司马光]] and the host reject treating Qiang people as inherently killable enemies and connect rebellion to long-running commandery exploitation.

The concept therefore allows force, but only when force is bounded by diagnosis and repair. Hanji 823 sharpens that boundary: the point is not to flatter rebels or waive public order; it is to know which grievance must be acknowledged, which coercive threat must remain credible, and which post-surrender arrangements make return to ordinary life possible.

## Key Claims

- Disorder diagnosis starts by asking what local conditions made rebellion, theft, or flight socially rational.
- Illegal extraction, harsh treatment, judicial corruption, palace waste, and blocked complaint channels convert ordinary subjects or frontier peoples into rebels.
- Personnel accountability can itself be pacification when abusive, obstructive, or incompetent officials are part of the reason people refuse trust.
- Clean political administration can matter more than heroic commanders when frontier disorder is fed by the governing side's conduct.
- Durable pacification requires administrative repair: clean officials, burden relief, reassurance, refugee recall, credible pardon, and sometimes land, residence, office, or resettlement arrangements.
- Force can be part of repair, but it should be targeted at violent leadership or immediate military danger rather than generalized revenge.
- Surrender policy itself needs diagnosis because conciliation can reduce killing while unintegrated surrender can create provisioning, settlement, or rebound risks.

## Evidence

- Domestic-rebel grievance and settlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-823-bingbuxueren-shou-zeikou-ta-ruhe-zuodao-de-luai1f0nhb-ddajli99dw4bxj9m2|Hanji 823]] says Zhang Gang acknowledges official abuse as the cause of Zhang Ying's anger, warns that rebellion remains illegitimate and militarily dangerous, and settles surrender through dispersal, housing, land, and office pathways.
- Local-bandit pardon and selective force: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] says Li Gu comforts and pardons Jingzhou thieves, honors the promise after Xia Mi and more than six hundred heads submit, and later pacifies Taishan by dismissing most troops while keeping a small capable force.
- Governance-before-heroics diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] has Huangfu Gui argue that clear politics and lawful, clean commandery administrators matter more for Qiang disorder than simply seeking brave generals or rereading war books.
- Negative personnel cases: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] shows Guo Hong's merit-seeking obstruction and Hu Hong's weak replacement command as internal defects that worsen the Qiang-front field.
- Qiang-front repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-856-lishi-gaosu-ni-ren-ruo-xinluan-zhenjiao-zi-luan-lo7m-9mz-pvstxtfgavrne4bszks|Hanji 856]] says Huangfu Gui first reassures an isolated, epidemic-hit army, then impeaches frontier officials tied to corruption, killing surrenderers, and incompetence, after which wider Qiang submission and road reopening follow.
- Court-level cause chain: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-866-zhenzheng-huomingbai-de-ren-dongde-sanbuzhan-lhhmmyjfo6mkf-qs6bhhzu8hhln6|Hanji 866]] has Liu Yu connect eunuch enfeoffment, palace women, fiscal waste, mansion construction, harsh labor, and judicial bribery to public distress and rebellion risk.
- Eastern Qiang policy dispute: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] contrasts Zhang Huan's surrender-and-peace recommendation with Duan Jiong's argument for continued suppression and opposition to inward resettlement.
- Eastern Qiang ethical boundary and root cause: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] uses Sima Guang's critique of Duan Jiong's campaign to reject treating frontier peoples as animals or grasses and accepts commandery exploitation as a cause of rebellion.
- Bandun Man diagnosis and repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-922-ruhe-zai-fanchen-zhong-xiulian-qingshang-lsxiqneyyeod6aeeqxuhs517pn1x|Hanji 922]] attributes the revolt to illegal taxes, forced household sales, ignored complaints, and blocked routes to the capital, then shows amnesty and Cao Qian's clean administration producing quick submission.
- Jiaozhi diagnosis and repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-927-zuo-shengyi-de-zuigao-jingjie-shi-shenme-lngfbjn6xui9k-9lp-ihdqkhpbpr|Hanji 927]] says Jia Cong investigates extraction and blocked complaints, reassures people, recalls refugees, exempts corvee labor, kills major violent leaders, and appoints clean county officials.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Root-cause pacification does not claim that every rebellion can be solved without coercion. The current sources include military defeat, execution of violent leaders, forced settlement, and suspicion that surrender can be temporary. Hanji 823 itself works because Zhang Gang combines grievance recognition with a credible multi-province military alternative, not because he treats rebellion as harmless. Hanji 822 has Li Gu keep a small capable force in Taishan, and Hanji 855 has Huangfu Gui defeat Qiang forces and still face renewed Shendi Qiang pressure. The concept's boundary is that coercion should follow diagnosis and repair needs; it should not become an excuse for extraction, blanket extermination, judicial sale, or denial that abused populations have intelligible grievances.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 823 as the full Zhang Gang-Zhang Ying negotiation and settlement case.
- Reframed durable pacification to require post-surrender livelihood and status pathways when disorder comes from official abuse.
- Preserved Hanji 822 as the Li Gu layer linking credible pardon to selected coercive capacity.
- Kept Hanji 855 and Hanji 856 as the Huangfu Gui frontier-diagnosis and personnel-repair branch.
- Kept the Bandun, Jiaozhi, Eastern Qiang, and court-level disorder cases inside the same bounded pattern.
