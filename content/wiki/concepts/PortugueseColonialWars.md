# Portuguese Colonial Wars

## Definition

The Portuguese Colonial Wars were the African conflicts through which the late [[EstadoNovoPortugal|Estado Novo]] attempted to retain its empire, especially in Angola, Mozambique, and Guinea.

## Current Synthesis

Across the sources, the wars bridge imperial identity, metropolitan poverty, and regime collapse. The earlier episode places uprisings in Angola, Mozambique, and Guinea-Bissau beside Goa's loss, alignment with South Africa and Rhodesia, and [[Eusebio|Eusébio]]'s symbolic prominence, showing how “pluri-continental” identity became harder to sustain. The later episode follows the institutional consequences: war absorbed state resources, repeatedly exposed officers to unwinnable or politically unwanted campaigns, elevated critics such as [[AntonioDeSpinola|Spinola]], radicalized figures such as [[OteloSaraivaDeCarvalho|Otelo]], and made rapid decolonization central after the coup.

## Key Claims

- Colonial war imposed severe fiscal and military strain on Portugal.
- The wars exposed the contradiction between a multiracial national claim and coercive imperial retention.
- Repeated African service politicized younger professional officers.
- Disagreement over whether and how to leave Africa fractured the armed forces and regime elite.
- Rapid decolonization after April 1974 helped widen the break between Spinola and [[VascoGoncalves]].
- More than half a million returnees from former colonies added social pressure inside Portugal.

## Evidence

- Imperial-crisis evidence: [[230-portugal-football-fado-and-fascism-part-4-glt9147423732]] connects uprisings, casualties, poverty, Goa's loss, and southern African alliances to late-Salazar strain.
- Symbolic contradiction: [[230-portugal-football-fado-and-fascism-part-4-glt9147423732]] places Mozambique-born [[Eusebio|Eusébio]] at the center of Portuguese prestige while colonial conflict escalates.
- Fiscal and personnel evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] gives the wars a very high budget share and locates key coup officers' political change in African service.
- Leadership evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] connects Spinola's dismissal to criticism of the African-war strategy.
- Transition evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] says decolonization accelerated under Goncalves and brought a large return migration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both episodes focus mainly on Portuguese leadership, officers, and metropolitan politics. African liberation movements, civilian experience, country-by-country chronology, and the consequences of decolonization require independent treatment. The reported casualty total, quoted 40 percent budget share, and claims about Rhodesian sanctions evasion remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added the wars' earlier imperial-identity crisis, regional alignments, and Eusébio symbolism.
- Preserved the wars as an institutional incubator of Portuguese regime change.
