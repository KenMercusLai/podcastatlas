# Home Router Security Lifecycle

Home router security lifecycle is the practical security problem created when household routers age out of support, stop receiving patches, or become too hard for ordinary users to inspect. [[tech-20260415-0415-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260415-0415-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[BrianKrebs]]' advice that consumers who have not replaced a router in about five years should consider doing so.

The concept does not claim that every old router is infected. Its point is operational: when compromise is difficult to detect and old hardware may not be secured, replacement can be a more realistic mitigation than asking consumers to perform forensic inspection.

## Key Claims
- Consumers often cannot easily tell whether a router or connected device has been compromised.
- Router age matters because support, automatic patching, and security defaults change over time.
- Newer routers may provide better Wi-Fi and a period of automatic security updates.
- Household router replacement can reduce [[IoTBotnetRisk]] even when it is not a perfect diagnostic tool.
