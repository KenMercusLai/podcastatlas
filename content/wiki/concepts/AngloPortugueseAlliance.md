# Anglo-Portuguese Alliance

## Definition

The Anglo-Portuguese alliance is the durable diplomatic relationship the source traces from medieval crusading contact and commerce through formal treaty and dynastic marriage.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents the alliance as layered rather than created by a single document. English crusaders helped [[AfonsoHenriques]] capture Lisbon in 1147; a 1353 commercial treaty linked London and Porto through cloth, wine, oil, salt, cork, and other goods; a 1372 treaty added royal diplomacy; and the 1386 Treaty of Windsor formalized cooperation.

[[PhilippaOfLancaster|Philippa of Lancaster's]] marriage to [[JohnIOfPortugal|João I]] then gave that framework a family and succession dimension. The alliance's durability rests in the episode on repeated commercial, military, diplomatic, and dynastic reinforcement rather than a timeless national friendship.

## Key Claims

- The alliance developed cumulatively across crusading, trade, treaty, and marriage.
- English participation at Lisbon functions as historical prehistory, not yet the complete later alliance.
- Commercial exchange between London and Porto helped make interstate cooperation materially useful.
- The Treaty of Windsor and Philippa-João marriage converted practical alignment into formal and dynastic commitment.

## Evidence

- Crusading contact: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] connects English crusaders to Afonso's 1147 capture of Lisbon.
- Commercial evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] cites the 1353 London-Porto treaty and its varied traded goods.
- Diplomatic evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] names the 1372 treaty and the 1386 Treaty of Windsor.
- Dynastic evidence: [[227-portugal-on-the-edge-of-the-world-part-1-glt1834337589]] presents Philippa's marriage as the alliance's royal seal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode calls this England's oldest alliance and one of the world's oldest surviving alliances, but it does not examine interruptions, treaty interpretation, asymmetric interests, or later imperial and commercial conflicts. The page therefore records the episode's layered origin account, not an unbroken claim of policy identity across every period.
