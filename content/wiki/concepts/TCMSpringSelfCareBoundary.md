# TCM Spring Self-Care Boundary / 中医春季养生边界

## Definition
TCM spring self-care boundary is the separation between low-risk, adaptable seasonal routines and [[MaoZheTCMDoctor|毛哲]]'s source-scoped liver, wind, dampness, constitution, food-therapy, herbal, and acupoint interpretations.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's most defensible practical layer is moderation. Spring temperatures can fluctuate, so clothing should follow actual conditions; gentle outdoor movement can be useful without turning “春捂” into overheating; ordinary seasonal food can take priority over premium ingredients; and sleep, mood, exercise, and diet slogans need personal limits rather than literal or extreme application.

The clinical boundary is equally important. The source organizes spring through the liver, growth, wind, cold, dampness, qi, blood, yin, yang, and constitutional patterning, then connects those categories to foods, teas, herbs, moxibustion, and acupoints. These are claims from one TCM podcast guest, not validated diagnostic rules. Severe allergy, prolonged amenorrhea, pallor, persistent gastrointestinal symptoms, recurrent illness, marked cold intolerance, or substantial mood change require qualified assessment rather than seasonal self-treatment.

## Key Claims
- Seasonal self-care should adapt to weather, tolerance, symptoms, place, and activity rather than follow one fixed calendar rule.
- Clothing, comfortable movement, regular sleep, ordinary food, and known-allergen avoidance form a lower-risk behavioral layer.
- The source's liver-and-growth model and its wind, dampness, qi, blood, yin, yang, and “发物” categories remain TCM interpretations rather than established diagnoses.
- Food, tea, herbs, patent medicines, moxibustion, and acupoints require indication, dose, interaction, contraindication, and evidence checks that the episode does not supply.
- Dangerous, prolonged, or unexplained symptoms override seasonal-wellness experimentation and need appropriate medical care.

- TCM framework: [[vol-103-zhongyike-zhongyi-dafu-jiao-ni-shipuo-chunji-yangsheng-yingxiao-pianju-fang-guomin-chunji-yinshi-zheme-chi-liocs7vqytyxu6y4oaqlwj57h0jp]] attributes liver, wind, dampness, constitutional, food, tea, moxibustion, and acupoint claims to Mao Zhe.
- Escalation boundary: [[vol-103-zhongyike-zhongyi-dafu-jiao-ni-shipuo-chunji-yangsheng-yingxiao-pianju-fang-guomin-chunji-yinshi-zheme-chi-liocs7vqytyxu6y4oaqlwj57h0jp]] treats severe allergy as potentially life-threatening and directs the listener reporting a year without menstruation toward medical care.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides no clinical validation that its seasonal signs identify a liver, wind, dampness, qi, blood, yin, yang, or constitutional pattern, and it does not establish the efficacy or safety of its foods, teas, herbs, patent medicines, moxibustion schedule, or acupoints. Weather-appropriate clothing and gentle activity are not treatments for allergy, amenorrhea, anemia, endocrine disease, infection, gastrointestinal disease, or mood disorders. “Late sleep” in the episode remains bounded and is not evidence that sleep restriction is health-promoting.
