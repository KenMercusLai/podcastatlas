# Medicine As Way-Seeking / 以医入道

Medicine as way-seeking / 以医入道 is [[LiChuang|李闯]]'s provisional phrase in [[shan-shang-shan-xia-jie-renjian-wushi-xiaoyao-ji-shenxian-lhjiguq74flhatdxnbrnzhjg-acp]] for approaching life, body, and natural order through medical study. The episode connects his long-term bodily discomfort, decision to study Chinese medicine, and county-hospital internship to a broader question: what can medicine see when illness is also shaped by diet, occupation, sleep, money, and social rhythm?

The concept is deliberately bounded. It does not turn Chinese medicine into settled scientific proof, and it does not reject modern clinical care. Instead, it extends [[MedicalKnowledgeBoundary]] by asking how diagnostic uncertainty, resource limits, and ordinary living conditions push people between hospitals, self-care, Daoist practice, and belief.

## Key Claims
- Medicine can be a way to ask what life and bodily order require, not only a professional track.
- The source treats illness as partly social: heavy labor, high-oil/high-salt food, long driving, sitting, and sleep disruption shape what clinicians encounter.
- Clinical uncertainty should produce humility and better observation, not automatic cynicism or mystical certainty.
- The episode's health advice stays practical: meals, sleep, movement, calm, and recovery matter before abstract doctrine.
