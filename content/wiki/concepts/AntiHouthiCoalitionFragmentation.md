# Anti-Houthi Coalition Fragmentation

## Definition
Anti-Houthi coalition fragmentation is the weakness created when forces opposing [[Houthis]] are divided by ideology, region, command rivalry, tribal alignment, and external-sponsor competition.

## Current Synthesis
[[strait-and-narrowing-the-houthis-striking-advance-6aa7c25aff74edfd002ca6c7]] argues that the Houthi advance along Yemen's western coast should not be read only as Houthi or [[Iran]]ian strength. [[GregCarlstrom]] emphasizes that the Saudi-backed side is a loose coalition of religious fundamentalists, southern separatists, tribal groups, and rival commanders. The split between [[SaudiArabia]] and the [[UnitedArabEmirates]] worsened that disunity after the UAE withdrew troops from Yemen.

The concept matters because fragmented opposition can make a battlefield shift look more decisive than the attacker's capacity alone would suggest. Retreats, mutual accusations of betrayal, and sponsor divergence create openings that a cohesive adversary can exploit.

## Key Claims
- Coalition heterogeneity can become operational weakness when factions defend different priorities.
- External sponsor disagreement can widen local military fractures.
- Retreats and betrayal accusations are symptoms of low command trust.
- Houthi gains are therefore a product of both offensive pressure and anti-Houthi disarray.

## Evidence
- Coalition composition: [[strait-and-narrowing-the-houthis-striking-advance-6aa7c25aff74edfd002ca6c7]] lists religious fundamentalists, southern separatists, and tribal groups among anti-Houthi forces.
- Sponsor split: [[strait-and-narrowing-the-houthis-striking-advance-6aa7c25aff74edfd002ca6c7]] says divisions widened after Saudi Arabia and the UAE fell out and UAE troops left Yemen.
- Battlefield behavior: [[strait-and-narrowing-the-houthis-striking-advance-6aa7c25aff74edfd002ca6c7]] says some coastal defenders retreated and rival commanders accused one another of betrayal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not claim fragmentation is the only cause of the Houthi advance. It keeps Iranian support, Houthi military capacity, and Saudi strategic choices in the frame, but treats coalition disunity as a major enabling condition.

## What Changed
- Added a coalition-failure concept for Yemen that separates Houthi strength from anti-Houthi organizational weakness.
