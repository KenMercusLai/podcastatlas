# AI Election Misinformation Risk

## Definition
AI election misinformation risk is the danger that generated media, messages, profiles, search results, or chatbot answers give voters false, outdated, biased, or misleading information about participation, candidates, ballot measures, or trustworthy information channels.

## Current Synthesis
The risk is broader than spectacular deepfakes. Campaigns can use AI for speed, scale, targeting, and persuasion; hostile actors can combine deceptive profiles, links, malware, and information operations to increase division and distrust; and search systems can surface synthetic pages about when, where, or how to vote. State disclosure and restriction laws address part of the media problem, but enforcement remains fragmented and constitutionally constrained.

The newest evidence adds an ordinary-use pathway. A voter asking a general-purpose chatbot about a current proposition can receive an outdated, misgrounded answer without any malicious operator being visible. Precise prompts, multiple systems, and citation inspection reduce exposure but do not prove correctness, because models can share stale sources, links can carry ideological framing, and agreeable language can disguise weak evidence. Election resilience therefore requires both upstream governance and downstream voter verification.

## Key Claims
- Election misinformation can target voting logistics, candidate facts, ballot measures, or trust in the process rather than only fabricate candidate media.
- AI increases the speed, scale, personalization, and local appearance of misleading content and campaign communication.
- General-purpose chatbots can create civic misinformation through temporal or contextual misgrounding even without a coordinated influence operation.
- Public voter data, search poisoning, and tailored answers can make false claims feel personally relevant or authoritative.
- State deepfake laws add disclosure and restriction tools, but uneven rules, limited penalties, liability uncertainty, and First Amendment constraints leave gaps.
- Cross-model comparison and source inspection improve voter resilience, but agreement, citations, and fluent prose are not independent proof.
- Public education and verification practices must be renewed as models, tactics, election contexts, and platform surfaces change.

## Evidence
- Operational campaign risk - [[tech-20260105-0105-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260105-0105-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports campaign use of AI for messaging, targeting, outreach, analysis, persuasion, and strategy while warning about personalized voting misinformation and search poisoning.
- Regulatory response - [[tech-20260814-tech-pod-128-tech-20260814-tech-pod-128]] describes state disclosure and restriction laws, constitutional challenges, limited penalties, and unresolved AI-tool liability.
- Information-operations expansion - [[tech-20260819-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260819-mp-tech-pod-128]] connects election distrust to deceptive profiles, reconnaissance, suspicious links, malware, and state-linked influence operations.
- Ordinary chatbot failure - [[tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that ChatGPT confused a current California proposition with a 2024 measure and made an incorrect eligibility statement.
- Voter verification limits - [[tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261019-1019-mp-tech-pod-128]] shows that model comparison and link inspection can expose disagreement or source positioning but cannot guarantee truth.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The 2024 U.S. campaign source says feared deepfake spectacle did not dominate that cycle, showing that risk forecasts need not become observed outcomes. State rules may deter some conduct, and chatbots can still help voters organize complex information. The sources do not quantify overall incidence, vote effects, or the relative contribution of AI versus older propaganda and ordinary human error. Cyber attribution, product behavior, legal validity, and election-specific facts remain time-sensitive and jurisdiction-dependent.
