## Definition

Embodied technology–class inequality is the conversion of unequal wealth or access into unequal bodily materials, functions, replaceability, or social status through technical modification.

## Current Synthesis

[[ATrueStory|《真实的故事》]] imagines eyes and reproductive organs that can be removed, exchanged, stored, or replaced. This weakens a simple boundary between natural body and external tool, but it does not make embodiment equal: the rich use ivory organs while the poor use wooden ones.

The episode therefore treats bodily liberation and stratification as compatible outcomes. Technology may reduce dependence on a given organ or reproductive arrangement, yet access, quality, durability, and prestige can write wealth differences more deeply into bodily life.

## Key Claims

- Replaceable organs blur the boundary between body and tool.
- Bodily flexibility does not guarantee equal access or equal quality.
- Material choice can turn wealth into visible embodied status.
- Relief from a biological constraint can coexist with redesigned social dependence.
- Identity questions about memory, consciousness, action, and relationship remain after bodily material changes.

## Evidence

- Detachable organs: [[371-liangqian-nianqian-jiu-you-ren-xiangxiang-chu-saiboge-nanren-duli-shengyu-xingji-zhanzheng-le-ma-zhenshi-de-gushi-lije6fkmg3ilr65bi-7zovbns-r1]] describes removable eyes and reproductive organs.
- Material stratification: [[371-liangqian-nianqian-jiu-you-ren-xiangxiang-chu-saiboge-nanren-duli-shengyu-xingji-zhanzheng-le-ma-zhenshi-de-gushi-lije6fkmg3ilr65bi-7zovbns-r1]] distinguishes ivory organs used by the rich from wooden organs used by the poor.
- Persistent hierarchy: [[371-liangqian-nianqian-jiu-you-ren-xiangxiang-chu-saiboge-nanren-duli-shengyu-xingji-zhanzheng-le-ma-zhenshi-de-gushi-lije6fkmg3ilr65bi-7zovbns-r1]] notes that extensive bodily modification leaves wealth and political rank intact.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The example is satirical fiction, not evidence about the distribution, performance, or social effects of modern prosthetics, implants, transplantation, or reproductive medicine. “Cyborg” is a retrospective analogy, and the source does not establish that Lucian predicted contemporary technology or developed a systematic theory of identity.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the episode's link between replaceable organs and unequal bodily materials.
