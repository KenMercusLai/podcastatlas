# AI Marketing Jargon

AI marketing jargon is the formulaic public language of AI companies when ads, billboards, and brand campaigns signal technical seriousness without clearly explaining the product. [[tech-20260714-tech-pod-128-tech-20260714-tech-pod-128]] grounds the concept through [[HarrisAlterman]] and [[DaveRoss]]'s [[FakeAISubwayAds]] project, which reproduced the style of real AI subway ads by recombining repeated phrases, logos, solid-color backgrounds, and vague transformation claims.

The concept is adjacent to [[AISlop]] but not identical to AI-generated content. In this source, the problem is not that a model wrote the copy; the problem is that public-facing corporate language can become so generic, investor-coded, and interchangeable that parody only has to rearrange familiar words. It also differs from [[AIGeneratedAdvertising]], which focuses on AI systems producing ad assets or campaigns.

The episode links jargon to legitimacy. A subway ad can confuse ordinary riders while still making the company look real, funded, and important. In that sense, AI marketing jargon can function as [[StartupLegitimacyTransfer]]: public visibility itself becomes a credibility signal even when the product remains opaque.

## Key Claims
- Vague AI ads can be polished and expensive while giving ordinary viewers little information about the actual product.
- Repeated terms such as cloud, safety, democratization, transformation, and "from X to Y" can make unrelated companies sound interchangeable.
- AI marketing may target investors, enterprise buyers, recruits, or the hype ecosystem more than the general public.
- Public transit, billboards, and other visible ad spaces can transfer credibility because viewers infer that advertised companies are real and well funded.
- Satire can reveal formulaic marketing language by preserving the surface design while removing any remaining product substance.
- AI marketing jargon can weaken [[TrustAsBusinessAsset]] if visibility substitutes for intelligibility.
