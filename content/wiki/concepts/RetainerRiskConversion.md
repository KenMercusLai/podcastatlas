# Retainer Risk Conversion / 门客风险转化

Retainer risk conversion / 门客风险转化 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-43-mengchangjun-qizi-touqing-bei-faxian-ta-zha-chuli-lr2idcktgfxtdkik1biaj9tiz8ix]] draws from [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君 / 田文]]'s handling of a retainer who has an affair with his wife. The episode does not treat the response as simple moral tolerance. It frames it as delayed, indirect risk management inside [[WarringStatesRetainerPatronage|战国养士]].

The risk is serious because the woman is Mengchang Jun's wife rather than a concubine, so the scandal threatens household order, authority, and possible succession legitimacy. Immediate punishment could advertise disorder or turn a retainer problem into factional fear; doing nothing would weaken Mengchang Jun's own authority. His solution is to preserve surface calm, then separate the retainer from the household with resources and a destination.

The conversion happens after the retainer reaches [[WeyState|卫国]]. Because Mengchang Jun sends him with material support and a ruler-facing introduction, a dangerous insider becomes an external asset. When Wey later considers joining an attack on [[QiState|齐国]], the retainer persuades the ruler to withdraw by invoking old Qi-Wey ties and Mengchang Jun's relationship.

## Key Claims
- A patronage network creates internal risks as well as usable talent: retainers can defend honor, damage order, or embarrass the household.
- Risk conversion depends on timing. Mengchang Jun neither reacts at the moment of discovery nor ignores the problem permanently.
- Redeployment works because the retainer keeps dignity, resources, and a career path instead of being treated only as a punished offender.
- The source's praise is pragmatic rather than sentimental: a personal scandal becomes diplomatic leverage.
- The episode keeps the story source-scoped because the Wey attack scene may contain narrative embellishment.
