# Remnant Resistance Base / 残余政权根据地

Remnant resistance base / 残余政权根据地 is the post-capital-fall pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-1-jintian-liaoliao-yingzheng-dou-you-naxie-canren-shouduan-lmqky6g3qi9nklpdnye4lk5rrbz6]] develops through [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]]'s flight to [[DaiState|代地 / 代国]]. After [[Handan|邯郸]] falls and [[ZhaoYoumiuWang|赵迁 / 赵幽缪王]] is captured, Zhao Jia takes several hundred clan members north, attracts Zhao officials unwilling to surrender to [[QinState|秦国]], and joins with [[YanState|燕国]] in the Shanggu area.

The source treats Dai as more than a hideout. It is a historically meaningful Zhao frontier space with strategic depth, local attachment, and memories of earlier Zhao expansion. That makes it capable of supporting a six-year resistance arc even after Zhao's capital and recognized king are gone.

The concept also shows how previous enmity can narrow under conquest pressure. Yan and Zhao have fought repeatedly in the earlier Qinji sequence, but Zhao Jia's remnant regime and Yan share an immediate survival problem once Qin controls most of Zhao's territory and [[WangJianQin|王翦]] is stationed at [[ZhongshanState|中山]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-2-chi-gua-le-wangshi-de-gua-bao-tian-lns7jt-kbv3qunm66hzltdgugbco]] broadens the pattern from a named remnant regime to displaced elite concentration. After [[ChuState|楚国]] falls, the episode warns that nobles, officials, and families from Chu and the Three Jin may flow toward still-intact [[QiState|齐国]]. Here the base is not yet a declared alternate kingdom; it is a receiving state where anti-Qin personnel and memory could accumulate.

## Key Claims
- A capital's fall does not automatically end resistance if a peripheral region has geography, elite refugees, and a usable political name.
- A remnant base needs more than sentiment; it also needs distance, defensible terrain, and personnel willing to attach themselves to the surviving claimant.
- Old interstate hostility can be overridden by survival pressure when the dominant conqueror becomes the more immediate threat.
- A remnant regime's title choice may reveal legal constraints created by a captured ruler who remains alive.
- A surviving state can become a remnant-resistance reservoir even before it hosts a formal claimant regime.
