# Competition Metric Value Compression / 竞赛指标压缩价值

## Definition
Competition metric value compression is the loss of meaningful difference that occurs when a system testing many capabilities must produce one winner through a narrow, short-horizon measure.

## Current Synthesis
The concept comes from the episode's reading of 《街头餐厅斗士》. The show tests menu choice, pricing, customer knowledge, marketing, inventory, staffing, equipment failure, service, and adaptation across locations. Yet a single-day final crowns a familiar, inexpensive, large-portion product with a free extra, making the result look like a proof of mass-market common sense rather than a judgment of the differentiated abilities the season exposed.

The problem is not that popularity or sales are irrelevant. It is that one outcome measure cannot simultaneously value repeat custom, target-segment fit, cultural distinctiveness, operational model, long-run reputation, and broad immediate appeal. Multiple categories or time horizons would preserve more of the tested value without eliminating competition.

## Key Claims
- A complex competition can gather rich evidence and still compress it at the final ranking step.
- Short-run sales favor familiar, affordable, generous, and easily legible offers.
- One-day performance cannot reliably measure repeat use, durable reputation, or segment-specific value.
- A universal winner can punish deliberate differentiation even when different contestants solve different customer problems well.
- Multiple victory conditions can improve evaluation when values are plural and not reducible to one scale.
- The same compression risk applies beyond entertainment to cultural markets and product metrics.

## Evidence
- Capability breadth: [[shi-yue-guangbo-dianshibao-wo-yijing-buzai-shi-wo-er-ni-que-yiran-shi-ni-gkwridooptsjbelolwtoxdnu]] describes repeated tests of menu, price, audience, marketing, cost, service, inventory, and live problem-solving.
- Final compression: [[shi-yue-guangbo-dianshibao-wo-yijing-buzai-shi-wo-er-ni-que-yiran-shi-ni-gkwridooptsjbelolwtoxdnu]] says the winning pork-cutlet offer combined familiar taste, low price, large portions, and free ice cream.
- Missing horizons: [[shi-yue-guangbo-dianshibao-wo-yijing-buzai-shi-wo-er-ni-que-yiran-shi-ni-gkwridooptsjbelolwtoxdnu]] notes that one service day cannot test repeat custom, long-run reputation, or each restaurant's value to its intended public.
- Alternative design: [[shi-yue-guangbo-dianshibao-wo-yijing-buzai-shi-wo-er-ni-que-yiran-shi-ni-gkwridooptsjbelolwtoxdnu]] proposes distinct awards for sales, ratings, culture, or operating model.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
A competition needs legible rules, and multiple awards can weaken suspense or permit organizers to avoid hard comparison. Mass appeal is a real form of value, not evidence of audience failure. The source evaluates an edited entertainment program rather than the restaurants' long-run businesses, so the general framework should be tested against actual evaluation goals and data.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the restaurant-competition critique.
- Distinguished narrow measurement from false measurement: the final result may be valid for immediate mass appeal while remaining incomplete for broader value.
