# Airport Route Subsidy Urban Branding / 航线补贴与城市国际化

## Definition
Airport route subsidy urban branding is the use of public support for airline routes to make a city appear more internationally connected, economically open, and visible to travelers or businesses.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats route subsidies as soft infrastructure. Building an airport does not automatically create global connectivity; cities need airlines to open and maintain routes, and local governments may subsidize international flights to make the city legible abroad.

This strategy is not presented as purely operational. Direct routes to cities such as Budapest, Vancouver, Vienna, or Brussels can become a city's international calling card, especially when geopolitical constraints limit some routes and push cities toward alternative corridors.

## Key Claims
- Routes are soft infrastructure layered on top of airports.
- Local governments may subsidize international routes to increase city visibility and perceived internationalization.
- Route strategy changes with geopolitics, such as post-pandemic U.S.-China route limits pushing attention toward Europe.
- Subsidized routes create branding value but still need durable demand to avoid becoming fragile public spending.

- Second-tier route examples: [[403-cong-bashi-nian-feixing-shi-kan-chengshi-shehui-he-women-ziji-de-gaibian-guotai-hangkong-x-shengdongjixi-53eaa77d]] discusses Ningbo-Budapest, Qingdao-Vancouver, and post-pandemic European route efforts.
- City-branding claim: [[403-cong-bashi-nian-feixing-shi-kan-chengshi-shehui-he-women-ziji-de-gaibian-guotai-hangkong-x-shengdongjixi-53eaa77d]] frames direct routes to European cities as internationalization and name-recognition tools.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not evaluate subsidy amounts, route survival after subsidy, passenger load factors, or opportunity cost. It should not be read as proof that route subsidies are generally efficient.

## What Changed
- Created a concept for international route subsidies as city branding and connectivity policy.
- Added a soft-infrastructure layer to the airport-development branch.
