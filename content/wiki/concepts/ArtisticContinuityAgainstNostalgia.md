# Artistic Continuity Against Nostalgia

## Definition
Artistic continuity against nostalgia is the principle that a living artist may honor the relationship created by older work without freezing that work into the arrangement, sound, or identity audiences first remember.

## Current Synthesis
[[CuiJian|崔健]] treats changing arrangements, current collaborators, unfinished new songs, and continued learning as parts of one responsibility: perform from the present rather than simulate the past. [[RenkeMusician|仁科]] similarly says he does not begin old-song decisions from the audience's position, but he concedes that this autonomy should not become absolute. Together the sources preserve final artistic authority while making audience memory, recognition, and the purpose of a large live show practical constraints rather than irrelevant demands.

## Key Claims
- A remembered version can be emotionally authoritative for a listener without becoming the only legitimate performance.
- Updating old work and creating new work can preserve an artist-audience relationship by keeping it alive rather than merely satisfying recognition.
- Anniversary framing intensifies nostalgia but does not require historical reconstruction.
- Artistic responsibility includes current judgment, technical readiness, and the freedom to revise until performance or recording.
- Present-tense freedom can be qualified by the event's communicative purpose and listeners' reasonable expectation of recognition.

## Evidence
- Present-tense performance: [[cuijian-luoyonghao-qu-tiantang-he-qu-diyu-dou-yao-daishang-yishujia-ljzslbjyhppwf11pkwo009u2hxii]] records Cui's preference to perform according to his current feeling even when older listeners want original arrangements.
- Continuing relationship: [[cuijian-luoyonghao-qu-tiantang-he-qu-diyu-dou-yao-daishang-yishujia-ljzslbjyhppwf11pkwo009u2hxii]] distinguishes caring about longtime listeners from returning to an earlier creative state.
- Revision boundary: [[cuijian-luoyonghao-qu-tiantang-he-qu-diyu-dou-yao-daishang-yishujia-ljzslbjyhppwf11pkwo009u2hxii]] says immature new songs should not enter the anniversary concert and that lyrics and music can remain adjustable until recording.
- Qualified audience constraint: [[lml-euxpi9ekjvk-ihnkfzwp4ysq-lml-euxpi9ekjvk-ihnkfzwp4ysq]] records Luo arguing that large-concert audiences may want familiar old-song versions and Renke acknowledging that the artist-first position cannot be taken to an extreme.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources primarily present artists' and one interviewer-listener's views. They do not measure audience satisfaction, compare concert formats, test whether extensive rearrangement weakens participation, or settle when reinterpretation becomes disregard for the work listeners paid to hear.

## What Changed
- Added Renke's concession that artistic autonomy should not become total disregard for audience expectation.
- Reframed nostalgia as a practical communicative constraint without granting listeners final artistic authority.
