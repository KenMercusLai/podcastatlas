# Command Continuity Damage Control / 统帅连续性危机处置

Command continuity damage control / 统帅连续性危机处置 is the crisis mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-1-liubang-geqiang-chaofeng-qi-de-xiangyu-shejian-touxi-lhs6miq2aa3avnny-prxtvkfxgvy|Hanji 164-1]] draws from [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s arrow wound at [[GuangwuChuHan|广武]]. After [[XiangYu|项羽]] secretly shoots him, Liu Bang claims the arrow hit his toe rather than his chest and returns to camp without revealing the wound's severity.

The deception is not battlefield trickery against the enemy in the usual sense. Its first audience is Liu Bang's own army. A visibly incapacitated ruler could make [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s next attack look imminent and make Han troops doubt whether command still holds, so the story of a minor foot wound protects the army's interpretation of the crisis.

[[ZhangLiang|张良]] then supplies visible continuity by patrolling the camp and calming soldiers. In the source's reading, the leader's body, the announced wound story, and a trusted strategist's public presence combine to prevent a tactical hit from becoming a morale collapse.

## Key Claims
- A commander wound can become a command-system crisis if soldiers believe the center has been disabled.
- Damage control may require controlling friendly interpretation before controlling enemy information.
- A proxy presence can help preserve continuity when the main leader cannot safely appear.
- The pattern is adjacent to [[BattlefieldInformationControl|战场信息控制]], but its primary problem is internal morale and command confidence rather than deceiving the opponent's army.
