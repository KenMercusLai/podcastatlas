## Definition

统帅连续性危机处置 / Command Continuity Damage Control is the practice of preventing a leader's injury, assassination, death, or visible incapacity from becoming collective command collapse. It focuses on public continuity signals, staff substitution, field handoff, and rapid successor or proxy performance.

## Current Synthesis

The concept now has nonfatal, fatal succession, and fatal field-command cases. At Guangwu, [[LiuBang|Liu Bang]] hides the seriousness of [[XiangYu|Xiang Yu]]'s arrow wound and uses [[ZhangLiang|Zhang Liang]] as a visible stabilizer. In Hanji 1045, [[SunCe|Sun Ce]] cannot hide the outcome, so damage control shifts to successor designation: seal transfer to [[SunQuan|Sun Quan]], [[ZhangZhaoLateHan|Zhang Zhao]]'s camp tour with the young heir, and [[ZhouYuLateHan|Zhou Yu]]'s armed presence in [[WuCommanderyLateHan|Wu Commandery]].

The battlefield-assassination case in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-710-shui-si-le-jiemi-donghan-zui-zhuming-de-ansha-shijian-lok1aczap1u-tmtyutx-upokaplz|Hanji 710]] shows continuity work under lethal pressure. [[LaiXiEasternHan|Lai Xi]] is fatally wounded by an assassin sent after [[HuanAnEasternHan|Huan An]]'s command-targeting proposal. Instead of letting Gai Yan's panic set the army's tone, Lai Xi summons him, stops the weeping, assigns military affairs, writes a final memorial, recommends a blunt official, and only then removes the blade and dies.

The new case clarifies that continuity work can occur before formal successor appointment. [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] later appoints [[MaChengEasternHan|Ma Cheng]] to succeed Lai Xi, but the immediate damage-control action happens in the wounded commander's tent, where the key problem is preserving current operations before the court can respond.

## Key Claims

- The first threat after a commander's injury can be morale and legitimacy collapse rather than the wound alone.
- Public narrative matters: a leader may minimize injury, recode the event, or project continuity through deputies.
- Trusted staff can substitute for the leader's visible presence when the principal cannot safely appear.
- When the leader is dying, the key move shifts from concealment to rapid succession performance.
- A fatally wounded commander can still perform field-level continuity by stopping panic, delegating authority, and writing final instructions before death.
- Formal successor appointment can come after immediate continuity work has already stabilized the command.
- The pattern follows [[LeaderPersonalExposure|领导者轻身涉险]] when exposed movement or combat creates damage to the command center.

## Evidence

- Nonfatal concealment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-1-liubang-geqiang-chaofeng-qi-de-xiangyu-shejian-touxi-lhs6miq2aa3avnny-prxtvkfxgvy|Hanji 164-1]] shows Liu Bang publicly minimizing Xiang Yu's arrow wound and presenting it as a minor toe injury.
- Deputy visibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-1-liubang-geqiang-chaofeng-qi-de-xiangyu-shejian-touxi-lhs6miq2aa3avnny-prxtvkfxgvy|Hanji 164-1]] has Zhang Liang patrol the camp to stabilize order while Liu Bang cannot openly expose weakness.
- Fatal succession handoff: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1045-ta-shi-jiangdong-baye-de-kaichuangzhe-kexi-si-de-tai-wonang-lgjjdmcfw1qkuba2xblgvvlfpy9d|Hanji 1045]] has Sun Ce hand the seal to Sun Quan and instruct advisers to assist him after the assassination wound.
- Visible successor performance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1045-ta-shi-jiangdong-baye-de-kaichuangzhe-kexi-si-de-tai-wonang-lgjjdmcfw1qkuba2xblgvvlfpy9d|Hanji 1045]] shows Zhang Zhao taking Sun Quan through the camps and Zhou Yu remaining as central guard.
- Fatal field handoff: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-710-shui-si-le-jiemi-donghan-zui-zhuming-de-ansha-shijian-lok1aczap1u-tmtyutx-upokaplz|Hanji 710]] shows Lai Xi stopping Gai Yan's panic, assigning military affairs, writing a memorial, and delaying removal of the blade until continuity steps are complete.
- Court-level replacement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-710-shui-si-le-jiemi-donghan-zui-zhuming-de-ansha-shijian-lok1aczap1u-tmtyutx-upokaplz|Hanji 710]] says Liu Xiu grieves over Lai Xi's final memorial and appoints Ma Cheng to continue the command.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Concealment, visible succession, and field-command handoff are different tools and should not be merged mechanically. Liu Bang's case preserves the existing leader's authority; Sun Ce's case transfers authority to a successor and staff team; Lai Xi's case stabilizes the army before formal court replacement. Hanji 710 proves that the assassination did not immediately dissolve command, but it does not reconstruct every later western-front decision made by Ma Cheng or Gai Yan.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 1045 as a fatal-successor case alongside Liu Bang's nonfatal wound concealment.
- Migrated the page to synthesis-v1 and broadened the concept from injury masking to command continuity after incapacity.
- Added Hanji 710 as a fatal field-command handoff case after Lai Xi's assassination wound.
- Distinguished immediate camp-level stabilization from later formal successor appointment.
