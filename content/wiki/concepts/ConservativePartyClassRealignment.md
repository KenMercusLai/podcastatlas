# Conservative Party Class Realignment

## Definition
Conservative Party class realignment is the episode's pattern in which the British right's social center of gravity shifts from patrician, landed, paternalist Toryism toward a more assertive middle-class, grammar-school, southern, free-market identity.

## Current Synthesis
[[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] uses [[MargaretThatcher|Thatcher]]'s 1975 victory to show a class and style change inside [[ConservativePartyUK|the Conservative Party]]. Thatcher and [[EdwardHeath|Heath]] shared some meritocratic features, but the hostility she received from senior Conservatives exposed class- and gender-coded prejudice. Her victory over Heath and [[WillieWhitelaw]] suggested that an older establishment idiom was losing control of the party's future.

## Key Claims
- Thatcher's rise exposed tension between patrician Conservative paternalism and aspirational middle-class Tory identity.
- Class prejudice and gender prejudice were intertwined in elite Conservative reactions to Thatcher.
- Heathite critics treated Thatcherism as a break with true Toryism, but the episode argues free-market liberalism was not alien to Conservative history.
- The 1975 vote revealed a social geography: Heath supporters were more public-school, landed, Scottish, or Welsh, while Thatcher supporters were more southern, grammar-school, middle-class, and right-wing.
- Domestic imagery around housework and household budgets translated economic argument into a class-coded political persona.

## Evidence
- Elite contempt: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] lists senior Conservative insults against Thatcher, including class- and gender-loaded descriptions.
- Shared meritocracy but different symbolism: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] describes both Heath and Thatcher as grammar-school, Oxford, serious, and ambitious, while still showing Thatcher as a different social signal.
- Voting pattern: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] contrasts Heath's supporters with Thatcher's supporters by education, geography, and class.
- Household messaging: [[662-britain-in-the-70s-the-rise-of-thatcher-part-1-glt1785737730]] says Thatcher presented herself as a wife and mother who understood shopping, household budgets, and inflation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a party-history concept derived from one episode, not a complete sociology of Conservative membership or voters. Heath himself was not simply an aristocrat, and Thatcher's victory depended on tactical contingency as well as class realignment.
