# Synthetic Happiness

## Definition
Synthetic happiness is genuine happiness generated through deliberate internal, behavioral, social, and environmental processes rather than only through obtaining a desired external outcome.

## Current Synthesis
[[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] contrasts synthetic happiness with natural happiness, which follows outcomes such as gaining a partner, degree, income, or meaningful work. The distinction is not external versus imaginary: resources, sleep, light, work, and relationships still matter, while attention, generosity, gratitude, environment, and post-choice framing influence how experience is interpreted and sustained.

The concept is most useful as an agency claim with boundaries. People can cultivate conditions that make happiness more available, but the episode does not say that mindset cancels poverty, trauma, illness, isolation, or unsafe circumstances. Synthetic happiness is therefore a supplement to material and relational support, not a reason to blame people for distress.

## Key Claims
- Happiness can be cultivated through processes as well as received from desired outcomes.
- Synthetic happiness is presented as genuine rather than imagined, forced, or merely performative gratitude.
- Attention, prosocial giving, contextual gratitude, social contact, and supportive surroundings are candidate inputs.
- External resources and internal practices interact; neither side is a complete explanation.
- The framework preserves the reality that trauma and unmet needs can substantially constrain wellbeing.

## Evidence
- Natural and synthetic distinction: [[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] defines natural happiness through desired outcomes and synthetic happiness through deliberate internal and behavioral processes.
- Prosocial and gratitude inputs: [[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] reports that spending on others and giving time, attention, effort, or help can support happiness, while gratitude effects depend on understood need and intention.
- Attention and environment: [[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] links focused attention, brief meditation, music, visual surroundings, and environmental cues to the capacity to cultivate happiness.
- Material and trauma boundaries: [[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] says money can buffer stress and rejects the claim that major trauma necessarily leaves long-term happiness unchanged.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source ranges across many studies without supplying full methods, effect sizes, replication history, or individualized context. Synthetic happiness should not be interpreted as proof that every practice works for every person, that happiness is fully controllable, or that structural deprivation, disability, trauma, or clinical illness can be resolved by reframing.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish cultivated happiness from happiness attached to obtaining a desired result.
- Made material conditions and trauma explicit limits on the agency claim.
