# Wen Wang Guanren Fa / 文王官人法

Wen Wang Guanren Fa / 文王官人法 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-1-lishishang-diyi-chounan-nixi-zuo-qinxiang-lvmdrgbhvpbiyngm-nviunour0sk]] as the host's cultural expansion from [[TangJu|唐举]]'s physiognomy of [[CaiZe|蔡泽]]. The episode cites the "文王官人" tradition in 《礼记》 as a systematic way to judge people for office, shifting the discussion from ugly appearance to deeper tests of character and ability.

The source does not develop the full method in detail; its wiki role is to mark a talent-assessment frame that belongs beside other personnel-judgment concepts. Unlike pure 相面, this frame is presented as an attempt to evaluate conduct, quality, and suitability for office. That makes it adjacent to [[SituationalCharacterTests|五观识人法]] and [[TalentReferralQuality|荐才层级质量]] rather than a synonym for fortune-telling.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-135-fanwaipian-3-jiao-ni-xiangmian-kanren-xiaojiqiao-ruhe-yi-mao-kantou-mingyun-lpupsu8fduwdwbdg8xq8gjxegztu|Qinji 135 番外篇3]] extends the same personnel-judgment neighborhood through [[ZengGuofan|曾国藩]] and [[Bingjian|《冰鉴》]]. That source leans much more heavily on visible spirit, bones, affect, and language. The useful link to 文王官人法 is the governance question of how to judge role fit; the important distinction is that physical appearance claims need the caution preserved by [[PhysiognomyMoralAppearanceFallacy|以貌定德谬误]].

## Key Claims
- Appearance can start a story about recognition, but it is not sufficient for judging political talent.
- Classical personnel assessment is treated as a governance problem: rulers need methods for identifying character and usable ability.
- The episode uses 文王官人法 as background context, so this concept remains source-scoped until later ingests add more detail.
- Later person-reading traditions may borrow the same personnel purpose while relying on weaker evidence, especially when they infer character from face or bone structure.
