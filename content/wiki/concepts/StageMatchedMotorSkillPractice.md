# Stage-Matched Motor Skill Practice

## Definition
Stage-matched motor skill practice is the principle that repetition speed, attentional target, external timing cues, and mental rehearsal should change as a learner moves from basic acquisition toward refinement.

## Current Synthesis
Beginners first need enough ordinary attempts to generate accurate proprioceptive and outcome feedback. Moving extremely slowly too early may distort the movement and produce too few informative errors. Once basic control exists, slow practice can isolate movement features, and attention can shift from only the result toward specific components of technique.

External cadence and visualization are secondary tools. A metronome can raise output or impose timing pressure when a learner can already respond to the cue. Mental rehearsal can strengthen movement-command representations, but it remains incomplete without muscle activation, spinal patterning, sensory feedback, and real-world correction.

## Key Claims
- Early learners benefit most from safe full attempts that produce authentic feedback and errors.
- Slow-motion practice is primarily a refinement tool after some proficiency exists.
- Attention should shift from broad success or failure toward specific movement features as skill improves.
- Metronomes can add cadence and productive pressure for learners who already have enough control.
- Visualization supplements but does not replace physical practice.

## Evidence
- Early-versus-later speed - [[essentials-how-to-learn-skills-faster-scim2224786015]] says ultra-slow practice may be less useful at very low success rates and more useful after basic proficiency.
- Attention shift - [[essentials-how-to-learn-skills-faster-scim2224786015]] moves attention from errors and outcomes toward selected movement components as performance improves.
- External cue - [[essentials-how-to-learn-skills-faster-scim2224786015]] presents metronomes as a way to increase cadence, output, and error-success cycles for intermediate or advanced learners.
- Rehearsal boundary - [[essentials-how-to-learn-skills-faster-scim2224786015]] distinguishes upper-motor-neuron rehearsal from the fuller biological loop of actual movement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode's approximate success-rate thresholds and metronome advantage are source-scoped rather than universal cutoffs. Appropriate progression depends on the task, injury risk, feedback quality, coach input, fatigue, and learner. Visualization may be useful, but the source does not establish that it transfers equally across skills or populations.

## What Changed
- Created a proficiency-dependent framework for choosing motor-practice tools.
- Preserved physical practice as the foundation beneath slow work, cadence cues, and visualization.
