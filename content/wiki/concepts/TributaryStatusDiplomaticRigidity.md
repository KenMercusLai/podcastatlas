## Definition

朝贡身份秩序外交僵化 / Tributary Status Diplomatic Rigidity is the failure mode where a court treats changed diplomatic practice as an attack on its ritual hierarchy and therefore protects symbolic subordination even at high fiscal, military, or strategic cost.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 422-2 uses [[XianfengEmperor|咸丰帝]] and the post-Treaty of Tianjin resident-envoy issue as the founding case. In the episode's telling, foreign ambassadors living in Beijing were ordinary in Western diplomatic practice, but Xianfeng read their presence through tribute hierarchy: if they would not kneel, then allowing them near the emperor would expose the weakening of "天朝上国" status.

The source's striking point is that status protection could become more irrational than the tribute subsidies themselves. Xianfeng briefly considers sacrificing all customs duties to keep foreign envoys out of Beijing, then moves toward war when that bargain is blocked. The concept therefore tracks a late-stage rigidity: the court is no longer merely paying for recognition; it is risking revenue, military defeat, and diplomatic credibility to preserve an inherited hierarchy.

## Key Claims

- Diplomatic modernization can be perceived as ritual demotion when a court is organized around tribute hierarchy.
- Kneeling, audience protocol, envoy residence, and capital access can become symbols of world order rather than mere etiquette.
- Status anxiety may make a ruler accept fiscal losses that ordinary policy calculation would reject.
- Internal officials may still recognize practical fiscal constraints even when the ruler frames the issue as face and hierarchy.
- The source treats late-Qing rigidity as a continuation of tribute-order imagination under treaty-era pressure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is based on one popular-history source's interpretation of Xianfeng's motives and the treaty crisis. It should not be used as a complete explanation of the Second Opium War, which also involved coercive foreign demands, military imbalance, imperial fiscal weakness, treaty enforcement, and broader Qing institutional constraints.
