# Trading Outcome Bias / 交易结果偏误

## Definition

Trading outcome bias is the error of judging a decision mainly by the profit or loss that followed, rather than by the information, sizing, risk limits, and process available when the trade was made.

## Current Synthesis

EP95 makes the bias visible through a lucky recovery. Kurumi abandons her intended exit, endures a painful reversal, hopes for a rebound, and eventually profits. The favorable result then teaches the wrong lesson: waiting and rule-breaking appear validated, so later confidence and size increase.

The corrective is not to ignore results. It is to evaluate them across comparable repetitions while separating decision quality from randomness. A moved stop can remain a poor decision even when price reverses; a planned small loss can remain a sound decision even when the asset later rebounds.

## Key Claims

- One profitable trade cannot validate the process that produced it.
- Lucky recovery can reinforce overconfidence more strongly than an immediate loss.
- Moving a stop changes the ex-ante risk contract rather than merely waiting for more information.
- Decision review should preserve the original thesis, evidence, size, and invalidation rule.
- Hit rate must be interpreted with payoff ratio, drawdown, costs, and ruin risk.
- Authority or another trader's profit does not transfer their suitability, information, or risk capacity to a follower.

### Repeated-game evaluation
- [[ep95-fx-zhanshi-jiuliumei-mama-kuidiao-de-qian-wo-yao-qinshou-zhuan-huilai-lgdurd85j7_cbimza4cegor-jfly|EP95]] argues that win rate alone is incomplete and should be paired with the size of wins and losses.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Outcome information still matters: repeated losses can reveal a bad model, poor execution, or changed regime. The bias lies in letting one realized path erase what was knowable before the trade, not in refusing to learn from results. The source offers a fictional teaching case rather than audited trade records, and it does not specify a universal optimal stop or payoff ratio.
