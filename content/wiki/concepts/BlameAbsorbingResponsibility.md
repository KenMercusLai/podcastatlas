## Definition

揽过式责任承担 / Blame-Absorbing Responsibility is the practice of voluntarily taking visible fault or procedural liability in order to protect a superior, stabilize a dangerous power situation, or preserve a morally important relationship without denying the underlying problem.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 832 gives the concept its first source case through [[ChenShiLateHan|陈寔]]. [[HouLanLateHan|侯览]] pressures [[GaoLunLateHan|高伦]] into an unsuitable appointment, and Chen recognizes both sides of the trap: the person should not hold office, but Hou Lan's power cannot be ignored without consequence. Chen privately absorbs the responsibility for the appointment document, letting Gao's reputation remain intact.

The episode's judgment is that this is not mere workplace submission or blind loyalty. Gao later tells the public that Chen gave the good name to his superior and took the fault onto himself. Chen still refuses to push blame back onto Gao. The concept therefore marks responsibility as a moral act under constraint: it can reduce damage and protect another person, but it also risks public misunderstanding and can blur accountability if treated as a general tactic.

## Key Claims

- Blame absorption differs from passive scapegoating when the actor knowingly chooses responsibility to reduce harm under constraint.
- The practice depends on moral credibility; without trust, it can look like corruption, cowardice, or opaque favoritism.
- It can protect a superior's reputation while preserving room for later truth-telling.
- It is politically risky because public observers may only see the visible fault, not the coercive pressure behind it.
- It should remain bounded to urgent responsibility under pressure, not generalized into covering up bad appointments.

## Evidence

- Coercive appointment setting: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-832-wei-lingdao-beiguo-ta-shi-sha-haishi-zhi-lmfpuixmi4odrf6lu31rf6-66-lq|Hanji 832]] says Hou Lan's request makes Gao Lun comply despite the candidate's unsuitability.
- Voluntary responsibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-832-wei-lingdao-beiguo-ta-shi-sha-haishi-zhi-lmfpuixmi4odrf6lu31rf6-66-lq|Hanji 832]] says Chen Shi proposes signing or handling the appointment responsibility himself.
- Public misunderstanding: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-832-wei-lingdao-beiguo-ta-shi-sha-haishi-zhi-lmfpuixmi4odrf6lu31rf6-66-lq|Hanji 832]] says local people criticize Chen because they do not understand why he appears to recommend an unsuitable person.
- Later truth-telling: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-832-wei-lingdao-beiguo-ta-shi-sha-haishi-zhi-lmfpuixmi4odrf6lu31rf6-66-lq|Hanji 832]] says Gao Lun later explains Chen's conduct publicly before departing for shangshu service.
- Continuing self-attribution: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-832-wei-lingdao-beiguo-ta-shi-sha-haishi-zhi-lmfpuixmi4odrf6lu31rf6-66-lq|Hanji 832]] says Chen still treats the matter as his own fault even after Gao's explanation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is morally narrow. It should not be used to justify routine cover-ups, bad personnel choices, or loyalty that hides harm from affected people. Hanji 832 is persuasive because the source emphasizes coercive power, later disclosure, and Chen Shi's broader moral reputation; without those conditions, blame absorption may become accountability evasion.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Chen Shi's Hanji 832 appointment-pressure case.
- Distinguished chosen responsibility from passive scapegoating and institutional cover-up.
