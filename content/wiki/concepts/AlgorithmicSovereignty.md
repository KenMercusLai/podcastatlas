# Algorithmic Sovereignty

## Definition
Algorithmic sovereignty is the capacity of a polity to understand, govern, and meaningfully influence the systems that rank information, allocate attention, shape public beliefs, and mediate collective action within its population.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's “Republic of Algorithms” frame shifts sovereignty beyond model ownership. Recommendation, identity formation, content distribution, data centers, capital, and cross-border digital tribes can make private systems politically consequential even when territorial government remains intact. Sovereignty therefore requires more than a domestic model: it includes transparent objectives, lawful authority, auditability, infrastructure continuity, plural access, and limits on manipulation. National control can reduce external dependence, but it can also centralize censorship or surveillance unless constrained by rights and accountable institutions.

## Key Claims
- Information-ranking systems can exercise political power without owning territory or holding formal office.
- Sovereignty concerns the objectives, data, infrastructure, interfaces, and accountability of algorithms, not only where a model was trained.
- Cross-border recommendation systems can organize identity and conflict faster than geographically bounded institutions can respond.
- Domestic control does not guarantee public legitimacy, technical independence, or rights protection.
- Effective governance must balance continuity and national agency with pluralism, transparency, due process, and interoperability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode offers a strategic argument rather than a comparative governance study. It does not measure algorithmic influence, specify institutional design, or show that national ownership improves outcomes. States, firms, open-source communities, and users can share control in different ways. Sovereignty language can protect democratic self-government, but it can also justify isolation, censorship, surveillance, protectionism, or inefficient duplication.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the episode's “Republic of Algorithms” and national-control argument.
- Distinguished algorithmic governance from model ownership alone.
- Added rights, accountability, and interdependence as qualifications on domestic control.
