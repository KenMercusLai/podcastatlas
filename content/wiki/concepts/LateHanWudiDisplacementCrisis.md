## Definition

汉武帝晚年流民危机 / late Han Wudi displacement crisis is the episode's nameable pattern in which costly war, luxury, and heavy fiscal extraction produce mass population flight, rebellion pressure, and an imperial policy reversal.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence comes from Hanji 397-2. The episode treats displacement not as an isolated natural-disaster story but as a political-economic consequence of Han Wudi's late governing line. Guandong displacement, rebellion, and "天下骚动" make the crisis large enough that the ruler must acknowledge error through the Luntai self-criticism turn.

The crisis also explains why the early Han Zhao regency can be framed as restoration rather than innovation. [[DuYannianWesternHan|Du Yannian]] tells [[HuoGuangWesternHan|Huo Guang]] that the people who left home have not fully returned, so the state should revive a Wen-era posture of frugality, lenience, and popular recovery. The later [[SaltAndIronConference|Salt and Iron Conference]] then becomes one institutional expression of this post-crisis adjustment.

## Key Claims

- Mass displacement is presented as a policy consequence of late Han Wudi-era spending, war, and extraction rather than a stand-alone calamity.
- Population flight becomes politically dangerous because it links rural exhaustion to rebellion and broad disorder.
- The Luntai self-criticism edict functions as an emergency signal that the old policy direction has become unsustainable.
- Recovery requires more than suppressing unrest; displaced people must be able to return and rebuild ordinary livelihood.
- Han Zhao-era "with the people at rest" policy is framed as a constrained correction of Han Wudi's legacy, not as a complete institutional reset.

- Scale of crisis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-397-2-rang-huangdi-dou-wei-zhi-bianse-de-liumin-cong-hechu-laide-ltmoqipqk61_cg8n7w1gwaprtitj|Hanji 397-2]] cites two million Guandong displaced people and another four hundred thousand unnamed or unregistered people.
- Disorder link: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-397-2-rang-huangdi-dou-wei-zhi-bianse-de-liumin-cong-hechu-laide-ltmoqipqk61_cg8n7w1gwaprtitj|Hanji 397-2]] connects mass displacement with rebellion and "天下骚动."
- Policy reversal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-397-2-rang-huangdi-dou-wei-zhi-bianse-de-liumin-cong-hechu-laide-ltmoqipqk61_cg8n7w1gwaprtitj|Hanji 397-2]] presents the Luntai edict as Han Wudi's admission and turn away from the crisis-driving line.
- Recovery program: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-397-2-rang-huangdi-dou-wei-zhi-bianse-de-liumin-cong-hechu-laide-ltmoqipqk61_cg8n7w1gwaprtitj|Hanji 397-2]] says Du Yannian urges Huo Guang to restore Wen-era frugality, lenience, and popular reassurance because displaced people have not all returned home.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page currently rests on one short source note. It does not prove the full demographic count, distinguish registered from unregistered displaced people in administrative detail, or isolate the separate roles of war cost, tax burden, climate, local official behavior, and market disruption. The episode gives a macro-causal synthesis and should be paired with later sources before becoming a full explanation of Western Han social crisis.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 397-2's linkage of late Han Wudi policy cost, mass displacement, rebellion risk, Luntai self-criticism, and Han Zhao recovery policy.
