# 外镇避祸式生存 / Peripheral Command Exit Strategy

## Definition
外镇避祸式生存 / peripheral command exit strategy is a political-survival pattern in which an exposed insider leaves a hostile succession center for an external office that creates distance while preserving troops, territory, legitimacy, or bargaining value.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1091 defines the pattern through [[LiuQiLateHan|刘琦]]. [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] uses the contrast between Shen Sheng's death inside the state and Chong'er's safety outside it to redirect Liu Qi away from a household struggle he cannot win. When [[HuangZu|黄祖]]'s death opens [[JiangxiaCommanderyLateHan|Jiangxia]], Liu Qi turns departure into an office-backed refuge.

The command matters because it avoids two weak forms of exit: remaining close enough to be controlled, and fleeing so completely that the actor loses protection and relevance. Jiangxia gives Liu Qi distance plus capacity. Later episodes show that this capacity can become coalition value, although Hanji 1091's claim that Zhuge Liang already designed the move for Liu Bei is not settled.

## Key Claims
- Succession exposure can make proximity to the ruler more dangerous than formal distance from the center.
- Indirect historical analogy can communicate survival advice when direct factional counsel is too risky.
- A useful exit destination preserves institutional standing rather than relying on unprotected flight.
- Geographic distance is strongest when joined to command authority, troops, territory, or revenue.
- External office can transform a threatened heir from a household target into a strategically relevant actor.
- Later coalition use does not by itself prove that the original adviser intended every downstream consequence.

## Evidence
- Exposure diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1091-zhugeliang-dianhua-liuqi-beihou-ancang-shenme-zhanlue-fubi-lj2tfbi8qd3dxta8ehh8a0kt7qpq|Hanji 1091]] presents Liu Qi as lacking the family and military backing available to Liu Cong's coalition.
- Indirect counsel: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1091-zhugeliang-dianhua-liuqi-beihou-ancang-shenme-zhanlue-fubi-lj2tfbi8qd3dxta8ehh8a0kt7qpq|Hanji 1091]] uses Shen Sheng and Chong'er to distinguish dangerous internal proximity from safer external distance.
- Capacity-preserving exit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1091-zhugeliang-dianhua-liuqi-beihou-ancang-shenme-zhanlue-fubi-lj2tfbi8qd3dxta8ehh8a0kt7qpq|Hanji 1091]] says Liu Qi obtains the Jiangxia command after Huang Zu's death.
- Downstream value: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1091-zhugeliang-dianhua-liuqi-beihou-ancang-shenme-zhanlue-fubi-lj2tfbi8qd3dxta8ehh8a0kt7qpq|Hanji 1091]] retrospectively connects Jiangxia to Liu Bei's later position and Sun Quan cooperation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The pattern currently rests on one popular-history source and one reconstructed motive chain. Liu Qi's appointment can be understood as immediate self-preservation without accepting the stronger claim of a concealed Liu Bei strategy. External command also creates new military exposure; it is not safety without cost.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Liu Qi's move from succession exposure to the Jiangxia command.
