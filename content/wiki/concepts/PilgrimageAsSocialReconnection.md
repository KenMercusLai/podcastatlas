# Pilgrimage as Social Reconnection

## Definition

Pilgrimage as social reconnection is the interpretation of collective sacred travel as a return to strangers, movement, storytelling, and shared life after periods of epidemic fear or isolation.

## Current Synthesis

The episode contrasts [[TheCanterburyTales]] with Boccaccio's *Decameron*. Where the latter's company withdraws from plague-stricken Florence, Chaucer's pilgrims meet at the Tabard Inn and travel together toward Thomas Becket's healing shrine.

The comparison makes pilgrimage both religious and social. Seeking cure and devotion creates a temporary community across occupation and rank, while the storytelling contest turns physical travel into negotiated coexistence. The connection to an ending plague season is suggestive but remains dependent on the episode's chronology.

## Key Claims

- Sacred travel can combine devotion, healing hope, mobility, and sociability.
- Temporary travel communities bring strangers and unequal social ranks into sustained contact.
- Storytelling can function as the social infrastructure of a journey.
- Epidemic context can make gathering and movement feel like a reversal of isolation.
- The reconnection reading supplements rather than replaces religious motives.

## Evidence

- **Gathering frame:** [[416-the-canterbury-tales-part-4-glt1956280616]] describes Chaucer meeting 29 other pilgrims at the Tabard Inn.
- **Healing destination:** [[416-the-canterbury-tales-part-4-glt1956280616]] identifies Thomas Becket's shrine with miracles and cure.
- **Isolation contrast:** [[416-the-canterbury-tales-part-4-glt1956280616]] contrasts Chaucer's outward journey with the *Decameron*'s retreat from plague.
- **Shared narration:** [[416-the-canterbury-tales-part-4-glt1956280616]] explains the tale-telling contest and common supper prize.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The poem's pilgrimage is a literary frame, and the episode's reading does not prove that post-plague pilgrims understood travel primarily as social reconnection. The April plague-season claim and comparison with the *Decameron* remain source-scoped interpretive supports.

## What Changed

- Established collective pilgrimage as a qualified counter-image to epidemic isolation.
