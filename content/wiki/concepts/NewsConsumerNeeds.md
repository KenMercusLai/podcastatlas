# News Consumer Needs

## Definition

News consumer needs are the goals of comprehension, control, and community that people may pursue through information alongside factual accuracy.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded source uses [[DannagalYoung]]'s framework to explain why news choice is not a neutral accuracy contest. People want a workable account of their surroundings, some sense of agency over events, and continued membership in meaningful groups. Political affiliation can satisfy the community need and shape which explanations feel credible or threatening.

The framework does not imply that truth is irrelevant or that consumers knowingly choose falsehood. It explains why sincere concern for facts can coexist with motivated interpretation, personal trust, partisan fairness judgments, and preference for sources that make one's social world intelligible.

## Key Claims

- Comprehension motivates people to seek an intelligible model of events.
- Control motivates information use that supports agency or reduces uncertainty.
- Community makes group belonging part of how information is interpreted.
- Accuracy can compete with identity protection without disappearing as a stated value.
- Media systems can exploit these needs through partisan framing and emotionally useful narratives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied summary does not present the underlying research or show how the three needs are measured, weighted, or distinguished from convenience, habit, entertainment, or material interest. The framework explains possible motives; it does not prove the motive behind any individual belief or excuse false claims.
