# Foreign Corrupt Practices Act

The Foreign Corrupt Practices Act appears in [[vol-268-liang-ge-lao-si-lai-si-1003563933]] as the U.S. legal response connected to investigations of overseas bribery by companies such as [[LockheedCorporation|Lockheed]]. The episode treats the [[LockheedBriberyScandal]] as part of the evidence that U.S. firms and defense contractors had used foreign payments to win business abroad.

In this wiki, the Act is attached to [[OverseasBriberyExportSales]]: when large export deals depend on state-linked buyers, political intermediaries, and national prestige, anti-bribery rules become part of market structure rather than only a corporate-ethics add-on.

## Key Claims
- Foreign bribery can become a domestic governance problem for the exporting country.
- Anti-bribery law changes how companies treat intermediaries, political access, and accounting around overseas sales.
- The source connects the Act to a broader post-Watergate investigative environment, but keeps individual political motives source-scoped.
