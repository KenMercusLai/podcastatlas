# Emotion Attentional Capture

## Definition
Emotion attentional capture is the functional property by which an emotional state takes priority over ongoing behavior and directs attention, action, and valuation toward an urgent challenge or opportunity.

## Current Synthesis
In the Adolphs episode, priority is one of the features that makes a state emotional. Fear can interrupt ongoing behavior when a threat appears, while media, advertising, art, and music can recruit emotion-related systems to hold attention or move action. The concept is useful because it links emotion science to everyday attention markets without reducing all emotion to manipulation.

## Key Claims
- Emotional states can interrupt ongoing behavior because priority is part of what they do.
- Attentional capture is adaptive in threat or opportunity contexts but can be exploited.
- Media and advertising often work by making emotion take priority for clicks, purchases, votes, or social attitudes.
- Art and music can also engage emotion-related systems without being reducible to persuasion.
- The concept connects emotion to attention selection, not just to subjective feeling.

## Evidence
- Priority feature - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] defines priority as emotion's ability to interrupt and take over behavior.
- Media and advertising - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] records Huberman and Adolphs discussing emotional capture in clicks, purchases, votes, and social attitudes.
- Art and music boundary - [[neuroscience-of-emotions-tools-for-improving-emotion-regulation-dr-ralph-adolphs-scim1222961716]] contrasts manipulative capture with abstract visual or auditory features that trigger emotion-related systems.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not claim all attention capture is harmful. It treats priority as a basic emotion function that can be adaptive, commercialized, or aesthetic depending on context.
