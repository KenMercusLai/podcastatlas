## Definition
Ordinary people historical visibility is the problem of how people without official rank, fame, or durable public archives become legible and consequential within historical explanation.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence joins recovery method to interpretive commitment. The Fuyou Tiandi case shows how rural girls become visible through letters, mission archives, questions, fieldwork, genealogy, descendants, and oral memory. The Xu Zhuoyun episode adds a broader historiographical choice: ordinary people need not enter history only as newly recovered names; their production, circulation, sacrifice, resilience, and moral judgment can organize the historian's account of how society works.

## Key Claims
- Ordinary people often enter history through accidental or institutionally mediated traces rather than records made for their own self-representation.
- Visibility improves when historians combine archives, maps, family memory, genealogy, oral accounts, field sites, and social-structural evidence.
- A small source or everyday observation becomes historically serious when connected to migration, work, gender, belief, exchange, war, or institutions.
- Historical visibility is also a matter of scale and emphasis: historians decide whether ordinary life merely illustrates elite history or helps explain the whole system.
- The method must preserve uncertainty; making people visible is not permission to invent their inner lives or romanticize suffering.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Archives, memories, and close observation remain selective. Mission records can preserve lives through unequal institutions, while a historian's childhood memory or assistant's retrospective can overrepresent vivid scenes. An ordinary-people focus should therefore widen explanation without treating anecdote as complete proof or ordinary people as morally uniform.

## What Changed
- Added Xu Zhuoyun's common-people historiography to the earlier archival-recovery model.
- Expanded visibility from finding missing names to making ordinary social action explanatory at systemic scale.
