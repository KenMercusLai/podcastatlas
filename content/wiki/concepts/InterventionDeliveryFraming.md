# Intervention Delivery Framing

## Definition

Intervention delivery framing is the separation of what a health intervention biologically does from how its physical form, label, routine placement, inconvenience, disclosure, and opt-out structure shape behavior and legitimacy.

## Current Synthesis

[[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio|Way to Go, Ohio]] uses [[IodizedSaltPopulationPrevention]] to ask whether some vaccine resistance attaches partly to injection, needles, clinical ritual, and the category word “vaccine,” rather than only to the intervention's biological effect. [[SeemaSagayar]] treats this as an empirical gap: alternative delivery could change acceptance, but the episode does not establish how large the effect is or whether findings about medical-form preferences in India transfer to the United States.

The imagined “no-choice salt, choice salt, needle” comparison joins several variables. Salt changes the route and sensory experience; “anti-COVID supplement” changes the label; routine food changes friction; optional or practically unavoidable salt changes agency; and incomplete information changes consent. The thought experiment therefore clarifies more than a delivery effect. It shows that uptake can rise while informed acceptance and trust fall, and that changing behavior without changing belief is ethically different when ease supports a voluntary action versus when concealment or near-unavoidability produces compliance.

## Key Claims

- Physical form can carry fear, status, ritual, and cultural meaning independently of an intervention's biological mechanism.
- Labels such as “vaccine” and “supplement” can activate different associations without changing the underlying medical claim.
- Lower friction can change behavior before belief changes, but observed uptake does not reveal informed endorsement.
- A valid comparison must separate route, framing, convenience, disclosure, and opt-out design rather than treating them as one variable.
- Cross-cultural preferences and needle-fear prevalence can motivate hypotheses but do not establish the cause of U.S. vaccine hesitancy.
- Consent and institutional trust are outcome dimensions, not external objections to be considered only after maximizing coverage.
- A historically successful fortification vehicle does not make covert or compulsory drug delivery scientifically or ethically transferable.

## Evidence

- Delivery-form gap: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] reports Sagayar's view that studies had not adequately tested injection against alternatives such as inhalation.
- Cultural meaning: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] reports an Indian hierarchy among powders, pills, injections, and intravenous treatment while preserving the transferability warning.
- Fear hypothesis: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] raises but does not establish the possibility that some stated ideological resistance masks needle discomfort.
- Framing and friction: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] imagines “anti-COVID supplement” labeling and routine salt consumption as ways to reduce category and effort barriers.
- Choice comparison: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] contrasts injection, optional fortified salt, and fortified salt without a practical opt-out.
- Trust boundary: [[way-to-go-ohio-way-to-go-ohio]] explicitly acknowledges deception, compulsory-action backlash, and the analogy to fluoridation controversy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The vaccine-in-salt intervention is scientifically unavailable in the scenario and cannot support an efficacy or acceptance conclusion. The three imagined arms do not isolate delivery form because they also alter description, routine, choice, disclosure, and exposure probability. The source supplies no study protocol, participant outcomes, or direct evidence that alternative delivery would reduce hesitancy. Needle fear may coexist with ideological, medical, political, religious, historical, and institutional concerns. Making avoidance costly can also shift behavior without creating legitimacy and may deepen distrust once concealment is discovered.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to separate route and sensory form from labeling, behavioral friction, disclosure, and choice architecture.
- Added informed consent and trust as coequal evaluation dimensions alongside uptake.
