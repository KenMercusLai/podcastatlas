# Acting Opportunity Readiness

## Definition
Acting opportunity readiness is the distinction between building the discipline, technique, interpretation, and initiative to use a role well and controlling whether auditions, casting access, networks, scripts, or type-breaking opportunities appear.

## Current Synthesis
[[AdahSharma]] and [[GuoXiaoting|郭晓婷]] both separate capability from allocation. Dance, theater, memory, physical control, formal acting study, script analysis, and camera experience can prepare someone to perform, but neither training nor a positively received role guarantees continuing work. Casting concepts, industry relationships, typecasting, project quality, market categories, reputation, and production decisions determine which skills become visible.

Readiness includes initiative without turning every outcome into personal merit. Sharma's audition helped overcome an initial appearance mismatch for *1920*. Guo traveled to audition for Lin Xiang and wrote producers an unusually direct account of her ambition and character reading. These actions can convert an opening, yet both careers include gaps or constraint after prior success. The current judgment is that an actor can widen the probability and quality of opportunity without controlling its arrival.

## Key Claims
- Formal and informal training build discipline, physical control, interpretive range, and camera readiness.
- Preparation improves the ability to use an opportunity but cannot guarantee that opportunities arrive or continue.
- Auditions and written character interpretation can sometimes overcome an initial casting mismatch or weak default positioning.
- Positive reception does not automatically defeat weak networks, typecasting, market categories, or changing production demand.
- A role capable of breaking a category still requires a gatekeeper willing to offer access.
- Readiness includes recovery, selective pursuit, and the judgment to recognize an opportunity worth substantial effort.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept rests on two actors' retrospective accounts from different industries and does not establish a universal casting model. Talent, preparation, networks, appearance, timing, market demand, luck, representation, family resources, and production decisions interact differently across careers. It should not imply that actors denied access were insufficiently prepared or insufficiently proactive.

## What Changed
- Added active pursuit—auditions, direct producer communication, and character interpretation—as a way readiness can convert but not create opportunity.
- Added script availability, category repetition, and data positioning to the structural limits around prepared skill.
