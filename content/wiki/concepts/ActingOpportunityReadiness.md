# Acting Opportunity Readiness

## Definition
Acting opportunity readiness is the distinction between building the discipline and skill to use a role well and controlling whether auditions, casting access, networks, or type-breaking opportunities appear.

## Current Synthesis
[[AdahSharma]]'s account separates capability from career access. Dance, theater, memory, physical control, imagination, language study, and camera relaxation prepared her to perform when an audition arrived, but neither training nor a positively received debut guaranteed continuing roles.

The current judgment is that preparation is necessary but structurally incomplete. Casting concepts, industry relationships, typecasting, project intuition, production choices, and market entry can determine which skills become visible. A new role can break a category, but only after someone gives the actor access to that role.

## Key Claims
- Formal training builds discipline, focus, physical control, imagination, and camera readiness.
- Training improves the ability to use an opportunity but cannot guarantee that opportunities arrive.
- Audition performance can overcome part of a casting mismatch, as Sharma says happened with *1920*.
- Positive reception does not automatically overcome weak industry networks or rapid typecasting.
- Breaking typecasting requires a sufficiently different role and a gatekeeper willing to offer it.
- Language preparation can convert one cross-market opportunity into further work when the first project succeeds.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept comes from one actor's retrospective account and does not establish a universal casting model. Talent, preparation, networks, appearance, timing, market demand, luck, representation, and production decisions interact differently across industries and careers. It should not imply that actors denied access were insufficiently prepared.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate performance preparation from opportunity allocation.
- Added typecasting and cross-language market entry as mechanisms between skill and career outcomes.
