## Definition

临机边疆裁量 / Emergency Frontier Discretion is the pattern in which a distant envoy or commander acts before specific authorization can arrive because timing, local knowledge, and escalation risk make ordinary reporting too slow. Broad permission to act does not remove the need for evidence, proportionality, post-action review, or control of precedent.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 427 provides a successful but bounded case. [[FengFengshiWesternHan|冯奉世]] learns that [[ShacheKingdomWesternHan|莎车国]] has killed Wannian and a Han envoy and blocked the southern route. He judges a Chang'an memorial too slow, invokes imperial authority without authorization, assembles Western Regions cavalry, and wins when overwhelming pressure produces internal surrender. [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] rewards the result but withholds marquisate-level endorsement after [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]] warns that unrestricted reward would normalize private frontier war.

Hanji 366-2 supplies the negative boundary. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] explicitly sends [[GongsunSuiWesternHan|公孙遂]] to correct a stalled campaign and permits situational action, but Gongsun Sui accepts [[XunZhiWesternHan|荀彘]]'s accusation, arrests [[YangPuWesternHan|杨仆]], and merges the armies. The resulting unified command increases military pressure, yet Wudi executes Gongsun Sui after his report. The case shows that a broad mandate is not blanket immunity and that solving a command bottleneck can still be judged as unsupported, disproportionate, or politically destabilizing.

Together the cases separate four questions that are often collapsed: Was delay dangerous? Was the evidence adequate? Did the intervention produce a verifiable result? What precedent and internal trust did it create? Emergency discretion is most defensible when all four are answered, and still may be rewarded cautiously rather than converted into a general license.

## Key Claims

- Distance and communication delay can make ordinary authorization too slow for a live frontier crisis.
- Local knowledge and borrowed imperial credibility can produce rapid coordination, but they also create legality and legitimacy risk.
- Broad situational authority does not validate every arrest, mobilization, or restructuring decision made under it.
- Outcome and process require separate review: immediate success can coexist with inadequate evidence or harmful precedent.
- Court response can distinguish achievement, personal liability, and precedent through limited reward, punishment, or explicit exception.

## Evidence

- Timing pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-427-yiren-zhengfu-shacheguo-xihan-fengfengshi-zenme-zuodao-de-loxxi2qynbrbmfqgokos30sypsxp|Hanji 427]] says Feng Fengshi is far from Chang'an when he learns Shache has rebelled, killed Han-linked figures, and blocked the route.
- Unauthorized but effective mobilization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-427-yiren-zhengfu-shacheguo-xihan-fengfengshi-zenme-zuodao-de-loxxi2qynbrbmfqgokos30sypsxp|Hanji 427]] says Feng invokes an imperial command, assembles allied cavalry, and induces internal surrender.
- Precedent control: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-427-yiren-zhengfu-shacheguo-xihan-fengfengshi-zenme-zuodao-de-loxxi2qynbrbmfqgokos30sypsxp|Hanji 427]] says Xuan promotes Feng but does not enfeoff him after Xiao Wangzhi warns against normalizing envoy-led war.
- Explicit situational mandate: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-366-2-hanwudi-henxin-zhansha-gongsunsui-ls1izjhow_j4ubgmm0lm8ruaurox|Hanji 366-2]] says Han Wudi authorizes Gongsun Sui to act according to circumstances in the stalled Joseon campaign.
- Intervention and repudiation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-366-2-hanwudi-henxin-zhansha-gongsunsui-ls1izjhow_j4ubgmm0lm8ruaurox|Hanji 366-2]] says Gongsun Sui arrests Yang Pu and merges the armies, then is executed after reporting the decision.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source supplies a general doctrine of emergency power. Feng's action succeeds, but the episode does not prove it was the only available response or that every allied mobilization was voluntary. Gongsun Sui's case records execution without a complete legal opinion, so the exact defect in his decision remains uncertain; the host's troop-morale explanation is not an imperial statement. The contrast supports a review framework rather than a simple rule that results justify discretion or that punishment proves the original action wholly wrong.

## What Changed

- Added Gongsun Sui as a negative boundary case where explicit situational authority does not shield a command intervention from execution.
- Expanded the concept from communication delay and precedent control to include evidence quality, proportionality, and internal command legitimacy.
