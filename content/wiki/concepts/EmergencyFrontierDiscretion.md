## Definition

临机边疆裁量 / Emergency Frontier Discretion is the pattern where a field envoy or commander acts before formal authorization can arrive because distance, timing, and local contagion risk make ordinary reporting too slow.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 427 creates the concept through [[FengFengshiWesternHan|冯奉世]]'s response to the [[ShacheKingdomWesternHan|莎车国]] rebellion. Feng learns at Yixun that Shache has killed Wannian and a Han envoy, and that the southern Silk Road route is blocked. His deputy raises the ordinary bureaucratic path of memorializing Chang'an, but Feng judges that distance, desert travel, and the risk of Shache strengthening make delay dangerous.

The source's key distinction is that emergency discretion can be strategically rational and institutionally hazardous at the same time. Feng's unauthorized command gathers Western Regions cavalry, creates overwhelming pressure, and wins without a long campaign. Yet [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] ultimately accepts [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]]'s warning that marquis-level reward would teach envoys that they may launch Western Regions wars whenever local conflict arises.

The concept therefore does not celebrate disobedience by itself. It names a narrow frontier condition: distant officials sometimes see time-sensitive risks before the capital can respond, but the court still has reason to cap rewards or mark the behavior as exceptional so emergency judgment does not become routine private war.

## Key Claims

- Emergency discretion arises when distance and communication delay make formal approval too slow for a live frontier crisis.
- The field actor's local knowledge can identify contagion risk before the center has enough information.
- Borrowed imperial credibility can mobilize allied forces quickly, but that same borrowing creates legality risk.
- Success can reduce immediate violence while still damaging command norms if rewarded without qualification.
- Court response can split result from precedent: praise the achievement, but limit honor to avoid authorizing repetition.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in one source note. It does not prove that Feng's action was the only possible response, that all frontier emergencies justify false commands, or that court authorization is dispensable. The source also shows why the court is cautious: successful emergency discretion can invite imitation by less capable or more self-interested envoys. The concept should be kept distinct from private forged-edict extraction and from ordinary delegated battlefield tactics.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 427's Feng Fengshi case, linking distance-driven emergency action to reward-precedent control.
