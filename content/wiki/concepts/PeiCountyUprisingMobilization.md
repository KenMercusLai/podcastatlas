# 沛县起义动员 / Pei County Uprising Mobilization

沛县起义动员 / Pei County uprising mobilization is the route [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-7-liubang-he-xiangyu-youhe-butong-llwcqatndck4zita2piufwhjpxp1|Qinji 128-7]] uses to show [[LiuBang|刘邦]] moving from fugitive leader to recognized anti-[[QinState|秦国]] commander. The process begins with a failed corvee escort, but it becomes political only when local officials, outlaws, and county elders all have reasons to shift against the Qin-appointed magistrate.

The mechanism has several steps. [[FanKuai|樊哙]] and a small group follow Liu Bang after he releases the remaining laborers. [[XiaoHe|萧何]] and [[CaoShen|曹参]] then advise the Pei County magistrate to recall these fugitives once the [[DazexiangUprising|大泽乡起义]] spreads. When the magistrate reverses course, Xiao He and Cao Shen defect to Liu Bang, and Liu Bang's letter frames killing the magistrate as the way to protect the county from harsher rebel punishment.

The key feature is local conversion rather than outside conquest. [[PeiCounty|沛县]] does not fall because Liu Bang storms it with overwhelming force; it turns because existing local actors decide the magistrate has become the greater risk. The result is a three-thousand-person force and the title Pei Gong, making [[GrassrootsOfficialNetwork|基层官吏网络政治资本]] operational.

## Key Claims
- Fugitive violence becomes political mobilization only when local officials and elders reinterpret it as county self-preservation.
- Qin's harsh law and labor system create the first rupture, but local legitimacy decides whether Liu Bang can re-enter Pei County.
- The episode contrasts this grassroots route with the Xiang family's commandery seizure through [[KuaijiCommanderyCoup|会稽郡守府夺权]].
- Liu Bang's early power depends on trust, threat perception, and county relationships more than aristocratic status or formal military command.
