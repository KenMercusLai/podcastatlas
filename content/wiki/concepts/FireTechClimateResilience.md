# Fire Tech Climate Resilience

Fire tech climate resilience is the startup and infrastructure market around wildfire detection, suppression, forest management, utility risk, home hardening, filtration, insurance, and community resilience. In [[socialradarss2-billclerico-final]], [[BillClerico]] develops the thesis after leaving [[WePay]], spending time on a Mendocino County ranch, and joining a volunteer fire department.

The source frames fire tech as an overlooked regulated market. Clerico says many investors dismiss wildfire as a government problem, while he sees potential buyers in utilities, insurers, fire agencies, homeowners, and communities. The hard part is not only technology; it is finding business models and first customers inside cautious institutions.

[[ConvectiveCapital]] is the source's institutional expression of this thesis, and [[OverStory]] is the concrete portfolio example. OverStory uses satellite imagery to help utilities identify vegetation risk around power lines more efficiently than manual inspection alone.

[[tech-20260112-0112-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260112-0112-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the homeowner and rebuilding layer through [[FireResilientConstruction]]. [[DavidBrancaccio]] compares [[CrossLaminatedTimber]], mineral wool, stucco, [[ConcreteFilledWallSystems]], and [[ThreeDPrintedHousing]] after L.A. wildfire losses, shifting the resilience question from fire detection and institutional buyers into the material choices of a rebuilt house.

[[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]] adds a public-health and global-risk layer through [[OliverMorton]]. The episode keeps detection, modeling, drones, aircraft, and best-practice sharing inside the fire-tech frame, but it also emphasizes that the largest human burden may be smoke exposure rather than direct burning.

## Key Claims
- Wildfire risk is a climate adaptation problem that needs many companies, not a single insurance product or a single government program.
- Fire tech resembles early fintech in Clerico's telling: regulated, institution-heavy, operationally unglamorous, and therefore underfunded before buyers learn how to adopt it.
- Conservative buyers can make go-to-market harder even when the economic exposure is large.
- Useful fire tech includes detection, suppression, vegetation management, resilience, home hardening, and air-quality protection.
- Home hardening also reaches into construction assemblies: mass timber, insulation, stucco, concrete forms, and design choices can become part of fire resilience after a neighborhood burns.
- Fire tech should be evaluated partly by whether it reduces [[WildfireSmokeHealthBurden]], not only by whether it protects structures.
- Rapid satellite detection and better spread modeling help only if institutions can convert signals into early action.
