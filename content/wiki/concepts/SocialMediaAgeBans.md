# Social Media Age Bans

Social media age bans are laws or proposals that block children below a threshold age from using social-media platforms. [[tech-20260724-0724-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260724-0724-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the international policy version: [[France]] is described as the first [[EuropeanUnion|EU]] country to ban social media for children younger than 15, following a similar [[Australia]] policy and related discussions in the [[UnitedKingdom|UK]], [[Canada]], and other EU countries.

The concept sits next to [[OnlineAgeVerification]] because access bans require enforcement. If platforms must prove a user is old enough, the system can shift from self-declared birthdays toward identity checks, age-estimation systems, or login controls. That enforcement layer creates [[SocialMediaAgeGateSpeechBurden]] and [[CivilLibertiesSurveillanceRisk]] even when the policy goal is child safety.

## Key Claims
- Child-safety concerns are real, but a categorical age ban does not automatically solve the harms.
- Young users may route around bans through fake accounts, alternate login methods, or VPNs.
- Age bans can push platforms toward stronger identity verification, affecting anonymity and privacy for adults as well as children.
- The policy becomes harder when platforms serve social, educational, political, family, entertainment, and creator functions at once.
- Defining the covered service is unstable when short-video, streaming, creator, and communication products converge.
