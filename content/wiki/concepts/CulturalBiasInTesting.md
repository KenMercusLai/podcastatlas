# Cultural Bias In Testing / 测试中的文化偏差

Cultural bias in testing / 测试中的文化偏差 is the episode's warning that a supposedly neutral test can still measure language, schooling, familiarity, exhaustion, class background, or local knowledge. In [[37-zhishang-ceshi-qingwen-ni-shi-zhili-poluomen-ma-608020679]], the problem appears most clearly in American immigration screening and school placement, where people arrived with different languages, education histories, and bodily conditions.

The source notes that psychologists tried to reduce bias through images, blocks, digit span, imitation, and other less language-dependent tasks. But the broader point remains: lower cultural load is not the same as context-free truth. When biased tests feed [[PsychometricSocialSorting|psychometric social sorting]], they can make social difference look like innate inferiority.

## Key Claims
- Test format, language, prior schooling, and fatigue can shape measured performance.
- Attempts to design "culture-free" tests can reduce some unfairness while leaving other contextual differences active.
- Bias matters most when scores are used for migration, schooling, employment, institutionalization, or law.
- Cross-country or cross-race IQ comparison is especially dangerous when historical and educational context is flattened.
