# Contract Farming Credit

## Definition

Contract farming credit is value-chain finance in which a buyer advances cash or production inputs to farmers and recovers the advance from the harvested crop.

## Current Synthesis

In the Zimbabwe case, tobacco companies substitute for conventional lenders by financing growers before harvest. The model supports [[LovemoreChiwa]] and helps explain why tobacco can recover faster than crops without an organized buyer-credit channel. Short production cycles and predictable repayment from crop sales also make tobacco an entry point for younger white farmers working through joint ventures.

The mechanism is a workaround, not a resolution of [[LandTenureCollateralCredibility]]. It can finance one contracted crop without creating secure long-term land rights, trusted partnerships, or patient capital for orchards and other multi-year investments.

## Key Claims

- Buyer advances can supply inputs and liquidity where banks will not lend against land.
- Repayment at harvest links credit to an identifiable crop and buyer.
- Short-cycle tobacco becomes easier to finance than long-horizon agricultural investment.
- A functioning contract channel can produce sectoral recovery without broad institutional repair.
- Dependence on one buyer or crop may shift rather than eliminate farmer risk.

## Evidence

- Smallholder finance: [[disorder-of-magnitude-frances-riots-454c603849c78e6a57bfa1c8bf33de7c]] describes tobacco firms advancing money and inputs and recovering them at harvest.
- Differential recovery: [[disorder-of-magnitude-frances-riots-454c603849c78e6a57bfa1c8bf33de7c]] links tobacco's finance and shorter cycle to record output and easier entry for returning farmers.
- Boundary: [[disorder-of-magnitude-frances-riots-454c603849c78e6a57bfa1c8bf33de7c]] says long-term investment remains constrained by title uncertainty, mistrust, and short joint-venture terms.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide contract prices, interest or input markups, default terms, bargaining power, rejection rates, farmer margins, labor conditions, environmental costs, or health externalities. Record output is not evidence that every contracted farmer benefits or that tobacco-led recovery is diversified and durable.

## What Changed

- Established buyer-linked credit as the mechanism separating tobacco's recovery from weaker agricultural finance elsewhere.
- Distinguished short-cycle crop finance from patient capital and secure land tenure.
