# Moral Beauty Awe

## Definition
Moral beauty awe is the source's claim that awe can arise from witnessing character and action, including courage, kindness, justice, strength, integrity, and a person's full-life expression.

## Current Synthesis
This concept broadens [[AweEmotion]] beyond size, nature, or spectacle. In the episode, Huberman's Joe Strummer example becomes evidence that awe can attach to moral and artistic integrity: lyrics, voice, breath, and lived commitment can make a person feel larger than the self. Keltner names moral beauty as a distinct source of awe, tying admiration to social orientation rather than mere status worship.

## Key Claims
- Awe can arise from admired character, not only from natural vastness or sensory scale.
- Moral beauty includes courage, kindness, justice, strength, integrity, and whole-person expression.
- The experience differs from celebrity status because the admired quality is ethical or expressive, not merely fame.
- Moral beauty links awe to social learning: people orient toward examples of how to live.
- The source keeps this as an emotion-science and lived-example claim, not a formal moral theory.

## Evidence
- Moral-source claim: [[cultivating-awe-emotional-connection-in-daily-life-dr-dacher-keltner-scim6985403733]] says Keltner identifies moral beauty, including courage, strength, kindness, and justice, as a source of awe.
- Joe Strummer example: [[cultivating-awe-emotional-connection-in-daily-life-dr-dacher-keltner-scim6985403733]] has Huberman describe Strummer as meaningful because of lyrics, voice, breath, integrity, and bringing his whole life into expression.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's examples are interpretive and personal. It does not establish a universal test for when admiration becomes awe, nor does it distinguish all cases of moral beauty from charisma, fandom, or ideology.

- Created the concept to preserve the source's expansion of awe into moral and character-based admiration.
