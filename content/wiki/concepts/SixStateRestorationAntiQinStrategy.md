# Six-State Restoration Anti-Qin Strategy / 六国复国式反秦策略

Six-state restoration anti-Qin strategy / 六国复国式反秦策略 is the route [[ZhangEr|张耳]] and [[ChenYu|陈馀]] recommend to [[ChenSheng|陈胜]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-3-chensheng-wuguang-zudui-chenggong-locnaq-fo1fztyud5nghwkteams|Qinji 128-3]]. After the rebels take Chen County and local elders ask Chen Sheng to become Chu king, Zhang Er and Chen Yu warn that immediate kingship will expose private ambition before the anti-Qin movement has widened.

Their alternative is to move west while searching for descendants of the six conquered royal houses and helping them restore their states. The proposed logic is simple: more restored states mean more enemies for [[QinState|秦国]], and more anti-Qin partners mean more party strength for Chen Sheng. The source's compressed formula is that enemy multiplication disperses Qin's armies, while party multiplication strengthens the rebel side enough to avoid fighting in open fields or defending isolated counties.

This concept is adjacent to [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] but not identical to it. Hezong is a coalition among still-existing states; this strategy is a post-unification attempt to recreate state centers after Qin's [[CommanderyCountyAntiEnfeoffment|郡县制反分封定局]] has erased them. It therefore turns old royal names into insurgent infrastructure.

The strategy also clarifies what [[ZhangChuRegime|张楚政权]] rejects. Chen Sheng could have appeared as the liberator who restores many old houses while delaying his own title. By calling himself Chu king and naming Zhang Chu instead, he chooses direct claimant status and lets the host read him as seeking a Chu-centered inheritance of the unified realm.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-5-wo-you-zhengju-zanmen-buneng-shuo-qinershi-sha-ye-bushi-ljuome-xea6sjv4hhzmrr0b0-1hg|Qinji 128-5]] shows the darker sequel to the same restoration logic. [[WuChen|武臣]] becomes Zhao king under advice from [[ZhangEr|张耳]] and [[ChenYu|陈馀]], but after [[ChenSheng|陈胜]] recognizes him, the Zhao branch does not become a coordinated anti-Qin front. Zhang Er and Chen Yu instead urge Wu Chen to enlarge Zhao before attacking Qin, turning restoration into [[AntiQinCoalitionFragmentation|反秦阵营诸侯化分裂]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-8-yi-ming-zhaoguo-chuishibing-nixi-chengwei-waijiao-dachen-lhfwk-hjge5iuqet6astmbclhyri|Qinji 128-8]] adds two concrete restoration branches. [[TianDanQinRebel|田儋]] invokes Qi royal descent to restore [[QiState|齐国]], while [[ZhouShiRebel|周市]] refuses to become king and insists on installing [[WeiJiu|魏咎]] as a surviving [[WeiState|魏国]] royal descendant. The episode therefore shows both self-claiming restoration and restraint in favor of old-house legitimacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-4-jiuquangui-zhangliang-ruhe-zai-liubang-shouxia-anshen-lp6qwg3ma5hdy15g4box7pe23jav|Qinji 129-4]] adds a repair version through [[ZhaoXie|赵歇]]. After [[LiLiangZhaoGeneral|李良]] kills [[WuChen|武臣]], [[ZhangEr|张耳]] and [[ChenYu|陈馀]] defeat Li Liang and then install Zhao Xie as an old Zhao royal descendant. Here restoration is not only initial mobilization against Qin; it is a way to rebuild legitimacy after a restored-state coup.

## Key Claims
- A revolt that begins from survival pressure still needs a political map if it wants to defeat an empire.
- Restoring old royal descendants can create allied legitimacy nodes faster than one rebel leader can govern all captured territory.
- The strategy works by multiplying Qin's fronts and diluting its ability to concentrate force.
- It differs from ordinary nostalgia for the six states because it is presented as a military-political method for defeating Qin.
- It differs from classical hezong because the old states no longer exist as sovereign actors; they must be reconstituted.
- The proposal is also a self-protection argument: delaying kingship lets Chen Sheng avoid looking like a private usurper at the movement's beginning.
- Chen Sheng's rejection makes Zhang Chu a contested strategic choice, not merely the natural next stage of the uprising.
- Qinji 128-5 shows that restoration can also become separate-state self-strengthening if no shared command or trust holds the anti-Qin camp together.
- Qinji 128-8 shows restoration multiplying across Qi and Wei, including a case where the military actor deliberately avoids personal kingship to make the restored state more legitimate.
- Qinji 129-4 adds that old royal names can also repair a rebel polity after internal collapse, as with Zhao Xie after Wu Chen.
