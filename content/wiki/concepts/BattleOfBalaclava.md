# Battle of Balaclava

## Definition

The Battle of Balaclava was the 25 October 1854 Russian attack on the exposed supply base supporting the British siege of Sevastopol, remembered for the Thin Red Line, the Heavy Brigade's victory, and the disastrous Charge of the Light Brigade.

## Current Synthesis

[[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] explains Balaclava first as a logistics problem. The port connected British troops outside Sevastopol to naval supply, but force allocation favored the siege. A single metalled road, rough tracks, six Ottoman-held redoubts, and incomplete artillery installation left the campaign dependent on a fragile corridor. The cavalry entered cold, short of shelter and fodder, restricted from aggressive reconnaissance, and divided by the Lucan-Cardigan rivalry.

[[724-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-into-the-valley-of-death-part-4-glt1598624240]] carries the battle through its famous actions. Russian forces captured the redoubts along the Causeway Heights, but [[ColinCampbellBritishArmy|Campbell]] used a two-deep rifle line to stop cavalry threatening the Balaclava road, and [[JamesYorkeScarlett|Scarlett]] led the Heavy Brigade uphill to defeat a larger mounted force. These successes show that British performance was not uniformly incompetent and that local discipline and initiative could work inside a fragile command system.

The later disaster turned on asymmetrical information. [[FitzRoySomerset1stBaronRaglan|Raglan]] could see the Russians preparing to remove captured British guns; [[GeorgeBingham3rdEarlOfLucan|Lucan]] below could not. An urgent order to prevent removal of the guns did not identify a target visible from Lucan's position, and [[LouisEdwardNolan|Nolan]] did not produce shared understanding before [[JamesBrudenell7thEarlOfCardigan|Cardigan]] was ordered toward the Russian battery at the far end of the North Valley. The Light Brigade reached the guns, but its limited tactical effect did not recover the Causeway Heights. Loss of the Vorontsov Road then forced British supply onto worse routes and linked the day's command failure to the winter siege's logistical crisis.

## Key Claims

- Balaclava was indispensable because it joined naval supply to the British siege army.
- Force allocation favored the siege over defense of the port and its road.
- Terrain, weather, and incomplete redoubt armament made the supply corridor vulnerable.
- The Thin Red Line and Heavy Brigade demonstrate effective local adaptation within a weakened wider system.
- Different lines of sight between the heights and valleys made an already ambiguous order operationally dangerous.
- The Light Brigade reached the visible Russian battery, but tactical penetration did not recover the lost road or justify the losses.
- Losing the Causeway Heights and Vorontsov Road worsened the siege's transport problem even though the port remained British.

## Evidence

- Supply dependence - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] identifies the port, road, rough tracks, and lack of railway or telegraph.
- Defensive weakness - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] contrasts siege and port allocations and describes Ottoman-held, incompletely armed redoubts.
- Russian threat - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] reports the concentration of infantry, cavalry, and artillery before the attack.
- Cavalry condition - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] connects shelter, fodder, reconnaissance restrictions, and command rivalry to the opening situation.
- Successful defense and counterattack - [[724-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-into-the-valley-of-death-part-4-glt1598624240]] follows the 93rd Highlanders and Heavy Brigade in stopping two Russian cavalry threats.
- Visibility and order failure - [[724-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-into-the-valley-of-death-part-4-glt1598624240]] contrasts Raglan's view of the captured guns with Lucan's view from below the Sapuni Heights.
- Charge and aftermath - [[724-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-into-the-valley-of-death-part-4-glt1598624240]] connects the North Valley attack, temporary silencing of the battery, failure to retake the heights, and loss of the paved supply road.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The two episodes are consecutive parts of one popular-history narrative rather than independent accounts. Troop and gun totals, road quality, redoubt readiness, Ottoman performance, exact order wording and gestures, casualty figures, Cardigan's conduct, Russian intentions, and the counterfactual effect of alternative decisions remain disputed or source-scoped. Unequal visibility and ambiguity distribute responsibility but do not erase the agency of commanders who transmitted and obeyed an order they regarded as disastrous.

## What Changed

- Completed the battle account with the Thin Red Line, Heavy Brigade victory, Light Brigade charge, and loss of the Vorontsov Road.
- Added unequal battlefield visibility and failed intent confirmation as the central mechanism linking terrain to command disaster.
