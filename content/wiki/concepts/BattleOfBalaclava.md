# Battle of Balaclava

## Definition

The Battle of Balaclava began on 25 October 1854 when Russian forces attacked the exposed supply base supporting the British siege force outside Sevastopol.

## Current Synthesis

[[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] explains Balaclava first as a logistics problem. The port connected British troops outside Sevastopol to naval supply, but roughly 5,000 defenders protected it while about 20,000 men served the siege. A single metalled road, rough connecting tracks, autumn mud risk, six Ottoman-held redoubts, and incomplete artillery installation left the campaign dependent on a fragile corridor.

The cavalry entered the battle cold, short of tents and fodder, restricted from aggressive reconnaissance, and divided by the Lucan-Cardigan rivalry. Russian concentration therefore met not only thin defenses but a command system burdened by fatigue, poor information, public criticism, and conflicting ideas about cavalry action. This installment ends as the Russian attack begins, so the Thin Red Line and the Heavy and Light Brigade charges remain outside its evidentiary scope.

## Key Claims

- Balaclava was indispensable because it joined naval supply to the British siege army.
- Force allocation favored the siege over defense of the port and its road.
- Terrain, weather, and incomplete redoubt armament made the supply corridor vulnerable.
- Russian capture of the port could have severed the siege force from outside supply.
- Cavalry shortages, reconnaissance limits, rivalry, and reputational pressure formed part of the battle's opening conditions.

## Evidence

- Supply dependence - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] identifies the port, road, rough tracks, and lack of railway or telegraph.
- Defensive weakness - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] contrasts siege and port allocations and describes Ottoman-held, incompletely armed redoubts.
- Russian threat - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] reports the concentration of infantry, cavalry, and artillery before the attack.
- Cavalry condition - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] connects shelter, fodder, reconnaissance restrictions, and command rivalry to the opening situation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode ends before the battle's best-known actions and therefore cannot independently support a complete account or final allocation of responsibility. Troop and gun totals, road quality, redoubt readiness, Ottoman performance, Russian intentions, and the counterfactual effect of capturing the port remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established logistics and force allocation as the battle's essential precondition rather than treating it only as the setting for famous cavalry actions.
