# BPC-157 Experimental Repair Peptide

## Definition
BPC-157 experimental repair peptide is the episode's frame for BPC-157 as a gastric-juice-derived 15-amino-acid peptide with broad animal repair signals but unresolved human efficacy, dosing, receptor, safety, and regulatory status.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats BPC-157 as worth studying but not proven. Bakri describes its origin in gastric protection and Croatian research, then explains why performance and bodybuilding communities became interested after animal injury and tissue-repair signals. The strongest plausible mechanisms in the episode involve endothelial and epithelial injury models, VEGF signaling, nitric oxide, cell migration, wound healing, and possible gut-protective effects.

The caution is as important as the promise. The Bakri episode notes high-dose animal studies without clear adverse effects, but also says LD50 and long-term human safety are unresolved. Human evidence is narrow: two small rectal-enema ulcerative-colitis phase 1/2 trials are discussed as limited abstracts rather than settled clinical proof. Koniver adds broad clinic anecdotes, systemic-injection claims, higher-dose schedules, growth-hormone-receptor hypotheses, and use alongside PRP or PRF. The solo Huberman episode reinforces angiogenesis, endothelial nitric-oxide synthase, fibroblast migration, and injury-site blood-flow mechanisms while explicitly describing rigorous human data as essentially absent. None closes the controlled-human-evidence gap. U.S. approval, compounding, state medical-board rules, sourcing reliability, and the possibility that VEGF or growth signaling could support an existing tumor remain major constraints.

## Key Claims
- BPC-157's original study context was gastric protection, not musculoskeletal performance recovery.
- Animal data suggest broad repair-related biological activity, but the receptor, best indication, and human dose are not settled.
- Existing human evidence in the source is limited and does not prove routine injury-repair use.
- Safety confidence is incomplete because high-dose animal tolerance does not establish LD50, long-term human risk, or product-specific quality.
- Regulatory and sourcing problems make consumer use a combined evidence, legal, and product-quality issue.
- Broad clinic use and dose escalation remain observational evidence rather than proof of efficacy or optimal dosing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources contain personal and clinical injury-recovery experiences, but those reports are not controlled human evidence. Claims about systemic action, growth-hormone-receptor upregulation, injected superiority, post-viral effects, tumor promotion, dosing cycles, or higher-dose schedules remain source-scoped. High animal tolerance does not establish long-term human safety. The page should not be read as recommending BPC-157 for injury, GERD, ulcerative colitis, alcohol-related effects, or any other condition.
