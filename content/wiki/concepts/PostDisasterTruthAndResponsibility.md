## Definition

Post-disaster truth and responsibility is the accountability frame for what happened after a catastrophe, which causal story becomes acceptable, who bears responsibility, and whether institutions disclose, repair, or shift the resulting harm.

## Current Synthesis

The concept begins with [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]]'s Chernobyl account. After the [[ChernobylNuclearPowerPlant]] explosion, the question was not simply what exploded, but which story would become official: operator error, design defect, managerial failure, or systemic secrecy. [[ViktorBryukhanov]], [[AnatolyDyatlov]], and [[ValeryLegasov]] mark different positions in that responsibility field.

Hanji 394-3 adds an aftermath-distribution layer through [[FukushimaDaiichiNuclearPowerPlant|Fukushima Daiichi]]. It frames wastewater discharge as a post-disaster responsibility failure: instead of containing or bearing the accident aftermath locally, the responsible state is accused of shifting risk to the ocean, neighboring publics, and future generations.

Together the sources make post-disaster responsibility both epistemic and distributive. Institutions must tell the truth about causality, but they must also decide who bears the continuing costs after the initiating event.

## Key Claims

- Disaster investigations can become fights over the acceptable causal story.
- Individual accountability can be necessary while still being used to conceal design and governance failures.
- Expert testimony is vulnerable when technical truth conflicts with state legitimacy.
- Public memory depends on whether later accounts can reopen distorted official narratives.
- Post-disaster responsibility includes the distribution of lingering risk, not only blame for the initial event.

## Evidence

- Causal-story conflict: [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]] separates operator error, design defect, managerial failure, and systemic secrecy in the Chernobyl aftermath.
- Responsibility figures: [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]] uses Bryukhanov, Dyatlov, and Legasov to show managerial exposure, control-room blame, and scientific truth pressure.
- Truth-telling burden: [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]] links Legasov's role to conflict between international reporting, design secrecy, conscience, and state pressure.
- Risk distribution: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-3-shenwa-lishi-heweiji-weihe-dou-fasheng-zai-riben-lspztkfaaq0hcq1no3yum1xdox8a]] uses the Fukushima discharge controversy to argue that post-disaster decisions can transfer risk outward rather than resolve it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The Fukushima layer is source-scoped and critical; it does not independently settle technical discharge safety or legal responsibility. The Chernobyl layer remains the stronger evidence for truth conflict inside official investigation. The concept should preserve both individual and institutional responsibility rather than using one to erase the other.
