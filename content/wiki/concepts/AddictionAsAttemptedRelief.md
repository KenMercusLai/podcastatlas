# Addiction as Attempted Relief

## Definition
Addiction as attempted relief is the functional view that a substance or behavior may initially regulate stress, pain, loneliness, shame, trauma, or dysregulated arousal before repetition and consequences turn that strategy into a problem of its own.

## Current Synthesis
[[RyanSoave]] resists treating addiction as either a moral defect or an isolated object. The useful question is what the substance or behavior does for the person and whether control has reversed: “does it have you or do you have it?” A strategy that provides rapid relief can narrow life, impair relationships and motivation, and create additional shame, stress, dependency, or trauma. Recovery therefore has to address both the addictive loop and the conditions for which it became “medicine.”

This frame applies across alcohol and drugs as well as gambling, gaming, pornography, food, work, shopping, caffeine, and stress-seeking, but the episode does not claim that every intense habit meets a clinical diagnosis. Functional impact, compulsion, failed attempts to stop, preoccupation, and loss of choice matter more than applying one label indiscriminately.

## Key Claims
- Addictive behavior can begin as a workable short-term solution to an underlying difficult state.
- Rapid relief can become organizing power, reversing the relationship between the person and the behavior.
- The resulting consequences can create more stress or trauma, strengthening the demand for relief.
- Assessment should examine control, preoccupation, abstinence difficulty, relationships, motivation, loneliness, and daily functioning.
- Recovery has to replace the function of the addictive strategy as well as interrupt its use.
- Intensity or enthusiasm alone does not establish addiction; impairment and loss of choice are central qualifications.

## Evidence
- Relief function - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] describes alcohol, drugs, gambling, gaming, pornography, food, and other behaviors as ways people may seek connection, wholeness, escape, or state change.
- Control test - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] uses “does it have you or do you have it?”, a trial period without the behavior, preoccupation, and life impact as practical screens.
- Self-reinforcing loop - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] links wound, stress, relief-seeking, and new addiction-produced harm.
- Positive replacement - [[tools-for-overcoming-substance-behavioral-addictions-ryan-soave-scim5476071523]] says recovery must move beyond removal toward purpose, connection, pleasure, and a sustainable life.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a functional treatment frame, not a substitute for formal diagnostic criteria or evidence that all substance use, gaming, work, exercise, sugar intake, caffeine use, or pornography consumption is addiction. Acute withdrawal, psychiatric crisis, and severe functional impairment require professional assessment; abrupt alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous.
