# Holodomor

## Definition

The Holodomor is the mass famine in Soviet Ukraine in the early 1930s, remembered as a central catastrophe in twentieth-century Ukrainian history and as a case of state policy, suppressed information, contested classification, and delayed recognition.

## Current Synthesis

[[69-england-v-ukraine-glt5469973001]] approaches the famine through [[GarethJonesJournalist|Gareth Jones]], whose travel and reporting challenged official Soviet concealment. The episode places his evidence against Walter Duranty's denial, showing that recognition depended not only on whether suffering was observed but also on which correspondents had prestige, access, and institutional authority.

The source establishes the famine's place within Ukraine's exceptionally violent twentieth century but does not itself adjudicate its full causes, scale, or legal classification. Those questions require demographic, archival, policy, and survivor evidence beyond this short episode.

## Key Claims

- The famine belongs to the history of Soviet state policy and mass suffering in Ukraine.
- Information control and correspondent access shaped what international audiences were prepared to believe.
- Direct reporting could challenge denial without immediately overcoming prestige and institutional incentives.
- Famine memory is inseparable from wider histories of repression, occupation, war, and displacement.
- Recognition of catastrophe requires broader Ukrainian testimony and scholarship as well as famous foreign witnesses.

## Evidence

- Journalistic witness - [[69-england-v-ukraine-glt5469973001]] presents Gareth Jones as reporting famine after direct travel in the Soviet Union and Ukraine.
- Denial and access - [[69-england-v-ukraine-glt5469973001]] contrasts Jones's reports with Walter Duranty's public position.
- Historical setting - [[69-england-v-ukraine-glt5469973001]] places the famine among the multiple armies, occupations, mass killings, and displacements affecting twentieth-century Ukraine.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page is bounded to one compressed narrative source. It does not determine mortality totals, the precise policy chain, geographic distribution, comparative Soviet famines, intentionality, genocide classification, or the full record of contemporary reporting. The episode's description should be extended with primary documents, Ukrainian testimony, demographic work, and specialist historiography.
