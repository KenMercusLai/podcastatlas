# Inter-Minority Conflict Displacement / 少数族裔冲突转移

## Definition

Inter-minority conflict displacement is the process by which institutional injustice or unequal economic ordering is experienced and narrated primarily as conflict between minority groups, making those groups immediate antagonists while higher-level allocation of power, protection, and punishment becomes less visible.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses the 1992 Los Angeles unrest to distinguish direct harm from complete explanation. Korean-owned businesses were deliberately attacked, and Black-Korean prejudice and conflict were real. But the source argues that unequal judicial outcomes, police violence, media circulation, neighborhood retail structure, and the position of small merchants between larger commercial systems and local consumers shaped why anger concentrated there. Cultural etiquette alone cannot account for the conflict, and institutional explanation does not excuse violence against shopkeepers.

## Key Claims

- Immediate inter-group hostility can be real without being the full cause of a conflict.
- Unequal legal treatment can make a local incident stand for a wider judgment about whose life receives protection.
- Minority merchants may occupy a visible intermediary position without controlling the larger economic or judicial system.
- Media framing can intensify group antagonism and narrow public attention, though intent requires separate evidence.
- Reconciliation requires both direct relationship work and accountability for the institutions structuring the conflict.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Structural context does not erase individual agency, prejudice, crime, or the specific losses suffered by Korean shopkeepers and Black residents. The episode's claims about police or media strategy are reported viewpoints rather than established intent. The concept is grounded in one summarized case and should not be generalized to all minority relations without local historical evidence.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to preserve both direct inter-group harm and the institutional conditions that redirect conflict.
- Distinguished economic visibility from control over policing, courts, capital, or public narrative.
