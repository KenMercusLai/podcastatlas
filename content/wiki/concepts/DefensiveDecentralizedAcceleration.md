# Defensive Decentralized Acceleration

## Definition
Defensive decentralized acceleration, or d/acc, continues technological progress while prioritizing protective capabilities and preventing excessive concentration of power.

## Current Synthesis
d/acc rejects both indiscriminate acceleration and safety through one trusted controller. It favors cybersecurity, pathogen detection, air filtration, rapid vaccines, and defenses that let plural societies detect, prevent, and recover from harm. Its central claim is resilience: defense that works only when one institution controls every advanced model creates brittle authority and failure points.

## Key Claims
- Progress, protection, and decentralization should be optimized together.
- Defensive tools can reduce harm without control of every AI system.
- Distributed detection and response increase agency across societies.
- Centralized model restrictions may create fragile institutional dependence.
- Defense remains empirical and does not guarantee attackers will be outpaced.

- **Distribution:** [[e257-duihua-vitalik-ai-anquan-beihoude-zhexue-sixiang-youxiao-lita-zhuyi-ruhe-yingxiang-guigu-7a1e61d3-f02c-498b-ac84-cf68f59b4ca9]] attributes to [[VitalikButerin|Vitalik Buterin]] the preference for giving people protective capability rather than controlling every model.
- **Comparison:** [[e257-duihua-vitalik-ai-anquan-beihoude-zhexue-sixiang-youxiao-lita-zhuyi-ruhe-yingxiang-guigu-7a1e61d3-f02c-498b-ac84-cf68f59b4ca9]] contrasts distributed defense with restricting biological knowledge through a few providers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not show defenses will arrive first, remain affordable, or address irreversible harms. Some capabilities are dual use, and decentralization can distribute offensive power. d/acc still needs testing, access design, accountability, coordination, and preventive layers.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a third path between unconditional acceleration and centralized safety control.
