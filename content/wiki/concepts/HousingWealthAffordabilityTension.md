# Housing Wealth Affordability Tension

Housing wealth affordability tension is the conflict between wanting homes to be cheap for new buyers and valuable for existing owners. [[can-trump-make-buying-a-home-more-affordable]] makes the tension explicit: [[DonaldTrump]] is described as wanting to make buying easier without broadly pushing down existing home prices, while [[CaitlinGorback]] says Americans often want affordability when young and large housing wealth when old.

The concept connects housing economics to political incentives. Supply abundance can make homes more affordable, but scarcity supports homes as stores of household wealth. That makes [[DemandSideHousingAffordabilityPolicy]] attractive because it promises buyer relief without openly attacking incumbent homeowner equity, even when [[HousingAffordabilitySupplyMechanics]] says more supply is the deeper fix.

## Key Claims
- Homeownership is treated as both shelter/autonomy and a wealth asset.
- Existing homeowners may resist policies that would lower home values, even when those policies improve entry affordability.
- Demand-side tools can soften the political conflict but may bid up scarce homes if supply is not added.
- The tension overlaps with [[FutureResidentHousingVoice]] because future buyers and renters have less political power than current owners.
