# Multi-City Itinerary Travel / 多城串游

## Definition
Multi-city itinerary travel is a trip pattern in which travelers book stays across two or more cities within one holiday rather than concentrating the journey in a single destination.

## Current Synthesis
The source describes the pattern as a joint product of time, connectivity, and preference. Long holiday-plus-leave combinations make extra stops feasible; denser high-speed rail, motorway, and aviation networks reduce transfer friction; and travelers seeking fewer crowds or deeper local experience can combine smaller cities and counties with hands-on craft activities.

The reported growth signal is hotel-booking based, so it captures a booking pattern rather than every traveler or a durable structural shift. Multi-city travel can distribute demand beyond famous destinations, but it can also increase coordination cost, transport spending, and dependence on connected booking platforms.

## Key Claims
- Longer continuous breaks make multi-stop itineraries more practical.
- Rail, road, and air connectivity can turn smaller cities into feasible linked destinations.
- Crowd avoidance and demand for local experience support county and craft-oriented stops.
- Multi-city hotel bookings provide one measurable proxy for the pattern.

## Evidence
- **Booking growth:** [[guoqing-jiaqi-duocheng-chuanyou-redu-pansheng-xingbake-yin-wutang-yinliao-zhong-hantang-bei-qisu-1021649123]] attributes more than 30% year-over-year growth in two-or-more-city hotel orders to Qunar reporting.
- **Connectivity mechanism:** [[guoqing-jiaqi-duocheng-chuanyou-redu-pansheng-xingbake-yin-wutang-yinliao-zhong-hantang-bei-qisu-1021649123]] links the pattern to improving high-speed rail, motorway, and aviation networks.
- **Experience preference:** [[guoqing-jiaqi-duocheng-chuanyou-redu-pansheng-xingbake-yin-wutang-yinliao-zhong-hantang-bei-qisu-1021649123]] names crowd avoidance, county travel, dyeing, and pottery-making as supporting demand.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One holiday-period booking report does not establish long-run traveler behavior, profitability for destinations, reduced overtourism, or net environmental benefit. Hotel orders can miss informal lodging, day trips, package travel, cancellations, and travelers who visit multiple cities without separate hotel bookings.
