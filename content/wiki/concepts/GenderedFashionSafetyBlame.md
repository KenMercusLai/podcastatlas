# Gendered Fashion Safety Blame

Gendered fashion safety blame is the source's frame for how dangerous clothing and cosmetics were often explained as women's vanity even when men designed, manufactured, sold, regulated, watched, and profited from the same fashion system. In [[16-shishang-shouhaizhe-jing-luse-huo-wei-zui-du-yanse-559889282]], this appears most clearly in the fire and dye sections: newspapers and social commentary could blame women for spectacle, color, skirts, and public display while leaving the industry and spectators less visible.

The concept extends [[FemaleBodyFreedom]] because the question is not only "why did women choose dangerous clothing?" It is also who had legal permission to wear trousers, who needed dress to signal class and respectability, who was mocked for practical clothing, and who lacked control over factory, theater, or household safety.

## Key Claims
- Blaming women for dangerous dress can hide the broader system that created, sold, normalized, and profited from the danger.
- Fashion danger can be real without becoming proof that the harmed person caused her own harm.
- Clothing reform, trousers, short skirts, and less restrictive garments can be safety technologies as well as style changes.
- Gendered blame narrows public health by turning systemic material and labor problems into individual moral failure.
