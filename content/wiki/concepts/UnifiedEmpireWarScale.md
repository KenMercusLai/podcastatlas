## Definition

大一统战争破坏范围 / unified empire war scale is Hanji 388-4's comparative idea that a political order organized around control of a unified realm can make succession and regime-change wars broader in scope than conflicts inside a more fragmented feudal order.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 388-4 uses Europe as a contrast case. The host acknowledges that medieval Europe was also violent, exploitative, conspiratorial, and unstable, but argues that its fragmented feudal structure often kept wars more local. By comparison, Chinese dynastic struggles could aim at "天下" and therefore pull more regions into total regime-change conflict.

The concept is source-scoped and comparative, not a settled universal law. Its value is to name a mechanism the episode uses: the scale of political imagination and institutional target matters. If the prize is a unified empire, war aims, recruitment, logistics, and devastation may scale up with the claim.

## Key Claims

- War destructiveness depends partly on the political scale of the prize being contested.
- A unified imperial target can make regime-change war wider than local noble conflict.
- Fragmented feudal authority can still be violent but may contain some conflicts within narrower territories.
- The concept helps explain the episode's repeated emphasis on ordinary people suffering during dynastic transition.
- The China-Europe comparison should remain source-scoped because the episode does not provide full comparative military-demographic evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one short episode. It should not imply that European wars were harmless, that all Chinese wars were total, or that political unity alone explains mortality. Technology, logistics, fiscal capacity, local geography, disease, famine, and state collapse all require separate evidence.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 388-4 to preserve the episode's political-scale explanation for dynastic-war devastation.
