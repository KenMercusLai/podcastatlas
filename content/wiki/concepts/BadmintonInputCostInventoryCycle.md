# Badminton Input-Cost Inventory Cycle

## Definition

The badminton input-cost inventory cycle is the feedback loop through which natural-feather supply, manufacturing capacity, dealer stock, retail price, and player participation amplify both price increases and later price declines.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded source describes a reversal after three years of badminton price increases. Feather reportedly represents about 70% of ordinary shuttlecock production cost and up to 80% for some premium products, so earlier poultry-capacity constraints raised input pressure. High prices then encouraged producers to expand and dealers to hold stock.

When goose and duck feather supply recovered, the system moved in the opposite direction: lower input cost met expanded production and inventory above actual consumption. Falling prices accelerated the release of accumulated stock, adding supply to the market and reportedly helping court activity recover. The durable mechanism is therefore not a single commodity-price pass-through but a delayed interaction among biological supply, capacity investment, inventory expectations, and demand response.

## Key Claims

- Feather availability is a dominant cost driver for natural-feather shuttlecocks.
- Biological supply recovery can reverse a prolonged input shortage with a lag.
- High prices encourage capacity expansion and speculative or precautionary inventory accumulation.
- Excess stock can amplify price decline once expectations turn and dealers release inventory.
- Lower equipment prices can support participation, creating a demand response after the supply-led correction.

- Cost structure: [[yumaoqiu-jiage-huiluo-wuyinliangpin-jiang-xiang-oumei-fuzhi-zhongguo-jingyan-gkwriueovjpsaflmdqt0zl6p]] says feathers account for roughly 70% of ordinary shuttlecock production cost and up to 80% for some high-end products.
- Supply reversal: [[yumaoqiu-jiage-huiluo-wuyinliangpin-jiang-xiang-oumei-fuzhi-zhongguo-jingyan-gkwriueovjpsaflmdqt0zl6p]] links recovered poultry capacity to improved goose- and duck-feather availability and roughly 20% year-over-year declines in popular natural-feather shuttlecock prices.
- Capacity and inventory: [[yumaoqiu-jiage-huiluo-wuyinliangpin-jiang-xiang-oumei-fuzhi-zhongguo-jingyan-gkwriueovjpsaflmdqt0zl6p]] says prior price increases encouraged production expansion and dealer stockpiling beyond actual consumption, while falling prices accelerated inventory release.
- Participation response: [[yumaoqiu-jiage-huiluo-wuyinliangpin-jiang-xiang-oumei-fuzhi-zhongguo-jingyan-gkwriueovjpsaflmdqt0zl6p]] reports recovering court popularity as shuttlecock prices declined.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide a feather-price series, production volumes, dealer inventories, brand-level pricing, substitution between feather and synthetic products, or measured court utilization. The cost shares, price decline, causal sequence, and participation recovery remain episode-scoped, and the same loop may differ by product quality, brand, region, and contract timing.
