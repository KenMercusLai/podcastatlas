# Public Experimental Philosophy

## Definition
Public experimental philosophy is [[JonathanKeats]]'s practice of carrying thought experiments into the world as situations, objects, rituals, or participatory experiments.

## Current Synthesis
In [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]], the concept contrasts with philosophy treated as a closed academic argument. Keats wants a thought experiment to generate public conversation and unknown conclusions; the artwork is not only the artifact but the shared interpretive situation it creates.

## Key Claims
- Public experimental philosophy treats thinking as something that can be staged socially without being reduced to a conventional object.
- Its experiments are deliberately open-ended rather than proofs of a predetermined point.
- It uses humor, absurdity, and speculative design to make hidden assumptions discussable.
- It depends on institutions only when they protect exploratory conditions rather than dictating conclusions.

## Evidence
### Staged Thinking
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] describes Keats sitting in a gallery for 24 hours, punching time cards to mark thoughts, and making the receipt rather than the thought the purchasable object.

### Open-Ended Thought Experiments
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] says Keats was dissatisfied with thought experiments used as argumentative devices and wanted them carried into the world with unknown conclusions.

### Institutional Boundary
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] praises [[LongNow]] and ZERO1-like support while criticizing top-down art-science models that use artists to illustrate existing institutional messages.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source frames these projects as philosophical and artistic experiments, not as controlled scientific tests. Their value lies in conversation, imagination, and interpretation, so conventional feasibility is often intentionally bracketed.
