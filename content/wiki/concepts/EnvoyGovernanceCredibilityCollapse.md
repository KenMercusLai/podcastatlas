## Definition

使团治理失控与外交信用崩塌 / envoy governance credibility collapse is the pattern in which rapid mission expansion outruns personnel screening, financial accountability, provisioning, and security, causing repeated low-quality contact to destroy the trust earlier diplomacy created.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-357-1-zhangqian-sihou-xihan-waijiao-luanxiang-lingren-zhazhe-lrjdmvyvoswgfduajch9om9r-b6b|Hanji 357-1]] grounds the concept in the contrast between [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]] and the missions following him. Zhang Qian's name and Bowang Hou title carry trust, but broad authorization lets poorly screened applicants recruit large parties for long journeys with weak oversight. Some reportedly appropriate gifts, sell goods, make unreliable claims, and arrive without sustainable supply arrangements.

The damage is cumulative. Local communities move from welcoming exchange to doubting envoys and refusing to feed large recurring parties; scarcity then produces conflict among the visitors themselves. At corridor chokepoints, distrust combines with Xiongnu pressure and turns into obstruction and raiding. Han's later cavalry sorties and commandery expansion address security and logistics, but the source does not show that force alone repairs the credibility already lost.

## Key Claims

- A trusted predecessor's reputation can create access, but borrowed reputation decays when later representatives do not meet the same standard.
- Mission volume is a governance variable: more delegations can reduce influence when screening, oversight, and host capacity do not scale with them.
- Diplomatic gifts and trade goods are credibility instruments, so appropriation, resale, and inferior quality damage both the mission and the sending state.
- Provisioning failure externalizes mission costs onto host communities and can turn hospitality into resistance.
- Repeated misconduct changes the meaning of later contact: envoys who arrive under a trusted banner can eventually be read as extractive or deceptive.
- Military protection and corridor infrastructure may reduce physical risk, but they do not by themselves restore relational trust.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a compact popular-history account and does not isolate misconduct from other causes. Local resource limits, large mission sizes, Xiongnu pressure, corridor geography, Han expansion, and small-state survival incentives all contribute to resistance. The concept therefore identifies a credibility mechanism, not a complete explanation for Han-Western Regions conflict. It also does not imply that every later envoy was corrupt or that military protection is irrelevant; the narrower claim is that security cannot substitute for trustworthy personnel, sustainable logistics, and accountable exchange.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 357-1's post-Zhang-Qian mission breakdown.
- Distinguished reputation inheritance from institutional reproduction of trustworthy diplomacy.
- Linked personnel, goods, provisioning, host burden, and corridor violence in one qualified escalation chain.
