## Definition

The Wugu Incident / 巫蛊之祸 is the late [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] succession trauma in which [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]] is destroyed and later presumed dead. In the current wiki it matters in fifteen bounded ways: Hanji 381-2 gives the early symbolic-succession signal around [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|刘弗陵]]'s "尧母门" birth aura while Liu Ju is still crown prince; Hanji 382-1 gives [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]]'s pre-Wugu rise through the [[LiuDanZhaoPrinceWesternHan|赵太子刘丹]] accusation, his [[XiuyiEnvoyImperialSurveillance|绣衣使者]] authority, and the Chi Road grievance with Liu Ju; Hanji 383-2 gives the immediate Gongsun-family chain from [[GongsunJingshengWesternHan|公孙敬声]]'s military-fund embezzlement to [[ZhuAnshiWesternHan|朱安世]]'s retaliatory wugu accusation; Hanji 384-2 gives Liu Ju's recognized-heir, lenient-governance, and pre-Wugu slander layer; Hanji 384-1 gives the upstream support-network purge around [[GongsunHeWesternHan|公孙贺]] and [[WeiKangWesternHan|卫伉]]; Hanji 385-1 gives the ritual-belief, palace mutual-accusation, imperial-illness, dream-fear, and Jiang Chong mandate precondition; Hanji 385-2 gives Jiang Chong's manufactured-evidence, torture, denunciation, palace-search, and [[ShiDeWesternHan|石德]] advice layer; Hanji 386-1 gives the blocked-appeal, Jiang Chong execution, [[EmpressWeiZifuWesternHan|卫子夫]] resource-support, [[SuWenWesternHan|苏文]] report, messenger-report, and [[LiuQuliWesternHan|刘屈氂]] suppression-order bridge; Hanji 386-2 gives the disputed-command, Chang'an fighting, city-gate escape, and Wei Zifu suicide layer; Hanji 387-1 gives the flight, court-silence, [[LinghuMaoWesternHan|令胡茂]] remonstrance, and failed-pardon bridge; Hanji 387-2 gives Liu Ju's death scene, household destruction, and [[EmperorXuanOfHan|刘病已]] survival hook; Hanji 388-2 gives the remorse-and-punishment aftermath; Hanji 391-1 uses it to explain the source-scoped hidden charge against [[LadyGouyiWesternHan|钩弋夫人]]; Hanji 390-1 shows the purge of Jiang Chong's side making [[MaHeluoWesternHan|马何罗]] and [[MaTongWesternHan|马通]] fear implication; and Hanji 396-2 uses its aftermath to explain why a later false Liu Ju return claim becomes politically explosive.

## Current Synthesis

The current wiki evidence remains narrow but now has an early symbolic-succession branch, a Jiang Chong career-and-grievance branch, a Gongsun-family immediate-causation branch, a recognized-heir and slander-channel branch before the support-network purge, an origin-condition branch before the manufactured-accusation stage, and then command-collapse, flight-and-remonstrance, death-and-survival, remorse, hidden-guilt, immediate-aftershock, and later-memory branches. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-381-2-huaitai-14geyue-chanzi-jiemi-gouyi-furen-de-chuanqi-rensheng-lvfj365_foqcmyintojfl7pj4f2t|Hanji 381-2]] says Han Wudi's "尧母门" gesture around Liu Fuling and Lady Gouyi is dangerous because ruler symbolism can invite ministers to infer hidden succession preference while Liu Ju remains in place. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-1-bazhan-shumu-hanwudi-zhizi-liudan-you-duo-huangtang-loi-6-s1z2okmskqvbfwryz-qjto|Hanji 382-1]] says Jiang Chong first gains Wudi's favor by accusing Zhao heir Liu Dan, is empowered to police powerful households as a special inspector, and forms an early grievance with Liu Ju by detaining and reporting Liu Ju's envoy for using the emperor-only Chi Road. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-383-2-chengxiang-gongsunhe-shi-ruhe-bei-erzi-kengsi-de-lt1yhocymjtgwjb7537yotbwoikz|Hanji 383-2]] says the Gongsun case begins with Gongsun Jingsheng's misuse of taipu office and embezzlement of North Army funds. Gongsun He tries to save him by capturing Zhu Anshi; Zhu retaliates by accusing Gongsun Jingsheng of adultery with Yangshi Princess and wugu cursing against Wudi, and Wudi's illness makes the curse charge lethal.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-384-2-you-duo-shanliang-jiu-you-duo-can-jiemi-bei-xianqi-de-taizi-liuju-lsu-7qabf34o5xza0axewim1quf0|Hanji 384-2]] says Liu Ju is not initially an untrusted or incapable heir: Wudi dislikes his lack of forcefulness but recognizes him as a humane stabilizer, delegates government to him during imperial travel, and accepts his handling of affairs. The same source shows why this strength becomes exposure: Liu Ju's lenient case handling wins popular support while angering harsh-law officials, Wei Zifu fears that mercy will be read as disobedience, and Su Wen and Chang Rong turn palace access into damaging reports about Liu Ju's morality and feelings toward Wudi.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-384-1-fanlian-wuqing-hanwudi-cuihui-weiqing-jiazu-lp-rwqpmccvlaochibspvikhb96z|Hanji 384-1]] says the crisis then begins politically before the open fighting: Gongsun He and his son are destroyed through curse and adultery accusations, two princesses and Wei Kang are implicated, and Wei Kang's inherited Wei Qing prestige plus military capacity make him a target as Liu Ju's possible outer support. The source treats [[DuZhouWesternHan|杜周]], [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]], Lady Gouyi, and Liu Fuling as part of a succession field in which officials recalculate around the possible replacement of Liu Ju.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-385-1-hanchao-diyi-yuanan-wugu-zhi-huo-shi-ruhe-fasheng-de-fvrg0w8fnhlcslizo-yuujgdnbbs|Hanji 385-1]] supplies the immediate precondition for Jiang Chong's later operation. Wugu is treated as hidden curse harm that a ruler could regard as politically real; in 91 BCE, fangshi and shamans gather in Chang'an, palace women accuse one another over buried wooden figures and rites, and Wudi answers by killing hundreds. The same source makes Wudi's age, illness, suspicious temperament, and dream of wooden figures part of the permission structure that lets Jiang Chong convert private fear of Liu Ju and Wei Zifu into a state-backed investigation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-385-2-wugu-zhi-huo-zui-da-de-muhou-heishou-juran-shi-ta-lstnjhb3l6atr-syl4_dhlsgqgte|Hanji 385-2]] supplies the bridge from pruning to open crisis. Jiang Chong allegedly builds the Wugu case through Hu shamans, planted blood, buried figures, torture, coerced confession, and denunciation that spreads fear beyond the palace. Wudi's illness and suspicion then let Jiang Chong claim the palace contains harmful "gu qi." The search moves through the palace, reaches Wei Zifu's side, and finally produces alleged wooden figures and written charges in Liu Ju's Eastern Palace. Shi De's advice shows the structural trap: if Liu Ju waits, he may be destroyed like Fusu by manipulated imperial authority; if he acts first, he risks looking like a rebel through forged command and minister-killing.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-386-1-hanwudi-shiqi-renci-de-taizi-liuju-weihe-zaofan-lkpwtth8485nbqcg18pvwzzxvmon|Hanji 386-1]] supplies the missing hinge between palace trap and open urban war. Liu Ju first wants to reach Wudi, plead, and explain himself; because Jiang Chong controls palace access, he follows Shi De's advice, sends forged imperial envoys, kills the resisting Han Shuo, arrests and executes Jiang Chong, and obtains Wei Zifu's backing for palace vehicles, weapons, and guards. The same source also shows how Wudi's interpretation hardens: Su Wen reports rebellion, Wudi initially still thinks Liu Ju is frightened and angry at Jiang Chong, but an envoy who does not enter Chang'an reports settled rebellion and triggers Wudi's order for Liu Quli to seal the gates and suppress the crown-prince force.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-386-2-diwang-wuqing-huanghou-weizifu-beipo-zisha-lrvdfib62oczcy7loqss4-prkgl|Hanji 386-2]] says the Wugu crisis becomes open urban war when Liu Ju treats an uncertain edict as a mortal trap and tries to mobilize officials, prisoners, Hu cavalry, the North Army, and city residents. Wudi's side distinguishes its tallies with yellow tassels, Ma Tong disrupts Liu Ju's cavalry command, Ren An does not send the North Army, rumor fixes the "crown prince rebels" frame, and Liu Ju is defeated after days of fighting near Changle Palace. Tian Ren's decision to let him out of Chang'an and Bao Shengzhi's attempt to slow punishment show a remaining father-son mitigation instinct, but Wudi treats both leniency and procedural delay as failure to punish rebellion. The same source makes Wei Zifu's suicide the immediate household result after Wudi orders her empress seal collected.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-387-1-taizi-liuju-taowang-hou-can-can-can-lodlsf5wtqtvoejjhe4apec7trku|Hanji 387-1]] says that after Wei Zifu's suicide, Wudi continues punishing Liu Ju's associates while frightened officials stay silent despite believing Liu Ju has been wronged. Linghu Mao's memorial reframes Liu Ju's action as fear-driven self-protection after Jiang Chong's persecution, and Wudi is moved without publicly pardoning the fugitive crown prince. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-387-2-hanwudi-yisheng-nanmo-de-wudian-taizi-liuju-zhisi-fqn_yfiyam5durgrlflosljaho1h|Hanji 387-2]] then says the crisis ends for Liu Ju with surrounded flight, suicide, the killing of two imperial grandsons, wider household destruction in Chang'an, and the survival of infant Liu Bingyi through [[BingJiWesternHan|丙吉]]'s protection. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-388-2-yin-chanyan-cuosha-taizi-hanwudi-wannian-zuidade-huihen-lm9e_lrbbcc7a6kf22hbid04_u73|Hanji 388-2]] says a later review finds many accusations false and allows Han Wudi to see Liu Ju as driven into fear by Jiang Chong. Tian Qianqiu's memorial reframes the crown prince's action as a father-son matter, after which Wudi punishes Jiang Chong's family, burns [[SuWenWesternHan|苏文]], pursues anti-Liu-Ju attackers, and builds 思子宫 and 归来望思之台. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-391-1-wei-le-li-taizi-hanwudi-weihe-xian-sha-chongfei-gouyi-furen-lhsva8zj7n7mfy6u4vyfhrwmbwzg|Hanji 391-1]] then says Han Wudi later investigates Liu Ju's death and, in the host's interpretation, discovers that Lady Gouyi helped Jiang Chong and Su Wen frame the crown prince. The source treats this as the hidden reason for Lady Gouyi's death but also says Han Wudi does not publicize it because her son [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|刘弗陵]] is the intended successor.

The immediate political aftershock appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-390-1-xihan-diyi-da-an-jinridi-ruhe-jiu-le-hanwudi-xingming-ls4snv189gvasy6yke7aw0kwhz8m|Hanji 390-1]]. Ma Heluo's friendship with Jiang Chong and Ma Tong's reward for fighting Liu Ju had once aligned the brothers with Han Wudi's anti-Liu-Ju response. When Wudi later punishes Jiang Chong's family and associates, that prior alignment becomes a liability, and fear of implication helps push the brothers toward rebellion and attempted assassination.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-2-hanwudi-zhizi-liuju-wugu-zhi-an-hou-fuhuo-le-lhj6dzjsikkmbbcjlfgnz1bbok-1|Hanji 396-2]] does not retell the full Wugu Incident. It uses the incident as inherited political memory: Liu Ju is presumed dead afterward, but that presumption remains important enough that a man claiming to be him can unsettle officials, attract a crowd to [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]], and force [[JunBuyiWesternHan|隽不疑]] to prioritize dynastic order over identity uncertainty.

## Key Claims

- The Wugu Incident functions as a family-case, succession, feedback, support-network, and panic-permission crisis before it becomes open fighting: Hanji 381-2 shows "尧母门" as an early symbolic-succession signal; Hanji 382-1 shows Jiang Chong's denunciation-based rise, Xiuyi authority, and Chi Road grievance with Liu Ju; Hanji 383-2 shows corruption, rescue bargaining, retaliation, and wugu accusation converging in the Gongsun case; Hanji 384-2 shows Liu Ju as a recognized but slander-exposed heir; Hanji 384-1 shows Gongsun He and Wei Kang removed as Liu Ju's outer supports; Hanji 385-1 shows hidden-curse belief, palace mutual accusation, Wudi's illness, and Jiang Chong's mandate; Hanji 385-2 shows manufactured evidence and palace search trapping Liu Ju; Hanji 386-1 shows blocked appeal and messenger failure hardening the rebellion frame; and Hanji 386-2 shows edicts, tallies, cavalry orders, prison releases, and the North Army deciding whether Liu Ju can plausibly act as crown prince rather than rebel.
- Manufactured evidence and coerced confession are central to the escalation: Hanji 385-2 makes Jiang Chong's staged objects, torture, denunciation, and palace-search authority the bridge between suspicion and armed crisis.
- Court silence and low-rank remonstrance matter: Hanji 387-1 treats Linghu Mao's memorial as an early route toward recognizing Liu Ju's grievance before the later Tian Qianqiu settlement.
- Hanji 388-2 makes Wudi's remorse operational: review, Tian Qianqiu's memorial, punishment of Wugu actors, and mourning architecture.
- Hanji 391-1 source-scopedly treats Lady Gouyi as a hidden participant behind Jiang Chong and Su Wen in Liu Ju's destruction, while Han Wudi's alleged concealment of that charge shows the incident continuing to shape Liu Fuling's succession legitimacy.
- The purge aftermath can turn earlier anti-Liu-Ju service into later danger, as shown by Ma Heluo and Ma Tong's fear of implication.
- Liu Ju's presumed death and unresolved emotional charge explain why the later "return" claim creates shock, briefly paralyzes officials, and forces Jun Buyi to prioritize dynastic order over physical-identity uncertainty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page should not be treated as a complete account of the Wugu Incident. Hanji 381-2 supplies a symbolic-succession background, not proof that the later Wugu case directly follows from "尧母门" alone. Hanji 382-1 supplies Jiang Chong's pre-Wugu career and Chi Road grievance with Liu Ju, not a complete biography or proof that the later Wugu case follows from that incident alone. Hanji 383-2 supplies a compact Gongsun-family causal chain, not the full legal dossier for Gongsun Jingsheng, Zhu Anshi, Yangshi Princess, North Army finance, or the wooden figure evidence. Hanji 384-2 supplies a compact recognized-heir, strict-lenient, palace-slander, and Wei Zifu anxiety prelude, not a full biography of Liu Ju, Wei Zifu, Su Wen, Chang Rong, Wang Bi, or Wudi's succession thinking. Hanji 384-1 supplies a compact support-network prelude, not the complete legal records of Gongsun He, Gongsun Jingsheng, Wei Kang, the princesses, Du Zhou, or every Wei-family descendant. Hanji 385-1 supplies a compact origin-condition account, not the full religious history of wugu, the complete 91 BCE palace case, or a complete biography of Jiang Chong before he receives investigative authority. Hanji 385-2 supplies a compact manufactured-evidence, torture, palace-search, and Shi De advice account, not the complete primary-source wording, full list of accused people, exact interrogation process, or a full biography of Shi De. Hanji 386-1 supplies a compact escalation bridge, not a full biography of Han Shuo, Zhang Gan, the unnamed messenger, Liu Quli, exact date wording, troop details, or legal procedure. Hanji 386-2 supplies a compact command-collapse and battle account, not the full order of battle, exact casualty record, legal process, or biographies of Ren An, Tian Ren, Bao Shengzhi, Liu Quli, Ma Tong, and Shangqiu Cheng. Hanji 387-1 supplies a compact flight-and-remonstrance bridge, not Linghu Mao's full memorial text, Hu County local mechanics, or a full list of punished associates. Hanji 387-2 supplies a compact death-scene and Liu Bingyi survival hook, not the full prosecution or household genealogy. Hanji 388-2 supplies a compact remorse-and-punishment account, not a full case dossier or primary-source comparison. Hanji 391-1's Lady Gouyi claim is source-scoped and not upgraded here into settled historiography. Hanji 390-1 uses Wugu aftermath to explain Ma Heluo's plot; it does not reconstruct the full Wugu prosecution or prove every participant's legal exposure. Hanji 396-2 does not reconstruct causes, accusations, participants, deaths, legal procedure, or historiographical debate; it only uses the aftermath to explain the false-Liu-Ju crisis under Han Zhao.
