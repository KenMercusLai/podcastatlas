## Definition

Controllable child accession / 可控幼主继位 is the succession pattern where court powerholders choose or welcome a young ruler not only because the genealogy is legitimate, but because the ruler's age makes regent, outer-relative, or palace control easier.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] creates the late-Han source case. After [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] dies without a son, [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]] settle on Liu Hong, later [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], who is presented as a legitimate Liu-clan heir but also only twelve years old. The episode's judgment is that youth becomes a temptation: it lets adults imagine they can hold the real power behind a correct accession form.

The concept is adjacent to [[SuccessionNonDesignationRisk|未定继承人风险]] but not identical. Huan's problem is direct-heir absence, not merely silence about a named heir. The key mechanism here is the incentive produced by a legitimate but governable child candidate.

## Key Claims

- A child candidate can satisfy lineage legitimacy while creating practical room for regent or outer-relative control.
- The controlling incentive can coexist with formal procedures such as consultation, dowager approval, envoy dispatch, guards, and escort ritual.
- Choosing a controllable ruler may look stabilizing at the moment of succession while creating later factional struggle.
- The pattern exposes the moral weakness of powerholders who treat public succession as a private control opportunity.
- The concept should be kept separate from usurpation: the source case still uses Han lineage and accession forms.

## Evidence

- Succession gap: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Emperor Huan dies without a son, forcing the court to find a successor elsewhere in the Liu clan.
- Child controllability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Dou Wu and Dou Miao favor Liu Hong in part because he is only twelve and easier to control.
- Formalization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Liu Shu and [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] are sent with palace and guard personnel to receive Liu Hong, turning the choice into an accession process.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source gives the controllability reading as the host's political interpretation. It does not prove that every participant held the same motive, and it does not deny that Liu Hong's lineage could make him a valid candidate.

## What Changed

- Hanji 879 creates a distinct concept for legitimate child accession chosen under a control incentive.
