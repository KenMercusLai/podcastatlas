## Definition

Controllable child accession / 可控幼主继位 is the succession pattern where court powerholders choose or welcome a young ruler not only because the genealogy is legitimate, but because the ruler's age makes regent, outer-relative, or palace control easier.

## Current Synthesis

The earlier late-Han case appears after [[EmperorChongOfHan|汉冲帝]] dies. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]], [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] urges [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] to select an older, wise, and worthy successor who can handle government personally. [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]] has the stronger reputation among senior officials, but Liang Ji and [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠 / 梁太后]] choose the eight-year-old Liu Zuan, later [[EmperorZhiOfHan|汉质帝]], instead.

The later late-Han case appears after [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] dies without a son. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]], [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]] settle on Liu Hong, later [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], who is presented as a legitimate Liu-clan heir but also only twelve years old. The episode's judgment is that youth becomes a temptation: it lets adults imagine they can hold the real power behind a correct accession form.

The concept is adjacent to [[SuccessionNonDesignationRisk|未定继承人风险]] but not identical. Both source cases involve succession gaps, lineage legitimacy, and formal procedures; the distinctive mechanism is the incentive to prefer a candidate who seems governable by the adults already near power.

## Key Claims

- A child candidate can satisfy lineage legitimacy while creating practical room for regent, outer-relative, or palace control.
- The controlling incentive can coexist with formal procedures such as consultation, dowager approval, envoy dispatch, guards, escort ritual, and public enthronement.
- Choosing a controllable ruler may look stabilizing at the moment of succession while creating later factional struggle.
- A candidate viewed as older, stricter, or independently capable can become threatening precisely because he would govern rather than merely legitimate existing power.
- The pattern exposes the moral weakness of powerholders who treat public succession as a private control opportunity.
- The concept should be kept separate from usurpation: the source cases still use Han lineage and accession forms.

## Evidence

- Older-ruler advice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] says Li Gu tells Liang Ji that the new ruler should be older, wise, virtuous, and able to govern personally.
- Rejected capable alternative: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] says Liu Suan is admired by senior officials but is sent back after Liang Ji and Liang Na choose Liu Zuan.
- Child accession: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] says Liu Zuan is only eight when he is installed as emperor.
- Later succession gap: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Emperor Huan dies without a son, forcing the court to find a successor elsewhere in the Liu clan.
- Later child controllability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Dou Wu and Dou Miao favor Liu Hong in part because he is only twelve and easier to control.
- Later formalization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Liu Shu and [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] are sent with palace and guard personnel to receive Liu Hong, turning the choice into an accession process.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The controllability reading is source-scoped in both source notes. Hanji 825 does not fully prove Liang Ji and Liang Na's private reasoning beyond their rejection of Li Gu's older-ruler argument and their choice of an eight-year-old; Hanji 879 similarly gives the control motive as the host's political interpretation. Neither case denies that Liu Zuan or Liu Hong had usable Han lineage.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 825 as the earlier Liang-family child-accession case after Emperor Chong's death.
- Reframed Hanji 879 as the later Dou-family recurrence rather than the concept's only source case.
- Strengthened the distinction between legitimate lineage and practical governability.
