# Performed Beauty Efficacy / 美妆功效表演

## Definition

Performed beauty efficacy is the design of claims, packaging, application steps, sensation, and immediate visual feedback so consumers can understand and feel that a product is working, without treating that perception as proof of long-term biological efficacy.

## Current Synthesis

[[e261-biaoyan-youxiao-zutuan-chuhai-hanguo-meizhuang-ruhe-qiangzhan-meiguo-shichang-9753c5f7-03dd-4cfa-8d4a-40531f7c4b79]] separates three layers: marketing recognition, immediate use experience, and long-term result. Korean beauty is presented as especially strong at compressing complex ingredient stories into memorable names, creating visually demonstrable formats, and giving users sensations or rituals that make care feel active. The source's PDRN example also supplies the boundary: a topical cosmetic cannot inherit the evidence or effect of an injected procedure merely by sharing an ingredient label.

Performance is not necessarily deception. Immediate hydration, texture, cooling, color, or appearance can be real product value, while mixing, applying, and removing a product can supply emotional satisfaction. The error arises when those experiences are allowed to stand in for controlled evidence about durable outcomes.

## Key Claims

- Efficacy communication begins with making a technical promise understandable.
- Packaging, texture, steps, and visible before-and-after contrast can make benefit easier to perceive and share.
- Ritual complexity can add emotional value by making care feel deliberate.
- Immediate sensory or cosmetic change and long-term biological change are different evidence categories.
- Ingredient names can borrow authority from medical procedures without establishing equivalent delivery or effect.
- Strong performed efficacy can accelerate trial while leaving retention and clinical validity unresolved.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not imply that Korean cosmetics lack long-term efficacy or that sensory design is inherently misleading. The source offers no formulation review, clinical trials, ingredient concentrations, delivery data, or comparative product testing, and its claim that Chinese brands may have a higher median long-term effect is a guest judgment rather than established evidence.

## What Changed

- Created a three-layer framework separating claim legibility, immediate experience, and durable efficacy.
