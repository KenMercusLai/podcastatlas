## Definition

Outer-relative tutorship restraint is a preventive court-governance method that places upright teachers and companions around suddenly elevated imperial in-laws so their habits, expectations, and use of status can be shaped before kinship access becomes an entrenched power bloc.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-2-lnz79ec6cz5s1n90tifnnxmfvi2i|Hanji 208 part 2]] presents [[ZhouBo|周勃]] and [[GuanYing|灌婴]] as reacting to the Lü-family crisis before the newly elevated Dou household has committed a comparable abuse. Because [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦皇后]] and [[DouGuangguoWesternHan|窦广国]] move abruptly from poverty or displacement into imperial kinship and honor, the ministers treat character formation and social environment as a security question.

The mechanism differs from barring a relative from office or punishing misconduct after it occurs. It tries to alter the household's future conduct through respected daily associates who can normalize humility and restraint. The source supplies the decision and intended purpose, but not evidence about who the teachers were, how long the arrangement lasted, whether the siblings accepted it, or how much it caused their later behavior.

## Key Claims

- Sudden elevation can create governance risk before a new outer-relative household has accumulated formal office or committed a punishable offense.
- Teachers and companions can function as political infrastructure because repeated social influence may shape how favored kin interpret rank, obligation, and deference.
- The intervention is preventive and behavioral rather than punitive: it acts on formation before a court faction hardens.
- Recent outer-relative disaster supplies the risk model, but analogy to the Lü clan does not prove that the Dou siblings would otherwise have followed the same path.

## Evidence

Preventive timing and mechanism:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-2-lnz79ec6cz5s1n90tifnnxmfvi2i|Hanji 208 part 2]] says Zhou Bo and Guan Ying respond immediately after the Dou siblings' reunion and elevation by selecting upright, principled people as their teachers and companions.

Risk model and intended effect:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-2-lnz79ec6cz5s1n90tifnnxmfvi2i|Hanji 208 part 2]] connects the arrangement to fear of another Lü-family-style outer-relative crisis and says the intended result is a more modest household rather than one made arrogant by sudden honor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is currently a one-source pattern, not proof that moral tutorship reliably restrains kin power. The episode does not name the selected teachers, document their teaching, compare the siblings before and after the intervention, or isolate its effect from the emperor's authority, court surveillance, institutional limits, and the Dou family's own choices. The Lü precedent explains the ministers' fear but does not establish that the Dou household posed the same level or type of threat.

## What Changed

- Created the concept as a focused preventive mechanism distinct from office denial, legal punishment, or later factional balancing.
- Kept the claimed success of education separate from the documented fact that ministers arranged the social intervention.
