# Atomic Time Redefinition

## Definition

Atomic time redefinition is the planned replacement of the caesium-133 transition used to define the SI second with a more precise atomic-clock standard that laboratories can operate and reproduce internationally.

## Current Synthesis

The best modern optical clocks are substantially more accurate than the caesium clocks that define the unit used to evaluate them. Replacing the standard would resolve that technical inversion and improve the precision available to science, finance, and navigation. The strongest laboratory result is not automatically the best standard, however: operational reliability, apparatus scale, cross-laboratory reproducibility, and international agreement matter alongside headline accuracy.

## Key Claims

- The current SI second equals exactly 9,192,631,770 cycles associated with a caesium-133 transition.
- Strontium-, ytterbium-, aluminium-, and lutetium-based clocks can substantially outperform the best caesium clocks.
- A successor standard must be reliably reproduced in multiple places, not merely demonstrated once at exceptional accuracy.
- Apparatus size and operational practicality can outweigh a small advantage in maximum measured precision.
- National-laboratory competition and prestige may shape the institutional choice without replacing the scientific criteria.
- Everyday durations would not noticeably change even though scientific and technological precision would improve.

## Evidence

- Present definition and uses: [[bf8d29dbc7b83c7daefff88e8666657c]] gives the caesium frequency and connects atomic time to finance and satellite navigation.
- Performance gap: [[bf8d29dbc7b83c7daefff88e8666657c]] reports that several modern clocks can be roughly 100 times more accurate than caesium standards.
- Selection tradeoff: [[bf8d29dbc7b83c7daefff88e8666657c]] contrasts a highly accurate room-sized lutetium clock with somewhat less accurate but more practical alternatives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not identify a final replacement, decision timetable, uncertainty budget, comparison protocol, or primary metrology publication. Accuracy figures and institutional-motive claims remain episode-attributed; the suggestion that national prestige influences the decision is an inference rather than a demonstrated voting cause.

## What Changed

- Established the case for replacing caesium as a mismatch between the defining standard and better clocks.
- Added reproducibility, reliability, and apparatus practicality as coequal selection criteria.
- Preserved institutional competition as a qualification rather than treating it as the scientific basis of the choice.
