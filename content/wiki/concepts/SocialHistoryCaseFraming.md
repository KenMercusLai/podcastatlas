# Social History Case Framing / 社会史案件叙事

## Definition

Social history case framing is the method [[NittanWuyu|日谈物语]] uses to make cases reveal groups, social types, historical conditions, and diffuse responsibility rather than treating events only as isolated tragedies or conventional true-crime puzzles.

## Current Synthesis

The current sources make this concept the explicit editorial premise for [[NittanWuyu|日谈物语]]'s 2026 autumn season. E01 says modern or near-modern historical events should help listeners think about present social uncertainty through the announced [[JapanAIDSBloodProductsIncident|日本艾滋血液药剂事件]]. E02 extends the frame to [[YokkaichiPollutionIncident|四日市公害事件]] by calling it a "serial murder case without a killer," a setup that points toward systemic harm and non-obvious responsibility. The frame is interpretive rather than evidentiary by itself: it says how to read a case, not what the case proves.

## Key Claims

- A case can work as "seeing the large through the small" when it connects one event to a wider social condition.
- Individual stories can reveal groups, social types, and the lives of people in a particular era.
- Historical events can become references for thinking about contemporary uncertainty and social worry.
- The method asks listeners to move beyond curiosity about tragedy toward deeper public reflection.
- The frame requires enough case evidence to avoid turning a title or premise into unsupported historical claims.
- The method can handle diffuse-harm cases where responsibility may not fit a single intentional offender.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources state an editorial ambition and case hooks, not completed demonstrations. Whether the method succeeds for the Japanese AIDS blood-products topic or the Yokkaichi pollution topic depends on evidence that is not present in the current partial transcripts.
