# Social History Case Framing / 社会史案件叙事

## Definition

Social history case framing is the method described in [[2026-qiuji-pian-e01-xueye-zhi-zai-riben-aizi-xueye-yaoji-shijian-fv-j4cvvj0tnhfhdinjtcmudcirj]]: using a case or event to see a group, a type of person, and the living conditions of a special historical moment, rather than treating the event only as an isolated tragedy.

## Current Synthesis

The current source makes this concept the explicit editorial premise for [[NittanWuyu|日谈物语]]'s 2026 autumn season. The hosts want modern or near-modern historical events to help listeners think about present social uncertainty, including the announced [[JapanAIDSBloodProductsIncident|日本艾滋血液药剂事件]]. The frame is interpretive rather than evidentiary by itself: it says how to read a case, not what the case proves.

## Key Claims

- A case can work as "seeing the large through the small" when it connects one event to a wider social condition.
- Individual stories can reveal groups, social types, and the lives of people in a particular era.
- Historical events can become references for thinking about contemporary uncertainty and social worry.
- The method asks listeners to move beyond curiosity about tragedy toward deeper public reflection.
- The frame requires enough case evidence to avoid turning a title or premise into unsupported historical claims.
