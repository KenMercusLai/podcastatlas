# Public Indifference Policy Drift

Public indifference policy drift is the pattern where an issue is serious enough to absorb government attention but not central enough to force a clear democratic settlement. In [[699-the-troubles-return-of-the-ira-part-3-glt9519811642]], British voters in the early 1970s often favored bringing troops home or abandoning [[NorthernIreland]] to its fate, but the issue ranked low among mainland public concerns.

The episode treats this indifference as one reason British policy could drift. [[EdwardHeath]] and [[ReginaldMaudling]] feared withdrawal would bring international condemnation and a sectarian bloodbath, yet mainland public pressure did not create a coherent alternative to continued military involvement, [[InternmentWithoutTrial]], and avoidance of direct rule.

## Key Claims
- Low public salience can preserve policy inertia even during severe violence.
- A government may keep choosing temporary crisis management when exit is feared but voters do not demand a detailed settlement.
- International criticism, such as [[TedKennedy]]'s intervention, can matter more because domestic attention is weak.
