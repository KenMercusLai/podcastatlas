# Restorative–Transformative Athletic Intervention

## Definition

Restorative–transformative athletic intervention is a case-based taxonomy distinguishing treatment aimed at returning an injured athlete toward prior function from intervention that creates materially new capacity and may coerce competitors into comparable risks.

## Current Synthesis

[[the-standard-case-the-standard-case|The Standard Case]] constructs the distinction from three cases. [[TommyJohn]]'s reconstructed elbow is the accepted restorative standard because the procedure allowed him to use existing strength and mechanics after an otherwise career-ending injury. [[BarryBonds]] anchors transformation through extensive alleged drug use, dramatic physical change, exceptional late-career output, and pressure on rivals to accept health risks.

[[AndyPettitte]] is the disputed middle case. His reported HGH use was brief, tied to one elbow rehabilitation, and followed by performance close to his prior baseline. If those premises hold, purpose, duration, effect, and competitive harm place the intervention nearer restoration than transformation even though it used a prohibited or stigmatized technology.

The taxonomy rejects a simple natural-versus-artificial boundary, but it does not make restorative intent sufficient. Classification must test the stated purpose against dosage, duration, resulting capacity, safety, access, rules, and effects on other competitors.

## Key Claims

- Medical technology alone does not determine moral status because accepted surgery and disputed drugs can both alter athletic capacity.
- Prior functional baseline supplies one reference point for distinguishing rehabilitation from the creation of materially new ability.
- Purpose matters when supported by use pattern and outcome, but self-reported intent is not conclusive.
- Scale, duration, health risk, access, rule compliance, and pressure on competitors remain morally relevant even when treatment begins with an injury.
- The distinction is a graded taxonomy for comparison, not a binary clinical test or automatic exemption.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The boundary depends on contestable baselines and counterfactuals. Surgery may increase durability beyond an untreated career, rehabilitation can improve an athlete beyond pre-injury condition, and a small intervention may still violate rules or confer unequal advantage. The Pettitte case depends on unverified intent and use history, while the Bonds comparison compresses disputed medical, legal, and performance evidence. The framework is an ethical heuristic, not clinical advice or a substitute for league policy.

## What Changed

- Established restoration, transformation, and a disputed intermediate case as a comparative taxonomy.
- Added competitive coercion and health-risk pressure to baseline and performance effects.
- Made corroboration of restorative intent an explicit qualification.
