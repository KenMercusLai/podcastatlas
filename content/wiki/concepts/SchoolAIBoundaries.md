# School AI Boundaries

## Definition
School AI boundaries are institutional rules that define when AI tools, screens, automated grading, companion-style chatbots, and student-facing generative AI may be used in school settings, especially for younger students.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded sources now show New York City as a concrete test case for school AI governance. The Marketplace Tech source frames the policy as a school-controlled line between tools that support learning and tools that replace student work, teacher judgment, or social protection. The All-In episode adds a sharper policy tradeoff: a one-year K-8 student-facing generative AI moratorium under [[ZohranMamdani]] may reduce shortcut and safety risks, but it could also widen the private-public gap if wealthier students retain access to adaptive AI tutoring while public-school students mostly learn suspicion, risk, and compliance. The synthesis is that restrictions need a literacy and supervised-use counterpart or they become defensive bans rather than education policy.

## Key Claims
- School AI policy should distinguish educational assistance from answer substitution.
- Age and setting matter because younger students face different developmental, privacy, and safety risks from AI and individual screens.
- Automated grading deserves a stricter boundary because evaluation carries authority, feedback quality, and student-trust implications.
- Companion chatbots require special caution because emotional attachment and always-available validation differ from ordinary homework help.
- Student-facing moratoria need an AI-literacy counterpart so students learn how to use, critique, and verify AI systems.
- AI tutoring and adaptive learning can improve access, but only if students remain cognitively engaged rather than outsourcing the work.

## Evidence
Educational and safety boundaries:
- [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the policy draws a line between useful digital tools and tools that do the work for students.
- [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes restrictions on AI and individual screens through middle school and treats companion chatbots as distinct from ordinary math-help tools.
- [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] says teachers are barred from using AI to grade assignments.

Moratorium and literacy tension:
- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-gpt-6-hits-agi-tech-euphoria-20-sf-mansion-shortage-nyc-bans-ai-in-schools-venezuela-oil-deal-42788250]] describes a one-year K-8 student-facing generative AI moratorium in [[NewYorkCityPublicSchools]], with high schools exempt and a 50,000-student high-school AI learning pilot.
- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-gpt-6-hits-agi-tech-euphoria-20-sf-mansion-shortage-nyc-bans-ai-in-schools-venezuela-oil-deal-42788250]] reports [[DavidSacks]]' concern that AI-literacy curriculum language may emphasize risk, bias, ethics, supervision, and vetted tools more than confident practical use.

Learning-design evidence:
- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-gpt-6-hits-agi-tech-euphoria-20-sf-mansion-shortage-nyc-bans-ai-in-schools-venezuela-oil-deal-42788250]] cites AI tutoring, adaptive pace, and [[BloomTwoSigmaProblem]] upside while also citing small-study evidence of cognitive offloading among LLM users.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source shows outcome evidence that New York City's approach improves learning or safety. School restrictions also do not control home-device use. The All-In critique is ideologically skeptical of bans and may underweight developmental and vendor-governance reasons for slower K-8 deployment.

## What Changed
- Added the K-8 one-year student-facing generative AI moratorium as a concrete boundary case.
- Added the high-school exemption and 50,000-student AI learning pilot as the policy's literacy counterpart.
- Added the private-public gap and cognitive-offloading tradeoff to the current synthesis.
