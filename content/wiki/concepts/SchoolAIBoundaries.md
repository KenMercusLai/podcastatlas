# School AI Boundaries

## Definition
School AI boundaries are institutional rules that define when AI tools, screens, automated grading, and companion-style chatbots may be used in school settings, especially for younger students.

## Current Synthesis
This concept is grounded by the Marketplace Tech discussion of New York City public schools. The strongest idea is not a blanket anti-technology stance; it is a school-controlled line between tools that support learning and tools that replace the work, judgment, or social protection that schools are responsible for preserving. The episode also keeps a caveat visible: school restrictions can create protected classroom space, but they do not solve home-device use or AI literacy on their own.

## Key Claims
- School AI policy should distinguish educational assistance from answer substitution.
- Age and setting matter because younger students face different developmental and safety risks from AI and individual screens.
- Automated grading deserves a stricter boundary because evaluation carries authority, feedback quality, and student-trust implications.
- Companion chatbots require special caution because emotional attachment and always-available validation differ from ordinary homework help.
- AI restrictions need a literacy counterpart so students learn how AI systems work rather than only what they may not use in class.

## Evidence
- Educational-boundary evidence: [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the policy draws a line between useful digital tools and tools that do the work for students.
- Age-and-setting evidence: [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes restrictions on AI and individual screens through middle school.
- Evaluation evidence: [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] says teachers are barred from using AI to grade assignments.
- Companion-risk evidence: [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] treats companion chatbots as meaningfully different from ordinary math-help tools.
- Literacy evidence: [[tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260904-0904-mp-tech-pod-128]] records the criticism that students may still need explicit AI education despite classroom restrictions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not show whether the policy improves learning outcomes, reduces misuse, or changes vendor behavior over time. Stern also notes that children can encounter companion chatbots and other AI tools outside school, so school boundaries are only one part of the safety and literacy problem.
