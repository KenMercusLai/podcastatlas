## Definition

修养型帝王能力 / cultivated imperial capacity is the source's frame for rulership where learning, military ability, personnel judgment, and moral self-command reinforce one another rather than being counted as separate talents.

## Current Synthesis

The concept is currently grounded in [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]. Liu Xiu's reputation rests on a combined pattern: his fallen-lineage and [[Taixue|太学]] study make learning practical, [[KunyangBattle|昆阳之战]] makes battlefield judgment visible under extreme imbalance, the [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] letter-burning scene makes trust an active post-victory choice, and his restraint after Liu Yan's death shows that grief and danger have to be governed before power can be used.

The concept is narrower than general talent or moral praise. The source does not prove that Liu Xiu outranks every emperor in learning, command, or personnel use. Its durable contribution is the evaluative standard: a capable ruler still needs cultivation, restraint, and public-minded use of people for ability to become stable governance.

## Key Claims

- Ruler capacity is composite: learning, military judgment, personnel use, and character are assessed together.
- Education matters when it becomes political judgment, not only cultural polish.
- Battlefield ability is valued partly by minimizing costs and choosing high-leverage action under asymmetry.
- Trust-building after victory can preserve coalition stability better than exposing every wavering subordinate.
- Emotional restraint is a governance capacity when visible grief, anger, or fear would create survival risk.
- The concept distinguishes ability from cultivated rule: clever, learned, or forceful rulers can still fail if character and restraint are weak.

- Composite standard: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-plus-jipin-huangdi-liuxiu-wu-neng-da-tianxia-wen-neng-zhi-guojia-lntw7hm3afitzt7revomgqs-qvph|Hanji 699 PLUS]] explicitly organizes Liu Xiu's evaluation around learning, personnel use, battlefield ability, and ruler character.
- Education as formation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-plus-jipin-huangdi-liuxiu-wu-neng-da-tianxia-wen-neng-zhi-guojia-lntw7hm3afitzt7revomgqs-qvph|Hanji 699 PLUS]] says Liu Xiu's Chang'an Taixue study broadened his political vision and became part of his later founding and governing capacity.
- Battle and cost discipline: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-plus-jipin-huangdi-liuxiu-wu-neng-da-tianxia-wen-neng-zhi-guojia-lntw7hm3afitzt7revomgqs-qvph|Hanji 699 PLUS]] presents Kunyang as a case where Liu Xiu breaks encirclement, gathers reinforcements, works morale, and attacks the command center despite extreme numerical inferiority.
- Trust and coalition repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-plus-jipin-huangdi-liuxiu-wu-neng-da-tianxia-wen-neng-zhi-guojia-lntw7hm3afitzt7revomgqs-qvph|Hanji 699 PLUS]] says Liu Xiu burns Wang Lang correspondence unopened to avoid turning old wavering into lasting suspicion.
- Self-command under danger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-699-plus-jipin-huangdi-liuxiu-wu-neng-da-tianxia-wen-neng-zhi-guojia-lntw7hm3afitzt7revomgqs-qvph|Hanji 699 PLUS]] says Liu Xiu conceals grief after Liu Yan is killed and apologizes to Liu Xuan, preserving survival under a threatening superior.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one evaluative PLUS episode and should not be treated as a completed emperor-comparison theory. The source's comparisons with Liu Bang, Kangxi, Li Shimin, Qin Shi Huang, Cao Cao, Sima Yi, Zhu Yuanzhang, Zhu Di, and Yuan Shikai are ranking frames, not bounded comparative biographies. Kunyang's meteor and cloud material belongs to legendary mandate narration unless independently grounded by other source notes.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 699 PLUS to capture the episode's ability-plus-cultivation ruler standard.
- Kept the emperor-ranking language source-scoped rather than converting it into a global hierarchy.
