# Budgeted Frontier Suppression / 预算化边疆清剿

## Definition

Budgeted frontier suppression / 预算化边疆清剿 is the strategic frame where a commander presents harsh frontier war as a bounded administrative project with specified enemy condition, terrain, force package, duration, and money cost.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence centers on [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] and the [[DongqiangLateHan|东羌]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]] gives the cleanest version: after Western Qiang are pacified but Xianling and other Eastern Qiang groups remain unsettled, Duan argues that repeated surrender and rebellion make soft handling costly. He claims the target is weakened, located on flatter terrain, and vulnerable to a defined campaign using cavalry, infantry, vehicle support, several seasons, and fifty-four hundred million cash.

The later sources keep that budget logic from becoming self-justifying. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-881-shi-shui-zai-fa-zhanzheng-hengcai-lipglslijnbp0cuqfpps88q5wit6|Hanji 881]] links successful attack and interim reward to campaign funding access, so the wiki treats the plan as both strategic calculation and institutional incentive. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] preserves [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]]'s surrender-first objection, [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] shows Duan using surrender-risk logic to justify attack, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] adds [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s anti-extermination and root-cause critique. Budgeting can make violence legible to the court; it does not prove violence solves the frontier problem.

## Key Claims

- A frontier commander can make hard suppression administratively attractive by giving troop numbers, cost, time, terrain, and expected finality.
- Budgeted suppression depends on assumptions about enemy weakness, geography, surrender reliability, and the cost of repeated rebellion.
- A lower projected campaign cost can make harsh violence look prudent when compared with decades of prior spending.
- Funding channels, rewards, and palace access can bias which military plan appears executable or desirable.
- Surrender-first and root-cause approaches remain live qualifications; the existence of a budget does not settle the moral or governance case.

## Evidence

- Initial budget logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]] says Duan proposes five thousand cavalry, ten thousand infantry, three thousand vehicle units, three winters, two summers, and fifty-four hundred million cash to settle the Eastern Qiang problem.
- Historical-cost comparison: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]] has Duan compare his projected cost with earlier Qiang rebellions that lasted many years and consumed much larger sums.
- Funding and reward incentive: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-881-shi-shui-zai-fa-zhanzheng-hengcai-lipglslijnbp0cuqfpps88q5wit6|Hanji 881]] says Duan's later victory brings cash, family appointment, Zhongcangfu campaign funding, and the `破羌将军` title, while the host reads large military funding as attractive to palace-access actors.
- Policy-dispute qualification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] records Zhang Huan's argument that residual Eastern Qiang are hard to eliminate and should be induced to surrender and peace.
- Surrender-risk execution: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] shows Duan rejecting a surrender mission as unreliable under hunger and provisioning pressure, then using surprise movement and encirclement.
- Ethical and root-cause critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] uses Sima Guang and the host's reading to argue that Qiang rebellion should be tied to harsh commandery governance rather than innate hostility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept records how hard suppression is made governable as a proposal, not whether Duan's estimate was objectively reliable. Hanji 883 and Hanji 892 preserve counterweights: surrender policy may reduce killing, root-cause governance may be necessary for durable peace, and Sima Guang's critique refuses to treat frontier populations as expendable. Hanji 881 also means budgeted suppression must be read alongside campaign finance incentives, not only military necessity.

## What Changed

- Established the concept from Hanji 880's explicit Duan Jiong force, duration, terrain, and cash memorial.
- Integrated Hanji 881, Hanji 883, Hanji 891, and Hanji 892 as bounded qualifications so the concept does not become a simple argument for force.
