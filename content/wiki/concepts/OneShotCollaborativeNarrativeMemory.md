# One-Shot Collaborative Narrative Memory / 一次性共同叙事记忆

## Definition
One-shot collaborative narrative memory is the shared experience created when a repeatable story framework is transformed by a particular group's information, interpretation, emotion, and choices into an event that cannot be reproduced exactly.

## Current Synthesis
EP194 treats the script as a scaffold rather than the whole work. Each participant receives a partial life and evidence set, then the group performs, withholds, discloses, negotiates, reasons, and reacts in real time. The written material can be replayed by another group, but the social event cannot: participants become variables in the narrative and later remember not only what the story said but what they did together.

This gives the format possible educational and reflective value without making those outcomes automatic. A session may embody history, culture, ethical conflict, legal questions, or unfamiliar life choices more concretely than exposition alone, yet what players learn depends on script accuracy, facilitation, interpretation, and post-play reflection.

## Key Claims
- A stable script can generate different experiences because participants are causal variables rather than interchangeable recipients.
- Shared memory includes interaction, mistakes, alliances, emotion, and collective discovery, not only the canonical ending.
- Partial information makes each player both audience and contributor to the group's account.
- Embodied perspective can make historical, cultural, ethical, or social questions feel concrete without guaranteeing accurate understanding.
- The singularity of the event can strengthen group identity through later references and retelling.
- One-shot value depends on avoiding spoilers and concealed prior knowledge that collapse uncertainty for other players.

## Evidence
- Player variability: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] says the same script changes because the people at the table and their interactions differ.
- Durable group memory: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] describes colleagues continuing to address one another by in-game numbers weeks after a remotely facilitated session.
- Perspective and inquiry: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] credits strong scripts with opening history, mythology, unfamiliar lives, and debates about social, ethical, and legal questions.
- One-shot boundary: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] compares prior-answer leakage and hidden replay knowledge to spoilers that destroy collective discovery.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Memorability does not prove factual learning, empathy transfer, moral improvement, or healthy group bonding. A poor script can reproduce stereotypes or historical errors; intense identification can crowd out critical distance; and group pressure can make emotional participation uncomfortable. The episode supplies examples and player interpretation rather than outcome research.

## What Changed
- Established the repeatable-script versus singular-event distinction.
- Connected asymmetric participation to durable shared memory.
- Bounded educational and empathetic value by accuracy, facilitation, reflection, and evidence limits.
