# Fishmeal Supply Chain

## Definition

The fishmeal supply chain converts small oily fish, especially [[PeruvianAnchoveta|Peruvian anchoveta]], into concentrated protein and oil inputs used in aquaculture, livestock feed, and some premium pet foods.

## Current Synthesis

[[shangye-xiaoyang-45-eerninuo-yueda-yu-yuegui-1000164352]] establishes the ecological and industrial chain: Peruvian anchoveta depend on cold-water upwelling and plankton, catches are processed into fishmeal and fish oil, and the resulting ingredients support farmed salmon, shrimp, sea bass, and other seafood. Supply concentration, short anchoveta life cycles, El Niño, juvenile-heavy catches, and resource-management closures can make the chain volatile.

[[e254-chaoji-eerninuo-laile-womende-richang-suoxu-zhenhui-yin-ta-zhangjia-ma-0b644f51-461c-4ddb-b707-d2a39b9a7d80]] extends the downstream map. Fishmeal cost can reach aquaculture only after feed formulation, and can also affect pig feed and high-end cat or dog food. Soy meal, rapeseed meal, and other ingredients offer partial substitution, but animal metabolism, amino-acid needs, performance, quality, and relative economics limit how far substitution can go.

## Key Claims

- **A small biological input can support large consumer categories.** Anchoveta-derived meal and oil sit upstream of global farmed seafood and other feed markets.
- **Ecology and policy jointly determine supply.** El Niño can reduce biological productivity while quotas and closures trade current supply for future stock protection.
- **Geographic concentration creates exposure.** Heavy reliance on Peru and neighboring producers makes regional disruption globally relevant.
- **Substitution is partial rather than binary.** Alternative proteins can reduce inclusion rates but may not reproduce nutrition, digestibility, growth, quality, or cost.
- **Transmission is delayed and indirect.** A fishmeal shock first enters feed cost and formulation before affecting harvest volume, seafood, livestock, or pet-food prices.

## Evidence

- **Ecology, concentration, and management:** [[shangye-xiaoyang-45-eerninuo-yueda-yu-yuegui-1000164352]] connects upwelling, plankton, anchoveta biology, Peruvian catch restrictions, regional concentration, and stock protection.
- **Aquaculture dependency and consumer path:** [[shangye-xiaoyang-45-eerninuo-yueda-yu-yuegui-1000164352]] traces feed into salmon, shrimp, sea bass, sushi, restaurants, and other seafood buyers.
- **Broader feed use and substitution boundary:** [[e254-chaoji-eerninuo-laile-womende-richang-suoxu-zhenhui-yin-ta-zhangjia-ma-0b644f51-461c-4ddb-b707-d2a39b9a7d80]] adds pig feed, premium pet food, formula adjustment, and nutritional and economic limits on plant-protein substitution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Inclusion rates, formulas, prices, catch rules, and biological conditions change over time and differ by species, life stage, producer, and jurisdiction.
- Catch restrictions can intensify immediate scarcity while improving the probability of future stock recovery; the short- and long-run price effects can point in different directions.
- Higher fishmeal prices do not imply immediate or equal increases in restaurant, seafood, meat, or pet-food prices because inventories, contracts, substitutes, margins, and demand intervene.
- The quantitative supply, catch, and price claims remain attributed to the two episodes rather than independently verified current data.

## What Changed

- Added livestock and premium pet food to the downstream demand map.
- Sharpened the nutritional, biological, and economic limits of feed substitution.
- Separated fishmeal-price movement from delayed and partial consumer-price transmission.
