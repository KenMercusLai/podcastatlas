# Portable Political Capital / 可携带政治资本

## Definition
Portable political capital is the capacity an actor carries between unstable patron relationships by converting borrowed office, troops, reputation, relationships, and followers into resources that survive the loss of any single platform.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1098 defines the pattern through [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s repeated movement among stronger patrons. The source does not treat each relationship as equivalent or each departure as costless. Its narrower claim is that Liu Bei's organization becomes more valuable because service under [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]], [[TaoQianLateHan|陶谦]], [[CaoCao|曹操]], [[YuanShao|袁绍]], and [[LiuBiao|刘表]] can add assets that are not fully owned by the host: personal loyalty, public reputation, learned political judgment, and a following able to regroup after territorial loss.

Portability depends on reputational boundaries. The episode contrasts Liu Bei's effort to leave relationships without killing former patrons with [[LyuBu|吕布]]'s murders of Ding Yuan and Dong Zhuo. In this account, restraint preserves the possibility that a future host will see the mobile actor as useful rather than automatically lethal. [[XinyeLateHan|新野]] supplies the clearest accumulation interval: limited shelter under Liu Biao becomes time to gather people and capacity before the next collapse.

## Key Claims
- Borrowed platforms can create durable capacity when relationships, skills, reputation, and followers attach partly to the actor rather than only to the office.
- Each move is valuable only if it adds an asset or improves bargaining position; repeated movement by itself is not accumulation.
- Reputation constrains portability because betrayal that destroys future trust can erase the value of newly acquired resources.
- Low-profile shelter such as Xinye can be an accumulation stage even when the actor remains strategically dependent.
- Portable capital preserves organizational continuity through base loss, but it does not substitute permanently for territory, revenue, or alliance.

## Evidence
- Patron sequence and resource conversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1098-liubei-de-tiaocao-miji-daizhe-ziyuan-yue-tiao-yue-zhiqian-ltasucqzwrono0qayzx8sonsqc8k|Hanji 1098]] frames Liu Bei's moves among stronger actors as cumulative acquisition of office, troops, networks, status, and followers.
- Reputational boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1098-liubei-de-tiaocao-miji-daizhe-ziyuan-yue-tiao-yue-zhiqian-ltasucqzwrono0qayzx8sonsqc8k|Hanji 1098]] contrasts Liu Bei's maintained exit relationships with Lü Bu's killing of former patrons.
- Accumulation under dependence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1098-liubei-de-tiaocao-miji-daizhe-ziyuan-yue-tiao-yue-zhiqian-ltasucqzwrono0qayzx8sonsqc8k|Hanji 1098]] presents the Xinye years as a period of follower, talent, family, and military accumulation under Liu Biao's protection.
- Reputation conversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1098-liubei-de-tiaocao-miji-daizhe-ziyuan-yue-tiao-yue-zhiqian-ltasucqzwrono0qayzx8sonsqc8k|Hanji 1098]] interprets benevolence as an asset that retains followers and attracts civilian attachment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current concept rests on one popular-history episode and its modern workplace/startup analogy. The patron list compresses different offices, alliances, refuges, and conquests into a single "job-hopping" frame. Exact resource transfers, troop totals, motives, and the causal force of benevolence remain source-scoped. A later victory can also reshape moral memory, as the episode itself acknowledges, so reputation should not be treated as an objective balance sheet.

## What Changed
- Established portability as the link between Liu Bei's repeated dependency and organizational continuity.
- Made reputational restraint a condition of resource-carrying mobility rather than a separate moral decoration.
- Identified Xinye dormancy as a long conversion interval within the broader pattern.
