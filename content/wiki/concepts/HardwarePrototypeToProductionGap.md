# Hardware Prototype-to-Production Gap / 硬件原型到量产鸿沟

## Definition
The hardware prototype-to-production gap is the operating distance between demonstrating that a device can work and repeatedly manufacturing, shipping, supporting, and financing a device that works reliably in real use.

## Current Synthesis
[[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] treats confusing a demo with a product as a formative error in [[Insta360|Insta360's]] early years. The missing capabilities included manufacturing-process design, yield, supplier cooperation, environmental and vibration reliability, battery-contact behavior, quality control, and the ability to absorb failures after launch. The Ambi interview adds the ecosystem-scale version: Shenzhen can make rapid sampling unusually accessible, but stable high-volume delivery still requires different capital, engineering, design, warehousing, logistics, risk reduction, and supply-chain coordination. [[ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli-ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli]] adds a two-generation scooter case: attractive design and successful crowdfunding did not prevent first-generation reliability problems, while the second generation required redesigned parts, prolonged development, debt, manufacturing learning, and an overseas partner's testing and quality support. A visually persuasive prototype proves possibility; production readiness proves repeatability under cost and service constraints.

## Key Claims
- A functional demo validates a technical path but not manufacturing repeatability, field reliability, or unit economics.
- Yield and process design can determine viability even when the product concept and prototype are strong.
- Reliability requirements arise from actual use conditions, such as vibration and replaceable-battery contact, not only laboratory operation.
- Supplier and manufacturing partners contribute specialized knowledge that a software-oriented founding team may not possess.
- Post-launch defects compound through replacements, support, reputation damage, and the time needed for later products to rebuild trust.
- Geographic prototype speed does not collapse the separate challenge of stable scaled delivery.
- A better second generation may require redesigning the system rather than patching the visible first-generation failure.

## Evidence
- Early operating gap: [[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] says the team initially treated making a demo and making a mass-produced product as too similar.
- Manufacturing evidence: [[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] describes low yield, weak process knowledge, reluctant factories, and later collaboration with an experienced GoPro manufacturer.
- Reliability evidence: [[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] uses action-camera vibration and removable-battery disconnection as an example of hidden field requirements.
- Reputation evidence: [[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] records Luo Yonghao's view that hardware quality failures may require several later products to repair trust.
- Prototype-ecosystem boundary: [[yi-ge-personal-agent-chuangyezhe-de-sikao-duitan-ambi-chuangshiren-zihao-lkr2rf-l-ft-p-i6f-tay5vlnt6e]] distinguishes Shenzhen's rapid prototyping advantages from the capital, supply-chain, engineering, logistics, and quality systems required for stable mass delivery.
- Scooter-generation evidence: [[ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli-ll4ekwtvjnpxit2gdkvcfphf0eli]] contrasts a light, well-received first design with weak manufacturing and quality, then describes extensive second-generation redesign and external quality-system learning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The exact gap varies by category, volume, certification regime, component maturity, and outsourcing model. Experienced contract manufacturers, customers, and dense supplier ecosystems can reduce lead time or teach missing practices but do not transfer away the brand owner's product, quality, cash-flow, and support responsibility. The sources supply practitioner testimony rather than comparative failure-rate, yield, or delivery data; the scooter account also lacks independent defect, shipment, and financial records.

## What Changed
- Added the two-generation scooter case showing that design appeal and crowdfunding can coexist with reliability failure.
- Added external customer engineering as one route for acquiring testing and quality-system capability.
