# AI Model Approval Regime

## Definition
An AI model approval regime is a government or government-adjacent process that requires powerful models to be reviewed, cleared, delayed, or restricted before broad release, often analogized in this episode to an "FDA for AI."

## Current Synthesis
The episode strongly rejects broad pre-release approval for general AI models while leaving room for narrow controls around specific dangerous capabilities. The hosts treat an FDA-style process as too slow, too capture-prone, and too likely to damage U.S. AI competition if applied broadly. At the same time, they acknowledge that cyber-capable systems may need logging, hardening, verified access, and coordination with security firms.

The resulting distinction is important: model approval is not the same as [[FrontierModelReleaseGovernance]]. Release governance can include staged rollout, red-teaming, self-certification, or access controls; an approval regime implies a more formal gate that can block release before users or customers receive the model.

## Key Claims
- The core objection is to broad government pre-clearance of models, not to every form of safety testing.
- FDA analogies can confuse the policy debate because software release cycles, model iteration, and cyber risk differ from drug approval.
- Approval gates can become [[AIRegulatoryCaptureRisk]] if large incumbents can afford compliance while smaller labs cannot.
- Narrower interventions are treated as more legitimate when they address a concrete risk such as cyber misuse rather than general intelligence capability.
- The politics of "voluntary" review matter because companies may treat informal government pressure as mandatory even without a formal licensing statute.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source discusses reported policy ideas and host conversations, not a final legal text. Some high-risk model capabilities may still justify pre-release evaluation, especially where cyber, biosecurity, national-security, or critical-infrastructure risks are concrete. The concept should not be used to dismiss all release governance.
