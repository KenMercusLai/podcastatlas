# Chinese Astrological Politics / 中国星占政治

Chinese astrological politics / 中国星占政治 is the episode-87 pattern for reading celestial phenomena as commentary on earthly rule. In [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997]], the source starts from early sky observation, sun-moon-star records, the twenty-eight mansions, five planets, Jupiter-year counting, and "分野" correspondences between sky regions and earthly states.

In [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-jie-shangji-704051550]], stars can correspond to officials, rulers, war, longevity, national fortune, and sudden crisis; unusual planets, meteors, guest stars, and star positions become political signs.

The concept matters because astrology is not presented as private entertainment. Figures such as [[JingFang|京房]] make star interpretation part of government criticism, while [[WangMang|王莽]] turns North Dipper orientation into ritualized crisis behavior.

## Key Claims
- Celestial signs can turn political judgment into cosmic diagnosis.
- Astrology and prophecy overlap when stars authorize or threaten legitimacy.
- Court actors can use star interpretation as evidence, warning, or ritual instruction.
- The source links technical observation to story motifs such as fallen general stars and hidden sword qi.
- The first part shows the older background in which regular astronomical observation becomes available for later political interpretation.
