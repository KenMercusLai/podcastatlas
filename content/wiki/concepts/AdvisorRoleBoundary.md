## Definition

Advisor role boundary is the distinction between diagnosing, persuading, administering, and deciding: an adviser can be substantively right yet politically ineffective or institutionally damaging when acting beyond delegated authority, while restraint can preserve command structure without making the underlying conduct morally acceptable.

## Current Synthesis

The Hongmen sources establish the failure case. [[FanZeng|范增]] correctly identifies [[LiuBang|刘邦]] as a future danger, but after [[XiangYu|项羽]] ignores his signals, he directs [[XiangZhuang|项庄]] to stage an assassination without securing ruler authorization, public reason, or a workable aftermath. When Liu Bang escapes, Fan Zeng's public rebuke further converts accurate diagnosis into humiliation of the decision-maker. Insight is therefore not equivalent to usable authority.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1148-fazheng-zuowei-zuofu-zhugeliang-weishenme-buguan-ljdigbpovfw6rh6oy30drk1idks9|Hanji 1148]] supplies the restraint case. [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] reportedly declines to override [[LiuBei|刘备]] over [[FaZheng|法正]] even when Fa Zheng uses office against former enemies. The episode argues that Liu Bei holds final personnel authority and that open intervention could damage a fragile Yizhou settlement. Role awareness can therefore mean not converting administrative responsibility into unilateral control of the ruler's core appointments.

The two cases do not make obedience the highest value. Fan Zeng's failure shows why unauthorized escalation can rupture command; Zhuge Liang's case shows why nonintervention can preserve political order. But the latter also exposes the concept's moral limit: respecting jurisdiction does not erase victim harm, unequal enforcement, or the duty to remonstrate through legitimate channels. The concept evaluates authority, sequencing, and consequence, not whether silence is automatically virtuous.

## Key Claims

- Correct diagnosis does not itself confer decision authority.
- Advisers must convert insight into leader buy-in, legitimate sequence, and contingency design.
- Unauthorized escalation can damage command trust even when the strategic concern is real.
- Public humiliation after a failed decision usually reduces rather than increases future influence.
- Role-aware restraint can protect institutional order when final appointment or punishment authority belongs elsewhere.
- Respect for jurisdiction does not excuse private retaliation, selective enforcement, or failure to use available remonstrance channels.

## Evidence

- Unauthorized action and legitimacy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-141-huanyuan-lishi-zhong-de-hongmenyan-lpmb-bcoocngtqjvtnpj0elm-siy|Hanji 141]] says Fan Zeng moves from ignored jade-ring signals to directing Xiang Zhuang's sword-dance attempt without Xiang Yu's settled authorization or a public pretext.
- Post-failure influence loss: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-142-zhechang-juhui-xiangyu-shudiao-le-yiqie-ln7dv2jxrk6lt1c4szylsrb41k3r|Hanji 142]] has Fan Zeng smash the jade dipper and rebuke Xiang Yu after Liu Bang's escape, deepening the commander-adviser rupture.
- Role-aware nonintervention: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1148-fazheng-zuowei-zuofu-zhugeliang-weishenme-buguan-ljdigbpovfw6rh6oy30drk1idks9|Hanji 1148]] interprets Zhuge Liang's refusal to restrain Fa Zheng directly through Liu Bei's personnel authority and the needs of early Yizhou consolidation.
- Moral boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1148-fazheng-zuowei-zuofu-zhugeliang-weishenme-buguan-ljdigbpovfw6rh6oy30drk1idks9|Hanji 1148]] alleges imprisonment and persecution of Fa Zheng's former enemies, showing that institutional restraint can coexist with uncorrected abuse.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept rests on two highly different popular-history cases. Hanji 141-142 do not establish that Fan Zeng lacked every form of delegated authority, and their judgments about Xiang Yu's face, legitimacy needs, and the banquet's alternative paths remain interpretive. Hanji 1148 does not establish the exact office boundaries, whether Zhuge Liang privately remonstrated, the scale or legality of Fa Zheng's acts, or whether nonintervention was necessary for consolidation. A role boundary can prevent unilateral overreach, but it can also become a rationalization for silence toward protected wrongdoing. The concept therefore requires separate judgment about lawful channels, urgency, reversibility, victim protection, and the ruler's capacity to hear correction.
