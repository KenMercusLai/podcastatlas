# Strategic Access Without Annexation

## Definition

Strategic access without annexation is the use of agreements, leases, investment, trade, infrastructure, or allied basing rights to secure resources, routes, warning systems, or military presence without acquiring sovereignty over inhabited territory.

## Current Synthesis

[[how-to-get-what-greenland-has-with-permission]] separates mineral processing, Arctic defense, and territorial ownership. It argues that the [[UnitedStates]] can pursue rare-earth resilience and military access through partners, existing agreements, and Greenlandic consent, while ownership rhetoric damages the cooperation on which those goals depend.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-graham-allison-on-the-global-realignment-iran-china-israel-greenland-40373425]] independently reaches the same operational conclusion. [[GrahamAllison]] says U.S. missile-defense and Arctic requirements can be met through long leases or a framework agreement, making invasion or ownership unnecessary. Together, the sources turn permission into strategy rather than treating it as a moral constraint added after strategic analysis.

## Key Claims

- Strategic need for a place does not establish a need for territorial ownership.
- Existing defense agreements and long leases can provide durable military access while preserving sovereignty.
- Mineral security depends on processing, infrastructure, capital, and partner networks, not merely control of geology.
- Threatening annexation can weaken the allied trust and local consent required for practical access.
- Local political agency remains part of strategic feasibility, not an obstacle that can be abstracted away.

## Evidence

### Permission-based defense access

- [[how-to-get-what-greenland-has-with-permission]] says U.S. defense access in Greenland already rests on longstanding agreements.
- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-graham-allison-on-the-global-realignment-iran-china-israel-greenland-40373425]] identifies long leases and a negotiated framework as sufficient tools for U.S. missile-defense needs.

### Resource access without ownership

- [[how-to-get-what-greenland-has-with-permission]] distinguishes geological deposits from processing capacity and identifies nearer-term partner alternatives.

### Consent and alliance effects

- [[how-to-get-what-greenland-has-with-permission]] centers Greenlandic agency and warns that control rhetoric makes cooperation harder.
- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-graham-allison-on-the-global-realignment-iran-china-israel-greenland-40373425]] argues that treating allies or their territory as adversarial targets is counterproductive during competition with China.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Leases and agreements can still create unequal bargaining, de facto control, or sovereignty disputes; permission is not automatically legitimate because a document exists. Neither source establishes the exact terms, durability, or local acceptability of a future framework. Mineral and defense problems also have different timelines and institutions, so success in one access channel does not solve the other.
