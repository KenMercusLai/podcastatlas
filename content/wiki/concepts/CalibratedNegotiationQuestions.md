# Calibrated Negotiation Questions

## Definition
Calibrated negotiation questions are open "how" and "what" prompts designed to slow reaction, surface constraints, and make a counterpart explain the practical path from demand or promise to implementation.

## Current Synthesis
[[ChrisVoss]] uses calibrated questions in [[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] as thinking tests rather than disguised speeches. "How am I supposed to do that?" shifts an aggressive demand back toward feasibility; "How do I know you're going to follow through?" asks the counterpart to build a credible future picture. The diagnostic value lies not only in the answer but in whether the person reflects, explains, and adapts or simply fires back.

## Key Claims
- "How" and "what" prompts can reduce direct confrontation while keeping a difficult issue active.
- A useful question makes the counterpart perform cognitive work instead of merely defend a position.
- Implementation detail can distinguish a considered commitment from a hollow promise.
- Response style is evidence: reflection and specificity differ from reflexive aggression or evasion.
- Repeated calibrated questions can slow a cutthroat exchange, but they do not neutralize coercion or danger by themselves.

## Evidence
- Aggressive demands - [[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] presents calibrated questions as a way to slow and wear down a forceful counterpart without frontal refusal.
- Follow-through - [[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] uses an implementation question to test whether promised compliance has a concrete mental model.
- Diagnostic response - [[how-to-succeed-at-hard-conversations-chris-voss-scim5142864264]] says the quality of thought behind the response can matter more than the literal answer.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Question forms can sound manipulative, passive-aggressive, or obstructive when tone, power, and context are ignored. They are not substitutes for explicit refusal, documentation, professional advice, or emergency action where safety or legal duties require a direct response.
