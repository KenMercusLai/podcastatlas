# Durand Line

## Definition
The Durand Line is the boundary drawn in 1893 between Afghanistan and British India that later became the principal border between [[Afghanistan]] and [[Pakistan]].

## Current Synthesis
[[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] presents the line as an imperial border-making settlement that divided Pashtun populations. It outlasted British India but did not produce an uncontested political boundary.

The episode connects the line's legacy to Afghan politics into the twentieth century and to later Pakistani influence in Afghanistan. In this account, the border is not only a map line: it links colonial strategy, transborder identity, state sovereignty, and the depth from which armed movements can draw support.

## Key Claims
- The line was created within a British imperial security framework.
- It divided rather than neatly bounded Pashtun communities.
- The end of British India did not settle Afghan acceptance of the border.
- Border contestation shaped Afghanistan-Pakistan relations after 1947.
- Transborder social and political networks complicate state control on both sides.

## Evidence
- Colonial origin: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] dates the line to 1893 and places it between Afghanistan and British India.
- Divided population: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] emphasizes the partition of Pashtun communities.
- Later consequence: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] connects the unresolved border issue to Afghan politics and Pakistani intervention.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives only a short account of the agreement and its afterlife. It does not reconstruct the negotiations, map the boundary in detail, represent all communities along it, or distinguish every phase of Afghan and Pakistani policy.
