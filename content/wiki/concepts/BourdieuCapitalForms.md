# Bourdieu Capital Forms / 布迪厄资本形式

Bourdieu capital forms are [[162-caifu-de-benzhi-yiji-ziyou-de-zhenzheng-hanyi-chuantai-shifen-xiyin-lofyxfhiprud7zvekufexsjhtust]]'s sociological expansion of wealth through [[PierreBourdieu|Pierre Bourdieu]]. The source uses economic capital, cultural capital, social capital, and symbolic capital to show why wealth is not only the money or assets someone owns, but also the recognized position, networks, tastes, credentials, and credibility that make resources convertible.

Economic capital covers money, assets, and property rights. Cultural capital covers education, taste, speech, aesthetic habit, embodied manners, books, art, and institutional credentials. Social capital covers durable relationships that can mobilize trust or support. Symbolic capital covers honor, reputation, authority, prestige, and legitimacy.

## Key Claims
- Economic capital is powerful but not sufficient; using money directly to buy recognition can be inefficient or socially discrediting.
- Cultural capital can make wealth look legitimate by embedding it in taste, education, manners, and institutional recognition.
- Social capital is not merely knowing people; it is a network of mutual recognition and mobilizable trust.
- Symbolic capital raises conversion efficiency because reputation and authority make other forms of capital more acceptable.
- The source uses the framework to explain both legitimate [[WealthAsCapabilityAndRights|wealth capacity]] and fragile performance, especially in [[AnnaSorokin|Anna Sorokin]]'s case.
