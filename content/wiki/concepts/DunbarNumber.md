# Dunbar Number / 邓巴数

Dunbar Number / 邓巴数 is the approximate limit on the number of stable familiar relationships a person can maintain, presented in [[83-shumao-bagua-ji-yuyan-de-jinhua-renji-guanxi-dengbashu-zan-bu-fu-bu-xing-698132861]] as about 150. The episode links the number to [[RobinDunbar|Robin Dunbar / 罗宾·邓巴]]'s comparison between primate group size and neocortex capacity, then uses it to explain why human social life needs scalable maintenance tools.

The source does not treat 150 as a magic rule. Its value is as a frame for relationship bandwidth: as groups grow, a person has to track more alliances, reputations, obligations, and third-party ties. [[LanguageAsSocialGrooming]] then becomes one answer to that scaling pressure.

## Key Claims
- The number is approximate and explanatory, not a strict cap on every person's relationships.
- Its importance lies in the relationship between group size, cognitive load, and maintenance cost.
- Larger social groups make [[MachiavellianIntelligence|third-party social tracking]] more important.
- Language, gossip, ritual, art, and media can be read as ways of stretching relationship-maintenance capacity.
- Modern urban life can fall below the social contact people need even when digital platforms create many weak or low-touch connections.
