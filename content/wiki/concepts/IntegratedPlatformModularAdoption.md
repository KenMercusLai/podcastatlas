# Integrated Platform, Modular Adoption

## Definition

Integrated platform, modular adoption is the strategy of keeping a shared technical architecture across many workflows while letting customers buy, deploy, and prove value through one narrow capability or outcome at a time.

## Current Synthesis

The [[Revo]] case separates product vision from commercial packaging. A shared data, semantic, memory, workflow, and agent foundation may reduce silos and enable deeper cross-product behavior, but it does not make an all-at-once migration commercially acceptable. Mid-market buyers with embedded systems, users, and processes can value the integrated destination while refusing the disruption required to reach it in one move. Modular entry points preserve the platform thesis while lowering initial switching cost and giving expansion a path through demonstrated value.

## Key Claims

- Technical integration and commercial bundling are separate design decisions.
- A broad platform can enter through one workflow without becoming a disconnected collection of point products.
- Outcome-oriented packages are easier to evaluate than an exhaustive menu of features and integrations.
- Incremental adoption reduces migration, training, procurement, and organizational risk for customers with embedded incumbents.
- Expansion remains credible only if the shared foundation creates cross-workflow value after the first module is adopted.
- Modular packaging is an experiment, not proof that platform depth, migration, or retention problems are solved.

## Evidence

Architecture-versus-packaging distinction:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] says Revo retained its system-of-record, platform, workflow, and agent-layer sequence while allowing customers to purchase individual capabilities.

Buyer-risk boundary:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] reports that 200-300 prospects largely rejected replacing an entire Salesforce-centered stack, even when many liked the integrated vision.

Outcome-oriented entry:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] describes three “golden workflows” around qualified pipeline, pipeline conversion, and team or management performance, with customers able to begin from one priority.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Modular adoption can recreate the fragmentation the integrated architecture is meant to solve if modules do not share data, semantics, identity, and workflow behavior in practice. A narrow entry product may also fail to expand, leaving the vendor with point-solution economics despite platform costs. Revo's reported early traction and faster onboarding do not independently establish migration success, cross-sell, retention, or total-cost advantage.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Revo's separation of integrated product architecture and incremental commercial adoption.
