# Micro-Duration Insurance

## Definition
Micro-duration insurance is coverage activated for a short, specific exposure such as a trip or rental period, often offered as an immediate add-on rather than a long-running insurer relationship.

## Current Synthesis
Short duration changes what the buyer values. Convenience, price, and immediate fit can matter more than knowing the carrier, while long-duration life or auto relationships place more weight on institutional trust and future claims capacity. AI may accelerate execution, but duration alone does not prove customer value or insurer profitability.

## Key Claims
- Coverage lasting days creates a different trust and relationship problem from promises lasting decades.
- Short-term policies can become commoditized add-ons whose carrier identity is secondary to convenience.
- On-demand activation can align coverage with a narrow exposure and reduce the need for a persistent relationship.
- Faster execution does not establish that the product is fairly priced, useful, or profitable.
- Contract clarity and claims delivery remain necessary even when purchase is nearly frictionless.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode gives examples and strategic interpretation, not adoption, claims, retention, or profitability data.
- A short contract can still involve material exclusions, claims friction, licensing, and counterparty risk.
- The source does not establish that AI is required for micro-duration products or that commoditization benefits buyers.
