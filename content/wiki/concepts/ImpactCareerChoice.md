# Impact Career Choice

Impact career choice is the attempt to compare careers by how much good they can do. [[brave-new-whirl-turkeys-opposition-overhaul-6a69c7766043035f89eb097f]] presents it through [[BenjaminTodd]]'s [[EightyThousandHours|80,000 Hours]], which draws on [[EffectiveAltruism]] and discusses fields such as pandemic prevention and AI regulation.

The source treats the frame sympathetically but cautiously. Maximizing global good is admirable, yet the reviewer warns that the approach can become demoralizing if every career step is measured against an overwhelming moral scoreboard. The episode's final balance is to build useful skills, help others, and avoid the passion trap without demanding perfect impact certainty.

## Key Claims
- Career choice can be evaluated by social usefulness, not only personal interest or prestige.
- High-impact fields may include prevention, safety, regulation, and other work whose value is indirect.
- Impact framing becomes brittle when it turns exploration into guilt or impossible optimization.
- Useful work and patient skill-building can preserve agency inside an impact-oriented career search.
