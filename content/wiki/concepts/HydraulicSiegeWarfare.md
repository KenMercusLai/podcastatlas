# Hydraulic Siege Warfare / 水攻围城

## Definition
Hydraulic siege warfare / 水攻围城 is the siege pattern in which an attacker turns rivers, canals, moats, or surrounding water infrastructure into the mechanism of wall failure, supply isolation, morale collapse, or communication control.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now spans Warring States and late-Han cases. Qinji 117-1 supplies the state-destruction version: [[WangBenQin|王贲]] uses the water environment around [[Daliang|大梁]] to break [[WeiState|魏国]]'s capital defense and force [[WeiWangJia|魏王假]] to surrender. Hanji 1025 supplies the Xiapi version: [[CaoCao|曹操]] diverts the Si and Yi rivers against [[Xiapi|下邳]], adding water pressure to [[LyuBu|吕布]]'s failing morale and internal mistrust.

Hanji 1062 adds a more communication-centered variant at [[YeLateHan|邺城]]. Cao Cao abandons earth hills and tunnels, digs a moat, deepens it overnight after its first shallow appearance is dismissed by [[ShenPeiLateHan|审配]], and diverts Zhang River water into the trench. The effect is not only physical pressure on walls. It cuts Ye from food, news, and ordinary contact with [[YuanShang|袁尚]], making [[LiFuLateHan|李孚]]'s disguised entry necessary.

## Key Claims
- Water can shift a siege from assault to engineered isolation or progressive structural pressure.
- Hydraulic attack works differently by setting: Daliang emphasizes wall collapse, Xiapi combines flood pressure with internal defection, and Ye emphasizes moat-backed isolation and communication failure.
- The tactic depends on control over surrounding terrain and labor, not only on tactical courage.
- Deceptive sequencing can matter: at Ye, the shallow-looking moat lowers defensive urgency before the overnight deepening.
- Hydraulic siege can create secondary information problems because the city and relief force cannot coordinate normally.
- Water pressure rarely acts alone; it combines with morale, supply, internal mistrust, or messenger-risk conditions.

## Evidence
- Daliang state-destruction case: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-1-papa-dalian-lishishang-shuangwen-de-kaishan-bizu-lr0h-kfeguvatattnj-mz8fgvnc|Qinji 117-1]] says Wang Ben diverts Yellow River water toward Daliang, damages the city wall after three months, and forces Wei Wang Jia's surrender.
- Xiapi morale-and-defection case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1025-bai-yu-caocao-lvbu-cuo-zai-jinjiuling-ljjt1t2mk77si6re-wew-hpl8qz|Hanji 1025]] says Cao Cao floods Xiapi with the Si and Yi rivers after Xun You and Guo Jia argue that Lü Bu's morale and planning window are weak.
- Ye moat-sequencing case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1062-shanzei-de-nixi-daozei-chushen-de-ta-shi-ruhe-zhandui-de-lvkhmezd68key-p-kup55zypbjet|Hanji 1062]] says Cao Cao lets the moat initially appear shallow, then deepens it overnight while Shen Pei does not disrupt the work.
- Ye isolation case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1062-shanzei-de-nixi-daozei-chushen-de-ta-shi-ruhe-zhandui-de-lvkhmezd68key-p-kup55zypbjet|Hanji 1062]] says Cao Cao diverts Zhang River water into the moat, cutting Ye off from supply and outside communication.
- Communication side effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1062-shanzei-de-nixi-daozei-chushen-de-ta-shi-ruhe-zhandui-de-lvkhmezd68key-p-kup55zypbjet|Hanji 1062]] makes Li Fu's authority-disguise entry a response to the information isolation created by the water perimeter.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Hydraulic siege is not a universal explanation for victory. In Xiapi, the water attack matters alongside Lü Bu's failed alliances, morale strain, suspicion, and subordinate betrayal. At Ye, Hanji 1062 does not yet narrate the city's fall; it shows isolation and a messenger breakthrough inside an ongoing campaign. Daliang, Xiapi, and Ye differ in scale, water sources, and political outcome, so the concept should preserve case-specific mechanics rather than flatten them into one technique.
