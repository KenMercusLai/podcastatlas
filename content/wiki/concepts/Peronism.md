## Definition

Peronism is the Argentine political movement organized around [[JuanPeron|Juan Perón]], labor and popular identification, and the durable symbolic power of [[EvaPeron|Eva Perón]], while containing rival ideological and armed factions.

## Current Synthesis

The source set presents Peronism as both organization and emotional regime. In 1951-52, unions, crowds, women's mobilization, religious imagery, and Evita's visible suffering converted political loyalty into embodied devotion. That emotional intensity strengthened Perón before the election and turned Evita's illness, renunciation, and death into public tests of belonging.

The movement later proved more resilient than any one institution or leader. It survived overthrow, legal proscription, exile, and suppression of its symbols through unions, personal networks, ritual, forbidden names, and Evita's memory. Durability did not mean coherence: by Perón's return, left-wing [[Montoneros]] and right-wing forces associated with [[JoseLopezRega|José López Rega]] invoked the same movement while fighting one another.

## Key Claims

- Peronism joined formal organization to emotional, bodily, and symbolic identification.
- Labor mobilization gave the movement mass power but did not override military veto capacity.
- Evita's illness and martyr image made sacrifice a form of Peronist legitimacy.
- Official mourning turned grief into a public measure of political belonging.
- Proscription suppressed expression without eliminating movement loyalty.
- Peronism's breadth supported survival while enabling violent internal conflict.

## Evidence

- Mass and emotional organization: [[497-evita-death-of-a-martyr-part-4-glt5046116399]] links the CGT, Cabildo Abierto, devotional practices, rallies, and Evita's illness to Peronist legitimacy.
- Martyrdom and mourning: [[497-evita-death-of-a-martyr-part-4-glt5046116399]] describes sacrificial rhetoric, compulsory public ritual, and the plan to preserve Evita's body.
- Survival under prohibition: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] describes bans on names, symbols, and organizations after 1955 alongside continuing underground loyalty.
- Symbolic persistence and factional rupture: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] follows Evita imagery, Ezeiza, Montoneros, López Rega, and the AAA.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

These episodes emphasize charisma, symbolism, leadership, exile, and violence more than labor institutions, social policy, economic doctrine, or Peronism after the 1970s. The sources also do not establish that all participants shared one ideology or experienced public mourning in the same way. Peronism should not be reduced to either a unified doctrine or a synonym for all Argentine politics.

## What Changed

- Added the pre-1952 mechanism connecting labor mobilization, Evita's visible illness, religious devotion, and electoral legitimacy.
- Distinguished popular emotional attachment from the coercive public performance of loyalty.
