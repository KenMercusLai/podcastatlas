## Definition

Peronism is the Argentine political movement organized around [[JuanPeron|Juan Perón]], labor and popular identification, and the durable symbolic power of [[EvaPeron|Eva Perón]], while containing rival ideological and armed factions.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents Peronism as more resilient than any one institution or leader. The movement survived Perón's overthrow, legal proscription, exile, and the suppression of its symbols because loyalty could be carried through unions, personal networks, ritual, forbidden names, and Evita's memory. That resilience did not guarantee coherence: by Perón's return, left-wing [[Montoneros]] and right-wing forces associated with [[JoseLopezRega|José López Rega]] could invoke the same movement while fighting one another.

## Key Claims

- Peronism joined formal political organization to emotional and symbolic identification.
- Proscription suppressed public expression without eliminating movement loyalty.
- Evita's posthumous cult helped preserve a portable Peronist legitimacy.
- Exile made intermediaries and household access unusually important to movement politics.
- Electoral return did not resolve left-right factional incompatibility.
- Peronism's breadth was a source of both durability and violent internal conflict.

## Evidence

- Emotional organization: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] argues that Evita performed political and emotional work Juan Perón could not easily replace.
- Survival under prohibition: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] describes bans on names, symbols, and organizations after 1955 alongside continuing underground loyalty.
- Symbolic persistence: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] follows monthly remembrance, Evita imagery, radical slogans, and demands for the body's return.
- Factional rupture: [[498-evita-the-mystery-of-the-missing-body-part-5-glt5587823308]] uses Ezeiza, Montoneros, López Rega, and the AAA to show incompatible left and right currents inside the returning movement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is derived from the fifth episode of an Evita series and therefore emphasizes symbolism, leadership, exile, and violence more than labor institutions, social policy, ideology, or Peronism after the 1970s. It should not be reduced to either a unified doctrine or a synonym for all Argentine politics.
