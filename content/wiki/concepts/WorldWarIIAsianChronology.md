# World War II Asian Chronology

## Definition

World War II Asian chronology is the historiographical problem of choosing and explaining the war's starting point when Japan's 1931 seizure of Manchuria, the sustained China-Japan war from 1937, the European war from 1939, and the Pacific war from 1941 form connected but non-identical beginnings.

## Current Synthesis

[[235-china-and-world-war-ii-part-1-glt2114682484]] makes the strongest case for 7 July 1937. The [[MarcoPoloBridgeIncident]] escalated into continuous large-scale war between China and Japan, followed by the [[BattleOfShanghai1937]], the fall of Nanjing, inland Chinese resistance, and eventual merger with the wider Allied war. On this account, Asian conflict is constitutive of the Second World War rather than a peripheral prelude.

The source nevertheless preserves rival chronologies. China officially emphasizes 1931 and Japanese left-wing historians have described a fifteen-year war, but the 1933 Tanggu Truce created a real if unstable interval and Chinese contemporaries in 1945 commonly described an eight-year resistance. European and American dates remain meaningful for the entry of different powers, not neutral measures of when the whole global conflict began.

## Key Claims

- A global chronology should include sustained Asian interstate war rather than begin automatically from European or American entry.
- The 1937 date is grounded in continuity from local escalation to full-scale war and later global coalition conflict.
- The 1931 date captures Japanese imperial seizure and China's longer resistance memory but crosses an unstable truce period.
- The 1939 and 1941 dates identify decisive geographic expansions rather than uniquely uncontested beginnings.
- Start dates organize responsibility, national memory, theatre centrality, and which populations appear foundational to the war.

## Evidence

- Sustained-war criterion: [[235-china-and-world-war-ii-part-1-glt2114682484]] connects the July 1937 crisis directly to Beijing's fall, Shanghai, and continuing national war.
- Alternative chronology: [[235-china-and-world-war-ii-part-1-glt2114682484]] records China's official 1931 position, the fifteen-year-war formulation, and the conventional 1939 and 1941 dates.
- Contemporary self-description: [[235-china-and-world-war-ii-part-1-glt2114682484]] uses the postwar Chinese description of an eight-year resistance to support 1937.
- Discontinuity qualification: [[235-china-and-world-war-ii-part-1-glt2114682484]] treats the Tanggu Truce as evidence against a seamless 1931-to-1945 war narrative.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The preferred date depends on the criterion being measured: imperial aggression, continuous large-scale war, European general war, or American-Pacific entry. The episode advances an interpretive synthesis rather than proving that one date should replace all others, and it does not erase earlier Japanese violence or later thresholds of globalization.

## What Changed

- Established a comparative framework for 1931, 1937, 1939, and 1941.
- Centered continuity, geographic scope, and memory politics as distinct criteria for wartime onset.
