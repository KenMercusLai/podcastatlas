# GLP-1 Consumer-Demand Spillover

## Definition
GLP-1 consumer-demand spillover is the downstream change in food, restaurant, snack, alcohol, serving-size, and spending behavior associated with wider use of appetite-regulating medicines.

## Current Synthesis
The episode extends [[GLP1Agonists|GLP-1 agonists]] from clinical and weight-management contexts into consumer business. It reports preferences for smaller meals and low- or no-alcohol beer, possible reductions in visit frequency and spend, and brand responses through smaller menu and package formats. Medication effects interact with inflation and cannot be cleanly inferred from one household comparison.

## Key Claims
- Appetite reduction can change portion and occasion demand beyond the pharmaceutical market.
- Restaurants face both lower ticket risk and an opportunity to redesign smaller, appropriately priced meals.
- Brewers can respond through serving size and alcohol-content portfolios.
- Smaller snacks may replace some full meals without necessarily reducing every food category equally.
- Inflation, income, treatment selection, adherence, and geography confound simple medication-to-spending claims.

## Evidence
### Hospitality and household spending
- [[dafeixin-guonei-hangsi-tiaozheng-hangxian-buju-jianfeiyao-liuxing-huo-yingxiang-pijiuye-gkwriaiotragi7matv3jls]] reports Heineken's customer guidance and a cited two-year U.S. household spending comparison.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source does not describe the household study's design, matching, attrition, drug indication, dosage, or category definitions.
- Reduced appetite does not establish permanent lower spending, and smaller formats may change price per unit or purchasing frequency.
- Clinical benefits and risks belong to medical evidence; this page addresses only the episode's business-demand claim.
