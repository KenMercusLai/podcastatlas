# VIE Employee Equity Mismatch / VIE 员工股权主体错位

VIE employee equity mismatch is the problem that appears when employee equity is granted by an offshore listing or holding company while the employee's labor contract is with a domestic operating entity. [[qiquan-zhe-zhang-bing-weishenme-yuelaiyue-nanchi-le-1]] makes this the legal and governance core of the source-described "某书" dispute.

The source argues that a company may be tempted to say the offshore grantor and domestic employer are unrelated when fighting an employee claim. That defense can create a larger problem: the company's [[VariableInterestEntity|VIE]] story depends on regulators, investors, employees, and other stakeholders believing those entities are practically connected through agreements and control.

## Key Claims
- The mismatch is structural: [[EmployeeStockOptions]] are often issued by the entity that might list, while employment paperwork belongs to a lower domestic company.
- Formal separation can be used opportunistically if the company wants to resist treating options as employee benefits.
- A court or regulator may look through pure form if the operating reality shows the entities worked as one business system.
- The same denial that helps in a labor dispute can damage [[PublicListingControlTradeoff|listing credibility]] because investors also depend on the claimed connection.
- The problem turns employee compensation into [[StartupGovernance]] because entity design, option plans, and litigation strategy interact.
