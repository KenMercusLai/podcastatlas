## Definition
The Polish Film School is a postwar film movement described here as a plural response to occupation, national history, political thaw, and dissatisfaction with a single Soviet-style socialist-realist model.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded source cautions against defining the school by one visual style. Its common ground was institutional and negative as much as aesthetic: filmmakers educated after wartime destruction gained room during political thaw to revisit national history and use modern cinematic language, yet developed markedly different approaches. Schools, studios, creative groups, festivals, and international circulation helped convert this varied production into a recognized movement.

## Key Claims
- Political thaw expanded the subjects and film language available after the Stalinist period.
- Shared rejection of one propaganda model did not produce a single positive aesthetic program.
- Wartime destruction and state-led rebuilding made the movement inseparable from postwar institutions.
- International festival selection helped establish the label abroad while excluding work that did not circulate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One podcast summary cannot settle the movement's membership, dates, style, or relation to neighboring national cinemas. A negative definition can clarify plurality but may understate shared themes or craft practices. International recognition is evidence of circulation, not a neutral measure of all domestic achievement.
