## Definition

中国传统学徒制 / traditional Chinese apprenticeship is the craft and occupational training relation in which a master transmits skills while the apprentice supplies labor, observes household rules, shows ritual deference, and often remains tied to the master after formal training.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-405-2-weihe-shuo-yiriweishi-zhongshenweifu-lsrigo7tzkp-ugt1mtbrud4etmko|Hanji 405-2]] presents apprenticeship as a mixed institution: education, family-like obligation, labor discipline, and industry gatekeeping are hard to separate. The apprentice learns a scarce craft, but also enters a hierarchy shaped by gifts, food, lodging, service, punishment, meal etiquette, and the master's power over future access.

The source gives a broad historical arc. Early craft knowledge is framed as family-preserved, later broadened into private master-apprentice agreements, official craft training after Qin-Han, Tang regulations for government artisans, and later guild-linked mechanisms under expanding commerce. By the modern industrial period, the episode says apprentices were increasingly turned into cheap labor as capitalist production challenged older guild arrangements.

The current judgment is deliberately ambivalent. Traditional apprenticeship solved real transmission problems in a world without mass schooling, but its personal and household dependence could expose apprentices to exploitation and make learning inseparable from obedience.

## Key Claims

- Apprenticeship preserved scarce craft knowledge before mass education and open technical documentation.
- The master-apprentice bond combined instruction, labor, lodging, food, etiquette, and long-term social obligation.
- Skill transmission often began inside families before expanding into private and official apprenticeship forms.
- Official craft training and later guild mechanisms made apprenticeship part of broader production and industry governance.
- Apprentice dependence could turn education into cheap labor, especially when custom or law treated the apprentice like a servant.
- The institution's value and harm come from the same structure: close personal transmission gives access, while close personal power creates vulnerability.

## Evidence

- Craft scarcity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-405-2-weihe-shuo-yiriweishi-zhongshenweifu-lsrigo7tzkp-ugt1mtbrud4etmko|Hanji 405-2]] says technical skills were precious and often protected from external transmission.
- Household dependence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-405-2-weihe-shuo-yiriweishi-zhongshenweifu-lsrigo7tzkp-ugt1mtbrud4etmko|Hanji 405-2]] describes apprentices living with teachers, doing household work, and receiving meals from the master.
- Ongoing obligation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-405-2-weihe-shuo-yiriweishi-zhongshenweifu-lsrigo7tzkp-ugt1mtbrud4etmko|Hanji 405-2]] says apprentices continued seasonal visits and could remain tied through job introductions and commissions.
- Institutional arc: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-405-2-weihe-shuo-yiriweishi-zhongshenweifu-lsrigo7tzkp-ugt1mtbrud4etmko|Hanji 405-2]] describes movement from family craft inheritance into folk apprenticeship, official craft training, Tang regulation, and guild-linked education.
- Servant-like discipline: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-405-2-weihe-shuo-yiriweishi-zhongshenweifu-lsrigo7tzkp-ugt1mtbrud4etmko|Hanji 405-2]] cites "学三年帮一年," beating, meal hierarchy, and the "三年奴隶" saying.
- Modern decline: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-405-2-weihe-shuo-yiriweishi-zhongshenweifu-lsrigo7tzkp-ugt1mtbrud4etmko|Hanji 405-2]] links the decline of traditional apprenticeship to industrial capital and the transformation of apprentices into low-cost labor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source compresses a long and regionally varied history into a short public-history narrative. Its Ming-Qing legal examples, Tang official-craft references, folk apprenticeship customs, and modern industrial critique should remain source-scoped until more specialized sources refine the chronology. The concept also should not imply that all apprenticeships were abusive or that family craft transmission always blocked outsiders; the current synthesis only preserves this episode's broad institutional contrast.
