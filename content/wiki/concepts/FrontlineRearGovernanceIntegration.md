## Definition

[[FrontlineRearGovernanceIntegration|前线作战与后方治理一体化]] is the principle that sustained campaigning depends on treating recruitment, supply, local administration, and political stability as parts of the same operational system.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1162 illustrates the mechanism through the Hanzhong campaign. Liu Bei's battlefield losses create a reinforcement and supply problem; Zhuge Liang worries that extraction may destabilize Yi; Yang Hong answers by reframing Hanzhong as protection for Yi itself and then administers the rear while troops and grain move forward. The case therefore does not reduce logistics to transport: it includes the governing capacity that makes extraction politically sustainable.

## Key Claims

- Tactical persistence is bounded by replacement manpower and food supply.
- Rear-area stability is an operational variable, not merely a postwar concern.
- Strategic framing can align local sacrifice with regional survival.
- Personnel selection links mobilization orders to credible implementation.

Rear-area constraint and response:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1162-jinyi-zhisi-xiang-ying-jiu-bie-xinji-lpjdh6wohap6bqbghnvltu2oey5i|Hanji 1162]] presents Zhuge Liang's stability concern, Yang Hong's strategic argument, and the combined mobilization-administration response.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One successful episode-attributed case does not show that maximum mobilization is always justified. Excess extraction can weaken the base it is meant to protect, and the summary does not quantify troop, grain, tax, or public-order costs. The principle requires balancing front-line need with the rear's regenerative capacity.
