# Headline Growth–Voter Experience Gap

## Definition
The headline growth–voter experience gap is the divergence between strong aggregate economic indicators and weak public satisfaction when gains are diluted, unevenly distributed, or overwhelmed by visible pressures such as housing scarcity and service failure.

## Current Synthesis
The Spain segment shows why fast GDP growth, wage policy, and lower unemployment may fail as an incumbent's electoral defense. When much of expansion comes from a larger labor force, housing construction lags household growth, and migration, transport safety, and corruption dominate daily political attention, voters may experience scarcity and institutional strain rather than the aggregate success described by national statistics.

## Key Claims
- Aggregate output growth does not establish rising per-person welfare or broad satisfaction.
- Labor-force expansion can lift total growth while diluting the gain felt by existing households.
- Housing scarcity can convert population and employment growth into rent, purchase, and crowding pressure.
- Visible service failures and integrity concerns can outweigh favorable macroeconomic messaging.
- Electoral interpretation requires both national indicators and household-level experience.

## Evidence
- Macro strength: [[left-in-the-lurch-spains-general-election-f9a9608c40ea7371c98aaab648d8d8b0]] says Spain grew at more than twice the euro-area average while minimum wages rose and unemployment fell.
- Dilution and housing: [[left-in-the-lurch-spains-general-election-f9a9608c40ea7371c98aaab648d8d8b0]] attributes much growth to a larger labor force and says insufficient homebuilding produced a serious shortage.
- Competing lived pressures: [[left-in-the-lurch-spains-general-election-f9a9608c40ea7371c98aaab648d8d8b0]] adds Ceuta migration, deadly high-speed-train crashes, and corruption cases involving Sanchez aides.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One election segment cannot identify how individual voters weight growth, wages, unemployment, housing, migration, safety, or corruption. The causal interpretation and all economic and polling comparisons are source-dated; strong aggregate performance may still benefit many households even when it does not secure incumbent support.

## What Changed
- Established a Spain-specific case separating aggregate economic success from household and electoral experience.
- Connected labor-force-led growth to housing and public-service capacity rather than treating GDP as a sufficient welfare measure.
