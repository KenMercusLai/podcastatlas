# Poetic Causality / 诗性因果

## Definition
Poetic causality is the use of causal or logical connectives to join images, moods, memories, and historical pressures that do not form strict proof but become meaningful through poetic adjacency.

## Current Synthesis
[[episode-228-suoyi-aodesai-8211241331-711602]] defines the current case through [[BecauseSong|"Because"]] and [[SuoyiSong|《所以》]]. Both songs take highly logical words and make them fail as logic while succeeding as poetry. "Because" joins world, wind, sky, and feeling; "所以" keeps moving through sea level, satellites, GPT, AI, data, chips, capital, war, truth, home, and affection without arriving at one settled conclusion.

The concept matters because it lets the episode connect song form to ethics. "所以" sounds like an answer, but the source reads it as a continuation marker: things have reached this point, and the next question is what follows.

## Key Claims
- Logical connectives can carry poetic force even when they do not prove a proposition.
- "Because" in the source connects given world conditions to emotion without requiring deductive validity.
- "所以" in the source points both backward to cause and forward to consequence.
- Repetition can turn causality from explanation into movement through a crowded world.
- Poetic causality becomes ethically charged when the connector asks what follows after irreversible action.

## Evidence
- Beatles comparison: [[episode-228-suoyi-aodesai-8211241331-711602]] argues that "Because" is not really a song asking for reasons.
- Luo Dayou comparison: [[episode-228-suoyi-aodesai-8211241331-711602]] says 《所以》 contains many "所以" turns, but they do not add up to formal derivation.
- Double direction: [[episode-228-suoyi-aodesai-8211241331-711602]] notes that "所以" can point to both result and ground.
- Ethical turn: [[episode-228-suoyi-aodesai-8211241331-711602]] reads "所以" as "things have arrived here; then what?"

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded in one interpretive episode and should not be treated as a general theory of all lyric causality. It also does not prove direct influence beyond the source's report that Luo Dayou presented 《所以》 as a tribute to "Because."
