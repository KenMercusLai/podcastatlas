# Reverse Transcription

## Definition
Reverse transcription is the enzyme-mediated copying of genetic information from RNA into DNA, allowing a retrovirus's information to become durably associated with an infected cell.

## Current Synthesis
In [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club|The Obscure Virus Club]], reverse transcription resolves [[HowardTemin]]'s central puzzle: an RNA tumor virus appeared able to impose lasting changes on a cell even though the period's prevailing account emphasized information flow from DNA to RNA to protein. Temin and [[DavidBaltimore]] independently detected reverse transcriptase in different tumor-virus systems in 1970. The discovery made retroviral causation mechanistically legible and later gave AIDS researchers a known viral class, assay target, and treatment target.

## Key Claims
- Reverse transcription copies RNA information into DNA rather than following the ordinary DNA-to-RNA direction.
- A DNA intermediate explains how an RNA tumor virus can produce stable cellular transformation.
- Independent detection in different tumor-virus systems strengthened the mechanism beyond a single laboratory or model.
- The discovery converted a disputed theoretical inference into an experimentally detectable enzyme activity.
- Knowledge of the mechanism later helped researchers recognize and target HIV as a retrovirus.

## Evidence
- Stable-transformation puzzle: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] reports Temin's inference that Rous sarcoma virus must move information from RNA into DNA.
- Independent detection: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] places Temin's Rous-sarcoma-virus experiment and Baltimore's mouse-leukemia-virus experiment in 1970.
- Virus-class consequence: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] says the enzyme and the retrovirus category made earlier tumor-virus observations coherent.
- HIV relevance: [[the-obscure-virus-club-the-obscure-virus-club]] presents reverse-transcriptase knowledge as one part of the scientific head start against AIDS.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode simplifies the historical central dogma and does not provide biochemical methods, primary papers, or a complete account of independent contributors. Reverse transcription alone did not identify HIV, establish every transmission route, produce a diagnostic test, or create effective therapy; those outcomes required additional virology, cell culture, epidemiology, clinical work, and drug development.

## What Changed
- Created a mechanism-level concept connecting tumor-virus anomalies to retroviral disease research.
