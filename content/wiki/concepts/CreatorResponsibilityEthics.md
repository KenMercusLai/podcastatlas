## Definition

Creator responsibility ethics asks what continuing duties arise when an agent intentionally brings a living or autonomous being into existence, especially when that being was made for an instrumental purpose and depends on a world the creator helped shape.

## Current Synthesis

[[Frankenstein|《弗兰肯斯坦》]] establishes the care-and-abandonment branch. Victor's failure is not technical creation alone but his refusal to recognize, educate, accompany, or take responsibility for the being he animates. The creature's later violence remains accountable, yet [[CreatureSocialExclusion]] matters because rejection, fear, appearance judgment, and isolation help produce the conditions in which harm develops. Responsibility is therefore distributed without becoming erased: creator, creation, and surrounding society can each bear different duties.

The exploitation-and-judgment branch appears in [[HairNiKuangNovel|《头发》]]. Its advanced civilization manufactures replacement bodies so existing minds can survive death. When those bodies develop autonomous consciousness and rebel, their makers exile them to Earth, disable their original capabilities, call them sinners, and later screen a minority for return. The creator's obligation now extends beyond care after birth to the legitimacy of the original purpose, treatment of autonomy, imposed punishment, and authority to judge traits that the creating system helped cause.

Together the sources reject a simple equation between superior knowledge and moral standing. Technical achievement creates asymmetric power and therefore stronger demands for recognition, repair, restraint, and justified governance. A creation's agency prevents creator responsibility from becoming total causal determinism, but the creation's later wrongdoing does not retroactively absolve abandonment, exploitation, exclusion, or collective punishment.

## Key Claims

- Making life or autonomous agency creates obligations that continue beyond technical success.
- A being created as an instrument can acquire claims to recognition that conflict with the maker's original design.
- Abandonment, exclusion, and imposed incapacity can help produce later harm without erasing the created being's agency.
- Technical superiority does not by itself authorize moral judgment, punishment, or selection for salvation.
- Creator responsibility includes the purpose of creation, conditions of development, response to autonomy, and willingness to repair caused harm.
- Responsibility can be distributed among maker, creation, and society without pretending their power or causal roles are equal.

## Evidence

### Abandonment and social formation

- [[141-zhizao-fulankensitan-kehuan-zhimu-he-tade-shenqi-shidai-857923931]] treats Victor's abandonment and the creature's education, loneliness, rejection, and denied companionship as central to the disaster.
- [[141-zhizao-fulankensitan-kehuan-zhimu-he-tade-shenqi-shidai-857923931]] connects bodily appearance and social fear to the production of monstrosity rather than assuming visible ugliness proves innate evil.

### Instrumental creation and autonomous rebellion

- [[ljbtvkfckw2lb-nykriz05lq35wz]] says the advanced civilization made bodies for consciousness transfer and immortality before those bodies developed independent minds and rebelled.
- [[ljbtvkfckw2lb-nykriz05lq35wz]] presents exile, disabled capacities, the “sinner” label, and selective return as judgments imposed by the makers on the beings their own project produced.

### Authority challenged from within the story

- [[ljbtvkfckw2lb-nykriz05lq35wz]] identifies Wesley's accusation that the creators manufactured the supposed sinners as the novel's sharpest ethical insight, while treating his later cooperation with them as an unresolved retreat.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both cases are literary thought experiments rather than real adjudications of artificial life, parenting, biotechnology, or machine agency. The Frankenstein account comes through a podcast using a secondary history of the novel's scientific context, while the 《头发》 account is a conversational retelling with informal theological and scientific criticism. Causal contribution is not sole responsibility: created beings can become agents, victims can cause harm, and surrounding societies can intensify exclusion. Nor does every act of making entail identical duties; capacity, dependence, foreseeability, control, consent, and the kind of created entity all matter.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept from post-creation abandonment to instrumental creation, autonomous rebellion, imposed incapacity, exile, and moral judgment.
- Distinguished causal responsibility from total control so creator and creation can remain accountable in different ways.
- Added technical superiority without moral legitimacy as the current cross-source judgment.
