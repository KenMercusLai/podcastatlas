# AI-Generated Advertising

## Definition
AI-generated advertising is the use of generative models to produce or transform public-facing brand imagery, video, audio, copy, characters, and campaign variants.

## Current Synthesis
AI can lower the cost and delay of producing ad variants, making generation useful in bounded testing and [[CreativeFatigue]] workflows. Acceptance depends less on generation alone than on who appears to author the work, what kind of product or message is involved, how the asset is reviewed, and whether the audience sees AI use as assistance, deception, cheapness, or displacement of creative labor. Disclosure is necessary in some settings but does not guarantee trust. At scale, the harder problem becomes governance: performance optimization must not silently rewrite brand identity, and nominal human approval must remain meaningful despite rising asset volume.

## Key Claims
- Human-led AI assistance can carry a smaller authenticity penalty than AI-led authorship followed by human editing.
- Disclosure helps audiences interpret synthetic media but cannot by itself produce acceptance, trust, or perceived fairness.
- Bounded, reviewed variants for ordinary campaign testing can be lower-risk than a fully synthetic flagship brand statement.
- AI advertising can create labor and ownership backlash even when its visual quality is convincing.
- Selective automation can generate new media or crop, select, and recombine existing human-produced brand libraries.
- Performance feedback needs [[BrandIdentityFeedbackIsolation]] so short-term optimization does not become uncontrolled brand drift.
- Human and automated quality checks reduce visible defects but do not establish authenticity or prevent deliberate fraud.

## Evidence
- Authorship and consumer response - [[tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports lower trust, authenticity, purchase intent, and word of mouth when AI appears to lead communication, with stronger concern for emotional and identity-linked products.
- Corporate backlash and disclosure - [[tech-20251212-1212-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251212-1212-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses the disclosed but withdrawn [[McDonaldsNetherlands]] Christmas ad to show that transparency does not settle labor, aesthetic, or trust objections.
- Bounded workflow use - [[tech-20260203-0203-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260203-0203-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes [[Clorox]] using generated variants for [[HiddenValleyRanch]] web advertising inside a narrower organizational workflow.
- Scale and brand governance - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-f627a6ffa1322b3e7fd3b466e8b10749-62499679-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-f627a6ffa1322b3e7fd3b466e8b10749-62499679]] adds selective automation, verifier and human review, creative-fatigue testing, and separation of campaign feedback from the brand hub.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide a common experimental design, audited cost comparison, or long-term brand-lift measure. Consumer response varies by product, generation role, audience, disclosure frame, and campaign prominence. A pulled flagship ad and a bounded web-variant workflow are different risk classes. The newest source's customer mix, asset counts, review process, and performance remain founder-reported.

## What Changed
- Added creative fatigue as a concrete demand driver for high-volume variant production.
- Added selective automation of existing human-made brand libraries as an alternative to full generation.
- Added feedback isolation as a guardrail against optimization-driven brand drift.
- Distinguished visible-defect review from fraud and authenticity verification.
