# Narco-State Risk

Narco-state risk is the danger that repeated drug flows corrupt enough officials, institutions, and enforcement systems that traffickers begin to shape state behavior. In [[taking-the-shine-off-albedo-and-global-temperatures-6a730fdaca067b295bab9760]], [[PauloSilva]] warns that cocaine reaching [[WestAfrica]] corrupts systems there and could create a problem for Europe if one of those states becomes a narco-state.

The source keeps the risk tied to institutions rather than moral panic. [[GregoryColeman]] says public trust in [[Liberia]] is low because officers and state actors have been identified or charged, while [[OriOgunbiyi]] says low pay and weak technology make officials easy to bribe.

## Key Claims
- Drug-route geography becomes a state-capacity problem when money can buy officials or protection.
- Major seizures can coexist with deeper institutional vulnerability.
- European demand can externalize corruption pressure into transit countries.
