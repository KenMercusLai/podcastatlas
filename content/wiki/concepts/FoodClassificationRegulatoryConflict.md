# 食品分类监管冲突 / Food Classification Regulatory Conflict

## Definition

Food classification regulatory conflict occurs when the same food is assigned to different legal categories across jurisdictions, changing which additives, standards, tests, or labels apply.

## Current Synthesis

The辣条 dispute in [[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] shows that safety enforcement can conflict even when laboratories agree on what a product contains. Hubei treated products containing potassium sorbate and sodium dehydroacetate as noncompliant under a national classification logic, while [[Weilong|卫龙]] said those additives were permitted under the Henan local standard it followed.

The underlying ambiguity was whether辣条 belonged with pastry, puffed food, or convenience-food seasoned flour products. A national category rule reduced the jurisdictional mismatch, illustrating why product identity is part of safety infrastructure rather than mere naming.

## Key Claims

- Classification determines which additive permissions and limits apply to a product.
- A producer can claim local compliance while failing an out-of-province inspection under another classification.
- Cross-jurisdiction blacklists may therefore combine genuine production failures with rule-application conflict.
- National classification reduces ambiguity but can impose costly recipe, facility, license, and supply-chain changes.
- Product names must also reflect actual category and cannot substitute misleading nutrition or sensational claims for legal identity.

## Evidence

- **2018 enforcement conflict:** [[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] contrasts Hubei's failure finding with Weilong's defense under the Henan standard.
- **Category ambiguity:** [[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] identifies pastry, puffed food, and convenience-food seasoned flour products as competing classifications.
- **National resolution:** [[no-221-zhongguo-latiao-jianghu-1020016798]] says national authorities required uniform convenience-food treatment in 2019 and a more detailed industry standard followed in 2022.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Classification consistency does not replace sanitation inspection, dose control, traceability, or accurate laboratory work.
- The source does not independently reproduce each provincial and national legal text; exact permissions remain episode-scoped.
- Not every blacklist entry is explained by classification conflict.

## What Changed

- Added辣条 as a case where legal category, not only ingredient presence, determined compliance.
