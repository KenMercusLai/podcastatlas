# Jinjiang Model / 晋江模式

## Definition
Jinjiang Model / 晋江模式 is the reform-era regional industrial pattern in which [[Jinjiang]] and [[ChendaiTown|陈埭镇]] converted diaspora resources, clan trust, pooled household capital, surplus labor, local-government tolerance, subcontracting exposure, and later brand competition into a footwear and sportswear cluster.

## Current Synthesis
The model is not simply Wenzhou-style individual commerce, Sunan-style collective industry, or Pearl River Delta foreign-capital processing. In [[500-jinjiang-chendaizhen-wangshi-huangziyi-tan-gaikai-jincheng-zhong-de-zhongguo-yundong-pinpai-lswtplovdbo4pl52yoxu6rgzyal2]], it appears as a hybrid: household and kinship finance supplied startup capital; overseas Chinese houses and remittances supplied space and money; Nike-linked subcontracting supplied process reference; local officials tolerated experiments while later correcting quality failures; and brands such as [[AntaSports|Anta]], [[Xtep]], and [[361Degrees]] moved from production toward national branding.

## Key Claims
- The model depends on historically accumulated local resources, not only on a sudden reform-policy opening.
- Clan and acquaintance trust helped early firms raise money and coordinate labor before formal finance fully reached them.
- Outward-facing processing linked local workshops to product and process knowledge from global sportswear.
- Local-government tolerance reduced early institutional risk, but reputation crises forced later quality governance.
- The model's brand phase emerged after manufacturing capability, imitation, advertising, athlete endorsements, and domestic sports consumption reinforced one another.
- Anta's later retail and multi-brand evolution shows the model can extend beyond low-cost manufacturing, though not without innovation and segmentation pressure.

## Evidence
- Formation mechanism: [[500-jinjiang-chendaizhen-wangshi-huangziyi-tan-gaikai-jincheng-zhong-de-zhongguo-yundong-pinpai-lswtplovdbo4pl52yoxu6rgzyal2]] identifies "three idle" resources, kinship capital, and overseas ties as startup conditions.
- Comparative model evidence: [[500-jinjiang-chendaizhen-wangshi-huangziyi-tan-gaikai-jincheng-zhong-de-zhongguo-yundong-pinpai-lswtplovdbo4pl52yoxu6rgzyal2]] explicitly contrasts Jinjiang with Sunan, Wenzhou, and Pearl River Delta patterns.
- Governance evidence: [[500-jinjiang-chendaizhen-wangshi-huangziyi-tan-gaikai-jincheng-zhong-de-zhongguo-yundong-pinpai-lswtplovdbo4pl52yoxu6rgzyal2]] links "five allows," quality scandals, self-inspection institutions, and Xiang Nan's "除虫护花" line.
- Brand evolution evidence: [[500-jinjiang-chendaizhen-wangshi-huangziyi-tan-gaikai-jincheng-zhong-de-zhongguo-yundong-pinpai-lswtplovdbo4pl52yoxu6rgzyal2]] uses Li-Ning, Anta, Xtep, 361 Degrees, and Qiaodan Sports to show the shift from factories to domestic sportswear brands.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source does not claim diaspora capital or clan society automatically produces successful industry; it treats them as enabling conditions that required timing, market demand, government tolerance, and later quality control.
- The model should not be generalized to all Fujian or Chinese manufacturing clusters without additional sources.
- The episode's comparison with other regional models is interpretive and source-scoped.
