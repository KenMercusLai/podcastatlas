# Scripted Murder Role-Play / 剧本杀

## Definition
Scripted murder role-play / 剧本杀 is a facilitated social game in which participants inhabit characters with private information and collectively reconstruct a story through reading, evidence, discussion, negotiation, performance, and deduction.

## Current Synthesis
EP194 presents 剧本杀 as a family of formats rather than a single whodunit. Mechanism games emphasize factions, private objectives, negotiation, and variable outcomes; emotional games use writing, relationships, and designed scenes to produce identification or reflection; hard-core deduction games distribute clues and require detailed reconstruction. A murder, a culprit, and role performance often supply the basic grammar, but identifying the killer can become secondary to the form's strategic, emotional, literary, historical, or social purpose.

The format differs from passive fiction because players hold non-redundant information and alter one another's experience. It also differs from thin hidden-role games because characters typically carry fuller histories, motives, and relationships. This makes 剧本杀 a structured collaboration with obligations: each participant must understand, disclose, interpret, and perform enough of a role for the group to recover the whole.

## Key Claims
- The format combines private character texts, asymmetric information, facilitator-led progression, evidence exchange, and collective reconstruction.
- Mechanism, emotional, and deduction-led games use the same broad format to prioritize different kinds of agency and reward.
- Character histories and relationships can make role interpretation more central than simply identifying a culprit.
- Every player's information and attention can affect the shared result, leaving few genuinely disposable roles.
- Reading volume, deduction difficulty, social intensity, and role compatibility vary enough that selection is part of experience design.
- Repeat play with concealed prior knowledge, spoilers, disengagement, or mid-session withdrawal violates the cooperative conditions supporting the game.

- Genre variation: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] distinguishes faction-and-mechanism play, emotional immersion, and hard-core deduction without treating the categories as exhaustive research taxonomy.
- Distributed responsibility: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] says each role carries unique information and identifies spoilers, divided attention, hidden replay knowledge, and withdrawal as group-level harms.
- Selection differences: [[ep194-zai-jubensha-li-chenjin-baoku-shi-yizhong-zenyang-de-tiyan-ckwriw4fquvtabaaaadtzwuv]] reports scripts ranging from a few pages to more than seventy and recommends disclosing reading and inference demands before play.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode offers an experienced-player introduction, not a representative industry survey. Its three-part taxonomy can overlap, and not every 剧本杀 centers a literal murder or gives every role equal narrative weight. Claims about typical duration, accessibility, format quality, and the superiority of particular online or offline arrangements remain dependent on script, facilitator, group, platform, and player preference.

## What Changed
- Established 剧本杀 as a facilitated family of role-play formats rather than a synonym for guessing a killer.
- Made asymmetric information and participant obligation central to the form.
- Preserved genre overlap and source-scope limits around typical practice.
