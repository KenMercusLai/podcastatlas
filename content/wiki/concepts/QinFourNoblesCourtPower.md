# Qin Four Nobles Court Power / 秦国四贵权力结构

Qin Four Nobles court power / 秦国四贵权力结构 is the configuration [[FanJu|范雎]] names in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-89-qinwang-jing-bei-ta-dui-mabaonan-ljugedx8qxhtuzd1ja-xiwxgse19]]. The four are [[WeiRan|穰侯魏冉]], [[HuayangJunQin|华阳君]], [[JingyangJunQin|泾阳君]], and [[GaolingJunQin|高陵君]], presented as close kin of [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] whose offices, envoys, retainers, and family position crowd out royal authority.

The concept extends [[ForeignRelativeCourtPolitics|楚系外戚政治]] and [[QueenMotherRegency|太后摄政]]. In Zhouji 60, Xuan Taihou and Wei Ran consolidate a young ruler's court; in Zhouji 89, Fan Ju argues that the same structure has become a mature power bloc that can make Qin appear strong while leaving the king personally isolated.

The episode's sharpest political claim is that strategy cannot be separated from court control. [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]] requires Qin to direct force toward nearby, holdable territory, but a figure like Wei Ran can convert state war into [[PrivateFiefExpansionThroughStateWar|private fief expansion]] if the ruler cannot overrule the kin bloc.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-90-jiemi-xianqin-zui-wanmei-de-fuchou-liu7gbl-bffknkw6dx5udqcq4tcm]] records the resolution but keeps it source-cautious. [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] can reduce the bloc and send [[WeiRan|魏冉]] back to [[Dingtao|陶 / 定陶]], which implies the king retained more latent capacity than a literal puppet would have. The episode also cautions that [[XuanTaihou|宣太后]] may not have been formally deposed and the four nobles may not all have been expelled in the same simple way.

## Key Claims
- A ruler can be formally sovereign while practical authority sits with queen-mother, maternal kin, and royal-brother networks.
- Overmighty kin are dangerous because they hold office, armed capacity, private retainers, and access to interstate communication.
- Strategy fails when the person ordering campaigns is not the same person who pays the state's costs and receives the state's durable gains.
- Fan Ju's attack on the four nobles is therefore not a moral complaint alone; it is a prerequisite for Qin's external strategy.
- The bloc's removal should be read as practical authority recovery with source-scope caution, not necessarily as a fully documented purge of every named figure.
