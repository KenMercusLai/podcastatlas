# Sports Officiating Automation

Sports officiating automation is the use of measurement, replay, tracking, or algorithmic systems to review or replace parts of human officiating. [[tech-20260330-0330-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260330-0330-mp-tech-pod-128]] grounds the concept through [[MajorLeagueBaseball]]'s [[AutomatedBallStrikeChallengeSystem|ABS]], where players can challenge a ball-strike call and camera tracking checks the pitch against a calibrated zone.

The source frames automation as a legitimacy tool. [[NolaAgha]] argues that fans are already accustomed to tracking in sports and daily life, and that fairness can matter more than preserving every older officiating ambiguity. At the same time, ABS keeps human umpires in the loop because missed or misrecorded pitches leave the human call standing.

## Key Claims
- Sports automation can be adopted to make outcomes feel fairer and more credible.
- Review systems must decide whether they are challenge tools, advisory tools, or full replacements.
- Adoption depends on fan expectations, player trust, official acceptance, and rule clarity.
- Automation can shift blame and tradition even when it improves accuracy.
- Officiating data may improve training, evaluation, and future rule design, but only if leagues govern how that data is used.
