## Definition

Talent Summons Ruler Self-Examination is the principle that a ruler who cannot attract worthy people after proper courtesy should inspect his own virtue, court order, adviser ecology, and sincerity before escalating to rewards, offices, or punishment.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] frames talent summons as a ruler-side diagnostic. A wise ruler seeks hidden people because their methods, virtue, wisdom, and governing contribution can benefit the court and the people, not because the ruler wants the prestige of collecting famous recluses.

The concept appears when [[FanYingLateHan|樊英]] keeps refusing even after the court offers conspicuous courtesy. [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s judgment, as presented by the episode, is that the ruler should not force the matter. Continued refusal should prompt self-examination: whether the ruler's virtue is clear enough, whether governance is disordered, whether奸佞 dominate court access, and whether the invitation is sincere.

The Xunzi fire-and-cicada analogy gives one mechanism. Bright fire attracts; merely shaking the tree does not. In political terms, rank, gifts, and threats recruit people attracted by rank, gifts, and fear. They do not reliably attract genuine gentlemen, and they can actively select for greedy or fearful candidates.

A second failure mode appears when a ruler seeks widely only after the established senior cohort has been depleted. The recruitment edict in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-369-2-gaiming-bikan-yinji-faze-yyds-foitbkrmhmc1xgeu0tk4btyvmi98|Hanji 369-2]] values unconventional people who can perform difficult military, civil, or diplomatic work, but the host contrasts that openness with [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s earlier severity toward subordinates. This makes self-examination retrospective as well as prospective: recruitment design must ask not only how to attract new people, but whether earlier punishment and control damaged the talent pool it now needs.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-318-jian-hanwudi-woshi-nide-pobiren-ljw371ou_jca8esjwuqngsdccaga|Hanji 318]] supplies the earlier event-level warning behind that retrospective diagnosis. [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] tells Wudi that worthy people are finite and cannot be killed without limit while the ruler simultaneously seeks them. The claim does not show that all punishment is wrong or identify each official involved; it establishes the institutional contradiction that recruitment cannot indefinitely offset ruler-created attrition.

## Key Claims

- Talent summons should pursue usable methods and public benefit rather than the symbolic possession of a famous hidden person.
- Courtesy matters, but courtesy without credible virtue and order may not be enough to attract worthy people.
- Persistent refusal by worthy people is evidence about the ruler and court as much as evidence about the candidate.
- Self-examination should precede coercion when recruitment fails after genuine respect.
- High office and punishment are poor filters because they attract the greedy and fearful more readily than principled people.
- The ruler's visible virtue is an attraction mechanism: talent comes toward a court that gives worthy people reason to trust service.
- Emergency recruitment can expose accumulated personnel-policy damage when a ruler seeks replacements only after experienced officials have been depleted; talent policy must account for retention and survivability because recruitment cannot indefinitely replace capable people lost through excessive punishment.

## Evidence

- Recruitment purpose: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] says wise rulers use hidden people's governing methods rather than their recluse identity.
- Failed conversion of courtesy into service: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] records Fan Ying's forced arrival, honors, lecture platform, teacher ritual, offices, and continued resistance.
- Ruler self-examination: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] says a ruler who cannot attract worthy people should ask about virtue, disorder,奸佞, and sincerity.
- Bad filters: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] rejects inducing with high office and threatening with punishment because those tools select for the wrong motives.
- Attraction analogy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-808-ta-jiujing-shi-mingjia-haishi-pianzi-lhmsw7zusdkqddk-zu27oj6hfs2m|Hanji 808]] invokes [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s cicada-catching comparison to argue that visible virtue attracts more effectively than empty movement.
- Vacancy-crisis recruitment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-369-2-gaiming-bikan-yinji-faze-yyds-foitbkrmhmc1xgeu0tk4btyvmi98|Hanji 369-2]] says Han Wudi orders broad local recommendation after Wei Qing's death and the depletion of senior civil and military officials.
- Retrospective ruler diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-369-2-gaiming-bikan-yinji-faze-yyds-foitbkrmhmc1xgeu0tk4btyvmi98|Hanji 369-2]] contrasts the new openness to unconventional talent with earlier harsh punishment, though the causal link remains the host's interpretation.
- Finite-talent warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-318-jian-hanwudi-woshi-nide-pobiren-ljw371ou_jca8esjwuqngsdccaga|Hanji 318]] says Ji An directly warns Wudi that the court cannot seek worthy people while treating the available talent pool as infinitely replaceable through execution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not deny that rulers can summon, honor, consult, appoint, discipline, or lawfully punish capable people. It narrows the escalation rule: after proper respect has failed, coercion is more likely to expose the court's disorder than to create loyal service, while punishment severe enough to consume experienced people can undermine the capacity recruitment is meant to build. Hanji 369-2 shows that an emergency call can be sensible even when it arrives late, but its causal explanation remains interpretive rather than a demonstrated personnel census. Hanji 318 does not name the punished officials, settle their guilt, or prove that every execution was avoidable. Hanji 808 leaves Fan Ying's personal quality partly open, so the principle should be applied to the ruler's method even when the candidate's own reputation is uncertain.

## What Changed

- Added Ji An's earlier finite-talent warning, connecting recruitment design to retention and survivability before Hanji 369-2's later vacancy crisis.
- Kept the causal claim bounded because the sources do not identify every punishment or provide a complete personnel census.
