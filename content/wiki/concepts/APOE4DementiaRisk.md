# APOE4 Dementia Risk

APOE4 dementia risk is the genetic-risk branch in [[working-memory-the-surprising-decline-of-dementia-6a6b0eeca585d3be78cd9d1b]]. The episode says about 25% of the population has one copy of APOE4 and faces roughly two-to-three times higher dementia risk, while about 2% has two copies and faces roughly ten-to-fifteen times higher risk.

The source's main point is that genetic risk is important but not deterministic. Many people with APOE4 do not develop dementia, and many people without APOE4 do, so genetics cannot explain the large cohort-level decline by itself.

## Key Claims
- APOE4 is presented as a strong risk factor rather than a diagnosis.
- The source uses APOE4 to show why individual risk and population trend need to be separated.
- APOE4 also matters for treatment risk because carriers are described as more vulnerable to brain swelling or bleeding from some amyloid-targeting drugs.
