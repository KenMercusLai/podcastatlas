# Condiment Outbound Acquisition / 调味品出海收购

## Definition
Condiment outbound acquisition is the strategy of using an acquired food or condiment brand to gain overseas distribution, local operating knowledge, and cultural legitimacy rather than relying only on exports from the home market.

## Current Synthesis
The source's case is [[HaitianFlavoring|海天味业]] buying [[AmoyFood|淘大]]. Haitian brings scale, R&D, supply chain, and channels; Amoy brings an older brand, overseas distribution, and local-market experience. The logic is capability complementarity, but the source keeps integration risk visible.

## Key Claims
- Acquisitions can buy overseas channels and local experience that are hard to build quickly.
- The acquired brand's history and existing distribution can reduce market-entry friction.
- The acquirer still has to solve brand division, team integration, and supply-chain coordination.
- Overseas revenue targets can make acquisition more attractive than slow organic expansion.

## Evidence
- Transaction evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says Haitian completed the Amoy acquisition.
- Capability evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says Haitian will open R&D, supply-chain, and channel resources while Amoy contributes overseas brand assets and local operating experience.
- Brand-history evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says Amoy originated in 1908, moved to Hong Kong, and built overseas markets from the 1950s.
- Target evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says Haitian aimed to raise overseas revenue share to 15% within three years.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove the combined company can keep Amoy's local trust while adding Haitian's scale. Integration and brand-positioning risks remain unresolved.
