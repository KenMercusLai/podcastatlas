# Forced Refugee Return

Forced refugee return is the humanitarian pressure described in [[afghanistan-five-years-on-our-correspondent-visits-6a7ee547049c692a6b3ee0a9]], where the source says almost six million Afghan refugees have been forcibly returned from [[Iran]] and [[Pakistan]], many arriving with nothing. The return flow adds to [[Afghanistan]]'s poverty, hunger, unemployment and health-care crisis under the [[Taliban]].

The concept matters because return is not only a border event. It transfers costs into households, villages, aid systems and cities that may already lack jobs, medical care, housing and school access. In the source, it is one channel through which [[HumanitarianIsolation]] becomes local.

## Key Claims
- Returning people without resources can intensify poverty even when open fighting has declined.
- Host-country deportation choices become part of the origin country's humanitarian burden.
- Refugee return pressure interacts with sanctions, aid cuts and weak health systems rather than standing apart from them.
- For women and girls, return can mean re-entering the same education and public-life restrictions they may have fled.
