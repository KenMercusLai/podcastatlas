## Definition

阵亡先锋遗属封赏 / Fallen Vanguard Heir Reward is the practice of compensating casualties and honoring the heirs of troops judged courageous even when their expedition ends in defeat.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 352-2 supplies the source-bounded case. [[HanQianqiuWesternHan|韩千秋]]'s force is annihilated near Panyu and has not completed a victorious achievement, but [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] still calls it the army's courageous vanguard, supports the dead soldiers' families, and grants titles to sons of the fallen leaders.

The episode interprets this distinction as both recognition and mobilization. Reward is attached to risk-bearing conduct rather than fabricated victory, allowing the state to show later troops that catastrophic service will not leave their families invisible. The mechanism therefore complements rather than replaces [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|因功赏罚]]: outcome remains negative, while courage and sacrifice receive a separate public judgment.

## Key Claims

- Military defeat and recognized courage can coexist when the court evaluates conduct separately from campaign outcome.
- Supporting the dead soldiers' families gives material form to the state's judgment of sacrifice.
- Granting titles to heirs preserves household status after the original actors can no longer receive rewards themselves.
- Public recognition can support later mobilization by reducing the signal that failed risk-taking will be forgotten.
- The episode does not prove that motivation was Han Wudi's only reason; compensation, mourning, legitimacy, and personnel politics may overlap.

## Evidence

- Outcome-conduct distinction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-352-2-hanwudi-weihe-fengshang-baijun-zhi-jiang-li16pfjb6p0owys5htecscfomhch|Hanji 352-2]] says Han Qianqiu's group has not completed merit but remains the bravest vanguard.
- Casualty support: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-352-2-hanwudi-weihe-fengshang-baijun-zhi-jiang-li16pfjb6p0owys5htecscfomhch|Hanji 352-2]] says Wudi compensates the families of those killed.
- Heir honors: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-352-2-hanwudi-weihe-fengshang-baijun-zhi-jiang-li16pfjb6p0owys5htecscfomhch|Hanji 352-2]] says [[HanYannianWesternHan|韩延年]] becomes marquis of Cheng'an and another fallen envoy's son becomes marquis of Longkang.
- Mobilization context: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-352-2-hanwudi-weihe-fengshang-baijun-zhi-jiang-li16pfjb6p0owys5htecscfomhch|Hanji 352-2]] places the rewards immediately before a reported ten-wan-person conquest force and the selection of [[LuBodeWesternHan|路博德]] and [[YangPuWesternHan|杨仆]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The evidence comes from one short popular-history episode. The source reports the reward sequence but does not establish the private deliberation behind it, measure its effect on recruitment or morale, or show that every casualty family was treated equally. The concept should not be generalized into a claim that all defeated commanders were rewarded: the episode depends on the court's exceptional designation of this group as a courageous vanguard.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish sacrifice-based family recognition from victory-based military merit.
- Preserved mobilization as an episode interpretation rather than a proven exclusive motive.
