# Elite Crisis Burden Sharing / 贵族危机共担动员

Elite crisis burden sharing / 贵族危机共担动员 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-4-lqs3pj2-rebp0uh6q7ayecpju3hk]] draws from [[LiTan|李谈 / 李同]]'s remonstrance to [[Pingyuanjun|平原君]] during the [[Handan|邯郸]] siege. A city under existential pressure cannot ask ordinary soldiers and commoners to keep suffering while a powerful household visibly preserves food, clothing, money, women, servants, and metal objects.

The concept differs from generic generosity. Li Tan's argument is strategic: if [[ZhaoState|赵国]] falls, Pingyuanjun's household loses its privileges anyway, so hoarded private resources are not actually safe. Turning wealth into rewards, metal into weapons, and household dependents into visible participation gives the defense a chance to buy time for outside relief.

The source treats Pingyuanjun's response as late but meaningful. After he distributes family wealth and sends his household into the army, Zhao recruits three thousand dare-death fighters. Morale rises because sacrifice no longer falls only downward. This connects to [[MilitaryMoraleThroughSharedHardship|同甘共苦式士气]], but the mechanism is civic and aristocratic rather than only commander-soldier: a protected elite must make its own stake visible before it can credibly ask others to die.

## Key Claims
- In an existential crisis, elite comfort can become a morale liability if it makes public sacrifice look one-sided.
- Private wealth can be strategically irrational to preserve when defeat would destroy the household's status and property anyway.
- Burden sharing becomes credible when it changes material arrangements, not only when leaders speak sympathetically.
- The mechanism can be morally belated and still operationally useful: Pingyuanjun acts only after rebuke, but the action still changes recruitment.
- This pattern overlaps with military morale, elite law compliance, and aristocratic honor, but its focus is emergency conversion of privilege into collective defense.
