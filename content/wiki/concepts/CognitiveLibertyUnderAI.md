# Cognitive Liberty Under AI

## Definition
Cognitive liberty under AI is the retained power to form, inspect, represent, and revise one's own thinking when knowledge access and cognitive work are increasingly mediated by privately controlled systems.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes capability loss from power loss. Navigation, calculators, and other tools can weaken practiced skills while increasing total capability, so de-skilling alone does not settle whether augmentation is harmful. The sharper question is who controls the systems through which people understand the world, what representation of the user those systems hold, whether users can choose when and how to delegate, and whether independent thought remains practically available. Cognitive liberty therefore joins skill retention with ownership, governance, exit, and collective decision-making.

## Key Claims
- Increased output can coexist with reduced control over cognition and knowledge infrastructure.
- Skill loss is not identical to agency loss, although repeated nonuse can make exit less realistic.
- Choice over representation and delegation is part of cognitive power.
- Private control of widely used thinking systems creates a collective governance issue.
- Retained alternatives, inspection, and independent practice matter alongside convenience and performance.

## Evidence
### Capability Versus Power
- [[02026-thurston-aguera-y-arcas-podcast-v4]] contrasts possible losses in navigation or arithmetic with broader gains in technological and collective intelligence.

### Control of Thought Infrastructure
- [[02026-thurston-aguera-y-arcas-podcast-v4]] shifts concern from what users can still do to who controls thought, knowledge, representation, and the systems used to interpret the world.

### Collective Choice
- [[02026-thurston-aguera-y-arcas-podcast-v4]] distinguishes capability from agency and includes collective decision-making in the latter.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not specify a legal right, technical architecture, or empirical threshold for cognitive liberty. Private tools can expand access and autonomy, while public or local systems can also constrain users. Retaining every unaided skill is neither possible nor necessary; the issue is whether delegation remains informed, reversible enough for the stakes, and governed by people affected by it.
