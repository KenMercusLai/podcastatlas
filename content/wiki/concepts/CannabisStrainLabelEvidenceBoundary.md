# Cannabis Strain-Label Evidence Boundary

## Definition
The cannabis strain-label evidence boundary is the distinction between botanical or commercial categories such as indica and sativa and demonstrated chemical composition or reliably blinded psychoactive effects.

## Current Synthesis
The current judgment follows the later corrective interview: indica and sativa do not reliably predict what a modern cannabis product contains or how it will affect a user. Reported differences can be shaped by seller descriptions, packaging, prior experience, and expectancy, while products sharing a label may not share cannabinoid or terpene profiles. The earlier solo episode presents sativa as broadly stimulating and indica as broadly sedating, even while acknowledging variable individual response; that is the claim this boundary now explicitly qualifies.

This does not prove that every product feels identical or that terpenes and minor cannabinoids have no biological effects. It means chemical and behavioral claims require composition data and blinded comparison rather than category folklore. A limonene study discussed in the episode suggests anxiety modification at high doses, but Hill questions whether those doses resemble typical cannabis products.

## Key Claims
- Indica and sativa are botanical terms, not reliable modern chemical profiles.
- Commercial strain names and categories can create expectancy effects before consumption.
- Subjective product differences do not by themselves identify the responsible molecule or mechanism.
- Entourage effects remain possible but are not established by labels or anecdotes.
- Blinded, composition-aware comparisons are more informative than seller-guided reports.
- High experimental terpene doses may not generalize to ordinary products.

- Expectancy pathway: [[how-cannabis-impacts-health-the-potential-risks-dr-matthew-hill-scim3230094101]] attributes some reported strain differences to labels and seller-provided expectations.
- Entourage qualification: [[how-cannabis-impacts-health-the-potential-risks-dr-matthew-hill-scim3230094101]] leaves room for interactions while emphasizing limited blinded evidence.
- Dose generalizability: [[how-cannabis-impacts-health-the-potential-risks-dr-matthew-hill-scim3230094101]] discusses limonene reducing high-dose THC anxiety at levels Hill questions as commercially representative.
- Earlier categorical framing: [[the-effects-of-cannabis-marijuana-on-the-brain-body-scim1203833427]] describes sativa as more stimulating and indica as more sedating while acknowledging that individual responses remain difficult to predict.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither episode provides a comprehensive chemical survey of cultivars or rules out reproducible effects from defined cannabinoid-terpene combinations. Expectancy bias can coexist with pharmacological differences. Individual variability in the earlier episode does not validate its categorical label predictions. The bounded conclusion is that familiar labels are insufficient evidence, not that composition never matters.
