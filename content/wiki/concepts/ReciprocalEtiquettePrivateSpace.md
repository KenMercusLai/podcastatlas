## Definition

礼仪互惠与私人空间 / reciprocal etiquette and private space is the principle that a norm cannot be applied only to the observed person: judgment must also account for setting, privacy, notice, and the observer's own conduct.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1143-guren-lihun-neng-you-duo-qipa-lutfgjyzwfwwh4ovzuevene8xupy|Hanji 1143]] develops the idea through a household anecdote. [[Mengzi|孟子]] treats his wife's relaxed “箕踞” posture as grounds for divorce, but his mother asks whether he announced himself before entering. Once he admits that he did not, the moral frame changes: the wife is relaxing in a private room, while the person judging her has crossed the threshold without warning.

The useful synthesis is not that private space suspends every norm. It is that context and reciprocity precede confident blame. A person should test whether their own entry, demand, or observation helped create the apparent offense before converting one moment into a judgment of character or relationship fitness.

## Key Claims

- Etiquette applies to the observer as well as the observed person.
- Private settings change what posture or conduct reasonably communicates.
- Notice before entry is a form of respect because it lets another person shift between private and social presentation.
- Self-examination can reveal that an apparent offense was partly produced by one's own boundary crossing.
- One context-bound act is weak evidence for a total judgment about character or a relationship.

## Evidence

- Private posture: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1143-guren-lihun-neng-you-duo-qipa-lutfgjyzwfwwh4ovzuevene8xupy|Hanji 1143]] places the wife's relaxed sitting posture inside the bedroom rather than a formal audience.
- Reciprocal inquiry: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1143-guren-lihun-neng-you-duo-qipa-lutfgjyzwfwwh4ovzuevene8xupy|Hanji 1143]] has Mencius's mother ask whether he knocked or gave notice before entering.
- Revised judgment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1143-guren-lihun-neng-you-duo-qipa-lutfgjyzwfwwh4ovzuevene8xupy|Hanji 1143]] says Mencius recognizes his own error and abandons the divorce proposal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode presents a famous moral anecdote rather than an independently verified household record. Its dialogue, ritual explanation, and attribution require textual comparison. Private context does not excuse coercion or harm; the narrower claim is that ordinary etiquette judgments should account for privacy, notice, and reciprocal obligation.
