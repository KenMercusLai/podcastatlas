## Definition

Product capability as corporate purpose is the principle that an organization should treat becoming able to make repeatedly better products as a governing end, while revenue, finance, reputation, and technology remain enabling means.

## Current Synthesis

Using martial-arts training as its metaphor, the source argues that craft improvement need not be justified only by a later capital, scale, or status outcome. A game company earns, finances, publishes, and builds a brand partly so that it can attempt the next stronger work. This reverses the period in which making games became a means to becoming a prestigious company owner or capital-market participant.

The principle is not anti-commercial. Sustainable revenue and financing preserve the opportunity to continue, while management converts creative capacity into delivery. The boundary is whether these instruments increase product capability or begin to displace it as the criterion for action.

## Key Claims

- Product capability includes creative judgment, technical execution, organization, and repeated delivery.
- Revenue and financing matter because they fund continuity and the next attempt.
- Corporate scale, market fashion, and capital status can become misleading proxy goals.
- Repeated work in one domain can compound capability even when a single project does not settle success.
- A useful strategic question is whether an action improves the team's ability to make the next product.

### Continuity

- [[156-xia-dui-ying-zhi-ren-ling-zhizuoren-liangqiwei-6-xiaoshi-fangtan-lvnhry4glcc3ut-6nmjn7qrmd-dan]] reports that valuing sustained work in the same domain helped reduce anxiety about judging the company through one project's result.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

“Better” remains contestable across creator intention, player experience, accessibility, employee welfare, profitability, technical quality, and cultural value. Product focus can become an excuse for founder dominance, overwork, or neglect of governance. The source provides a normative founder philosophy, not comparative proof that this purpose produces superior companies.

## What Changed

- Established product capability as an end rather than only a route to financial outcomes.
- Preserved commerce, management, and financing as necessary enabling disciplines.
- Added governance and labor limits to the craft-centered philosophy.
