# Wide-Bracket Goal Review

## Definition

Wide-bracket goal review considers several life domains or a longer span of activity together so priorities, trade-offs, and cumulative progress become visible.

## Current Synthesis

[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8462fe97eef50d4a98b4f9b755916f20-43682002-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-8462fe97eef50d4a98b4f9b755916f20-43682002|You 2.0: Our Pursuit of Happiness]] contrasts a wide bracket with narrow target focus. A pop-up exercise used limited basket space to force career, education, relationship, home, and lifestyle choices into one view, making incompatibilities and priorities discussable with a trusted companion. A second example reviewed several weeks of handwritten lists so completed work remained visible beside unfinished tasks.

The useful distinction is stage and question. Narrow focus can support execution toward a defined nearby endpoint; a wide bracket is better suited to deciding which endpoint deserves effort, seeing costs transferred across domains, and judging progress across enough time to avoid overweighting today's unfinished work. Broad review is not automatically wise: categories, time window, and social input can still bias the conclusion.

## Key Claims

- Decisions in one life domain can consume time, money, or attention needed in another.
- A constrained shared representation can make otherwise hidden incompatibilities easier to discuss.
- Trusted companions can help articulate a person's own priorities without requiring maximal ambition in every domain.
- Reviewing several periods together can keep accumulated progress from disappearing behind the current unfinished list.
- Wide review is a selection and recalibration tool, while narrow focus is often an execution tool.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode summary does not provide sample size, assignment, measures, durability, or evidence that pop-up-shop choices became later behavior. A basket exercise simplifies obligations and opportunities that may not be commensurable, while a companion can support or distort preference expression. Longer review windows can correct recency bias but can also conceal deteriorating current conditions. The bracket should match the decision and remain open to constraints that the representation omits.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to separate broad priority and progress review from narrow execution focus.
- Added cross-domain trade-offs and longer-horizon self-assessment as two uses of the same perspective shift.
