# Cooking Oil Selection and Handling

## Definition
Cooking oil selection and handling is a household decision framework that matches oil composition, processing, flavor, cooking temperature, package size, light and oxygen exposure, storage time, and reuse history rather than ranking oils by one prestige label.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats purchase, cooking, and storage as one chain. A stable everyday oil, a lower-heat or cold-use oil, and occasional flavor-specific oils can serve different purposes; no bottle is best for every temperature, dish, health need, or household consumption rate. Refining and extraction can trade some flavor or accompanying compounds for impurity removal, consistency, and stability, so “artisanal,” “cold-pressed,” and “unrefined” are not automatic safety guarantees.

Oxidation risk rises with heat, light, oxygen, time, and repeated use, especially for highly unsaturated oils. Practical controls include avoiding the habit of heating oil until it smokes, buying a size that can be finished promptly, using dark and well-sealed packaging where appropriate, refrigerating especially fragile oils, and treating rancid odor, persistent darkening, or degradation after repeated heating as stop signals. Normal low-temperature crystallization is distinct from spoilage and should be judged with temperature reversibility and odor rather than appearance alone.

## Key Claims
- Choose oil for the intended cooking method, not through one universal health ranking.
- Greater unsaturation can increase sensitivity to heat and oxidation, while greater stability does not make unlimited intake healthy.
- Refining, pressing, extraction, and blending involve different tradeoffs and cannot be judged by a single “natural” label.
- Brand, factory, ingredients, blend disclosure, grade, production date, packaging, and hydrogenation or trans-fat information provide different pieces of the purchase decision.
- Smoke, rancid odor, sustained darkening, and repeated high heat are degradation signals; low-temperature clouding or crystallization may be reversible and benign.
- Package size should match household use so opened oil is not stored far longer than necessary.
- Limited frying-oil reuse requires filtration, sealed dark storage, low ambient heat, prompt use, and rejection when degradation is evident.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not supply comparative oxidation measurements, smoke-point tables, contaminant testing, product certifications, or a complete evidence review of frying reuse. Oil behavior varies with cultivar, refinement, antioxidants, package, temperature, duration, food moisture, and reuse history. The source's shelf-life ranges and three-month opened-use suggestion are practical heuristics, not universal safety cutoffs; the product label and sensory degradation signs still matter.

## What Changed
- Created an end-to-end household framework joining purchase, cooking temperature, storage, and reuse.
- Distinguished processing tradeoffs from prestige marketing.
- Separated reversible cold crystallization from oxidation and rancidity.
- Kept shelf-life and reuse thresholds source-scoped.
