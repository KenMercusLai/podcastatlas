# Pain-Attention Reframing

## Definition
Pain-attention reframing is the deliberate redirection of attention and interpretation during bounded discomfort so that pain is not the only information occupying awareness.

## Current Synthesis
During a difficult underwater exercise, [[AndreaFuentes]] says she shifted attention away from pain alone and toward light, music, space, teammates, progress, and bodily adaptation. She summarizes the lesson as pain being unavoidable while suffering can be optional. In coaching, she uses the same move to help athletes notice improvement and adaptation rather than continually rehearse discomfort.

The useful core is attentional flexibility, not a universal claim that suffering is chosen. Attention can change the experienced context of safe, expected training discomfort, but it cannot determine whether pain reflects injury, hypoxia, illness, trauma, coercion, or excessive load. The practice therefore requires a safety boundary and permission to stop.

## Key Claims
- Attention can amplify pain when awareness repeatedly narrows around discomfort alone.
- Widening attention can restore environmental, relational, and progress-related information during bounded effort.
- Interpretation changes experience without proving that the underlying physiological signal is harmless.
- Practice may help athletes distinguish expected effort from panic, but it must not suppress reporting.
- “Optional suffering” is a personal performance frame, not a moral judgment about people in pain.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies personal experience rather than controlled evidence. Reframing must not be used to dismiss another person's report, normalize abusive training, override injury or medical warning signs, or encourage breath-hold persistence near blackout. Pain, suffering, attention, and danger are related but not interchangeable. Severe, unexplained, escalating, or persistent symptoms require qualified assessment.
