# Knowing Enough

## Definition
Knowing enough is the restraint practice of defining the point at which more revenue, products, trading, external validation, achievement, or status no longer serves the person, relationship, company, or trust system it was supposed to support.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence joins a governance case with a life-practice case. In the Youzhi Youxing discussion, enough protects financial users: a founder or platform must know when channel fees, extra products, trading encouragement, or higher take rate would damage alignment and trust. In the Stumpf discussion, enough protects life: pursuing a major goal can become self-defeating when it consumes family, relationships, health, and ordinary experiences.

The shared synthesis is that enough is not passivity. It is an active boundary against scoreboards that never stop. In finance, that boundary needs governance, incentives, product friction, and successor structures. In personal achievement, it needs pre-defined tradeoffs and honest pricing of success. [[YeTan|叶檀]] adds a household and illness test: enough can mean that stopping work does not threaten living or treatment, a parent is cared for, ordinary spending is not a recurring anxiety, stable cash flow covers life, and remaining resources support worthwhile action. The number remains personal; the durable principle is that additional wealth should expand usable choice rather than manufacture new obligations and proof demands.

## Key Claims
- Enough protects trust when more monetization would shift a platform away from user outcomes.
- Enough protects life when more achievement would damage relationships, health, or presence.
- The boundary must be defined before pressure, temptation, or external validation makes escalation feel automatic.
- Founder character can create restraint, but durable restraint needs governance, incentives, and authority design.
- Personal restraint is active discernment, not resignation or lack of ambition.
- Success should be priced against what it costs, not only measured by whether the stated goal was reached.
- A usable definition of enough includes security, care obligations, ordinary cash flow, and refusal power rather than a universal asset target.

## Evidence
- Financial-platform restraint: [[e44-li-xiaobo-duihua-mengyan-zheci-jiu-zheyang-ba-lpcrvfgnseaed-eambk9ofnvuq2]] frames Youzhi Youxing's refusal of homepage, banner, slot, or advertising-style fund-channel fees as a trust-preserving decision.
- Investor friction: [[e44-li-xiaobo-duihua-mengyan-zheci-jiu-zheyang-ba-lpcrvfgnseaed-eambk9ofnvuq2]] treats questions before fund purchase as suitability friction that lowers conversion but supports user understanding.
- Governance boundary: [[e44-li-xiaobo-duihua-mengyan-zheci-jiu-zheyang-ba-lpcrvfgnseaed-eambk9ofnvuq2]] says a limited-liability structure does not by itself protect mission because founder capital, board authority, ownership, and successor incentives matter.
- Better-life test: [[e44-li-xiaobo-duihua-mengyan-zheci-jiu-zheyang-ba-lpcrvfgnseaed-eambk9ofnvuq2]] asks whether investment attention damages sleep, relationships, family presence, or ordinary joy.
- Achievement pricing: [[the-mental-frame-specific-daily-actions-to-succeed-andy-stumpf-scim2498586099]] has Stumpf prefer someone who falls slightly short of a massive goal while remaining fulfilled over someone who reaches it after sacrificing family, relationships, and life experience.
- Money boundary: [[the-mental-frame-specific-daily-actions-to-succeed-andy-stumpf-scim2498586099]] distinguishes money's ability to buffer some stress from its inability to buy real connection or solve every form of helplessness.
- Personal sufficiency test: [[e249-duihua-yetan-wo-buzai-shi-rensheng-de-guokeren-li8lfk6ayg48zk63bpnh4qtkmn9x]] defines enough through work-optional living and treatment, care for Ye Tan's mother, ordinary spending, stable cash flow, and resources for valued action rather than endless valuation and growth.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Knowing enough does not settle how much money, growth, ambition, or achievement is appropriate in every case. Scarcity, dependents, debt, competitive pressure, medical needs, longevity, inflation, governance duties, and survival risk can make "more" rational. Ye Tan's threshold reflects unusual illness and career circumstances and is not a planning formula. The concept is a boundary against unexamined escalation, not a rule that less is always morally better.

## What Changed
- Added a concrete personal sufficiency test covering treatment security, family care, ordinary cash flow, refusal power, and worthwhile use.
- Clarified that enough is person-specific and should convert wealth into usable choice rather than another status target.
