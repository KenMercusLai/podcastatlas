# Childhood Survival Rule Generalization / 童年生存规则泛化

Childhood survival rule generalization / 童年生存规则泛化 is [[ep273-npc-haishi-npd-dou-bie-xiang-nazou-women-rensheng-de-yaokongqi-lqqnd-e29xvhx2mtf6uuzx13wwvf]]'s account of how early family experience can become mistaken for the whole world's rules. [[GongXueping|龚学平]] says children may first experience parents as the whole world, and some adults later keep living as though the whole world works like their parents did.

This explains why some people seek love from harsh, withholding, or unreliable figures and distrust easier forms of care. If approval once came only from a painful source, then "flowers on thorns" can feel more believable than direct affection. The episode treats this as a realism problem as much as an attachment problem: the person is using an old survival map in a larger world where other choices and other people exist.

## Key Claims
- Childhood patterns become adult traps when they are generalized beyond the family conditions that produced them.
- A person may keep trying harder with someone who cannot love well because harsh approval feels more credible than simple care.
- Rationalizing harm can preserve psychic survival in childhood, but the same mechanism can keep adults in avoidable suffering.
- [[TherapyRelationshipAndBoundaries|Therapy]] helps by making the old rule visible, emotionally believable as old, and behaviorally replaceable.
