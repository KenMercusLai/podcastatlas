## Definition

阴德福报因果 / Hidden virtue reward causality is Hanji 430's moral frame for the claim that good done without public display can later return as protection, reward, or descendant benefit.

## Current Synthesis

The concept is grounded in [[XiahouShengWesternHan|夏侯胜]]'s explanation of [[BingJiWesternHan|丙吉]]'s illness. Bing Ji had quietly protected the future [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] and did not advertise the merit; Xiahou Sheng says such hidden virtue will be answered before death and extend to descendants. The episode then links this to [[LiaofanSixun|《了凡四训》]], Sima Guang's family teaching, and 《文昌帝君阴骘文》.

The wiki stores this as a source-scoped moral worldview. Its practical claim is stable inside the episode: hidden, cumulative, non-performative virtue is treated as better moral capital than visible reward-seeking. The stronger metaphysical claim that virtue directly causes recovery or fortune remains the host's interpretive frame.

## Key Claims

- Hidden good action matters precisely because it is not performed for immediate status.
- Bing Ji's quiet protection of Liu Xun is the episode's main historical example.
- Xiahou Sheng interprets Bing Ji's recovery as deferred repayment of hidden virtue.
- The frame extends virtue across descendants, not only the actor's own life.
- The episode ties the idea to Liaofan-style fate change and later family-instruction texts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept records the host's moral-causal worldview, not an empirical rule. Bing Ji's illness and recovery are not medically explained by the source, and the source does not prove that all hidden virtue leads to worldly protection or descendant benefit.
