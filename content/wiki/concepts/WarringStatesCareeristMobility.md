# Warring States Careerist Mobility / 乱世职业经理人式流动

Warring States careerist mobility / 乱世职业经理人式流动 is [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-38-gao-jianzhi-de-zhangyi-bu-yiban-lobhcpz679psy54k0iduccqkfcrn]]'s frame for reading [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]], [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]], and similar persuaders. The episode argues that readers should not explain every move only through fixed state loyalty or state interest. In the Warring States court market, personal功名, office, access, and survival also shape behavior.

The frame is most useful at [[WeiState|魏国]]. Wei has been damaged by [[QiState|齐国]] in the east and [[QinState|秦国]] in the west, yet [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]] can still accept figures who once served Qin or harmed Wei. The episode's answer is pragmatic: a weakened state may need mobile specialists who understand enemy systems, even when their loyalty is ambiguous.

Zhang Yi and Gongsun Yan show different versions of the same mobility. Gongsun Yan is presented as genuinely seeking a role in Wei, while Zhang Yi's Wei post is "part-time" because Qin still seems to keep space for him. The concept therefore tracks both opportunity and suspicion: the same portability that makes a strategist valuable also makes other courts doubt who he really serves.

## Key Claims
- Warring States persuaders can act as mobile political professionals rather than modern nation-state loyalists.
- Personal achievement and reputation can be causal, not incidental.
- State weakness can make former enemies useful if they bring knowledge of rival courts and military-political systems.
- Cross-court mobility creates trust problems because skill and access are portable while allegiance may remain unclear.
- The concept qualifies, but does not erase, [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] and [[LianhengStrategy|连横]]: policy labels coexist with individual career incentives.
