# Community Integrated Care / 社区综合照护

Community integrated care is the Japanese shift from hospital-complete care to community-complete care in [[vol-120-riben-yiliao-tixi-de-bengkui-yu-zhongsheng-yiyuan-yaopin-yibao-sanjiao-kunju-de-yizhong-jiefa-lp-p5p1cybbldphe0qajteggsitt]]. The episode links the 1997 long-term care insurance law and 2003 community care system to a broader attempt to join medical care, long-term care, life support, and elder security.

The concept extends [[AgingInPlaceSupportSystem]] from individual planning into public system design. Community care can delay institutionalization, reduce unnecessary acute-care stays, and make older people safer at home if clinics, pharmacies, nursing support, rehabilitation, and emergency pathways are actually available.

[[147-zaitan-riben-yiliao-yu-zhaohu-hangye-zhi-wo-ceng-zai-beihaidao-de-yiyuan-dang-hushi-lgokb-l-nvnqsstfzlqwhtnkqzhp]] adds the ward-facing bridge into this community system. [[QiongQiong|琼琼]] describes [[MedicalSocialWorkDischargeCoordination]] as the role that helps patients move from hospital to home, long-term care, welfare support, public assistance, or other institutions instead of leaving families to coordinate everything alone.

## Key Claims
- Community care is not a soft substitute for medicine; it reallocates hospital capacity toward people who need acute treatment.
- Aging-in-place depends on professional and local infrastructure, not only family willingness.
- Chronic disease and post-acute recovery require coordination across clinic, hospital, pharmacy, home care, and welfare systems.
- Hospital social work and discharge planning are practical mechanisms that make community care more than a policy slogan.
