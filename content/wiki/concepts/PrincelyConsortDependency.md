## Definition

Princely consort dependency / 诸侯妃妾依附风险 is the risk that a consort's security, dignity, and child's prospects depend less on her own agency than on the survival, desire, and succession politics of the prince or king who currently controls the household.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-2-yisheng-zuoguo-sanwei-zhuhou-feizi-gudai-chaomei-nuoji-de-beiqing-rensheng-lt06-as48q9qlpt_-dfdx_jsidjy|Hanji 382-2]] creates the concept through [[NuoJiWesternHan|淖姬]]. The episode repeatedly calls attention to her beauty and rare experience of being favored by three formal princes/kings, but its deeper pattern is dependency. Favor protects only while the protector holds household power.

The transition from [[LiuFeiJiangduKingWesternHan|刘非 / 刘飞]] to [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|刘建]] shows the most abrupt form of the risk. Liu Fei's affection and status give Nuo Ji a protective setting; his death creates an opening in which Liu Jian can seize her. Liu Jian's later collapse then transfers her exposure into [[LiuPengzuZhaoKingWesternHan|刘彭祖]]'s Zhao household.

The succession endpoint adds a second dependency layer. Nuo Ji's son [[LiuNaoziWesternHan|刘淖子]] is visible because of her later relationship with Liu Pengzu, but his chance at Zhao kingship can be undone by an external character report. Maternal favor and beauty do not translate into durable political protection when imperial selection and male kin testimony decide the heir.

## Key Claims

- Beauty and favor can increase a consort's value while also increasing exposure to powerful men's desires.
- A consort's protection can collapse immediately when the prince who values her dies.
- Household succession can turn a protected consort into an object seized by the next male authority.
- Royal favor does not guarantee the succession prospects of the consort's child.
- External imperial review and male kin testimony can override the consort branch's apparent advantage.
- The concept is about dependency under royal-household power, not a general claim that every consort lacks all agency.

Favor and value:

Protection collapse:

Succession dependency:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-2-yisheng-zuoguo-sanwei-zhuhou-feizi-gudai-chaomei-nuoji-de-beiqing-rensheng-lt06-as48q9qlpt_-dfdx_jsidjy|Hanji 382-2]] says Liu Pengzu fathers Liu Naozi with Nuo Ji, but Han Wudi's succession review rejects Liu Naozi after Nuo Ji's brother gives an adverse desire-based assessment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one compact episode and should not be generalized to every Western Han consort without additional cases. The source gives little direct evidence of Nuo Ji's own voice, consent, strategies, or emotional life. It also uses host interpretation for the claim that Liu Fei's burial object proves deep attachment. The useful claim is structural: in this source, consort security follows royal-household power shifts rather than stable personal rights.
