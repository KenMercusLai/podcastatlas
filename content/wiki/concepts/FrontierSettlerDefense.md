## Definition

Frontier settler defense / 移民实边式边防 is a state-building strategy that relocates households to fortified border communities and joins permanent residence, production, welfare, local knowledge, incentives, and military training into a standing local defense capacity.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-222-chaocuo-xiang-wendi-tichu-de-sanda-qimou-daodi-you-duo-niu-2-li4ia2aijr2ff4u_zwtx3dvl7jj0|Hanji 222 part 2]] presents [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]]'s design as an answer to [[NomadicCavalryRaidingAsymmetry|nomadic cavalry raiding asymmetry]]. Distant rotating troops pay mobilization and transport costs, arrive slowly, and do not know the terrain or enemy. Permanent settlers instead live at the point of danger and accumulate the information, relationships, and immediate stakes needed for rapid defense.

The design is therefore more than a military colony. The court must first build walls, ditches, houses, and tools, then absorb relocation risk through pardons, ranks, tax relief, clothing, grain, and supplies. Doctors, ritual specialists, marriage, births, deaths, housing, and humane officials make residence socially reproducible rather than temporary. Nested local units, locally trusted leaders, riding and archery training, and rewards for rescuing people or livestock convert the community into a force that can produce in peace and fight in crisis.

Its central logic is aligned attachment: settlers defend because household, kin, neighbors, land, property, and rewards are concentrated in the same place. That can reduce response delay, but it also makes success depend on competent administration, suitable recruits, sustained supply, and credible delivery of promises.

## Key Claims

- Permanent frontier residence can replace some of the delay, ignorance, and transport cost of rotating distant levies.
- Fortifications and material preparation must precede recruitment because households will not absorb danger and relocation costs without credible support.
- Family formation, health care, ritual life, housing, and burial are part of defense capacity because they determine whether settlement persists.
- Local mutual defense becomes more durable when kinship and neighborhood duty are reinforced by recoverable property and explicit rewards.
- Repeated training and stable small-group organization turn familiarity into coordination, recognition, and willingness to support one another.
- The design remains administration-dependent: unfit recruitment, abusive officials, broken supply, or delayed readiness can consume resources without creating defense.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on one popular-history source note describing a proposal and an imperial recruitment order. It does not establish settlement scale, retention, fiscal cost, combat performance, coercion hidden inside “voluntary” recruitment, or long-run effects on existing frontier populations. Adoption should not be confused with complete implementation or success.

The concept also differs from later military-agricultural [[TuntianSystem|屯田制]] arrangements. Both connect production to security, but the supplied evidence here centers household migration, community reproduction, mutual defense, and state-supported permanent residence rather than a general claim that all屯田 follows the same institutional design.

## What Changed

- Established the concept from Chao Cuo's integrated settlement, welfare, incentive, and militia proposal.
- Distinguished durable household-community defense from temporary troop rotation and from broader屯田 systems.
