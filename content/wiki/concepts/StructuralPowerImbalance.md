# Structural Power Imbalance / 结构性权力不平衡

## Definition
Structural power imbalance is an institutional asymmetry where one party can impose time, evaluation, punishment, attention, or risk on another party without facing the same practical exposure in return.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.214 uses school and workplace cases to show why conflict cannot be judged only by surface symmetry. A parent can call a teacher repeatedly because the teacher's professional role constrains retaliation; a supervisor can pressure a worker because evaluation, assignment, and termination authority sit above the worker.

The concept matters because it changes what counts as a realistic response. Mirroring the same conduct may feel satisfying in drama, but in real institutions it can expose the weaker party to disciplinary, legal, or reputation risk. Protection therefore requires reading the power map before choosing silence, documentation, complaint, exit, or confrontation.

Episode 347 extends the concept from institutions into everyday speech. It accepts that preaching often occurs in unequal roles such as parent-child, teacher-student, boss-worker, elder-younger, male-female, and success-failure relationships. Its qualification is equally important: power structure is a necessary reading frame, not a total explanation for every disliked opinion or intrusive comment.

The Jin Yong teacher roundtable adds a relationship boundary. An active teacher can shape evaluation, access, protection, reputation, and future opportunities, so affection does not make a romance structurally equal. A relationship after instruction is not automatically legitimate either; the relevant test is whether authority and dependency have genuinely ended and both people now act as mature, free, equal adults.

## Key Claims
- The same action can have different meaning when one actor has formal authority, role protection, or institutional backing and the other does not.
- Parent-teacher, teacher-student, professor-student, and boss-worker relationships all need asymmetry-aware analysis before assigning equal responsibility.
- Dramatic reversal can reveal harm emotionally, but real-life retaliation may create new liability for the already-exposed party.
- Structural imbalance can make victims self-blame because the harm is framed as evaluation, discipline, education, or performance management.
- Effective response starts by identifying who controls time, records, evaluation, complaint channels, contracts, and future opportunities.
- Structural analysis should not flatten every uncomfortable statement into oppression; individual clumsiness, fear, and weak listening can also produce harmful communication.
- Mutual affection does not by itself cancel teacher-student asymmetry while authority, evaluation, protection, or resource control remains active.

- Workplace hierarchy: [[vol-214-weishenme-yue-youxiu-de-ren-yue-rongyi-zai-zhichang-bei-pua-dao-huaiyi-ziji-cong-tiequan-jiaoyu-liaodao-tiequan-zhichang-lmx4ewrw7kvcqpyh-q3bur35ybgz]] treats supervisor pressure as partly rooted in accountability and labor-use structures rather than only individual cruelty.
- Academic hierarchy: [[vol-214-weishenme-yue-youxiu-de-ren-yue-rongyi-zai-zhichang-bei-pua-dao-huaiyi-ziji-cong-tiequan-jiaoyu-liaodao-tiequan-zhichang-lmx4ewrw7kvcqpyh-q3bur35ybgz]] includes graduate-student stories of public humiliation by teachers, extending the imbalance beyond ordinary employment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Power imbalance does not prove that every complaint by the less powerful party is accurate or complete. The concept is a risk-reading tool: it says that formal roles, dependency, evidence channels, and retaliation costs must be considered before judging a conflict as mutual, personal, romantic, or harmless. Episode 347 adds the inverse caution: when structure is real, it still should not erase individual responsibility, interpersonal incompetence, or the possibility that some disliked claims are ordinary disagreement rather than domination. The literary roundtable offers an ethical principle, not jurisdiction-specific professional rules or a universal test for when former authority has ended.

## What Changed
- Added active teacher-student romance as a case where mutual feeling cannot neutralize authority and dependency.
- Added genuinely ended authority and mature equal standing as necessary, though not sufficient, conditions for reassessment.
