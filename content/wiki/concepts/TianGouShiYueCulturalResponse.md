## Definition

Tian Gou Shi Yue cultural response / 天狗食月文化应对 is the folk interpretation and ritual handling of a lunar eclipse as if the Moon were being eaten by a destructive heavenly dog. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-566-buxiang-jianglin-weihe-guren-weiju-tiangou-shiyue-lkukbe4hu71ti_lmasca-mpbffxj|Hanji 566]] uses it to explain how fear of unfamiliar sky events could become both popular ritual action and elite political omen language.

## Current Synthesis

The concept sits between astronomical observation, folklore, and disaster politics. The source presents lunar eclipse fear as a human response to visible cosmic disorder: people saw the Moon's shape change and explained it through a vivid agent, the "天狗," whose imagined attack made the event feel morally and ritually urgent.

At the folk level, the response is practical-symbolic. The episode says people might beat gongs and drums, raise torches, and try to drive the heavenly dog away so the Moon could become whole again. The wiki treats this as ritualized uncertainty management, not as evidence that the practice affected the eclipse.

At the elite level, the same eclipse sits near [[LiXunWesternHan|李寻]]'s disaster interpretation for [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]. The Moon can symbolize yin-associated powers such as empresses, ministers, and princes; lunar change therefore becomes a language for warning about inner-court disorder and external-relatives' influence.

The source closes by giving a modern physical account: solar and lunar eclipses occur when the Sun, Earth, and Moon align so that one body blocks light. That explanation narrows the causal claim while preserving the cultural point: "天狗食月" records awe, fear, imagination, and early attempts to make the unknown intelligible.

## Key Claims

- Lunar eclipses could be interpreted through a vivid folk-agent story rather than through orbital mechanics.
- Ritual noise and light functioned as an enacted response to uncertainty and fear.
- Elite omen reading could turn the same lunar event into criticism of court order and yin-associated political power.
- Modern astronomical explanation can correct physical causation without erasing the cultural meaning of the old response.

- Ritual response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-566-buxiang-jianglin-weihe-guren-weiju-tiangou-shiyue-lkukbe4hu71ti_lmasca-mpbffxj|Hanji 566]] says ancient people beat gongs and drums and lit torches to drive away the heavenly dog.
- Political interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-566-buxiang-jianglin-weihe-guren-weiju-tiangou-shiyue-lkukbe4hu71ti_lmasca-mpbffxj|Hanji 566]] says Li Xun reads repeated lunar changes as signs of yin disorder involving inner court, ministers, and outer relatives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently source-scoped to one podcast episode. The source does not provide a full history of eclipse records, regional ritual variation, or the transmission history of the "天狗" image. Its modern explanation corrects the physical mechanism but does not settle how all ancient communities understood or practiced eclipse response.
