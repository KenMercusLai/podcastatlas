# Export-Control Alliance Durability / 出口管制联盟耐久性

## Definition
Export-control alliance durability is the ability of a coalition to keep members enforcing common restrictions when they bear unequal trade losses, receive different compensation, or suspect that partners are capturing exceptions.

## Current Synthesis
[[2b2e96d8aea7-2b2e96d8aea7]] argues that shared security language is insufficient to sustain controls. Durability depends on domestic enforcement, compensation for foregone trade, credible burden sharing, and limits on self-serving exceptions. The [[CoCom]] examples suggest that controls loosen when exports and employment become more valuable than alliance benefits or when the rule-maker appears to sell what it prevents allies from selling. Applied to AI, this is a hypothesis about coalition incentives, not proof that modern chip, compute, API, and model controls will repeat the same trajectory.

## Key Claims
- Coalition enforcement weakens when member losses are concentrated but strategic benefits are diffuse.
- Aid, market access, security guarantees, or other compensation can support compliance but may not outweigh profitable trade indefinitely.
- Exceptions undermine legitimacy when members believe the coalition leader reserves commercial opportunities for itself.
- Economies with deeper domestic supply chains may be harder to coerce through upstream technology denial than import-dependent states.
- Intangible model access adds identity, cloud, intermediary, and copying problems that physical-goods regimes did not face in the same form.

## Evidence
Compensation and defection:
- [[2b2e96d8aea7-2b2e96d8aea7]] uses U.S. aid, British and Japanese pressure to relax China controls, and employment-sensitive export interests to explain member behavior.

Exceptions and credibility:
- [[2b2e96d8aea7-2b2e96d8aea7]] argues that U.S. product exceptions during rapprochement with China made uniform sacrifice harder to defend.

Weak enforcement signal:
- [[2b2e96d8aea7-2b2e96d8aea7]] treats the [[ToshibaMachine]] outcome as evidence that late-stage CoCom penalties no longer strongly deterred commercial defection.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not measure aid against trade gains, distinguish formal rules from national implementation in detail, or prove that commercial incentives alone caused policy changes. Security threats, diplomacy, technology diffusion, legal constraints, and alliance politics may also explain relaxation. Modern semiconductor chokepoints and cloud infrastructure can be more observable than generic software copying, so the historical analogy should not be treated as a forecast.
