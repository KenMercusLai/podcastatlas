## Definition

Apocalyptic prophecy and crisis legitimation is the mechanism by which a warning gains political and moral authority when later events resemble its predicted punishment, while unmet extensions and failed public tests can reverse that authority.

## Current Synthesis

[[GirolamoSavonarola|Savonarola]] warned of punishment, plague, hunger, war, and a sword over Italy before [[CharlesVIIIOfFrance|Charles VIII]] invaded. Because informed observers could already see French intervention as plausible, the episode rejects pure miracle without reducing the warning to irrelevance. Political perception, vivid religious imagery, and subsequent events worked together.

Apparent fulfillment changed Savonarola's civic position. The Medici fled, his delegation helped negotiate with Charles, and Florence avoided a sack. Many Florentines could therefore treat him as prophet and mediator with God; the episode argues that he probably shared this belief.

Legitimation became fragile when prophecy expanded beyond the observed event. Charles did not renew Italy or restore Pisa, hunger and plague continued, and demand for bread displaced the appeal of repentance. The failed ordeal by fire then denied the crowd the decisive proof it expected. Crisis-confirmed prophecy creates authority through interpretation, but also expectations whose failure can concentrate blame.

## Key Claims

- A forecast can gain authority when a later crisis resembles its imagery and timing.
- Political foresight and sincere religious interpretation can coexist.
- Successful mediation after apparent fulfillment strengthens public credibility.
- Followers may extend a partly confirmed warning into a broader providential program.
- Continuing misery can shift demand from moral explanation toward material relief.
- A failed public proof ritual can reverse legitimacy faster than slow doctrinal dispute.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is grounded in one interpretive episode and does not establish supernatural prediction, conscious fraud, or a universal model. Savonarola's authority also depended on Medici failure, French force, negotiation, constitutional reform, faction, and economic conditions. Exact sermon chronology, public belief, and private certainty require specialist study.

## What Changed

- Created a mechanism connecting apparent prophetic fulfillment to temporary civic legitimacy.
- Added unmet extension, material suffering, and failed public proof as routes of reversal.
