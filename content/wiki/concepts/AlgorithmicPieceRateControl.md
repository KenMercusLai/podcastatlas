# Algorithmic Piece-Rate Control / 算法化计件控制

## Definition
[[AlgorithmicPieceRateControl]] is a work regime in which systems divide labor into measurable units and combine per-item pay with expected speed, accuracy thresholds, peer comparison, ranking, and corrective training.

## Current Synthesis
In the Yonghe case, clients tested how many items a worker should complete per hour, while the system displayed co-workers' progress and audited quality. Workers could be questioned for moving too slowly, suspected of poor quality when moving too fast, or removed for retraining when accuracy fell below a project threshold.

This makes digital annotation resemble a production line even though the physical setting is an office. Measurement does not resolve the tension between speed and accuracy; it distributes that tension to workers and makes both dimensions continuously enforceable.

## Key Claims
- Fine-grained digital tasks allow output and quality to be monitored simultaneously.
- Target ranges can discipline workers from both directions: too slow signals inefficiency and too fast can trigger quality suspicion.
- Peer-visible progress turns measurement into social comparison and self-intensification.
- Public failure lists and retraining convert quality control into reputational and emotional pressure.
- Advanced technology can coexist with traditional factory-like labor discipline.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide the scoring software, exact thresholds, error distribution, payroll records, or customer rationale. Quality controls can be necessary for reliable data; the evidence supports the reported burden and incentive structure, not the claim that all measurement is illegitimate or every annotation workplace uses the same regime.

## What Changed
- Added a named mechanism joining piece rates, speed bands, accuracy gates, comparison, and retraining.
- Added the two-sided pressure created when both unusually slow and unusually fast work attract scrutiny.
