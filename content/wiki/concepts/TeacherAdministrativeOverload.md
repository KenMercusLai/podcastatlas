# Teacher Administrative Overload / 教师事务性过载

## Definition
Teacher administrative overload is the accumulation of meetings, duty shifts, invigilation, marking, event work, documentation, and student incidents around classroom instruction until preparation, recovery, bodily care, or family time is compressed.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] rejects the simple picture of teaching as lessons plus long holidays. Young teachers may combine dense timetables with monitoring, activities, examinations, gate duty, and immediate student issues. Voice strain, missed meals, limited toilet breaks, early arrival, and conflict with caring for one's own child make workload a health and boundary issue rather than only a time-management problem.

## Key Claims
- Teaching load cannot be inferred from classroom hours alone.
- Fragmented duties reduce preparation and recovery even when each task appears small.
- Young teachers may receive disproportionate coordination and duty work because they are assumed to have more available time or energy.
- Occupational strain includes voice use, hydration, meals, sleep, toileting, and psychological vigilance.
- Reform that returns supervision or tutoring functions to schools must account for staffing, time, and compensation.

## Evidence
- Hidden duty stack: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] lists meetings, duty, invigilation, marking, activities, and gate supervision around ordinary teaching.
- Bodily cost: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] describes continuous morning lessons and student issues leaving little time to drink, eat, or use the toilet, alongside voice damage and health concerns.
- Policy spillover: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] argues that school-based supervision after “double reduction” may erode holidays or extend duty without attractive compensation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not measure workload across schools, career stages, regions, or subjects, and some duties may be seasonal or shared differently elsewhere. It also does not establish that every reform necessarily increases net workload. The concept requires task, staffing, schedule, and recovery evidence rather than assuming that any non-teaching duty is illegitimate.
