## Definition
Urban wildlife conflict management / 城市野生动物冲突治理 is the set of public, community, and design responses used when wild animals in cities begin to injure people, damage property or crops, suffer from unhealthy human contact, trigger fear, or exceed a habitat's practical carrying capacity.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] presents conflict management as a portfolio problem. The episode discusses feeding bans, fines, volunteer persuasion, official controlled feeding, contraception, sterilization, relocation, hunting, complaint-triggered removal, animal welfare objections, cat-food rack design, tree-species adjustment, and ecological corridors. No tool is sufficient alone because each species has different reproduction, risk, public sympathy, mobility, and habitat patterns.

The governing standard is not simply "protect animals" or "protect humans." The source repeatedly shows that the two aims are linked: if animals become habituated, overfed, sick, or too visible in the wrong places, human fear and injury risk can push institutions toward more violent control. Better management tries to keep distance, reduce attractants, and preserve movement routes before crisis politics narrows the options.

## Key Claims
- Management should begin with attractant control and public norms because late-stage removal or killing is ethically and politically harder.
- Species biology matters: contraception may be more plausible for slower-breeding monkeys than for fast-breeding boars.
- Legal bans need implementable authority; rules can shift norms even when immediate enforcement is weak.
- Animal welfare and human safety are not separate ledgers when a method such as dog-assisted hunting may injure animals, disrupt ecosystems, or create secondary danger.
- Urban planning can prevent some conflicts by changing food sources, movement passages, and habitat interfaces.

## Evidence
- Early attractant control: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] describes bans on feeding, volunteer persuasion, controlled official feeding, and cat-food platforms as ways to reduce risky contact.
- Species-specific tools: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] contrasts Hong Kong monkey contraception with boar fertility and later selective killing of repeatedly harmful boars.
- Legal and institutional limits: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] notes Shanghai's explicit feeding ban and Guizhou's ban alongside the Qianling Mountain management office's lack of enforcement power.
- Welfare and secondary risk: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] discusses boar trapping, fencing, dog pursuit, stabbing, animal welfare criticism, disturbance to other animals, and possible follow-on harm.
- Planning interface: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] links bird droppings, boar dispersal, animal passages, and low-cost retrofits to urban design.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not settle which intervention is best in each locality. Some numbers and thresholds are reported as uncertain, and the humane answer may differ between a park macaque population, a rural crop-damage boar population, a residential raccoon dog group, and low-threat balcony birds. Complaint-driven control can also undercount ecological value and overcount fear.

## What Changed
- Added a governance concept for the practical policy bundle around urban wildlife conflict.
- Made enforcement authority, species biology, animal welfare, and urban design part of the same management frame.
