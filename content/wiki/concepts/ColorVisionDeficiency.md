# Color-Vision Deficiency / 色觉障碍

## Definition
Color-vision deficiency is reduced or altered ability to distinguish colors, encompassing partial and complete, congenital and acquired forms rather than one uniform state of “seeing no color.”

## Current Synthesis
The episode combines clinical classification with [[YinXinNutritionGuest|尹鑫]]'s first-person account. Congenital red-green deficiency is presented as common relative to other forms and linked to X-chromosome inheritance, while acquired color changes can follow damage to cones or other visual pathways.

Functional impact must be assessed by task. A person may perceive hues differently yet distinguish traffic signals or perform particular work through position, brightness, contrast, labels, and learned cues. Other people asking “what color is this?” does not measure the full condition, and confusing color discrimination with finger counting reveals a category error between color vision and acuity.

Color-enhancing lenses may alter spectral contrast for some users, but the episode does not establish restoration of typical color perception or suitability for every form. Accessible systems should therefore avoid color-only communication and provide redundant cues.

## Key Claims
- Color-vision deficiency contains multiple congenital and acquired types with different functional effects.
- Red-green deficiency is not equivalent to total monochromatic vision.
- Diagnosis alone does not determine whether a person can perform a particular visual task.
- Position, contrast, symbols, text, and other redundant cues reduce dependence on color alone.
- Color-enhancing lenses may change contrast for selected users but should not be described as a universal correction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide formal color-testing results, occupational standards, device validation, or independent evidence for lens effectiveness. The cited prevalence and sex difference are condensed educational claims. Acquired or changing color perception can signal eye or neurological disease and belongs with qualified assessment rather than self-diagnosis.

## What Changed
- Established a spectrum-and-function account that separates color discrimination from acuity and total blindness.
