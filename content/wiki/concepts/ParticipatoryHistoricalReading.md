## Definition
Participatory historical reading is the practice of entering historical people, situations, and interpretive disputes rather than treating history as fixed conclusions to memorize.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 591-2 turns "how to read history efficiently" away from speed alone. The episode says readers need a source map, but also warns against "dead reading": history becomes useful when readers imagine concrete people and events, notice that historians have viewpoints, and accept that readers in different eras or life stages may argue over the same episode. This makes disagreement part of historical vitality rather than only a problem to eliminate.

## Key Claims
- Effective history reading combines source orientation with emotional and situational entry into events.
- Different readers can draw different interpretations from the same historical episode because life experience and historical moment matter.
- Argument over interpretation is not a failure of history reading; it can keep the past alive and morally demanding.
- Participatory reading still needs source discipline, otherwise interpretation becomes free projection.

- Reader plurality: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-591-2-ruhe-geng-gaoxiao-di-dudong-lishi-gudai-biannian-jianzan-juequ-qiannian-fengliu-yunyan-lruv48fgfpo7u0tjsqb264k0y7yq|Hanji 591-2]] says historians, different readers, and people in different periods can form different views of one event.
- Life guidance frame: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-591-2-ruhe-geng-gaoxiao-di-dudong-lishi-gudai-biannian-jianzan-juequ-qiannian-fengliu-yunyan-lruv48fgfpo7u0tjsqb264k0y7yq|Hanji 591-2]] closes by linking historical reading to noticing human weaknesses, life blind spots, and practical wisdom.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Participatory reading is not license for unconstrained presentism. The same episode begins with source and genre orientation, so participation should follow evidence, text form, and source boundaries.
