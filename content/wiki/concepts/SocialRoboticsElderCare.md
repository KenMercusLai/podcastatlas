# Social Robotics in Elder Care

## Definition
Social robotics in elder care is the use of physically present robots to support older adults through conversation, companionship, cognitive engagement, affect sensing, reminders, or caregiver/family support while respecting dignity, consent, privacy, and safety.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep-42-when-ai-meets-robotics-building-machines-that-care]] makes [[RyanSocialRobot|Ryan]] the central case. The source argues that an elder-care robot is not evaluated only by whether it can talk, walk, or execute tasks. It must produce measurable benefits in engagement, wellness, staff support, or family support while staying affordable, reliable, safe, secure, and transparent.

The strongest synthesis is care augmentation. Ryan may reduce loneliness or support cognitive interaction, and residents may form meaningful bonds, but the source keeps the robot inside a human care system rather than treating it as a replacement for caregivers or family members.

## Key Claims
- Elder-care social robots are judged by wellness, engagement, support, dignity, and trust, not only by technical capability.
- Physical presence changes the stakes because users may respond to the robot as a companion rather than as software.
- Consent, guardian involvement, coercion checks, privacy, and transparency are core deployment conditions when users may have cognitive impairment.
- LLM-enabled conversation increases the robot's range but requires hallucination, overconfidence, compute, and cloud-dependence controls.
- Emotional bonds can be beneficial engagement signals and also create responsibilities around withdrawal, expectation management, and honesty.
- Robots should augment caregivers and families rather than replace human care.

## Evidence
- Ryan case evidence: [[ep-42-when-ai-meets-robotics-building-machines-that-care]] describes Ryan as a social companion robot for older adults with loneliness, depression, cognitive impairment, and dementia.
- Pilot evidence: [[ep-42-when-ai-meets-robotics-building-machines-that-care]] reports nearly 900 hours of interaction in clinical pilots.
- Ethics evidence: [[ep-42-when-ai-meets-robotics-building-machines-that-care]] describes institutional review, resident consent, guardian consent for some residents, coercion checks, privacy, transparency, and trust.
- Attachment evidence: [[ep-42-when-ai-meets-robotics-building-machines-that-care]] says some residents laughed with Ryan and became upset when researchers removed it after a study period.
- Adoption evidence: [[ep-42-when-ai-meets-robotics-building-machines-that-care]] says adoption depends on measurable benefits plus affordability, hardware cost, reliability, safety, and security.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish broad clinical efficacy, cost-effectiveness, long-term retention, or scalable staffing outcomes. Its evidence is strongest as a product and research-program case. Emotional attachment should not be read as automatic benefit because vulnerable users also need clear expectations, human support, and careful handling when a robot leaves.
