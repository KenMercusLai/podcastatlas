## Definition
香港足球外援升级 / Hong Kong football foreign-player escalation is the source's account of how professional Hong Kong clubs moved from regional import experiments toward higher-profile foreign recruitment, before foreign-player limits and league ecology constrained the model.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents Hong Kong as an early Asian professional destination whose import market changed over time. Early foreign players came mainly from Southeast Asia and Korea, with mixed sporting results. By 1970, Rangers' Scottish contacts brought a more explicit European professional channel, and by the early 1980s [[SeikoFootballClub|Seiko]] and Bulova pushed the market toward bigger names, including recent World Cup players.

The escalation was not self-sustaining. The source says Bulova opposed reducing foreign-player numbers because it relied on imports to compete with Seiko, then exited after the rule changed. The broader lesson is league ecology: richer teams need credible opponents, so spending races and import limits are political economy questions as much as talent questions.

## Key Claims
- Foreign-player sourcing initially leaned on Southeast Asia and Korea before European professional routes became more visible.
- Hong Kong's early professional status made it attractive to players from places where professional options were thinner.
- Seiko and Bulova raised the import-player ceiling in the early 1980s by pursuing better-known foreign players.
- Foreign-player limits could protect local-player space but also damaged clubs whose competitive model depended on imports.
- A spending race in football cannot simply eliminate competitors because league value depends on rival survival.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide full wage data, transfer records, or import-player performance metrics. It also distinguishes Seiko's exit from foreign-player limits, saying that exit was more likely an owner-cycle decision than a direct all-Chinese-squad-policy consequence.
