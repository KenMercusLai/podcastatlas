## Definition
Morning-market urbanism is the organization of food access, low-cost trade, social contact, and temporary work through a market that occupies shared urban space for a tightly bounded part of the day.

## Current Synthesis
The source shows that a morning market survives when several systems align: consumers value price, freshness, variety, and early bulk buying; vendors accept a short operating window in exchange for accessible space; streets, parking lots, compounds, or underpasses can change function; and managers make arrival, noise, waste, traffic, and departure governable. The market's apparent spontaneity therefore rests on repeated coordination, while its compressed hours intensify both commerce and social recognition.

## Key Claims
- Time is part of the infrastructure: a strict opening and closing window lets one place support several urban uses.
- Market continuity depends on practical alignment among customer habits, vendor costs, street form, transport, and management capacity.
- Morning markets can provide re-employment and low-entry-cost livelihoods, but their history should not be reduced to informality alone.
- Regular contact makes markets social infrastructure as well as distribution infrastructure.
- Governance is constitutive rather than external: stall placement, closures, noise controls, sanitation, and resident complaint handling shape whether the market remains viable.

## Evidence
- Timed space reuse: [[ep287-yiqi-guang-zaoshi-xunzhao-beifang-zui-xianhuo-de-zaochen-lpuox4b-b02k-awt-hsjuph-froi]] describes Shenyang streets and underpasses and Taiyuan parking or antique-market spaces returning to other uses after trade.
- Economic fit: [[ep287-yiqi-guang-zaoshi-xunzhao-beifang-zui-xianhuo-de-zaochen-lpuox4b-b02k-awt-hsjuph-froi]] connects northern stock-up habits, perceived low prices and freshness, avoided indoor stall fees, and Shenyang's layoff history to continued demand on both sides of the market.
- Social density: [[ep287-yiqi-guang-zaoshi-xunzhao-beifang-zui-xianhuo-de-zaochen-lpuox4b-b02k-awt-hsjuph-froi]] records dialect calls, tastings, familiar vendors, household news, credit requests, complaints, and returnees' sense of home.
- Managed coexistence: [[ep287-yiqi-guang-zaoshi-xunzhao-beifang-zui-xianhuo-de-zaochen-lpuox4b-b02k-awt-hsjuph-froi]] describes timed road closure, designated positions, saw-cutting or cushioning to reduce meat-cutting noise, cleanup, and unresolved resident conflict.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source compares only three cities and gives Shenyang the fullest treatment. Climate and northern stock-up habits do not by themselves explain market survival; regulation, road design, vendor economics, alternative retail, and local history also matter. A well-managed temporary market can still disturb residents, exclude large-volume shoppers through transport rules, or become less useful when visitor food stalls displace produce.

## What Changed
- Established the concept from a bounded three-city northern China comparison.
- Made temporal coordination and reversible space use central to the market's infrastructure.
- Kept livelihood, sociality, and governance in one model rather than treating the market as only retail.
