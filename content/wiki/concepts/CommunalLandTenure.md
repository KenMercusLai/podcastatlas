# Communal Land Tenure

[[CommunalLandTenure]] is the land-governance pattern [[sand-heists-and-property-rights-in-the-caribbean-summer-school]] explores through [[Barbuda]]. In the source, Barbudans describe land as something residents use collectively rather than buy, sell, lease, or pay taxes on in the ordinary private-property sense.

The episode does not treat communal tenure as automatically inefficient or automatically ideal. It can preserve community control, food security, environmental value, and limits on overuse, but it can also create weak incentives for costly individual investments when private returns are hard to capture.

## Key Claims
- Communal tenure can be a property institution, not simply the absence of property.
- Collective control may protect a shared resource from outside sale or overexploitation.
- The investment problem appears when banks, builders, or households need individualized claims to finance improvements.
- The Barbuda case shows how communal tenure is [[PathDependence]]: it reflects slavery, abolition, colonial neglect, and later legal formalization under the [[BarbudaLandAct]].
