# 骑墙式战略暴露 / Fence-Sitting Strategic Exposure

骑墙式战略暴露 / fence-sitting strategic exposure enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1027-jingzhou-zhizhu-liubiao-weihe-nan-cheng-bazhu-ll-ebguqllsf-5ycvpdogihzn3lj|Hanji 1027]], where [[LiuBiao|刘表]] accepts title recognition from the Li-Guo-controlled court while staying close to [[YuanShao|袁绍]]. The source accepts that this kind of posture can protect a region in the short run, but argues that it becomes exposure when the actor lacks initiative, reliable internal integration, or a clear trigger for decisive alignment.

The episode's mechanism is concrete. [[DengXiLateHan|邓羲]] sees the two-sided posture as a risk, but Liu Biao treats the warning as nitpicking. Later, [[ZhangXianLateHan|张献]] and [[HuanJieLateHan|桓阶]] turn [[SouthernJingzhouLateHan|southern Jingzhou]]'s local commandery fissures into an attachment to [[CaoCao|曹操]], forcing Liu Biao to react through [[HuangZu|黄祖]]'s navy and [[WuxiMan|五溪蛮]] support rather than shaping the Guandu-era field directly.

This concept qualifies [[DefensiveLocalGovernance|保境安民式守成治理]]. A ruler may keep local peace and still become strategically exposed if internal elites can choose outside patrons and if neutrality merely makes the territory a buffer between stronger powers.

## Key Claims
- Neutrality can be a short-term safety strategy without being a durable state-building strategy.
- Fence-sitting becomes dangerous when an actor has no clear decision rule for leaving the middle position.
- Local fissures make neutrality exploitable because outside powers can attach discontented subregions without conquering the whole territory.
- Corrective advisers are early warning signals; losing them makes the ruler's strategic self-image harder to challenge.
- Defensive governance must be paired with internal integration and strategic initiative if the region is not to become another actor's battlefield.
