## Definition

Empty reputation-reality gap / 空誉违实 is the condition where public fame, elite praise, or cultivated scarcity is treated as evidence of worth even though conduct, achievement, and responsibility have not been tested enough to support that reputation.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 862 grounds the concept through [[HuangYunLateHan|黄允]] and [[JinWenjingLateHan|晋文经]]. The problem is not fame by itself. Fame becomes risky when it starts doing institutional work before it is backed by observable conduct. Huang Yun's reputation cannot withstand his wife's public exposure of hidden faults, while Huang Yun and Jin Wenjing together show how untested fame can influence official selection opinion when high officials seek their praise or criticism.

[[FuRongLateHan|符融]] gives the concept its clearest diagnostic language by warning [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] that the two men have unproven conduct and an empty reputation that violates reality. The current synthesis is therefore two-sided: reputation can be useful evidence when grounded in conduct, but it becomes a governance hazard when scarcity, self-presentation, and elite attention substitute for proof.

## Key Claims

- Reputation is politically useful only when tied to observable conduct, achievement, or responsibility.
- Elite praise can amplify a weak reputation before anyone has tested the person's character.
- Scarcity and refusal can simulate depth when public actors mistake inaccessibility for worth.
- Empty reputation can distort appointment decisions by making untested private judgment look authoritative.
- Public exposure collapses empty reputation faster than ordinary criticism because it supplies concrete contrary evidence.
- The concept is a negative qualification on moral reputation, not a rejection of all reputation-based judgment.

## Evidence

- Talent-praise risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-862-ta-yin-yiju-hua-xiuqi-zuizhong-shenbai-minglie-lrvklai847fvlp1l28coofzgbsyq|Hanji 862]] says Guo Tai praises Huang Yun but warns that reputation will be lost without strict self-correction.
- Concrete exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-862-ta-yin-yiju-hua-xiuqi-zuizhong-shenbai-minglie-lrvklai847fvlp1l28coofzgbsyq|Hanji 862]] says Huang Yun's wife publicly exposes many hidden faults after he discards her for a hoped-for Yuan-family marriage.
- Scarcity-based prestige: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-862-ta-yin-yiju-hua-xiuqi-zuizhong-shenbai-minglie-lrvklai847fvlp1l28coofzgbsyq|Hanji 862]] says Huang Yun and Jin Wenjing refuse office, claim illness in Luoyang, and become hard-to-access prestige figures.
- Selection distortion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-862-ta-yin-yiju-hua-xiuqi-zuizhong-shenbai-minglie-lrvklai847fvlp1l28coofzgbsyq|Hanji 862]] says officials seek the two men's opinions for appointment choices.
- Explicit diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-862-ta-yin-yiju-hua-xiuqi-zuizhong-shenbai-minglie-lrvklai847fvlp1l28coofzgbsyq|Hanji 862]] records Fu Rong's warning to Li Ying that their fame does not match known conduct.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not be used to dismiss every form of reputation. Other wiki pages show reputation as a real political resource when grounded in action, sacrifice, enforcement, or public trust. Hanji 862 narrows the warning to reputation that has become institutionally consequential without enough conduct evidence.
