# Chivalric Tournament Culture / 骑士比武文化

Chivalric tournament culture is the episode's bridge between older formal combat and later [[HonorDuelCulture|honor duel culture]]. In [[78-xifang-juedou-shi-chujian-ba-weile-rongyu-687731000]], tournaments are described as celebration, spectacle, aristocratic identity work, and military training after the tenth century.

The episode uses [[HenryIIOfFrance|Henry II of France / 亨利二世]] to show the danger inside this spectacle. Even ceremonial combat could maim or kill because the performance of courage depended on real weapons, bodily risk, and public recognition.

## Key Claims
- Tournament combat connects military skill to noble status and public honor.
- Spectacle does not remove danger; it can intensify pressure to continue despite risk.
- Court and festival settings make violence socially visible rather than private.
- Later honor dueling inherits tournament ideals of courage, fairness, weapon competence, and audience recognition.
- The same culture can train restraint and normalize unnecessary risk at the same time.
