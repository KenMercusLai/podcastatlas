# Stoic Control Allocation

## Definition
Stoic control allocation is the practice of separating what is substantially chosen, what cannot be controlled, and what is only partly influenceable so that effort and success criteria can be placed where agency remains.

## Current Synthesis
[[WilliamIrvine]] presents a trichotomy rather than a simple control/no-control split. Values and choices sit closest to direct agency; events such as sunrise sit outside it; competition, health, work, and most social outcomes mix preparation with other people, chance, and conditions. The practical move is not to pretend mixed outcomes are irrelevant, but to pair an external aim with an internal one: prepare well, use available resources, and act consistently with chosen values even when victory is not guaranteed.

The framework is future-facing. Completed history cannot be reversed, but the next action may still be shaped. That can reduce futile replay and preserve action after a setback. Yet control categories are not fixed or morally neutral: illness, coercion, poverty, discrimination, disability, trauma, and power differences can sharply narrow available choices, while collective action can sometimes make an apparently fixed condition changeable.

## Key Claims
- Useful planning distinguishes chosen action, external fact, and mixed outcome rather than treating everything as controllable or helpless.
- Internal goals can preserve agency when external success depends on opponents, weather, institutions, health, or chance.
- Preparation and resource use are legitimate success criteria without making outcome irrelevant.
- Attention to the next influenceable action can reduce unproductive replay of completed events.
- Acknowledging injustice is compatible with choosing how to respond; agency does not erase responsibility for the harm.
- Control is context-dependent and can be expanded or constrained by support, power, resources, institutions, and collective action.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The categories can be hard to identify in real time, and calling a response controllable may overstate agency under acute emotion, mental illness, trauma, disability, danger, dependency, or material deprivation. Internal goals can protect effort but can also rationalize exclusion or shift responsibility from institutions to harmed people. Historical anecdotes do not establish intervention effects. The framework should complement, not replace, safety planning, care, accommodation, accountability, resource provision, or organized attempts to change external conditions.
