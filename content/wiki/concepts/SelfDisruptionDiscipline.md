# Self-Disruption Discipline

## Definition

Self-disruption discipline is the operating habit of treating the next platform shift as a threat even when the current business is successful, then redirecting or cannibalizing that business early enough to build the replacement.

## Current Synthesis

Altos, Templafy, and BlackBerry separate three parts of self-disruption. Leaders must recognize the shift, build a replacement suited to the new platform, and win authorization for the transition. [[AltosComputer]] missed personal computers and Ethernet after succeeding in microcomputers. [[Templafy]] rebuilt rather than making [[Omnidocs]] superficially cloud-compatible. [[JimBalsillie]] recognized BlackBerry's handset threat and developed an open messaging alternative, but the board did not approve the move. Strategic foresight is therefore necessary but insufficient when structure, identity, and decision power remain attached to the incumbent business.

## Key Claims

- A company that wins one disruption wave can become psychologically and organizationally attached to that wave.
- Strong current metrics can delay recognition that the platform or business model is changing underneath the company.
- Self-disruption requires willingness to cannibalize or redirect the incumbent business before competitors force the transition.
- A real transition may require a new product and organizational foundation rather than cosmetic adaptation.
- Recognition, replacement capability, and institutional authorization are distinct requirements.
- Moving too far ahead of customer or board readiness can still fail even when the long-run direction is plausible.

## Evidence

### Failure to recognize the next wave

- [[tsr-s3-ronconwayep1-v4final-tsr-s3-ronconwayep1-v4final]] says Altos disrupted minicomputers but later dismissed personal computers and missed the Ethernet shift.

### Rebuilding for a new platform

- [[enterprise-sales-with-no-product-landing-a-big-four-customer]] says Templafy was built on a new cloud foundation rather than making Omnidocs merely “cloud-ish,” and later treated AI as another organizational reset.

### Recognition without authorization

- [[the-blackberry-problem-the-mistakes-series-the-blackberry-problem-the-mistakes-series]] says Balsillie developed a cross-platform BBM service in response to Apple and Google but failed to win board support for abandoning hardware.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Self-disruption is not automatically superior to improving an incumbent business. Platform forecasts can be wrong, premature transitions can destroy profitable assets, and a replacement may lack product quality, distribution, economics, or customer readiness. The BlackBerry case is especially counterfactual: BBM scale and carrier interest make the services proposal plausible, but do not prove success. The evidence consists of participant retrospectives rather than comparative outcome research.

## What Changed

- Added BlackBerry as a case of recognizing disruption and building an alternative without securing institutional authorization.
- Split self-disruption into recognition, replacement capability, and approval rather than treating foresight as sufficient.
- Preserved the risk of moving prematurely or overclaiming a counterfactual pivot.
