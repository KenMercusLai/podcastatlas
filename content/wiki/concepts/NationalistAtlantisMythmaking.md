# Nationalist Atlantis Mythmaking

## Definition

Nationalist Atlantis mythmaking is the use of [[Atlantis]] to manufacture a flattering national, racial, or civilizational origin story.

## Current Synthesis

The source shows Atlantis becoming politically movable. Once interpreters stop treating Plato's location and moral structure as binding, Atlantis can be shifted toward America, Sweden, northern Europe, or an Aryan homeland depending on the identity project at hand.

The key mechanism is not only bad geography. Nationalist Atlantis theories select the version of antiquity that gives a present community deeper age, higher prestige, or civilizational priority. The theory then reorganizes textual details, place names, racial language, and archaeological gaps around the desired origin story.

## Key Claims

- Nationalist Atlantis theories relocate Atlantis to make the myth useful for present identity.
- The same story can be made colonial, Swedish, Aryan, or racial depending on who needs the origin.
- The identity payoff encourages selective reading of [[Plato]], place names, and ancient remains.
- Nationalist mythmaking can overlap with esotericism, but its core function is prestige and origin.
- The source treats this as a reception-history pattern rather than a credible location method.

## Evidence

- Swedish Atlantis example: [[315-atlantis-legacy-of-the-lost-empire-part-2-glt2807981945]] describes Olof Rudbeck arguing that Atlantis was Sweden and that Uppsala was its capital.
- Americas and origin speculation: [[315-atlantis-legacy-of-the-lost-empire-part-2-glt2807981945]] traces early modern attempts to connect Atlantis to America, Aztlan, Aztec origins, Maya origin stories, or Atlantean survivors.
- Thule and Aryan homeland: [[315-atlantis-legacy-of-the-lost-empire-part-2-glt2807981945]] presents [[ThuleSociety]] and radical German nationalist thinkers as shifting Atlantis northward because an Atlantic location was less useful for their mythology.
- Race-coded diffusion: [[315-atlantis-legacy-of-the-lost-empire-part-2-glt2807981945]] records [[IgnatiusDonnelly]]'s racialized Atlantis language as part of the same origin-making tradition.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Not every political use of an ancient story is nationalist pseudohistory, and the source distinguishes utopian literary reuse from racial-national origin claims. The concept names the narrower case where Atlantis is relocated or reinterpreted to make a modern people look older, purer, central, or civilizationally primary.
