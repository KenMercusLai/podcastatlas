# Fertilizer Supply-Chain Shock

## Definition
Fertilizer supply-chain shock is the risk that energy disruption, maritime chokepoints, damaged production facilities, or financing stress can sharply reduce fertilizer availability and transmit into crop yields, food prices, and farmer solvency.

## Current Synthesis
The episode turns fertilizer into a hidden strategic input. [[DavidFriedberg|David Friedberg]] argues that nitrogen fertilizer is tightly linked to natural gas, that [[StraitOfHormuz]] disruption can affect a large share of global fertilizer flow, and that farmers in poorer regions may face acute access problems while U.S. farmers face input-cost pressure. The broader synthesis is that agricultural resilience requires energy, production capacity, logistics, reserves, and repair timelines, not only crop management.

## Key Claims
- Nitrogen fertilizer is an energy-linked input because natural gas is central to production.
- Maritime chokepoints can affect fertilizer and food systems even when public attention focuses on oil.
- Fertilizer shortages can hit low-income farming regions through availability and affordability at the same time.
- Production facilities are slow to repair or build, so shocks can outlast the initial conflict.
- Strategic resilience may require excess local capacity or reserves for agricultural inputs.

## Evidence
Energy-fertilizer linkage:
- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-spacex-ipo-iran-war-fallout-quantum-bitcoin-hack-the-space-opportunity-40728755]] explains nitrogen fertilizer through natural gas dependence.

Chokepoint exposure:
- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-spacex-ipo-iran-war-fallout-quantum-bitcoin-hack-the-space-opportunity-40728755]] says a major share of global nitrogen fertilizer passes through the [[StraitOfHormuz]] and links war escalation to urea-price pressure.

Food-system consequence:
- [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-spacex-ipo-iran-war-fallout-quantum-bitcoin-hack-the-space-opportunity-40728755]] warns that farmers in Africa and South Asia may lack fertilizer while U.S. corn farmers face high input costs and low corn prices.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode gives a strategic-risk frame rather than a verified global balance sheet. Actual impact depends on inventories, contracts, rerouting, substitution among fertilizer types, domestic gas access, crop choice, subsidies, credit, weather, and government intervention.

## What Changed
- Added fertilizer as a standalone supply-chain shock concept tied to energy, Hormuz, and food-system resilience.
