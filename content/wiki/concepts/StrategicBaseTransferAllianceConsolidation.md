## Definition

Strategic base transfer alliance consolidation / 战略基地转移式联盟巩固 is the source-bounded pattern in which a stronger coalition member lets a weaker partner control territory, revenue, and strategic depth so that the partner becomes a durable counterweight against a common enemy.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1118-chibi-zhanbai-hou-caocao-zui-pa-fasheng-shenme-shi-lqbhhqtfhghodojnxksa3ozt9yqz|Hanji 1118]] applies the pattern to [[SunQuan|孙权]], [[LuSu|鲁肃]], and [[LiuBei|刘备]] after [[RedCliffsBattle|赤壁之战]]. The episode's central distinction is between winning one battle and changing the structure of later competition. Red Cliffs checks [[CaoCao|曹操]] militarily; allowing Liu Bei to hold a larger [[Jingzhou|荆州]] base gives the Liu side territory, revenue, recruitment, and depth that portable reputation and followers alone could not provide.

The transfer is therefore not presented as charity. It strengthens an anti-Cao partner and makes divide-and-defeat strategy harder, but it also creates a more autonomous ally with its own territorial interests. Alliance consolidation and future distribution conflict can arise from the same decision.

## Key Claims

- A coalition becomes more durable when its weaker member gains resources that let it survive without continuous rescue.
- Territory changes alliance capability through revenue, recruitment, administration, logistics, and strategic depth, not through symbolic possession alone.
- Strengthening a partner can raise deterrence against a common enemy while reducing the stronger member's control over that partner.
- The strategic effect comes from the postwar distribution of capacity, so it should not be collapsed into the battlefield outcome that preceded it.
- A base-sharing decision can consolidate cooperation and simultaneously plant a later dispute over ownership, return, or expansion.

## Evidence

- Territorial empowerment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1118-chibi-zhanbai-hou-caocao-zui-pa-fasheng-shenme-shi-lqbhhqtfhghodojnxksa3ozt9yqz|Hanji 1118]] says Sun Quan accepts Lu Su's advice to let Liu Bei hold Jingzhou territory, giving Liu Bei a durable foothold after repeated base loss.
- Enemy response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1118-chibi-zhanbai-hou-caocao-zui-pa-fasheng-shenme-shi-lqbhhqtfhghodojnxksa3ozt9yqz|Hanji 1118]] treats Cao Cao's reported shock as recognition that Sun Quan and Liu Bei are becoming harder to isolate and defeat separately.
- Alliance tradeoff: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1118-chibi-zhanbai-hou-caocao-zui-pa-fasheng-shenme-shi-lqbhhqtfhghodojnxksa3ozt9yqz|Hanji 1118]] credits Lu Su's anti-Cao logic while also locating the decision inside a contested postwar territorial settlement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source uses the conventional shorthand “借荆州,” but the territorial sequence, jurisdiction, and later ownership claims were more complicated than a simple bilateral loan. The episode's tactical-versus-strategic contrast is analytically useful, not proof that Cao Cao feared only this development or that it alone produced the tripartite order. A strengthened partner can later become a competitor, so the mechanism is compatible with [[InterestStructuredAllianceFragility|利益结构式联盟脆弱性]] rather than evidence of permanent trust.

## What Changed

- Established the concept from the post-Red-Cliffs Sun-Liu territorial settlement.
- Distinguished battlefield victory from the redistribution of durable strategic capacity.
- Made partner empowerment and later alliance conflict simultaneous consequences rather than opposites.
