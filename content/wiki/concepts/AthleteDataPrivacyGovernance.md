# Athlete Data Privacy Governance

Athlete data privacy governance is the set of privacy, ownership, release, security, and labor rules around sports performance data. [[ep-12-insightful-conversation-with-a-football-analytics-professional]] grounds the concept through [[AnnaDSouza|Anna D'Souza]]'s discussion of event data, tracking data, wearable data, and other records tied to human athletes.

The source distinguishes sports analytics from generic business analytics because player data can describe bodies, movement, workload, health-adjacent performance, and career value. Anna points to Europe as a stricter privacy environment, notes that U.S. rules can vary by state, and mentions California as more similar to Europe.

Governance also includes who can release, distribute, host, or monetize data. The source mentions caution around leagues such as [[MajorLeagueSoccer|MLS]], possible collective-bargaining treatment of data ownership and sharing, and cybersecurity dependencies on platforms or hosting providers such as [[AmazonWebServices|AWS]] or [[Oracle]].

## Key Claims
- Sports data can be personal because it tracks human bodies, movement, and performance.
- Privacy constraints differ across jurisdictions and can affect how analysts collect or use data.
- Data release and distribution need clear authority, especially at league or federation level.
- Collective bargaining can become a venue for defining athlete data ownership and use.
- Cybersecurity is part of privacy governance when sports data is stored on external platforms.
