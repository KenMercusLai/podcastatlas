# Judicial Duel / 司法决斗

Judicial duel is the legal-combat form discussed in [[78-xifang-juedou-shi-chujian-ba-weile-rongyu-687731000]]. The episode presents it as a formal procedure in which parties could resolve criminal accusations, property disputes, inheritance conflicts, or other serious claims by fighting under the authority of a king, lord, or court-like setting.

Its religious background is [[TrialByOrdeal|trial by ordeal]]: if God was believed to reveal truth through dangerous tests, then combat could be imagined as another truth-disclosing procedure. The hosts still call the system bloody and primitive, but they also note its relative procedural meaning compared with ordeals where an accused person had little active chance to contest the result.

The concept matters for the wiki because it complicates [[LawMoralityBoundary]]. Judicial duel shows a world where law did not yet sharply separate truth, divine judgment, public ritual, and controlled violence.

## Key Claims
- Judicial duel is not private brawling; it is combat authorized by a legal or political superior.
- Its legitimacy depends on a worldview where divine judgment can appear through physical outcome.
- It can include proxies for people unable to fight, which makes it more procedural than spontaneous.
- Its decline shows legal authority moving away from bodily combat toward evidence, courts, and state-managed procedure.
- Judicial duel is a bridge toward [[HonorDuelCulture]], but honor dueling later loses much of the formal judicial claim.
