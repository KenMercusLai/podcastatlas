# Hypertension Diagnosis and Individualized Targets / 高血压诊断与个体化目标

## Definition
Hypertension diagnosis and individualized target-setting is the process of distinguishing transient high readings from sustained disease, assessing cause and overall risk, and choosing a control goal that reflects comorbidity, age, organ function, treatment tolerance, and current clinical guidance.

## Current Synthesis
The source rejects two opposite shortcuts: one high reading is not automatically a chronic diagnosis, but not measuring or feeling well does not prove normal pressure. Repeated measurements under interpretable conditions establish the pattern, after which clinicians consider primary versus potentially secondary hypertension, existing cardiovascular or renal disease, diabetes, pregnancy, age-related pressure patterns, and evidence of organ damage. The episode uses numerical thresholds to teach the framework, but its durable claim is that diagnostic cutoffs and treatment targets serve different purposes and that the latter must be individualized.

## Key Claims
- Temporary exertion, emotion, fatigue, or poor technique can elevate one reading, so diagnosis depends on a repeated pattern rather than an isolated number.
- Primary hypertension is common, while a smaller secondary-hypertension branch warrants cause-directed evaluation when clinical context suggests it.
- Diagnostic thresholds and treatment goals are not interchangeable; a patient with cardiovascular, renal, metabolic, or pregnancy-related risk may need a different goal and monitoring plan.
- Age-related arterial stiffness can widen pulse pressure, while younger adults may show a different systolic-diastolic pattern without implying that a drug selectively lowers only one component.
- Risk stratification should connect the measured pattern to comorbidity and [[HypertensionTargetOrganDamage|target-organ damage]], not only label the number.
- Home and clinical measurements need qualified interpretation when rhythm disturbance, symptoms, severe elevation, or technique problems affect reliability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This framework comes from one public-education episode, not a current clinical guideline or complete diagnostic workup. Its numerical thresholds, category labels, prevalence estimates, secondary-cause examples, diabetes target, pregnancy statements, and age patterns should not be converted into self-diagnosis or self-treatment. Guidelines can change, and ambulatory monitoring, laboratory testing, imaging, medication history, pregnancy status, frailty, symptoms, and clinician judgment can alter interpretation.

## What Changed
- Created a framework separating transient elevation, formal diagnosis, risk stratification, and individualized treatment goals.
