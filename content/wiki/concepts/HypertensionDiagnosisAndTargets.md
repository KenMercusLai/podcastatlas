# Hypertension Diagnosis and Individualized Targets / 高血压诊断与个体化目标

## Definition
Hypertension diagnosis and individualized target-setting is the process of distinguishing transient high readings from sustained disease, assessing cause and overall risk, and choosing a control goal that reflects comorbidity, age, organ function, treatment tolerance, and current clinical guidance.

## Current Synthesis
The sources reject two opposite shortcuts: one high reading is not automatically a chronic diagnosis, but not measuring, feeling well, or finding another explanation for the original symptom does not prove normal pressure. Repeated home, clinical, or ambulatory measurements under interpretable conditions establish the pattern, after which clinicians consider primary versus potentially secondary hypertension, existing cardiovascular or renal disease, diabetes, pregnancy, age-related pressure patterns, and evidence of organ damage.

Younger adults can show predominantly diastolic elevation or a rapid rise that deserves attention to vascular resistance, heart rate, metabolic context, medicines, sleep apnea, renal or endocrine causes, and other secondary possibilities. VOL.224 adds a retrospective safety-netting case: resolving dizziness after fixing a drafty room may explain the discomfort, but it does not erase repeated elevated readings or prior 24-hour monitoring. Numerical thresholds teach the framework, while the durable claim is that symptoms, diagnostic cutoffs, cause assessment, risk stratification, and treatment targets are distinct decisions.

## Key Claims
- Temporary exertion, emotion, fatigue, or poor technique can elevate one reading, so diagnosis depends on a repeated pattern rather than an isolated number.
- Primary hypertension is common, while a smaller secondary-hypertension branch warrants cause-directed evaluation when clinical context suggests it.
- Diagnostic thresholds and treatment goals are not interchangeable; a patient with cardiovascular, renal, metabolic, or pregnancy-related risk may need a different goal and monitoring plan.
- Age-related arterial stiffness can widen pulse pressure, while younger adults may show isolated diastolic elevation without implying that it is benign or that a drug selectively lowers only one component.
- Risk stratification should connect the measured pattern to comorbidity and [[HypertensionTargetOrganDamage|target-organ damage]], not only label the number.
- Home and clinical measurements need qualified interpretation when rhythm disturbance, symptoms, severe elevation, or technique problems affect reliability.
- Symptom resolution or an alternative explanation for discomfort does not invalidate a prior abnormal measurement pattern; the pattern should be carried forward for reassessment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This framework comes from public-education episodes, not a current clinical guideline or complete diagnostic workup. Their numerical office and home thresholds, category labels, prevalence estimates, secondary-cause examples, diabetes target, pregnancy statements, age patterns, isolated-diastolic risk figures, and guest history should not be converted into self-diagnosis or self-treatment. Guidelines can change, and ambulatory monitoring, laboratory testing, imaging, medication history, pregnancy status, frailty, symptoms, and clinician judgment can alter interpretation. VOL.224 does not confirm that the guest currently has hypertension.

## What Changed
- Added younger-adult isolated diastolic elevation and rapid-onset disease to the cause-assessment and risk framework.
- Expanded the secondary-cause examples while keeping all thresholds and risk estimates source-scoped.
- Added symptom-resolution and prior-abnormal-history boundaries through the guest's abandoned ambulatory-monitoring case.
