# Cognitive Trust Allocation / 认知信任分配

## Definition
Cognitive trust allocation is the ability to decide which cognitive tasks to keep, which to delegate, to whom, under what risk level, and with what ongoing checks.

## Current Synthesis
The episode argues that modern media literacy cannot stop at suspicion. In information-rich environments, a person cannot personally verify medicine, law, climate, news, philosophy, culture, film formats, and relationships from first principles. Some delegation is therefore rational, but only when the delegate has a relevant knowledge advantage and the decision's stakes are understood.

Trust allocation is dynamic. A film blogger may be a reasonable guide for choosing a theater format because the risk is low and the expertise is bounded. The same logic does not automatically make a relationship influencer a reliable guide for intimate decisions, because the domain, risk, incentives, and evidence standard differ.

## Key Claims
- Delegating some knowledge work can be rational rather than lazy.
- The scarce skill is not only doubt but distribution: assigning trust to the right person for the right task.
- Risk level matters; low-stakes format choice and high-stakes intimate or medical advice should not use the same delegation threshold.
- Trust should be revisable when the authority's task fit, incentives, or performance changes.
- Trying to verify everything personally can be less rational than using well-chosen intermediaries.

## Evidence
- Scarcity of attention: [[mianfei-de-quanwei-fufei-de-tiyan-cmtwkgbmq01wt01tp3mgj78e4]] says cognition consumes limited mental energy and cannot cover all domains.
- Delegation frame: [[mianfei-de-quanwei-fufei-de-tiyan-cmtwkgbmq01wt01tp3mgj78e4]] asks when there is sufficient reason to hand part of a cognitive task to someone else.
- Task-specific example: [[mianfei-de-quanwei-fufei-de-tiyan-cmtwkgbmq01wt01tp3mgj78e4]] contrasts delegating theater-format choice to a movie blogger with not easily delegating emotional life to an emotional-advice influencer.
- Intermediary role: [[mianfei-de-quanwei-fufei-de-tiyan-cmtwkgbmq01wt01tp3mgj78e4]] notes the paper's interest in epistemic intermediaries as a way to improve authority-audience matching.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not give a complete procedure for auditing every authority. Trust allocation can fail when incentives are hidden, audiences overgeneralize expertise, or intermediaries are optimized for visibility rather than reliability. The concept therefore depends on [[DigitalPublicSphereEpistemicAuthority]] and [[ScientificSkepticism]] rather than replacing them.

- Created the concept to capture the episode's final claim that distribution of trust is as important as suspicion.
