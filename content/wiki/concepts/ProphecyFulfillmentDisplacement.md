# 预言兑现错位 / Prophecy Fulfillment Displacement

预言兑现错位 / prophecy fulfillment displacement is the pattern where a prediction is heard correctly at the surface level but attached to the wrong person, route, or mechanism. The wiki names it from [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-135-fanwaipian-2-nvxiangshi-yin-kanming-nengli-bei-liubang-fenghou-lgu1fr1zrzmc0o72wtbpxmajb7s|Qinji 135 番外篇2]] and the earlier [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-1-papa-dalian-lishishang-shuangwen-de-kaishan-bizu-lr0h-kfeguvatattnj-mz8fgvnc|Qinji 117-1]] Bao Ji branch.

The clearest case is [[WeiBao|魏豹]] hearing [[XuFuPhysiognomist|许负]] say that [[BaoJi|薄姬]] will bear a future emperor. Wei Bao treats the prophecy as news about his own restoration and dynastic prospects, but the source fulfills it through [[LiuBang|刘邦]] and [[LiuHeng|刘恒]], not through Wei.

The same structure appears in the starvation predictions. [[DengTong|邓通]] looks too rich to starve while [[LiuHeng|Emperor Wen]] protects him, but his wealth depends on a patron who can die. [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] looks too noble to starve, but the source routes hunger through imprisonment and voluntary refusal of food rather than ordinary poverty.

This concept records narrative logic, not proof that prediction is reliable. Its value is in tracking how stories make fate, interpretation, patronage, succession, and institutional reversal interact.

## Key Claims
- A prophecy can be "fulfilled" by shifting the implied subject, patron, dynasty, or causal path.
- Political listeners tend to read predictions through their own ambition or security needs.
- Patronage can make a fate look impossible in one reign and possible in the next.
- The wiki should preserve the distinction between a source's narrative payoff and historical proof of divinatory accuracy.
