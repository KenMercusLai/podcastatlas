# Self-Similarity In Intimacy / 亲密关系中的自我相似

## Definition
Self-similarity in intimacy is the relationship preference for a partner who feels very much like oneself, whether through shared background, taste, values, emotional style, or the comfort of known defects.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence separates self-similarity as a felt safety preference from similarity as a measured predictor of attraction. The gender-swap conversation treats a highly similar partner as attractive because recognition, shared values, and predictable flaws can lower explanation burden. The Eastwick episode qualifies that intuition by arguing that measured actual similarity is generally weak at predicting whether two real people will click.

The stronger synthesis is therefore not that people should choose copies of themselves. Similarity can help when it supports truth, recognition, and daily ease, but it can also amplify shared blind spots. What often matters more is [[PerceivedSimilarityInRelationships]]: the pair-specific feeling that "we have a lot in common," which may emerge after attraction and shared experience rather than before them.

## Key Claims
- Similarity can be attractive because it promises recognition before persuasion.
- Known defects can feel safer than unknown incompatibilities, especially in imagined partner choice.
- Shared values reduce daily conflict only when shared weaknesses do not become mutually reinforcing.
- Actual measured similarity is not a reliable complete predictor of whether two people will click.
- Similarity works best with [[RelationshipNeedDiversification]] so one partner is not asked to supply every form of sameness, novelty, companionship, and care.

## Evidence
- Felt safety and recognition: [[chuantai-ni-chi-xiangcai-ma-nvshengban-ni-yuanyi-he-xingzhuan-zhihou-de-ziji-zai-yiqi-ma-555771723]] has XiaoShi and KeZi initially prefer a gender-swapped self because common background, taste, values, and known defects make intimacy feel lower-risk.
- Blind-spot risk: [[chuantai-ni-chi-xiangcai-ma-nvshengban-ni-yuanyi-he-xingzhuan-zhihou-de-ziji-zai-yiqi-ma-555771723]] warns that similarity can become an intimate [[InformationCocoon]] when shared weaknesses reinforce each other.
- Empirical qualification: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] says actual measured similarity is generally poor at predicting whether people will click.
- Felt similarity distinction: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] defines perceived similarity as a general sense of commonality that people can attach to what matters once they like someone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The gender-swap source is playful and reflective rather than empirical. The Eastwick source is research-oriented but the note does not supply methods or datasets, so its similarity claims remain source-scoped. The concept should not erase genuine deal-breakers such as children, safety, values, or life constraints; it only rejects a simple formula where matched traits automatically predict attraction or relationship quality.
