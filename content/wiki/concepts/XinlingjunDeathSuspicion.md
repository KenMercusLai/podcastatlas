# 信陵君非正常死亡疑云 / Xinlingjun Death Suspicion

信陵君非正常死亡疑云 / Xinlingjun death suspicion is the source-scoped hypothesis from [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-106-jingren-faxian-xinlingjun-de-feizhengchang-siwang-lsj3kd2tb4tqtuvhwvsdwkh-h5el]] that [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]] may not have died naturally. The episode starts from [[Shiji|《史记》]]'s sequence: Xinlingjun and [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]] die in the same year, with Xinlingjun dying first.

The episode does not deny the earlier account preserved in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-103-2-simaqiande-idol-weihe-siyu-jiuse-lvmqg1ojmka9pqagfhgvo4albpjx|Qinji 103-2]], where Xinlingjun is slandered, removed from command, claims illness, and spends his last years in wine and women. Instead it treats that account as the surface layer and asks whether the final step may have been political: Wei Anxi Wang, near death himself, could have removed the one figure whose prestige might threaten [[WeiJingminWang|魏增]]'s succession.

The concept is useful because it ties character biography to late-state survival. If the hypothesis is kept only as gossip, it is weak; if framed as source-scoped inference, it clarifies how [[CounterintelligenceRumorWedge|反间流言楔入]], [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]], and royal succession security can converge around a single death notice.

## Key Claims
- The death order is the evidentiary trigger: Xinlingjun dies before Wei Anxi Wang in the same year.
- The proposed motive is succession security for Wei Zeng, not simple personal hatred.
- The hypothesis extends rather than replaces the "frustrated withdrawal" reading from Qinji 103-2.
- It should remain source-scoped because the episode gives inference and circumstantial reasoning, not decisive proof.
