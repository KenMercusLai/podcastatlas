# Target-Market Validation

## Definition

Target-market validation is the requirement that demand, payment, and adoption evidence come from the customer segment whose constraints and behavior the company actually intends to serve, rather than from easier adjacent adopters alone.

## Current Synthesis

[[Revo]] shows why real adoption can still answer the wrong market question. Smaller startups could accept a broad replacement because they lacked deeply embedded systems, large user populations, revenue-operations leadership, and enterprise requirements. Mid-market prospects faced much higher switching and organizational costs. Startup demand therefore validated that the product could be useful, but not that the intended mid-market customer would adopt the same scope or packaging. Validation must cover the target segment's buying process, installed base, risk tolerance, migration burden, and willingness to change behavior.

## Key Claims

- Positive customer behavior is only decisive for the market conditions under which it occurred.
- Adjacent early adopters can conceal switching costs, governance needs, procurement friction, or organizational resistance in the intended segment.
- Friendly praise is weaker than payment, but payment from the wrong segment can still mislead strategy.
- Validation should test product value and adoption path separately because buyers may want the outcome while rejecting the proposed migration.
- Founders need direct discovery to understand which segment assumptions a signal actually supports.
- Segment-specific evidence can justify changing packaging without abandoning the underlying product architecture.

## Evidence

Adjacent-adopter mismatch:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] says smaller startups adopted Revo broadly because they had fewer established systems and enterprise requirements, while mid-market buyers resisted replacing Salesforce or HubSpot-centered stacks.

Weak conversational evidence:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] describes acquaintances and early prospects who said they liked the idea but declined when asked to pay, invoking [[TheMomTest]] problem of leading questions and politeness.

Packaging correction:
- [[2bbd2ffd]] reports that Revo treated the mismatch as a commercial-packaging problem and moved toward individual “golden workflows” for the intended market.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Adjacent segments are not useless: they can reveal product defects, implementation needs, willingness to pay, and unexpected markets. A startup may also rationally change its target market when another segment shows stronger economics. The concept only warns against transferring evidence across segments without accounting for material differences in workflow, installed systems, budget, governance, and switching cost.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Revo's contrast between startup adoption and mid-market replacement behavior.
