# Expert Uncertainty Communication

## Definition
Expert uncertainty communication is the practice of stating what evidence supports, how wide the plausible range remains, and where judgment exceeds direct knowledge when audiences expect a simple authoritative answer.

## Current Synthesis
Banerjee describes Nobel recognition as creating “oracular status”: people may defer to an expert beyond the domain or precision the evidence warrants, while the expert may find that authority tempting. At the same time, silence is not automatically responsible when decisions or lives may depend on informed judgment.

The communication problem is therefore not solved by adding a disclaimer after a point estimate. Audiences may remember the central number and discard the range, source conditions, or alternative explanations. Responsible communication should bind the headline to its uncertainty, distinguish measured findings from extrapolation, name the relevant population and setting, and make clear what evidence would change the conclusion.

## Key Claims
- Prestige can expand perceived authority beyond the evidence or the expert's domain.
- Audiences often retain a central estimate more readily than its uncertainty range.
- Experts face a real tradeoff between overconfident speech and unhelpful silence.
- Uncertainty should shape the headline claim rather than appear only as a detachable caveat.
- Domain, population, setting, alternatives, and revision conditions make uncertainty more actionable.

## Evidence
- Prestige effect - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] records Banerjee's description of Nobel-linked “oracular status” as dangerous and tempting.
- Range neglect - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] says audiences may remember a central estimate while overlooking the interval around it.
- Speech-versus-silence tension - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] frames expert communication as a choice under consequences, not a simple rule to avoid public judgment.
- Experimental boundary - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] connects uncertainty to the possibility that researchers framed an incomplete set of alternatives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers Banerjee's reflection rather than a systematic study of uncertainty formats or audience comprehension. More caveats can obscure urgent guidance, while false precision can mislead; the appropriate balance depends on stakes, audience, decision window, and evidence quality. Expertise remains valuable even when it is bounded, and uncertainty does not imply that all claims are equally plausible.

## What Changed
- Created a focused framework for prestige, range neglect, and bounded expert speech.
- Distinguished useful uncertainty from silence, false equivalence, and detachable caveats.
