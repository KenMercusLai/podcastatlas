# Energy-Aware Work Design / 能量感知型工作设计

## Definition
Energy-aware work design treats productive capacity as a changing combination of attention, activation, emotion, recovery, task fit, and avoidable friction rather than as a fixed number of schedulable hours.

## Current Synthesis
The two sources converge on self-observation rather than a universal productivity schedule. [[no-214-dinengliangren-de-dianchi-shiyong-zhinan-gkwridonkrmva2ctsqrp2-zn]] uses a battery model: reduce drain by protecting task contexts, batching small obligations, simplifying collaboration, and removing repeated friction; increase charging through interest, ability, feedback, and believable possibility. [[scim3646778859-scim3646778859]] adds [[DanielEk|Daniel Ek]]'s rejection of generic rules about wake time, meetings, sleep amounts, and work intensity in favor of learning when specific activities, exercise, recovery, or changes of scene raise or lower energy.

The combined model does not ask people to maximize output or imitate a permanently high-energy person. It asks which demands are necessary, which are artifacts of switching or coordination, which states call for recovery or a different task, and which rhythms make meaningful work sustainable.

## Key Claims
- Clock time is an incomplete planning unit because equal hours can carry different attention, activation, and recovery capacity.
- Long-context work benefits from uninterrupted blocks and task-type batching when the role permits it.
- Small open loops should be deliberately batched and closed so they do not become continuous background load.
- State matching can mean recovery, changing scenery, exercise, or choosing a lower-friction task rather than forcing identical output every day.
- Better tools, collaboration design, or selective money-for-time tradeoffs can reduce recurring energy loss.
- Sustainable effort is more likely when skill, interest, feedback, meaning, and future possibility reinforce one another.

## Evidence
- Attention and friction - [[no-214-dinengliangren-de-dianchi-shiyong-zhinan-gkwridonkrmva2ctsqrp2-zn]] describes one-take work, delayed message checking, task-type days, simpler collaboration, and concentrated cleanup of small obligations.
- Motivation and state - [[no-214-dinengliangren-de-dianchi-shiyong-zhinan-gkwridonkrmva2ctsqrp2-zn]] links useful activation to task fit, feedback, recovery, and bounded experiments.
- Individual rhythm - [[scim3646778859-scim3646778859]] has Ek reject universal schedules, describe a failed polyphasic-sleep trial, and identify workouts and time away as useful inputs to energy and ideas.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These are two creators' retrospective heuristics, not evidence that people can always choose their schedules or that fatigue is solved by workflow design. Employment, caregiving, disability, illness, depression, ADHD, financial scarcity, emergency duties, and collaborative dependencies can sharply restrict autonomy. Sleep experimentation is not medical guidance, paying to save time assumes disposable income, and avoiding every disliked task can transfer cost to others.

## What Changed
- Added individualized schedule, exercise, rest, and scenery-change evidence from Ek.
- Clarified that energy management supports ambition through self-observation rather than a single high-performance routine.
