# Energy-Aware Work Design / 能量感知型工作设计

## Definition
Energy-aware work design treats productive capacity as a changing combination of attention, activation, emotion, recovery, task fit, and avoidable friction rather than as a fixed number of schedulable hours.

## Current Synthesis
In [[no-214-dinengliangren-de-dianchi-shiyong-zhinan-gkwridonkrmva2ctsqrp2-zn]], [[LiuFei|刘飞]] uses a battery metaphor to join two design problems. “Reduce drain” means protecting full task contexts, batching messages and small obligations, simplifying collaboration, choosing tools or travel conditions that reduce repeated friction, and matching demanding work to usable states. “Increase charging” means finding work supported by interest, ability, feedback, and believable future possibility.

The model is therefore not a request to maximize output or imitate a permanently high-energy person. It asks which demands are necessary, which are artifacts of switching or coordination, which states call for recovery or a different task, and which activities create enough meaning or feedback to sustain action. It overlaps with [[TaskSwitchingResidue]], [[CoordinationTax]], and [[MotivationRewardEffortCalculation]] but keeps conservation and replenishment in one practical frame.

## Key Claims
- Clock time is an incomplete planning unit because equal hours can carry different attention, activation, and recovery capacity.
- Long-context work benefits from uninterrupted blocks and task-type batching when the role permits it.
- Small open loops should be deliberately batched and closed so they do not become continuous psychological background load.
- State matching can mean resting during a genuinely poor state or choosing a lower-friction, intrinsically engaging task rather than forcing identical output every day.
- Better tools, connectivity, travel conditions, or outsourced work can save energy when recurring time and frustration exceed the monetary cost.
- Sustainable effort is more likely when skill, interest, feedback, and future possibility reinforce one another.

## Evidence
- Attention conservation - [[no-214-dinengliangren-de-dianchi-shiyong-zhinan-gkwridonkrmva2ctsqrp2-zn]] describes “one take” writing and recording, delayed message checking, task-type days, and concentrated cleanup of small obligations.
- Friction reduction - [[no-214-dinengliangren-de-dianchi-shiyong-zhinan-gkwridonkrmva2ctsqrp2-zn]] links simpler collaboration, better frequently used tools, and selective money-for-time tradeoffs to lower cognitive and emotional drain.
- State and motivation - [[no-214-dinengliangren-de-dianchi-shiyong-zhinan-gkwridonkrmva2ctsqrp2-zn]] describes resting or changing task type on poor days and treating interest, competence, feedback, and possibility as charging inputs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is one creator's retrospective heuristic, not evidence that people can always choose their schedule or that fatigue is solved by workflow design. Employment, caregiving, disability, illness, depression, ADHD, financial scarcity, emergency duties, and collaborative dependencies can sharply restrict autonomy. Paying to save time assumes disposable income, and avoiding every disliked task can transfer cost to other people or weaken necessary commitments.

## What Changed
- Created a combined conservation-and-replenishment frame for attention, state, friction, and motivation.
