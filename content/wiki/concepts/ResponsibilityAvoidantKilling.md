# Responsibility-Avoidant Killing / 避责式杀害

Responsibility-avoidant killing / 避责式杀害 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-66-zhongguo-lishishang-naxie-diwang-shi-bei-huohuo-e-si-de-2-ltsw2eilkegs5yjtrp6gkhrmsxsg]] draws from the end of [[ShaqiuCrisis|沙丘之变]]. [[ZhaoChengWarringStates|赵成]] and [[LiDui|李兑]] do not directly strike [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王 / 赵主父]], because open regicide would carry heavy political and moral risk. Instead, they empty the palace, prevent him from leaving, deny food, and wait until starvation finishes what direct violence would have done.

The concept is not an acquittal. The point is that actors can avoid the visible form of killing while still arranging the conditions that make death predictable. In the episode, the distinction between "not daring to kill" and "not letting him live" is the political center of the ending.

This mechanism belongs near [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]] because it appears after a failed power exit. Once Zhao Cheng and Li Dui have killed [[ZhaoZhang|赵章]] and surrounded Zhao Wuling Wang, retreat would expose them to later revenge. Their solution converts self-preservation into a delayed, deniable death sentence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-69-bikan-gudai-diwang-qipa-sifa-da-pandian-lukvjsktg-pgfjhizvypwmdmw-13]] places the starvation case inside a broader [[RulerNonNaturalDeathTypology|帝王非命死法类型]]. The episode lists starvation beside poisoning, strangling, and weapon killing as remembered ways rulers die by non-natural means, with [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]] serving as one of the clearest wiki cases where indirect deprivation is politically arranged.

## Key Claims
- Avoiding a direct blow does not remove responsibility when the outcome is deliberately arranged.
- Political actors may prefer indirect lethal conditions when direct killing would create a forbidden label such as regicide.
- Delay can distribute blame across silence, guards, logistics, and time.
- Responsibility-avoidant killing depends on control over movement, supplies, and witnesses rather than only weapons.
- The pattern is especially likely when a former ruler retains symbolic legitimacy but loses practical rescue capacity.
- Starvation cases belong in a broader typology of ruler deaths, but this concept tracks the responsibility problem when death is arranged through control of food, exits, and guards.
