# Gynecological Symptom Triage / 妇科症状分诊

## Definition
Gynecological symptom triage is the episode's practical framework for interpreting pain, bleeding, discharge, cycle change, and abdominal symptoms through onset, timing, recurrence, progression, severity, and associated signs before deciding whether observation or qualified assessment is appropriate.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.83 rejects both automatic panic and automatic normalization. Menstrual or ovulatory timing can explain some recurring pain, spotting, or brown discharge, but a new pattern, progressive worsening, bleeding outside an expected window, bleeding after sex or exercise, unusually heavy or prolonged flow, anemia signs, or sudden severe lower-abdominal pain changes the decision.

The framework is deliberately non-diagnostic. Similar descriptions can arise from physiological variation, pregnancy-related conditions, cervical or uterine disease, ovarian events, endocrine disorders, gastrointestinal causes, medication effects, or other problems. The public-education task is therefore to notice pattern and urgency, preserve relevant history, and seek the right care rather than assign a condition from one symptom.

## Key Claims
- Timing within or outside the menstrual cycle helps interpretation but does not settle diagnosis.
- New, progressive, severe, irregular, unusually heavy, or prolonged symptoms lower the threshold for qualified assessment.
- Bleeding should be interpreted with amount, duration, trigger, recurrence, anemia signs, age, and pregnancy possibility.
- Sudden severe lower-abdominal pain is an escalation problem, not a reliable self-differentiation exercise.
- Symptom descriptions are incomplete evidence, so podcast guidance should produce questions and care routing rather than certainty.
- Preserving prior reports and comparing change over time can improve later clinical interpretation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is a broad Q&A, not a validated triage rule or current guideline. Normal ranges, testing, cervical screening, imaging, biopsy or curettage decisions, pain medication, hormone treatment, and emergency thresholds depend on age, pregnancy possibility, medical history, severity, local guidance, and clinician assessment. Severe pain, fainting, heavy bleeding, pregnancy concern, fever, or neurological symptoms can require urgent care.
