# Gynecological Symptom Triage / 妇科症状分诊

## Definition
Gynecological symptom triage is a practical framework for interpreting pain, itching, bumps, bleeding, discharge, cycle change, and abdominal symptoms through onset, location, timing, recurrence, progression, severity, associated signs, and exposure context before deciding whether observation or qualified assessment is appropriate.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.83 rejects both automatic panic and automatic normalization. Menstrual or ovulatory timing can explain some recurring pain, spotting, or brown discharge, but a new pattern, progressive worsening, bleeding outside an expected window, bleeding after sex or exercise, unusually heavy or prolonged flow, anemia signs, or sudden severe lower-abdominal pain changes the decision.

VOL.82 extends this approach to vulvar itching and bumps. Lack of sexual activity may lower the likelihood of some sexually transmitted infections but does not diagnose the cause; inflammation, irritation, folliculitis, dermatologic disease, and other conditions remain possible. Gentle care may be reasonable for a mild short-lived follicular bump, while persistence, worsening, marked pain, abnormal discharge, skin change, or infection concern supports examination.

The framework is deliberately non-diagnostic. Similar descriptions can arise from physiological variation, pregnancy-related conditions, cervical or uterine disease, ovarian events, endocrine disorders, gastrointestinal causes, medication effects, infection, irritation, or skin disease. Public education should therefore produce better observations and care routing rather than certainty from one symptom or one presumed exposure.

## Key Claims
- Timing within or outside the menstrual cycle helps interpretation but does not settle diagnosis.
- New, progressive, severe, irregular, unusually heavy, or prolonged symptoms lower the threshold for qualified assessment.
- Bleeding should be interpreted with amount, duration, trigger, recurrence, anemia signs, age, and pregnancy possibility.
- Sudden severe lower-abdominal pain is an escalation problem, not a reliable self-differentiation exercise.
- Symptom descriptions are incomplete evidence, so podcast guidance should produce questions and care routing rather than certainty.
- Preserving prior reports and comparing change over time can improve later clinical interpretation.
- Sexual history and exposure context can change probability but cannot identify every cause of vulvar itching, bumps, inflammation, or discharge.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episodes are broad Q&A, not a validated triage rule or current guideline. Normal ranges, testing, cervical screening, infection testing, imaging, biopsy or curettage decisions, topical or oral treatment, pain medication, hormone treatment, and emergency thresholds depend on age, pregnancy possibility, medical and sexual history, examination, severity, local guidance, and clinician assessment. Severe pain, fainting, heavy bleeding, pregnancy concern, fever, spreading redness, ulcers, or neurological symptoms can require prompt care.
