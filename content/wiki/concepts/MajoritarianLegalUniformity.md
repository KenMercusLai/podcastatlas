# Majoritarian Legal Uniformity

Majoritarian legal uniformity is the pattern where equal-law language produces conformity to the majority's legal or cultural norms rather than a genuinely neutral settlement among groups. [[settler-bet-a-west-bank-flashpoint-6a842bb539e73ea8f3c13a2d]] adds the concept through [[UniformCivilCodeIndia]], where [[KiraHuyu|Kira Huju]] says BJP-ruled states present uniform civil codes as universal rights but largely require [[IndianMuslims]] to change family-law practices.

The concept does not deny that a uniform code can contain liberal reforms. The source specifically notes more equal inheritance for Muslim daughters and criminalization of polygamy. Its warning is about asymmetry: if tribal communities are exempt and majority practices are already close to the new law, "uniformity" can become a politically attractive word for minority adjustment.

## Key Claims
- The same legal change can be rights-expanding in one dimension and majoritarian in another.
- Exemptions matter because they reveal whether uniformity is a principle or a selective pressure tool.
- A majority-rooted code can deepen minority mistrust even when its text uses universal language.
- The pattern is strongest when state governments link law reform to a larger national or religious identity project.
