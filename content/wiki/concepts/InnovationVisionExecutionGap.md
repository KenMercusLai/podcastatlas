# Innovation Vision-Execution Gap

## Definition

The innovation vision-execution gap is the distance between an attractive technological or moral theory of change and the system's ability to produce that result under real operating conditions.

## Current Synthesis

The Bomber Mafia example shows how narrow focus can be both the source of invention and a liability in deployment. Advocates imagined that bomber range and targeting accuracy could replace mass slaughter with precise destruction, but implementation introduced weather, accuracy, logistics, enemy action, command pressure, and consequences the original vision did not contain.

[[AdamGrant]] sharpens the distinction by separating how difficult an initiative is to attempt from how difficult it is to execute well. Ambition deserves neither dismissal nor automatic credit: evaluation must trace whether the necessary operating system, feedback, adaptation, and safeguards exist.

## Key Claims

- An innovation's enabling insight rarely contains the full conditions for reliable deployment.
- The intense focus that produces a breakthrough can hide system interactions and implementation needs.
- Attempting a difficult project and executing it well are separate achievements.
- Moral intent must be evaluated against operational effects, not only stated purpose.
- Feedback and revision are necessary when real conditions violate the original model.

## Evidence

- Precision-bombing theory - [[malcolm-gladwell-and-adam-grant-malcolm-gladwell-and-adam-grant]] presents bombers and accurate targeting as a proposed substitute for mass civilian warfare.
- Unanticipated complications - [[malcolm-gladwell-and-adam-grant-malcolm-gladwell-and-adam-grant]] says the advocates' narrow technological focus did not anticipate implementation conditions.
- Attempt versus execution - [[malcolm-gladwell-and-adam-grant-malcolm-gladwell-and-adam-grant]] records Grant and Gladwell distinguishing ambitious initiation from successful completion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source provides one historical narrative rather than a general comparative study of innovation. Some complications are unforeseeable, and failure does not prove that an underlying idea was worthless; conversely, noble intent does not justify harmful experimentation at others' expense.

## What Changed

- Established a systems-level distinction between breakthrough vision, implementation capacity, and morally relevant outcome.
