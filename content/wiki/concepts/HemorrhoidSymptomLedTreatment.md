# Hemorrhoid Symptom-Led Treatment / 痔疮症状导向治疗

## Definition
Hemorrhoid symptom-led treatment distinguishes common hemorrhoidal anatomy from symptomatic disease and matches observation, conservative care, or procedures to bleeding, pain, prolapse, hygiene burden, function, anatomy, preferences, and recovery cost.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.165 rejects both automatic surgery and indefinite neglect. Hemorrhoidal vascular tissue is common, while clinically important disease begins when symptoms such as recurrent bleeding, pain, prolapse, itching, soiling, incomplete evacuation, or life disruption warrant assessment. Because bright-red blood is not specific to hemorrhoids, the framework begins with diagnosis rather than self-attribution.

Treatment then proceeds proportionately. Bowel and activity habits, hygiene, diet, and selected medicines may be enough for milder symptoms. When conservative care fails or bleeding and prolapse persist, injection, ligation, arterial control, excision, or staged combinations can be considered according to anatomy and patient priorities. “Minimally invasive” is therefore an outcome-and-burden judgment, not merely the name of a device, and treatment aims at acceptable symptom control with preserved function rather than a promise that normal vascular structures will never become symptomatic again.

## Key Claims
- Normal hemorrhoidal tissue is common and does not require treatment in the absence of clinically meaningful symptoms.
- Repeated bright-red bleeding may fit hemorrhoids but still requires proportionate assessment because appearance alone cannot establish the source.
- Conservative management addresses stool consistency, toileting, hygiene, activity, diet, and selected medication before procedure escalation when appropriate.
- Procedure choice should match internal and external anatomy, symptom burden, continence, pain, wound size, recovery time, work, appearance preferences, and patient acceptance.
- Tissue-preserving injection, ligation, or vascular-control approaches may reduce symptoms for selected disease, while larger or unsuitable tissue may require excision.
- Treatment reduces current disease burden but does not guarantee permanent eradication of hemorrhoidal vascular anatomy or recurrence risk.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This framework comes from one conversational specialist episode, not a treatment guideline or comparative trial. Hemorrhoid grade, bleeding severity, anemia, pregnancy, anticoagulation, inflammatory bowel disease, cancer risk, infection, continence status, previous surgery, and local procedural expertise can change assessment and treatment. The source's recurrence estimate and procedure descriptions remain source-scoped; no approach is universally least painful, most durable, or most appropriate.

## What Changed
- Created a distinction between common hemorrhoidal anatomy and symptom-led treatment need.
- Added recovery burden and patient preference to the meaning of minimally invasive care.
