# Oil Company Enclave

## Definition

An oil company enclave is a concentrated zone in which a foreign-linked oil company builds modern infrastructure and employment while retaining spatial, informational, industrial, or status separation from the wider host society.

## Current Synthesis

[[chevron-venezuela-and-the-paradox-of-plenty]] supplies the clearest lived example: [[CreolePetroleum]]'s Caripito camp offered U.S.-style amenities but separated senior staff, junior staff, and workers. [[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] adds an economy-wide version in which the [[AngloIranianOilCompany]] and Abadan refinery created Iran's first major modern industry yet overshadowed other development and kept crucial profit information beyond Iranian inspection. The enclave problem is therefore both spatial and institutional: visible modernization can coexist with hierarchy and weak host-country control.

## Key Claims

- Company enclaves can deliver infrastructure, jobs, and technical capacity while controlling access and status.
- Spatial segregation and accounting opacity are distinct forms of separation from the host society.
- A dominant oil complex can crowd out or overshadow broader industrial development.
- Enclave inequality makes foreign-led modernization politically vulnerable even when it creates real benefits.

## Evidence

Spatial hierarchy:
- [[chevron-venezuela-and-the-paradox-of-plenty]] records [[MiguelTinkerSalas]]'s account of separate Caripito spaces for senior staff, junior staff, and workers.

Institutional and industrial concentration:
- [[259-iran-england-the-little-satan-glt8347276204]] describes Abadan and the [[AngloIranianOilCompany]] as foundational modern industry that overshadowed other development while Iranian authorities lacked access to profit accounts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source treats foreign oil development as purely extractive: it also brought infrastructure, expertise, employment, and industrial capacity. The Iranian episode does not give enough labor or urban detail to establish that Abadan reproduced the same residential segregation as Caripito, so the comparison is limited to institutional concentration and hierarchy.
