## Definition

Deception as patron trust collapse / 欺瞒式靠山信任崩塌 is the pattern where a protected subordinate makes an underlying offense worse by lying to the patron whose protection depends on accurate private information.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 412-1 defines the pattern through [[TianYannianWesternHan|田延年]] and [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]]. Tian's alleged embezzlement is serious, but the episode argues that he still had a possible path to survival because Huo Guang had both motive and capacity to shield a trusted subordinate. That path closes when Tian denies the charge directly to Huo Guang.

The concept therefore separates legal guilt from trust-system damage. A patron can sometimes manage, conceal, or reduce punishment for a guilty client. But if the client lies to the patron, the patron's own information order is threatened: protecting the liar teaches other subordinates that private reports to the power center can be false without consequence. In this pattern, deception converts a manageable liability into a relationship failure the patron may need to punish to protect command reliability.

## Key Claims

- Patronage protection depends on the patron knowing the real facts before deciding how to act.
- Lying to a protector can be more dangerous than admitting guilt because it attacks the information channel inside the patronage system.
- Prior service and personal closeness do not automatically cancel a later breach of trust.
- A patron may choose formal procedure not from legal purity alone, but to preserve internal truth-telling discipline.
- The pattern is strongest where the patron still has discretion and the subordinate's survival depends on that discretion.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-412-1-xihan-zhengzhi-mingxing-tianyannian-weihe-zai-dianfeng-shi-yunluo-lsvrke2gbdpo-6hvjrlh36i5a0cv|Hanji 412-1]] says Tian Guangming's indirect probe through [[DuYannianWesternHan|杜延年]] shows Huo Guang still has decisive influence over whether the matter can be reduced to a fine.

System discipline:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-412-1-xihan-zhengzhi-mingxing-tianyannian-weihe-zai-dianfeng-shi-yunluo-lsvrke2gbdpo-6hvjrlh36i5a0cv|Hanji 412-1]] explicitly reasons that if Huo Guang rescued a subordinate who had deceived him, future subordinates would have less reason to tell him the truth.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is based on one podcast source and should not be generalized into a claim that patrons always punish deception or that formal punishment is always about truth-system discipline. The source also treats Huo Guang's likely willingness to protect Tian as an inference from power position and relationship, not as a documented confession of intent. The underlying embezzlement remains serious on its own; the concept does not excuse corruption by framing the fatal trigger as deception.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 412-1's explanation of why Tian Yannian's denial to Huo Guang made the case fatal.
