# Terminal Value Narrative / 终局空间叙事

## Definition
Terminal Value Narrative is the investment frame that reduces a priced equity narrative to the company's eventual profit or cash-flow ceiling and the discount rate applied to it. It asks not only how fast a business can grow, but what durable scale, margin, payout, and risk the market is ultimately pricing.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that the end state often controls valuation more than the path. A company that reaches a low terminal ceiling quickly can be worth less than a company that grows more slowly toward a much larger ceiling, especially when the discount rate and terminal multiple differ.

This concept strengthens the wiki's existing growth and value-investing branches by separating near-term earnings momentum from terminal business space. It also explains why narrative change can be more price-relevant than one period of strong results.

## Key Claims
- The terminal ceiling of profit or cash flow can dominate valuation more than the speed of reaching that ceiling.
- A growth story is incomplete unless it specifies the eventual market size, competitive position, margin structure, and payout or cash-flow conversion.
- Discount rate changes can raise or lower valuation even when operating assumptions are unchanged.
- Narrative upgrades occur when the market revises terminal space, durability, or risk, not merely when reported growth is high.
- Reverse DCF can expose whether the current price already assumes a demanding terminal state.

## Evidence
- **Terminal-state dominance:** [[caibao-de-gen-guzhi-de-jing-xushi-de-guoshi-lolxytqwzzxq9kzjbtkmfolafavb]] uses simplified DCF examples to argue that terminal profit multiple and discount rate can outweigh growth speed.
- **Narrative as end state:** [[caibao-de-gen-guzhi-de-jing-xushi-de-guoshi-lolxytqwzzxq9kzjbtkmfolafavb]] records the source's reduction of narrative to the terminal state that the market expects.
- **Reverse valuation:** [[caibao-de-gen-guzhi-de-jing-xushi-de-guoshi-lolxytqwzzxq9kzjbtkmfolafavb]] describes inferring future scale and implied assumptions from the current market price.
- **Growth qualification:** [[caibao-de-gen-guzhi-de-jing-xushi-de-guoshi-lolxytqwzzxq9kzjbtkmfolafavb]] explains why high earnings growth can fail as an investment thesis if terminal space is revised downward.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Terminal value is highly assumption-sensitive and can become a place where wishful thinking hides.
- The source gives simplified examples rather than a complete company-by-company valuation model.
- Some short-term market moves may still be driven by liquidity, positioning, policy, or sentiment before terminal-value logic reasserts itself.
- Terminal ceiling estimates require industry and competitive evidence that this single episode does not fully supply.
