# Assisted Death Concept Boundary

## Definition

Assisted death concept boundary is the distinction among active euthanasia, assisted suicide, treatment withdrawal or so-called passive euthanasia, terminal sedation, and other medical decisions near death. The categories differ in who performs the final act, what treatment is started or stopped, what the patient requests, and how medicine and law assign responsibility.

## Current Synthesis

"安乐死" is too broad to classify every medical act associated with a patient's death. [[119-anlesi-xianchang-zhengyin-shengming-ruci-zhengui-770771087]] shows that patient self-administration in [[Switzerland]], physician-administered voluntary euthanasia in the [[Netherlands]], withdrawal of treatment, and terminal sedation distribute agency and professional liability differently. [[2026-qiuji-pian-e03-zunyan-si-riben-yisheng-beipan-sharen-shijian-flop0zzkutdjvbj11zpa-cz62ijj4]] reinforces the classification rule by explicitly describing its 2005 Japanese murder case as not euthanasia in the ordinary sense, although its truncated transcript does not disclose enough facts to place the act in a more precise category.

## Key Claims
- Naming the practice precisely is the first safeguard against false certainty.
- The final actor matters: patient self-administration, physician injection, treatment withdrawal, and consciousness-lowering care create different legal and moral problems.
- A source's rejection of the ordinary euthanasia label is a reason to investigate the act, consent, treatment status, and causal mechanism—not permission to invent a substitute category.
- Classification connects directly to [[AssistedDyingSafeguards]] and [[AssistedDeathClinicalResponsibility]] because safeguards and professional duties depend on what the doctor and patient actually did.

### Refusal of an overbroad label

- [[2026-qiuji-pian-e03-zunyan-si-riben-yisheng-beipan-sharen-shijian-flop0zzkutdjvbj11zpa-cz62ijj4|日谈物语 E03]] says its Japanese case concerns a patient's life and death but is not euthanasia in the ordinary sense.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The 2026 episode excerpt does not reveal the clinical act or judicial reasoning, so the wiki cannot yet classify the case as treatment withdrawal, sedation, assisted suicide, direct killing, or another practice.
- Vocabulary does not by itself resolve whether an act was consensual, clinically justified, lawful, or ethical; those judgments require facts and safeguards beyond the category name.
