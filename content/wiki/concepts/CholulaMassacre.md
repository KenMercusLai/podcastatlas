## Definition

The Cholula massacre was the 1519 killing, enslavement, rape, pillage, and burning carried out by [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]]' force and its [[Tlaxcala|Tlaxcalan]] allies in Cholula during the inland advance toward Tenochtitlan.

## Current Synthesis

The episode treats the massacre as both an atrocity and a coalition event. Cholula was a major pilgrimage center associated with Quetzalcoatl and had shifted from alignment with Tlaxcala toward the [[MexicaEmpire|Mexica Empire]]. A warning relayed through [[Malinche]] said the Cholulans planned an attack, but the surviving account cannot establish the warning's truth, its original source, or the interests shaping its transmission.

The violence nevertheless had intelligible political effects. It let Tlaxcalan allies strike a regional rival and let Cortes advertise his force as a terrifying participant in Mesoamerican politics. Explaining those incentives does not excuse the mass killing or convert an unresolved threat report into evidence of necessity.

## Key Claims

- The massacre emerged from a coalition whose participants had overlapping but non-identical goals.
- Cholula's recent alignment and rivalry with Tlaxcala made local Indigenous politics causal rather than incidental.
- The alleged attack plan was mediated through interested parties and cannot be treated as independently established fact.
- Killing, burning, enslavement, rape, and pillage made the event exemplary terror as well as battlefield action.
- The massacre helped Cortes project power before the coalition entered the Valley of Mexico.

## Evidence

- Regional setting and target: [[386-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-city-of-gold-part-3-glt2951201852]] identifies Cholula as a pilgrimage center that had moved away from Tlaxcala toward Tenochtitlan.
- Warning and uncertainty: [[386-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-city-of-gold-part-3-glt2951201852]] says Malinche relayed a reported plot connected to Tlaxcalan information without establishing its truth.
- Coalition violence and effects: [[386-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-city-of-gold-part-3-glt2951201852]] attributes massacre, sack, burning, enslavement, rape, and pillage to the allied force and interprets terror as politically useful to both partners.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- This page is bounded to one retrospective podcast episode rather than a full comparison of Spanish, Tlaxcalan, Cholulan, and Mexica accounts.
- The episode does not establish whether an attack was planned, who originated the warning, or how Malinche evaluated and translated it.
- Recognizing Tlaxcalan agency should not make Indigenous actors collectively responsible or reduce Spanish command responsibility.
- Strategic effect does not prove a fully premeditated terror plan; private motives and the fine sequence of the violence remain unresolved.

## What Changed

- Established the massacre as a coalition atrocity shaped by regional rivalry, mediated intelligence, and exemplary violence.
- Preserved the alleged Cholulan plot as unresolved rather than using it to justify or dismiss the attack.
