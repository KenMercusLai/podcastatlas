# Roman Allied Citizenship Crisis / 罗马同盟者公民权危机

## Definition

Roman allied citizenship crisis / 罗马同盟者公民权危机 is the late-republic problem in which Italian allies share Rome's wars, markets, and imperial profits while remaining legally and politically second-class, making citizenship reform central to the [[LateRomanRepublicCrisis]].

## Current Synthesis

Episode 702 makes citizenship the domestic counterpart to military command. Italian elites can profit through Roman expansion, especially in Asia, and Italian soldiers help Rome fight. But the allies lack full voting rights and Roman legal standing. [[GaiusMarius]]'s selective citizenship grant to about a thousand Italian allies after the northern victory exposes the larger question of why all Italian allies cannot be enfranchised.

The crisis first appears as a reform problem under [[MarcusLiviusDrusus]], whose 91 BC proposal tries to solve manpower and stabilization concerns by expanding citizenship. Its defeat and his assassination push the conflict into the [[SocialWar]]. Later, [[PubliusSulpiciusRufus]] uses Italian citizenship policy as part of a bargain with Marius, linking rights expansion to the transfer of [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]]'s eastern command.

## Key Claims

- Citizenship exclusion can destabilize an imperial republic even when excluded allies benefit economically from empire.
- Selective grants make general exclusion harder to justify because they expose citizenship as politically discretionary.
- Reform supporters treat enfranchisement as manpower and stabilization policy, not only moral inclusion.
- Opponents fear that Rome's city-state institutions cannot absorb a peninsula-scale citizen body.
- Failed reform helps produce the Social War, while later citizenship agitation becomes tied to command politics.

## Evidence

- Second-class allied status: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] says Italians profited abroad but lacked full voting rights and legal status at home.
- Selective grant pressure: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] says Marius illegally granted citizenship to about a thousand Italian allies after the northern victory.
- Reform argument: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] says Drusus's supporters saw enfranchisement as a way to increase manpower and stabilize relations.
- Institutional objection: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] says opponents feared millions of new citizens would overwhelm Rome's city-state institutions.
- War and command linkage: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] connects Drusus's assassination to the Social War and later connects Sulpicius's citizenship policy to Marius's command bid.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not claim that Italian allies were excluded from every benefit of Roman power. The episode stresses the opposite complication: elites among the allies could profit overseas even while remaining politically second-class. It also does not settle the final legal settlement after the Social War.

## What Changed

- Created the citizenship-crisis concept to connect Italian allied resentment, Drusus's reform failure, the Social War, and Sulpicius's later bargain with Marius.
