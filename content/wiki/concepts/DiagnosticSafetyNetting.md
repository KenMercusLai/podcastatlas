# Diagnostic Safety Netting / 诊断安全网

## Definition
Diagnostic safety netting is the follow-up structure that keeps an uncertain first visit from becoming a lost patient: the patient leaves with a time window, warning signs, report-tracking route, and return or emergency plan.

## Current Synthesis
The VOL.218 episode treats "observe for now" as a clinical workflow rather than a soft dismissal. A first doctor may not be able to settle the diagnosis immediately, especially when symptoms are nonspecific, disease is early, or tests have limited sensitivity. The safety-netting obligation is to explain what is currently suspected, what dangerous possibilities are not fully excluded, what changes matter, and what should happen if reports are delayed or symptoms change.

The concept is shared between clinicians, patients, and systems. Doctors need to document and communicate pending tests, red flags, and report responsibilities. Patients can ask direct questions about timing, report access, return thresholds, and who to find next. Hospitals can reduce missed handoffs through contact information, critical-value messages, and habits for reviewing unfinished results.

## Key Claims
- "Observe" needs a bounded time window and concrete symptoms to monitor.
- Follow-up advice should name what requires return, what requires emergency care, and where the patient should go.
- Negative or nonspecific test results still need interpretation in light of symptom change and disease timing.
- Pending reports and critical values are part of diagnosis, not administrative afterthoughts.
- Low-cost checks and clear documentation can prevent rare but high-loss errors.
- Safety netting depends on both patient questions and system routines; it is not only individual vigilance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Safety netting does not mean every first visit can or should trigger exhaustive testing. Some tests are invasive, risky, expensive, or clinically irrelevant without history and examination. The source also does not turn every delayed diagnosis into negligence; it treats uncertainty as unavoidable while requiring clearer handoffs, return rules, and report responsibility.
