# Conceptual and Experimental Innovation

## Definition

Conceptual and experimental innovation is [[DavidGalenson]]'s attributed distinction between work executed from a relatively clear prior conception and work whose aim is discovered through attempts, dissatisfaction, revision, and return.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses Picasso and Paul Simon to represent rapid, conception-led work, while Cézanne, [[LeonardCohen]], and [[ElvisCostello]] represent prolonged experimental search. In the conceptual path, evaluation can begin against an articulated goal; in the experimental path, each version helps reveal what the goal is. Repetition therefore need not be a march toward a predetermined endpoint.

The most defensible use is project-level and descriptive. A creator may work differently across projects, and a work can mix prior conception with discovery. Experimental methods also face a recognition problem: a version that is genuinely unfinished may be judged as a completed failure, while persistence, collaboration, institutions, and chance determine whether another attempt occurs.

## Key Claims

- Conceptual innovation begins with a comparatively explicit idea and emphasizes execution against that conception.
- Experimental innovation uses versions and failures to discover both the solution and the problem being solved.
- Repetition can change the destination rather than merely improve movement toward a fixed target.
- An unsuccessful early version may indicate unfinished search, but it can also be an ordinary failure with no latent masterpiece.
- Institutions can more easily evaluate polished, self-evident work than experimental work whose value appears only through later versions.
- The distinction describes methods and projects more safely than permanent classes of artist.

### Recognition asymmetry

- [[encore-hallelujah-encore-hallelujah]] connects rejected, abandoned, or poorly received versions to the risk that institutions mistake a stage for a final verdict.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources offer selected artistic histories rather than a representative sample or direct evaluation of Galenson's scholarship. The original release and encore are overlapping editorial provenance, not independent support. Creator categories may hide mixed methods, retrospective storytelling, selection on famous successes, and projects that remain unsuccessful despite extensive iteration. Time spent, number of drafts, age at achievement, and later popularity do not by themselves establish an experimental method or artistic quality.

## What Changed

- Established a project-level distinction between executing a conception and discovering through attempts.
- Added recognition asymmetry as a practical vulnerability of experimental work.
- Preserved mixed methods and ordinary failure against a rigid two-type taxonomy.
