# Cognitive-Advisory Feedback

## Definition
Cognitive-advisory feedback is a deliberate request for a trusted person to examine, challenge, and broaden one's interpretation instead of providing reassurance or agreement alone.

## Current Synthesis
[[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] argues that self-knowledge is built from incomplete life data and can improve through diverse relationships, uncomfortable conversations, and perspective distance. A useful adviser asks what evidence supports the story, what alternatives it excludes, and how the person's behavior affects others.

Third-person reflection and challenge-oriented conversation serve the same function: they create distance from the first-person account without claiming that outsiders are automatically correct. Comfort can regulate distress, but repeated venting that only confirms one interpretation may preserve the blind spot the conversation needs to test.

## Key Claims
- Self-knowledge is incomplete because a person has observed only a limited set of roles and situations.
- Diverse observers can reveal patterns that familiar or uniformly agreeable groups miss.
- Feedback becomes more diagnostic when the recipient explicitly requests challenge rather than reassurance alone.
- Third-person perspective can reduce identification with a threatened or hostile interpretation.
- How one's behavior affects other people is evidence relevant to self-understanding.
- Useful challenge preserves agency: advisers test a story, while the person remains responsible for judgment and action.

## Evidence
- Blind-spot frame - [[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] recommends diverse people and uncomfortable conversations as evidence beyond one's current self-view.
- Adviser request - [[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] distinguishes comfort-only venting from asking friends to poke holes in an interpretation.
- Perspective distance - [[how-to-shape-your-identity-goals-dr-maya-shankar-scim1420437673]] cites third-person reflection as a way to approach personal conflict with less hostility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Challenge is not automatically accurate, safe, or constructive. Feedback quality depends on trust, expertise, incentives, power, specificity, and the adviser's access to relevant facts. People facing abuse, coercion, discrimination, or acute distress may first need safety and validation; “consider another perspective” should not become pressure to excuse harm.

## What Changed
- Added a dedicated distinction between comfort, venting, and challenge-oriented cognitive advice.
- Added perspective distance as a bridge between self-reflection and interpersonal feedback.
