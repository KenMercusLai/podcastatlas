## Definition

Forensic toxicology uses chemical, biological, medical, scene, and contextual evidence to evaluate whether a poison, drug, gas, or other exposure contributed to impairment, injury, or death.

## Current Synthesis

The forensic-science source presents toxicology as one part of a wider evidentiary chain. Blood, tissues, organ damage, scene conditions, witnesses, and a substance's chemical behavior may all matter because some agents metabolize, disperse, or leave indirect injury patterns. Insulin, carbon monoxide, cyanide, volatile solvents, nitrous oxide, and other inhaled hazards illustrate why neither a dramatic symptom nor a suspected substance is usually sufficient on its own.

Hanji 331-4 extends the same discipline to historical poisoning claims. Reports that pepper or “closed-mouth” pepper caused throat obstruction, foaming, coldness, or death do not establish that ordinary culinary pepper is categorically lethal. The causal question requires identity, variety, condition, preparation, route, dose, timing, symptoms, competing explanations, and trustworthy documentation. A proposed nerve or respiratory mechanism can make an account conceivable without proving that the reported exposure occurred or caused the death.

The combined judgment is therefore evidentiary rather than dismissive: old poison reports can be investigated, but textual repetition, ingredient naming, and mechanistic plausibility must not be collapsed into a verified toxicological case.

## Key Claims

- Toxic exposure is inferred most reliably from a chain joining specimen results, medical findings, scene evidence, timing, symptoms, and chemical behavior.
- Some poisons are difficult to identify because they metabolize, dissipate, or produce indirect damage rather than a unique visible sign.
- Substance identity alone does not determine harm; variety, condition, preparation, exposure route, dose, and host context can materially change risk.
- Historical symptom descriptions and repeated anecdotes are leads, not substitutes for validated identification, exposure reconstruction, or differential explanation.
- A plausible biological mechanism does not by itself establish that a substance caused a particular injury or death.
- Public poisoning can threaten unintended people and animals, making prevention part of toxicology's civic relevance.

## Evidence

- Multi-evidence investigation: [[121-jijian-fayi-jianshi-shouce-yiqie-fanzui-jieyou-henji-775064343]] describes insulin, carbon monoxide, cyanide, volatile solvents, nitrous oxide, blood and tissue testing, organ findings, and scene context as parts of toxicological reasoning.
- Detection limits: [[121-jijian-fayi-jianshi-shouce-yiqie-fanzui-jieyou-henji-775064343]] emphasizes that gases and volatile agents may disperse or leave indirect injury patterns before investigators obtain a simple result.
- Dose, route, and preparation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-4-chi-huajiao-neng-zisha-bie-sha-le-lhto8ydkeetl8sjwoxyxkkehi66e|Hanji 331-4]] distinguishes ordinary culinary contact from alleged large, powdered, or decocted exposures and uses modern everyday experience to challenge categorical pepper-lethality claims.
- Historical-source boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-4-chi-huajiao-neng-zisha-bie-sha-le-lhto8ydkeetl8sjwoxyxkkehi66e|Hanji 331-4]] reports old descriptions of closed-mouth pepper and poisoning symptoms while leaving the underlying texts, plant identity, compound, and lethal threshold unverified.
- Public-safety extension: [[121-jijian-fayi-jianshi-shouce-yiqie-fanzui-jieyou-henji-775064343]] treats toxins released in shared space as risks to adults, children, and animals rather than only as private or veterinary harms.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not supply laboratory reports, clinical case records, dose-response data, or a toxicology review for pepper. Hanji 331-4's neuromuscular and respiratory explanation is a source-attributed hypothesis, and its distinctions among pepper varieties and preparations require botanical and scientific verification. Conversely, ordinary culinary safety does not prove that every concentrated preparation or exposure pattern is harmless. The earlier forensic source is a broad public explainer, not a complete analytical-toxicology manual. Neither source establishes a universal lethal dose, diagnostic symptom set, or historical cause of death.

## What Changed

- Added historical-text evaluation as an explicit toxicology use case.
- Added substance identity, variety, preparation, route, and dose as separate causal checks.
- Clarified that mechanistic plausibility does not verify a historical poisoning event.
