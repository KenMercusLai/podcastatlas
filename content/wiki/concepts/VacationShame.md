# Vacation Shame / 休假羞耻感

Vacation shame is [[ep265-dang-qing3xiu13-chongshang-resou-women-ruhe-gei-rensheng-fang-yige-changjia-lsloczlnkmcrrfpn9coh8rrqo-wb]]'s name for the guilt, unease, or vigilance people can feel while taking leave. The episode starts from the popular "请3休13" calendar trick, but the deeper question is why paid or arranged time off does not automatically become rest.

The source treats the shame as social and embodied. Work pressure follows people through phones, coworkers may still expect responses, bosses may not protect time off, and some cultural habits make intense pleasure feel unsafe or morally suspect. That places vacation shame between [[LaborMoralAmbivalence]] and [[WorkRestBoundary]]: work is moralized, and rest has to justify itself.

## Key Claims
- A person can want a holiday and still feel that taking it is irresponsible.
- Vacation shame is intensified when leave time remains reachable by work messages and unfinished tasks.
- Shame can be cultural as well as organizational: pleasure may be treated as fragile, excessive, or inviting later punishment.
- Treating rest as maintenance rather than indulgence can reduce shame, but only if the workplace also respects the boundary.
- Vacation shame is different from ordinary planning stress; it is a moral pressure around whether one is allowed to stop.
