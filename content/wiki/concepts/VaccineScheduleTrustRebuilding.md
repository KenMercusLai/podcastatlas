# Vaccine Schedule Trust Rebuilding

## Definition
Vaccine schedule trust rebuilding is the public-health task of preserving vaccine uptake while making timing, priority, combination, risk, and evidence questions discussable rather than treating every concern as illegitimate.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence gives one policy-reform version and one immunologist-communication version. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-supercharging-a-new-fda-marty-makary-on-science-power-patients-39750050]] uses [[MartyMakary|Marty Makary]] to argue that public-health authorities can increase trust by distinguishing core essential vaccines from lower-priority recommendations, explaining evidence, and comparing schedules across developed countries. The most concrete example is hepatitis B at birth when the mother is hepatitis B negative, which Makary and [[DavidFriedberg|Dave Friedberg]] use to argue for risk-specific explanation.

[[how-your-immune-system-works-how-to-improve-it-dr-max-krummel-scim7009523546]] gives a compatible but less policy-specific version through [[MaxKrummel]]. Krummel says he vaccinated his own children and points to smallpox and measles as diseases whose severity can be forgotten once vaccination makes them less visible. At the same time, he acknowledges reasonable questions about timing, combinations, public communication, mandates, pharma incentives, and harmed families who feel unheard. The combined synthesis is not anti-vaccine; it is pro-explanation, pro-evidence, and aware that trust can be lost when communication treats complex schedules as rhetorically flat.

## Key Claims
- Vaccine confidence depends on distinguishing evidence strength, disease severity, timing, and population risk rather than presenting every recommendation in the same rhetorical register.
- Schedule prioritization can be framed as trust repair without implying that lower-priority vaccines have no value.
- Public memory of vaccine-prevented disease severity can fade when vaccination succeeds, making risk communication harder.
- Parents and harmed families need risk-specific answers for timing, combinations, and adverse-experience questions before trust can be rebuilt.
- Mandates, poor communication, and pharma incentives can damage trust even when many scientists and clinicians act in good faith.
- The concept belongs with [[MedicalDogmaTrustRepair]] and [[ScienceCommunicationTrustRepair]] because the central problem is evidence plus humility, not evidence abandonment.

## Evidence
- Policy hierarchy: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-supercharging-a-new-fda-marty-makary-on-science-power-patients-39750050]] says Makary presented a hierarchy of core essential vaccines while noting that the [[CentersForDiseaseControlAndPrevention|CDC]] still recommends all vaccines.
- Concrete schedule question: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-supercharging-a-new-fda-marty-makary-on-science-power-patients-39750050]] uses the newborn hepatitis B shot, when the mother is hepatitis B negative, as a case for differentiated explanation.
- Immunologist stance: [[how-your-immune-system-works-how-to-improve-it-dr-max-krummel-scim7009523546]] has Krummel say he vaccinated his own children while still acknowledging reasonable timing, combination, and trust questions.
- Forgotten disease risk: [[how-your-immune-system-works-how-to-improve-it-dr-max-krummel-scim7009523546]] points to smallpox and measles as severe diseases whose danger can become less visible after successful vaccination.
- Communication and incentives: [[how-your-immune-system-works-how-to-improve-it-dr-max-krummel-scim7009523546]] connects distrust to mandates, poor communication, pharma incentives, and families who feel unheard.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The bounded sources are both sympathetic to trust-repair critiques, so this page should not be treated as a full vaccine-policy evidence review. Krummel does not conclude that vaccines cause autism, and Makary's hierarchy claim remains source-scoped rather than a replacement for CDC guidance. The page records a communication and trust mechanism, not individualized medical advice.
