# Vaccine Schedule Trust Rebuilding

Vaccine schedule trust rebuilding is the source's argument that public-health authorities can increase vaccination trust by distinguishing priorities and explaining uncertainty. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-supercharging-a-new-fda-marty-makary-on-science-power-patients-39750050]], [[MartyMakary|Marty Makary]] says the administration reviewed vaccine recommendations across 20 developed countries and presented a hierarchy of core essential vaccines, while the [[CentersForDiseaseControlAndPrevention|CDC]] still recommends all vaccines.

The source makes hepatitis B at birth the most concrete example. Makary and [[DavidFriedberg|Dave Friedberg]] discuss declining the newborn hepatitis B shot when the mother is hepatitis B negative, using it to argue that parent questions should receive risk-specific explanation rather than absolutist dismissal.

## Key Claims
- Treating every vaccine recommendation as rhetorically identical can reduce trust among parents who have concrete risk questions.
- A hierarchy of essential vaccines is presented as a trust-building strategy, not as a claim that other vaccines have no value.
- International comparison is used to argue that schedule design can vary across developed countries.
- The concept belongs with [[MedicalDogmaTrustRepair]] because the political problem is humility, explanation, and differentiated evidence.
