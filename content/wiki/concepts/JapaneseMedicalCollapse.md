# Japanese Medical Collapse / 日本医疗崩坏

Japanese medical collapse is the 1990s crisis frame in [[vol-120-riben-yiliao-tixi-de-bengkui-yu-zhongsheng-yiyuan-yaopin-yibao-sanjiao-kunju-de-yizhong-jiefa-lp-p5p1cybbldphe0qajteggsitt]]. The episode describes a system built around younger acute-care patients running into deep aging, chronic disease, fiscal tightening after recession, hospital overuse, and rising doctor-patient conflict.

The source's important claim is that the collapse was not only a shortage of doctors. It was a mismatch between demand, payment, care categories, doctor incentives, and drug policy. That is why later reform had to combine [[JapaneseMedicalTriageSystem]], [[CommunityIntegratedCare]], [[DPCDRGPaymentReform]], [[JapaneseDrugPricingReform]], and [[PharmacistAsSecondDoctor]].

## Key Claims
- Aging was intensified by chronic disease, not just by a larger number of older people.
- [[SocialHospitalization]] made hospitals absorb care and welfare demand that should have had separate channels.
- Cost-cutting and harsher doctor punishment could worsen defensive medicine and patient dissatisfaction when used as isolated fixes.
- Reform needed to change system incentives, not only increase capacity.
