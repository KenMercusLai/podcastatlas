## Definition

[[PublicSignalIntentInference|公开信号下的意图判断]] is the practice of evaluating a declared threat by asking whether its visibility fits the operational requirements of the action it claims to announce.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1162 presents Cao Xiu reasoning that a real attempt to cut Cao Hong's rear should normally be concealed. Zhang Fei's conspicuous announcement therefore lowers its credibility as literal intent, allowing Cao Hong to attack Wu Lan instead. The durable lesson is not that publicity proves deception, but that analysts should compare communication behavior with what the threatened operation would need to succeed.

## Key Claims

- Signals gain meaning from their fit with operational requirements.
- Publicity can impose a credibility cost on actions that depend on secrecy.
- Signal interpretation becomes useful only when tied to a concrete allocation decision.
- A successful reading remains probabilistic and context-dependent.

## Evidence

Signal-operation mismatch:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1162-jinyi-zhisi-xiang-ying-jiu-bie-xinji-lpjdh6wohap6bqbghnvltu2oey5i|Hanji 1162]] attributes to Cao Xiu the inference that Zhang Fei would hide a genuine rear attack.

Decision consequence:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1162-jinyi-zhisi-xiang-ying-jiu-bie-xinji-lpjdh6wohap6bqbghnvltu2oey5i|Hanji 1162]] links the assessment to Cao Hong's choice to attack Wu Lan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Public threats can be double bluffs, coercive signals, or cover for real action, so concealment is not a universal test. The episode does not disclose all intelligence available to Cao Xiu or show that the publicity mismatch alone justified the decision.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded signal-analysis concept from Cao Xiu's Hanzhong judgment.
