# Migraine Medication Choice

## Definition
Migraine medication choice is the individualized selection and bounded use of acute or preventive treatment according to diagnosis, attack burden, evidence, safety, cost, access, and patient circumstances.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-157-zui-touteng-de-wenti-jiushi-touteng-ft-dawu-shiye-banmajiang-lrkqhlcwhxomw_aegpd_rzco8vio|VOL.157]] resists both therapeutic novelty bias and blanket medication fear. Conventional NSAID-type acute treatment remains useful for many patients when selected and used appropriately. Medication-overuse or medication-related headache is a real boundary, but the source associates greater concern with unsupervised and poorly bounded use rather than treating all analgesia as harmful.

CGRP-related therapies expand the option set and are presented as having favorable evidence and some advantages for selected patients. They do not become a default answer: indication, response, cost, access, trial participation, and long-term decision-making remain relevant. Pregnancy, lactation, and other special circumstances narrow options and require clinician guidance.

The Huberman headache episode adds a mechanism-oriented treatment menu, including light-environment changes for photophobia, caffeine's bidirectional effects, omega-3s, curcumin, and acupuncture, while also warning that aspirin-like approaches may not fit every migraine. That warning qualifies rather than overturns the established-treatment synthesis: vessel dilation alone does not determine drug choice, and the source does not provide a validated algorithm for selecting or avoiding an NSAID.

## Key Claims
- Established acute treatments remain a practical foundation for many patients.
- Medication-overuse concern should improve supervision and limits rather than create universal avoidance.
- CGRP-related therapies may offer meaningful advantages for selected patients.
- Newer does not automatically mean appropriate, affordable, or necessary.
- Special physiological or medical contexts require individualized selection rather than copied advice.
- Mechanistic plausibility and personal response can guide clinical discussion but do not establish efficacy, dose, contraindications, or a self-treatment rule.

## Evidence
- Established treatment: [[vol-157-zui-touteng-de-wenti-jiushi-touteng-ft-dawu-shiye-banmajiang-lrkqhlcwhxomw_aegpd_rzco8vio]] presents common NSAID-type analgesia as an effective mainstream option for many patients.
- Use boundary: [[vol-157-zui-touteng-de-wenti-jiushi-touteng-ft-dawu-shiye-banmajiang-lrkqhlcwhxomw_aegpd_rzco8vio]] distinguishes clinician-guided use from repeated self-selection and home drug combinations associated with medication-related headache risk.
- New-drug tradeoff: [[vol-157-zui-touteng-de-wenti-jiushi-touteng-ft-dawu-shiye-banmajiang-lrkqhlcwhxomw_aegpd_rzco8vio]] discusses CGRP therapy through evidence, safety, cost, access, and research participation.
- Conditional non-drug and supplement menu: [[how-to-stop-headaches-using-science-based-approaches-scim3934172473]] discusses photophobia-sensitive light, caffeine, omega-3s, curcumin, and acupuncture while repeatedly directing chronic headache and supplement changes to physician review.
- NSAID qualification: [[how-to-stop-headaches-using-science-based-approaches-scim3934172473]] warns against assuming aspirin fits every migraine, but does not supply evidence sufficient to reverse the other source's clinician-bounded support for common acute treatment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide a complete acute-versus-preventive algorithm, drug list, dose, contraindication map, pregnancy safety review, supplement-interaction review, or current formulary and reimbursement status. Migraine cannot be reduced to vasodilation alone, and caffeine, omega-3, curcumin, acupuncture, and light claims are not interchangeable with medication evidence. Relative-effectiveness, medication-overuse, and aspirin-fit statements remain source-scoped and do not authorize self-prescribing, switching, or stopping treatment.

## What Changed
- Added a migraine-specific treatment framework balancing established analgesia, medication-overuse risk, and CGRP innovation.
- Made affordability and access part of the clinical choice rather than treating efficacy alone as decisive.
- Added a mechanism-based intervention menu while explicitly preserving the uncertainty around aspirin and NSAID selection.
