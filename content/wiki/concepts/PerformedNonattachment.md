# Performed Nonattachment

## Definition
Performed nonattachment is the strategic display of caring less than another person so that an intimate encounter appears casual and the actor avoids the status cost of vulnerability, repetition, or romantic interest.

## Current Synthesis
Meaninglessness is not an automatic property of an encounter. In Wade's account, participants produce it through intoxication or its appearance, avoidance of sober planning, reluctance to repeat encounters, delayed or absent contact, social demotion of the partner, and denial of genuine interest. The display can matter even when private feelings differ.

Because each person anticipates the other's stereotype, detachment can escalate recursively. Men may distance themselves because they expect women to seek commitment; women may intensify their distance to avoid appearing desperate. The result gives social advantage to the person who seems least invested, suppresses direct requests, and can make kindness or emotional honesty look like failure.

The concept is not a diagnosis of every casual encounter. People can mutually prefer limited contact, and boundaries or disinterest need not be performances. It identifies the narrower case where status pressure makes participants conceal preferences and treat care itself as disqualifying evidence.

## Key Claims
- Casual meaning is often communicated through conduct before and after sex rather than contained in the act itself.
- Appearing less invested can confer status and bargaining advantage.
- Gender stereotypes can generate recursive distancing even when neither person wants it.
- Fear of appearing desperate can suppress direct requests for care, repetition, or relationship.
- Kindness and vulnerability become costly when detachment is treated as proof of competence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Distance may express genuine preference, safety, incompatibility, or a clear boundary rather than concealed attachment. Repeated contact is not inherently caring, and romantic pursuit is not owed after sex. The source does not supply direct measures separating strategic performance from authentic disinterest, nor does it establish that every gender follows the same pattern. The mechanism should therefore be used to interpret incentive structure, not to infer a particular person's hidden feelings.
