## Definition
Outer-relative court power is the late-Han pattern in which imperial in-laws use ceremonial privilege, kinship surveillance, punitive humiliation, oversized reward, empress marriage, clan rank, harem access, military command, palace surveillance, protected retainers, and office appointments to dominate government. Its liquidation usually requires palace secrecy and force because ordinary remonstrance, social contact, and law enforcement have already become dangerous, even though formal ritual and office procedure can sometimes create brief public checks.

## Current Synthesis
The earliest current Liang layer now begins with a formal court-law check. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-835-donghan-diyi-quanchen-weihe-yao-gui-ta-lpwezt-z-2qloitwd3t4gyxznqbo|Hanji 835]], [[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]] enters the 151 CE New Year palace audience wearing a sword; [[ZhangLingLateHan|Zhang Ling]] rebukes him, orders guards to remove the sword, and memorializes an impeachment. Liang kneels and apologizes, but [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]] reduces the outcome to one year's salary forfeiture. This adds a limited counterpressure: ritual procedure can make outer-relative overreach publicly actionable, while the ruler's light penalty shows that the check is not yet systemic control.

The next Liang layer begins with family surveillance and social-contact punishment. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-836-xiongdi-fanmu-beihou-ruzhe-he-jianchen-de-jiaofeng-lq9wjn5c8mwt-onrtslum4fw53eq|Hanji 836]], Liang Ji makes [[LiangBuyiLateHan|Liang Buyi]]'s withdrawn household a monitored political site. When [[MaRongLateHan|Ma Rong]] and [[TianMingLateHan|Tian Ming]] visit Liang Buyi on the way to office, Liang Ji's side turns ordinary elite contact into accusation, hair-removal punishment, flogging, exile, and in Tian Ming's case death on the road. This shows outer-relative power reaching into kinship, scholar association, and public dignity before the later reward, marriage, crime-record, and coup layers.

The reward-access layer follows in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-838-tanlan-de-youhuo-rang-ta-kuangbiao-zai-zuosi-de-lushang-lrydilyni6o6yzhk1-e74m6aigwr|Hanji 838]]. Huan's court grants Liang Ji extraordinary reward and access in 151 CE before the later marriage, crime, and coup evidence. Huang Qiong limits the territorial proposal but not the wider privilege package, and Liang remains dissatisfied. This shows outer-relative power as a reward-expectation and access problem before it appears as household violence and liquidation.

The pre-coup marriage layer centers on [[EmpressLiangYingLateHan|梁盈]] in Hanji 845. She is presented as Huan's first empress because of Liang-family backing, and even her pre-marriage body inspection does not make the marriage personally or politically healthy. Her childlessness, harem jealousy, and Huan's avoidance after the Liang dowager's death show outer-relative power working through the emperor's household before it appears as the later crime-record and coup problem.

The everyday coercive layer behind the later anti-Liang coup is visible in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]]. [[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]] is not merely influential through marriage ties: the Liang clan fills harem ranks, marquisates, military command, and senior offices, while spies watch the emperor's movements and tribute flows through Liang household selection before court distribution. Protected retainers and retaliatory killings make local officials, appointees, remonstrants, and kinship associates vulnerable, so feedback collapses before formal court correction can work.

The same structure appears from inside the household in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]]. [[SunShouLateHan|Sun Shou]] and [[EmpressDengHuanLateHan|Empress Deng Huan]] demonstrate that an outer-relative bloc can use harem rivalry, kinship discipline, and sexual politics to shape succession danger. The emperor can be central to the dynasty yet operationally constrained by the in-law family that surrounds him.

The liquidation problem appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]]. [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]] can only move by sealing information, aligning with eunuch insiders, using imperial tokens, and removing many Liang-family officeholders at once. The cure is therefore not a clean restoration of normal rule: defeating one outer-relative bloc strengthens eunuch-centered crisis power.

## Key Claims
- Court ritual, kinship surveillance, and visitor punishment can expose outer-relative dominance before direct coercive terror becomes visible.
- Overlarge reward and ceremonial privilege can normalize outer-relative dominance before its violence becomes explicit.
- Outer-relative power fuses empress marriage, family rank, harem position, military command, and office appointment into a single governing bloc.
- Protected retainers, monitored social contact, and retaliatory violence turn ordinary law enforcement, remonstrance, and association into personal danger.
- Harem and marriage conflict are not private background; they are mechanisms by which clan power pressures imperial succession and timing.
- An emperor may hold formal sovereignty while lacking safe channels for open correction.
- Liquidating an outer-relative bloc requires control over palace communication, command symbols, and appointment authority.

## Evidence
- Court-law check: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-835-donghan-diyi-quanchen-weihe-yao-gui-ta-lpwezt-z-2qloitwd3t4gyxznqbo|Hanji 835]] says Zhang Ling publicly rebukes Liang Ji for wearing a sword into the palace audience and orders guards to remove it.
- Bounded sanction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-835-donghan-diyi-quanchen-weihe-yao-gui-ta-lpwezt-z-2qloitwd3t4gyxznqbo|Hanji 835]] says Liang Ji kneels and apologizes, but Emperor Huan imposes only a one-year salary forfeiture rather than Zhang Ling's requested Tingwei punishment.
- Brother-household surveillance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-836-xiongdi-fanmu-beihou-ruzhe-he-jianchen-de-jiaofeng-lq9wjn5c8mwt-onrtslum4fw53eq|Hanji 836]] says Liang Ji keeps disguised watchers outside Liang Buyi's closed household after Liang Buyi resigns from office.
- Visitor punishment and humiliation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-836-xiongdi-fanmu-beihou-ruzhe-he-jianchen-de-jiaofeng-lq9wjn5c8mwt-onrtslum4fw53eq|Hanji 836]] says Ma Rong and Tian Ming are accused, shaved, flogged, and exiled after visiting Liang Buyi, with Tian Ming dying in exile.
- Reward and access excess: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-838-tanlan-de-youhuo-rang-ta-kuangbiao-zai-zuosi-de-lushang-lrydilyni6o6yzhk1-e74m6aigwr|Hanji 838]] says Emperor Huan accepts Huang Qiong's limit on Liang Ji's territorial reward while still granting exceptional etiquette, wealth, residence, court seating, and Shangshu access.
- Unbounded expectation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-838-tanlan-de-youhuo-rang-ta-kuangbiao-zai-zuosi-de-lushang-lrydilyni6o6yzhk1-e74m6aigwr|Hanji 838]] says Liang Ji remains dissatisfied after the privilege package, making reward appetite part of the outer-relative problem before the later crime-record layer.
- Empress marriage and body inspection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-845-cong-dangdai-hunyin-guan-liao-gudai-de-hunjian-zhidu-lr0i25twkoxm2wen7pnfbwt3qdsn|Hanji 845]] shows Liang Ying's family-backed empress title, pre-marriage inspection, childlessness, and death as the household layer before Huan's anti-Liang opening.
- Clan saturation and office control: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]] describes the Liang family's concentration of marquisates, harem titles, generals-in-chief, and high offices as the administrative base of Liang Ji's dominance.
- Palace surveillance and tribute preemption: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]] presents eunuch-spy monitoring and Liang's first pick of tribute goods as signs that private household power bypasses ordinary palace procedure.
- Retainer shielding and retaliation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]] links [[WuShuWanMagistrateLateHan|吴树]], [[HaoMengDongjunLateHan|郝猛]], [[YuanZhuRemonstrantLateHan|袁著]], [[HuWuTaiyuanLateHan|胡武]], [[HaoJieTaiyuanLateHan|郝杰]], [[GengChengLinruHouLateHan|耿承]], and [[CuiQiLateHan|崔琦]] to the coercive environment that blocks correction.
- Household and harem mechanisms: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]] shows [[SunShouLateHan|Sun Shou]] and [[EmpressDengHuanLateHan|Empress Deng Huan]] turning private conflict into political vulnerability.
- Palace coup mechanics: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]] records sealed communications, eunuch alignment, command-symbol seizure, and Liang-family office removal as necessary to defeat Liang Ji.
- Institutional aftereffect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]] also shows the anti-Liang victory strengthening eunuch recipients of rank and reward.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is grounded mainly in the Liang-family case and should not be treated as a complete typology of all Eastern-Han outer-relative politics. Hanji 835 shows that formal court procedure can still check Liang Ji in public, but the lighter imperial sanction means the scene does not prove durable institutional control. Hanji 836's brother-surveillance and Ma Rong/Tian Ming punishment layer is source-scoped and should not be expanded into a complete Liang Buyi faction. Hanji 838's reward package is source-scoped and shows one early Liang Ji settlement, not every mechanism of outer-relative dominance. Hanji 845's marriage-inspection detail and "first recorded inspected empress" claim are source-scoped. The numerical portrait of Liang clan saturation and the crime-record examples are source-scoped to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]]. The episode does not resolve the existing timing tension between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]], where Xuan survives a failed assassination attempt and helps catalyze the anti-Liang move, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]], where Xuan is compressed into a death-triggered lead-up.

## What Changed
- Hanji 835 moves the concept back to a 151 CE court-law check where ceremonial breach creates a public constraint but not durable control.
- Hanji 836 and Hanji 838 add the same year's kinship surveillance, monitored social contact, scholar-official humiliation, overreward, and privilege-appetite layers.
- Hanji 845 adds the Liang Ying marriage-inspection and childless-empress layer before the already ingested Liang crime and coup sequence.
- Hanji 846 adds the coercive everyday layer: family-office saturation, surveillance, tribute capture, protected retainers, and repeated retaliatory violence.
- Hanji 847 and Hanji 848 make harem politics and liquidation mechanics central: household conflict leads into secrecy, command symbols, mass office removal, and eunuch-mediated aftereffect.
