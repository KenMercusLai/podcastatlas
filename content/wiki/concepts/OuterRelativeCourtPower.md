## Definition
Outer-relative court power is the late-Han pattern in which imperial in-laws convert dowager authority, military prestige, death-news control, child-ruler selection, office succession, accession choice, marriage, harem rank, retainers, estates, tribute access, reward claims, appointment access, and succession control into a governing bloc. Its danger is not only private enrichment: it can exclude a stronger heir, purge former allies, monopolize offices, become an unofficial authorization center, install a known-risk family successor, protect corrupt officials, survive truthful direct impeachment, kill a ruler who threatens control, choose the next ruler for self-protection, distort reward and appointment, criminalize upright ministers, punish social contact, suppress warnings, and try to control public memory after death.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-766-lishi-jiaogei-ni-weishenme-yao-guyi-fa-piqi-lrs-mguyfmnxe970hdwjvmevl8oc|Hanji 766]] now supplies the earliest current Dou-family branch under [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]]. [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s outer-relative status and Northern Xiongnu achievement let his household intimidate officials, while [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]], [[RenKuiEasternHan|Ren Kui]], [[ZhouRongEasternHan|Zhou Rong]], and [[DingHongEasternHan|Ding Hong]] keep opposition visible. The source's concrete sign of court capture is administrative: new governors and two-thousand-shi officials first visit Dou's household before taking office.

The same branch shows liquidation mechanics. Emperor He cannot safely consult ordinary ministers, so he uses [[ZhengZhongEasternHan|Zheng Zhong]] and [[LiuQingQingheKing|Liu Qing]] for secret planning, then closes palace gates, mobilizes guards, executes immediate conspirators, strips Dou Xian's general-in-chief seal, and sends the Dou brothers away. The success solves one outer-relative bloc while opening the eunuch-access risk that later Han politics will magnify.

The Yan-family branch in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] supplies an earlier non-Liang outer-relative pattern. After [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] dies on tour, [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|Yan Hou / Yan Taihou]] and [[YanXianLateHan|Yan Xian]] use concealed death, the choice of [[LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan|Beixiang Hou]], Liu Bao's exclusion, Yan Xian's military appointment, and the purge of [[GengBaoGrandGeneralLateHan|Geng Bao]], [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|Fan Feng]], and [[WangShengPalaceActorLateHan|Wang Sheng]] to turn succession control into family monopoly. The same case makes the backfire risk visible: when the Yan side takes old allies and remaining palace actors out of the settlement, excluded eunuchs have stronger incentive to counter-mobilize later.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] supplies the first Liang-family office-succession layer. After [[LiangShangLateHan|梁商]] dies, [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] appoints [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] as Grand General and [[LiangBuyiLateHan|梁不疑]] as Henan Yin. The source also preserves Sima Guang's criticism that Shun empowered Liang Ji despite knowing his violent character, making outer-relative power visible before the later protected-corruption and succession-control sequence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-822-chailang-dangdao-ta-jiang-maotou-zhizhi-huangdi-yuefu-lqhpmayvd3p07byr-zoiv-jyhdv|Hanji 822]] then defines the earliest protected-coercion layer. Under [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]], corrupt Nanyang officials bribe [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] for protection, Liang has [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] moved away from the case, and [[ZhangGangLateHan|张纲]] directly impeaches Liang Ji and [[LiangBuyiLateHan|梁不疑]]. The ruler knows the charges are true but cannot immediately punish them, showing outer-relative power as protected impunity before it becomes full succession domination.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] defines the first imperial-murder layer. Liang Ji's dominance has already reached the point where [[EmperorZhiOfHan]] can publicly name him "跋扈将军"; Liang responds by poisoning the child emperor, blocking water at the deathbed, and treating Li Gu's grief and questioning as a cover-up risk. The source also shows senior officials first recommending [[LiuSuanLateHan]] before [[CaoTengLateHan]]'s private resentment begins to undo that recommendation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-828-da-jiangjun-liangji-shanquan-feili-de-donghan-jianchen-llqnqijagtmdlgitulddxspncu0e|Hanji 828]] then shows how murder becomes succession capture. Cao Teng persuades Liang to choose Liu Zhi over Liu Suan because Liu Suan would threaten Liang's guests and eunuch fortunes. Liang pressures senior officials into ratification, while Li Gu and [[DuQiaoLateHan]] resist and Li Gu is removed through [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan]]'s authority.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-829-junzi-weishenme-doubuguo-xiaoren-lthvnstkfu8n9thx1sz6yezl4yqq|Hanji 829]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-830-donghan-ligu-wo-sui-si-yourong-lt-j7n9gotiw-negqxojoacf6ji|Hanji 830]] show the next stages. [[EmperorHuanOfHan]] rewards Liang and allied actors for the accession settlement, Du Qiao criticizes unmerited reward, and Liang later frames Li Gu and Du Qiao as conspirators until both die in prison. Yet body display and mourning bans also reveal the limit of terror: students, friends, and retainers convert the intended warning into [[FuneraryDefianceUnderPoliticalTerror]].

Hanji 833 and Hanji 834 show the same power ecology after Liang Na's later transition. Emperor Huan increases Liang Ji's estate base, [[SunShouLateHan]] receives revenue-bearing honor, [[QinGongLateHan]] turns household service into office-access brokerage, and Liang estates become sites where private pleasure and property extraction take on lethal public force. [[ZhuMuLateHan]] warns Liang to restrain extraction and appointments, while [[ChenFanLateHan]] and [[YanDuLateHan]] show that officials can still punish Liang-linked agents only in bounded cases.

Hanji 835 through Hanji 848 add public check, kinship surveillance, reward appetite, frontier-witness suppression, empress marriage, household crisis, crime-record saturation, and liquidation. [[ZhangLingLateHan]] can make Liang's sword-bearing breach visible, but sanction is limited; Huan can limit only part of Liang's reward while granting extraordinary access; [[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan]]'s warning dies before policy; and Huan finally defeats Liang only by sealing communications, aligning with eunuch insiders, seizing command symbols, and abolishing the Liang office network. The cure weakens one in-law bloc while strengthening eunuch-centered crisis power.

## Key Claims
- Outer-relative dominance can begin as military prestige plus household authorization, or as dowager-led death-news control, child-ruler selection, heir exclusion, and family office concentration, before later branches escalate into protected impunity, ruler killing, and self-protective succession selection.
- Marriage, dowager authority, household access, estates, and harem rank make private kinship a public governing channel.
- Warnings, lawful enforcement, visitor contact, omitted deference, property refusal, and mourning can all become punishable when a family bloc monopolizes political safety.
- Formal ritual and office procedure can expose overreach, but bounded sanctions do not equal systemic control.
- Reward and ceremonial privilege can normalize dominance without satisfying the favored powerholder.
- Terror can kill critics while failing to control moral reputation and funeral memory.
- Liquidation of an entrenched outer-relative bloc requires control over palace communication, command symbols, and appointment authority.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is still grounded mainly in the Dou, Yan, and Liang cases and should not be treated as a complete typology of all Eastern-Han outer-relative politics. Hanji 766 broadens the evidence to a He-era Dou-family branch, but it does not reconstruct Dou Xian's full rise or every actor in the palace operation. Hanji 802 broadens the evidence to the Yan-family succession-control branch, but it does not reconstruct the full biographies of every Yan brother, palace actor, or purged minister. Hanji 821 gives the first Liang office-succession layer but does not yet show full Liang domination. Hanji 822 gives the protected-corruption and direct-impeachment layer but does not preserve the exact fifteen charges or show full Liang domination yet. Hanji 827 gives the direct child-emperor poisoning layer but does not by itself reconstruct every mechanism of concealment after the death. Hanji 828 gives the direct accession-choice layer but does not reconstruct every participant's private reasoning. Hanji 830 shows that public reputation can resist terror, but it does not show that reputation alone can protect ministers from prison death. Hanji 833-846 preserve source-scoped numbers, income figures, estate details, punishment forms, and victim lists. The episode set also keeps an existing timing tension between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-847-nvren-biande-haoming-cong-dongde-zhe-liang-zi-kaishi-lrlov43jtz0vq-o-5m8r2cqo0etx|Hanji 847]], where Xuan survives a failed assassination attempt and helps catalyze the anti-Liang move, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-848-shunjian-kuatai-wo-weihe-shuo-ta-si-you-yu-gu-lgqvgmxlwga2fknmtxpj3ya9chk0|Hanji 848]], where Xuan is compressed into a death-triggered lead-up.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 766 as the earliest current Dou-family branch: household authorization, visible anti-Dou warnings, secret palace planning, and seal-centered liquidation.
- Preserved Hanji 802 as the Yan-family branch: concealed death, Beixiang Hou installation, Liu Bao exclusion, Yan office concentration, and purge of old networks.
- Preserved Hanji 821-822 as the first Liang office-succession and protected-corruption layers.
- Reframed Hanji 827-828 as imperial murder followed by succession capture after earlier protected impunity.
- Kept Hanji 829-848 as reward distortion, ministerial criminalization, terror, privilege expansion, household saturation, and palace-coup liquidation.
