## Definition

Eurasian Military Technology Exchange / 欧亚军事技术交流 is the source's frame for reading Qin-Han cavalry and related military developments as part of a wider ancient Eurasian field of possible contact, parallel development, and later two-way transmission.

## Current Synthesis

The episode argues that Qin-Han warfare should not be sealed inside a purely Chinese narrative. Zhang Shiping points to roughly contemporary Mediterranean and Central Asian warfare, possible early shock-cavalry traces in Chu-Han sources, the organizational requirements of no-stirrup cavalry, and later stirrup transmission from east to west. The synthesis is cautious: the evidence does not prove a simple line of influence, but it makes military technique, agrarian-state organization, and frontier interaction visible at the Eurasian scale.

## Key Claims

- Qin-Han military history can be compared with contemporary Mediterranean and Central Asian warfare without collapsing comparison into direct proof of borrowing.
- Early shock-cavalry clues in Chu-Han narratives are inferential but worth tracking because they cluster in the Chu-Han phase rather than earlier anti-Qin fighting.
- No-stirrup shock cavalry requires organization, training, horses, and disciplined formation, not only mounted individual courage.
- Military technology exchange is not one-way; later stirrup diffusion shows that eastern innovations could also travel west.

## Evidence

- Comparative frame: [[494-zhang-shi-ping-tan-zhan-zheng-shi-shi-jiao-xia-de-qin-mo-han-chu-hun-zhan-lrc9hn-yrw9lkwujl65ummq-qykj]] invokes wider ancient Eurasian warfare and the separation between Chinese history and world history as a problem to overcome.
- Cavalry-clue evidence: [[494-zhang-shi-ping-tan-zhan-zheng-shi-shi-jiao-xia-de-qin-mo-han-chu-hun-zhan-lrc9hn-yrw9lkwujl65ummq-qykj]] notes multiple Chu-Han descriptions that may indicate impact cavalry, while saying earlier anti-Qin episodes have fewer such traces.
- Organization evidence: [[494-zhang-shi-ping-tan-zhan-zheng-shi-shi-jiao-xia-de-qin-mo-han-chu-hun-zhan-lrc9hn-yrw9lkwujl65ummq-qykj]] argues that early no-stirrup impact cavalry often depends on agrarian-state organization, mobilization, and training.
- Two-way evidence: [[494-zhang-shi-ping-tan-zhan-zheng-shi-shi-jiao-xia-de-qin-mo-han-chu-hun-zhan-lrc9hn-yrw9lkwujl65ummq-qykj]] uses later east-to-west stirrup movement as a reminder that exchange can run in both directions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source treats the exchange claim as plausible and comparative, not settled. It does not prove that Hellenistic cavalry directly caused Chu-Han cavalry tactics, and it keeps archaeological and textual clues distinct from firm transmission history.
