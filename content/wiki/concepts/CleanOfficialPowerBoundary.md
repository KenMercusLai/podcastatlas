## Definition

清官权力边界 / clean official power boundary is the governance principle that personal incorruptibility, administrative courage, real enforcement success, and popular support do not legitimate private retaliation, unsupported killing, or counter-investigation through public authority.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 426 defines the concept through [[ZhaoGuanghanWesternHan|赵广汉]], while Hanji 415 backfills why his reputation had substance. Zhao's clean-official reputation is treated as real but insufficient: he had used anonymous reporting, elite-splitting tactics, subordinate management, and a fast information network to suppress strongman families in [[YingchuanCommandery|颍川郡]] and impose order in Chang'an. That positive capacity gives him legitimacy, yet the Su Xian, Rong Xu, and Wei Xiang branches show how a forceful official can convert information access, legal procedure, and coercive capacity into a weapon for personal anger.

The concept separates three qualities that are often collapsed in good-official stories: not taking bribes, being administratively effective, and using power lawfully. Zhao may have the first two in parts of his career, but fails the third. [[YinWengguiWesternHan|尹翁归 / 尹翁规]] supplies the contrast: strictness and clean conduct can still belong to ordered local governance when they are tied to public rules, equal treatment, and controlled discipline rather than revenge.

## Key Claims

- Clean reputation is not a sufficient standard for judging official goodness.
- Real anti-strongman enforcement success can coexist with later abuse of the same coercive and information tools.
- Anti-corruption tools and information networks can become abusive when redirected toward private retaliation.
- Killing or punishing on suspicion is a stronger boundary violation than harsh but rule-bound discipline.
- Popular affection for a capable official can become politically dangerous if it appears to challenge sovereign judgment.
- The good-official standard must combine integrity, capacity, procedural restraint, and humane limits.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not reject clean government, strong anti-corruption enforcement, or information-rich local administration. Hanji 415 shows those tools can protect ordinary people against entrenched families. The boundary appears when the same tools lose public-rule discipline and become revenge. Hanji 426 is also not a general defense of every execution of a popular official under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]; the source treats Zhao's misconduct as serious, while other Xuan-era deaths such as [[HanYanshouWesternHan|韩延寿]] and [[YangYunWesternHan|杨恽]] carry different proportionality questions.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 415's positive Zhao layer so the concept covers the boundary of genuinely useful enforcement tools, not only obvious misconduct.
- Clarified that anonymous reporting and information networks require public-rule discipline because their value and danger come from the same capacity.
