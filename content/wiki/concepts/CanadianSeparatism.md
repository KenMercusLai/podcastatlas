# Canadian Separatism

## Definition
Canadian separatism is the episode’s umbrella for provincial movements seeking independence from Canada, with distinct economic and cultural mechanisms in Alberta and Quebec.

## Current Synthesis
The source resists treating separatism as one national mood. Alberta’s pressure is tied to oil wealth, fiscal contribution, federal limits on resource development, and resentment of distant government. Quebec’s movement is tied to French language, culture, and civil law. Both become more politically dangerous when an external trade shock weakens growth and gives provincial actors reasons to blame Ottawa.

## Key Claims
- Alberta and Quebec separatism arise from different grievance structures.
- Referendum vote share matters even when a ballot is non-binding because it can preserve a movement’s momentum.
- Minority support can still create federal pressure when a regional government has incentives to intensify conflict.
- American trade pressure can amplify internal fragmentation by distributing sector and regional losses unevenly.
- Federal economic delivery and credible trade diversification are part of the unity response, not separate policy domains.

## Evidence
- Alberta’s resource and federation grievance - [[independence-eh-canadas-referendum-d1db236273c4dba7fc1e1a7b66cd08a1]].
- Quebec’s cultural basis, reported support level, and Parti Québécois strategy - [[independence-eh-canadas-referendum-d1db236273c4dba7fc1e1a7b66cd08a1]].
- External-shock interaction - [[independence-eh-canadas-referendum-d1db236273c4dba7fc1e1a7b66cd08a1]] connects separatist pressure to automotive, steel, aluminium, and wider trade uncertainty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source reports thresholds and political expectations rather than referendum outcomes. It does not show that either province will leave Canada, that trade conflict is the main cause of either movement, or that tax cuts and infrastructure acceleration will reduce separatist support.

## What Changed
- Created a comparative synthesis separating Alberta’s economic-resource case from Quebec’s cultural-national case.
