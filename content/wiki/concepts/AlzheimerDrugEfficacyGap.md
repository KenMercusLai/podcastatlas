# Alzheimer Drug Efficacy Gap

Alzheimer drug efficacy gap is [[working-memory-the-surprising-decline-of-dementia-6a6b0eeca585d3be78cd9d1b]]'s contrast between expensive drug development and modest treatment results. The episode says pharmaceutical companies have focused on drugs targeting protein tangles in Alzheimer patients' brains, but results have been disappointing: only a few months of improved cognition, with high cost and serious risk.

The source specifically notes that people carrying [[APOE4DementiaRisk|APOE4]] are more likely to experience brain swelling or bleeding when taking these drugs. It also says early studies of [[GLP1Agonists|GLP-1 drugs]] have not helped people who already have dementia, while leaving open whether earlier obesity or metabolic-risk intervention could matter.

## Key Claims
- Treatment gains for established dementia are described as limited in the source.
- Safety, cost, and genotype-specific risk matter when judging drug value.
- The gap strengthens the case for prevention research, not therapeutic nihilism.
