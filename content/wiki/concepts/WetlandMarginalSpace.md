# Wetland Marginal Space / 湿地边缘空间

## Definition
Wetland marginal space / 湿地边缘空间 is the literary-social frame in which a marsh or wetland becomes both habitat and social edge: a place that shelters excluded people while also marking them as outside ordinary community, property, school, law, and respectability.

## Current Synthesis
The Culture Limited episode uses [[WhereTheCrawdadsSing|《蝲蛄吟唱的地方》]] as the source case. The marsh lets [[KyaClark|基亚]] survive, observe, hide, and form knowledge, but it also gives the town a reason to treat her as dirty, low, and suspicious. The wetland is therefore not just romantic nature; it is a contested boundary where poverty, race, land ownership, development, schooling, evidence, and moral order all meet.

## Key Claims
- A wetland can be a refuge and a stigma at the same time.
- Marginal space produces local knowledge that formal institutions may fail to understand.
- Property and development pressure can turn ecological marginality into legal and economic vulnerability.
- Social hierarchy can treat people from edge spaces as suspect even when they share the dominant racial category.
- Literary wetlands can make nature/civilization conflict concrete without reducing nature to purity.

- Local knowledge: [[vol-350-lagu-yinchang-de-difang-nengbuneng-rangwo-ananjingjing-dangge-bianyuanren-gkwridooapxpaz2h0wtphrfr]] contrasts Kya's tidal, hiding, specimen, and natural-notebook knowledge with police procedure.
- Property/development pressure: [[vol-350-lagu-yinchang-de-difang-nengbuneng-rangwo-ananjingjing-dangge-bianyuanren-gkwridooapxpaz2h0wtphrfr]] discusses tax payment and developers' interest in the marsh.
- Hierarchy beyond race: [[vol-350-lagu-yinchang-de-difang-nengbuneng-rangwo-ananjingjing-dangge-bianyuanren-gkwridooapxpaz2h0wtphrfr]] says Kya remains suspect to town whites because marsh poverty lowers her status.
- Nature/civilization conflict: [[vol-350-lagu-yinchang-de-difang-nengbuneng-rangwo-ananjingjing-dangge-bianyuanren-gkwridooapxpaz2h0wtphrfr]] treats the marsh as the novel's central symbolic space.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is literary and social, not a general ecological claim about all wetlands. It should stay separate from [[WetlandAdaptation]], which focuses on water-management livelihoods and floodplain systems rather than literary marginality.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the episode's reading of marsh, exclusion, land, and survival.
