## Definition

Outer-relative preventive restraint is the court-governance pattern of blocking or warning against a ruler's maternal or in-law relatives turning kinship into office, title, guest networks, spending privilege, marriage leverage, or legal exemption before they become an entrenched power bloc.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 709 now supplies an early Guangwu-era Yin-family version of the pattern before the later Ma-family evidence. [[YinXingGuangwuMaternalKin|阴兴]] refuses reward and tells [[YinLihua|阴丽华]] that their family must be satisfied and restrained rather than using imperial proximity for offices, titles, marriages, or display. The source makes restraint a household survival strategy as well as a public-order requirement: outer-relative excess can create resentment and future danger.

Hanji 745 adds the warning version before Hanji 748's stronger household restraint. After [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]] succeeds [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]], he wants to appoint [[MaLiaoEasternHan|Ma Liao]], [[MaFangEasternHan|Ma Fang]], and Ma Guang to visible offices. [[FifthLunEasternHan|Fifth Lun]] sees officials and scholars attaching themselves to the [[MaFamilyZhangEraEasternHan|Ma family]], reports large private gifts of cloth, cash, sheep, grain, and meat, and frames the warning as a way to protect both the state and Empress Ma's household before the pattern hardens.

Hanji 748 defines the most elaborated preventive version through [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Mingde Empress Ma]] in the first year of Zhangdi's reign. Zhangdi wants to enfeoff his maternal uncles as an old Han custom and as a public signal of generosity. Empress Ma refuses because she reads the same act through dynastic risk: Former Han failed after outer relatives became too powerful, [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]] and Mingdi avoided that mistake, and the Ma brothers have not earned central political power.

The restraint is active rather than symbolic. It can appear as Yin Xing's refusal and family advice, Fifth Lun's warning against guests and gifts, or Empress Ma's direct blocking of enfeoffment, allowance cuts, legal instructions against clan interference, correction of an over-height family tomb, and rewards for frugal royal relatives. The concept therefore sits before [[OuterRelativeCourtPower|outer-relative court power]] and [[OuterRelativeCommandAppointmentRisk|outer-relative command appointment risk]]: it names the preventive moment when honor can still be separated from power.

## Key Claims

- Outer-relative danger can be reduced before formal capture by refusing title, office, spending, marriage leverage, and legal exemptions for natal kin.
- Restraint can come from within the favored household as well as from ministers or the ruler's spouse.
- Historical precedent matters because earlier consort-family disasters turn a seemingly generous family reward into a dynastic-risk signal.
- Preventive restraint must govern household conduct as well as court appointments; luxury, tomb scale, attendants, gifts, and allowances all communicate privilege.
- Guest networks and large gifts are early warning signs because they let private kinship status become public access before formal capture.
- Omen or disaster language can be used to launder a favor demand, so restraint requires reading who benefits from the proposed interpretation.
- Positive example and negative enforcement work together: frugal conduct is rewarded, while kinship interference is made legally punishable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source set proves a prevention-and-warning pattern, not a permanent solution. Hanji 709 shows Yin Xing as an unusually self-limiting beneficiary, not proof that all favored households will restrain themselves. Hanji 745 is a ministerial warning about appointment and guest-network risk, not proof that the Ma brothers have already captured policy. Later Hanji 749-752 material shows that Ma-family command, title, and post-Empress-Ma overexposure still became problems, so preventive restraint needs succession and continuity beyond one disciplined patron. The drought argument is preserved as a court claim in Hanji 748, but the wiki treats Empress Ma's skepticism as the source's controlling interpretation rather than endorsing or falsifying omen causation independently.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 709 as the early Yin-family refusal and household-advice layer.
- Broadened the concept from Ma-family restraint to a recurring Eastern Han outer-relative prevention pattern.
- Preserved Hanji 745 as the early Fifth Lun warning layer around Ma-family office, guests, and gifts.
- Preserved Hanji 748 as the most elaborated Empress Ma household-restraint case.
