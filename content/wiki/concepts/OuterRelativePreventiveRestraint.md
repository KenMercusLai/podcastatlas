## Definition

Outer-relative preventive restraint is the court-governance pattern of blocking or warning against a ruler's maternal or in-law relatives turning kinship into office, title, guest networks, spending privilege, or legal exemption before they become an entrenched power bloc.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 745 adds the warning version of the pattern before Hanji 748's stronger household restraint. After [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]] succeeds [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]], he wants to appoint [[MaLiaoEasternHan|Ma Liao]], [[MaFangEasternHan|Ma Fang]], and Ma Guang to visible offices. [[FifthLunEasternHan|Fifth Lun]] sees officials and scholars attaching themselves to the [[MaFamilyZhangEraEasternHan|Ma family]], reports large private gifts of cloth, cash, sheep, grain, and meat, and frames the warning as a way to protect both the state and Empress Ma's household before the pattern hardens.

Hanji 748 defines the preventive version through [[EmpressMaMingdeEasternHan|Mingde Empress Ma]] in the first year of Zhangdi's reign. Zhangdi wants to enfeoff his maternal uncles as an old Han custom and as a public signal of generosity. Empress Ma refuses because she reads the same act through dynastic risk: Former Han failed after outer relatives became too powerful, [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]] and Mingdi avoided that mistake, and the Ma brothers have not earned central political power.

The restraint is active rather than symbolic. Empress Ma rejects a drought-based memorial that treats failure to enfeoff outer relatives as a cosmic problem, cuts her brothers' annual allowances, orders senior ministers to punish Ma clan members who use kinship to disturb government, has [[MaLiaoEasternHan|Ma Liao]] correct an over-height family tomb, and rewards frugal royal relatives as public examples. The concept therefore sits before [[OuterRelativeCourtPower|outer-relative court power]] and [[OuterRelativeCommandAppointmentRisk|outer-relative command appointment risk]]: it names the preventive moment when honor can still be separated from power.

## Key Claims

- Outer-relative danger can be reduced before formal capture by refusing title, office, spending, and legal exemptions for natal kin.
- Historical precedent matters because earlier consort-family disasters turn a seemingly generous family reward into a dynastic-risk signal.
- Preventive restraint must govern household conduct as well as court appointments; luxury, tomb scale, attendants, and allowances all communicate privilege.
- Guest networks and large gifts are early warning signs because they let private kinship status become public access before formal capture.
- Omen or disaster language can be used to launder a favor demand, so restraint requires reading who benefits from the proposed interpretation.
- The ruler's affection for a raising-based maternal family can be legitimate while still needing institutional limits.
- Positive example and negative enforcement work together: frugal conduct is rewarded, while kinship interference is made legally punishable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source set proves a prevention-and-warning pattern, not a permanent solution. Hanji 745 is a ministerial warning about appointment and guest-network risk, not proof that the Ma brothers have already captured policy; later Hanji 749-752 material shows that Ma-family command, title, and post-Empress-Ma overexposure still became problems, so preventive restraint needs succession and continuity beyond one disciplined patron. The drought argument is preserved as a court claim in Hanji 748, but the wiki treats Empress Ma's skepticism as the source's controlling interpretation rather than endorsing or falsifying omen causation independently.
