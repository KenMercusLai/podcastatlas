# Institutional Forgetting by Omission

## Definition

Institutional forgetting by omission is the reduction of public responsibility and visibility when an institution or cultural narrative leaves out an origin, actor, victim, or causal link without explicitly denying that the event occurred.

## Current Synthesis

[[goodbye-farewell-and-amen-goodbye-farewell-and-amen]] supplies two mechanisms. Harvard celebrates many inventions but leaves napalm without a plaque or comparable institutional history, separating technical achievement from its destructive legacy. *M*A*S*H* shows an incendiary attack but does not identify its source, allowing Americans to occupy the remembered position of victims while their air campaign disappears from the scene.

Omission is powerful because the remaining facts can still look true. The scientists had distinguished later careers, the television scene remained anti-war, and the Korean War could still be described as tragic. What changes is the distribution of agency and accountability. Survivor testimony and a dedicated museum can restore visibility, but small corrective institutions may not match the reach of elite universities, official honors, or mass television.

## Key Claims

- Forgetting can occur through missing attribution rather than false assertion.
- Institutional celebration selects which innovations receive public lineage and which consequences remain detached from prestige.
- Universal moral framing can weaken specific accountability when it removes who acted upon whom.
- Later respectable or beneficial work can crowd out unresolved attention to earlier destructive work.
- Corrective museums and testimony restore names and consequences but operate under unequal conditions of reach.

### Corrective memory

- [[goodbye-farewell-and-amen-goodbye-farewell-and-amen]] closes at Tokyo's small air-raid museum, where material evidence and survivor testimony return civilians to the story.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Absence does not prove a coordinated decision to suppress history. A missing plaque, sparse historiography, or ambiguous fictional scene can result from many motives and constraints. Universal anti-war storytelling can also reach audiences who would reject partisan attribution. The concept identifies an effect on visibility and responsibility; it does not establish intent without further evidence.

## What Changed

- Established omission as a mechanism that can preserve surface facts while redistributing agency, victimhood, and accountability.
