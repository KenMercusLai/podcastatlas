## Definition

限期赦免式叛军分化 / deadline amnesty for rebellion fragmentation is a counterinsurgency mechanism in which a ruler offers followers immunity if they surrender before an advancing punitive force arrives, separating their survival incentives from the rebel leader's fate.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-2-lntutfvfj3vdirbrjxlg1d5vsiip|Hanji 213]] supplies the current case. As [[ChaiWu|柴武]] advances against [[LiuXingjuWesternHan|刘兴居]], [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] reportedly pardons Jibei officials and residents who submit before the army arrives. The deadline makes delay costly, offers a lawful exit to people who can claim they were misled, and isolates the prince from the broader population and administrative apparatus needed to sustain rebellion.

The mechanism complements rather than replaces coercion. The pardon is credible because a large field force is approaching, while military victory becomes cheaper if prospective defenders defect first. The episode attributes the rebellion's loss of confidence and fighting capacity to this interaction, though it does not quantify defections or establish the pardon as the sole cause of defeat.

## Key Claims

- A surrender deadline turns the punitive army's approach into pressure for immediate defection.
- Selective pardon distinguishes followers described as misled from the rebel leader who remains liable.
- The policy attacks political cohesion and administrative compliance before attacking every position militarily.
- Amnesty and force are mutually reinforcing: force supplies urgency, while amnesty can reduce resistance costs.
- Causal claims about decisive effect require evidence about actual surrender behavior, not only an edict and later victory.

Claimed cohesion effect:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-2-lntutfvfj3vdirbrjxlg1d5vsiip|Hanji 213]] interprets the offer as destroying rebel confidence and combat cohesion before Liu Xingju's defeat and suicide.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One compact episode cannot isolate the amnesty's causal effect from the approaching army, command decisions, local loyalty, resource differences, or battlefield outcomes. The source gives no surrender count, participant testimony, enforcement record, or comparison with a similar rebellion lacking amnesty. “Decisive” therefore remains the host's interpretation; the durable claim is narrower—that a credible deadline can change follower incentives while coercive capacity remains visible.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the Jibei surrender-edict sequence.
- Distinguished cohesion effects from the stronger unproven claim that pardon alone defeated the rebellion.
