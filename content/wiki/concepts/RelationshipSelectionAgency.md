## Definition
Relationship Selection Agency is the source-scoped idea that a person's life direction is partly shaped by the spouses, partners, bosses, friends, and everyday environments they choose or are forced to stay near. Agency appears less as total control over fate and more as disciplined selection of relational and situational influences, with strong qualifications where hierarchy or family power limits real choice.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 845 adds a marriage-specific branch. The opening treats marriage as a legal, social, economic, and household arrangement rather than only romance, then uses late marriage and family pressure to show why spouse choice is felt as consequential. The historical contrast is intentionally sharp: Liang Ying's imperial marriage is not a modern elective partnership but a family-backed empress arrangement where body inspection, status, jealousy, childlessness, and Huan's avoidance replace mutual selection.

Hanji 847 opens with "choice" as a practical lens: choosing a partner affects marriage, choosing friends and bosses affects one's social field, and remaining near pessimistic, complaining, petty, or excuse-driven people lets their habits become part of one's own environment. The concept is therefore about selective exposure and boundary-setting rather than isolated willpower.

The historical narrative then turns the same frame political. Sun Shou's household power, Deng Meng's palace placement, Xuan's appeal to Emperor Huan, and Huan's choice of Tang Heng and other close attendants all show that outcomes depend on which relational channels are selected, trusted, or resisted.

## Key Claims
- People are shaped by repeated proximity to partners, friends, bosses, and habitual social environments.
- Spouse choice is a high-stakes version because marriage joins affection, law, economics, household labor, kin expectation, and future children.
- Discernment about whom to trust is a form of agency even when larger structures remain unequal.
- Relationship choice can protect a person from corrosive habits, but it can also pull them into coercive networks.
- Palace politics is an extreme version of the same pattern: access, favor, and danger travel through chosen or inherited relationship channels.
- The concept fits best as a source-scoped bridge between self-governance advice and late-Han political narrative, not as a deterministic theory of personal fate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not be treated as a claim that individuals fully control outcomes through attitude or social choice. Hanji 845's survey statistics are source-scoped because city, institution, and sampling detail are incomplete in the supplied note. Hanji 845 and Hanji 847 both include coercion, harem dependence, family power, assassination risk, and imperial surveillance, all of which limit agency.

The episode title frames the lesson around women becoming "better fated"; this page retains the broader selection-and-environment mechanism while treating that gendered advice frame as source-specific.

## What Changed
- Hanji 845 adds a marriage-specific branch and clarifies the concept's constraint boundary through Liang Ying's non-elective dynastic marriage.
- Hanji 847 creates the concept as the episode's opening interpretive frame and connects it to late-Han household, harem, and palace-access politics.
