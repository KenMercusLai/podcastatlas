# Masonic Secrecy–Conspiracy Feedback

## Definition

Masonic secrecy-conspiracy feedback is the cycle in which ritual concealment, symbolic eclecticism, and translocal networks invite hostile totalizing interpretation, which in turn increases defensiveness, mystery, persecution, and the apparent plausibility of hidden-power claims.

## Current Synthesis

The cycle does not require extraordinary hidden doctrine. Dramatic oaths and restricted degrees can make ordinary moral instruction feel sacred, while borrowed symbols and mythic ancestry supply outsiders with ambiguous material. Political or religious opponents then reinterpret ambiguity as evidence of one coordinated enemy, and repression or secrecy produces further gaps that the theory can absorb.

The cycle must retain a reality check. [[LeoTaxil]] shows that fabricated revelation can exploit the mechanism; Francoist repression shows its coercive consequences; [[PropagandaDue|P2]] shows that a real secret network can sometimes occupy the space imagined by broader theories. A demonstrated case should narrow inquiry toward its evidence, not license a universal story.

## Key Claims

- Concealment can increase perceived significance even when the concealed content is conventional.
- Eclectic symbols and invented antiquity create interpretive surplus for both members and opponents.
- Existing institutional conflict makes fabricated insider revelation easier to accept.
- Repression can convert speculative enemy categories into administrative and penal harm.
- A real covert network can validate a specific allegation while misleadingly amplifying unrelated claims.
- Evidence-bound analysis distinguishes particular coordination from a total hidden-order worldview.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Secrecy alone does not prove wrongdoing, and exposure of a hoax does not prove institutional innocence. The episode's accounts of Catholic belief, Francoist totals, fake intelligence, and P2 wrongdoing remain source-scoped. The concept applies only when it preserves differences among ambiguity, fabrication, reasonable suspicion, documented coordination, and overgeneralization.
