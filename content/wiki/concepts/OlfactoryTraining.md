# Olfactory Training

## Definition
Olfactory training is the practice of repeatedly and attentively smelling safe, non-noxious odors to tune smell perception and support smell recovery or refinement.

## Current Synthesis
The Q&A episode treats smell as trainable because olfactory neurons turn over across life and smell-related tuning is activity-dependent. The recommended practice is deliberately simple: use safe odors such as lemon, rose, chocolate, or coffee; sniff repeatedly; inhale longer; close the eyes when possible; and direct attention toward the odor.

The concept fits the wiki's broader sensory branch by showing that smell is not only branding or memory atmosphere. It can also be a health-relevant sensory system where attention, repeated exposure, and early practice after illness or injury may matter.

## Key Claims
- Smell ability is presented as improvable through repeated exposure to non-noxious odors.
- Attention is part of the training; the person should focus on the odor rather than sniff passively.
- Longer inhales and closing the eyes are framed as ways to increase sensory attention.
- Olfactory neurons turning over across life makes smell a plausible target for activity-dependent tuning.
- The practice may be especially relevant after respiratory illness or some smell-related injury when started early.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not make olfactory training a universal cure for anosmia, neurological disease, toxic exposure, or chronic sinus problems. Loss of smell after illness or injury can require medical evaluation, and the episode's examples remain practical guidance rather than a complete treatment protocol.
