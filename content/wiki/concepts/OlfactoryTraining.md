# Olfactory Training

## Definition
Olfactory training is repeated, attentive exposure to safe, non-noxious odors intended to refine smell perception or support recovery after some forms of smell loss.

## Current Synthesis
Across the three episodes, smell is treated as trainable through repeated exposure and focused attention. Suggested materials include safe familiar odors such as lemon, rose, chocolate, coffee, or orange; the method emphasizes deliberate sniffing, longer inhalation, eye closure when practical, and noticing increasingly fine perceptual detail.

The evidence is strongest as a low-risk sensory practice and qualified rehabilitation adjunct, not a universal cure. Olfactory fibers can be damaged after head trauma, illness can alter smell, and prognosis varies with cause and duration. The Sobel conversation says training has stronger support than alpha-lipoic acid and describes smell as use-dependent, while preserving a guarded recovery horizon after severe traumatic loss.

## Key Claims
- Repeated exposure to safe odors can refine smell discrimination and perceptual detail.
- Attention is part of the training; passive exposure is not treated as equivalent practice.
- Longer inhales and eye closure are framed as optional ways to increase sensory attention.
- The practice may be relevant after respiratory illness or some smell-related injury when matched to cause and timing.
- After head injury, smell recovery may be informative but cannot independently establish neurological recovery.
- Training is better supported in the bounded source than alpha-lipoic acid, but neither guarantees recovery.

## Evidence
- Practice materials and method - [[your-top-health-questions-answered-scim2882548864]] recommends safe familiar odors, repeated sniffing, longer inhalation, eye closure, and focused attention.
- Biological rationale - [[your-top-health-questions-answered-scim2882548864]] frames smell tuning as activity-dependent, while [[essentials-how-smell-taste-pheromones-shape-behavior-scim3853502826]] uses repeated orange smelling to illustrate rapid perceptual refinement.
- Injury context - [[essentials-how-smell-taste-pheromones-shape-behavior-scim3853502826]] and [[how-smells-influence-our-hormones-health-behavior-dr-noam-sobel-scim2074118783]] connect smell loss to olfactory-fiber injury near the cribriform plate.
- Comparative support - [[how-smells-influence-our-hormones-health-behavior-dr-noam-sobel-scim2074118783]] presents olfactory training as better supported than alpha-lipoic acid and makes prognosis conditional on duration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not make olfactory training a cure for anosmia, neurodegenerative disease, toxic exposure, chronic sinus disease, or traumatic brain injury. Sudden or persistent smell loss and smell change after injury can require medical evaluation. The exact protocol, effect size, ideal start time, cause-specific response, and recovery cutoff are not established by these condensed source notes.
