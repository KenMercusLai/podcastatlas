# 徙木立信 / Moving the Log to Build Trust

徙木立信 / moving the log to build trust is the public-credibility episode [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-1-lgmjapc2eecefbdshnpenzfcv6ga]] uses before [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] issues the new law. Shang Yang places a three-zhang log at the capital's south gate and promises gold to whoever moves it to the north gate. When people do not believe the notice, he raises the reward; when someone moves the log, he pays.

The point is not the task itself. The episode reads it as an engineered proof that [[QinState|秦国]]'s reform government will honor public rewards and punishments literally. That makes it adjacent to [[SmallTrustBuildsLargeTrust|小信诚则大信立]], but with a sharper legalist edge: small credibility is built to prepare society for coercive, high-stakes enforcement.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-2-lk-d9uibhneerfol6z2rrle-6ypm]] returns to the story through [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s praise of "信." In that commentary, Ximu Lixin becomes one of four political credibility cases alongside [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]], [[JinWenGong|晋文公]], and [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]. The source emphasizes that Sima Guang can condemn [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]'s harshness while still recognizing the governing force of a kept promise.

## Key Claims
- Public trust can be built through a deliberately simple, observable transaction.
- A state's reward promise becomes more believable when it is fulfilled in front of skeptical people.
- Trust is used instrumentally here, as a precondition for enforcing [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]].
- The story contrasts credibility based on kept promises with legitimacy based on consultation or custom.
- Sima Guang's later use of the story places Legalist credibility inside a wider statecraft argument about ruler trustworthiness.
