# Memory Reconsolidation Psychiatry

## Definition
Memory reconsolidation psychiatry is the treatment hypothesis that trauma-linked memories or maladaptive rules can become temporarily revisable when reactivated in a highly plastic or altered state.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses PTSD and trauma to explain why the brain may hold rules that once protected survival but later become maladaptive. [[NolanWilliams]] says battlefield hypervigilance and related responses can make sense in one environment and become disabling in ordinary life. Psychedelic or MDMA-assisted states are discussed as possible ways to re-experience memory, self-judgment, and threat responses with enough plasticity to update them.

This concept is related to [[Neuroplasticity]] but narrower. Its distinctive claim is that the target is not only mood or insight, but the reconsolidation of memory-linked rules: fear, guilt, responsibility, empathy, and self-forgiveness may be processed differently under carefully supported conditions. The strongest caution is that the source treats this as hypothesis plus early clinical evidence, not as a proven universal trauma protocol.

## Key Claims
- Trauma symptoms can preserve once-adaptive survival responses after the original context has ended.
- Altered or highly plastic states may allow memories to be re-experienced and reconsolidated.
- MDMA is presented as having anti-PTSD signals after one to a few clinical sessions.
- Ibogaine is linked to life-review experiences and moral-injury processing, but its data and safety risks remain bounded.
- Psilocybin and ketamine are discussed as overlapping with network and plasticity mechanisms, but durability differs.
- Memory reconsolidation claims require clinical support because re-experiencing trauma can be destabilizing.

## Evidence
- PTSD adaptation frame - [[essentials-psychedelics-neurostimulation-for-brain-rewiring-dr-nolan-williams-scim5296180263]] describes battlefield-adaptive responses becoming maladaptive at home.
- MDMA and plasticity - [[essentials-psychedelics-neurostimulation-for-brain-rewiring-dr-nolan-williams-scim5296180263]] says MDMA and psilocybin may place people in a plastic state where memories can be re-experienced and updated.
- Moral injury branch - [[essentials-psychedelics-neurostimulation-for-brain-rewiring-dr-nolan-williams-scim5296180263]] describes ibogaine life-review reports and self-forgiveness in special-operations participants while noting incomplete analysis and cardiac risk.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page does not establish a treatment protocol for PTSD, depression, trauma, moral injury, or guilt. The source explicitly ties psychedelic use to rigorous trials and clinical supervision, and ibogaine is singled out as higher-risk because of cardiac effects.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish trauma-memory updating from general psychedelic, meditation, or neuroplasticity claims.
