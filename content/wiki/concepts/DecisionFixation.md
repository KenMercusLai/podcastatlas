## Definition

Decision fixation is persistence in an initial interpretation or action path after accumulating evidence should trigger reassessment.

## Current Synthesis

[[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1|Memorial Day, 2020 | Part 1]] presents fixation as more than overlooking a warning. A fixed interpretation can absorb each warning as confirmation: respiratory struggle becomes resistance, a first responder becomes a threat, weakening speech becomes compliance, and immobility fails to trigger a medical reset.

The corrective is a deliberate restart. When observed conditions no longer fit the working account, the decision maker reconstructs the situation from current evidence rather than merely adjusting the original story. In policing, the episode argues, this is especially important because control scripts can turn fear, distress, and medical crisis into apparent defiance.

## Key Claims

- An initially plausible interpretation can become dangerous when it is treated as a fixed frame rather than a revisable hypothesis.
- Fixation can reinterpret disconfirming evidence as further support for the original path.
- Repeated, independent warnings strengthen the case for stopping and reconstructing the situation.
- Expertise is protective only when it includes a practiced ability to restart analysis.
- High-stakes control settings require active discrimination among resistance, fear, ambiguity, and medical distress.

## Evidence

### Warnings absorbed into the original script

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] describes warnings from Floyd's condition, bystanders, an off-duty firefighter, another officer, and a missing pulse, while Chauvin continued the restraint.

### Deliberate analytical restart

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] uses [[GaryKlein]]'s intelligence and medical examples to contrast assumption-preserving interpretation with starting the analysis over after unexpected evidence.

### Repeated pattern and institutional significance

- [[memorial-day-2020-part-1-memorial-day-2020-part-1]] compares Floyd's restraint with Chauvin's reported 2017 treatment of a 14-year-old, making failure to recognize the pattern an institutional problem as well as an in-the-moment error.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The framework does not establish Chauvin's private thoughts, developmental history, or motive, and it does not make racism or direct agency irrelevant. Some apparent resistance and danger are real, so the corrective is evidence-sensitive reassessment rather than automatic benign interpretation. The source does not independently validate the trial, medical, training, or 2017-incident details it summarizes.

## What Changed

- Established fixation as an evidence-update failure in which warnings can be recoded as confirmation.
- Added deliberate restart and repeated-pattern review as individual and institutional safeguards.
