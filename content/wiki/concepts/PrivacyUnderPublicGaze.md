# Privacy Under Public Gaze / 公共凝视下的隐私

[[PrivacyUnderPublicGaze|Privacy under public gaze]] is the privacy problem [[12-man-dang-milan-kundela-yi-ju-zhengjing-meiyou-549335675]] develops around [[MilanKundera|Milan Kundera]] and [[SlowKundera|《慢》]]. The episode contrasts political police intrusion in the Soviet sphere with journalist and media intrusion in the West, while also showing characters inside the novel chasing or fearing cameras.

The concept links two pressures that can look ideologically opposite. In one setting, privacy is broken by state surveillance and political repression; in another, it is consumed by publicity, celebrity, journalism, and audience hunger. Kundera's own attempts to avoid photographs, interviews, and ordinary identification become part of the same source-scoped tension.

## Key Claims
- Privacy can be destroyed by coercive state monitoring or by publicity systems that turn a person into content.
- The wish to escape the public gaze can itself become a public posture.
- Media visibility can help victims and causes, but it can also make selfhood dependent on audience management.
