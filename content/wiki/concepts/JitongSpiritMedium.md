# Jitong Spirit Medium / 鸡童（乩童）

Jitong spirit medium / 鸡童（乩童） is the ritual-mediation role foregrounded in [[ep249-minnan-gucheng-youshenji-chuantong-yishi-beihou-de-xiangtu-wangluo-lpk1vb9-zmgmuous1nyzefgapgww]]. The source uses the spelling "鸡童" and treats the role as the point where a deity communicates through a human body during Minnan ritual practice.

[[CaiXiaochuan|蔡小川]] describes early-morning [[Chenghuang|城隍]] temple scenes in which a person identified as possessed is taken into the main hall, changes clothes, and uses swords, axes, whips, or similar implements on the body. Blood is wiped onto yellow or gold paper, and believers compete for the resulting talisman-like paper. The point is not spectacle alone; the episode treats the body, blood, waiting, night timing, and crowd response as evidence of "god present" experience.

[[ZhangCongzhi|张从志]] gives the interpretive frame by citing the view that without a medium, folk ritual cannot complete its communication function. That is why an official cultural-festival version without jitong could be criticized in the episode as lacking local groundedness: it displayed heritage but removed one of the mechanisms through which participants recognized the ritual as effective.

## Key Claims
- Jitong is a mediumship role, not merely a performer in a procession.
- The role gives [[ChineseFolkReligionLayering]] a bodily communication channel between local deity and human community.
- Official or tourism-facing ritual can look cleaner and still feel incomplete if it removes the mediumship layer.
- Professional jitong hiring can solve a labor shortage while making some locals doubt the practice's groundedness.
- The source keeps the role gendered in practice: women appear in many surrounding ritual activities, but the episode does not observe women as core jitong figures in Yongning.
