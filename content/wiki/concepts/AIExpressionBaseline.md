# AI Expression Baseline / AI 表达基线

## Definition
AI expression baseline is E46's claim that AI has raised the minimum quality and accessibility of knowledge gathering and ordinary expression. Human expression remains valuable when it carries importance, sincerity, personal experience, and a meaningful departure from what a model or consensus baseline can already produce.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is a creative-standard page, not a productivity metric. [[MengYan]] says he has continued reading, working, watching films, and building software with AI, but feels less need to publish unless the thought departs from the new baseline and matters enough to say. This connects the episode to [[AIAuthorshipPresence]] and [[AIContentDevaluation]]: AI abundance does not erase human voice, but it raises the burden on the speaker to make their attention and lived frame visible.

## Key Claims
- AI lowers the effort needed to retrieve knowledge and express competent views.
- The value of human expression moves toward sincerity, personal experience, and non-generic framing.
- Not publishing can be a standard-setting choice when the speaker does not yet believe the thought matters enough.
- AI assistance is compatible with authorship when the human remains responsible for the question, judgment, and deviation.

## Evidence
- Creative threshold: [[e46-women-congwei-kanjian-bici-lmliksaiwxakmjbn-p2voaaoenxh]] opens with Meng Yan explaining that AI has raised the content baseline and made him more selective about writing or recording.
- Human voice condition: [[e46-women-congwei-kanjian-bici-lmliksaiwxakmjbn-p2voaaoenxh]] frames the episode as worth making because the issue feels important and personally grounded.
- AI continuity: [[e46-women-congwei-kanjian-bici-lmliksaiwxakmjbn-p2voaaoenxh]] does not reject AI; it mentions using AI to write software while asking what remains worth saying as a person.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a universal quality threshold for creators. The baseline is a first-person standard for Meng Yan's podcast practice, so it should be applied carefully to other domains where speed, accessibility, or routine summarization may still be valuable.
