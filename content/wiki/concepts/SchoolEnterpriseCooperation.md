# School-Enterprise Cooperation / 校企合作

School-enterprise cooperation / 校企合作 is the training arrangement in which schools and companies jointly shape vocational learning, practice scenes, and hiring pipelines. [[ep241-xiaoqi-hezuo-shi-xinyidai-de-tiefanwan-ma-lnmpsbvoqbexeardxyx88hywufig]] treats it as a serious but conditional path rather than an automatic "iron rice bowl."

The source compares [[Bosch|博世]], [[LiAuto|理想汽车]], and elder-care institutions to show that cooperation is only strong when the enterprise has a real reason to invest, the school can adapt curriculum and management, students recognize the occupation, and the industry still has demand. Weak cooperation can become a nameplate, a one-time internship, or a project that disappears when the company or school strategy changes.

## Key Claims
- Employers use cooperation to bring recruiting earlier, teach firm-specific standards, and create a long-term talent reservoir.
- Schools use cooperation to improve employment outcomes and keep majors aligned with changing local industries.
- Cooperation depth varies: [[OrderClassVocationalEducation|订单班]], [[IndustrialCollege|产业学院]], shared training bases, and [[DualSystemVocationalEducation|双元制]] carry different obligations.
- Student retention is a hard constraint because companies may invest heavily and still lose students to other firms, exams, further study, or other industries.
- The stable-job promise works only when enterprise demand, skill progression, pay, and student fit all remain credible.
