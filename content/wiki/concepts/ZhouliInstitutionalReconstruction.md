## Definition
Zhouli Institutional Reconstruction is Western Wei's use of offices, dress, and political language modeled on the *Rites of Zhou* to build a new administrative order.

## Current Synthesis
The episode rejects a simple opposition between “Xianbeiization” and classical restoration. Bestowed Xianbei identities could bind military forces, while Zhouli language recruited culturally skilled Han officials, supplied legitimacy, and authorized a break with the accumulated complexity of Han–Wei offices. Because no earlier dynasty had fully implemented the textual scheme, apparent archaism could function as institutional innovation. The practical claims—less duplication and greater efficiency—remain plausible programmatic aims rather than measured outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Classical restoration and Xianbei political symbolism could be complementary rather than contradictory.
- Zhouli language supplied legitimacy for reorganizing offices and dress.
- A never-fully-realized ancient model could authorize genuinely new institutions.
- The system could lower barriers created by inherited Hu–Han identities and bureaucratic ranks.
- Criticism of Han–Wei complexity supported simplification and removal of duplicate offices.
- Administrative efficiency is an intended effect in the source, not a demonstrated quantitative result.

## Evidence
- Parallel programs: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] places surname revival beside Zhouli-based offices and dress.
- Coalition function: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] attributes military cohesion to one idiom and literate recruitment to the other.
- Innovation reading: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] notes that prior dynasties had not fully practiced the textual structure.
- Simplification aim: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] reports Yuwen Tai's criticism of proliferating Han–Wei offices.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source summary does not map textual offices to every functioning institution or measure administrative performance. “Breaking Hu–Han identity” is an interpretive effect and should not imply that ethnic and status distinctions disappeared.

## What Changed
- Established archaizing Zhouli reform as a coalition-building innovation as well as a legitimacy claim.
