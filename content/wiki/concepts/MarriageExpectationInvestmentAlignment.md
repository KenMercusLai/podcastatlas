# Marriage Expectation-Investment Alignment

## Definition
Marriage expectation-investment alignment is the source's principle that what partners ask a marriage to provide should be matched by the time, attention, money, psychological energy, and wider support available to sustain those functions.

## Current Synthesis
The episode places this alignment problem inside a historical transition. Marriage once coordinated survival, labor, property, and alliance; romantic companionship later became central; contemporary couples may additionally expect esteem, authenticity, identity support, novelty, and self-actualization. The higher aspirations are not judged inherently excessive, but they require more flexible and attentive relational work than fixed economic or gender roles alone.

Misalignment produces the episode's “suffocation” problem: couples retain ambitious expectations while limited time, parenting demands, exhaustion, financial strain, or weak social networks reduce the resources available to meet them. Realignment can occur through more investment, different interpretations of difficulty, or selective demand reduction supported by [[RelationshipNeedDiversification]].

## Key Claims
- Marriage expectations are historically variable rather than timeless.
- Romantic love, companionship, esteem, authenticity, and self-development require different resources from economic coordination or fixed role specialization.
- High expectations can increase both fragility and the upper range of possible fulfillment.
- Resource scarcity from parenting, overwork, exhaustion, or financial strain can create mismatch without proving lack of love or compatibility.
- Couples can respond by increasing investment, improving interpretation and repair, or selectively meeting some needs outside the marriage.
- Expectation reduction can strengthen commitment when it removes role overload rather than suppressing essential needs or tolerating harm.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a broad U.S.-centered historical and psychological model, not a universal sequence for every society or couple. Earlier marriages could contain love and growth, while modern couples differ in what they want from marriage. Lowering expectations is not an answer to abuse, coercion, neglect of basic commitments, or chronic danger; resource alignment also cannot substitute for material support when poverty and overwork constrain relationship investment.

## What Changed
