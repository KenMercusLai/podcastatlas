# Internet History Fragility

Internet history fragility is the risk that the record of the web disappears, narrows, or becomes unreliable because preservation depends on vulnerable institutions, permissions, and technical systems. [[tech-20260421-0421-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260421-0421-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through news publishers blocking the [[WaybackMachine|Wayback Machine]] and through [[AndrewDeck]]'s warning that important publications may fall out of the public archive.

The episode treats fragility as both institutional and political. A nonprofit archive can be underfunded or blocked by rights holders, while a government archive such as the [[LibraryOfCongress|Library of Congress]] can raise concerns about state influence over what is preserved or made visible.

## Key Claims
- Web history is fragile because online pages can vanish, change, move behind paywalls, or become uncrawlable.
- A small number of archivists and institutions can carry an outsized burden for preserving public memory.
- Publisher blocking can create gaps precisely in high-value public-interest material such as news archives.
- Government archiving can add durability but also creates concern about official control over the historical record.
- Journalists lose verification capacity when removed or changed pages cannot be compared against archived versions.
