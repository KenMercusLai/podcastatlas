# Scientist Mode Thinking

## Definition
Scientist mode thinking is a stance in which opinions are treated as hypotheses and decisions as experiments, so the aim becomes learning what is true rather than defending identity or winning approval.

## Current Synthesis
[[AdamGrant]] contrasts scientist mode with three common social postures: preaching to protect a belief, prosecuting to defeat another view, and politicking to win approval from an existing audience. Scientist mode changes the success condition from appearing right to updating well.

The practice therefore depends on exposure to reasoned disagreement, explicit tests, and willingness to revise. Following people with intellectual integrity can reveal blind spots, but social-media signals are easily mistaken for endorsement and stripped of context. The method works better when claims, tests, and revision criteria are concrete rather than when "experiment" is used as a metaphor for unstructured trial and error.

## Key Claims
- Opinions become easier to revise when separated from moral identity and social status.
- Decisions can generate evidence when predictions and outcomes are made explicit.
- Reasoned disagreement is useful when the other person's process has integrity.
- Preaching, prosecuting, and politicking can each substitute identity defense for inquiry.
- Updating well is a distinct achievement from having guessed correctly at the outset.

## Evidence
- Four modes - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] presents preacher, prosecutor, politician, and scientist as contrasting reasoning postures.
- Hypothesis frame - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] describes opinions as hypotheses and decisions as experiments.
- Disagreement practice - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] recommends following people one disagrees with when their reasoning shows integrity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Not every decision can be safely or ethically experimental, and ambiguous outcomes can reinforce prior beliefs rather than correct them. Social media rarely supplies full context, while following an account may be publicly interpreted as endorsement. The source offers a reasoning posture, not evidence that adopting the label reliably removes bias.

- Created a bounded rethinking framework that distinguishes inquiry from identity defense.
