# 负臣 / Corrective Disobedient Minister

负臣 / corrective disobedient minister is the ruler-minister pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-2-jiemi-xinlingjun-ping-shenme-shi-si-gongzi-zhi-shou-lodqntthbil9o8ezppt66lywzd-f]] uses to explain [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]] after [[StealingTigerTalliesToRescueZhao|窃符救赵]]. In the episode's definition, a negative minister resists the ruler's order, takes extraordinary power, and tries to correct a royal mistake when normal command would ruin the state.

The concept is positive and dangerous at once. The episode cites [[Xunzi|荀子]] for the idea that such a minister can be a ruler's treasure, because he may save the polity from the ruler's bad decision. But it immediately treats that as an idealized standard: once a subject has shown that he can override the ruler and still be publicly vindicated, the ruler may find him impossible to trust or contain.

Xinlingjun is the source case because his action saves [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and indirectly protects [[WeiState|魏国]], yet it also makes him a criminal from [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]]'s perspective. The page therefore links the moral cost of emergency success to a political coexistence problem: even a state-saving subject may need distance from the ruler whose authority he bypassed.

## Key Claims
- A ruler can need correction from below, but the correction may require disobedience that damages royal authority.
- A successful correction is especially hard to absorb because public outcome can make the disobedient subject look more right than the ruler.
- The episode treats Xunzi's praise of the pattern as a normative ideal and Xinlingjun's exile-like stay in Zhao as the practical political reality.
- The concept overlaps with [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]] but is narrower: the danger comes from having corrected the ruler through unauthorized power, not merely from having accumulated enemies or merit.
