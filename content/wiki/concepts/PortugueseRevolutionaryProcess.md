# Portuguese Revolutionary Process

## Definition

The Portuguese Revolutionary Process was the unstable post-[[CarnationRevolution]] struggle over decolonization, property, representation, military authority, and the form of the new Portuguese state.

## Current Synthesis

The fall of dictatorship released more actors and demands than the coup leadership could contain. Workplace and land occupations, mass return from Africa, nationalization, communist organization, conservative regional backlash, rival military factions, and Cold War intervention fears produced a genuinely open transition; the defeat of Otelo's forces on 25 November 1975 and later elections moved that uncertainty toward pluralist democracy.

## Key Claims

- Social mobilization outran Spinola's plan for controlled liberalization.
- Rapid decolonization and nationalization divided the military coalition.
- Southern large-estate collectivization and northern anti-left backlash revealed strong regional differences.
- Foreign governments prepared for communist victory or civil war but disagreed over the danger.
- The Group of Nine offered a democratic-socialist, pluralist alternative to both restoration and council rule.
- The 25 November 1975 confrontation strengthened military moderates without battlefield casualties.
- Eanes's electoral victories helped convert military stabilization into democratic legitimacy.

## Evidence

- Social and economic evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] records occupations, broad nationalization, collective farming, and return migration.
- Regional and international evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] contrasts Alentejo collectivization with northern attacks on the left and describes Western contingency planning.
- Settlement evidence: [[273-portugal-the-carnation-revolution-glt3297148398]] follows the Group of Nine, 25 November defeat of Otelo's forces, and [[AntonioRamalhoEanes|Eanes]]'s 1976 victory.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode favors a centrist resolution narrative and gives less detail to parties, unions, African actors, constitutional design, and the social bases of competing projects. CIA funding and contemplated foreign arms support are reported from the source's reading of declassified records and remain source-scoped here.

## What Changed

- Added an open-outcome model in which democratic consolidation followed, rather than automatically inhered in, the April coup.
