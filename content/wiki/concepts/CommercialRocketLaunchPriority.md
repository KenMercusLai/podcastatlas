# Commercial Rocket Launch Priority

Commercial rocket launch priority is [[YaoSong]]'s ordering for [[Orienspace]] in [[173-duihua-yao-song-shenjian-dongfang-kongjian-zaichufa-tiancai-shaonian-shinian-hou-1-173-1]]: "好多快省," meaning high launch success rate, enough carrying capacity, fast market entry, and only then cost. The source treats the order as a response to China's expected satellite-internet and future communications demand.

The concept complements [[ReusableRocketEconomics]] but is not identical to it. Reusability and cost matter, yet Yao's source-scoped claim is that a young rocket company first has to prove reliability, payload relevance, and timing before pure cost optimization can dominate.

## Key Claims
- Reliability comes first because launch failure can destroy customer trust and company credibility.
- Payload capacity matters because constellation deployment needs useful mass to orbit, not only a technically successful small launch.
- Speed matters because market windows around satellite internet and communications infrastructure can close.
- Cost becomes more decisive after the company has proven it can serve real missions.
