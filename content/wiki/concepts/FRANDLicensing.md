# FRAND Licensing

FRAND licensing means patent licensing should be fair, reasonable, and nondiscriminatory. In [[the-invention-invention]], the concept emerges from antitrust concern around the [[GlassContainerPatentPool]] and becomes the legal discipline that makes later standards pools more acceptable.

For standards, FRAND matters because implementers often cannot route around [[EssentialPatents]] once a shared technical standard becomes widely adopted. A pool can therefore support [[StandardsBasedInnovation]] only if access is not used to exclude rivals, force coercive terms, or extract cartel-like control.

## Key Claims
- FRAND turns a necessary-patent position into a more open licensing obligation.
- Fairness and nondiscrimination protect implementers who need the standard.
- Reasonableness limits the pool's ability to turn essential rights into excessive gatekeeping.
- The concept helps distinguish lawful [[CollaborationVsCollusion|collaboration]] from unlawful collusion.
