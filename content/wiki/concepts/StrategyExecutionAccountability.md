# Strategy–Execution Accountability

## Definition
Strategy–execution accountability is the principle that strategic thought, operational implementation, and outcome ownership must remain connected across boards, executives, advisers, and delivery teams.

## Current Synthesis
[[FedericaFornaciari]] argues that boards should establish long-term direction while CEOs and senior teams turn that direction into processes, action, and measurable results. Consultants can add independent perspective and agencies can integrate specialized delivery, but neither should become a mechanism for executive responsibility transfer. RACI role clarity and outcome-linked adviser fees may help, yet they cannot replace active leadership through implementation.

## Key Claims
- Strategy without an internal implementation layer does not create organizational change.
- Executives remain accountable when implementation work is delegated to middle managers or external advisers.
- Adviser value rises when independent diagnosis is joined to employee operating knowledge.
- Responsibility, accountability, consultation, and information rights should be explicit across the delivery chain.
- Shared incentives can improve accountability but do not eliminate attribution and measurement problems.

## Evidence
- Executive ownership - [[defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ba1d684ae37ef5b6d033e83ad9620d0a-120327886-defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ba1d684ae37ef5b6d033e83ad9620d0a-120327886]] distinguishes board vision, executive implementation, and middle-management delivery.
- Consulting and agency contrast - [[defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ba1d684ae37ef5b6d033e83ad9620d0a-120327886-defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ba1d684ae37ef5b6d033e83ad9620d0a-120327886]] compares abstract reports with multidisciplinary agency work judged against visible outcomes.
- Role and incentive design - [[defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ba1d684ae37ef5b6d033e83ad9620d0a-120327886-defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ba1d684ae37ef5b6d033e83ad9620d0a-120327886]] proposes RACI and outcome-linked fees as mechanisms for making ownership clearer.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework comes from practitioner reflection rather than comparative causal evidence. Outcome-linked fees can reward short horizons or encourage disputes over attribution, and executives may lack the technical knowledge to direct every implementation detail. Delegation remains necessary; the claim is about retained accountability, not centralized execution of every task.

## What Changed
- Established a synthesis joining strategic thought, executive ownership, adviser incentives, and implementation design.
