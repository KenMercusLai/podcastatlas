# Alcohol Demand Reset

## Definition
Alcohol demand reset is the structural adjustment facing drinks and hospitality companies as consumers drink less, go out less often, use appetite-regulating medicines, or choose smaller, non-alcoholic, packaged, and alternative mood-oriented formats.

## Current Synthesis
The source set treats weaker alcohol sales as competition over occasions and formats rather than the disappearance of social-drink demand. Alcohol-free beer, draft cocktails, premium cans, CBD or mushroom-extract drinks, and smaller servings are attempts to preserve the occasion while changing alcohol content, package, venue, or function.

[[GLP1ConsumerDemandSpillover]] is a possible additional demand driver: [[Heineken|Heineken]] expects more small servings and low- or no-alcohol choices among GLP-1 users, but the scale and persistence of this effect remain uncertain and entangled with inflation and broader moderation trends.

## Key Claims
- Declining alcohol demand can pressure beer, wine, spirits, and hospitality simultaneously.
- Consumers may retain social-beverage demand while reducing alcohol quantity or frequency.
- Taste and occasion fit remain decisive constraints on non-alcoholic substitution.
- Serving size is a portfolio lever alongside alcohol content and packaging.
- GLP-1 adoption may reinforce existing moderation trends without being their sole cause.
- Category boundaries blur when beer, spirits, ready-to-drink, and functional beverages compete for the same occasion.

## Evidence
### Cross-category pressure and new formats
- [[drone-truth-russia-has-the-upper-hand-6aad03f98a1508074de8337e]] connects weaker sales and younger consumers' lower drinking and nightlife participation to alcohol-free beer, draft cocktails, premium cans, and alternative functional drinks.

### GLP-1 and serving-size response
- [[dafeixin-guonei-hangsi-tiaozheng-hangxian-buju-jianfeiyao-liuxing-huo-yingxiang-pijiuye-gkwriaiotragi7matv3jls]] reports Heineken guidance favoring smaller servings and low- or no-alcohol beer as GLP-1 use grows.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Neither source establishes whether demand decline is permanent, uniform across countries, or equally severe in every beverage category.
- Alternative mood drinks are emerging possibilities rather than proven large-scale substitutes.
- The GLP-1 claim lacks Heineken's underlying data and cannot be separated cleanly from inflation, age, health awareness, or changes in nightlife.
- Smaller servings can raise price per unit or create new occasions, so lower volume need not translate mechanically into lower revenue.

## What Changed
- Added GLP-1 use as a qualified demand-side contributor.
- Added serving size as a portfolio and venue response alongside alcohol-free formulation.
- Preserved broader moderation, taste, geography, and pricing qualifications.
