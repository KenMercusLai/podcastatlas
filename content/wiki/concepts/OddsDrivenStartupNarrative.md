# Odds-Driven Startup Narrative / 优化赔率

Odds-driven startup narrative is the founder and investor pattern [[Albert]] criticizes in [[youhua-shenglv-erfei-peilv-ba-yi-jian-shi-zuodao-lilun-shang-gaiyou-de-yangzi-duitan-lianxu-chuangyezhe-albert-lu0vamaawctwva3qblnsf99esar2]]. It starts from the magnitude of possible upside: if AI creates a new platform, network effect, or massive market, the company could become very valuable.

Albert says this logic is tempting because venture capital itself often optimizes for payoff size, and because Chinese startup exits make intermediate outcomes harder to value. The problem is not that big outcomes are bad; it is that a "next [[Douyin]]" story can hide weak user demand, weak [[ProductContainer]] fit, or poor control over the actual variables that make a company work.

## Key Claims
- The most visible form of odds optimization is telling a big story before the first user problem is clear.
- AI makes odds-driven stories especially attractive because model capability changes can be projected into future platforms, media formats, or operating systems.
- In content markets, odds narratives are fragile when generated experiences cannot beat existing games, [[Douyin]], [[TikTok]], or other mature entertainment options.
- [[WinRateStartupStrategy]] does not deny upside; it argues that payoff should emerge from real progress rather than substitute for it.
