# Guaranteed Home Delivery Crisis / 保交楼危机

## Definition
Guaranteed home delivery crisis is the social and governance problem created when developers have sold homes before completion but lack the cash, materials, contractors, or credit to finish and hand over those homes.

## Current Synthesis
EP93 defines the crisis through [[Evergrande]] after the company's financing chain broke. The episode moves the story from corporate debt to household exposure: buyers had paid for homes, contract liabilities remained on the balance sheet, suppliers stopped providing materials, contractors stopped work, and governments had to intervene to prioritize delivery.

The crisis is therefore not only a construction problem. It is the endpoint of [[HousingPresaleFinancingRisk]], [[DeveloperHiddenDebtStack]], and [[RealEstateHighTurnoverLeverage]] colliding. The same money that appeared to support fast development became unavailable when it was needed to finish projects already sold to households.

## Key Claims
- Home-delivery failure turns developer leverage into a direct household welfare and legitimacy issue.
- Presale makes the crisis socially acute because buyers may owe mortgages or have paid significant funds before receiving a finished home.
- Supplier and contractor stress can stop construction even when demand for the home already exists.
- Government intervention can prioritize delivery, but handover statistics may hide delays, quality disputes, incomplete works, or unpaid compensation.
- The crisis reframes property from an investment assumption into a delivery and consumer-protection problem.

## Evidence
- Construction stoppage channel: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] says stalled sites, supplier cutoff, and contractor stoppage made the home-delivery crisis visible after Evergrande's funding sources were nearly cut off.
- Buyer obligation channel: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] says contract liabilities represented large amounts of already-paid but not-yet-delivered housing obligations.
- Scale claim: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] cites media estimates of many affected owners and hundreds of stalled projects while keeping those numbers as reported figures.
- Intervention and qualification: [[ep93-yanjian-hengda-qi-gaolou-zhuanyan-shoufu-ru-gaoqiang-lhsjpbzdfjta0phfxrtnybq57o4c|EP93]] says central and local governments pushed delivery, but also notes cases of delayed completion, quality problems, key-first handover, and rights-protection disputes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should distinguish reported delivery progress from lived completion quality. EP93 says Evergrande later claimed or reported high delivery progress, but the episode explicitly asks for more owner feedback and cautions that some handovers may not mean fully finished, high-quality, dispute-free homes. The page therefore treats "保交楼" as a priority and crisis-management frame, not as proof that every affected household was made whole.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to capture the household-facing endpoint of Evergrande's property-finance collapse.
