# Command Disobedience Under Battlefield Uncertainty

## Definition

Command disobedience under battlefield uncertainty is a subordinate's decision to disregard or reinterpret an order because local information suggests compliance would defeat the commander's wider purpose or create greater danger.

## Current Synthesis

At Copenhagen, [[HydeParker]] saw a damaged British attack from farther away and signaled withdrawal, [[HoratioNelson]] judged that disengaging under Danish fire would be more dangerous and kept “engage the enemy more closely” flying, and [[EdwardRiou]] reluctantly obeyed because his rank gave him less latitude. The contrast shows that celebrated initiative is not a general license to ignore orders. Its defensibility depends on information asymmetry, clarity of higher intent, consequences, rank, and whether success later biases judgment. Riou's death also shows that identical signals can impose unequal risks on differently positioned units.

## Key Claims

- Formal authority and local situational awareness can diverge sharply during combat.
- Disobedience is most defensible when it preserves a credible higher intent rather than serving personal impulse.
- Rank and delegated trust distribute the practical freedom to reinterpret orders unevenly.
- Withdrawal can be more dangerous than continued engagement when units must turn or expose themselves under fire.
- Successful outcomes can romanticize disobedience and obscure the costs borne by officers who comply.

### Unequal discretion and consequences

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept rests on one victorious, hero-centered case. Outcome bias can make successful insubordination look wiser than it was, while failed disobedience may be judged more harshly on similar information. Parker's full reasoning, the feasibility of withdrawal, and the counterfactual result of compliance remain uncertain. The case does not support routine disregard of command authority.

## What Changed

- Established a framework distinguishing locally grounded reinterpretation from disobedience as a general virtue.
- Added rank-dependent discretion and compliant subordinates' exposure as central qualifications.
