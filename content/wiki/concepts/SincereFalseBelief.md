# Sincere False Belief / 诚实误信

## Definition

Sincere false belief is the condition in which a speaker accurately reports what they believe while that belief remains incomplete, causally mistaken, or false about the external world.

## Current Synthesis

In [[PaintingThrushGuLong|《画眉鸟》]], 柳无眉 experiences suffering and believes 石观音 poisoned her, while [[ShuiMuYinJi|水母阴姬]] tells [[ChuLiuxiang|楚留香]] that no poison exists. The episode argues that the statements need not establish a liar: 柳无眉 may truthfully describe her conviction and symptoms while misunderstanding their cause, and 水母阴姬 may truthfully deny the alleged poison.

The distinction separates honesty, experience, diagnosis, and causation. Subjective suffering can be real even when its explanation is wrong; a correct causal denial does not by itself explain the suffering. The source's suggestion that fear or conditioning produced the symptoms is one possible narrative interpretation, not an independently established clinical account.

## Key Claims

- Truthfulness about one's belief does not guarantee that the belief matches external conditions.
- Conflicting testimony does not always imply deliberate deception by one party.
- Lived symptoms and causal explanation should be evaluated as distinct claims.
- Correctly rejecting one cause does not automatically identify the actual cause.
- Fear, dependence, authority, and prior manipulation can stabilize a mistaken explanation.
- Narrative reversals become richer when they redistribute knowledge rather than simply reveal a liar.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supplies a fictional case and does not establish which character has complete knowledge, what produced the symptoms, or how 柳无眉 died. Sincerity itself is inferred from the story's presentation. Outside fiction, contradictory health claims require evidence and qualified assessment; the concept should not be used to psychologize symptoms or dismiss reported suffering.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish honest testimony, accurate belief, real experience, and correct causal explanation.
