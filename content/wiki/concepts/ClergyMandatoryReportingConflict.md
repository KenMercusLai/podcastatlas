# Clergy Mandatory Reporting Conflict

Clergy mandatory reporting conflict is the legal and moral clash in [[under-strain-breaking-the-british-state-6a799563523b3dfcd83c7271]] over whether clergy should have to report suspected child abuse learned through private spiritual communications, including Catholic confession. The source places [[PeterEisley]]'s survivor account beside the [[CatholicChurch|Catholic Church]]'s defense of the seal of confession and First Amendment religious-freedom arguments.

The conflict differs from ordinary mandated reporting because clergy often receive protected spiritual disclosures, while child-protection advocates argue that abuse prevention is urgent enough to override privilege. The episode presents the U.S. legal landscape as a state patchwork, with some states overriding penitent privilege in child-abuse cases and others preserving narrower protections. It does not resolve the constitutional question; it treats future bills and lawsuits as likely.

## Key Claims
- Mandated reporting requires designated adults to report suspected child abuse learned through their work.
- Clergy become a special case because spiritual counseling and Catholic confession have historically been treated as privileged.
- Child-safety advocates argue that preventing abuse is a compelling state interest that should override confession protections.
- Church lawyers argue that forced reporting from confession violates religious liberty, especially if other privileged relationships remain exempt.
- The source frames the issue as unresolved because both sides rely on high-order principles: child protection and religious freedom.
