# Electrolyte Drink Use Boundary / 电解质饮料使用边界

## Definition
Electrolyte drink use boundary is the distinction between replacing meaningful fluid-mineral loss and treating electrolyte beverages as ordinary daily water.

## Current Synthesis
The episode frames electrolytes as minerals lost more noticeably in heavy sweating, diarrhea, vomiting, heat, or demanding exercise. In those contexts, an electrolyte drink or sports drink can have a purpose, and sugar may appear because fast energy can be useful during actual sport or exertion.

The boundary is that ordinary low-consumption activity does not automatically justify repeated electrolyte or sports-drink intake. People with kidney problems, high sodium intake, or high blood pressure may carry extra risk from treating these drinks as water, while sweetened sports drinks can add energy that is not used and may be stored as fat. The source therefore makes use context, sodium burden, sugar, and medical status more important than the product's hydration halo.

## Key Claims
- Electrolyte replacement is most plausible after meaningful mineral loss through heavy sweating, diarrhea, vomiting, heat, or sustained exercise.
- Sports drinks may include sugar for quick energy in exercise contexts, so sugar is not automatically evidence of a bad formulation.
- Low exercise consumption changes the calculation because added sugar and energy may exceed what the person uses.
- Kidney dysfunction, high sodium intake, and high blood pressure are caution conditions for treating electrolyte drinks as daily water.
- Label reading still matters because sodium, carbohydrate, serving size, and total daily intake determine the actual burden.
- The concept is a context boundary, not a universal anti-electrolyte rule.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not specify exercise-duration thresholds, sodium targets, renal criteria, or blood-pressure protocols. It should be read as public education: use context and medical status matter, and people with disease, medication, heat illness, prolonged gastrointestinal loss, or serious dehydration should seek qualified care.
