# Sullan Dictatorship / 苏拉独裁

## Definition

Sullan dictatorship / 苏拉独裁 is [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]]'s open-ended revival of the Roman dictatorship after civil-war victory, using an old emergency office to remake the [[RomanRepublic|Roman Republic]] through extraordinary power.

## Current Synthesis

Episode 704 treats the dictatorship as the central paradox of Sulla's settlement. Sulla wants constitutional legitimacy and says he is restoring the republic, but he does so after massacres, [[SullanProscriptions]], and military victory. The dictatorship is ancient in form but revolutionary in scale because the traditional six-month limit is removed.

The reform program is conservative in stated purpose. Sulla strengthens senatorial authority, expands the Senate from 300 to 600, includes Italian elites, imposes a ten-year gap before another consulship, regularizes age minimums for office, and weakens the tribunate by stripping bill-proposal power and making the office a career dead end. These reforms are meant to close the channels by which [[GaiusMarius]], [[LuciusCorneliusCinna]], and [[GaiusMariusYounger]] had broken republican restraint. Yet the settlement also teaches later commanders that dictatorship, army-backed victory, and constitutional redesign are possible.

## Key Claims

- Sulla revives an old office but removes the time restraint that made it republican.
- The dictatorship depends on prior military victory and terror, not merely legal appointment.
- Sulla's reform aims are conservative: strengthen the Senate and constrain disruptive popular and office routes.
- The settlement incorporates Italian elites while punishing political enemies.
- The tribunate is deliberately weakened because Sulla sees it as a path for destabilizing popular politics.
- Abdication makes Sulla unlike later autocrats, but also proves that extraordinary one-man power can be achieved.

## Evidence

- Legal form and scale: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says Sulla revives the dictatorship and receives it without the traditional six-month limit.
- Senate strengthening: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says Sulla expands the Senate from 300 to 600 and includes Italian elites.
- Office control: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says he sets age minimums and a ten-year gap before repeat consulship.
- Tribunate restriction: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says he bars tribunes from proposing bills and turns the office into a political dead end.
- Abdication: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says Sulla lays down the dictatorship after two years and becomes a private citizen.
- Legacy contrast: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says Caesar later judged Sulla foolish for relinquishing dictatorship.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not present Sulla as a simple proto-emperor. It argues that he did not found an autocracy because he remained a conservative who believed in constitutional norms, even though his means permanently widened what future commanders could imagine.

## What Changed

- Created the concept for Sulla's emergency-office settlement, reforms, abdication, and later Caesar/Augustus contrast.
