# Choreographic Space / 编舞空间组织

## Definition
Choreographic space is the organization of bodies, distance, direction, time, speed, vibration, attention, and movement so that dance temporarily builds an architecture rather than merely displaying steps.

## Current Synthesis
EP286 makes space a primary object of dance attention. A theatre's recurring three-wall and “fourth wall” form does not predetermine the work, because choreographers can occupy, divide, trace, empty, and redirect the same volume differently. Moving dance into museums or public space changes both spatial relations and audience composition. The source resists making dancers illustrate nearby art: dance should remain an independent work whose placement creates coexistence rather than forced explanation.

## Key Claims
- Choreography organizes spatial relations as much as bodily motion.
- The same stage architecture can support radically different temporary worlds.
- Time, speed, information, vibration, distance, and stillness can all function as choreographic material.
- Museums and public spaces change encounter conditions and reach publics beyond habitual theatre audiences.
- Site change should not reduce dance to illustration of another artwork or exhibition thesis.
- Watching how dancers occupy space can give non-specialists a practical entry into contemporary dance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The architecture metaphor is an interpretive model, not a claim that choreography and building design are interchangeable. Site-specific work can intentionally converse with visual art even though the source warns against forced illustration. Audience diversification through venue change is plausible but not measured in this episode.
