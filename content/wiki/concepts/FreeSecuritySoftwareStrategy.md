# Free Security Software Strategy / 免费安全软件策略

## Definition
Free security software strategy is the use of free antivirus, cleanup, browser, and system-protection tools to build user trust, distribution, and traffic power rather than charging users directly for the core protection product.

## Current Synthesis
The source's main case is [[Qihoo360|Qihoo 360]]. Free security worked because it attacked a pain point users understood, undercut paid antivirus incumbents, and gave 360 privileged desktop authority. The strategy also carried a structural risk: once a security tool can label, remove, or block other software, competitors may treat it as both user protection and platform weapon.

## Key Claims
- Free security converts a paid utility category into a user-acquisition and trust-building product.
- The strategy depends on credible protection claims, because users give security software high system-level permissions.
- Anti-rogue-software positioning can reverse reputational damage when the actor was previously associated with browser-plugin controversy.
- Browser, navigation, and software-management products can monetize or leverage the free security base indirectly.
- Free security can trigger industry backlash from paid antivirus vendors and platform companies whose software becomes subject to warnings, blocking, or deletion.
- The strategy is vulnerable to mobile-era distribution shifts because handset app stores and OS-level security can displace independent PC security entry points.

## Evidence
- Category disruption: [[no-216-shi-bu-sha-yi-ren-hongyi-dapao-zhouhongyi-de-sanshinian-quanmian-zhanzhengshi-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-27-1008693437]] says Zhou decided in late 2007 to launch 360 free antivirus against Rising, Jiangmin, Kingsoft, and other paid antivirus incumbents.
- User traction: [[no-216-shi-bu-sha-yi-ren-hongyi-dapao-zhouhongyi-de-sanshinian-quanmian-zhanzhengshi-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-27-1008693437]] says 360 antivirus active users reached 29% of Chinese netizens by late 2009 and 63.5% by 2011.
- Trust reversal: [[no-216-shi-bu-sha-yi-ren-hongyi-dapao-zhouhongyi-de-sanshinian-quanmian-zhanzhengshi-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-27-1008693437]] says 360 Security Guard's negative software list included CNNIC, [[Baidu]], Alibaba, and Yahoo Assistant, the last of which was formerly 3721.
- Traffic leverage: [[no-216-shi-bu-sha-yi-ren-hongyi-dapao-zhouhongyi-de-sanshinian-quanmian-zhanzhengshi-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-27-1008693437]] ties 360 Security Guard, 360 browser, navigation, and software manager into a larger entry-point system.
- Backlash and limits: [[no-216-shi-bu-sha-yi-ren-hongyi-dapao-zhouhongyi-de-sanshinian-quanmian-zhanzhengshi-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-27-1008693437]] links this authority to [[ThreeQWar|3Q 大战]], then says 360 mobile products were later squeezed by handset-maker app stores.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Free security is not automatically user-aligned. The same permissions that help protect users can be used to steer distribution, attack competitors, or turn software removal into platform conflict.
