## Definition

The art-family tradeoff is the conflict among concentrated creative work, caregiving, domestic responsibility, intimacy, and recognition when time and obligation cannot be allocated without loss.

## Current Synthesis

[[AlissaEberhardt|Alissa Eberhardt / 阿丽莎·艾伯哈特]] and [[RolandBaines|Roland Baines / 罗兰·贝恩斯]] receive different goods and pay different costs. Alissa escapes a maternal model of abandoned writing and becomes an important novelist, but loses intimacy with her child and later family companionship. Roland fails to realize several public ambitions but raises their son and retains late family warmth. The comparison exposes gendered standards and real opportunity cost without making abandonment necessary for art or care sufficient to erase every other failure.

## Key Claims

- Creative time and caregiving time can compete under unequal domestic arrangements.
- Gendered inheritance shapes which sacrifice appears normal and which departure appears monstrous.
- Artistic achievement cannot repay another person automatically for abandonment or lost care.
- Care work can be a genuine achievement even when public status ignores it.
- Comparing entire lives requires plural values rather than one success scale.

## Evidence

### Rejected inherited sacrifice

- [[vol-358-gangqinke-wo-yijing-xiangkai-le-xiwang-ni-ye-xiangkai-yidian-gkwrijeotl1aaype0qtvn81u]] links Alissa's departure to seeing her mother give up professional writing through marriage and motherhood.

### Divergent outcomes

- [[vol-358-gangqinke-wo-yijing-xiangkai-le-xiwang-ni-ye-xiangkai-yidian-gkwrijeotl1aaype0qtvn81u]] contrasts Alissa's books and isolation with Roland's weak career record, single fatherhood, and later family closeness.

### Refusal of exchange

- [[vol-358-gangqinke-wo-yijing-xiangkai-le-xiwang-ni-ye-xiangkai-yidian-gkwrijeotl1aaype0qtvn81u]] says Roland would not trade his family life for Alissa's works and reputation even after recognizing her achievement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept emerges from fictional lives and does not establish that art and family are universally incompatible. Institutional support, money, partners, childcare, health, genre, and working method can change the tradeoff. Gendered constraint can explain pressure without excusing abandonment, and valuing care must not naturalize unequal care burdens.

## What Changed

- Created a plural-value frame for artistic achievement, caregiving, gendered inheritance, and irreversible relational cost.
