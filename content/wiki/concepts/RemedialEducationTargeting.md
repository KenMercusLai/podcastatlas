# Remedial Education Targeting

Remedial education targeting is the education-policy lesson drawn from [[MichaelKremer]]'s textbook study in [[piles-of-cash-and-a-town-of-solutions-in-kenya-nigeria-summer-school]]. The [[BusiaKenya|Busia]] trial found that textbooks did not improve average test scores, though high-scoring students benefited.

The implication is that learning materials alone can miss students who are already behind. The source says the result encouraged more attention to remedial education, making [[HumanCapitalDevelopment]] depend on matching intervention design to actual student level rather than assuming every input helps every learner equally.

## Key Claims
- Education spending can be ineffective on average when students cannot use the input provided.
- Mixed trial results can redirect policy away from broad input provision and toward targeted support.
- [[RandomizedControlledTrials]] made the textbook result harder to dismiss as anecdote.
- The concept connects education quality to [[EvidenceBasedDevelopmentPolicy]], not only school enrollment.
