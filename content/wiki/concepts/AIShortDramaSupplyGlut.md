# AI Short Drama Supply Glut / AI短剧供给过剩

## Definition
AI short-drama supply glut is the market condition where generative tools make production and launch so easy that output grows faster than attention, platform payouts, and production-cost recovery.

## Current Synthesis
AI short-drama economics now require a profitability correction alongside the wiki's earlier production-access frame. Lower production barriers and wider genre experimentation can coexist with supply saturation, rising model costs, falling revenue-share rates, added compliance cost, platform intake limits, accumulated finished works, and producer headcount contraction. Production access is no longer the binding constraint once audience time and distribution remain scarce.

## Key Claims
- Low production barriers can flood the market rather than create broad profitability.
- Playback volume alone is insufficient if breakeven thresholds are high and payout per view falls.
- Rising model prices can offset some of the cost advantages that made AI short drama attractive.
- Regulation can add another cost layer just as platform economics weaken.
- Platform release capacity and audience attention can become the bottlenecks after production becomes abundant.

## Evidence
- Supply evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] cites a DataEye report saying more than 220,000 AI short dramas launched across the web in the first half of 2026.
- Profitability evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says about 90% of AI short-drama companies are currently loss-making and fewer than 1.3% of works can breakeven at a 50-million-view line.
- Payout evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says platform revenue share per 10,000 views fell from RMB30-100 in late 2025 to RMB5-10.
- Regulatory evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says micro-short-drama management rules took effect on September 1, classifying works by investment size and topic.
- Platform and staffing evidence - [[vol-277-2026-jinzizhaopai-teji-youxiao-chuangxin-gkwrijiopvjbaug1zwtoebjv]] says platforms began limiting releases, works accumulated, and [[JiangyouWenhua]] reduced headcount from roughly 1,200 to roughly 600 after rapid AI-production scaling.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources rely on cited media, report figures, and business commentary rather than audited industry accounts. The reported staffing sequence is not dated precisely enough to prove a single causal mechanism. It does not mean every AI short-drama studio is structurally doomed; distinctive story, distribution skill, localization, operating discipline, or platform access may still matter.

## What Changed
- Added platform intake, accumulated inventory, audience time, and producer contraction to the earlier payout-compression account.
- Made the bottleneck shift from production capacity to distribution and demand explicit.
