# AI Short Drama Supply Glut / AI短剧供给过剩

## Definition
AI short-drama supply glut is the market condition where generative tools make production and launch so easy that output grows faster than attention, platform payouts, and production-cost recovery.

## Current Synthesis
AI short-drama economics now require a profitability correction alongside the wiki's earlier production-access frame. Lower production barriers and wider genre experimentation can coexist with supply saturation, rising model costs, falling revenue-share rates, added compliance cost, platform intake limits, accumulated finished works, and producer headcount contraction. The newest studio account adds a portfolio response: domestic one-click output can be cheap and fast yet still lose money, while a small minority of expensive boutique works can generate most revenue. Production access is no longer the binding constraint once audience time, differentiation, distribution, and platform payout remain scarce.

## Key Claims
- Low production barriers can flood the market rather than create broad profitability.
- Playback volume alone is insufficient if breakeven thresholds are high and payout per view falls.
- Rising model prices can offset some of the cost advantages that made AI short drama attractive.
- Regulation can add another cost layer just as platform economics weaken.
- Platform release capacity and audience attention can become the bottlenecks after production becomes abundant.
- Supply pressure can split strategy between maximum-volume one-click production and higher-cost boutique differentiation.

## Evidence
- Supply evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] cites a DataEye report saying more than 220,000 AI short dramas launched across the web in the first half of 2026.
- Profitability evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says about 90% of AI short-drama companies are currently loss-making and fewer than 1.3% of works can breakeven at a 50-million-view line.
- Payout evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says platform revenue share per 10,000 views fell from RMB30-100 in late 2025 to RMB5-10.
- Regulatory evidence - [[tulasi-zishengtang-qisu-pingjia-shangchao-aoleqi-alo-qianyue-wangyibo-1014854463]] says micro-short-drama management rules took effect on September 1, classifying works by investment size and topic.
- Platform and staffing evidence - [[vol-277-2026-jinzizhaopai-teji-youxiao-chuangxin-gkwrijiopvjbaug1zwtoebjv]] says platforms began limiting releases, works accumulated, and [[JiangyouWenhua]] reduced headcount from roughly 1,200 to roughly 600 after rapid AI-production scaling.
- Studio portfolio evidence - [[gkwrirwo23ybaabwuwuauyns-gkwrirwo23ybaabwuwuauyns]] reports single-digit-RMB payouts per 10,000 plays on some platforms, hundreds to thousands of daily releases on others, mostly loss-making domestic one-click trials, and roughly 90% of company revenue from fewer than 10% of boutique works.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources rely on cited media, report figures, and practitioner commentary rather than audited industry accounts. The reported staffing sequence is not dated precisely enough to prove a single causal mechanism, while platform payout, launch-volume, ROI, cost, and revenue-concentration figures use different scopes. The evidence does not mean every studio or low-cost creator is structurally doomed; distinctive story, distribution skill, localization, operating discipline, platform access, or exceptional creator experience may still produce profitable outliers.

## What Changed
- Added studio-level one-click losses, concentrated boutique returns, and payout compression.
- Added strategic divergence between volume production and quality differentiation.
