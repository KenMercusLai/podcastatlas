# Theatrical Window Bargaining

Theatrical window bargaining is the negotiation over how long films remain exclusive to cinemas before moving to streaming or other home-viewing channels. In [[zhongguo-xiaofeizhe-daidong-lafu-laolun-zengzhang-donghang-youhua-jipiao-tuigaiqian-zhengce-1005631805]], [[Paramount]] reportedly offered [[AMCTheatres|AMC]] and [[RegalCinemas|Regal]] a three-year commitment if it completed a [[WarnerBrosDiscovery|Warner Bros. Discovery]] acquisition.

The source says the offer included 30 theatrical releases per year, at least 45 days of theatrical exclusivity, and streaming availability only after 90 days. That makes windowing part of [[StreamingConsolidation]] politics: a studio can use theatrical commitments to reassure exhibitors, workers, and regulators that a merger will not simply push films into a streaming funnel.

## Key Claims
- Theatrical exclusivity is not only a release calendar; it is a bargaining tool among studios, exhibitors, streamers, workers, and regulators.
- Cinemas care about predictable film volume as much as about any single title.
- A merger can intensify exhibitor anxiety if the combined company might cut releases, shorten windows, or favor streaming libraries.
- Window promises can help a studio gather stakeholder support, but they do not by themselves resolve antitrust or employment concerns.
