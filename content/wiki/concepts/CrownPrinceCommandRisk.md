# Crown-Prince Command Risk / 太子挂帅风险

Crown-prince command risk / 太子挂帅风险 is the succession and military-command problem [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-19-maling-zhi-zhan-lhbapt09x2ptdqx0uopkp8apbada]] raises when [[WeiState|魏国]] appoints [[PrinceShenWei|太子申]] to command against [[QiState|齐国]]. The episode says that by the Spring-Autumn and Warring States transition, rulers had strong reasons to avoid giving heirs independent military authority.

The risk has two sides. If the heir succeeds in war, he may gain officer loyalty and public prestige before succession. If he fails or dies, the state loses the person meant to stabilize future rule. The episode uses a [[WilliamShakespeare|Shakespeare]] and Macbeth digression to make the broader point that close blood relation does not remove political threat inside power systems.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-20-pangjuan-si-ci-shu-xia-ls-8-3jhz5q1c5ca11mhdzmz4wg4]] makes the battlefield-exposure side concrete. After [[PangJuan|庞涓]] dies at Maling and [[WeiState|魏国]]'s army is broken, [[QiState|齐国]] captures [[PrinceShenWei|太子申]]. The source does not trace Wei succession effects in detail, but it turns the episode-19 appointment puzzle into a visible cost of putting an heir in the campaign.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-187-yingbu-panluan-liubang-weihe-tuobingti-qinzheng-lvjafotlnvmyqmwaeav3novz1dox|Hanji 187]] adds an early Western Han version through [[LiuYing|刘盈]]. [[LiuBang|刘邦]] is ill during [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]]'s rebellion and considers sending the crown prince as commander, but the prince's advisers and [[LuZhi|吕雉]] argue the post cannot help him much and can damage him badly. The risk here is not only battlefield capture: an heir who cannot command senior founding generals against a major rebel may lose succession credibility, while any victory still leaves him only crown prince. [[ZhangLiang|张良]]'s compromise is to give Liu Ying a general title and Guandong command without making him substitute for Liu Bang's personal authority at the front.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 187 adds that crown-prince command risk can involve authority insufficiency over veteran ministers, not only a prince becoming too strong or being captured.
- A crown prince is both a successor and a possible rival.
- Military command can give an heir networks and prestige beyond court appointment.
- The heir also functions as a political insurance asset; battlefield risk can endanger succession stability.
- Family intimacy does not cancel power logic when inheritance, legitimacy, and command converge.
