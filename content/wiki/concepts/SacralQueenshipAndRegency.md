# Sacral Queenship and Regency

## Definition

Sacral queenship and regency is the episode's framework for female political authority grounded in consecration, dynastic position, public piety, intercession, and the right to govern for an absent or underage ruler.

## Current Synthesis

The tenth-century cases in the episode show elite women acting through recognizable offices and expectations rather than only exceptional personality. Anointing gave queens a public religious status; intercession, mercy, care, charity, and access to the ruler created routine authority; regency could then convert that standing into government during succession crisis.

[[Theophano]] and [[AdelaideOfItaly]] also show that this institution produced rivalry. Both possessed legitimate dynastic roles, and the conflict concerned who would direct the court and protect the succession.

## Key Claims

- Consecration made queenship a public and sacral office.
- Intercession and mercy were political functions, not merely private virtues.
- Dynastic motherhood could support formal government during a minority.
- Female authority was institutional enough to survive one empress's death through another dowager.
- Rivalry among elite women does not negate the wider legitimacy of their office.

## Evidence

- Public office - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] describes anointing, intercession, charity, piety, and care as expected queenly duties.
- Regency - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] credits Theophano with preserving Otto III and Adelaide with taking over after 991.
- Contest - [[460-the-empress-of-the-apocalypse-glt9103397168]] presents Theophano and Adelaide competing for influence over Otto II.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source focuses on exceptional elite women and does not establish the reach of female authority below royal courts. Formal status did not eliminate misogyny, xenophobia, faction, or dependence on dynastic proximity. Its “century of women” label is an interpretive generalization rather than a complete survey.

## What Changed

- Created a framework that explains Theophano's regency as an institutional form of queenship rather than a personal anomaly.
