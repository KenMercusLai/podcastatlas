# Bessent Impossible Triangle / 贝森特不可能三角

[[182-quanqiu-hongguan-he-ziben-shichang-2026-sanjidu-fupan-yu-zhanwang-duochong-qiutu-kunjing-lllsfo2sf5jdqirx5o9dn55uqhd8]] extends the triangle into a broader policy-prisoner's-dilemma reading. [[Ricky]] treats [[ScottBessent|Bessent]] as trying to solve near-term political and financing problems under [[DonaldTrump]], while [[FederalReserve]] independence, [[KevinWarsh]], long-end yields, debt rollover, and midterm-election incentives make Treasury, monetary policy, and electoral politics harder to separate.

[[179-xian-jiu-riyuan-zai-jiu-changzhai-jiuhuoduizhang-beisente-zai-zou-yi-tiao-zenyang-de-gangsi-lqjc6vz-1ungnkpdw9d5kbsa-ouo]] defines [[BessentImpossibleTriangle]] as the tension between three U.S. goals: reducing trade deficits and manufacturing outflow, keeping dollar-financial dominance, and preserving foreign demand for [[USTreasury|U.S. Treasuries]]. The source argues that the old globalization loop made those goals mutually reinforcing only because foreign trade surpluses created dollar balances that could be recycled into Treasuries.

The contradiction is that a more nationalist trade order can reduce the very external dollar accumulation that helps finance U.S. fiscal deficits. The episode reads [[ScottBessent|Scott Bessent / 贝森特]] as trying to bridge this by creating or mobilizing substitute Treasury buyers through [[TreasuryDemandSubstitution]].

## Key Claims
- Trade-deficit reduction and reshoring can weaken foreign dollar recycling into Treasuries.
- Dollar dominance still requires liquid, credible, and widely held Treasury markets.
- Foreign official holders, especially [[Japan]], matter not only as allies but as balance-sheet actors.
- The triangle pushes Treasury policy toward stablecoins, bank balance-sheet rules, repo facilities, buybacks, maturity choices, and Fed pressure.
- Episode 182 adds that the triangle becomes politically sharper when high long-end yields, AI infrastructure borrowing, and election-year pressure all increase the cost of preserving growth and financing credibility at the same time.
