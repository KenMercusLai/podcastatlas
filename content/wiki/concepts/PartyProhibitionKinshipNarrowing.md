# 党锢亲属牵连缩限 / Party Prohibition Kinship Narrowing

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-907-suoyou-de-bing-doushi-ni-de-xinbing-ltqzyohcauduwuanlvqph9l81d79|Hanji 907]] supplies the wider rule that this concept later narrows. After [[CaoLuanLateHan|曹鸾]]'s memorial, [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] expands party-prohibition liability through students, old subordinates, close kin, and relatives within five degrees of mourning, creating [[PartyProhibitionKinshipExpansion|党锢亲属牵连扩大]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-917-ruhe-xunsu-zhaodao-shengzhi-jiaxin-de-pojudian-lk2zt5c3onvui9a4ppbowshy66tg|Hanji 917]] opens this concept through [[HeHaiLateHan|何海]]'s petition to [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. He Hai argues that party-prohibition collateral punishment should not reach kin who share a great-grandfather but do not share a grandfather, because that degree of kinship is already too remote for collective liability.

The resulting policy change is a narrow repair, not a full rehabilitation. It reduces the blast radius of party-prohibition punishment from the uncle/grandfather generation downward, while leaving the larger prohibition system in place until later crisis pressure produces [[PartyBanCrisisAmnesty|党锢危机赦免]].

## Key Claims
- Hanji 907 supplies the prior expansion: the court first widens party-prohibition liability into kinship and social-network categories.
- Collateral punishment can be narrowed by redefining which kinship ties count as politically responsible.
- A limited legal repair can succeed even in an episode dominated by ignored remonstrance and palace-favorite protection.
- The concept is distinct from amnesty: it reduces implicated kin categories rather than restoring all banned figures.
- The source frames the repair as accepted policy, but not as a reversal of late-Han factional politics.
