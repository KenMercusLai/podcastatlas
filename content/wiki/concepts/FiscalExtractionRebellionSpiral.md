## Definition

财政压榨叛乱螺旋 / fiscal extraction rebellion spiral is the governance failure in which a state answers crisis by increasing extraction from already stressed people, converting fiscal need into flight, death, banditry, and still deeper insecurity.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-656-zhichang-buhui-shuohua-de-ren-hen-nan-hunchutou-lpn15smz7r1glekugzwtm2tpw-tl|Hanji 656]] grounds the concept in [[WangMang|王莽]]'s late-[[XinDynasty|新朝]] tax decision. [[TianKuangXinOfficial|田邝]] reports that local property estimates contain too much concealment, and Wang Mang responds by imposing another levy at one-thirtieth. The policy is legible as revenue repair from the center, but the episode presents the social result as intensified flight and rebellion.

The concept matters because it links fiscal extraction to security failure. Qingzhou and Xuzhou civilians cannot absorb the combined weight of tax and labor demands; weaker people die on the road, and stronger people join armed groups. Rebellion is therefore not treated as a separate moral defect but as a predictable outcome of state pressure exceeding survival capacity.

## Key Claims

- Extraction can be administratively rational from the center while socially destructive at the edge.
- Rewarding officials for finding new taxable capacity can bias the state toward harsher extraction.
- When taxation and labor demands exceed survival capacity, flight and banditry become responses to governance rather than mere criminality.
- The spiral worsens feedback quality because success is measured by reported revenue opportunities rather than social stability.
- The concept should be used only where a source links fiscal pressure to mass disorder, not for any unpopular tax.

## Evidence

- Tax trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-656-zhichang-buhui-shuohua-de-ren-hen-nan-hunchutou-lpn15smz7r1glekugzwtm2tpw-tl|Hanji 656]] says Tian Kuang reports undercounted property and Wang Mang imposes another one-thirtieth levy.
- Reward distortion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-656-zhichang-buhui-shuohua-de-ren-hen-nan-hunchutou-lpn15smz7r1glekugzwtm2tpw-tl|Hanji 656]] says Tian Kuang is promoted to a noble rank and receives a large cash reward after the report.
- Social breakdown: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-656-zhichang-buhui-shuohua-de-ren-hen-nan-hunchutou-lpn15smz7r1glekugzwtm2tpw-tl|Hanji 656]] says Qingzhou and Xuzhou civilians flee, weaker people die, and stronger people become bandits under heavy tax and labor pressure.
- Governance linkage: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-656-zhichang-buhui-shuohua-de-ren-hen-nan-hunchutou-lpn15smz7r1glekugzwtm2tpw-tl|Hanji 656]] presents later amnesty, harsh street prohibitions, and office-title proliferation as failing to solve the disorder created by burden and fear.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in one late-Xin source note. It does not claim all taxation produces rebellion, nor does it quantify the exact causal share of tax relative to drought, famine, corvee, local administration, or rebel organization. The strongest claim is narrower: when extraction increases under acute livelihood stress, fiscal policy can become a rebellion accelerator.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 656's tax-reward and Qing-Xu flight/banditry sequence.
