# Fiber-Label Quality Gap / 纤维标签与成衣品质差距

## Definition

The fiber-label quality gap is the difference between what a garment's standardized component label discloses—fiber names and proportions—and what a buyer would need to know to judge the finished product's hand feel, function, durability, construction, and value.

## Current Synthesis

A care label answers an important but narrow question: which regulated fiber categories make up the product, and in what proportions? It generally does not reveal polymer subtype, fiber fineness or geometry, yarn quality, weave or knit, density, finishing, heat setting, blending execution, patternmaking, sewing, quality control, or performance testing. Garments labeled “100% polyester” can therefore range from ordinary lining to fine outerwear, fleece, technical sports fabric, or permanent-pleat design.

The gap creates two symmetrical errors. Consumers may treat a disliked fiber name as proof of poor quality, or accept an evocative merchandising name as proof of comfort and function. A better judgment keeps standardized composition disclosure as a baseline, then tests construction, intended use, actual feel and behavior, care requirements, durability evidence, and price justification. This improves interpretation without pretending that shoppers can recover every hidden manufacturing variable at the point of sale.

## Key Claims

- A fiber-content label identifies composition more reliably than it identifies quality.
- The same declared fiber can produce materially different garments because important manufacturing variables remain outside the label.
- Standardized fiber names and merchandising names perform different functions and can create conflicting impressions.
- Negative experience with low-quality finished goods can be misattributed to an entire fiber category.
- Consumer judgment should combine composition with construction, use case, feel, care, observed performance, and credible product evidence.

## Evidence

- Disclosure-boundary evidence - [[dafeixin-bei-tucao-yishen-suliao-guodong-juzhixianwei-zuode-yifu-weihe-wuchubuzai-gkwriw4os8-qaitnmqtvn8yz]] says component labels do not specify fiber fineness, weave, or finishing even though those variables change performance and cost.
- Same-label diversity evidence - [[dafeixin-bei-tucao-yishen-suliao-guodong-juzhixianwei-zuode-yifu-weihe-wuchubuzai-gkwriw4os8-qaitnmqtvn8yz]] notes that lining, quick-dry fabric, fleece, fine down-jacket shells, and designer pleats may all carry a 100% polyester label.
- Naming-gap evidence - [[dafeixin-bei-tucao-yishen-suliao-guodong-juzhixianwei-zuode-yifu-weihe-wuchubuzai-gkwriw4os8-qaitnmqtvn8yz]] contrasts standardized fiber disclosure with product names such as “云感棉” and “太空棉” that may foreground feel, process, or imagery instead of composition.
- Category-reputation evidence - [[dafeixin-bei-tucao-yishen-suliao-guodong-juzhixianwei-zuode-yifu-weihe-wuchubuzai-gkwriw4os8-qaitnmqtvn8yz]] links cheap shine, stiffness, static, and pilling to a generalized distrust of polyester.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Composition labels remain useful and legally important; the gap is not an argument for ignoring them. Nor does missing process detail prove deception or justify a price premium. The source does not provide a validated field protocol for assessing garments, quote the governing standard directly, or establish how much information can feasibly fit on a label. Sensory inspection also cannot reveal every durability, chemical, labor, or environmental property.

## What Changed

- Created a consumer-information framework separating standardized composition from multidimensional garment quality.
- Added the risk of both fiber-name prejudice and merchandising-name overinterpretation.
