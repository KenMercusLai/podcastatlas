## Definition

保护换信任式边疆安抚 / frontier trust through protection is the pacification pattern where an official protects threatened dependent or neighboring groups, or uses credibility-preserving restraint toward a frontier actor, thereby converting safety and room to move into allegiance, information, withdrawal, or later military cooperation.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 756 supplies a narrow release-before-protection case. [[HaoWuQiangLeader|Hao Wu]] is captured after a failed Longxi incursion and tells [[ZhangYuEasternHan|Zhang Yu]] that killing him will not weaken the Qiang, while releasing him can end the attack. Zhang Yu releases him, Hao Wu disperses the troops, and [[MiWuQiangLeader|Mi Wu]] withdraws north of the Yellow River. This is not yet Deng Xun-style dependent protection, but it shows how credible restraint can make a frontier actor useful.

Hanji 757 supplies the negative prehistory. Zhang Yu later kills Mi Wu and many Qiang leaders after surrender contact and banquet hospitality, after which [[MiTangQiangLeader|Mi Tang]] reconciles groups, intermixes marriages, exchanges hostages, and concentrates renewed rebellion. That prior breach makes later trust-building begin from damaged credibility rather than from a neutral frontier field.

Hanji 760 supplies the positive protection case through [[DengXunEasternHan|Deng Xun]]. When Mi Tang threatens Hu communities near the frontier, other officials argue that letting Hu and Qiang groups hurt one another would benefit Han. Deng rejects that calculation, names prior official bad faith as a cause of rebellion, opens his office to protect Hu wives and children, and sends troops to defend them.

That protection becomes political capital. Hu leaders describe Deng's treatment as parental protection and submit to his direction; Deng then uses surrendered Qiang intermediaries and rewards to pull more groups away from Mi Tang before attacking and defeating him. The concept therefore overlaps with people-based security and root-cause pacification, but it is narrower: the trust is built by taking a protective or credibility-preserving stance when vulnerable or defeatable actors are deciding whether Han authority is safer than rebel coercion.

## Key Claims

- Protection can be a frontier-governance tool when threatened groups are deciding whether Han authority is safer than rebel coercion.
- Tactical release can create a first bridge to withdrawal when the released actor can credibly change group behavior.
- Protective trust-building is especially important after earlier state bad faith has made surrender or contact dangerous.
- Refusing to exploit intergroup violence can build more durable allegiance than watching rivals weaken one another.
- Trust is operational: it produces withdrawal, submission, intelligence, intermediaries, troops, and coalition fracture.
- Protective pacification still allows force against the coercive actor; Deng's policy protects Hu families and then attacks Mi Tang.
- The pattern depends on credibility after prior state bad faith, so a single conciliatory gesture without trust depth may fail.

- Protective action: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-760-lishi-jiemi-huanghou-chugui-bei-zhua-xiachang-can-bucan-lgz2sc9xt8ulltvwemiz2uzfghqi|Hanji 760]] says Deng opens his office to endangered Hu wives and children and sends soldiers to guard them.
- Submission effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-760-lishi-jiemi-huanghou-chugui-bei-zhua-xiachang-can-bucan-lgz2sc9xt8ulltvwemiz2uzfghqi|Hanji 760]] says Hu leaders submit and promise to follow Deng's arrangements because he treats them with grace and trust.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not say frontier protection or restraint is sufficient by itself. Hanji 756's Hao Wu release works temporarily, but Hanji 757 shows Zhang Yu later destroying surrender trust, and Hanji 760 includes rewards, surrendered intermediaries, direct attack, pursuit, and postwar garrison farming. Later Hanji 767 also shows that copying a soft posture without Deng's accumulated legitimacy can fail in Nie Shang's case. Protection works here because it is paired with credible conduct, incentives, and force.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 756 as the release-before-protection case where restraint creates temporary withdrawal.
- Added Hanji 757 as the bad-faith prehistory that makes Deng Xun's protective trust-building legible.
- Created the concept from Hanji 760's Deng Xun protection-and-submission sequence.
- Distinguished protective trust-building from both passive divide-and-rule and wishful conciliation.
