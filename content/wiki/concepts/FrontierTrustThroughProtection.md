## Definition

保护换信任式边疆安抚 / frontier trust through protection is the pacification pattern where a ruler or official protects threatened dependent or neighboring groups, materially supports a submitted frontier actor, or uses credibility-preserving restraint toward a frontier actor, thereby converting safety and room to move into allegiance, information, withdrawal, patrol service, or later military cooperation.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 727 supplies the strongest current state-protection case. [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] accepts Han hierarchy through kneeling edict ritual and hostage rotation, but the relationship becomes durable because [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] pairs subordination with material safety: gifts, grain, livestock, guard forces, a safer Meiji settlement, winter support, and a patrol role across border commanderies. The episode's compressed lesson is that submission is not just humiliation when it brings reliable protection.

Hanji 756 supplies a narrower release-before-protection case. [[HaoWuQiangLeader|Hao Wu]] is captured after a failed Longxi incursion and tells [[ZhangYuEasternHan|Zhang Yu]] that killing him will not weaken the Qiang, while releasing him can end the attack. Zhang Yu releases him, Hao Wu disperses the troops, and [[MiWuQiangLeader|Mi Wu]] withdraws north of the Yellow River. This is not yet Deng Xun-style dependent protection, but it shows how credible restraint can make a frontier actor useful.

Hanji 757 supplies the negative prehistory. Zhang Yu later kills Mi Wu and many Qiang leaders after surrender contact and banquet hospitality, after which [[MiTangQiangLeader|Mi Tang]] reconciles groups, intermixes marriages, exchanges hostages, and concentrates renewed rebellion. That prior breach makes later trust-building begin from damaged credibility rather than from a neutral frontier field.

Hanji 760 supplies the positive protection case through [[DengXunEasternHan|Deng Xun]]. When Mi Tang threatens Hu communities near the frontier, other officials argue that letting Hu and Qiang groups hurt one another would benefit Han. Deng rejects that calculation, names prior official bad faith as a cause of rebellion, opens his office to protect Hu wives and children, and sends troops to defend them.

That protection becomes political capital. Hu leaders describe Deng's treatment as parental protection and submit to his direction; Deng then uses surrendered Qiang intermediaries and rewards to pull more groups away from Mi Tang before attacking and defeating him. The concept therefore overlaps with people-based security and root-cause pacification, but it is narrower: the trust is built by taking a protective or credibility-preserving stance when vulnerable or defeatable actors are deciding whether Han authority is safer than rebel coercion.

## Key Claims

- Protection can be a frontier-governance tool when submitted or threatened groups are deciding whether Han authority is safer than rival coercion.
- Material support, guard forces, and patrol roles can turn formal submission into practical allegiance.
- Tactical release can create a first bridge to withdrawal when the released actor can credibly change group behavior.
- Protective trust-building is especially important after earlier state bad faith has made surrender or contact dangerous.
- Refusing to exploit intergroup violence can build more durable allegiance than watching rivals weaken one another.
- Trust is operational: it produces withdrawal, submission, intelligence, intermediaries, troops, and coalition fracture.
- Protective pacification still allows force against the coercive actor and depends on trust depth, so a single conciliatory gesture without credibility may fail.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not say frontier protection or restraint is sufficient by itself. Hanji 727 includes hierarchy, hostages, guards, and later relocation after Northern Xiongnu pressure, so its protection is backed by force and supervision rather than goodwill alone. Hanji 756's Hao Wu release works temporarily, but Hanji 757 shows Zhang Yu later destroying surrender trust, and Hanji 760 includes rewards, surrendered intermediaries, direct attack, pursuit, and postwar garrison farming. Later Hanji 767 also shows that copying a soft posture without Deng's accumulated legitimacy can fail in Nie Shang's case. Protection works here because it is paired with credible conduct, incentives, and force.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 727 as the strongest current state-protection case: Southern Xiongnu submission becomes durable through hostages, provisioning, guards, patrol duties, and credibility-preserving Northern Xiongnu refusal.
- Preserved Hanji 756 as the release-before-protection case where restraint creates temporary withdrawal.
- Preserved Hanji 757 as the bad-faith prehistory that makes Deng Xun's protective trust-building legible.
- Preserved Hanji 760's Deng Xun protection-and-submission sequence as the Qiang/Hu branch.
- Distinguished protective trust-building from both passive divide-and-rule and wishful conciliation.
