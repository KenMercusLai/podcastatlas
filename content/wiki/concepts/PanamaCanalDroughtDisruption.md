# Panama Canal Drought Disruption

## Definition

Panama Canal drought disruption is the logistics shock created when low rainfall reduces the freshwater available for canal operations, constraining daily transit capacity and changing queues, fees, routes, delivery times, and freight costs.

## Current Synthesis

[[e254-chaoji-eerninuo-laile-womende-richang-suoxu-zhenhui-yin-ta-zhangjia-ma-0b644f51-461c-4ddb-b707-d2a39b9a7d80]] uses the Panama Canal to show that climate can tighten effective supply without reducing factory or farm output. Fewer transit slots create choices among paying for priority, waiting, diverting around longer routes, changing load or schedule, and passing some cost to customers.

The disruption is asymmetric. Routes that depend more heavily on the canal, including some Asia-to-U.S.-East-Coast, inter-coastal U.S., liquefied-natural-gas, and grain flows, face greater marginal exposure than trade using other corridors. The event should therefore be modeled as a bottleneck with substitutions and queues, not as a uniform percentage loss of world shipping capacity.

## Key Claims

- **Water availability is operating capacity.** Low rainfall can reduce canal throughput even when ships, ports, and cargo remain available.
- **Queues create differentiated costs.** Priority fees, waiting time, diversion distance, fuel, schedules, and cargo value shape the realized impact.
- **Exposure is route-specific.** Canal-dependent origin-destination pairs and time-sensitive cargoes face larger effects than trade with viable alternatives.
- **Logistics can create a supply shock without a production shock.** Goods may exist but arrive later or at higher delivered cost.
- **The impact is partial and adaptive.** Rerouting, scheduling, load changes, and alternative ports prevent a simple one-for-one reduction in global shipping.

## Evidence

- **Capacity and queue mechanism:** [[e254-chaoji-eerninuo-laile-womende-richang-suoxu-zhenhui-yin-ta-zhangjia-ma-0b644f51-461c-4ddb-b707-d2a39b9a7d80]] links low water to fewer daily transits, priority payments, waiting, and diversion.
- **Asymmetric trade exposure:** [[e254-chaoji-eerninuo-laile-womende-richang-suoxu-zhenhui-yin-ta-zhangjia-ma-0b644f51-461c-4ddb-b707-d2a39b9a7d80]] highlights selected Asia-U.S. East Coast, U.S. inter-coastal, LNG, and grain routes while rejecting a uniform global-capacity interpretation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode characterizes a stage-specific shock; transit rules, rainfall, queues, and freight markets change over time.
- Alternative routes reduce dependence but may add distance, fuel, emissions, congestion, and schedule uncertainty.
- Cargo classes differ in value of time, vessel fit, contract structure, and ability to absorb fees or diversion.
- The source does not estimate a current global price effect and should not be read as a live shipping forecast.

## What Changed

- Added a logistics-specific climate mechanism separating route bottlenecks from global production loss.
