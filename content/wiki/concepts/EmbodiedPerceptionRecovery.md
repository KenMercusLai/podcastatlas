# Embodied Perception Recovery / 身体感知恢复

## Definition
Embodied perception recovery is the deliberate restoration of attention to breath, heartbeat, touch, weight, posture, movement, fatigue, and bodily shape when screen-heavy information life has made the body feel like a silent tool.

## Current Synthesis
EP286 frames digital life as a condition in which people become practiced at processing information and seeking virtual recognition while noticing less of their own physical state. Dance is one route back, but the source deliberately lowers the entry threshold: recovery can begin with breathing inward, releasing held tension, moving fingers, closing the eyes, touching the face and torso, or noticing how weight is carried. These practices do not prove that bodily signals are always correct. Their narrower value is to make bodily experience available again for reflection, relation, and action.

## Key Claims
- Persistent screen and information attention can crowd out awareness of breath, posture, touch, and changing bodily state.
- Recovery starts through specific sensation rather than an abstract command to “be embodied.”
- Breath, release, self-touch, walking, and simple movement can restore body-shape and state awareness without requiring performance skill.
- Inward attention can support outward action by letting movement arise from carried weight and released tension rather than copied form.
- Bodily attention can reconnect self-perception with other people, space, and the material world.
- Recovery should treat signals as information for inquiry, not as infallible diagnosis or mystical truth.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers experiential exercises, not controlled evidence that digital media uniquely causes reduced interoception or that dance treats a clinical condition. Closing the eyes, touching the body, or changing breath may be uncomfortable or unsuitable for some people, including those with trauma, pain, dizziness, or sensory differences. The practices are invitations to notice, not replacements for medical assessment or individualized instruction.
