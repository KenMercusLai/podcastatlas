# AI Infrastructure Supply-Chain Bullwhip / AI 基建供应链牛鞭效应

AI infrastructure supply-chain bullwhip is the [[175-gongmu-jijin-erjibao-jizhi-de-baotuan-yu-gelie-zhihou-lv20uprxrcw0tde5ugohu29tmlg6]] risk frame for AI hardware and upstream material expectations. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] borrows the supply-chain bullwhip idea to warn that small changes in end demand or demand expectations can be amplified through cloud providers, model companies, chip vendors, component suppliers, and capacity decisions.

The concept adds a physical-supply-chain layer to [[AIEquityValuationRisk]]. The source does not deny that AI usage, AI Coding, and model-company ARR can validate demand. It warns that each layer sees only part of the order chain, so upstream tightness can temporarily look like unlimited final demand even when timing gaps, inventory, or future capacity change the actual return path.

## Key Claims
- Bullwhip risk is highest when investors infer durable final demand from upstream shortages without enough visibility into end-user adoption and monetization.
- A bottleneck link can have real pricing power and still be cyclical if capacity, substitution, or downstream cost pressure later changes.
- The frame fits [[AICapexReturnWindow]] because markets want one-to-three-year evidence that compute spending becomes revenue, not only order growth.
- The source treats verification in steps, such as capex, usage, ARR, and then tight materials, as more convincing than a one-step "demand is infinite" story.
- [[DeepSeek]] and AI Coding appear in the source as demand and uncertainty signals: they can change expected compute need, overseas capex assumptions, and the perceived path from application growth to infrastructure demand.
