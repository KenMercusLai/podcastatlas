# State-Managed Prostitution / 国家管制女闾

State-managed prostitution / 国家管制女闾 is the source-scoped historical claim [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-2-qiongshe-jichi-de-zushiye-guanzhong-lo8uei2u1qwj9tszftel1b4lswoa]] makes about [[GuanZhong|管仲]]. The episode says Guan Zhong brought the sex trade into visible state regulation under the term "女闾" and divided it into managed establishments.

The page records the political function claimed by the episode, not a general history of prostitution. In the host's telling, the policy is meant to maintain social order, create revenue, supplement military funds, attract or host talent, and entertain envoys and [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]]. That makes it part of the same pragmatic state-capacity package as [[StateResourceMonopoly|state resource monopoly]], but with a stronger moral and evidentiary caution.

## Key Claims
- The source treats "女闾" as a state-regulated institution, not merely private vice.
- Revenue, order, hospitality, and court service are presented as the policy's political purposes.
- The episode's "first" claim stays source-scoped; the wiki does not treat it as settled institutional chronology.
- The policy shows the morally mixed side of Guan Zhong's practicality: state capacity can absorb socially stigmatized activity when revenue and control matter.
- The concept should be linked to Guan Zhong's reform package, not generalized beyond this source without later evidence.
