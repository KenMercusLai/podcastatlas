# Castleman Disease

## Definition
Castleman disease is a rare group of lymph-node disorders discussed in the current source through [[DavidFajgenbaum|David Fajgenbaum]]'s severe relapsing systemic illness and treatment research.

## Current Synthesis
The episode supplies a patient-research narrative rather than a complete clinical account. Fajgenbaum describes enlarged lymph nodes, profound fatigue, abdominal pain, fluid accumulation, organ failure, transfusion and dialysis needs, and multiple near-fatal relapses before and after diagnosis by lymph-node biopsy. Chemotherapy and an experimental drug produced only temporary control in his account.

Faced with another relapse, he stored longitudinal blood samples, analyzed immune-cell and serum-protein signals, and examined lymph-node tissue. He reports identifying mTOR activation and asking his physician to prescribe sirolimus, an mTOR inhibitor used for transplant rejection. His subsequent long remission is an important hypothesis-generating case and the origin story for a wider patient-research network, but it is not by itself a universal Castleman treatment rule.

## Key Claims
- Castleman disease can involve lymph-node pathology and severe systemic inflammatory illness.
- Diagnosis in the reported case followed biopsy after lymphoma and autoimmune disease were considered.
- Longitudinal patient samples helped generate a mechanism-linked treatment hypothesis.
- Fajgenbaum reports durable remission after physician-supervised sirolimus use.
- A single response cannot establish subtype-specific efficacy, dose, safety, or generalizability.
- Patient organizations can connect samples, experts, research priorities, and treatment learning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not specify the complete Castleman classification, diagnostic criteria, differential workup, laboratory values, pathology, treatment sequence, dose, adverse effects, or primary-study evidence. Fajgenbaum's account should not be generalized across subtypes or patients. Sirolimus is an immunosuppressive prescription drug requiring specialist judgment and monitoring; response attribution is not independently established by this summary.
