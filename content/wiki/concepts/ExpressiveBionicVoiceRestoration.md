## Definition
Expressive bionic voice restoration is the use of electronic speech hardware and AI to restore not only intelligible words after voice loss but also timing, pitch, emotion, non-word vocalization, personal identity, and rapid participation in conversation.

## Current Synthesis
The source shifts the evaluation target from speech output to social interaction. A device can make words understandable yet still fail when it delays turn-taking, flattens intonation, makes affection sound mechanical, or cannot support laughter, crying, shouting, quiet speech, and singing. Restoration therefore joins acoustic output to bodily control, conversational latency, recognizability, and the user's ability to express an intended emotional stance.

The proposed architecture is non-invasive and hybrid. Physical and acoustic knowledge constrains the model; training can occur off-device; a smaller model runs locally; the user supplies intention and emotion; and AI preserves or reshapes vocal identity. This promises greater agency but does not by itself establish clinical benefit, safety, affordability, or transfer across different causes of speech loss.

## Key Claims
- Intelligibility is necessary but insufficient for socially complete voice restoration.
- Prosody, timing, laughter, crying, shouting, affection, and silence carry meaning outside lexical content.
- Conversational latency is an accessibility requirement because delayed entry can exclude a user even when output quality is high.
- Non-invasive systems can widen access relative to implants, though comparative effectiveness remains unestablished here.
- Physical speech constraints can reduce reliance on unconstrained data-driven inference.
- The user's intention and emotional control should remain visible in the generated output.
- Evaluation should include participation, agency, identity, social acceptance, and real-world usability alongside acoustic quality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a founder-user interview rather than a comparative clinical study. Reported latency ranges, user totals, performance, timelines, and population size are not independently established. Different users may prioritize intelligibility, effort, safety, discretion, speed, vocal identity, or surgery differently, so one founder's successful control model should not be universalized.

## What Changed
- Established expressive and conversational participation as the restoration target beyond word intelligibility.
