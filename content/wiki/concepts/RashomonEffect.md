## Definition

The Rashomon effect is the fracture of an event into viewpoint-shaped accounts whose conflicts expose self-interest, shame, limited knowledge, or narrative position. It is strongest when no account can simply be treated as an unmediated view from nowhere.

## Current Synthesis

[[InAGrove|《竹林中》]] supplies the foundational testimony machine in the bounded sources: the bandit, wife, and dead husband offer incompatible but internally persuasive versions of a killing. [[RashomonFilm|黑泽明《罗生门》]] joins that structure to the setting and title of [[RashomonAkutagawa|芥川龙之介《罗生门》]], causing the film's title to become the broader cultural name for fractured testimony.

A qualified wuxia case appears in [[XueshanFeihu|《雪山飞狐》]]. Yan Ji's self-protective suicide story conflicts with later evidence, but Miao Ruolan, Ping A-si, and Tao Baisui also contribute different portions of the causal chain. Their accounts progressively move toward a poisoned-weapon explanation rather than preserving permanent indeterminacy.

The comparison therefore requires two axes: contradiction and completion. Interest-shaped conflict can produce a genuine Rashomon effect, while distributed witnesses may instead form a staged reconstruction in which each limited perspective supplies a missing fact. A work can use both mechanisms without every discrepancy remaining irresolvable.

## Key Claims

- Testimony can diverge because speakers protect identity, status, desire, or innocence, not only because one person invents everything.
- Narrative position and limited access can make individually incomplete accounts useful even when they do not directly contradict.
- A later adaptation can rename how an earlier literary technique is remembered across cultures.
- Additional viewpoints do not automatically produce truth; their independence, incentives, and access still require evaluation.
- A Rashomon-like opening can resolve into cumulative reconstruction, so permanent indeterminacy is not a necessary result of multi-perspective form.

## Evidence

### Irreconcilable testimony and cultural naming

- [[17-diyubian-zhongsheng-jie-ku-yi-nian-ru-mo-561780565]] distinguishes Akutagawa's two stories and explains how Kurosawa's film made "Rashomon" the shorthand for the contradictory testimony structure largely derived from 《竹林中》.

### Self-protection and progressive correction

- [[loilowe1olu1lvqymkulih_qakxr-loilowe1olu1lvqymkulih_qakxr]] contrasts Yan Ji's false account with later testimony that identifies the poisoned wound, the poison's route, and Tian Guinong's direction.

### Contradiction versus supplementation

- [[loilowe1olu1lvqymkulih_qakxr-loilowe1olu1lvqymkulih_qakxr]] shows that multiple perspectives can combine competing versions with witnesses who simply possess different pieces of the event.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

"Rashomon" is a useful comparison but can become too broad if applied to any story with several narrators. The 《雪山飞狐》 case eventually supports a relatively stable causal explanation, unlike the stronger truth fracture attributed to 《竹林中》. The source also reports that Jin Yong did not claim direct borrowing from Kurosawa and instead cited older narrative traditions; formal resemblance is not evidence of direct influence.

## What Changed

- Added a wuxia case combining self-interested contradiction with cumulative revelation.
- Distinguished permanent truth fracture from distributed evidence that eventually stabilizes causation.
- Clarified that formal comparison does not establish direct influence.
