# Reputation-Signaling Honorary Appointment / 声望信号式荣誉任用

## Definition
Reputation-signaling honorary appointment is the grant of visible rank and respect to a famous but operationally distrusted person so that treatment of the individual supports the regime's broader talent reputation without transferring corresponding executive power.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1146 supplies the pattern through [[XuJingShuHan|许靖]]. [[LiuBei|刘备]] reportedly judges Xu Jing's ability, conduct, and loyalty harshly, but [[FaZheng|法正]] argues that public neglect of a nationally famous man will be interpreted as contempt for worthy people. The appointment is therefore designed for two audiences: Xu Jing receives status, while potential recruits observe a ruler who appears respectful of talent.

The source says Liu Bei gives Xu Jing high position but withholds substantive military and administrative authority. This separates symbolic inclusion from operational trust. It can reduce reputational cost, but it also risks confusing title with confidence, rewarding fame over competence, or creating a merely performative talent policy.

## Key Claims
- Treatment of a famous individual can shape outsiders' beliefs about a ruler's entire talent policy.
- Public reputation may constrain appointments even when the ruler's private assessment is negative.
- Rank, honor, and substantive authority are separable design choices.
- A bounded honorary role can preserve signaling value while limiting operational risk.
- Reputation management can widen recruitment without proving the appointee's competence or moral worth.

## Evidence
- Negative private assessment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] says Liu Bei distrusts Xu Jing after his attempted surrender and judges him poorly.
- External-audience constraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] attributes to Fa Zheng the warning that outsiders may read neglect as hostility to talent.
- Authority separation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] says Xu Jing receives high status without substantive military or administrative responsibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish Xu Jing's exact office, duties, influence, competence, or the recruitment effect of his treatment. The distinction between honor and real authority is the episode's interpretation and requires institutional evidence. Honorary appointment may preserve reputation, but it can also weaken merit signals, create resentment, or give an apparently powerless figure informal influence.

## What Changed
- Established a focused concept separating talent-market signaling from operational delegation.
