## Definition
Shared pain technology boundary is the ethical limit around tools that let intimate partners split negative emotional experience, where support, burden transfer, emotional transparency, and possible abuse become inseparable.

## Current Synthesis
The current source makes pain-sharing difficult because the tool is bidirectional and automatic. It is not only a device for compassion; it also lets one person's depression, anxiety, business stress, or other suffering enter the other person's body or mind.

The key boundary is consent under asymmetry. A person may want to help a suffering partner, but the same mechanism can punish a partner with pain they did not choose, expose whether someone is "really" suffering, or enable exploitation if one person routes pain through several intimate ties. The panel therefore treats pain-sharing as more morally unstable than ordinary care or emotional support.

## Key Claims
- Pain-sharing differs from empathy because it transfers felt burden rather than only understanding or support.
- Bidirectional sharing can be unfair when one partner's pain load is persistently higher.
- Automatic emotional transparency may reduce lying but can also destroy privacy and ordinary relational buffer.
- Abuse risk rises if the technology can be distributed across multiple partners or used without mutual capacity.
- Positive emotion sharing is ethically easier than negative emotion sharing because it lacks the same burden-imposition problem.

## Evidence
- Burden asymmetry: [[ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur-ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur]] has Xuanzong reject sharing because she does not want business stress and decision loneliness transferred to her partner.
- Capacity-based acceptance: [[ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur-ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur]] has Liu Hongwei accept sharing because he presents himself as low in negative emotion and able to generate happiness.
- Transparency risk: [[ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur-ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur]] turns the device into a way to detect whether a partner's claimed pain is real, raising privacy concerns.
- Abuse risk: [[ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur-ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur]] has Sun Shuheng worry that bad actors could make multiple partners share their pain.
- Conditional altruism: [[ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur-ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur]] has Luo Yonghao answer according to which partner has more pain, treating the device as a harm-minimization calculation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The device is hypothetical. The source does not show that emotions could be measured, transferred, split, delayed, or consent-managed in reality. It also does not replace ordinary mental-health support or couple communication; it isolates one ethical problem created by making emotional burden mechanically shareable.
