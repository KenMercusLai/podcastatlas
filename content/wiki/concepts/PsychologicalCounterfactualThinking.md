# Psychological Counterfactual Thinking

## Definition
Psychological counterfactual thinking compares what happened with an imagined alternative, changing the meaning and emotional evaluation of the actual outcome even though the past remains fixed.

## Current Synthesis
The episode distinguishes upward counterfactuals, in which reality is compared with a better result, from downward counterfactuals, in which it is compared with a worse one. Upward comparison can identify a possible cause or future improvement, while downward comparison can create relief or gratitude. Neither function is automatically appropriate: psychological distance, reversibility, current distress, and the ease of imagining a nearby alternative affect which comparison becomes useful or painful.

Near misses show why satisfaction is relational rather than outcome-only. A silver medal can make gold especially accessible, a bronze medal can make no medal accessible, and a grade just below a threshold can feel worse than a lower score with the same letter grade. The lesson is not that these examples settle every reaction, but that the mentally available comparison point can organize emotion.

## Key Claims
- Upward counterfactuals imagine a better result and can support causal learning or future adjustment.
- Downward counterfactuals imagine a worse result and can support relief without necessarily identifying an improvement path.
- The ease and proximity of an imagined alternative influence which comparison dominates attention.
- Psychological distance can make learning-oriented comparison easier, while emotionally close or irreversible events may increase the need for comfort.
- Counterfactuals can clarify causal hypotheses but can also exaggerate control and responsibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The imagined alternative is unobserved and does not prove that the selected cause would have changed the outcome. Downward comparison can become avoidance or minimize real harm; upward comparison can produce learning but also deepen rumination, envy, or self-blame. The medal, grade, distance, and frequency claims remain episode-scoped because the supplied summary does not include study methods, samples, effects, or replication evidence.
