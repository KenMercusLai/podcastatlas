# Recurring Layoff Management Burden

## Definition
Recurring layoff management burden is the cumulative professional and moral strain on managers who must repeatedly decide which employees lose their jobs.

## Current Synthesis
[[ChrisBell]]'s account broadens the wiki's layoff branch beyond the workers directly cut. A manager may retain title and employment while repeatedly implementing organizational contraction, yet still experience the work as a fundamental departure from the role originally chosen. When the cycle becomes routine, career exit can become a way to stop participating in decisions the manager no longer accepts as sustainable or meaningful.

## Key Claims
- Layoffs create a burden for the managers selecting employees as well as for the employees who lose work.
- Repetition matters: an exceptional crisis decision can become a durable role characteristic when cuts recur almost annually.
- The burden is not reducible to workload because it concerns responsibility for consequences imposed on other people's livelihoods and teams.
- Financial capacity and family alternatives can determine whether dissatisfaction becomes an actual career exit.

## Evidence
- Recurring decision duty - [[tech-20260907-0907-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260907-0907-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports Bell's statement that almost every year required deciding whom to lay off from a team.
- Role mismatch - [[tech-20260907-0907-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260907-0907-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the repeated cycle eventually felt like work he had not signed up to do.
- Exit pathway - [[tech-20260907-0907-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260907-0907-mp-tech-pod-128]] connects the burden with a financially possible, family-supported retirement decision rather than proving it alone caused the exit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies one manager's retrospective and does not compare industries, employers, layoff processes, or managers who remain. It supports the existence of the burden in Bell's case, not a prevalence estimate or a universal causal claim about management turnover.

## What Changed
- Added a manager-side layoff framework distinct from employee displacement and post-layoff recovery.
