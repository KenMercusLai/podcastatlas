# Sexual Agency and Refusal / 性自主与拒绝权

## Definition
Sexual agency and refusal is the capacity to recognize one's own desire or aversion, choose whether and how to participate, withdraw participation, and have that decision respected without treating relationship status or affection as permanent permission.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats refusal as part of sexual agency rather than evidence that intimacy has necessarily failed. Some women may previously have endured sex that was painful, unequal, or inattentive; their ability to name dissatisfaction and say no can reduce activity while improving bodily autonomy.

Agency is wider than refusal. It includes knowing one's body, expressing preferences, evaluating hygiene and health risk, choosing external stimulation or non-penetrative intimacy, and deciding whether adult products support solo or partnered experience. Economic dependence and unequal household power can narrow these choices, which makes a nominal yes or no insufficient without attention to the surrounding relationship.

## Key Claims
- Relationship status, love, and past participation do not erase the right to refuse a current act.
- Lower sexual activity can sometimes reflect increased agency rather than reduced wellbeing.
- Agency includes expressing desire and preference, not only protecting against unwanted contact.
- Health, pregnancy, contraception, disease, pain, and reputational risks can be distributed unequally between partners.
- Economic and relational power affect whether refusal can be exercised safely and meaningfully.

## Evidence
- Refusal as progress - [[ep293-xing-bu-rongyi-qinmi-geng-nan-women-que-de-shi-yuwang-haishi-heshi-de-ren-ljrvwz7kavgep3r4t8uxelvhxbql]] reports the view that some reductions in activity follow women no longer tolerating unwanted or low-quality sex.
- Unequal risk - [[ep293-xing-bu-rongyi-qinmi-geng-nan-women-que-de-shi-yuwang-haishi-heshi-de-ren-ljrvwz7kavgep3r4t8uxelvhxbql]] connects women's emphasis on fairness and autonomy to asymmetric contraception, pregnancy, and disease burdens.
- Positive agency - [[ep293-xing-bu-rongyi-qinmi-geng-nan-women-que-de-shi-yuwang-haishi-heshi-de-ren-ljrvwz7kavgep3r4t8uxelvhxbql]] encourages women to understand their bodies and act as participants with preferences rather than passive evaluators of male performance.
- Power boundary - [[ep293-xing-bu-rongyi-qinmi-geng-nan-women-que-de-shi-yuwang-haishi-heshi-de-ren-ljrvwz7kavgep3r4t8uxelvhxbql]] notes that refusal may be one of few available powers for a financially dependent partner with low household status.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Refusal is not proof that a relationship is emancipatory, and participation is not proof that it is coercive. Agency depends on specific conduct, safety, power, communication, and freely revisable choice. The source's gendered examples illuminate unequal patterns but do not establish that all women or men share the same risks, preferences, or bargaining position.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to treat refusal, preference expression, and bodily knowledge as parts of the same agency framework.
