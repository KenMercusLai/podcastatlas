# AI-Generated Portrait Identity and Authorization / AI生成肖像识别与授权

## Definition

AI-generated portrait identity and authorization is the problem of deciding when a synthetic, face-swapped, or blended depiction is identifiable as a particular natural person and whether its creation, adaptation, promotion, and distribution fall within valid consent.

## Current Synthesis

Synthetic media weakens the old assumption that portrait misuse must copy one photograph. A generated face may combine or alter features yet still evoke a particular person through facial structure, styling, context, labeling, voice, or audience recognition. Identification is only the first gate: permission for one photograph, acting job, model-training use, or campaign does not automatically settle derivative generation, sublicensing, duration, territories, cover art, or later platform distribution.

The episode's reported Zhao Liying lawsuit makes this a platform-scale governance issue. Recruitment markets that “buy a face” cheaply through thin agreements create upstream ambiguity, while platforms must distinguish lawful licenses, parody or transformation, misleading association, and unauthorized commercial use across large volumes. The current source identifies the problem but does not settle the legal test.

## Key Claims

- Exact photographic duplication is not the only route by which a synthetic image can identify a person.
- Identifiability and authorization are separate questions: a depiction can be recognizable while a license remains absent, narrow, expired, or disputed.
- Face-data agreements need explicit scope for training, generation, modification, sublicensing, promotion, duration, territory, and revocation.
- Platform responsibility grows when synthetic-face disputes occur at review volumes too large for purely ad hoc handling.
- Low acquisition prices and thin contracts can shift risk downstream without creating informed, durable consent.
