# Psychedelic Church Harm Reduction

Psychedelic church harm reduction is [[JohnRapp]]'s defense of [[ZydorChurch]] in [[a-trip-to-the-magic-mushroom-megachurch]]. He says he believes Zydor is a true church and also an exercise in harm reduction, contrasting access to high-dose mushrooms with alcohol, cocaine, opioids, and uncontrolled drug markets.

The concept matters because it partly reframes the source's legal question. [[PsychedelicChurches]] may be sincere religious organizations, but they may also justify themselves as safer access points when prohibition pushes people toward riskier substances, uncertain potency, or unsupported settings.

## Key Claims
- Harm reduction can support a church's public-interest argument without proving religious sincerity by itself.
- A safer-access argument remains exposed to [[MedicalRiskManagement]] concerns around dosing, screening, ceremonies, injuries, and liability.
- The frame can make Zydor look less like a loophole and more like an alternative to more dangerous drug markets.
- Internal critics can accept some harm-reduction value while still worrying that scale and permissive access confuse law enforcement and the public.
