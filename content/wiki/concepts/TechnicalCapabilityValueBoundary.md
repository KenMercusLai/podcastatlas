## Definition

Technical capability-value boundary is the distinction between knowing how to make or optimize something and judging whether its purpose, deployment, distribution, or consequences are worth accepting.

## Current Synthesis

[[Hephaestus]] can forge an effective chain but does not thereby gain authority to decide whether [[Prometheus]] should be bound. The episode uses this scene as a compact model of technical work under power: a system can function accurately and still serve surveillance, coercion, or another contested objective.

The boundary also limits broad promises about technology. Extending life does not decide what makes life worth living; improving production does not decide how gains should be distributed; optimizing a task does not establish that the task deserves completion. These remain questions of judgment, institutions, consent, and responsibility.

Pandora adds the stronger case of a system that works precisely as commissioned. Multiple divine specialists give her form, skills, attraction, and persuasive capacity, and [[Epimetheus]] accepts her; functional success is therefore compatible with a purpose designed to harm the recipient. Evaluation must reach upstream to objective setting and downstream to consequences, not stop at performance.

A user-side purpose failure appears when a person acquires unusual power without acquiring more worthy ends, then spends it on spectacle, status, humiliation, and distraction. [[FaustusMarlowe|Faustus]] makes the boundary applicable even when a capability is not imposed by an external commander: desire satisfaction, efficiency, and expanded options cannot independently establish what a life or project should serve.

## Key Claims

- Effectiveness is not proof of ethical legitimacy.
- Makers may possess implementation power without authority over goals.
- Technical neutrality is weakened when design choices make a contested purpose easier to execute.
- Prediction of consequences does not equal control of consequences.
- Value judgment must address purpose, consent, distribution, and acceptable risk.
- A system can meet its specification while making the specification itself the central ethical failure.
- Capability can magnify trivial or harmful desire when the user has not examined the purpose being served.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The distinction does not imply that technical facts are irrelevant to ethics or that values can ignore feasibility. Design, error rates, reversibility, access, security, and operational constraints materially change ethical evaluation. Mythic and dramatic analogies frame the boundary but do not supply a complete governance procedure or establish facts about modern AI systems. Desire and purpose can also improve through new capability; the claim is that improvement is not automatic.

## What Changed

- Added Pandora as the case where excellent execution fulfills a morally corrupt specification.
- Extended evaluation across objective setting, persuasive design, acceptance, and external consequences.
- Added Faustus as the user-side case where extraordinary capability serves unexamined or diminishing ends.
- Extended the boundary from institutional goals to life purpose, desire, and the use of additional time or efficiency.
