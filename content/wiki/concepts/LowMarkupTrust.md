# Low Markup Trust

Low markup trust is the [[costco-1]] idea that a retailer can turn restrained pricing into a durable trust asset. [[Costco]] caps markups on most goods and keeps [[KirklandSignature]] close to the same discipline, giving members reason to believe the company is sharing savings rather than searching for hidden margin.

This is why the episode treats no-loss-leader pricing as important. If a retailer sells one product below cost and recovers margin elsewhere, shoppers must decode the game. Costco's everyday-low-markup approach tries to make the whole basket feel trustworthy.

## Key Claims
- Markup caps function as internal rules against short-term profit temptation.
- Low prices become more credible when paired with [[MembershipRetail]], because customers already paid for access.
- The model can leave profit on the table in the short term while strengthening [[TrustAsBusinessAsset]] and retention.
- Private label is most trust-preserving when it improves value or quality rather than merely widening margin.
