# Open Data And Transparency

## Definition
Open data and transparency describe making public information accessible, interpretable, and reusable so citizens, journalists, firms, and officials can scrutinize decisions and build services on shared facts.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames openness as both a democratic value and an efficiency infrastructure. Disclosure can improve accountability, unlock geospatial and transactional uses, and widen participation, but useful openness requires attention to privacy, security, data quality, and the difference between releasing records and making them usable.

## Key Claims
- Access is the core public value of open data.
- Journalistic demands for disclosure can be as important as technical advocacy in creating open-government capacity.
- Previously restricted geospatial or administrative data can become infrastructure for mobility, markets, and public services.
- Transparency tools can improve traceability, but technology does not by itself establish legitimacy or correct use.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The interview does not establish causal proof that open data produced the named companies or markets.
- Disclosure can conflict with privacy, security, commercial confidentiality, or data-protection duties.
- Blockchain and similar tools preserve records only as well as their inputs, governance, and institutional enforcement.

## What Changed
- Established openness as a joined access, accountability, and economic-infrastructure concept.
