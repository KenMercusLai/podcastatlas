## Definition

Adaptation reception memory is the influence of first-viewing age, broadcast access, repetition, music, and generational media context on later judgments about which version of a familiar work feels definitive or best.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded [[LegendOfTheCondorHeroes|《射雕英雄传》]] survey shows that adaptation preference is neither pure craft judgment nor pure nostalgia. The 1983 TVB series can be remembered through childhood repetition, theme songs, and wide rebroadcast while also displaying strong rhythm, conflict design, and character construction on rewatch. Conversely, a later generation may treat the 1994, mainland, 2008, or 2017 version as its primary visual reference because those were the versions available during formative viewing.

The [[TaiChiMaster1993|《太极张三丰》]] retrospective shows that the mechanism also operates within one film. VHS, VCD, television repeats, piracy, and incomplete cuts can determine which scenes exist in a viewer's remembered version. Music, imitated gestures, and a vivid water-vat image may survive while the psychological breakdown and recovery linking them disappear. Adult rewatching then compares not only work and nostalgia, but the complete film with a materially partial childhood object.

Reception memory therefore changes the baseline against which casting, voice, costume, pacing, deviation, and even plot continuity are judged. It does not invalidate criticism; it requires critics to separate what a work demonstrably does from the familiarity, access, copy completeness, and life stage that make its choices feel natural.

The Jin Yong favorites roundtable adds a cross-work test. A host can prefer the 1995 television adaptation of 《神雕侠侣》 for performers, pacing, romance, and music, or the CCTV 《笑傲江湖》 for landscape, production scale, ensemble, and atmosphere, while films such as 《东邪西毒》 or 《笑傲江湖Ⅱ：东方不败》 are valued for transformative style. Preference therefore combines remembered attachment with explicit craft criteria and need not track literal fidelity.

The [[AChineseGhostStory1987|《倩女幽魂》(1987)]] retrospective adds an image-first case. Viewers retain VCD or television impressions, songs, actors, white fabric, wind, water, and monster fragments while confusing sequel events or forgetting the full narrative. A performance and visual design can become the default image of a literary character whose source description was less fixed.

## Key Claims

- First-viewing age can make one adaptation the default image of characters and scenes.
- Rebroadcast frequency and regional access determine which versions become shared generational reference points.
- Songs, performers, and repeated family or childhood viewing can preserve a version beyond its production limitations.
- Nostalgia can amplify genuine craft strengths rather than merely substitute for them.
- Rewatching can revise a nostalgia-only explanation when pacing, staging, and characterization remain effective.
- Cross-generational disagreement is evidence about reception context, not by itself proof that one group has judged incorrectly.
- Format quality, missing scenes, and repeated fragments can create materially different remembered versions, while autonomous cinematic style or ensemble scale can make a less literal adaptation the favorite.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is grounded in four multi-host retrospectives and cannot quantify how much preference comes from craft, age, region, marketing, repetition, copy completeness, star image, or circulation. Nostalgia should not dismiss aesthetic judgment, nor should popularity prove superiority or influence. Streaming and rewatching can loosen original boundaries. Production credits, version histories, and influence claims remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established first-viewing age and broadcast access as explicit variables in adaptation judgment.
- Separated nostalgia from craft while showing how the two can reinforce one another.
- Extended the concept from version preference to partial copies, home-video formats, false shot memory, and childhood imitation.
- Added cross-work favorites that make performance, music, scale, landscape, and transformative style explicit alongside formative attachment.
- Added image-first memory and screen performance becoming a default cultural image of a literary character.
