## Definition

教化先于刑法 / Moral Education Before Punishment is a governance model in which rulers, schools, officials, ritual, and custom shape conduct before wrongdoing reaches the penal system. Punishment remains a deterrent, but it cannot by itself repair predatory administration or a social order that has ceased to cultivate shared norms.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-247-dongzhongshu-hanwudi-wo-jiao-ni-ruhe-yang-erzi-lp_re0k2i0x9r0xe9mgd4enbdu77|Hanji 247]] presents the model through [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]]'s answer to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. The sequence begins with ruler self-correction, passes through court and bureaucratic conduct, and reaches households through schooling, ritual, custom, and local officials. The episode's water-and-dike analogy treats unformed self-interest as a predictable pressure that institutions must channel rather than as misconduct that punishment can eliminate after the fact.

The institutional branch joins [[Taixue|太学]], capable teachers, recurring policy examination, and long-horizon talent formation. Its local branch makes commandery and county officials public models: if they exploit people or ignore vulnerable households, imperial benevolence cannot travel through the administrative chain. Education-first governance is therefore not merely moral exhortation; it depends on personnel, institutions, implementation, and credible official conduct.

## Key Claims

- Governance begins upstream with ruler and official conduct because public instruction is weakened when the state models the opposite behavior.
- Education, ritual, and custom can reduce the volume of conduct that reaches criminal enforcement, but do not abolish law or deterrence.
- Talent cultivation must precede urgent recruitment; schools and examinations are state-capacity infrastructure rather than decorative learning.
- Local officials transmit or block central policy through their daily conduct toward ordinary and vulnerable people.
- Severe punishment cannot compensate for predatory officials, collapsed custom, or the absence of credible education.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on one compact popular-history source and records Dong Zhongshu's argument rather than independently proving its effects. The source does not show that education alone prevents crime, that light punishment is always sufficient, or that Zhou prisons were empty for decades. Its Qin comparison compresses a large institutional history into a moral contrast, and its Heaven-response language is persuasive theology rather than verified governance causation. Education can also reproduce hierarchy or official orthodoxy, while punishment may remain necessary for immediate protection and accountability.

- Established an upstream governance model that connects ruler conduct, schooling, talent formation, local administration, and penal limits.
