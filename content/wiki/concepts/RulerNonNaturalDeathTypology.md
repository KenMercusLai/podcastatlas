# Ruler Non-Natural Death Typology / 帝王非命死法类型

Ruler non-natural death typology / 帝王非命死法类型 is the source-scoped classification [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-69-bikan-gudai-diwang-qipa-sifa-da-pandian-lukvjsktg-pgfjhizvypwmdmw-13]] uses to preview [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]]'s coming end. The episode groups remembered ruler deaths into poisoning, strangling, weapon killing, and starvation, then says Qi Min Wang's later death will be worse than the ordinary examples.

The concept is a narrative and comparative device, not a settled catalog of every ruler's death. Its value in the wiki is to connect the Zhouji sequence's political lessons about power, legitimacy, and violence: rulers are not only killed by enemies at the frontier; they can be killed by court factions, family struggle, lost rescue capacity, or carefully arranged deprivation.

The starvation category links especially closely to [[ResponsibilityAvoidantKilling|避责式杀害]]. The episode lists [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]] and [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]] among rulers remembered as starved to death, while the Zhao Wuling Wang branch already shows how indirect confinement can function as a death sentence.

## Key Claims
- Non-natural ruler deaths are usually political events, not only bodily events.
- The method of killing can reveal the killer's legitimacy problem: poison, strangling, weapons, and starvation distribute visibility and blame differently.
- Preserving a whole corpse or avoiding a direct blow can be a political calculation rather than mercy.
- Starvation and confinement matter because they make death slower, more deniable, and dependent on control over access.
- The source uses typology to build suspense for Qi Min Wang while keeping the examples comparative and compressed.
