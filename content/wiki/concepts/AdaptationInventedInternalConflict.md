# Adaptation-Invented Internal Conflict

## Definition

Adaptation-invented internal conflict is the creation of scenes, choices, or complicity absent from a source so adapted characters must confront contradictions within themselves rather than only external obstacles.

## Current Synthesis

[[CharlesRandolph]] argues that factual sequence alone may leave drama morally flat. In [[BirthdayPartyFilm|The Birthday Party]], he invents a confrontation in which Stanley's abstract claim that opportunity is available is tested by an open door that he cannot safely cross, while Lucky's threat tests whether the captors can become killers. The invention converts a kidnapping plot into reciprocal, unequal moral pressure.

The method is neither neutral nor automatically truthful. Giving a real subject a new contradiction can illuminate complicity or erase the subject's own interpretation; giving perpetrators interiority can restore agency or soften responsibility. Its ethical value therefore depends on disclosed invention, retained consequence, source awareness, and whether affected perspectives can challenge the adaptation.

## Key Claims

- External conflict does not by itself establish a character's moral or dramatic complexity.
- Invented choices can reveal contradiction that a factual record does not stage explicitly.
- Internal conflict can restore agency to people otherwise depicted only as victims or threats.
- The same invention can misrepresent a living subject by assigning beliefs, guilt, or empathy they reject.
- Dramatic usefulness does not settle historical accuracy or ethical legitimacy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The evidence is one screenwriter's account of an unproduced screenplay rather than comparison among memoir, screenplay, film, subject testimony, and audience response. Internal contradiction is not the only route to full characterization, and invention can create false equivalence between unequal harms. The concept identifies a craft choice and its ethical burden, not a presumption that invention yields deeper truth.
