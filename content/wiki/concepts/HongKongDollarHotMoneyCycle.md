# Hong Kong Dollar Hot-Money Cycle / 港元热钱周期

Hong Kong Dollar hot-money cycle / 港元热钱周期 is the macro-finance layer added by [[vol-269-xiao-lishi-bu-yao-pa-shi-jishu-xing-tiaozheng-1004793119]]. The source says Hong Kong's 1972 decision to move away from a weak sterling link toward the U.S. dollar, followed by a 1973 adjustment from 5.65 to 5.08 per dollar after U.S. dollar weakness, helped draw liquidity into Hong Kong assets.

The concept links currency confidence to local equity speculation. When a currency anchor changes and purchasing power appears to rise, funds may treat the local market as a temporary shelter; if those flows reverse, the same equity market can lose support quickly. In the episode, this amplifies the [[HongKong1973StockMarketCrash]] rather than fully causing it.

## Key Claims
- Currency-anchor changes can affect equities through confidence, purchasing power, and cross-border liquidity.
- Hot money can validate a bull market while it enters, then intensify panic when it exits.
- Local market booms should be read alongside [[CurrencyRisk]] and [[CurrencyAnchorTransition]], not only company earnings or investor mood.
