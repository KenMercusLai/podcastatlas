# Battlefield Cohesion and Rout

## Definition

Battlefield cohesion and rout describes how alignment, mutual support, confidence, and command keep formations functional, and how local gaps, panic, leader flight, crowding, and pursuit can turn a setback into system-wide collapse.

## Current Synthesis

At [[BattleOfIssus|Issus]], formation integrity matters on both sides. Alexander repeatedly halts his deployment to prevent exploitable gaps, yet the river crossing still disrupts the Macedonian phalanx and lets Greek mercenaries attack exposed flanks. Local reinforcement stabilizes the center. On the Persian side, collapse near Alexander and Darius's departure spread fear through formations that may not yet have been physically defeated. Narrow retreat routes then transform disorder into trampling and killing by pursuers.

## Key Claims

- Formation gaps can negate nominal strength by exposing flanks and separating mutual support.
- Psychological shock can make troops flee before prolonged physical contact.
- Local reserves or lateral reinforcement can stop a cohesion failure from spreading.
- A ruler or command symbol leaving the field can become an information shock beyond its immediate tactical effect.
- Most casualties may occur during pursuit, when fugitives can no longer defend themselves as an organized force.
- Constricted escape routes convert individual flight into crowd disaster.

## Evidence

- Macedonian deployment and gaps: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] connects alignment pauses, river-crossing disorder, exposed flanks, officer losses, and Coenus's response.
- Persian collapse: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] links the right-wing breakthrough and Darius's departure to spreading flight.
- Post-collapse killing: [[dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv-dchha74-mania-for-subjugation-iv]] emphasizes bottlenecks, trampling, cavalry pursuit, and the distinction between battle and rout casualties.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Ancient accounts moralize Darius's departure and cannot securely establish the exact sequence or scale of panic. Cohesion also depends on training, unit structure, terrain, fatigue, and command communication; fear is not a complete explanation by itself.

## What Changed

- Created a cross-cutting mechanism connecting formation integrity, leader signals, rout, and casualty asymmetry.
