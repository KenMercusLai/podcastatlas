# Political Bias In AI Procurement

Political bias in AI procurement is the risk, raised by [[DavidSacks|David Sacks]] in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-inside-americas-ai-strategy-infrastructure-regulation-and-global-competition-39846955]], that government use of AI systems could subtly shape what people learn, think, or know. The source treats politically biased AI as a public-power problem even if private companies retain broad speech rights to build opinionated systems.

The concept extends [[AIModelBiasGovernance]] from demographic, data, and deployment bias into viewpoint and institutional-power bias. In Sacks's frame, the federal government should not buy or deploy systems that carry partisan or ideological answer-shaping, because procurement can turn model behavior into state-backed information infrastructure.

## Key Claims
- Political bias in AI systems can matter even when it appears as subtle answer framing rather than explicit censorship.
- Government procurement creates a stronger governance burden than private consumer use because the state may make the model part of public services, education, or official workflows.
- The problem overlaps with [[CensorshipIndustrialComplex]] when official or quasi-official channels pressure what information systems show.
- A First Amendment defense for private model builders does not automatically justify government purchase of politically biased systems.
