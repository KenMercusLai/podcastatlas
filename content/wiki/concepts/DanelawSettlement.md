# Danelaw Settlement

## Definition

The Danelaw settlement is the episode's later Anglo-Danish territorial and legal accommodation, associated with an 886 treaty dividing areas governed by English law from those governed by Danish law.

## Current Synthesis

The settlement belongs to a sequence rather than one decisive moment. The [[BattleOfEdington]] and Chippenham siege forced [[Guthrum]] into withdrawal and baptism in 878, while the departure of another Viking force in 879 gave [[AlfredTheGreat|Alfred]] breathing space. A later agreement then expressed coexistence as a boundary between different legal-political zones.

This arrangement qualified victory. Alfred preserved and rebuilt Wessex, but substantial Danish power remained, and England did not yet exist as a unified kingdom. The coexistence nevertheless helped create conditions in which West Saxon institutions and successors could later expand.

## Key Claims

- The Danelaw emerged from negotiated coexistence after warfare rather than from total victory by either side.
- Legal difference and territorial division became tools for stabilizing competing English and Danish authority.
- The settlement preserved Danish rule while giving Alfred time to strengthen Wessex.
- Later English expansion depended on this interim arrangement without being predetermined by it.

## Evidence

- Military precondition - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] links the post-Edington terms to Guthrum's withdrawal and baptism.
- Breathing space - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] notes the 879 departure of another Viking force and a long rebuilding interval.
- Legal-territorial division - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] describes an 886 treaty distinguishing English-law and Danish-law lands.
- Long-run consequence - [[251-alfred-the-great-return-of-the-king-part-2-glt9621369023]] places the settlement before Alfred's heirs extended West Saxon power.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page follows the episode's compressed chronology. It does not settle the exact treaty text, boundary, date, enforcement, local legal variation, relationship between the 878 and 886 agreements, or whether “Danelaw” was a contemporary unified institution. The later success of Alfred's dynasty does not make that outcome inevitable.

## What Changed

- Established the Danelaw as a negotiated legal-territorial interval rather than simply conquered or surrendered land.
