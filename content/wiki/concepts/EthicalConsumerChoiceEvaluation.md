# Ethical Consumer Choice Evaluation

## Definition

Ethical consumer choice evaluation compares products across performance, labor practice, environmental impact, supply-chain boundaries, and the quality of supporting evidence without treating the buyer's identity or lifestyle as proof for or against the choice.

## Current Synthesis

The detergent dispute shows why neither product performance nor a green corporate image is sufficient on its own. [[BrendanFrancisNewnam]] offers wages, carbon claims, buildings, supply chains, smell, and cleaning effectiveness as reasons to prefer [[Ecover]], while [[MalcolmGladwell]] answers with a manufacturing-energy claim for [[ProcterGamble|Procter & Gamble]]. The criteria are relevant, but the teaser does not make the measures comparable; the durable judgment is methodological rather than a brand ranking.

## Key Claims

- Ethical product comparison should keep functional performance and corporate conduct visible at the same time.
- Environmental claims require explicit scopes, baselines, time periods, supply-chain boundaries, and realistic alternatives before two firms can be ranked.
- Labor, buildings, factory energy, total emissions, supply-chain footprint, scent, and cleaning results answer different questions and cannot be collapsed into one unsupported score.
- A consumer's location or environmental identity does not validate the choice, but neither should it be used to dismiss the reasons offered.
- Humor and self-critique can reopen a public recommendation, but they do not substitute for the evidence missing from the original or follow-up account.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The source does not contain the original episode, full bonus episode, lifecycle assessments, wage records, building certifications, emissions reports, or cleaning tests.
- Corporate sustainability performance can vary by product, facility, geography, ownership period, and accounting boundary; one favorable indicator does not determine the total comparison.
- Consumer identity can shape motives and social meaning, but rejecting stereotype does not make every ethical-consumption claim accurate.
- The source supports a framework for asking better questions, not a conclusion that Ecover is categorically better than P&G.

## What Changed

- Initial synthesis separates relevant decision criteria from the evidentiary work required to compare them.
- Adds an explicit boundary between evaluating a person's reasons and stereotyping the person's identity.
