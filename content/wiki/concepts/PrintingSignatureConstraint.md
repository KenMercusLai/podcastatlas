# Printing Signature Constraint

Printing signature constraint is the production rule that books are usually printed in multi-page sections rather than as isolated single pages. [[our-book-vs-the-global-supply-chain]] adds the concept through [[TomMayer]]'s explanation that the [[PlanetMoneyBook]] is printed in 16-page signatures, which makes a one-page specialty-paper idea much more expensive than it first sounds.

The concept matters because it turns a creative feature into an imposition and materials problem. If the physical production unit is a section, then page count, inserts, paper changes, illustration placement, and trim-size changes all have to respect that unit.

## Key Claims
- Printing signatures make the book cheaper and manufacturable at scale, but they reduce page-by-page freedom.
- A single specialty page can force a larger specialty section or additional manufacturing step.
- Signature structure helps explain why page count and book thickness are production decisions, not just editorial length decisions.
- The concept links visible design to invisible factory layout.
