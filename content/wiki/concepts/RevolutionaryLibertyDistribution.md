# Revolutionary Liberty Distribution

## Definition

Revolutionary liberty distribution is the analysis of how a revolution can expand political capacity for some groups while withholding, narrowing, or reversing it for others.

## Current Synthesis

The episode rejects a single net-benefit answer. Radical state constitutions widened political power for many ordinary white men, and a small number of property-owning women voted in New Jersey. Yet women's war labor produced little durable formal equality, Native nations lost the limited restraint Britain had placed on settler expansion, and Black experience divided across Continental service, British emancipation offers, Loyalist evacuation, re-enslavement, and uneven abolitionist change.

## Key Claims

- Political revolution does not distribute liberty uniformly across race, gender, class, place, or allegiance.
- Household and support labor can be militarily indispensable without yielding equivalent citizenship.
- Expanded white male participation can coexist with intensified Indigenous dispossession.
- Wartime emancipation opportunities can coexist with capture and return to enslavement.
- Abolitionist momentum after the Revolution qualifies, but does not erase, the preservation of slavery.

## Evidence

- White male participation: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] cites radical Pennsylvania politics as a gain for poorer white men.
- Women's limits: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] pairs essential camp and household labor with brief, limited property-based female voting in New Jersey.
- Native loss: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] argues independence removed British attempts to balance settler expansion against Indigenous nations.
- Black divergence: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] discusses Black Loyalists, Harry Washington, post-Yorktown returns to enslavers, and abolitionist growth in northern states and Britain.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source offers a high-level distributional map, not a comprehensive group-by-group history. New Jersey voting involved few property-owning women; Native nations had different alliances and strategies; and Black Patriot, Loyalist, enslaved, freed, evacuated, and re-enslaved experiences cannot be collapsed into one outcome.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to compare gains, exclusions, and reversals without declaring one uniform revolutionary beneficiary.
