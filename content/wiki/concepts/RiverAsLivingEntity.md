# River As Living Entity / 河流作为生命

## Definition
River as living entity / 河流作为生命 is the interpretive and legal-ecological frame that treats a river not merely as water, scenery, resource, or infrastructure, but as a living system with its own relations, memories, vulnerability, and possible standing.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses [[IsARiverAlive|Is a River Alive?]] to connect nature writing with rights-of-nature law and environmental activism. A river becomes carrier-bag-like because it gathers waves, sediment, animals, plants, microorganisms, boats, migration memory, legends, place names, and cities into one flowing system; damaging it can therefore be framed not only as engineering change but as harm to a living relation.

## Key Claims
- A river can be read as a living system rather than only as a resource channel.
- Rights-of-nature and legal-personhood examples make ecological relation visible inside legal language.
- Treating dam construction or river alteration as harm to life changes the moral and political frame of infrastructure debate.
- The concept links [[CarrierBagNarrative|carrier-bag narrative]] to ecology because both depend on gathered relations rather than isolated objects.
- Nature writing can prepare readers for legal and political imagination by making nonhuman systems legible as participants.

Carrier-bag ecology:
- [[episode-225-yidai-yilu-8212959555-438866|Episode 225: 一袋一录]] says a river is itself a carrier bag containing material, living beings, memories, practices, legends, and settlements.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not independently verify the legal status, scope, or practical effects of the rights-of-nature examples. Future legal or environmental sources should distinguish symbolic recognition, enforceable standing, governance structure, and ecological outcome.
