# New-Economy Household Transmission / 新经济向家庭传导

## Definition

New-economy household transmission is the employment, service-business, wage, tax, transfer, public-service, and capital-income channels through which technology-led productivity and corporate gains become resident income, security, wealth, and consumption capacity.

## Current Synthesis

China's emerging industries show strength most clearly in supply, productivity, exports, engineering, and lower costs. Unlike the earlier property cycle, they have not yet produced an equally broad loop across investment, employment, household wealth, local revenue, and consumption. Property adjustment simultaneously weakens perceived wealth and job confidence, encouraging precautionary saving even when new industries expand.

Transmission can improve through labor-intensive or household-facing services, new occupations, wages, household capital ownership, taxation, transfers, public provision, and social protection. Episode 189 corroborates the diagnosis from an external comparative perspective: unusually strong supply needs stronger ordinary-household consumption, and income and security must improve for demand to match capacity. Overseas success adds another condition because external profits must return through households, fiscal systems, or high-value domestic work rather than remaining a corporate aggregate.

## Key Claims

- Higher productivity, exports, and cheaper goods do not automatically create household income or demand.
- Property losses and employment uncertainty weaken consumption through wealth and expectations.
- Service-sector application can convert technology into household-facing value and new work.
- Taxes, transfers, public services, and social protection can broaden concentrated production gains.
- Household capital income matters when wage and employment multipliers are weak.
- Overseas corporate success needs explicit domestic return channels.
- AI may initially widen the gap if substitution arrives before new demand and occupations.

## Evidence

- Supply-first pattern: [[lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave-lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave|Episode 186]] and [[lvnr1afmbkgg3gjzumqricvnleu8-lvnr1afmbkgg3gjzumqricvnleu8|E256]] say new-industry gains are clearer in capability, productivity, cost, and profits than in household security.
- Commercial and institutional channels: [[lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave-lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave|Episode 186]] identifies education, health care, entertainment, occupations, taxation, transfers, public services, and capital participation.
- Expectations and savings: [[lvnr1afmbkgg3gjzumqricvnleu8-lvnr1afmbkgg3gjzumqricvnleu8|E256]] treats future income, job security, and rebuilding a comfortable savings buffer as more durable consumption drivers than temporary purchase timing.
- Property wealth and consumption: [[lowv9gi-pcc-knbwwwk1mhjxabzw-lowv9gi-pcc-knbwwwk1mhjxabzw|Episode 189]] contrasts the earlier property wealth and employment loop with current weaker confidence and higher saving.
- Supply-demand rebalance: [[lowv9gi-pcc-knbwwwk1mhjxabzw-lowv9gi-pcc-knbwwwk1mhjxabzw|Episode 189]] argues that strong Chinese supply capability needs stronger household demand and security and links overseas expansion to local and domestic benefit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources offer mechanisms rather than measured causal effects or a settled policy design. Lower prices do raise real purchasing power, and export income can support employment and taxes even when direct household transmission is hard to observe. Service jobs can be automated, capital ownership is unequal, transfers carry fiscal tradeoffs, and the property-consumption relationship varies across owners, renters, regions, and generations.

## What Changed

- Added property wealth effects and precautionary saving as the household-demand side of the transmission gap.
- Added overseas-profit return as a channel connecting globalization to domestic consumption capacity.
- Added independent corroboration that supply strength and household demand can diverge.
