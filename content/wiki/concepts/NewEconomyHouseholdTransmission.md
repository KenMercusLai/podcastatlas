# New-Economy Household Transmission / 新经济向家庭传导

## Definition

New-economy household transmission is the set of employment, service-business, wage, tax, transfer, public-service, and capital-income channels through which technology-led productivity and profits become resident income, security, and consumption capacity.

## Current Synthesis

The episode argues that China's emerging industries currently show their strength most clearly in supply, productivity, exports, and lower costs. Unlike the earlier property cycle, they have not yet produced an equally broad loop connecting investment, employment, wealth, consumption, and local fiscal revenue.

Transmission can improve when technology combines with labor-intensive or household-facing services such as education, health care, and entertainment; when workers and households participate in capital returns; and when taxation, transfers, and public provision convert corporate or productivity gains into security and purchasing power. The problem is institutional and commercial, not simply technological.

## Key Claims

- Higher productivity and cheaper goods do not automatically create household income or demand.
- Service-sector application can turn technology into new jobs, occupations, and household-facing value.
- Taxes, transfers, and public services can convert concentrated production gains into broader consumption capacity.
- Household participation in capital income matters when wage and employment multipliers are weak.
- Consumption recovery depends on expected income and job security more than one-off purchase timing.
- AI may initially widen the transmission gap if substitution arrives before new demand and occupations.

## Evidence

- **Supply-first pattern:** [[lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave-lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave]] and [[lvnr1afmbkgg3gjzumqricvnleu8-lvnr1afmbkgg3gjzumqricvnleu8]] say new-industry progress is currently clearer in supply capability, productivity, cost reduction, and profits than in resident income or security.
- **Commercial channels:** [[lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave-lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave]] identifies education, health care, entertainment, and new occupations as possible bridges.
- **Institutional channels:** [[lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave-lpwbbhak2umzdd-u4t2wgstlmave]] identifies taxation, transfers, public services, and capital participation as distribution mechanisms.
- **Consumption boundary:** [[lvnr1afmbkgg3gjzumqricvnleu8-lvnr1afmbkgg3gjzumqricvnleu8]] treats future income, employment expectations, and a comfortable savings buffer as more durable drivers than temporary trade-in subsidies.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The two summaries offer possible channels rather than independent evidence, measured causal effects, or a settled policy design. Service-sector job creation can itself be automated, capital ownership is unequal, and transfers or public equity introduce fiscal and governance tradeoffs. Lower prices do raise real purchasing power, but that benefit may be offset by weak income or employment security.
