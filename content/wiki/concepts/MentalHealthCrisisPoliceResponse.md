# Mental Health Crisis Police Response

Mental health crisis police response is the source's concrete version of [[MentalHealthCrisisInterventionBoundary]] when emergency calls are answered by police rather than health or social-service responders. [[kyiv-brawl-ukraines-top-general-fired-6a609aec4fac21e67f1be165]] develops the concept through [[JoseArmas]]'s death in [[Albuquerque]] after his family called for help during a suicide threat.

The episode presents this as a systems mismatch. Families may call emergency services seeking care, de-escalation, or social workers, but a police-led response can bring armed enforcement logic into a fragile mental-health situation.

## Key Claims
- Suicide threats and behavioral crises need trained response pathways, not only armed containment.
- Family expectations can diverge sharply from what emergency dispatch actually sends.
- Police may be asked to handle social, medical, and drug-related problems that enforcement cannot solve alone.
- Tactical patience matters when the immediate threat is limited and time could reduce danger.
- Crisis response should be evaluated alongside police-killing data because many fatal encounters begin as care calls.
