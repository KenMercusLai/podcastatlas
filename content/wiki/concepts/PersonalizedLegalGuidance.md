# Personalized Legal Guidance

Personalized legal guidance is [[BenjaminAlarie]]'s distinction between tailoring how law is explained and changing what law provides. In [[tech-20260805-0805-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260805-0805-mp-tech-pod-128]], he says personalization should still mean the same rights and same rules for everyone; what changes is the explanation, context, and route through the legal system.

The source's example is speed-limit guidance that can vary by roadway, time of day, and weather conditions. The broader point is that AI could make legal instructions more context-sensitive without making rights person-specific. That makes [[PersonalizedLegalGuidance]] a useful but risky part of [[AIAccessToJustice]]: it can improve comprehension, but it may require large amounts of personal, social, and case data.

## Key Claims
- Personalization should adapt explanations and pathways, not core legal rights.
- Context-sensitive guidance can make law more usable for ordinary people.
- Personalization becomes risky when it requires data collection that creates [[CivilLibertiesSurveillanceRisk]].
- The concept depends on [[LegalAIVerificationAuditability]] because tailored legal output is harder to trust if users cannot see or check why guidance differs.
