# Atherosclerosis Prevention Targets

## Definition
Atherosclerosis prevention targets are the source's practical risk-control levers for reducing cumulative arterial injury before symptomatic cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease appears.

## Current Synthesis
The episode makes atherosclerotic cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease the largest mortality category in Attia's "death bar" analysis. The prevention frame is therefore built around silent, measurable drivers rather than waiting for symptoms: blood pressure, smoking or inhaled irritants, and ApoB-bearing lipoproteins.

The source treats vascular injury as both mechanical and chemical. High blood pressure stresses the endothelium mechanically; smoking, cannabis smoke, vaping aerosols, nicotine-delivery context, and particulate pollution raise inhaled-exposure concerns; ApoB particle burden supplies the lipid-entry side of plaque formation. The synthesis is not that one number solves cardiovascular risk, but that earlier measurement and cumulative exposure reduction can matter more than late reaction.

VOL.111 adds a clinician-facing public explanation of the downstream sequence: endothelial injury and lipid deposition contribute to plaque, plaque can narrow and eventually occlude a vessel, and rupture can generate embolic events. Its practical prevention message converges on blood pressure, glucose, lipids, smoking avoidance, movement, and stress management while rejecting foods, alcohol, creams, or supplements as literal vessel-cleaning or vessel-softening treatments.

## Key Claims
- Atherosclerotic disease is treated as the largest mortality category in the episode's risk map, supporting risk management before symptoms or a high 10-year event score.
- Blood pressure is a central modifiable driver of vascular injury, and measurement technique matters because office readings can be distorted by context.
- Smoking and inhaled irritants are treated as chemical injury to vascular and lung systems.
- ApoB-bearing lipoproteins are treated as a causal, measurable atherosclerosis target.
- Plaque, stenosis, occlusion, thrombosis, and embolism should remain distinct terms even when they form part of one disease pathway.
- Risk-factor control is not equivalent to a promise that established vascular change can be reversed by a food, drink, cream, or supplement.

## Evidence
- Mortality priority: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] says atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease causes roughly 18 to 19 million global deaths per year, compared with about 11 million from cancer.
- Stroke and pressure: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] distinguishes ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke and identifies hypertension as a leading hemorrhagic stroke driver.
- Measurement protocol: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] says Attia favors sitting quietly before measurement and, in his practice, two readings per day for two weeks when assessing blood pressure.
- Injury channels: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] contrasts smoking's chemical endothelial irritation with high blood pressure's mechanical irritation.
- Lipid target: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] identifies ApoB-bearing particles as the lipid target for preventing atherosclerosis.
- Disease progression and public prevention: [[vol-111-xueguan-waike-ni-hai-zai-xin-zhexie-xueguan-jiankang-chanpin-ma-ni-hai-bu-zhidao-zhege-keshi-ma-ls8ocppuxuf_oxws1tcioduomzmb]] connects endothelial injury and lipid deposition with plaque, stenosis, occlusion, and embolic events while prioritizing blood pressure, glucose, lipids, smoking avoidance, movement, and stress management.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide individualized cardiovascular treatment advice. Blood-pressure, glucose, and lipid targets; hydration; exercise; stress interventions; medications; side effects; kidney function; pregnancy; age; existing plaque; family history; diabetes; smoking history; and overall risk require qualified clinical interpretation. The river and sediment analogy is explanatory, not a complete account of plaque biology.
