# Strategic War Gaming

## Definition

Strategic war gaming is the use of interactive, adjudicated crisis scenarios to test assumptions, reveal unconsidered interactions, and make strategic choices operationally concrete without treating play as an exact forecast.

## Current Synthesis

[[a-serious-game-a-serious-game|A Serious Game]] presents war gaming as disciplined preparation for surprise. Roles distribute agency across actors; dice can interrupt habitual reasoning; repetition tests whether an uncomfortable result depends on one group; and scenario detail turns broad objectives into problems of time, movement, manpower, civilians, contamination, emotion, and incomplete information.

The value lies in expanding the set of plausible questions an institution can see. A game succeeds when it exposes a possibility or constraint worth investigating, even if the played outcome never occurs. That same logic requires restraint: rules, players, adjudication, and scenario boundaries shape what can appear, so a result is not self-validating evidence about the future.

## Key Claims

- Interactive roles reveal strategic interdependence that solitary analysis can omit.
- Random events can disrupt habitual reasoning without supplying calibrated prediction.
- Repetition across participants can identify a robust planning concern inside a scenario design.
- Operational detail converts abstract confidence into testable requirements and tradeoffs.
- Surprise is useful when it produces new questions, contingency work, and revised assumptions.
- Scenario outcomes remain conditional on game design, player behavior, adjudication, and evidence quality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source emphasizes revelatory failures more than false alarms, participant bias, rule sensitivity, adjudicator discretion, learning transfer, or the risk that vivid scenarios crowd out less dramatic possibilities. Repeated outcomes within one model do not establish external validity. Games should therefore generate hypotheses and contingency questions for further analysis, not substitute for intelligence, empirical research, exercises, diplomacy, or accountable command judgment.

## What Changed

- Established war gaming as an assumption-testing and question-generating method rather than a prediction engine.
- Added chance, repetition, operational detail, and multi-system interaction as distinct learning mechanisms.
