# Margin Trading Forced Selling

## Definition

Margin trading forced selling is the mechanism by which falling collateral or rising account loss requires more cash or compulsory position reduction, turning market movement into a financing deadline.

## Current Synthesis

The Planet Money source separates cause from amplification. Margin need not create the first decline: once prices fall far enough, collateral rules shorten decision time and force sales that can deepen a move even when the underlying business thesis remains intact. India and South Korean cases make this a market-structure mechanism rather than only an individual budgeting error.

EP95 adds the retail FX account. Because a leveraged currency position is continuously marked against posted margin, the broker may close it before the investor's hoped-for recovery. This makes liquidation categorically different from [[StopLossDiscipline|a voluntary planned stop]]: the investor no longer controls the timing, and external borrowing only adds another repayment obligation.

## Key Claims

- Borrowing against a position converts price movement into a collateral deadline.
- Forced sales can amplify a shock without being its original cause.
- A correct long-term thesis can still fail as a trade when financing cannot survive the path.
- Broker liquidation is different from a precommitted voluntary exit.
- Margin totals alone do not time a crash; growth, collateral quality, liquidity, rules, and triggers matter.
- Borrowing outside a margin account can stack household debt on top of market leverage.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source establishes that margin caused the initial market decline it discusses. Liquidation thresholds, negative-balance treatment, eligible collateral, and broker discretion vary by product and jurisdiction. Forced selling can also arise through funds, derivatives, redemptions, or institutional mandates, so not every [[LiquidityDrivenVolatilityCascade]] is a retail margin event.

- Sharpened the distinction between voluntary stop loss and broker-imposed liquidation.
- Added the risk of stacking consumer debt on top of derivative leverage.
