# Species-Appropriate Animal Care

## Definition
Species-appropriate animal care is an ethology-first approach that designs housing, feeding, activity, touch, social contact, and training around what an animal evolved and learned to perceive and do, then adjusts for breed and individual history.

## Current Synthesis
The framework begins with functional questions: Is the species predator or prey? Solitary, loosely social, or group living? Which senses dominate? How does it obtain food, avoid threats, rest, play, and regulate proximity? Welfare problems can arise when domestic routines block those systems even while meeting basic survival needs.

In practice, this turns enrichment from generic entertainment into behavioral opportunity. Dogs may need different safe outlets along the predatory sequence; horses need social contact and extended foraging; cats need hunting-like play, safe territorial organization, scent continuity, and gradual introductions; parrots may redirect blocked foraging into destructive behavior. Care is therefore a fit problem, not a one-size-fits-all checklist.

## Key Claims
- Natural history and sensory ecology are necessary context for interpreting behavior.
- Meeting food quantity is not identical to supporting food-seeking and foraging behavior.
- Breed selection can emphasize parts of a shared behavioral sequence without erasing individual variation.
- Blocked motivated behavior can reappear as stress, destructive behavior, or conflict in the wrong context.
- Enrichment should create safe opportunities for the animal to act, choose, search, manipulate, and disengage.
- Captive-welfare judgments must consider how far-ranging, social, predatory, or otherwise difficult-to-house a species is.

## Evidence
- Horse needs - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] contrasts herd life and long foraging periods with early weaning, isolation, and compressed feeding.
- Dog predatory sequence - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] links breed tendencies to sniffing, stalking, chasing, grabbing, killing, dissecting, and eating, then proposes matched outlets such as nose work, carrying, or tearing.
- Cat ecology - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] treats cats as solitary hunters that may form loose social groups and need territorial, feeding, litter, and introduction arrangements consistent with that history.
- Captive and redirected behavior - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] uses zoo species and destructive parrots to show how range and food-getting needs constrain welfare.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Species-level patterns do not dictate every individual's preference. Breed labels, wild analogies, or predatory-sequence stories can become stereotypes if pain, learning history, age, environment, and direct observation are ignored. Some enrichment claims in the episode, including early nose-work findings, are promising rather than definitive, and dangerous predatory behaviors require safe substitutes rather than literal expression.

## What Changed
- Created an ethology-first welfare framework spanning housing, feeding, enrichment, touch, and social context.
- Distinguished behavioral opportunity from merely supplying exercise or calories.
