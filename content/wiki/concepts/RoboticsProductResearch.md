# Robotics Product Research / 机器人产品研究

## Definition
Robotics product research is a strategy in which a long-run product thesis selects research priorities and real product operation supplies evidence about which model, data, control, hardware, or workflow limitation should be solved next.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded source positions product research between two incomplete extremes. Deployment without repeatable product-market fit can become custom integration that does not raise general capability, while research without product constraints can disperse effort across interesting but non-decisive problems. Dyna's proposed bridge is a directional loop: choose a product end state, build the smallest commercially meaningful task, observe field failures, revise the technical allocation, and lengthen the workflow only as capability and economics permit. In the source's main example, napkin folding exposed recovery and output constraints, which reportedly led Dyna to increase pretraining investment and broaden the product from a manipulation skill into a workflow.

## Key Claims
- A product end state can narrow an uncertain robotics research space without pretending the entire technical path is known.
- Deployment is valuable as research evidence only when it exposes consequential capability boundaries rather than merely increasing installation count.
- Product quality includes recovery time, output presentation, surrounding material flow, and human supervision, not just task-level success.
- Technical allocation should change when field evidence identifies a different bottleneck; in Dyna's account, robustness evidence shifted emphasis toward pretraining.
- The research loop needs commercial discipline because a bounded product supplies sustained access to real variation, failures, and customer priorities.
- Product constraints should guide inquiry without turning one current workflow into a permanent ceiling on general capability.

## Evidence
### Strategic reconciliation
- [[lsso9j-p6l7zgwawhib14zjxksfq-lsso9j-p6l7zgwawhib14zjxksfq]] reports that [[DynaRobotics]] moved beyond an internal research-versus-deployment split by adopting a longer product horizon that could improve intelligence and support a commercial loop.

### Field feedback changes research
- [[lsso9j-p6l7zgwawhib14zjxksfq-lsso9j-p6l7zgwawhib14zjxksfq]] says rare double-grabs, long recovery, stack neatness, and daily output exposed limitations invisible in demonstrations and shifted resources toward pretraining.

### Product expansion
- [[lsso9j-p6l7zgwawhib14zjxksfq-lsso9j-p6l7zgwawhib14zjxksfq]] describes the move from folding alone toward a complete laundry workflow as the next research and commercialization target.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source describes a company strategy, not a controlled comparison with pure research labs, deployment integrators, or other full-stack robotics firms. Product direction can prematurely narrow research, customer requests can overfit the roadmap, and vertical integration can raise capital and coordination costs. The interview does not yet show that Dyna's loop produces transferable intelligence, repeatable customer economics, or durable product-market fit.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Dyna's explicit “product research” framing.
- Defined deployment as a source of research evidence rather than as an end in itself.
- Added the risk that current product constraints can over-narrow general research.
