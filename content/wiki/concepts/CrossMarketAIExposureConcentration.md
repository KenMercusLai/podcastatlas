# Cross-Market AI Exposure Concentration

## Definition
Cross-market AI exposure concentration is the hidden portfolio dependence created when holdings listed in different countries, currencies, or supply-chain layers ultimately rely on the same AI capital-expenditure, customer-demand, or financing cycle.

## Current Synthesis
Geographic labels do not establish economic diversification. A U.S. compute operator and a Chinese hardware supplier can react to different local policies and currencies while still depending on the same hyperscaler, model-lab, data-center, or equipment spending. If the common customer cuts capex or loses financing access, both positions may fall together even though they sit in different markets.

The concept is a look-through test, not a claim that every U.S.-China AI pairing is identical. Domestic substitution, local customers, valuation, balance sheets, product mix, and policy support can create genuine differences. Diversification improves only when those independent drivers are material enough to offset the shared cycle.

## Key Claims
- Country, exchange, and currency diversification can coexist with concentrated end-demand exposure.
- Supply-chain positions should be mapped through customers and capex budgets rather than classified only by issuer domicile.
- Employment, employer stock, and technology funds can add a human-capital layer to the same concentration.
- Domestic substitution may reduce shared exposure over time without protecting near-term earnings from a global demand slowdown.
- The relevant test is how positions fail under the same scenario, not how different their labels appear in normal markets.

## Evidence
- **Shared customer cycle:** [[lt6ytc8utffpes_crf7i-rmxfxzp-lt6ytc8utffpes_crf7i-rmxfxzp]] argues that U.S. compute companies and Chinese hardware suppliers can depend on the same AI customer capex and therefore do not necessarily diversify one another.
- **Near-term China linkage:** [[lt6ytc8utffpes_crf7i-rmxfxzp-lt6ytc8utffpes_crf7i-rmxfxzp]] distinguishes long-run domestic substitution from nearer-term order dependence on U.S. demand and global risk appetite.
- **Household concentration:** [[lt6ytc8utffpes_crf7i-rmxfxzp-lt6ytc8utffpes_crf7i-rmxfxzp]] adds technology employment and employer equity to technology-fund holdings as exposures that should be counted together.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Cross-market correlation is regime dependent, not permanent. Chinese policy support, domestic customers, currency moves, product substitution, and different balance sheets can separate returns. The source does not quantify revenue overlap or estimate correlations, so the concept is a portfolio diagnostic rather than a measured factor model. It should not be used to assume that all AI-linked securities are interchangeable.

- Added employment and employer equity as part of the same household concentration test.
