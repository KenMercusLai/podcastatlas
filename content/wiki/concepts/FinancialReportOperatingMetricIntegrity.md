# Financial Report Operating Metric Integrity / 财报运营指标完整性

## Definition
Financial report operating metric integrity is the requirement that non-statutory operating indicators in a company's report remain traceable over time, comparable with peers, and verifiable from disclosed base data.

## Current Synthesis
[[shangye-xiaoyang-48-buyao-zai-caibao-zhong-chuangzao-zhibiao-1009836716]] makes the concept concrete through [[LaoPuGold|老铺黄金]]. The episode accepts that companies may supplement accounting statements with operating indicators, but argues that investor-useful metrics need a stable denominator, stable scope, peer-relevant comparison set, and enough underlying data to check the calculation. When a report keeps producing impressive rankings while changing whether the comparison is gold jewelry, jewelry, or global luxury; China mainland or global; store, shopping mall, revenue, productivity, or sales-area efficiency, the metric becomes harder to distinguish from positioning language.

## Key Claims
- Supplemental operating indicators can improve financial reports when they explain business reality that accounting lines do not show clearly.
- A metric loses integrity when its scope, denominator, or definition changes across filings without enough explanation for trend comparison.
- Third-party rankings remain weak evidence if investors cannot reproduce or sanity-check them from disclosed store counts, sales, floor area, and treatment of renovations.
- Peer comparison fails when the named peer group does not disclose the same metric or does not operate with the same reporting denominator.
- Metric invention becomes especially trust-sensitive when a company is trying to shift investor perception from one category narrative to another.

## Evidence
- Traceability standard: [[shangye-xiaoyang-48-buyao-zai-caibao-zhong-chuangzao-zhibiao-1009836716]] states that operating metrics in financial reports should be historically traceable and peer-comparable.
- Changing scope: [[shangye-xiaoyang-48-buyao-zai-caibao-zhong-chuangzao-zhibiao-1009836716]] says Lao Pu Gold's materials move from gold-jewelry single-store ranking to broader jewelry and global luxury-group per-mall/productivity framing while retaining "ranking first" style conclusions.
- Verification gap: [[shangye-xiaoyang-48-buyao-zai-caibao-zhong-chuangzao-zhibiao-1009836716]] says the host could not find enough conventional sales, operating-area, and adjustment-treatment data to recalculate the highlighted metrics.
- Denominator problem: [[shangye-xiaoyang-48-buyao-zai-caibao-zhong-chuangzao-zhibiao-1009836716]] notes that Lao Pu Gold operates multiple outlets in some malls, so a mall-level denominator can produce a different picture from a store-level denominator.
- Luxury-comparison problem: [[shangye-xiaoyang-48-buyao-zai-caibao-zhong-chuangzao-zhibiao-1009836716]] argues that luxury peers such as Hermès, Louis Vuitton, Cartier, Bvlgari, and Tiffany generally do not disclose the same productivity indicators, weakening comparability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove Lao Pu Gold's operating data is false, and it does not present a full valuation or accounting model. The concept should therefore be used as a disclosure-quality screen, not as automatic evidence of fraud or business weakness.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate investor-useful operating disclosure from hard-to-verify promotional metrics in financial reports.
