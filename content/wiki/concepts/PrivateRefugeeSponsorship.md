# Private Refugee Sponsorship

## Definition

Private refugee sponsorship is a resettlement model in which citizen, religious, or community groups undertake practical and financial support for admitted refugees alongside public admission and oversight.

## Current Synthesis

The Canadian case distributes resettlement across housing, employment, money, paperwork, meals, language learning, education, and companionship. Its governing principle is additionality: private effort should add admissions and support capacity rather than allow government to withdraw its own commitment.

The model works through task fit and community continuity rather than one sponsor doing everything. Its promise therefore depends on both [[DistributedKindness]] and institutional support, while its risks include uneven capacity, informal burden, exclusion, weak accountability, and the possibility that government substitutes private charity for public obligation.

## Key Claims

- Sponsorship converts willingness to welcome into concrete resettlement tasks.
- Additionality protects public responsibility by making private admissions supplementary.
- Complementary roles let groups contribute according to skill, resources, and relationships.
- Durable belonging requires more than initial housing or employment.
- Public institutions remain necessary for admission, safeguards, schools, and services.
- Oversight should test meaningful capacity without making low-risk details a barrier to admission.

## Evidence

### Additionality and task distribution

- [[i-was-a-stranger-and-you-welcomed-me-i-was-a-stranger-and-you-welcomed-me]] attributes additionality to the Canadian model and follows a community dividing housing, employment, paperwork, education, meals, and social welcome.

### Institutional complement

- [[i-was-a-stranger-and-you-welcomed-me-i-was-a-stranger-and-you-welcomed-me]] also shows a school board supplying language support and staff capacity, preventing private care from becoming the whole resettlement system.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a moral and narrative case rather than a comparative program evaluation. It does not establish average outcomes, costs, selection effects, sponsor failure rates, refugee preferences, or the optimal balance of screening and speed. Additionality is a normative design rule, not evidence that every program preserves it in practice.

## What Changed

- Established additionality and complementary task allocation as the model's core safeguards and operating mechanisms.
