## Definition

旁支继承的连锁内乱风险 / collateral succession disorder risk is the danger that replacing a ruler's established descendant line with a sibling or other collateral branch does not eliminate competing claims but carries them into later generations, where reciprocal restoration demands can produce repeated conflict.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-231-hanjingdi-jiuhou-jing-zaojiu-wulong-chuanwei-shijian-1-lluz1oxfqrazahrdas5sl5ybouwj|Hanji 231 part 1]] now supplies the expectation-forming beginning. Before naming a crown prince, [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] reportedly tells his younger full brother [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]] during a drinking banquet that Liu Wu will inherit. Liu Wu declines formally but is pleased, while [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] welcomes the statement. [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] objects that Han succession should pass from father to son and loses favor. The promise is not a completed designation, but sovereign speech plus family approval can create a politically durable claim before formal procedure acts.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-240-hanjingdi-kusu-xiongnu-he-taihou-sha-le-wode-ying-lvl4t-2etswvi3ts9gbvor5dh8mi|Hanji 240]] presents the later warning through [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]]'s remonstrance to the dowager. Yuan answers the continuing preference with the Song Xuangong precedent: choosing a brother over a son creates obligations and grievances between two lines, so a later return to the original line can provoke the collateral heir's descendants and extend disorder across generations.

The mechanism is therefore not simply “an unusual heir is bad.” The risk comes from leaving multiple lines with plausible claims, remembered promises, and armed or elite supporters. A decision motivated by immediate family affection can appear to solve one succession moment while transferring the unresolved conflict forward.

Historical analogy also functions as a safer court technology. Yuan can warn the dowager about Liu Wu without directly accusing her beloved son of present rebellion. By translating current preference into an older multigenerational pattern, he turns “small affection versus great duty” from a moral slogan into a forecast of institutional consequences.

## Key Claims

- Collateral succession can multiply rather than settle legitimate claimant lines.
- Informal sovereign speech can create a remembered collateral claim before any formal designation exists.
- Reciprocal promises between sibling branches may bind one generation but become grievances for their descendants.
- Immediate family affection can externalize its political cost into future civil conflict.
- A stable succession warning must consider later restoration claims, not only the current candidate's ability or popularity.
- Historical analogy can make a dangerous court objection discussable without treating the analogy as deterministic proof.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on two podcast notes covering an early promise and a later historical analogy. It does not establish that every collateral successor produces disorder, that direct descent always prevents conflict, or that the Song sequence maps mechanically onto Western Han institutions. The banquet setting, intoxication, exact promise, private reactions, dismissal mechanism, Song genealogy, number and duration of disturbances, wording of Yuan's remonstrance, Dowager Dou's decision process, and Liu Wu's intention require comparison with transmitted records. Sovereign speech can create expectations without becoming a legal designation; historical analogy identifies a mechanism and persuasion strategy rather than proving the outcome by itself.

## What Changed

- Backfilled the mechanism from later warning to initial expectation formation through sovereign speech, a receptive candidate, and senior-family approval.
- Distinguished an informal promise with political effects from a completed legal designation.
- Preserved historical analogy as a low-directness method for confronting the resulting royal preference.
