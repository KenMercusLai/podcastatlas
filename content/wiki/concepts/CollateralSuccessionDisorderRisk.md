## Definition

旁支继承的连锁内乱风险 / collateral succession disorder risk is the danger that replacing a ruler's established descendant line with a sibling or other collateral branch does not eliminate competing claims but carries them into later generations, where reciprocal restoration demands can produce repeated conflict.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-240-hanjingdi-kusu-xiongnu-he-taihou-sha-le-wode-ying-lvl4t-2etswvi3ts9gbvor5dh8mi|Hanji 240]] presents the idea through [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]]'s remonstrance to [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]]. The dowager favors her younger son [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]] as successor to [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]. Yuan answers with the Song Xuangong precedent: choosing a brother over a son creates obligations and grievances between two lines, so a later return to the original line can provoke the collateral heir's descendants and extend disorder across generations.

The mechanism is therefore not simply “an unusual heir is bad.” The risk comes from leaving multiple lines with plausible claims, remembered promises, and armed or elite supporters. A decision motivated by immediate family affection can appear to solve one succession moment while transferring the unresolved conflict forward.

Historical analogy also functions as a safer court technology. Yuan can warn the dowager about Liu Wu without directly accusing her beloved son of present rebellion. By translating current preference into an older multigenerational pattern, he turns “small affection versus great duty” from a moral slogan into a forecast of institutional consequences.

## Key Claims

- Collateral succession can multiply rather than settle legitimate claimant lines.
- Reciprocal promises between sibling branches may bind one generation but become grievances for their descendants.
- Immediate family affection can externalize its political cost into future civil conflict.
- A stable succession warning must consider later restoration claims, not only the current candidate's ability or popularity.
- Historical analogy can make a dangerous court objection discussable without treating the analogy as deterministic proof.

Western Han application:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-240-hanjingdi-kusu-xiongnu-he-taihou-sha-le-wode-ying-lvl4t-2etswvi3ts9gbvor5dh8mi|Hanji 240]] says Yuan uses the precedent against Dowager Dou's preference for Liu Wu and that she later sends the Liang king back to his fief.

Remonstrance method:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-240-hanjingdi-kusu-xiongnu-he-taihou-sha-le-wode-ying-lvl4t-2etswvi3ts9gbvor5dh8mi|Hanji 240]] frames the choice as whether preserving immediate affection is worth risking wider dynastic disorder.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one podcast's compact retelling of a historical analogy. It does not establish that every collateral successor produces disorder, that direct descent always prevents conflict, or that the Song sequence maps mechanically onto Western Han institutions. The exact Song genealogy, number and duration of disturbances, wording of Yuan's remonstrance, Dowager Dou's decision process, and Liu Wu's current intention require comparison with transmitted records. Historical analogy identifies a mechanism and a persuasion strategy; it is not outcome proof by itself.

## What Changed

- Created a mechanism-level distinction between resolving one succession moment and multiplying future claimant lines.
- Identified historical analogy as a low-directness method for confronting dangerous royal preference.
