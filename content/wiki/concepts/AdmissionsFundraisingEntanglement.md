# Admissions–Fundraising Entanglement

## Definition

Admissions–Fundraising Entanglement is the overlap among student selection, athletic recruitment, family-wealth research, relationship management, and anticipated donations within an educational institution.

## Current Synthesis

The Georgetown case shows why formal separation cannot be inferred from separate job titles or databases. Athletics-development emails discussed prospective recruits' home values and family resources, and staff later assessed the Khoury family's giving capacity after Catherine's admission. These practices make fundraising operationally relevant around athletic recruitment, yet donor cultivation is not equivalent to authorizing a coach's private payment, so institutional hypocrisy and criminal liability remain distinct questions.

## Key Claims

- Family wealth and institutional ties can become operational data around an admitted student.
- Athletic recruitment can connect prospective admissions decisions as well as admitted students to development opportunities.
- Prospective-donor research complicates claims that money and admission occupy wholly separate systems.
- Institutional inconsistency does not legalize or authorize a private payment.
- Timing, approval, decision rights, and evidentiary authentication are necessary to move from contextual overlap to a specific causal or legal claim.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The bounded sources do not show that fundraising staff caused Catherine's admission, knew about the payment, or promised favorable treatment. A university may lawfully cultivate donors while prohibiting private payments to coaches; the ethical tension does not by itself resolve the criminal elements of a case.

## What Changed

- Extended the evidence from post-admission donor research to pre-admission discussions of recruits' family wealth.
- Preserved the distinction between systemic financial overlap and proof of a specific authorized exchange.
