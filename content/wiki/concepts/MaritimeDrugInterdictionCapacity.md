# Maritime Drug Interdiction Capacity

Maritime drug interdiction capacity is the ability to detect, track, board, and stop drug shipments moving through ports and at sea. [[taking-the-shine-off-albedo-and-global-temperatures-6a730fdaca067b295bab9760]] uses [[WestAfricaCocaineTransit]] to show how this capacity can lag behind traffickers' logistics.

[[OriOgunbiyi]] says governments lack advanced security technology for ports, maritime borders, and fast-boat interception, and that some countries lack even sniffer dogs at airports. [[PauloSilva]] adds that organized crime groups increasingly use powerful go-fast boats, which are dangerous to intercept.

## Key Claims
- Maritime capacity is not only ships; it includes port scanning, intelligence, dogs, pay, training, and fast response.
- Go-fast boats and at-sea transfers make interdiction physically dangerous and technically demanding.
- Weak capacity increases the corruption pressure that can lead to [[NarcoStateRisk]].
