# Humanistic Science Communication

Humanistic science communication is the source's reading of [[CarlSagan|Carl Sagan]] as someone who can explain science accurately while preserving literary force, cultural memory, emotional warmth, and human responsibility. In [[sp-01-yuzhou-kaer-sagen-de-xingchen-dahai-he-renlei-mengxiang-543901074]], [[CosmosBook|《宇宙》 / Cosmos]], [[ContactNovel|《接触》 / Contact]], [[SETI]], the [[VoyagerGoldenRecord|Voyager golden record]], and [[NuclearWinter]] all show science as a way of locating human beings inside a larger cosmos rather than reducing them to dust.

The concept is not anti-skeptical. The source's Sagan refuses superstition, UFO certainty, and pseudoscience, but he also refuses a cold style of science communication that drains curiosity of human meaning. The working balance is evidence plus wonder: scientific claims remain answerable to proof, while public explanation is allowed to be beautiful, moving, and civilizationally serious.

## Key Claims
- Good science communication needs accuracy, clarity, story, and emotional orientation.
- Wonder becomes stronger, not weaker, when it stays connected to evidence and correction.
- Humanistic science communication differs from romanticized pseudoscience because it does not let beauty substitute for testability.
- Sagan's work makes cosmic scale a source of responsibility rather than nihilism.
- The source treats public science as civic work: it can defend exploration, warn against nuclear self-destruction, and help people imagine themselves as part of one civilization.
