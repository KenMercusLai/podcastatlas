## Definition

篡位后旧皇族收束 / post-usurpation royal house containment is the pattern in which a new regime reduces the displaced royal house's capacity by separating royal kinsmen from territorial command, confining or managing inherited claimants, and downgrading titles while preserving enough ceremonial language to make the transition look orderly.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-635-bei-wangmang-toujia-hou-liushi-huangzu-hequ-hecong-lhldulizw9ksijjbfzwjmxfsyhqp|Hanji 635]] grounds the concept in [[WangMang|王莽]]'s opening [[XinDynasty|新朝]] handling of the [[WesternHanDynasty|西汉]] Liu royal house. The episode begins with the puzzle that many Liu descendants existed after two centuries of Han rule, yet few openly challenged Wang Mang at accession.

The source's answer is not that Liu royal identity ceased to matter. It shows Wang Mang shrinking the practical channels through which that identity could become action: Liu-surname commandery governors are moved into central advisory posts, [[RuziYing|孺子婴 / 刘婴]] is placed inside a tightly managed Ding'an Palace environment, and former Han princes and marquises are downgraded before later losing titles.

This makes containment different from simple extermination. Wang Mang still needs forms, ranks, and controlled old-dynasty symbols because [[PostUsurpationLegitimacyConsolidation|post-usurpation legitimacy consolidation]] requires continuity as well as replacement. The old royal house is therefore kept visible enough to certify transition, but stripped of local command, open communication, and high-status title language.

## Key Claims

- A displaced royal house remains politically dangerous because lineage can become a rallying point even after formal accession.
- Removing royal kinsmen from local governorships weakens their ability to convert name prestige into troops, tax base, and official networks.
- A child claimant can be neutralized through managed residence, attendants, and speech control while still being treated as a ceremonial survivor.
- Title downgrading changes public hierarchy: old kings and marquises become lesser ranks before the new order fully strips them.
- Containment works through a mixture of continuity and coercion rather than through either mercy or violence alone.
- The same mechanism can later backfire if preserved dynastic memory becomes material for restoration politics.

## Evidence

- Royal office transfer: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-635-bei-wangmang-toujia-hou-liushi-huangzu-hequ-hecong-lhldulizw9ksijjbfzwjmxfsyhqp|Hanji 635]] says Liu-surname royal kinsmen serving as commandery governors are reassigned as remonstrance officials after Wang Mang's accession.
- Claimant confinement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-635-bei-wangmang-toujia-hou-liushi-huangzu-hequ-hecong-lhldulizw9ksijjbfzwjmxfsyhqp|Hanji 635]] presents Liu Ying's Ding'an Palace arrangement as practical house arrest despite its formal protective appearance.
- Communication control: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-635-bei-wangmang-toujia-hou-liushi-huangzu-hequ-hecong-lhldulizw9ksijjbfzwjmxfsyhqp|Hanji 635]] says Wang Mang restricts the people around Liu Ying from speaking with him, producing an isolated upbringing.
- Title downgrading: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-635-bei-wangmang-toujia-hou-liushi-huangzu-hequ-hecong-lhldulizw9ksijjbfzwjmxfsyhqp|Hanji 635]] says former Han princes are lowered from kings to dukes and Han marquises to sons before later title removal.
- Continuity problem: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-635-bei-wangmang-toujia-hou-liushi-huangzu-hequ-hecong-lhldulizw9ksijjbfzwjmxfsyhqp|Hanji 635]] links these moves to the larger puzzle of why Liu royal resistance is initially limited, without claiming every Liu kinsman's motive is known.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one episode's account of Wang Mang's 9 CE measures. It explains capacity reduction better than inner motive: the source does not survey all Liu royal branches or prove that every non-rebel was deterred by these policies. Later Han restoration claimants show that containment did not eliminate dynastic memory. Exact lists of transferred officials, palace controls, downgraded titleholders, and regional Liu reactions remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 635's account of Wang Mang's early handling of Liu royal kinsmen, Liu Ying, and former Han titles.
