# Small Trust Builds Large Trust / 小信诚则大信立

Small trust builds large trust / 小信诚则大信立 is the trust principle emphasized in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-05-guangcai-duomu-de-weiwenhou-1-lmkxzbmwhw3nhurape-eebydqcdl]]. The episode uses [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]'s rainy hunting appointment to argue that political credibility is built from minor promises as well as formal declarations.

The source extends the claim through [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s criticism of a Song court reward case: if the state promises office to whoever captures a major robber, then replacing that promise with money damages public credibility even if the immediate case looks administratively small. The source also keeps a practical tension visible: in real statecraft, aligning small promises with large interests is harder than the slogan suggests, and a [[LegalistRulerTechnique|Legalist ruler-technique]] frame may treat high-stakes affairs more conditionally.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-1-lgmjapc2eecefbdshnpenzfcv6ga]] adds a sharper Qin variant through [[XimuLixin|徙木立信]]. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] proves that the state will pay a strange but public reward before issuing the reform law. The episode treats this as credibility built for coercive execution, not only as ruler virtue.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-2-lk-d9uibhneerfol6z2rrle-6ypm]] makes the principle explicit through [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s commentary on "信." The episode strings together [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]] returning land after coercive pressure from Cao Mo, [[JinWenGong|晋文公]] withdrawing from Yuan despite imminent victory, [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]] keeping the rainy hunting appointment, and [[XimuLixin|徙木立信]]. The shared point is that state order depends on rulers keeping terms even when reneging would bring an immediate advantage.

The same source distinguishes moral and instrumental trust. Confucian statecraft treats trust as a foundation for ruling people and relating to the world; Legalist statecraft can treat trust as a tool that makes rewards and punishments believable. Shang Yang's case sits on the Legalist side, but Sima Guang still treats the credibility itself as politically real.

## Key Claims
- Minor promises function as public tests of whether a ruler's word means anything.
- State credibility is damaged when a government changes promised terms after people rely on them.
- Trust is not only personal virtue; it becomes a condition for stable rule.
- The concept links Confucian moral politics to [[SocialTrustAsStateCapacity]], but the episode keeps its claim source-scoped rather than generalizing it into a universal rule.
- In the Shang Yang case, a small kept promise prepares people to believe later rewards and punishments will also be enforced.
- The Zhouji 13 part 2 cases show that trust can override short-term advantage in coercion, siege, appointment, and reform contexts.
- Confucian and Legalist trust differ in justification, but both require visible consistency between promise and action.
