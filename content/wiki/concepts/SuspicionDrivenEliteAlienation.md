## Definition

Suspicion-driven elite alienation is the failure mode where a ruler's reaction to possible disloyalty publicly exposes insecurity, punishes or over-retains credible insiders, and pushes elite opinion closer to outside alternatives.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-691-gan-duo-jiangshan-de-weixiao-weihe-zhuding-shu-gei-liuxiu-ljazchpqnxcgav6cmlbm7-d-gv8|Hanji 691]] supplies a pre-departure choice layer. Ma Yuan remains Wei Xiao's envoy, but his Chengdu and Luoyang observations teach him that capable insiders choose rulers by bearing, trust, and usable authority. That judgment pushes Wei Xiao toward Liu Xiu for now while also revealing why Wei Xiao's unresolved position could later lose such people.

The precursor to the later personnel problem is already visible before departures. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-1-lishishang-gan-yu-liuxiu-zhengxiong-de-shi-shui-lq1dq1srokorkzeib-oattni3sme|Hanji 689-1]] has Liu Xiu courting Wei Xiao through Lai Xi and high ritual courtesy, but the source still describes Wei Xiao as deeply suspicious. The evidence does not yet show advisers leaving, so it is treated as an early trust-risk layer: suspicion makes even favorable treatment unstable because insiders and counterparties cannot know whether the relationship will become safe enough to rely on.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-698-wangchao-xingshuai-qishilu-weiao-de-yihan-yu-jinchao-zhi-jing-lqim2hbjtdzb0wwmsaxcyzj7u8ff|Hanji 698]] supplies an earlier Eastern Han version through [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|Wei Xiao]]. Wei Xiao does not yet openly punish advisers, but his refusal to decide between [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] and rival claimants makes his camp unsafe enough that [[ZhengXingEasternHan|Zheng Xing]] leaves through filial framing, [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] takes his household east, and other followers ask to support themselves around Chang'an. The alienation is quieter than Liu Biao's later coercion, but it still turns suspicion and indecision into personnel loss.

Hanji 1036-1037 make the same concept a [[LiuBiao|刘表]] problem in two harsher stages. First, [[HanSongLateHan|韩嵩]] and [[LiuXianLateHan|刘先]] warn that neutrality between [[YuanShao|袁绍]] and [[CaoCao|曹操]] is becoming dangerous; Han Song then explains that a mission to [[Xudu|许都]] will create a court-duty conflict unless Liu Biao has already chosen submission. Liu Biao treats the warning as fear or refusal and forces the mission.

The second stage is public coercion. Han Song returns from Xudu praising the court and Cao Cao, which threatens Liu Biao's authority not only as a single envoy's opinion but as a signal to Jingzhou elites. Liu Biao's public execution preparation, torture of mission staff, and imprisonment after no betrayal is proven do not restore control. They make his suspicion visible and give elites more reason to doubt the regime's judgment before the Guandu-era sorting.

## Key Claims

- Elite alienation can begin when a ruler treats correction, exit, or role-duty warning as personal disobedience.
- Elite alienation can begin as trust risk before physical exit: capable insiders and counterparties compare ruler bearing, suspicion, and future safety before they leave.
- Punishing or coercing a respected insider can damage the ruler's legitimacy even when formal control remains intact.
- Public suspicion reveals weakness because it shows the ruler fears opinion and comparison with outside powers.
- Coercive interrogation may fail to prove betrayal while still proving that the regime is unsafe.
- The effect is cumulative: indecision, exit pressure, pre-mission coercion, post-return punishment, and later defection pressure reinforce each other.

- Elite consequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]] explicitly reads the episode as accelerating Jingzhou elite disaffection and foreshadowing later pro-Cao alignment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources are interpretive about psychological cause and downstream speed. Hanji 689-1 shows suspicion before departure rather than a completed alienation event, and Hanji 691 shows Ma Yuan's judgment before departure rather than a completed alienation event, so both are precursor layers. Hanji 698 supports a quieter talent-drain pattern around Wei Xiao, but it does not show public punishment or reconstruct every follower's motive. Hanji 1036-1037 give the stronger coercive case: Liu Biao forces Han Song to go despite a duty warning, later reacts harshly to Han Song, finds no proof, and imprisons him. The broader claim is retained as a source-backed synthesis rather than as a settled measurement of every elite's motive.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 689-1 as the first Wei Xiao trust-risk layer, where high courtesy and a personal channel still leave suspicion unresolved before later exits.
- Added Hanji 698 as a quieter Wei Xiao case where suspicion and non-decision produce adviser and household departures before open rebellion.
- Preserved Hanji 1036 as the Liu Biao pre-coercion layer, showing that suspicion begins before Han Song's return from Xudu.
- Preserved Hanji 1037 as the public punishment layer and kept the concept focused on ignored warning, forced exposure, and punitive reaction.
