# 猜疑驱动的士族离心 / Suspicion-Driven Elite Alienation

## Definition
Suspicion-driven elite alienation is the failure mode where a ruler's reaction to possible disloyalty publicly exposes insecurity, punishes credible insiders, and pushes elite opinion closer to outside alternatives.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1036-1037 make this a [[LiuBiao|刘表]] problem in two stages. First, [[HanSongLateHan|韩嵩]] and [[LiuXianLateHan|刘先]] warn that neutrality between [[YuanShao|袁绍]] and [[CaoCao|曹操]] is becoming dangerous; Han Song then explains that a mission to [[Xudu|许都]] will create a court-duty conflict unless Liu Biao has already chosen submission. Liu Biao treats the warning as fear or refusal and forces the mission.

The second stage is harsher. Han Song returns from Xudu praising the court and Cao Cao, which threatens Liu Biao's authority not only as a single envoy's opinion but as a signal to Jingzhou elites. Liu Biao's public execution preparation, torture of mission staff, and imprisonment after no betrayal is proven do not restore control. They make his suspicion visible and give elites more reason to doubt the regime's judgment before the Guandu-era sorting.

## Key Claims
- Elite alienation can begin when a ruler treats correction or role-duty warning as personal disobedience.
- Punishing or coercing a respected insider can damage the ruler's legitimacy even when formal control remains intact.
- Public suspicion reveals weakness because it shows the ruler fears opinion and comparison with outside powers.
- Coercive interrogation may fail to prove betrayal while still proving that the regime is unsafe.
- The effect is cumulative: pre-mission coercion, post-return punishment, earlier indecision, and later defection pressure reinforce each other.

## Evidence
- Warning mishandled as evasion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1036-liubiao-weihe-yao-bi-hansong-jian-caocao-lnifeizgoebis2gdovgmzg6dojaz|Hanji 1036]] shows Liu Biao forcing Han Song to go after Han Song explains why an undecided mission could alter his formal duty.
- Triggering advice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]] has Han Song praise Cao Cao and urge Liu Biao to send a son to court, making Liu Biao fear political and opinion loss.
- Coercive reaction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]] reports Liu Biao preparing to kill Han Song, torturing mission staff, and still imprisoning him after no betrayal is found.
- Elite consequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]] explicitly reads the episode as accelerating Jingzhou elite disaffection and foreshadowing later pro-Cao alignment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are interpretive about the psychological cause and downstream speed of elite alienation. The grounded event is narrower: Liu Biao forces Han Song to go despite a duty warning, later reacts harshly to Han Song, finds no proof, and imprisons him. The broader claim is retained as a source-backed synthesis rather than as a settled measurement of every Jingzhou elite's motive.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1036 as the pre-coercion layer, showing that Liu Biao's suspicion problem begins before Han Song's return from Xudu.
- Reframed the concept from a single punishment scene into a sequence of ignored warning, forced exposure, and punitive reaction.
