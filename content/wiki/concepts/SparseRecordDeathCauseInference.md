## Definition

史料稀缺下的死因推断 / Death-Cause Inference Under Sparse Records is the practice of separating what a source establishes about a death from the more specific disease, accident, murder, or conspiracy explanations that later readers may propose.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-336-1-zhanshen-huoqubing-weihe-24sui-turan-baobi-lhsmxfi72dzs8ucjd4n9vhfuhvmq|Hanji 336-1]] uses [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] to distinguish four evidentiary levels. His death in 117 BCE is treated as the secure event; a transmitted statement that he died from illness supplies a broad category; the specific illness remains unknown; and later epidemic, accident, murder, or imperial-involvement stories require evidence not present in the compact record.

Negative evidence can narrow a hypothesis without identifying the true cause. The episode challenges the epidemic story through an approximately two-year interval and the absence of a corresponding outbreak in the biographies it names. That weakens this particular mechanism, but neither archival silence nor chronological difficulty proves an alternative such as ordinary disease or assassination.

## Key Claims

- A secure death date and a broad illness report do not amount to a specific medical diagnosis.
- A hypothesis should be tested against chronology, expected corroborating events, and the records in which those events would plausibly appear.
- Absence from the surviving narrative can weaken a claim without proving the opposite claim.
- The prominence of the deceased makes a dramatic explanation psychologically attractive but does not increase its evidentiary support.
- Competing unsupported explanations should remain explicitly unresolved rather than be ranked by narrative appeal alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on one short podcast summary, not a complete comparison of *Shiji*, *Hanshu*, archaeological evidence, medical history, or later commentarial traditions. The episode's argument from silence depends on assumptions about record survival and authorial selection. Its two-year objection weakens the specific epidemic scenario described there but does not rule out every infectious disease, chronic illness, or delayed complication. The title/body age difference also remains unresolved.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to preserve the distinction between a reported death, a broad illness category, a rejected mechanism, and an unknown specific cause.
