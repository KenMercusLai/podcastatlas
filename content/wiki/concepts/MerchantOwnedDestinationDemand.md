# Merchant-Owned Destination Demand / 商户自有目的地流量

## Definition

Merchant-owned destination demand is demand created because customers deliberately want to visit and revisit a particular place, allowing the merchant to use platforms for discovery or navigation without depending on platform vouchers for the transaction.

## Current Synthesis

The concept is the merchant-side answer to [[InStoreDealCompetition]]. Standardized packages are easy to compare and subsidize; a venue that combines atmosphere, culture, social permission, varied dayparts, and recurring programming is harder to reduce to a few yuan of coupon difference. [[DongmaojieTeahouse|东茅街茶馆]] and [[Tianfutai|天福台]] illustrate two versions: a focused local teahouse ritual and a multi-level, day-to-night urban living room.

This does not mean complete platform independence. Information pages, maps, reviews, and social sharing can still support discovery. The stronger claim is narrower: when the venue itself is the product, checkout and repeat demand need not be purchased continuously through a discounted group-buying bundle.

## Key Claims

- Distinctive social and cultural use can make the place, rather than a standardized SKU, the reason for travel.
- Long-dwell and repeat-local use are stronger evidence of destination value than one-time photo traffic.
- Multi-period programming can raise utilization but also increases operating complexity.
- Platform discovery can coexist with merchant-controlled pricing, payment, and customer relationships.
- Destination demand can reduce coupon dependence, but it does not remove rent, service, quality, novelty, or execution risk.

## Evidence

- Focused social ritual - [[ep149-ep149]] presents Dongmaojie Teahouse's tea, snacks, conversation, and local atmosphere as a destination without group-buying vouchers.
- Multi-format operation - [[ep149-ep149]] presents Tianfutai's food, tea, alcohol, performance, vegetarian dining, and late-night use as a way to serve multiple occasions.
- Platform boundary - [[ep149-ep149]] says both venues retain information visibility on Meituan while keeping vouchers and platform checkout outside the model.
- Place-operation thesis - [[ep149-ep149]] argues that underused commercial property needs continuing activity and content, not only renovation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The two examples come from one favorable podcast account with promotional and investment proximity. No audited economics, repeat rates, customer acquisition costs, or failure cases are supplied. A unique atmosphere can be copied superficially, decay after novelty, or become too operationally complex to sustain. Avoiding coupons is evidence of a chosen channel model, not proof of profitability.

## What Changed

- Created a focused merchant-autonomy concept linking experiential place design to reduced coupon dependence.
- Distinguished platform-assisted discovery from platform-controlled transaction demand.
