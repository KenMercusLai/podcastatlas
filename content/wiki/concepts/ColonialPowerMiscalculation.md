# Colonial Power Miscalculation / 殖民强权误判

## Definition
Colonial power miscalculation is the strategic error of judging an expanding industrial-colonial power through an older regional hierarchy, tribute logic, or past military confidence, causing a state to underestimate the opponent's capacity and escalation threshold.

## Current Synthesis
The Myanmar episode uses early nineteenth-century Burma as its case. Burma had been a formidable regional military power, but the episode argues that this prior strength became misleading once expansion put it beside British India. Demands that British Bengal submit, plans to take Calcutta, and confidence before the First Anglo-Burmese War are presented as signs that the Burmese court interpreted Britain through a familiar dominance frame rather than through the material and imperial capacity Britain could actually mobilize.

The pattern is not simply "small state loses to large empire." Its analytical value is in the transition: a state that had real regional power can still make a disastrous judgment if its inherited maps of status, tribute, distance, and force no longer match the adversary's operating system.

## Key Claims
- Past regional dominance can become a cognitive trap when the strategic environment shifts.
- Territorial expansion can create new border contact with an opponent whose scale and methods are poorly understood.
- Diplomatic demands based on older hierarchy can escalate conflict when the other side operates from a different sovereignty and empire logic.
- A single military defeat can become a sovereignty shock when it produces indemnities, territorial concessions, and permanent diplomatic intrusion.
- The concept should remain source-scoped unless corroborated by fuller historical evidence, because the episode note compresses complex Anglo-Burmese history into a short interpretive arc.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short podcast episode summary, not a specialist military history. It does not fully document British logistics, Burmese court debates, Arakan's local politics, casualty figures, treaty text, or competing explanations for Myanmar's later decline. Use the concept as an interpretive pattern drawn from this episode, not as a complete causal account of Myanmar's modern history.
