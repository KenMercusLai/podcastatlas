# Local Civic Institution Capture

## Definition

Local civic institution capture is a coordinated strategy of entering an open association, recruiting a disciplined bloc, exploiting participation rules and member attrition, and converting procedural control into wider policy influence.

## Current Synthesis

The [[JohnBirchSociety]] PTA campaign shows why formal participation and democratic intent are not identical. Joining meetings, recruiting supporters, debating policy, and voting are ordinary civic actions; their capture function emerged when coordinated actors prolonged proceedings until opponents left, called votes after the balance changed, paired meeting strategy with intimidation, and sought downstream control of school boards, textbooks, and programs.

The case also shows a structural asymmetry. A broad volunteer association depends on limited time, ordinary trust, and willingness to accept procedure, while a highly motivated faction can concentrate attendance and tolerate longer conflict. Defensive pamphlets and meeting rules can reduce vulnerability, but procedure alone may be insufficient when coercion changes the cost of participation.

## Key Claims

- Open membership can create leverage for a coordinated minority with greater time, discipline, or tolerance for conflict.
- Meeting delay and exhaustion can change the effective electorate without changing formal voting rules.
- Capture targets are chosen for downstream influence; PTAs offered routes toward schools, boards, curricula, and public legitimacy.
- Formal compliance does not make a strategy democratically neutral when harassment or threats drive opponents out.
- Procedural defenses matter but must account for participation safety and unequal attrition costs.
- Capture claims require careful attribution because organized strategy does not prove central direction of every allied or ambient act.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Energetic recruitment, long debate, and winning a vote do not by themselves establish illegitimate capture. The concept applies when there is evidence of coordinated entry, strategic attrition, or coercive reduction of participation, and it should not be used merely to stigmatize an unpopular faction. This source provides selected dramatic cases rather than comparative data on how often the strategy succeeded or which safeguards worked.

## What Changed

- Created a local-institution model linking membership entry, meeting attrition, formal control, and downstream policy leverage.
- Added participation safety as a condition of meaningful procedural openness.
