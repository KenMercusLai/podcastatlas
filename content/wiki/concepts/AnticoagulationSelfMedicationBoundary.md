# Anticoagulation Self-Medication Boundary / 抗凝自行用药边界

## Definition
Anticoagulation self-medication boundary is the safety distinction between clinician-directed use or peri-procedural adjustment of antiplatelet, anticoagulant and thrombolytic therapies and a patient's casual starting, stopping or repurposing of those medicines.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.204 separates antiplatelet drugs such as aspirin, anticoagulants that limit clot extension and new clot formation, and thrombolysis or fibrinolysis that targets clot breakdown. Its public boundary is conservative: prior thrombosis or another high-risk context may change an individual's plan, but ordinary listeners should not imitate clinician-directed medication as routine travel prevention.

VOL.75 adds the reverse error before dental extraction: independently stopping a prescribed drug because bleeding is feared. Dental procedural risk, local bleeding control, the exact medicine, its indication and the person's systemic thrombotic risk must be assessed together, sometimes with the prescribing specialist. “Do not stop independently” is not a universal instruction to continue every regimen unchanged; it is a coordination rule.

The reason is asymmetric risk. Clotting and bleeding both matter, so choosing drug type, timing, dose, indication, interruption and restart belongs inside qualified care rather than a public shortcut.

## Key Claims
- Aspirin is an antiplatelet drug and should not be treated as equivalent to anticoagulation for venous-thrombosis prevention.
- Anticoagulation mainly limits clot propagation and new thrombosis rather than directly dissolving an existing clot.
- Thrombolytic treatment requires professional balancing against bleeding and coagulation risks.
- Prior thrombosis or another high-risk context may change a plan but does not generalize into routine self-medication.
- Patients should not independently stop antiplatelet or anticoagulant drugs before dental extraction or surgery.
- Peri-procedural decisions depend on the medicine, indication, procedure, systemic risk and available bleeding control.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide individualized doses, contraindications, laboratory thresholds, bridging instructions, interruption schedules or restart rules. Antiplatelet and anticoagulant choices differ by drug, indication, kidney and liver function, combined therapy, recent thrombosis, valve or stent history, procedure and current local guidance. The concept records a self-management boundary, not a treatment protocol.

## What Changed
- Expanded the boundary from casual initiation for travel to self-directed stopping before a procedure.
- Added dental-prescriber coordination while preserving drug, indication and procedure specificity.
