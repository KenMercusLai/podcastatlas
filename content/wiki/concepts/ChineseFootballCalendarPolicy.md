## Definition
中国足球赛历政策 / Chinese football calendar policy is the design problem of arranging Chinese professional football seasons, including whether to adopt a cross-year calendar and how to avoid dense fixtures or disruptive long breaks under the current calendar.

## Current Synthesis
The sources converge on caution: cross-year football should be researched but is not ready for simple adoption. The near-term priority is to improve the existing non-cross-year season through more meaningful matches, fewer fixture crunches, fewer long interruptions, and more regular exposure for fans and media. The Asian-competition source adds that calendar quality must support continental clubs without turning every national-team or multi-sport event into a prolonged domestic shutdown.

## Key Claims
- Cross-year calendar research is legitimate because future adoption should be based on known constraints rather than reflexive rejection.
- Immediate adoption is unattractive because northeast weather, Xinjiang travel and integration, winter training conditions, and fairness of long away stretches remain hard.
- Winter or summer breaks can reduce some climate pressure, but they also shift rather than eliminate scheduling tradeoffs.
- Calendar policy must coordinate with basketball, concerts, venue use, public security, and national-team or multi-sport-event demands.
- The current non-cross-year calendar already needs repair because dense months and long pauses weaken league rhythm.
- Domestic and continental calendars need coordination so clubs can compete in Asia without making ordinary league continuity expendable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither episode rules out a future cross-year calendar. They treat many difficulties as design problems that might be mitigated by breaks, home-away sequencing, facilities, and better coordination, but those mitigations can create fairness and cost problems of their own. Vol.227's continental comparison is observational and does not establish that calendar format alone causes Asian results.

## What Changed
- Added the club-versus-country boundary and the need to coordinate domestic continuity with Asian competition.
