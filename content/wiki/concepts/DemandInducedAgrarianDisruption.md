# Demand-Induced Agrarian Disruption / 需求诱导式弃农

Demand-induced agrarian disruption / 需求诱导式弃农 is the mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-3-gudai-de-jingji-zhanzheng-neng-you-duo-kepa-liybgoz8lspypax6dhuke2js0pxf]] extracts from Guan Zhong's economic-war stories. A state creates unusually profitable demand for a rival's non-grain activity, waits for labor and policy attention to move away from farming, then withdraws demand or blocks trade after the rival's food base has weakened.

The cloth case uses [[LuState|鲁国]] and [[LiangState|梁国]]: [[QiState|齐国]] elevates Lu gauze while forbidding its own Qi silk, encouraging neighboring rulers and people to chase cloth income. The deer case uses [[ChuState|楚国]]: high Qi prices for live deer make catching deer more attractive than timely field work. The episode treats both as the same structure, even though the goods differ.

## Key Claims
- The attack works through opportunity cost: people pursue the high-paying activity and neglect grain production.
- It depends on timing; the buyer waits until old grain is consumed, fields are missed, or recovery to farming is too late.
- It can look like enrichment before it becomes vulnerability.
- The mechanism targets livelihood structure rather than only the ruler's treasury.
- It is distinct from ordinary trade because the buyer intends to reverse or weaponize the demand.
