# Short-King Dating Data

Short-king dating data is [[under-strain-breaking-the-british-state-6a799563523b3dfcd83c7271]]'s test of whether the online "short king" meme has changed observed dating and marriage outcomes for shorter men. [[DougDawson]] treats the meme as a cultural claim that can be checked against dating-app messages, marriage probabilities, survey preferences, and height gaps among heterosexual couples.

The source's conclusion is that the meme has not meaningfully moved real-world matching patterns. Height still matters in dating and marriage: very tall men receive more attention on dating apps, shorter men are less likely to marry by their mid-thirties than taller peers, and couples where the woman is taller than the man remain much less common than random pairing would predict.

## Key Claims
- Online language can celebrate shorter men without changing the underlying preference structure very much.
- The relevant norm is not simply maximum male height; it is often a preferred gap where the man is a few inches taller than the woman.
- Surveyed preferences and observed couples point in the same direction: height matters more in dating than many people say it should.
- The source uses the segment as a compact example of cultural-meme claims meeting demographic evidence.
