## Definition

High merit threat management / 功高不震主 is the two-sided practice of preventing real contribution from becoming a perceived challenge to the superior who controls recognition, command, and punishment, while still preserving useful capacity when discipline can restore a workable hierarchy.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1065 defines the contributor-facing side by extending [[XuYouLateHan|许攸]]'s fatal public credit claim into a general power-field rule. Merit remains protective only while it serves current organizational need without making reputation, entitlement, troops, secrets, private networks, or public familiarity look like rival authority. Its examples propose returning credit upward, reducing visibility, sharing credit, surrendering command, or withdrawing after success.

Hanji 356-1 adds the superior-facing side through [[YangPuWesternHan|杨仆]]. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] does not simply reward the victorious commander or discard him. The episode says he converts alleged credit inflation, incomplete pursuit, boasting, delayed return, and failed reporting into a formal rebuke, elicits an offer of renewed service, and then sends Yang Pu against [[YuShanDongyueKing|余善]]. In this model, the ruler's response manages both threat and capability: achievement does not erase discipline, but discipline need not waste scarce ability.

Together the sources make hierarchy repair more important than simple modesty. The contributor can lower perceived threat through credit, posture, timing, reporting, and command surrender; the superior can clarify faults, demand a credible obedience signal, and condition continued authority on renewed performance. Neither source proves that every accusation is fair or that ruler suspicion is inherently legitimate.

## Key Claims

- High merit becomes dangerous when current usefulness falls, or when visible entitlement and independent authority rise faster than the superior will tolerate.
- Public credit claims, familiar address, boasting, delayed response, and weak reporting matter because they can turn achievement into visible hierarchy damage.
- Returning credit upward, sharing credit outward, reducing visibility, surrendering command, or withdrawing can lower contributor-side threat.
- A superior can preserve capacity by separating accountability for conduct from the decision to continue using demonstrated skill.
- Conditional redeployment can function as hierarchy repair when reprimand is followed by a credible obedience signal and bounded new responsibility.
- Crisis and consolidation apply different personnel standards: exceptional ability is often tolerated while urgently needed, whereas loyalty, etiquette, and control grow more salient as alternatives expand.
- The sources' workplace formulas and motive readings are interpretive heuristics, not measurable universal laws or proof that every superior's fear is justified.

- Contributor-side mitigation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1065-zhichang-shengcun-de-dingji-zhexue-xue-xunyu-jiaxu-or-guoziyi-lpoopyy7kaefeewej1wlf5ge2t17|Hanji 1065]] uses Xun Yu, Jia Xu, Fan Li, Zhang Liang, Wei Qing, and Guo Ziyi to illustrate credit return, lower visibility, exit, humility, and command surrender.
- Achievement without immunity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-356-1-budong-yuren-ni-ping-sha-dang-lingdao-lsaa6j1jskivfhyvvpbpmnorq-jm|Hanji 356-1]] says Yang Pu's Nanyue merit does not prevent Han Wudi from listing five alleged faults involving credit, pursuit, boasting, recall, and reporting.
- Hierarchy repair through continued use: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-356-1-budong-yuren-ni-ping-sha-dang-lingdao-lsaa6j1jskivfhyvvpbpmnorq-jm|Hanji 356-1]] says Yang Pu offers renewed military service and is assigned to the Dongyue campaign rather than immediately discarded.
- Phase and ratio heuristic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1065-zhichang-shengcun-de-dingji-zhexue-xue-xunyu-jiaxu-or-guoziyi-lpoopyy7kaefeewej1wlf5ge2t17|Hanji 1065]] summarizes safety as rising with indispensability and falling with perceived threat, then contrasts crisis ability with settled-order loyalty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both sources are interpretive podcast episodes, not a controlled comparison of rulers and high performers. Hanji 1065 compresses many biographies into advisory examples, while Hanji 356-1 does not independently establish the proportionality of all five accusations or Han Wudi's private motive. Caution, dissent, credit-seeking, and delayed compliance are not automatically disloyalty, and insecure superiors can manufacture threat even when contributors behave carefully. The concept therefore describes a power calculation and possible repair mechanism, not a moral presumption that hierarchy is always right.

## What Changed

- Expanded the concept from contributor self-protection to the superior's paired problem of enforcing accountability without wasting scarce ability.
- Added conditional redeployment as a hierarchy-repair mechanism, while retaining the possibility of unfair accusation or excessive ruler suspicion.
