# Election Interference Preparedness

## Definition
Election interference preparedness is the advance rehearsal of legal, operational, communication, and security responses to intimidation, disruption, unauthorized access, material seizure, or pressure during voting and counting.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats preparedness as institutional muscle memory. Election officials cannot predict whether a fire, threatening visitor, federal monitor, warrant, immigration action, or attempt to seize materials will occur, but they can decide beforehand who has authority, what documentation is required, when counsel or courts should be engaged, how voters are protected, and how evidence remains controlled. This does not eliminate political distrust: presidents lack administrative authority over elections yet can still exploit delay and ambiguity through public influence.

## Key Claims
- Tabletop exercises expose unclear authority and missing procedures before a real incident compresses decision time.
- Preparedness must combine physical security, election law, documentation, communications, voter access, and evidence custody.
- A federal title or warrant should trigger verification and legal review rather than automatic compliance or blanket refusal.
- Plans for unlikely scenarios can be valuable when their consequences would be severe and improvisation would endanger voters or records.
- Accurate administration alone does not prevent legitimacy attacks during close or prolonged counts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The scenarios are preparedness exercises, not proof that the described federal interventions will occur. The supplied summary does not provide the complete exercise materials, governing statutes, warrant protocols, monitor credentials, or county plans, so it cannot resolve each legal question. Excessively dramatic rehearsal could itself amplify fear if scenario likelihood and authority boundaries are communicated poorly.

## What Changed
- Established advance simulation as a bridge between election law and operational resilience.
- Distinguished verification of asserted authority from either reflexive compliance or reflexive obstruction.
- Added voter protection and public communication to physical and evidentiary preparedness.
