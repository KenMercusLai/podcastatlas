# Relational Self-Worth Feedback / 关系反馈与自我价值

## Definition
Relational self-worth feedback is the process by which specific, credible responses from other people help someone notice strengths, correct hostile assumptions, and build a more grounded sense of value without outsourcing all judgment to approval.

## Current Synthesis
[[shi-di-fu-shuo-478-zixunshi-huixin-zenme-tuifan-fumu-shi-ai-wode-zhishi-buhui-biaoda-ai-zhezhong-guihua-lktmqgqmon5sjhxl8dz4xkhmn-dn]] distinguishes introversion from pre-emptive self-rejection. Silence, fear of mistakes, repeated checking, and reluctance to contact colleagues may protect a fragile sense of value: if the person says or does less, there is less material that could confirm feared inadequacy.

The interaction can then become circular. Colleagues may reduce contact because they are uncertain how to engage, while the quiet person reads that distance as dislike. Carefully chosen feedback from one or two mature, friendly people can replace mind-reading with information. The aim is not instant confidence or dependence on universal approval, but a gradual inventory of real capacities, limits, and workable social signals.

## Key Claims
- Self-worth is partly relational because people often first recognize strengths through other people's concrete responses.
- Abilities that feel easy may be hard to identify as strengths without comparison, use, and feedback.
- Silence and overchecking can protect against shame while also reducing the information needed to revise negative beliefs.
- Mutual uncertainty can produce distance even when neither side dislikes the other.
- Feedback is most useful when it is specific, credible, limited to trustworthy people, and combined with direct experience.

## Evidence
- Protective silence - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-478-zixunshi-huixin-zenme-tuifan-fumu-shi-ai-wode-zhishi-buhui-biaoda-ai-zhezhong-guihua-lktmqgqmon5sjhxl8dz4xkhmn-dn]] interprets a new worker's silence, fear of mistakes, repeated confirmation, and social comparison as possible protection of a depleted sense of value.
- Strength discovery - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-478-zixunshi-huixin-zenme-tuifan-fumu-shi-ai-wode-zhishi-buhui-biaoda-ai-zhezhong-guihua-lktmqgqmon5sjhxl8dz4xkhmn-dn]] says people may overlook abilities that come easily and may need relationship and comparison to recognize them.
- Interaction correction - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-478-zixunshi-huixin-zenme-tuifan-fumu-shi-ai-wode-zhishi-buhui-biaoda-ai-zhezhong-guihua-lktmqgqmon5sjhxl8dz4xkhmn-dn]] proposes asking one or two friendly, mature colleagues for direct feedback rather than treating reduced interaction as proof of rejection.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Feedback can be biased, coercive, vague, or shaped by workplace hierarchy, prejudice, and popularity norms. The concept does not make other people the final judges of a person's worth. Persistent anxiety, impaired functioning, bullying, depression, or unsafe work may require support beyond informal colleague feedback.
