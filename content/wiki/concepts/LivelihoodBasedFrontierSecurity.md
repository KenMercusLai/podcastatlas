## Definition

生计型边疆安全 / livelihood-based frontier security is the attempt to stabilize a contested border region by making ordinary survival and production more viable, so that defense rests partly on economic order, local stake, and reduced incentive for revolt rather than coercion alone.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1165-zhangji-zhuan-didiao-caishi-dingji-shengcun-zhihui-lr7x5qltfi_ikktqabowt1garphl|Hanji 1165]] attributes this pattern to [[ZhangJiCaoOfficialLateHan|张既]] in Liangzhou. Alongside suppressing rebellion and defending the western approach, he is said to develop salt and iron, encourage farming and sericulture, and improve the material conditions available to local communities. The host compresses the claim as “prosperity is the strongest border defense.”

The concept does not make prosperity a substitute for institutions, consent, military capacity, or fair distribution. It identifies a security mechanism: where households can produce, exchange, and expect continuity, the state may face less resistance and gain more local cooperation. The same episode's migration policy warns that livelihood governance can coexist with displacement and strategic manipulation of population.

## Key Claims

- Frontier security can depend on production, subsistence, and predictable local order as well as troops and fortifications.
- Salt-and-iron activity, agriculture, sericulture, and viable household livelihoods can increase local stake in stability.
- Economic development reduces conflict only when benefits are sufficiently accessible and not overwhelmed by extraction, coercion, or unequal control.
- Livelihood-based stabilization can coexist with harsher tools such as suppression, strategic non-intervention, and population relocation.

## Evidence

Production and local stability:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1165-zhangji-zhuan-didiao-caishi-dingji-shengcun-zhihui-lr7x5qltfi_ikktqabowt1garphl|Hanji 1165]] links Zhang Ji's Liangzhou rule to salt and iron, farming, sericulture, and improved conditions for frontier communities.

Mixed security toolkit:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1165-zhangji-zhuan-didiao-caishi-dingji-shengcun-zhihui-lr7x5qltfi_ikktqabowt1garphl|Hanji 1165]] places livelihood measures beside rebellion suppression, defense, inter-faction waiting, and relocation rather than presenting a purely economic policy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on one episode summary and does not measure output, distribution, tax burden, local consent, rebellion frequency, or causal effect. Economic activity can finance coercion or benefit elites without securing ordinary households. The phrase “prosperity is the strongest border defense” is a presenter maxim, not evidence that prosperity alone prevents war, revolt, external invasion, or later instability.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded concept connecting livelihood capacity to frontier stability.
- Made explicit that development, coercion, and displacement can coexist in the same governing program.
