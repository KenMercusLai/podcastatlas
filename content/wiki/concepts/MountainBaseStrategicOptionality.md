# 山地根据地战略余地 / Mountain Base Strategic Optionality

## Definition
山地根据地战略余地 / mountain base strategic optionality is the pattern in which an armed actor preserves bargaining room by holding difficult terrain, sustaining a population or troop base there, and remaining costly to eliminate but useful enough to incorporate.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is grounded in [[ZhangYanLateHan|张燕]] and the [[HeishanArmy|黑山军]]. Hanji 933 shows the first layer: the Black Mountain force grows large enough in the northern Yellow River mountain zone that [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s court grants Zhang Yan office instead of destroying him. Hanji 970 shows the coercive value of that terrain-backed force when Yuan Shao has to campaign against Black Mountain actors and Zhang Yan with help from [[LyuBu|吕布]].

Hanji 1062 turns the pattern into a survival-and-alignment synthesis. Zhang Yan's advantage is not simple invincibility; it is that the Taihang mountain base, farming-fighting mix, and partially legalized status keep him from becoming either an easy target or an exhausted fugitive. Once Yuan Shao's heirs are distracted and Cao Cao's advantage becomes clearer, Zhang Yan can offer cooperation and convert remaining military value into renewed office and security.

## Key Claims
- Difficult terrain can make suppression expensive enough that office or negotiation becomes attractive to stronger powers.
- A mountain base matters more when it supports food, followers, and retreat, not only when it offers hiding places.
- Strategic optionality depends on remaining useful; an armed group that can no longer affect the wider field loses bargaining power.
- Earlier semi-legal recognition can make later incorporation easier because the actor already has a vocabulary of office, reporting, and delegated order.
- Alignment timing matters: Zhang Yan's Cao-side move becomes rational only after Yuan-family decline makes his old environment less protective.
- The pattern sits between insurgent autonomy and surrender; the actor trades durable nuisance value for security under a stronger hierarchy.

## Evidence
- Initial mountain incorporation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-933-weiguan-zhidao-yousuo-wei-yousuo-buwei-lopulnufsagoqyx7nngvcqi2nhrj|Hanji 933]] says Zhang Yan gathers Black Mountain groups into a large mountain force and receives court office because direct suppression is unavailable.
- Coercive terrain-backed presence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-970-yuanshao-ansha-lvbu-weihe-hui-shibai-ln2pnvit-qgpnhn-pne7o-p1irrs|Hanji 970]] makes Zhang Yan a major opponent whom Yuan Shao fights in the Changshan field with Lü Bu's temporary help.
- Farming-fighting durability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1062-shanzei-de-nixi-daozei-chushen-de-ta-shi-ruhe-zhandui-de-lvkhmezd68key-p-kup55zypbjet|Hanji 1062]] says the Black Mountain force survives through mountain protection and a farming-fighting mix.
- Timed alignment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1062-shanzei-de-nixi-daozei-chushen-de-ta-shi-ruhe-zhandui-de-lvkhmezd68key-p-kup55zypbjet|Hanji 1062]] has Zhang Yan approach Cao Cao after Yuan Shao's heirs are weakened by internal conflict.
- Value-for-security exchange: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1062-shanzei-de-nixi-daozei-chushen-de-ta-shi-ruhe-zhandui-de-lvkhmezd68key-p-kup55zypbjet|Hanji 1062]] interprets Zhang Yan's later rank and marquis treatment as a conversion of remaining military value into legitimate protection.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept should not romanticize mountain survival. Hanji 970 shows that Zhang Yan can still be attacked hard enough to withdraw, and Hanji 1062 says Cao Cao eventually demonstrates military superiority before settlement. The current evidence is concentrated in the Black Mountain branch, so the concept should not be generalized to every mountain force without further sources. Exact troop counts, farming arrangements, and Zhang Yan's final title sequence remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the Zhang Yan and Black Mountain evidence cluster.
- Connected earlier court incorporation, Yuan Shao's campaign, and Zhang Yan's final Cao-side alignment into one terrain-backed optionality pattern.
