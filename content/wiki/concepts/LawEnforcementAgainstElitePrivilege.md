## Definition

执法碰撞权贵特权 / law enforcement against elite privilege is the problem of making formal limits bind high-status people, their relatives, and entourages. It includes both preventive rules against private elite coercion and the later risk that an enforcer will be punished when status recodes enforcement as disrespect.

## Current Synthesis

The preventive branch appears under [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] reports an order barring specified relatives of marquises and kings, and senior officials, from privately arresting commoners or imposing levies. The order acknowledges a status-linked coercion problem, but its enforcement is unknown and the episode questions whether its reported wording leaves the marquises themselves outside the named restriction.

The enforcement branch is [[BaoXuanWesternHan|鲍宣]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-599-gan-dui-qianguize-shuo-bu-chengxiang-wangjia-you-duo-yong-lpptx-fzls9xbakw52b8n1n6xow0|Hanji 599]]. When [[KongGuangWesternHan|孔光]]'s entourage uses the emperor's central roadway, Bao Xuan treats the act as punishable and confiscates carriage and horses. The later charge treats Bao's enforcement as humiliating the chancellor and violating ministerial etiquette.

The concept matters because it shows how formal law can remain present while status changes the consequences of applying it. The legal rule is not denied; instead, the enforcer is punished for the social meaning of enforcing it against someone too powerful.

## Key Claims

- Preventive rules can name elite relatives and officials while still leaving coverage or implementation uncertain.
- Enforcement against elite privilege can be punished without openly denying the underlying rule.
- Retaliation may be routed through etiquette language such as humiliation or disrespect.
- Entourages and retainers can carry the privilege of the principal official into everyday enforcement scenes.
- The pattern exposes a gap between formal legality and status-protected political reality.

## Evidence

- Preventive restriction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] reports that specified elite relatives and senior officials may not privately arrest commoners or impose levies.
- Coverage and implementation boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] does not establish enforcement and flags the reported omission of marquises themselves as a possible loophole rather than a proven legal entitlement.
- Rule enforcement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-599-gan-dui-qianguize-shuo-bu-chengxiang-wangjia-you-duo-yong-lpptx-fzls9xbakw52b8n1n6xow0|Hanji 599]] says Bao Xuan arrests Kong Guang's attendants and confiscates carriage and horses after they use the imperial central roadway.
- Retaliation framing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-599-gan-dui-qianguize-shuo-bu-chengxiang-wangjia-you-duo-yong-lpptx-fzls9xbakw52b8n1n6xow0|Hanji 599]] says Bao Xuan is then accused of humiliating the chancellor, rejecting messengers, and violating ministerial etiquette.
- Punishment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-599-gan-dui-qianguize-shuo-bu-chengxiang-wangjia-you-duo-yong-lpptx-fzls9xbakw52b8n1n6xow0|Hanji 599]] says Bao Xuan's death penalty is reduced but he still receives penal punishment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The two cases are separated by centuries and do not prove a single stable enforcement regime. Hanji 220 does not supply the complete decree, prevalence data, enforcement outcomes, or proof that unnamed marquises retained unlimited coercive authority. Hanji 599 does not prove Kong Guang's personal role in the punishment, the exact legal text governing central-roadway use, or whether Bao Xuan's reported gate-closing conduct independently violated procedure.

## What Changed

- Added the earlier preventive-regulation branch under Liu Heng.
- Distinguished a possible textual coverage gap from demonstrated non-enforcement or legal immunity.
