## Definition

执法碰撞权贵特权 / law enforcement against elite privilege is the pattern where a formal rule is enforced against a high-status person's entourage or interest, but the enforcement is reframed as disrespect, humiliation, or etiquette violation toward the protected elite.

## Current Synthesis

The current source case is [[BaoXuanWesternHan|鲍宣]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-599-gan-dui-qianguize-shuo-bu-chengxiang-wangjia-you-duo-yong-lpptx-fzls9xbakw52b8n1n6xow0|Hanji 599]]. When [[KongGuangWesternHan|孔光]]'s entourage uses the emperor's central roadway, Bao Xuan treats the act as punishable and confiscates carriage and horses. The later charge treats Bao's enforcement as humiliating the chancellor and violating ministerial etiquette.

The concept matters because it shows how formal law can remain present while status changes the consequences of applying it. The legal rule is not denied; instead, the enforcer is punished for the social meaning of enforcing it against someone too powerful.

## Key Claims

- Enforcement against elite privilege can be punished without openly denying the underlying rule.
- Retaliation may be routed through etiquette language such as humiliation or disrespect.
- Entourages and retainers can carry the privilege of the principal official into everyday enforcement scenes.
- The pattern exposes a gap between formal legality and status-protected political reality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one episode note. The wiki does not yet prove Kong Guang's personal role in the punishment, the exact legal text governing central-roadway use, or whether Bao Xuan's reported gate-closing conduct independently violated procedure.
