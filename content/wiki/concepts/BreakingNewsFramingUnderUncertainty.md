## Definition
Breaking-news framing under uncertainty is the editorial problem of describing a developing event accurately enough to communicate its apparent severity while keeping labels proportional to what has been independently verified.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's Flydubai discussion exposes a real tradeoff. Early terms such as “altercation” may avoid outrunning confirmed facts, yet can become materially misleading if available evidence already indicates an attempted mass-casualty attack. Stronger labels such as “terrorist attack” can clarify apparent intent but require evidence about action, motive, and attribution.

Good developing-story practice is therefore iterative: state what is observed, attribute unverified accounts, mark uncertainty, update language as evidence improves, and explain meaningful wording changes. Similar phrasing across outlets may reflect shared verification constraints or source dependence; it does not alone establish centralized narrative coordination.

## Key Claims
- Initial caution is legitimate when motive, sequence, and attribution are not independently confirmed.
- Neutral language can understate danger when it omits well-supported actions or apparent intent.
- Descriptions should become more specific as verified evidence accumulates.
- Apparent terrorism should be framed as an investigated hypothesis until intent and relevant facts are established.
- Similar wording across outlets is insufficient evidence of coordinated narrative control.
