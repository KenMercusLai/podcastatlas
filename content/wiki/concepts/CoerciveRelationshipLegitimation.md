# Coercive Relationship Legitimation / 强迫关系合法化

## Definition

Coercive relationship legitimation is the use of romance, marriage, destiny, family, parenthood, or promised benefit to make an imposed intimate relationship appear valid while displacing a person's present refusal.

## Current Synthesis

In the Baoxiang Kingdom reading of [[TaibaiJinxingYouDianFan|《太白金星有点烦》]], past-life romance and marital language do not answer the decisive question: whether Baihuaxiu agrees now. Mao Ri Xingguan's “family matter,” heavenly benefit, and mother-child arguments reframe captivity as care, while Baihuaxiu's own testimony reveals sustained harassment and coercion across both lives. The concept therefore treats consent as continuing and person-specific rather than inherited from an earlier identity or inferred from status.

The [[HeavenSwordDragonSaber|《倚天屠龙记》]] discussion adds a harder evidentiary case. Both [[JiXiaofuJinYong|纪晓芙]] and [[YangXiaoJinYong|杨逍]] refer to “forcing,” yet the summarized source does not establish the act's sequence or content. Ji's later love, their child, her refusal to kill Yang, and his grief establish attachment but cannot retroactively answer the earlier consent question. The correct boundary is neither automatic romanticization nor unsupported reclassification: name the coercion language, preserve the missing evidence, and refuse to let later feeling substitute for consent at the earlier time.

## Key Claims

- Present, voluntary agreement cannot be supplied by a past-life story, legal label, family role, or third-party interpretation.
- “For your own good” can legitimate control when the claimed beneficiary is denied the authority to define their own good.
- Parenthood does not erase a victim's right to leave a coercive relationship or require continued contact with children born within it.
- Institutional recognition of a relationship can normalize coercion without changing the underlying absence of consent.
- Victim testimony can reverse a perpetrator-centered romance narrative by restoring the sequence of refusal, harassment, confinement, and harm.
- Later affection, loyalty, parenthood, or grief cannot retroactively prove consent to an earlier disputed act.
- When the underlying scene is absent or ambiguous, analysis should preserve the coercion language without claiming a more specific event than the evidence supports.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The evidence comes from literary podcast discussions, not a general empirical study of coercive relationships. The concept does not imply that marriage, parenthood, reconciliation, or benefit claims are always coercive; the problem arises when they override refusal or are used to erase an unresolved earlier violation. In the Yang–Ji case, the available summary cannot determine whether “forcing” denotes sexual violence, broader domination, or another imposed act. The secret relocation of Baihuaxiu's children is a fictional compromise and does not establish a general real-world rule for custody or trauma response.

## What Changed

- Added the temporal rule that later attachment cannot retroactively establish earlier consent.
- Added an evidence boundary against making the ambiguous act more specific than the supplied source supports.
