## Definition

Decisive terrain preemption / 争地抢先式决胜 is a battlefield pattern where a commander identifies the one site whose first occupation will change the whole local contest, moves there before the enemy, and turns a defensive position into a surprise attack. In the current wiki evidence, the pattern is grounded by [[FengYiEasternHan|冯异]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-702-fengyi-de-yichang-ju-ruhe-zhansha-weixiao-liangwanren-lqckx-aclchkzqqe3pttnklu9vot|Hanji 702]].

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 702 shows the pattern under pressure rather than from a secure plan. After the Han side retreats from Longshan, [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|隗嚣]] sends Wang Yuan and Xing Xun with more than 20,000 troops down the mountain to take a target city. Feng Yi's officers are afraid because of the previous defeat and advise stopping in favorable ground, but Feng Yi judges that if the enemy takes the city first, Sanfu will be shaken.

The central move is to redefine the decision. Feng Yi does not argue for heroic attack; he says the Han side may be weaker on offense but still strong enough on defense, which means the real danger is arriving second. He hurries forward, secretly occupies the city, closes the gates, and hides his force before Xing Xun arrives.

The result depends on information as much as terrain. Xing Xun's force behaves as if the city is still open; Feng Yi waits until the enemy is exposed, then reveals drums and flags and sorties suddenly. The source treats the routed enemy, Ji Zun's parallel victory, and Beidi-Shangjun-Anding realignment as the political effect of a local decision point being seized first.

The concept is therefore narrower than speed in general. It requires a place where first occupation changes the cost of attack and defense, a commander willing to move through subordinate fear, enough defensive capacity to hold the site, and an information gap that lets the first mover choose the moment of exposure.

## Key Claims

- A "争地" becomes decisive when the first occupier gains defensive leverage that the second mover must attack at a disadvantage.
- The useful decision is often not "fight or avoid fight" but "arrive before the opponent changes the terrain problem."
- Defensive capacity can be converted into initiative if the commander reaches the site first and controls when the enemy learns it.
- Information control, such as hidden entry and concealed force, is part of the terrain advantage rather than a separate trick.
- The political payoff can exceed the tactical kill count when local actors treat the result as evidence that one side now controls the regional trend.
- The failure mode is fear-driven delay: camping safely can lose the very position that would have made later defense possible.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in one source note, so it should remain a narrow Feng Yi case rather than a universal doctrine. The source summary does not clearly name the target city, and the exact local geography remains source-scoped. The pattern also does not mean every key city should be rushed: it depends on whether the first mover can hold the position, whether the enemy will arrive exposed, and whether moving faster creates an advantage greater than the risk of isolation. It is adjacent to operational tempo and fortified patience, but its distinct feature is first occupation of a single local decision point.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 702 to capture Feng Yi's city-first ambush logic.
- Framed the pattern as terrain plus information control rather than generic speed or courage.
- Kept the concept source-bounded until additional cases support broader generalization.
