## Definition

廷尉传召即赴死 / court summons as death sentence is the court-politics pattern where formal transfer to judicial custody carries an unofficial expectation that a senior minister will kill himself before trial, preserving hierarchy and face while turning procedure into lethal pressure.

## Current Synthesis

The current source case is [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]] across [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-598-gudai-chaotang-de-douzheng-you-duo-canku-luof-1bxomtcsrkcvmuplpugaw4d|Hanji 598]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-599-gan-dui-qianguize-shuo-bu-chengxiang-wangjia-you-duo-yong-lpptx-fzls9xbakw52b8n1n6xow0|Hanji 599]]. After [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] orders him summoned to the Tingwei prison, Wang Jia's household officials cry and prepare poison. His clerk tells him that generals and chancellors do not plead before judicial officers and that a marquis should self-kill. The summons therefore functions not only as legal transfer but as an etiquette-coded death command.

The sources also record resistance to the norm. Wang Jia breaks the poison cup and says that as a Three Excellencies minister entrusted with state service, if he must die he should be executed publicly in the marketplace rather than privately by poison. Hanji 599 then shows the cost of exposing the convention: Aidi is furious that Wang Jia arrives alive, the interrogation presumes guilt, and Wang Jia dies in prison after continuing to defend legal review and attack Dong Xian's disruptive influence.

## Key Claims

- A formal judicial summons can carry an informal death expectation when rank and court convention make public trial humiliating.
- The norm converts procedure into coercion without requiring an explicit imperial order to commit suicide.
- Household officials and clerks may enforce the norm by presenting suicide as the proper elite response.
- Refusal exposes the coercion by insisting that punishment, if real, should occur openly under law.
- Living through the summons can make the unofficial norm visible, but it does not make the formal process fair.
- The pattern overlaps with remonstrance risk because officials targeted for speech or personnel advice can be pushed toward self-erasure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page is currently grounded in one continuous Wang Jia case across two episode notes. It should not be generalized to all Han judicial summonses. The exact legal status of the norm, how often it was followed, and whether every Tingwei summons carried the same expectation remain source-scoped.
