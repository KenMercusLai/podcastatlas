# Remigration Politics

## Definition

Remigration politics is far-right policy language that recasts immigration restriction as a project of removing non-citizens or people with migration backgrounds, with the affected population depending on how legal obligation, protection status, criminality, and national belonging are defined.

## Current Synthesis

The source introduces remigration through [[AlternativeForGermany|AfD]]'s Saxony-Anhalt campaign and program. It appears alongside demands to end asylum rights, anti-immigration politics, pro-Russian foreign policy, and attacks on elite or "cartel" parties.

In these episodes, remigration is not treated as a normal border-management term. It marks a harder nationalist position that affects coalition acceptability, public fear, civil-society preparation, and the question of what a state government could actually implement under German federal constraints. The election-night source adds that this language now sits inside a party with a reported 44% state result, raising the stakes of the [[BrandmauerCordonSanitaire|Brandmauer]] debate.

The [[AliceWeidel]] interview demonstrates why scope must be tested with concrete categories. She first defines remigration around illegal entrants, rejected asylum applicants, and foreign criminals, then includes Syrians and Afghans whose protection she says should end and Ukrainians after the war. Her rough total of four to five million is substantially broader than the approximately 260,000 people the interview identifies as already legally required to leave. The concept therefore includes a recurrent gap between a narrow enforcement formulation and a broader political removal horizon.

## Key Claims

- Remigration politics intensifies immigration backlash by shifting from entry control to removal-oriented national identity politics.
- A narrow opening definition can conceal a much broader affected population once temporary protection, nationality, and postwar return are specified.
- The term can help make a party unacceptable to mainstream coalition partners even when it attracts electoral support.
- State-level remigration promises can exceed the formal powers available to a regional government.
- The language can produce concrete local costs when residents, workers, or organizations fear they would be targeted.
- Electoral success can move removal-oriented rhetoric from outsider messaging into government-formation negotiations.

## Evidence

- AfD program: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] says AfD promotes remigration while opposing immigration and asylum rights.
- Coalition boundary: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] places those positions inside the broader explanation for the Brandmauer.
- State-power limit: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] notes that state government cannot directly control many national AfD positions.
- Social fallout: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] reports that some people in Saxony-Anhalt say they or people they know would consider leaving under AfD rule.
- Post-election stakes: [[all-right-on-the-night-afds-victory-6a9e8573fe3b7e34fd2d258f]] says AfD's 44% showing intensifies the debate over whether the party should be treated as normal opposition or an anti-system threat.
- Definition expansion: [[an-interview-with-alice-weidel-027be4d01e889c07f016e332ffe9a77e]] records Weidel moving from illegal entrants, rejected applicants, and foreign criminals to Syrians, Afghans, and eventually Ukrainians.
- Scale implication: [[an-interview-with-alice-weidel-027be4d01e889c07f016e332ffe9a77e]] contrasts roughly 260,000 people already required to leave with Weidel's four-to-five-million estimate for the broader categories she names.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not provide a complete AfD policy history, verify the population estimates, establish that every named person could legally or safely be returned, or show how the program would be implemented. Weidel also distinguishes these groups from qualified, law-abiding work migrants and acknowledges Germany's demographic need for foreign professionals. The concept should remain tied to far-right nationalist removal politics rather than ordinary migration administration.

## What Changed

- Clarified the gap between a narrow enforcement definition and a broader refugee-return program.
- Added concrete affected categories and the resulting order-of-magnitude scope change.
