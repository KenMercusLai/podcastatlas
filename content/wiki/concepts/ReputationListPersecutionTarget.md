# Reputation List as Persecution Target / 名望榜单迫害目标化

## Definition

Reputation list as persecution target / 名望榜单迫害目标化 is the pattern where a public honor or endorsement list that helps a moral community recognize worthy people also makes those people legible to hostile power as targets.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 892 supplies the pre-crackdown visibility layer. Li Ying remains respected after dismissal, and士人 circulate honor categories such as 三君, 八俊, 八顾, 八及, and 八厨. In that moment the lists still function as moral recognition, peer sorting, and reputation memory, but the episode already frames public fame as exposure.

Hanji 893 supplies the conversion layer. After the first party-prohibition disaster,士人 continue resisting eunuch power through reputation and public opinion. Their "贤达榜" praises people such as [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] and [[FanPangLateHan|范滂]], but under [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]'s interpretive control the same list becomes a sign of dangerous organization. The concept's reusable point is that visibility cuts both ways: reputation ranking can create solidarity, memory, and recruitment, but it can also lower the search cost for repression when the state treats independent public prestige as factional threat.

## Key Claims

- Public reputation systems make a moral community easier for allies and opponents to identify.
- Honor lists begin as moral recognition, peer sorting, and social memory before they become punitive objects.
- Lists become dangerous when hostile authorities reinterpret public esteem as evidence of political organization.
- Under captured power, reputation can shift from protection or capital into exposure.
- The list format lowers the practical cost of targeting because it has already selected visible names.
- The resulting persecution can intensify名节 because death-facing actors treat inclusion as moral identity rather than only danger.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not claim that every public honor list becomes dangerous. It claims that list-based prestige can become a targeting aid when a hostile state reads independent reputation as factional organization. Hanji 892's rapid catalog includes several names that remain source-scoped because this episode does not disambiguate every list member into a canonical page.

## What Changed

- Hanji 892 adds the pre-crackdown honor-list catalog and fame-risk framing before Hanji 893's conversion into arrest targets.
- The current judgment now starts earlier: targetability begins when praise standardizes names, even before coercive power formally recodes the list.
