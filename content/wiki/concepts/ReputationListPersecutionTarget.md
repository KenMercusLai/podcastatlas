# Reputation List as Persecution Target / 名望榜单迫害目标化

## Definition

Reputation list as persecution target / 名望榜单迫害目标化 is the pattern where a public honor or endorsement list that helps a moral community recognize worthy people also makes those people legible to hostile power as targets.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] supplies the current source case. After the first party-prohibition disaster,士人 continue resisting eunuch power through reputation and public opinion. Their "贤达榜" praises people such as [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] and [[FanPangLateHan|范滂]], but under [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]'s interpretive control the same list becomes a sign of dangerous organization.

The concept's reusable point is that visibility cuts both ways. Reputation ranking can create solidarity, memory, and recruitment, but it can also lower the search cost for repression when the state treats independent public prestige as factional threat. In this source, the honor list becomes a death list because reputation moves from social recognition into administrative and punitive legibility.

## Key Claims

- Public reputation systems make a moral community easier for allies and opponents to identify.
- Honor lists become dangerous when hostile authorities reinterpret public esteem as evidence of political organization.
- Under captured power, reputation can shift from protection or capital into exposure.
- The list format lowers the practical cost of targeting because it has already selected visible names.
- The resulting persecution can intensify名节 because death-facing actors treat inclusion as moral identity rather than only danger.

## Evidence

- Public honor function: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says士人 mutually praise worthy people and produce a "贤达榜" to recognize moral and talented peers.
- Target conversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says that after the second party-prohibition disaster breaks out, people on the list become eunuch-side targets and the honor list becomes a death list.
- Reputation exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] links Li Ying and Fan Pang's fame to their inclusion in the arrest and martyrdom field.
- Moral identity effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] says Jing Yi self-reports because omission from Li Ying's student-linked liability feels dishonorable.
- Power interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-893-rongyu-bang-ruhe-biancheng-siwang-mingdan-lmxzqoauf79-swwnk-0cevav0gq|Hanji 893]] ties the list's danger to Cao Jie's ability to recode party association as ruler-threatening conspiracy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one episode's interpretation of the late-Han "贤达榜" and "死亡名单" contrast. It does not claim that every public honor list becomes dangerous, only that list-based prestige can become a targeting aid when a hostile state reads independent reputation as factional organization.

## What Changed

- Hanji 893 creates the concept to separate reputation-as-protection from reputation-as-targeting under captured power.
