# Rocket Recovery Route Choice

Rocket recovery route choice is the engineering and economic tradeoff among different ways of bringing a reusable booster back: landing legs, tower catch, sea-net capture, parachutes, airbags, or other schemes. [[ruhe-douzhu-yike-huojian-s10e21-b66fdf0f-d428-4f0c-8412-b0c7581132d0]] frames this through [[Falcon9]], [[Starship]], and [[LongMarch10B]].

The route choice is really a choice about where to put mass, precision, cost, and failure risk. [[Falcon9]] keeps landing capability on the rocket, which is mature and flexible but consumes payload mass and demands precise terminal landing. [[Starship]] tries to remove landing legs and enable fast pad-side reuse, but makes the tower and catch timing extremely high-consequence. [[SeaNetRocketRecovery]] moves damping and some capture burden to a sea platform, improving rocket-side mass tradeoffs while adding ship logistics and recovery-platform cost.

[[zhongting-zhuque-sanhao-fuzongshi-huojian-huishou-zhiyou-cheng-yu-bai-meiyou-zhongjiantai-s9e41-d9e11e3b-7c7e-4beb-b19f-6f90fff38714]] adds the [[Zhuque3]] landing-leg route from an insider design perspective. [[DongKai]] treats [[Falcon9]] as the mature operational reference and says Zhuque-3's current route is not confused, but he also says very large future rockets may outgrow landing legs and move toward a [[Starship]]-style catch method.

The source also adds [[RecoveryRangePayloadTradeoff]] to route choice. A landing-leg route gives a private launch company a proven recovery path, but the selected landing range still shifts propellant reserve, payload, safety boundary, and eventual launch-site infrastructure requirements.

## Key Claims
- A recovery route is not only a landing mechanism; it determines payload penalty, infrastructure risk, operations cadence, and test tolerance.
- The best route depends on institutional constraints. A private launch company may prefer a proven leg-landing path, while a resource-concentrated system may attempt a platform-heavy net route.
- Recovery success is an intermediate milestone. [[ReusableRocketTurnaround]] and repeated mission demand decide whether a route becomes economical.
- Scale can change the preferred route: landing legs may be practical for a Zhuque-3 or Falcon 9 class vehicle, while superheavy full-reuse systems may push more burden into tower catch or other ground infrastructure.
