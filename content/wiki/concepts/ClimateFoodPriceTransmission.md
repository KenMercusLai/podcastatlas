# Climate Food Price Transmission

## Definition

Climate food price transmission is the process by which weather or climate changes affect biological output, intermediate inputs, logistics, policy, processing, and firm decisions before appearing—or failing to appear—in household food prices, package sizes, formulas, or availability.

## Current Synthesis

The sources show that climate rarely moves directly from weather to a checkout price. [[shangye-xiaoyang-45-eerninuo-yueda-yu-yuegui-1000164352]] provides the clearest hidden-input chain: [[ElNino|El Niño]] weakens Peruvian upwelling, anchoveta supply tightens, fishmeal becomes more expensive, aquaculture feed absorbs the shock, and farmed seafood can eventually cost more. [[reeses-heir-vs-chocolate-skimpflation]] adds a cocoa case in which weather-linked scarcity can surface not only as price but as package shrinkage, compound coating, reformulation, and consumer-trust pressure.

[[e254-chaoji-eerninuo-laile-womende-richang-suoxu-zhenhui-yin-ta-zhangjia-ma-0b644f51-461c-4ddb-b707-d2a39b9a7d80]] generalizes the pathway. Transmission depends on the affected region's tradable weight, the biological timing of the shock, inventory and substitutes, export and logistics policy, chain length, raw-material cost share, contracts, hedging, formulation, demand, and pricing power. This explains why the same climate event can cause a strong commodity move, a weak retail move, a quality change, or no visible consumer change at all.

## Key Claims

- **The binding node may be indirect.** Feed, fertilizer, water, energy, transport, or an intermediate ingredient can matter more than the final food itself.
- **Concentration and tradable surplus amplify shocks.** A disruption matters more when production or exports are geographically concentrated and inventories are low.
- **Biological timing is decisive.** Rainfall or heat has different meaning before planting, during sensitive growth, at harvest, and during storage or transport.
- **Retail transmission is filtered.** Inventories, contracts, hedges, substitutes, processing, promotions, margins, demand, and brand strategy change timing and magnitude.
- **Consumer inflation can hide outside the posted price.** Shrinkage, reformulation, lower quality, fewer promotions, or reduced availability can carry the adjustment.
- **Policy can weaken the global buffer.** Export restrictions can reduce internationally available supply even when stocks still exist inside producing countries.

## Evidence

- **Fishmeal and aquaculture chain:** [[shangye-xiaoyang-45-eerninuo-yueda-yu-yuegui-1000164352]] traces upwelling, anchoveta, fishery restrictions, fishmeal, feed substitution limits, aquaculture, restaurants, and seafood prices.
- **Cocoa, formulation, and labels:** [[reeses-heir-vs-chocolate-skimpflation]] links West African weather pressure and cocoa-butter cost to price, package, and recipe choices, including legally meaningful chocolate labels.
- **General transmission framework:** [[e254-chaoji-eerninuo-laile-womende-richang-suoxu-zhenhui-yin-ta-zhangjia-ma-0b644f51-461c-4ddb-b707-d2a39b9a7d80]] compares palm oil, sugar, coffee, rubber, grains, fishmeal, transport, inventory, substitution, hedging, and retail cost stacks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Climate is one contributor among demand, currency, energy, fertilizer, disease, agronomy, capital investment, policy, speculation, and broader logistics.
- A commodity-price increase does not establish that the same percentage will reach households, and price stability does not prove that quality or quantity was unchanged.
- Substitution is bounded by nutrition, performance, regulation, consumer expectations, and the relative price of alternatives.
- Numerical magnitudes in the sources are time-specific episode claims rather than general pass-through coefficients.

## What Changed

- Generalized the concept from seafood and cocoa cases into a conditional multi-stage transmission framework.
- Added inventory, export policy, logistics, hedging, chain length, and raw-material cost share as explicit filters.
- Added beneficial regional weather outcomes and weak-demand absorption as reasons transmission may fail.
- Distinguished shelf-price inflation from reformulation, shrinkage, promotion, and availability changes.
