# Psychedelic Integration

## Definition
Psychedelic integration is the supported, ongoing process of interpreting an acute psychedelic experience and translating relevant insight, emotion, or behavioral possibility into ordinary life.

## Current Synthesis
In the source, integration is not a short administrative debrief and not proof that every intense experience is meaningful. [[RobinCarhartHarris]] describes the acute session as potentially making difficult psychological work easier, while insisting that it does not complete that work. Meditation, mindfulness, therapy, spiritual practice, and repeated attention may help a person sit with what arose and test whether it supports durable change.

This makes integration the bridge between [[PsychedelicTherapyMechanism]] and outcome durability. A temporary state can reveal a different pattern of feeling or self-relation, but [[TherapeuticStateLearning]] requires repetition, interpretation, context, and maintenance. Relapse in treatment-resistant depression and the possibility of destabilizing ego-boundary change both argue against treating the peak experience as a self-validating cure.

## Key Claims
- Integration begins after, but is not limited to, the dosing session.
- Acute insight or emotional release is a starting point rather than completed treatment.
- Ongoing reflective or therapeutic practice may help translate experience into behavior and ordinary awareness.
- Relapse shows that a powerful session does not guarantee durable remission.
- Difficult, confusing, grandiose, or destabilizing experiences need careful interpretation rather than automatic endorsement.
- Integration remains subordinate to clinical screening, legal context, crisis support, and compound-specific evidence.

## Evidence
- Ongoing-work frame - [[the-science-of-psychedelics-for-mental-health-dr-robin-carhart-harris-scim8487331019]] says integration can last a lifetime because the session may ease psychological work without finishing it.
- Practice bridge - [[the-science-of-psychedelics-for-mental-health-dr-robin-carhart-harris-scim8487331019]] names meditation, mindfulness, and spiritual practice as possible complements for staying with experience rather than suppressing it.
- Durability limit - [[the-science-of-psychedelics-for-mental-health-dr-robin-carhart-harris-scim8487331019]] reports that many treatment-resistant-depression participants relapsed after three months or more despite some lasting benefit.
- Stability boundary - [[the-science-of-psychedelics-for-mental-health-dr-robin-carhart-harris-scim8487331019]] presents ego dissolution as potentially powerful but potentially unstable when not integrated.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish one validated integration protocol, dose, duration, practitioner credential, or causal contribution to clinical outcome. Meditation or spiritual practice is not universally suitable and does not replace psychiatric or emergency care. Integration language can also overinterpret drug effects or encourage unsupported certainty, so experiences should be evaluated against functioning, evidence, relationships, safety, and time.

## What Changed
- Created a durable-learning account that separates the acute psychedelic state from later interpretation, practice, and maintenance.
