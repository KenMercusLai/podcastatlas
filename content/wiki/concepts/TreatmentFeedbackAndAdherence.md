# Treatment Feedback and Adherence

## Definition
Treatment feedback and adherence is the hypothesis that timely, understandable evidence about treatment response can help patients continue appropriate therapy and identify ineffective or unsafe options sooner.

## Current Synthesis
The source contrasts visible feedback, such as weight change, with medicines whose benefits are difficult to perceive day to day. [[DiogoRau]] proposes that sensors, scales, wearables, or other measurements could close the loop between taking a medicine and understanding whether it is working. Faster feedback may reinforce continued use or prompt earlier review when response is absent.

The mechanism is plausible but unproven in this source. Feedback can motivate, confuse, create anxiety, overemphasize short-term metrics, or encourage unsafe self-adjustment. The useful form is therefore a governed loop: valid measurement, interpretable context, appropriate cadence, clinician escalation where needed, and explicit recognition that surrogate movement is not always the outcome that matters.

## Key Claims
- Treatment effects that patients can perceive may support engagement more readily than invisible or delayed benefits.
- Wearables and home measurements can shorten the interval between treatment and interpretable response.
- Feedback can support adherence only when the measured signal is valid and relevant to the therapeutic goal.
- Rapid signals may also help identify nonresponse and trigger proportionate review.
- More data does not automatically improve adherence and can produce anxiety, alert fatigue, or false confidence.
- Feedback should not authorize unilateral dose changes or replacement of clinical judgment.

## Evidence
- Adherence hypothesis: [[the-future-of-healthcare-technology-with-eli-lilly-and-companys-diogo-rau-the-future-of-healthcare-technology-with-eli]] explicitly presents improved adherence from feedback as a hypothesis.
- Visible-versus-invisible response: [[the-future-of-healthcare-technology-with-eli-lilly-and-companys-diogo-rau-the-future-of-healthcare-technology-with-eli]] contrasts scales and weight-management medicines with statin or cholesterol treatment whose effect is not usually visible from one day to the next.
- Continuous loop: [[the-future-of-healthcare-technology-with-eli-lilly-and-companys-diogo-rau-the-future-of-healthcare-technology-with-eli]] describes clinical-trial devices, continuous measurement, and rapid indication of whether treatment is working.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode gives no randomized comparison, validated adherence measure, persistence duration, clinical endpoint, or evidence that the proposed loop improves outcomes. Short-term changes can be noisy or clinically misleading, and delayed-benefit therapies may be effective without perceptible daily movement. Device access, comfort, accuracy, privacy, interpretation, and regulatory status also constrain the model.
