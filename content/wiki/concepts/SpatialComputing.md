# Spatial Computing / 空间计算

## Definition

Spatial computing lets a system understand, anchor, and manipulate digital information relative to physical space, objects, and user position rather than treating interaction as only text, taps, or flat images.

## Current Synthesis

The RayNeo source explains why glasses may need spatial understanding: first-person AI must interpret surroundings, position, and physical relationships. The Specs source shows the consumer-hardware consequences of implementing that idea through optical display, tracking, gestures, shared anchors, and mixed-reality applications.

The strongest present value is not generic AI access but spatially specific action: placing guidance on objects, showing assembly or repair steps in context, supporting training, or letting multiple users interact with the same anchored content. Those functions can justify a display in ways that object recognition and voice answers alone may not.

Spatial capability nevertheless intensifies the hardware problem. Stable anchoring, low latency, sufficient field of view, compute, battery, heat management, and comfortable optics must work together. Specs therefore supports the technical case for spatial glasses while weakening the claim that technical feasibility already equals mass-market readiness.

## Key Claims

- Spatial computing makes physical relationships part of the interface.
- Glasses can use spatial understanding for guidance, anchored overlays, gestures, and shared experiences.
- Display is most defensible when information must appear at a physical location rather than only be spoken.
- Stable interaction depends on sensing, compute, field of view, latency, power, heat, and optics as one system.
- Task-specific spatial tools may become useful before all-day phone-replacing glasses.
- Current evidence demonstrates capability more strongly than adoption.

## Evidence

- **First-person distinction:** [[no-221-leiniao-ceo-xinjishu-yuelaiyueduo-women-weishenme-hai-xuyao-yifu-zhineng-yanjing-gkwridon6uwmaoosszqset9vd]] argues that glasses AI differs from phone AI because it works with surrounding physical context.
- **Integrated implementation:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] describes spatial positioning, hand gestures, optical overlays, shared AR, and mixed-reality applications in Specs.
- **Task-specific value:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] identifies assembly, repair, training, sports guidance, and shared games as possible spatially specific jobs.
- **System constraints:** [[snap-zuole-shinian-yanjing-zhongyu-dengdao-ta-de-shidai-le-ma-s10e30-f98c4370-6653-4af3-9a43-386bf0ea0547]] reports latency, drifting or unstable virtual content, limited field of view, weight, battery, and compute tradeoffs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Short demonstrations do not establish robust tracking across environments or long-duration use.
- Some listed applications are demos or speaker-imagined uses rather than retained customer behavior.
- Camera-plus-audio AI can provide recognition and advice without spatial display, so not every first-person task requires spatial computing.
- The sources do not provide technical benchmarks for mapping, localization, reconstruction, or shared-anchor reliability.

## What Changed

- Specs adds an integrated consumer-glasses implementation beyond the earlier practitioner definition.
- The synthesis now identifies spatially anchored guidance as the main argument for carrying a display.
- Hardware-system constraints and task-specific adoption are now explicit.
