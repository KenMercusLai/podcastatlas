# Japanese Condo Maintenance Reserve / 日本公寓修缮基金

Japanese condo maintenance reserve is the source's owner-cost and quality-preservation mechanism for apartment buildings. In [[131-wo-zai-riben-mai-le-yi-tao-zizhu-fang-llofkgaj-hyajksrpkoylakuocxz]], the developer gives a long-term repair plan covering exterior walls, equipment, public systems, and later replacement cycles, and the monthly reserve rises as the building ages.

The concept matters because it makes Japanese housing ownership less like a one-time purchase. Management fees and repair reserves raise carrying cost, but they also make building aging visible, planned, and collectively funded. That is why the source connects old-condo value not only to age and location, but also to maintenance governance.

## Key Claims
- The reserve turns future deterioration into a monthly obligation rather than a surprise collective bill.
- Rising reserve schedules make long-lived buildings more expensive to hold over time.
- Clear management-fee and repair-plan disclosure helps buyers understand what building quality will cost.
- A well-managed older condo may preserve value better than one with weak reserves, poor repair execution, or neglected common areas.
