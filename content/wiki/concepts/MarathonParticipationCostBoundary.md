# Marathon Participation Cost Boundary / 马拉松参赛成本与能力边界

## Definition
Marathon participation cost boundary is the judgment that entering a race requires counting money, time, preparation, recovery, and physical suitability together, and that the full marathon is one optional distance rather than the required endpoint of recreational running.

## Current Synthesis
[[lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl-lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl|EP96]] reframes the apparently low entry fee as only one line in a runner's budget. Shoes, clothing, monitoring devices, transport, hotels, training weeks, repeated sessions, stretching, recovery, and possible treatment can exceed the registration charge. Away races join this personal budget to [[MarathonDestinationConsumption]].

Cost does not create an obligation to finish. Cutoff rules, injury, illness, inadequate preparation, or a mismatch between distance and ability are reasons to stop or choose a shorter event. Equipment may improve comfort or information, but spending more does not establish readiness, and the episode treats health runs and half marathons as legitimate participation rather than incomplete versions of the full distance.

## Key Claims
- Entry fees understate the runner's total financial commitment because equipment, travel, lodging, recovery, and recurring participation matter.
- Training time and recovery load are real costs even when no payment is made.
- Equipment should be matched to use and body needs, but expensive gear is neither necessary nor sufficient for safe completion.
- Cutoff vehicles and voluntary withdrawal are safety boundaries, not personal failures.
- Shorter distances can preserve exercise, community, and event atmosphere without imposing the full marathon's preparation and physical load.

## Evidence
- **Full personal budget:** [[lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl-lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl|EP96]] lists transport, lodging, shoes, compression clothing, watches, and heart-rate devices beyond the entry fee and recounts an episode-level equipment spend before a first full marathon.
- **Time and recovery:** [[lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl-lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl|EP96]] describes a multi-month training cycle, multiple weekly runs, stretching, icing, and physiotherapy as part of the participation cost.
- **Equipment qualification:** [[lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl-lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl|EP96]] notes shoe specialization while explicitly rejecting the claim that premium equipment is necessary for entry-level participation.
- **Stopping boundary:** [[lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl-lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl|EP96]] explains staged cutoff times and argues that injury or illness should trigger withdrawal rather than forced completion.
- **Distance choice:** [[lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl-lv8ss3l8pykybfppj7hf90kgb7fl|EP96]] repeatedly presents a 5-kilometer health run or half marathon as a valid alternative for people whose interest, training, or physical condition does not support 42.195 kilometers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is not a medical screening, training plan, equipment test, or injury study. Its spending averages, equipment example, training duration, and frequency are episode-reported and should not be universalized. Individual readiness depends on health, prior training, pace, course, weather, event rules, and professional advice where appropriate. A shorter distance reduces but does not eliminate exercise or event risk.

## What Changed
- Made non-fee spending and unpaid training time part of the participation-cost calculation.
- Treated withdrawal and cutoff compliance as risk management rather than failed consumption.
- Established shorter race formats as complete participation choices, not merely steps toward a full marathon.
