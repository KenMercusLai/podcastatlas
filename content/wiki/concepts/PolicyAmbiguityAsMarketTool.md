# Policy Ambiguity As Market Tool

[[179-xian-jiu-riyuan-zai-jiu-changzhai-jiuhuoduizhang-beisente-zai-zou-yi-tiao-zenyang-de-gangsi-lqjc6vz-1ungnkpdw9d5kbsa-ouo]] adds [[PolicyAmbiguityAsMarketTool]] as the practice of avoiding a clear policy defense line so markets cannot easily construct a one-way speculative target. The source derives this from the contrast between the 1992 sterling crisis and [[ScottBessent|Scott Bessent / 贝森特]]'s later Treasury posture around yen support and Treasury buybacks.

## Key Claims
- Clear defense thresholds can invite speculative concentration when fundamentals and policy are inconsistent.
- Sudden intervention, broad toolkits, and unclear reaction functions can make speculative attacks harder to price.
- Ambiguity can buy time but may also reduce democratic and market transparency.
- In the episode, Treasury ambiguity supplements the existing idea of a vague [[FederalReserve]] reaction function.
