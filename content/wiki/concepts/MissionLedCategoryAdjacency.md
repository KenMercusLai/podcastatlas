# Mission-Led Category Adjacency / 使命驱动的品类邻接

## Definition
Mission-led category adjacency is expansion into a neighboring product category because it advances the same customer outcome and reuses accumulated capabilities, even when the move creates direct competition with a stronger incumbent.

## Current Synthesis
[[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] grounds the concept in [[Insta360|Insta360's]] decision to develop panoramic drones despite [[DJI]]'s dominance. The argument is not that mission overrides economics: recording and sharing supply the direction, while imaging capability, an unmet workflow, financing, long-cycle readiness, commercialization, and survivability determine whether the adjacency is responsible. Mission is therefore a selection rule for which hard problems deserve investment, not permission to ignore risk.

## Key Claims
- A durable mission can define a customer outcome more broadly than the company's current product form.
- Adjacent expansion is stronger when existing technical, product, channel, or brand capabilities transfer to the new category.
- An incumbent's dominance does not erase an unmet workflow, but it raises the evidence and execution threshold for entry.
- Competition can be framed as market expansion while still requiring preparation for price, patent, channel, and product conflict.
- Public-company duties require mission-led bets to remain compatible with survival, efficiency, customer value, employee development, and shareholder return.

## Evidence
- Mission continuity: [[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] says the company defined its mission around helping users record and share life.
- Customer-outcome adjacency: [[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] presents drones as another route to making people look good and enabling spatial exploration, not merely another camera specification.
- Capability and cycle evidence: [[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] argues for building capability stacks so the company is ready for a later cycle rather than optimizing only annual profit.
- Governance boundary: [[nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352-nk78yhgkzrza-pj5ex42hwz7n352]] explicitly distinguishes the founder's creative preference from the obligations of a listed company.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Mission language can rationalize overexpansion after the fact. Capability transfer may be weaker than expected, and a dominant incumbent can impose price, channel, patent, talent, and ecosystem costs. The source does not establish the panoramic-drone venture's eventual market outcome, and its strategic case is primarily the founder's account.

## What Changed
- Established mission as a bounded category-selection rule rather than an unlimited mandate.
- Added public-company survival and return obligations as explicit checks on adjacency.
