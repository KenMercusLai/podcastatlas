# Performance Supplement Hierarchy

## Definition
Performance supplement hierarchy is a foundations-first method for deciding whether a targeted compound adds value after sleep, hydration, whole-food nutrition, training design, and recovery are already adequate.

## Current Synthesis
The source sorts performance aids by job rather than by marketing category: fuel supports available energy, stimulants alter arousal or output, and fatigue blockers may delay a limiting process. It then adds a second distinction between acute tools such as caffeine and compounds such as creatine or beta-alanine that require consistent intake to build tissue stores or downstream capacity.

The decision rule is diagnostic. Identify the actual constraint, select one ingredient with a plausible role, use a measurable dose and a reliable product, and assess benefit or adverse reaction before combining ingredients. This makes [[ThirdPartySupplementTesting]] and [[SupplementLabelAccuracy]] part of performance practice, especially for tested athletes, without treating certification as proof of efficacy.

Supplements remain subordinate to causes. A compound that masks poor sleep, insufficient food, dehydration, excessive training load, or another unresolved problem may improve a symptom while preserving the limitation. Regular use is therefore easier to justify for a well-characterized foundational aid than for a stimulant or cortisol-modulating product that creates dependence or pushes physiology in the wrong direction.

The solo supplementation episode generalizes this logic beyond sport. It adds goal and budget triage, accepts a zero-supplement protocol, and treats broad foundational formulas as the limited exception to a preference for isolated ingredients. Sleep, hormone, cognition, and nutritional products should be evaluated as separate experiments rather than bundled into a stack whose effects cannot be attributed.

## Key Claims
- Sleep, hydration, whole-food nutrition, training, and recovery outrank supplementation, and a rational protocol can contain no supplements.
- Performance aids should be selected for a defined role such as fuel, stimulation, fatigue buffering, or recovery support.
- Acute and accumulation-dependent supplements require different expectations about timing and consistency.
- Single-ingredient products make dose adjustment, benefit attribution, and adverse-reaction detection easier.
- Third-party testing can reduce content and contamination uncertainty but does not establish effectiveness.
- Symptom relief is not the same as resolving under-fueling, sleep loss, dehydration, overload, or another cause.
- Dependence, budget, medical state, sport rules, product quality, and individual response can reverse an apparently favorable risk-benefit calculation.

## Evidence
- Foundations and categories - [[guest-series-dr-andy-galpin-optimal-nutrition-supplementation-for-fitness-scim1024405440]] places sleep, food, hydration, training, and recovery first and groups aids as fuel, stimulants, and fatigue blockers.
- Acute versus cumulative use - [[guest-series-dr-andy-galpin-optimal-nutrition-supplementation-for-fitness-scim1024405440]] contrasts acute caffeine-like effects with creatine and beta-alanine, which require consistent intake.
- Targeted products - [[guest-series-dr-andy-galpin-optimal-nutrition-supplementation-for-fitness-scim1024405440]] favors single-ingredient supplements so users can identify what works, change the dose, and locate adverse effects.
- Quality control - [[guest-series-dr-andy-galpin-optimal-nutrition-supplementation-for-fitness-scim1024405440]] emphasizes independent testing, especially where banned-substance contamination matters.
- Cause and dependence - [[guest-series-dr-andy-galpin-optimal-nutrition-supplementation-for-fitness-scim1024405440]] distinguishes short-term symptom management from correcting a cause and identifies caffeine as capable of physiological dependence.
- Generalized trial design - [[developing-a-rational-approach-to-supplementation-for-health-performance-scim5857890354]] extends the hierarchy across foundational nutrition, sleep, hormones, and focus while favoring minimum-effective-dose, one-ingredient-at-a-time trials.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The structured episode notes do not supply complete trials, effect sizes, product assays, contraindications, interaction data, or sport-governing rules for every named supplement. Evidence and risk differ across creatine, caffeine, beta-alanine, bicarbonate, beetroot, rhodiola, omega-3s, glutamine, tart cherry, probiotics, melatonin, nootropics, adaptogens, and hormone-active herbs; membership in one hierarchy does not imply equal efficacy. Pregnancy, adolescence, disease, medications, fertility treatment, eating disorders, injury, sleep disorders, competitive testing, and adverse symptoms require qualified guidance.
