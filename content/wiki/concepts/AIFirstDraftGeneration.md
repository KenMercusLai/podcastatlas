# AI First-Draft Generation

AI first-draft generation is the use of a model to produce an initial draft that a human then edits, verifies, personalizes, and rehearses. In [[ep-17-ais-impact-on-creativity-a-consumers-perspective]], [[MarkDataScienceWithSam|Mark]] says a seven- to eight-minute Toastmasters speech that once took about 90 minutes can begin from a [[ChatGPT]] draft and be completed with rehearsal in roughly 20 to 30 minutes.

This concept is distinct from [[FirstDraftThinking]]. In education, first-draft thinking protects the learner's initial cognitive work before AI help. In Mark's volunteer and professional context, AI first-draft generation is useful because the human already owns the goal, audience, and acceptance standard, then uses AI to reduce blank-page friction.

## Key Claims
- AI-generated drafts are most useful when they shorten the distance to a workable artifact without becoming the final artifact.
- The workflow still needs [[AIVerification]] because fluent drafts can contain false claims, awkward lines, or generic framing.
- Human editing preserves voice, audience fit, factual grounding, and ownership of the result.
- First-draft generation can save time for recurring volunteer, community, and professional communication tasks.
- The same pattern can become risky in learning contexts if it replaces the user's first attempt rather than assisting a task they already understand.
