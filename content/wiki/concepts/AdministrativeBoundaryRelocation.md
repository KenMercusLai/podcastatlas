## Definition

Administrative boundary relocation is the expansion of a state's practical core by moving the checkpoint that distinguishes an inner, protected, or directly supervised zone from an outer one.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 356-2 grounds the concept in [[YangPuWesternHan|杨仆]]'s proposal to move [[HanguPass|函谷关]] east to Xin'an. The episode treats the pass as more than masonry or terrain: wherever the line is placed affects who counts as inside, which lands fall under closer imperial supervision, and how titles tied to inner-zone status are valued.

The proposal is presented as incentive alignment. Yang Pu gains the status he wants, while [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] gains a larger inner territory and stronger reach toward eastern elites. That motive pairing remains interpretive, but the durable mechanism is that a territorial state can expand administratively by redefining a boundary even before describing the act as fresh conquest.

Hanji 269-2 adds a frontier-network variant. [[SimaXiangru|司马相如]]'s western embassy reportedly removes older gates and establishes new ones farther outward while roads, a bridge, officials, and counties extend behind the line. Boundary movement here is not an isolated decree: it depends on negotiated access and infrastructure, and it follows a revolt that demonstrates how formal reach can outrun governable capacity.

## Key Claims

- Strategic checkpoints can function as political and administrative boundaries, not only military defenses.
- Moving a boundary changes the status of people and land without requiring every location to be newly conquered.
- Personal title incentives can align with a ruler's centralization interest.
- Boundary expansion can increase supervision burdens as well as formal reach.
- Frontier gates become meaningful only when roads, officials, supply, and local cooperation make the new line administratively usable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both sources are short interpretive podcast summaries with corrupted or unstable place-name transcription. They do not independently prove the emperor's private motive, the exact legal consequences of moving a line, the western gates' precise locations, or that newly enclosed land instantly received uniform administration. Hanji 269-2's preceding labor revolt is direct counterevidence to treating a relocated boundary as effective control. Boundary relocation should therefore be treated as a mechanism to investigate, not automatic proof of incorporation.

## What Changed

- Added a southwestern case in which gate relocation is bundled with diplomacy, roads, bridge-building, and local administration.
- Made the gap between formal boundary movement and governable capacity explicit.
