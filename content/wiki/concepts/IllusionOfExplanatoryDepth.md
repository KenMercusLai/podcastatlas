# Illusion of Explanatory Depth

## Definition
The illusion of explanatory depth is the tendency to believe one understands a familiar object, system, or policy more completely than one can demonstrate when asked to explain its mechanism step by step.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-d08500083dda91b814b053f30bd7b557-87666361-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-d08500083dda91b814b053f30bd7b557-87666361|The Story of Stories]] uses familiar objects such as zippers and bicycles to distinguish recognition, use, and confidence from mechanistic knowledge. The illusion becomes visible when an attempted explanation reaches a missing link.

The same gap can matter in public judgment. A policy label can feel understood even when its causal steps, tradeoffs, or implementation are unclear. [[SelfExplanationKnowledgeCalibration]] turns this failure into a learning opportunity, but explanation alone remains vulnerable to fluent confabulation and therefore needs evidence and rival accounts.

## Key Claims
- Familiarity and successful use do not establish mechanistic understanding.
- Step-by-step explanation can reveal gaps hidden by a global confidence judgment.
- The effect can apply to everyday devices and abstract policies.
- Discovering a gap can improve humility and direct further learning.
- Fluent explanation is not sufficient unless its causal steps are externally supported.

- Policy understanding - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-d08500083dda91b814b053f30bd7b557-87666361-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-d08500083dda91b814b053f30bd7b557-87666361]] reports that attempting to explain political policies can reveal knowledge gaps and soften confidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied summary does not provide experimental methods, effect sizes, domain differences, or durability. Failure to verbalize a mechanism may sometimes reflect communication difficulty rather than total ignorance, while a polished verbal account can still be false. Calibration should distinguish tacit skill, factual knowledge, causal understanding, and rhetorical fluency.

## What Changed
- Established the distinction between familiarity-based confidence and demonstrated mechanistic understanding.
