# Parliamentary Attainder

## Definition

Parliamentary attainder is the use of legislation to condemn a named person, commonly for treason, with penalties that could include death, forfeiture, corruption of blood, and exclusion of heirs without an ordinary judicial trial.

## Current Synthesis

The episode places attainder between medieval factional conflict and Tudor statecraft. Under Edward II, political enemies could be destroyed along with their families' property rights; from the Wars of the Roses onward, parliamentary attainder became a recurring way to settle contests whose legal meaning depended on the victorious claimant. Henry Tudor's post-Bosworth treatment of the dead Richard III makes the circularity visible: once king, the victor could define resistance to his claim as treason.

Under [[HenryVIII]], Thomas Cromwell's condemnation shows that attainder remained available against a minister without an ordinary trial. The procedure was legally parliamentary, but that form did not remove the effects of unequal power or supply the protections associated with adversarial defence.

## Key Claims

- Attainder fused political condemnation with property forfeiture and hereditary exclusion.
- It allowed Parliament to impose punishment without an ordinary criminal trial.
- Regime victory could determine retrospectively whose conduct counted as treason.
- The procedure made family inheritance part of punishment, not merely a consequence of death.
- Its parliamentary form did not guarantee evidentiary fairness or political independence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode is a thematic survey and does not distinguish every statutory form, parliamentary stage, reversal, or period-specific legal consequence of attainder.
- Calling the procedure political does not prove every factual allegation false; the central qualification is that guilt could be imposed without ordinary trial safeguards.
- Parliament's participation gave condemnation institutional form but did not make it equivalent to modern legislative or judicial due process.

## What Changed

- Established attainder as a bridge among treason, regime victory, property seizure, and hereditary punishment.
- Distinguished parliamentary authorization from ordinary trial and evidentiary protection.
