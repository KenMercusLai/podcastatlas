# News Finds Me / 新闻找到我

News finds me is the media-consumption pattern where people encounter news through platforms, feeds, social sharing, or recommendation rather than by intentionally seeking news sources. In [[164-suanfa-de-tuzi-dong-weishenme-ni-zong-zai-kanwan-xinwen-hou-huaxiang-yule-duitan-huangshengchun-jiaoshou-lo6ik8cj2njvu-cy2jdj-kl1jpuq]], [[HuangShengchun|黄圣淳]] uses it to explain why [[IncidentalExposure]] makes contemporary media environments more complicated than a simple [[FilterBubble]] model.

The concept is ambivalent. It can keep users loosely connected to public affairs even when they are not active news consumers. But it can also weaken intentional news habits, especially when [[RecommendationSystemProductization]] and [[AlgorithmicEntertainmentRedirect]] make public information compete with easier entertainment in the same flow.

## Key Claims
- News exposure can happen without news-seeking identity or routine.
- The pattern complicates "people only see what they choose" because platform and peer distribution can bring unchosen information.
- The same pattern can reduce agency if users rely entirely on feeds to decide which public issues matter.
- News-finds-me consumption needs [[AlgorithmicMediaLiteracy]] because users must understand both source reliability and platform routing.

## Connections
- [[IncidentalExposure]] — broader accidental-contact mechanism.
- [[PublicRelevanceAlgorithms]] — platform systems that decide what news becomes visible.
- [[AlgorithmicEntertainmentRedirect]] — competing drift away from public information.
- [[FeedCuration]] and [[AutonomyUnderInformationFlow]] — user-side response when news arrives through feeds rather than deliberate habits.
