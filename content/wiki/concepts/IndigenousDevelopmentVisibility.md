# Indigenous Development Visibility

Indigenous development visibility is the source's claim that a modern, high-density project can express Indigenous presence rather than erase it. In [[the-skyscrapers-that-nimbys-and-zoning-couldnt-stop]], [[JacobLewisIII]] describes the [[SquamishNation|Squamish Nation]]'s towers at [[SinakVillageSite|Sinak]] as a response to years of erasure: visitors should understand whose land they are on.

The concept matters because some criticism in the episode questioned why Indigenous people would build concrete high-rises. The source treats that critique as a legitimacy dispute, not just an aesthetic preference. [[SovereignHousingDevelopment]] becomes culturally visible when architecture, artwork, density, and revenue generation all assert that Indigenous economic life can be contemporary.

## Key Claims
- Cultural visibility can be built into commercial and rental housing, not only museums or traditional-looking structures.
- Modern density does not automatically contradict Indigenous identity in the source's framing.
- The episode treats design motifs, tower form, and public recognition as part of economic development.
- Racialized expectations can narrow what outsiders consider an acceptable Indigenous project.
- Visibility strengthens the link between land return, wealth creation, and public memory.
