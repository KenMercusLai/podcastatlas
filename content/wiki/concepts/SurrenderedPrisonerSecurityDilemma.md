# Surrendered Prisoner Security Dilemma / 杀降安全困境

Surrendered prisoner security dilemma / 杀降安全困境 is the frame [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-3-ljwz8ebk0iyfleivkefvvoonrqb9]] builds around [[BaiQi|白起]]'s killing of surrendered [[ZhaoState|赵国]] troops after the [[ChangpingBattle|长平之战]]. The issue is not whether the act is morally defensible; the episode explicitly acknowledges that later readers condemn Bai Qi as brutal. The analytical question is why a commander would destroy hundreds of thousands of captured labor-age men after winning.

In the source's account, Bai Qi's stated security logic has two parts. [[Shangdang|上党]] had already been made a Qin objective before its people attached themselves to Zhao, and Zhao soldiers were regarded as brave enough that they would not quietly accept captivity. A surrendered army could therefore become a future stability problem if left concentrated, resentful, and militarily experienced.

The dilemma connects moral judgment to post-victory control. Keeping captives preserves manpower and may reduce hatred, but it creates guarding, feeding, relocation, and revolt risks. Killing captives removes the immediate security problem but intensifies [[WarringStatesWarBrutalization|战国战争残酷化]], poisons political legitimacy, and turns military success into remembered atrocity. The source leaves the host's promised "three reasons" undeveloped, so this page stays source-scoped around the explicit stability-versus-morality tension.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-1-baiqi-zhisi-lrqoq1sbxljbvuayq3o1hxebxhu6]] adds the retrospective moral return of the dilemma. At 杜邮, [[BaiQi|白起]] asks why he deserves death and then answers himself by recalling that Zhao surrendered soldiers after [[ChangpingBattle|长平之战]] and he killed them. The episode therefore makes the prisoner-security calculation reappear as self-condemnation: whatever short-term control logic the earlier episode analyzed, the act becomes the moral reason Bai Qi himself cannot deny.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-139-ta-zaici-yingxiang-hanchao-lp1zujuq0o3ohxc79xqi1phpb5zo|Hanji 139]] adds Xiang Yu's late-Qin version at [[XinanMassacre|新安坑降]]. [[XiangYu|项羽]] fears that the large surrendered Qin force, already mistreated by coalition soldiers and anxious about family punishment, may rebel or rejoin Qin near [[HanguPass|函谷关]]. He therefore decides with [[YingBu|英布]] and [[PuGeneral|蒲将军]] to kill the common surrendered soldiers while sparing [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]], [[SimaXin|司马欣]], and [[DongYiQin|董义]]. The episode treats this as a security calculation that succeeds only by creating an unrecoverable [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|people-support]] loss in Qin's old heartland.

## Key Claims
- Surrender does not end a commander's security problem when the surrendered force remains large, cohesive, experienced, and hostile.
- The labor value of captives can conflict with the cost of feeding, guarding, integrating, or dispersing them.
- Deception before killing can solve short-term control while deepening the moral severity and historical memory of the act.
- The concept is narrower than general brutality: it concerns the choice after battlefield victory, not violence required to win the battle itself.
- A security rationale can persist in analysis while still returning as moral guilt in later memory and self-judgment.
- Hanji 139 adds that prisoner killing can divide senior surrendered commanders from their soldiers, solving a camp-control fear while making the victor politically unacceptable to the defeated population.
