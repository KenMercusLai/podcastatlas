# School-Safe CPG Positioning

School-safe CPG positioning is the strategy of building a food product around institutional allergy rules, parent trust, child taste, and social inclusion without letting compliance language become the whole brand. In [[madegood-salma-and-nima-fotovat-lost-their-first-business-they-grew-their-next-one-into-a-snack-giant-cf5715bf-e9e0-45e0-a8a9-1256a7a4ed0d]], [[NimaFotovat]] identifies [[MadeGood]] from the school-snack tension: schools restrict allergens, parents want healthier food, and children still want snacks that feel like treats.

This concept is adjacent to [[DietaryConstraintProductInsight]] but different. The constraint is not only one founder's body or diet; it is a shared institutional setting where one child's allergy changes what every family can send to school.

## Key Claims
- School rules can create a market gap by making ordinary snacks unusable even when families like them.
- The buyer is often the parent, but the user is the child and the gatekeeper may be the school.
- Taste still leads because safety alone does not produce repeat purchase if children reject the product.
- The positioning can generalize beyond families with children when the product also works as an organic, convenient, better-ingredient snack.
- Retailers can recognize the opportunity faster when buyers have direct experience with allergy or school restrictions.
- Owned manufacturing can strengthen the claim because allergen-free trust depends on process control, not only label copy.
