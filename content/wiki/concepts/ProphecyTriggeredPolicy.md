## Definition

Prophecy-triggered policy / 预言触发政策 is the pattern in which a prophecy, omen text, astrological prediction, or fate claim compresses uncertainty into a concrete state action. It is narrower than [[ChenweiPolitics|谶纬政治]] because the concept tracks the policy output: campaign, movement, relocation, purge, office destination, or ritual performance.

## Current Synthesis

The earliest current case is Qin Shi Huang's "亡秦者胡" branch in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8|Qinji 123-1]]. A fangshi-carried warning enters court, Qin Shi Huang reads "Hu" as the northern peoples, and the sign helps frame northern military action. The source keeps hindsight separate: later readers can reinterpret the same word through Hu Hai, but that is not the contemporaneous policy reading.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-1-lgtlhau6ixqglk1ict1fs0jexjwe|Qinji 126 part 1]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-2-ln15p5qb7lc20goni1ibcpmn3wca|Qinji 126 part 2]] move from enemy identification to fate management. "今年祖龙死" points toward Qin Shi Huang's own death, and the divination result "游徙吉" makes movement itself the countermeasure. The policy output becomes household migration toward the northern frontier and the emperor's final tour, not a simple campaign against a named enemy.

The late-Han cases show the mechanism working without a Qin-style immortality setting. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-939-donghan-luanju-de-fengkuang-sanguo-xuqu-lthmvw8ihk96fuez9vamlryzcngy|Hanji 939]] has [[DongFuLateHan|董扶]] redirect [[LiuYanLateHan|刘焉]] from Jiaozhi toward [[YizhouLateHan|益州]] by claiming Yizhou has imperial qi. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-957-ta-ceng-xiongba-yifang-weihe-ruonuo-dao-zai-cesuo-zijin-lnkhah144822mc4aa9bnezklgvoo|Hanji 957]] makes a darker court version: a prediction that a minister will die lets [[DongZhuo|董卓]] convert a vague omen into a named accusation and public killing of [[ZhangWenLateHan|张温]].

The ritual-policy version comes through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-730-zhongguo-baijiaxing-juran-you-diwu-xing-lsm3xean8fzidhhhxi6-ppphexxh|Hanji 730]]. Liu Xiu reads the `Hetu Huichang Fu` line about the "ninth Liu" meeting destiny at Daizong, orders chenwei corroboration collected, and approves Taishan fengshan. The action is not war, exile, or purge; it is a high-cost ritual project that turns restoration legitimacy into imperial travel, sacrifice, document sealing, and era change.

Across the concept, prophecy rarely acts alone. It gives urgency, direction, or public authorization to choices already shaped by frontier pressure, ruler anxiety, office reform, coercive motive, or legitimacy need. The strongest synthesis is therefore interpretive: prophecy can be decisive in policy framing without being the only causal input.

## Key Claims

- A prophecy becomes politically consequential when it identifies an action path, not merely when it predicts an outcome.
- The same warning can generate different policy types: military action, migration, touring, office destination, purge, or ritual.
- Interpretation matters more than wording alone; "胡" can mean northern peoples in the moment and Hu Hai in hindsight.
- Prophecy can intensify practical choices already supported by security, survival, ambition, or legitimacy concerns.
- Hanji 730 adds that a prophecy-triggered policy can be ritual and legitimating rather than defensive or coercive.
- The mechanism is dangerous because vague signs can be narrowed into named targets by the ruler or adviser who controls interpretation.

## Evidence

- Frontier target selection: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8|Qinji 123-1]] says Qin Shi Huang reads "亡秦者胡" as a northern threat and treats the warning as part of the case for action against Xiongnu.
- Personal death warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-1-lgtlhau6ixqglk1ict1fs0jexjwe|Qinji 126 part 1]] says "今年祖龙死" forces Qin Shi Huang to interpret a death prophecy aimed at himself.
- Movement as countermeasure: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-2-ln15p5qb7lc20goni1ibcpmn3wca|Qinji 126 part 2]] says "游徙吉" leads to household migration and the final imperial tour.
- Destination choice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-939-donghan-luanju-de-fengkuang-sanguo-xuqu-lthmvw8ihk96fuez9vamlryzcngy|Hanji 939]] says Dong Fu's Yizhou imperial-qi warning redirects Liu Yan's requested provincial post.
- Purge action: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-957-ta-ceng-xiongba-yifang-weihe-ruonuo-dao-zai-cesuo-zijin-lnkhah144822mc4aa9bnezklgvoo|Hanji 957]] says Dong Zhuo turns a minister-death prediction into an accusation and killing of Zhang Wen.
- Ritual action: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-730-zhongguo-baijiaxing-juran-you-diwu-xing-lsm3xean8fzidhhhxi6-ppphexxh|Hanji 730]] says Liu Xiu's reading of chenwei around the ninth Liu leads to Taishan fengshan, Liangyin sacrifice, amnesty, and era change.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept records policy framing, not proof that prophecy was true or that it was the sole cause. Qinji 123-1 explicitly separates contemporaneous interpretation from later hindsight. Qinji 126 part 2 treats Qin Shi Huang's final tour as a fate-management interpretation, but not as independently proven inner psychology. Hanji 939 shows prophecy routing a career and destination choice inside an already-real institutional reform. Hanji 957 shows how coercive rulers can weaponize vague prediction by naming the target themselves. Hanji 730 shows Liu Xiu's fengshan had old precedent and court advocacy as well as chenwei; the prophecy gave the action legitimacy pressure, not a monocausal explanation.
