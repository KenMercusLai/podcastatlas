# Airport PPP Concession / 机场PPP特许经营

## Definition
An airport PPP concession is a long-term arrangement in which government and private or commercially operated partners divide airport financing, construction, maintenance, operating rights, revenue, and risk, with control commonly reverting at the end of the term.

## Current Synthesis
The episode frames PPP as a response to airport economics: runways, terminals, parking, and related systems require large upfront capital, while returns depend on decades of passenger demand and commercial execution. Governments can obtain finance and professional operation without fully relinquishing a public transport asset, and operators can turn scarce management experience into long-duration revenue. The same duration that enables cost recovery also extends exposure to traffic misses, cost inflation, policy change, financing conditions, and local commercial demand.

## Key Claims
- Airport capital intensity and long payback periods make short contracts poorly matched to project economics.
- PPPs can separate airfield assets from landside terminals, parking, or commercial facilities rather than privatizing an airport as one indivisible unit.
- Mature economies may use concessions to reduce fiscal pressure, while emerging markets may use them to build infrastructure otherwise delayed by funding or operating-capability gaps.
- Private participation transfers some construction, maintenance, operating, and financial risk but does not eliminate public control or public-service obligations.
- Longer contracts can support patient investment while compounding regulatory, political, macroeconomic, demand, and renegotiation risk.

## Evidence
- Historical precedent - [[gaojiesi-dingji-jichang-dao-haiwai-kai-fendian-jichang-yunying-ruhe-bianshen-kuaguo-shengyi-1023323963]] cites the 1987 sale of the British Airports Authority as an early move away from direct public financing and management.
- Asset and responsibility division - [[gaojiesi-dingji-jichang-dao-haiwai-kai-fendian-jichang-yunying-ruhe-bianshen-kuaguo-shengyi-1023323963]] says governments may retain runways and aprons while concessioning terminals and parking.
- Contract duration - [[gaojiesi-dingji-jichang-dao-haiwai-kai-fendian-jichang-yunying-ruhe-bianshen-kuaguo-shengyi-1023323963]] links greater capital commitment to terms commonly longer than 30 years.
- Operator strategy - [[gaojiesi-dingji-jichang-dao-haiwai-kai-fendian-jichang-yunying-ruhe-bianshen-kuaguo-shengyi-1023323963]] says [[IncheonInternationalAirportCorporation]] improved its overseas business after reorganizing around PPP projects.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source emphasizes the rationale more than comparative outcomes. It does not establish that PPP delivery is cheaper, more efficient, or more accountable than public provision, and it gives limited treatment to currency risk, refinancing, labor, monopoly pricing, political renegotiation, failure cases, or conflicts between service quality and investor return.

## What Changed
- Created a dedicated airport-concession concept that separates financing structure from operating capability and commercial execution.
