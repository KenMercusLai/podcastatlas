# Ingredient-Flavor Label Transparency / 食材与风味标签透明度

## Definition
Ingredient-flavor label transparency is the consumer-information boundary between a product containing a named ingredient and a product delivering its recognizable flavor mainly through oils, extracts, powders, seasoning, or manufactured aroma.

## Current Synthesis
The black-truffle case shows why a front label can be semantically true yet still leave consumers with the wrong quantitative impression. Products may contain natural truffle, a tiny amount plus flavor amplification, or mainly truffle-flavored seasoning. Trust depends on making those differences legible enough that consumers can choose based on actual composition rather than the prestige of the largest words on the package.

## Key Claims
- Ingredient presence and flavor presence are different product facts.
- Tiny natural inclusion can coexist with a much stronger front-of-pack ingredient signal.
- Compound flavor systems are not inherently deceptive, but vague presentation shifts the information burden to consumers.
- Premium associations make quantitative ambiguity more commercially consequential.
- Repeated mismatch between impression and formula can damage category trust.

## Evidence
- Formulation range - [[tulasi-kafeidou-yuebing-shupian-dou-dashang-heisonglu-biaoqian-gaoduan-shicai-weihe-biande-wuchubuzai-1016110497]] distinguishes natural truffle, small natural additions plus seasoning, powders, oils, and primarily flavor-based products.
- Quantitative example - [[tulasi-kafeidou-yuebing-shupian-dou-dashang-heisonglu-biaoqian-gaoduan-shicai-weihe-biande-wuchubuzai-1016110497]] cites a comparison in which one product contained about 0.023% natural black truffle.
- Trust risk - [[tulasi-kafeidou-yuebing-shupian-dou-dashang-heisonglu-biaoqian-gaoduan-shicai-weihe-biande-wuchubuzai-1016110497]] argues that consumers cannot infer composition from the front label alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not analyze the governing labeling rules or prove that the cited packages violate them. A small ingredient percentage may still contribute meaningfully alongside aroma compounds, and consumers may knowingly prefer flavor over authenticity. The concept concerns legibility and expectation, not a blanket fraud finding.

## What Changed
- Added black truffle as a case separating named-ingredient content from flavor-system design.
- Added quantitative impression and prestige signaling as consumer-trust variables.
