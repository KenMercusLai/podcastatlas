# Wanbi Guizhao Diplomacy / 完璧归赵式外交

Wanbi Guizhao diplomacy / 完璧归赵式外交 is the diplomatic pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-74-huanyuan-wanbi-guizhao-zhenxiang-lg1irvbbc1wp8oph2kmpatfoxebk]] draws from [[LinXiangru|蔺相如]]'s mission with [[HeShiBi|和氏璧]]. A weaker state accepts a stronger state's formal exchange proposal so it is not blamed for refusing, then uses the stronger state's non-performance to shift liability back to the stronger party.

In the episode, [[ZhaoHe|赵惠文王赵何]] fears both options: refusing [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] may anger [[QinState|秦国]], while accepting may lose the jade without receiving the promised fifteen cities. Lin Xiangru reframes the choice: Zhao should accept, because Qin must then either perform or expose its own bad faith.

The pattern also separates state asset from personal envoy risk. Lin Xiangru sends the jade back to [[ZhaoState|赵国]] through a concealed route while he remains in Qin. The object is preserved, the claim of Qin bad faith is made visible, and the envoy's possible punishment becomes the remaining bargaining and reputation problem.

## Key Claims
- In asymmetric diplomacy, refusing an apparently formal exchange can make the weaker side look unreasonable even when it expects bad faith.
- Accepting can be a way to test performance and move the burden of proof onto the stronger party.
- Protecting the asset and protecting the envoy may require different routes and different risk allocations.
- The strategy works only if the envoy can credibly explain the stronger party's non-performance and survive long enough for the new liability structure to matter.
- The idiom's later moral force depends on this sequence: intact return is not just retrieval, but retrieval after exposing a failed promise.
