# Truth-Default Theory

## Definition

Truth-Default Theory is [[TimLevine]]'s account, as presented in [[the-queen-of-cuba-the-queen-of-cuba|The Queen of Cuba]], that people generally assume others are truthful and leave that state only when accumulated doubt becomes strong enough to trigger disbelief.

## Current Synthesis

The theory separates doubt from a change in judgment. A person can notice inconsistencies, entertain suspicion, and still classify the speaker or situation as basically genuine because each anomaly has an innocent explanation. This tendency makes ordinary communication workable, but it creates vulnerability when a deceiver can supply enough plausible alternatives to prevent suspicions from combining into a decisive pattern.

The Montes case gives the theory institutional stakes. [[RegBrown]] noticed a cluster of warning signs, and [[ScottCarmichael]] detected unusual reactions, yet Montes's prestige, ordinary finances, passed polygraph, corroborated details, and plausible explanations kept espionage below the action threshold. Decoded communications plus searchable travel and system-access records later supplied a specific match that could no longer be explained away as demeanor or coincidence.

## Key Claims

- Interpersonal judgment begins with a practical presumption of truth rather than neutral clue-by-clue inference.
- Suspicion can coexist with continued belief; doubts matter decisively only when they combine into a trigger.
- Plausible alternative explanations can preserve truth-default even after multiple red flags appear.
- Prestige, familiarity, and institutional validation can raise the subjective threshold for believing serious deception.
- Independently verifiable records can provide a stronger trigger than confidence, nervousness, silence, or other behavioral cues.
- Hindsight compresses the original ambiguity and makes previously rationalizable details look conclusive.

## Evidence

### Experimental truth bias

- [[the-queen-of-cuba-the-queen-of-cuba]] reports that observers of Levine's quiz interviews often labeled both truthful and deceptive speakers as truthful, producing much stronger truth recognition than lie recognition.

### Doubt without disbelief

- [[the-queen-of-cuba-the-queen-of-cuba]] uses [[StanleyMilgram]]'s staged shock experiment to show that many participants entertained doubts while very few became certain that the apparent shocks were unreal.

### Counterintelligence case

- [[the-queen-of-cuba-the-queen-of-cuba]] follows Montes's colleagues and investigators as they repeatedly reconciled anomalies with innocence until a database match supplied a discriminating trigger.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The theory is presented through one podcast summary, selected experiments, and a retrospective espionage narrative. The source does not supply Levine's complete model, boundary conditions, methods, replications, cultural variation, or comparison with rival deception theories. A default toward truth does not excuse failures to improve controls, share warnings, preserve audit trails, or investigate high-risk access. It also does not imply that skepticism is always accurate or that every cluster of anomalies warrants accusation.

## What Changed

- Created a synthesis distinguishing noticed doubt from a trigger strong enough to change belief.
- Connected interpersonal truth bias to institutional prestige, alternative explanations, and records-based correction.
