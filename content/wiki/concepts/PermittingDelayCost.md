# Permitting Delay Cost

Permitting delay cost is the source's claim that slow, uncertain approval processes raise housing costs before physical construction begins. In [[the-skyscrapers-that-nimbys-and-zoning-couldnt-stop]], [[PlanetMoney]] cites a working paper by economists at [[MIT]] and [[PrincetonUniversity|Princeton University]] finding that Los Angeles developers paid substantially more for land with permits already attached.

The episode uses that research to explain why [[SovereignHousingDevelopment]] can change project economics. If normal fights, lawsuits, and discretionary approvals raise the price of buildable land, then a project with clearer land-use authority can move faster and preserve more of the site's value for housing and long-term owners.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-adam-carolla-on-californias-collapse-fires-failed-leadership-and-gyno-fascism-39710360]] adds a post-disaster version through [[CaliforniaPostFireRebuildingDelay]]. [[AdamCarolla|Adam Carolla]] argues that [[LosAngeles|Los Angeles]], [[Malibu]], and [[CaliforniaCoastalCommission|California Coastal Commission]] approval paths can make rebuilding after fire so slow that owners leave rather than wait.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-spencer-pratt-on-fixing-la-wildfires-homelessness-corruption-the-fight-to-take-it-back-41236810]] adds a reform proposal through [[PermitAutomationForZonedProjects]]. [[SpencerPratt|Spencer Pratt]] argues that when a project already meets zoning criteria, [[LosAngeles|Los Angeles]] should use AI or automatic approvals to reduce administrative delay for housing, rebuilding, and small businesses.

## Key Claims
- Approval uncertainty can become capitalized into land prices.
- Faster permission is not only administrative convenience; it can change project feasibility and financing.
- The source says Los Angeles permitting delay was estimated to account for about a third of a project's construction costs.
- Permitting cost reinforces [[HousingAffordabilitySupplyMechanics]] by making process speed part of supply.
- The concept does not say all review is waste; it asks when review uncertainty becomes a cost driver large enough to suppress housing.
- The All-In source adds that delay has a recovery-cost dimension: after disaster, slow approval can turn temporary displacement into permanent community loss.
- The Pratt source adds that permit automation is one proposed response, but it still depends on clear zoning data, reliable inspection capacity, and rules for exceptions.
