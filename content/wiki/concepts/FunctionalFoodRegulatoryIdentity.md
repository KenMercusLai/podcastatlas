# Functional Food Regulatory Identity / 功能食品监管身份

## Definition
Functional food regulatory identity is the consumer-literacy practice of asking what a health-coded product legally and practically is before accepting what its marketing implies it can do.

## Current Synthesis
The source's core distinction is that "functional food" is not itself a strict China-facing regulatory category in the way drugs, foods for special medical purposes, special dietary foods, and blue-hat health foods are. That makes product identity the first filter: the same shelf can contain ordinary foods, health foods, special dietary products, clinical nutrition products, and drugs with very different evidence, label, registration, and supervision expectations.

This identity check changes how consumers should read claims. Foods for special medical purposes are framed as professional nutritional support for people with eating, absorption, metabolic, or disease-state needs under doctor or clinical-nutrition guidance. Special dietary foods fit defined life-stage or activity scenarios. Blue-hat health foods speak to health or subhealth needs but still should not be treated as treatment. Ordinary foods and market-branded functional foods need extra skepticism when they borrow medical language without matching the regulatory burden.

## Key Claims
- Product identity should be checked before product promise because foods, health foods, special-use foods, and drugs carry different evidence and use-context burdens.
- Foods for special medical purposes are professional nutritional-support products for specific medical or disease-state contexts, not ordinary wellness foods.
- Special dietary foods remain foods but are formulated for defined populations such as infants, older adults, pregnant people, or exercisers.
- Blue-hat health foods can make health-function claims within their category but should not be treated as disease treatment.
- "Functional food" can work as market shorthand, but its vagueness makes label, evidence, and applicable-population checks more important.
- Regulatory identity does not decide personal need by itself; medication use, disease status, diet pattern, and clinician guidance can still change the decision.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a public podcast summary, not a full legal guide to Chinese food, drug, or health-product regulation. The concept should therefore be used as a first-pass consumer-literacy filter, not as a substitute for checking the current product registration, label, indication, contraindication, or medical advice.

## What Changed
- Added a China-facing product-identity concept for functional foods, special medical-use foods, special dietary foods, blue-hat health foods, ordinary foods, and drugs.
- Clarified that "functional food" is a marketing shorthand unless tied to a specific regulatory identity and use context.
