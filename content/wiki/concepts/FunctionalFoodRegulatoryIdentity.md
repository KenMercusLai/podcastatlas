# Functional Food Regulatory Identity / 功能食品监管身份

## Definition
Functional food regulatory identity is the consumer-literacy practice of asking what a health-coded product legally and practically is before accepting what its marketing implies it can do.

## Current Synthesis
The source's core distinction is that "functional food" is not itself a strict China-facing regulatory category in the way drugs, foods for special medical purposes, special dietary foods, and blue-hat health foods are. That makes product identity the first filter: the same shelf can contain ordinary foods, health foods, special dietary products, clinical nutrition products, and drugs with very different evidence, label, registration, and supervision expectations.

This identity check changes how consumers should read claims. Foods for special medical purposes are framed as professional nutritional support for people with eating, absorption, metabolic, or disease-state needs under doctor or clinical-nutrition guidance. Special dietary foods fit defined life-stage or activity scenarios. Blue-hat health foods speak to health or subhealth needs but still should not be treated as treatment. Ordinary foods and market-branded functional foods need extra skepticism when they borrow medical language without matching the regulatory burden.

A second identity question comes from [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]]: products that would face a slower registration path as health foods in China are instead placed in cosmetics, snacks, drinks, and milk tea as food ingredients, and a foreign supplement can enter China as a personal-use item through cross-border retail rather than as an imported health product. Identity is therefore not only a China-facing classification issue, since the same ingredient can be sold under several regimes and the choice of regime is partly a commercial decision.

## Key Claims
- Product identity should be checked before product promise because foods, health foods, special-use foods, and drugs carry different evidence and use-context burdens.
- Foods for special medical purposes are professional nutritional-support products for specific medical or disease-state contexts, not ordinary wellness foods.
- Special dietary foods remain foods but are formulated for defined populations such as infants, older adults, pregnant people, or exercisers.
- Blue-hat health foods can make health-function claims within their category but should not be treated as disease treatment.
- "Functional food" can work as market shorthand, but its vagueness makes label, evidence, and applicable-population checks more important.
- Regulatory identity does not decide personal need by itself; medication use, disease status, diet pattern, and clinician guidance can still change the decision.

## Evidence
- Category distinction: [[vol-209-bie-wei-gongnengxing-shangtou-ni-yiwei-de-jingzhun-yangsheng-qishi-yue-bu-yue-zao-liao-liao-daicanfen-dianjiezhi-dengdeng-ljlwqq2yhtcsa4r3nvnzaa6p5i9o]] separates drugs, foods for special medical purposes, special dietary foods, blue-hat health foods, ordinary foods, and market-facing functional foods.
- Special medical-use boundary: [[vol-209-bie-wei-gongnengxing-shangtou-ni-yiwei-de-jingzhun-yangsheng-qishi-yue-bu-yue-zao-liao-liao-daicanfen-dianjiezhi-dengdeng-ljlwqq2yhtcsa4r3nvnzaa6p5i9o]] says foods for special medical purposes serve people with eating, digestion, absorption, metabolic, or special disease-state needs under doctor or clinical-nutrition guidance.
- Regulatory hierarchy: [[vol-209-bie-wei-gongnengxing-shangtou-ni-yiwei-de-jingzhun-yangsheng-qishi-yue-bu-yue-zao-liao-liao-daicanfen-dianjiezhi-dengdeng-ljlwqq2yhtcsa4r3nvnzaa6p5i9o]] describes stronger registration and review expectations for foods for special medical purposes than for broader special dietary or blue-hat categories.
- Marketing ambiguity: [[vol-209-bie-wei-gongnengxing-shangtou-ni-yiwei-de-jingzhun-yangsheng-qishi-yue-bu-yue-zao-liao-liao-daicanfen-dianjiezhi-dengdeng-ljlwqq2yhtcsa4r3nvnzaa6p5i9o]] treats "functional food" as a market and consumer-understanding phrase rather than a precise legal class.
- Personal-use boundary: [[vol-209-bie-wei-gongnengxing-shangtou-ni-yiwei-de-jingzhun-yangsheng-qishi-yue-bu-yue-zao-liao-liao-daicanfen-dianjiezhi-dengdeng-ljlwqq2yhtcsa4r3nvnzaa6p5i9o]] warns people who are taking medication or are unhealthy to ask qualified clinicians before adding health products.
- Regime choice as commercial strategy: [[vol-275-aozhou-baojianpin-shi-zenme-huoqilai-de-1015773861]] describes collagen and probiotic ingredients moving into cosmetics, snacks, drinks, and milk tea because the food route is easier than the health-food route, and describes cross-border personal-use classification letting Australian supplements reach Chinese consumers without ordinary import registration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are public podcast summaries, not full legal guides to Chinese, Australian, or cross-border food and health-product regulation. The concept should therefore be used as a first-pass consumer-literacy filter, not as a substitute for checking the current product registration, label, indication, contraindication, or medical advice, and it should not be read as an argument that every regime choice is an evasion - ordinary food categories exist for legitimate reasons too.

## What Changed
- Added a China-facing product-identity concept for functional foods, special medical-use foods, special dietary foods, blue-hat health foods, ordinary foods, and drugs.
- Clarified that "functional food" is a marketing shorthand unless tied to a specific regulatory identity and use context.
- Added the regime-choice layer: the same ingredient can be sold as food, as a health product, or through cross-border personal-use channels, so identity is also a commercial decision.
