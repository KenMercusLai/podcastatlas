# Employee Stock Options / 员工期权

Employee stock options are compensation grants that give employees the right, but not the obligation, to buy company shares or share-linked equity later at a specified exercise price. [[qiquan-zhe-zhang-bing-weishenme-yuelaiyue-nanchi-le-1]] explains them by borrowing the basic choice logic of [[OptionContractMechanics]], then separating employee grants from liquid market options.

In the source, employee options are useful only when the employee can understand and eventually realize the upside. Vesting schedules, expiration, exercise cost, tax treatment, repurchase clauses, private-company transfer restrictions, [[VariableInterestEntity|VIE]] structure, and listing or acquisition timing all decide whether the right becomes money.

## Key Claims
- Employee options are not the same as exchange-traded calls: they are embedded in employment, company plans, and private governance.
- The upside is asymmetric only if the employee can leave the option unexercised when the company value is below the exercise cost.
- Options can recruit talent when cash is scarce and the company has a credible [[StartupTimingWindows|timing window]] or exit path.
- Options can become weak compensation when the employee gives up cash salary for an opaque, illiquid, or revocable future claim.
- The instrument depends on trust: a company that can cheaply avoid, repurchase, or dispute employee grants can turn equity from alignment into [[EmployeeStockOptionLiquidityRisk]].
