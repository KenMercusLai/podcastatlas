# Female Oral Storytelling / 女性口头叙事

Female oral storytelling is the episode 38 frame for taking mothers, grandmothers, and old wives seriously as transmitters of folk knowledge. In [[38-anjila-kate-jingguai-gushiji-o-mirende-hanfu-609607139]], the hosts begin from their own childhood memories of women telling island, rural, western fairy-tale, and strange stories, then use that experience to read [[AngelaCarter|Angela Carter / 安吉拉·卡特]] and [[JingguaiGushiji|《精怪故事集》]].

The concept does not mean every story told by women is automatically feminist or gentle. The source's point is almost the opposite: female transmission can preserve jokes, cruelty, desire, household labor knowledge, anti-husband tricks, child-protection tactics, and everyday survival intelligence that polite literary or religious traditions may suppress.

## Key Claims
- Oral domestic transmission can be a serious knowledge channel even when later books are edited, collected, or published by men.
- Grandmotherly storytelling can carry women's tone, pleasure, mockery, and practical wisdom without needing formal theory.
- The source connects Carter's literary work to this oral lineage: she writes and edits like someone reviving old-wife story power.
- Female oral storytelling overlaps with [[FolkloreAsSocialMemory]] because domestic story preserves work, marriage, danger, children, and bodily life.
