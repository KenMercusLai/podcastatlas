# Stretching and Mobility Safety / 拉伸与灵活性安全

## Definition
Stretching and mobility safety / 拉伸与灵活性安全 is the use of targeted, context-aware stretching, relaxation, and mobility work to reduce tightness and support movement without forcing the body into positions or loads that create injury.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] presents stretching as useful but not simplistic. The doctors verbally encourage stretching, especially as training becomes more regular, longer-running, or higher-intensity, while admitting that people often skip it for time or inertia.

The episode's practical rule is specificity. Runners need more calf, thigh, and glute work; cyclists may need hip-flexor and iliotibial-band release; tennis players should activate and stretch calves and feet; people who sit for long periods may need waist, core, anterior-hip, and posterior-chain attention. Poorly chosen stretching can cause harm, so mobility work remains part of load management rather than a universal cure.

## Key Claims
- Stretching can reduce tightness and soreness for some people, especially when training is regular, long-running, or high-intensity.
- The target muscle group should follow the activity: back training, leg training, running, cycling, tennis, and sedentary work create different mobility demands.
- Long sitting can tighten the front of the body and weaken or lengthen posterior muscles, so mobility needs to be paired with strength and activation.
- Stretching is relevant to overuse patterns such as tennis elbow, calf tightness, hip-flexor tightness, iliotibial-band tension, and lower-limb movement tracking.
- Simple movements such as cat-cow-style stretching can be useful for core and back comfort, but they are not a substitute for diagnosis when pain or injury signs appear.
- Improper stretching can injure tissue, so more force or deeper range is not automatically better.

## Evidence
- Activity specificity: [[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] distinguishes stretching needs for sedentary people, runners, cyclists, tennis players, back training, and leg training.
- Recovery and soreness: [[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] says stretching can relax tense muscles and fascia and may reduce next-day discomfort for some exercisers.
- Overuse mechanism: [[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] uses tennis elbow, calf strain, hip-flexor tightness, iliotibial-band issues, and knee-valgus-related movement tracks as examples where tight or weak tissues matter.
- Stretching risk: [[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] explicitly warns that inappropriate stretching can cause injury.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not settle a universal stretching prescription or claim every exerciser must stretch the same way. Stretching advice is public education, not physical therapy. Pain, swelling, locking, acute injury, neurological symptoms, or persistent functional limitation should not be managed only with stretching.

## What Changed
- VOL.212 adds a mobility-safety concept that treats stretching as targeted recovery and movement preparation, not a generic ritual or guaranteed injury-prevention tool.
