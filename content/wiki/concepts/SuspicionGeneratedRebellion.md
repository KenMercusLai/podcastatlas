## Definition
Suspicion-generated rebellion is a defection pattern in which an actor interprets an ambiguous signal as evidence of distrust, punishment, or exclusion, then rebels before verifying the signal or exhausting communication channels.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] presents [[PangMengEasternHan|Pang Meng]] as a trusted subordinate whose revolt begins inside his own interpretation of an imperial command. [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] had praised and trusted him, yet Pang Meng reads an edict that names Gai Yan rather than himself as proof that ruler and colleague have turned against him. Because he does not clarify the matter, suspicion becomes action: he attacks Gai Yan, joins Dong Xian, and claims kingship.

The pattern differs from ruler-driven elite alienation. The ruler's prior stance is trust, not escalating persecution; the decisive motion comes from the subordinate's unverified inference. It also differs from ordinary command mutiny because the rebellion turns on imagined threat and status interpretation more than tactical disagreement.

## Key Claims
- Ambiguous command signals can become dangerous when high-status actors read omission as impending punishment.
- Prior personal trust is insufficient if the actor treats silence or procedural wording as hidden hostility.
- The failure point is communicative: suspicion hardens because the actor does not seek clarification before acting.
- Rebellion can become self-confirming when the suspected danger is created by the suspicious actor's own defection.
- The pattern helps separate internally generated suspicion from cases where rulers actively force elites into rebellion.

## Evidence
- Trusted baseline: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] reports Liu Xiu's unusually favorable judgment of Pang Meng before the revolt.
- Ambiguous signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] makes an edict naming Gai Yan rather than Pang Meng the trigger for Pang Meng's suspicion.
- Conversion into revolt: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] follows Pang Meng from suspicion to attacking Gai Yan, joining Dong Xian, claiming Dongping kingship, and taking Pengcheng.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The current evidence is one episode; broader use of the concept should require additional cases.
- The episode emphasizes Pang Meng's suspicion, but the exact bureaucratic reason for the omitted command signal is not fully reconstructed.
- This concept should not be merged with all distrust politics: it is about rebellion generated by the suspecting actor's inference, not by a ruler's documented coercion.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Pang Meng's revolt in Hanji 693.
