# Trusted Flagger System

Trusted flagger system is the source's term for regulatory arrangements in which selected organizations receive privileged channels for reporting content to platforms. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-under-secretary-of-state-sarah-b-rogers-on-dismantling-the-censorship-industrial-complex-39828360]] adds the concept through [[SarahBRogers|Sarah B. Rogers]]'s criticism of the [[EuropeanUnionDigitalServicesAct|EU Digital Services Act]].

The concern is not that outside groups should never flag harmful content. It is that privileged status changes the moderation power structure: a report from a recognized actor may be handled faster, weighted more heavily, or interpreted by risk-averse platforms as a regulatory signal rather than ordinary criticism.

## Key Claims
- Trusted status can turn some NGOs into quasi-regulatory moderation actors.
- The legitimacy of a flagging channel depends on transparency, appeal, evidence quality, and whether political disagreement is protected.
- Trusted flaggers can intensify over-removal when platform penalties are high and content standards are vague.
- The mechanism sits between public complaint, private moderation, and state-backed enforcement.
