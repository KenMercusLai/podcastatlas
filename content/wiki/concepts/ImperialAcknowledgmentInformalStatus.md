## Definition

帝王承认与非正式身份 / imperial acknowledgment of informal status is the process by which a ruler's public words, gestures, seating, dress, hospitality, or access privileges make an otherwise private and institutionally ambiguous relationship socially recognizable without creating a regular office or title.

## Current Synthesis

The current case is [[DongYanWesternHan|董偃]]. [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]] can support him through household wealth and proximity, but those resources alone do not settle how outsiders should treat her companion. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] changes that field by asking to see the “主人翁,” raising Dong Yan, granting clothing, permitting him to sit and drink, and treating the couple's arrangement as publicly tolerable.

Recognition here is performative rather than statutory. Liu Piao and Dong Yan first lower themselves through servant clothing, bare-footed apology, prostration, and plain presentation; Wudi can then elevate them through visible clemency and favor. The ritual preserves hierarchy while authorizing an exception.

The effect is real but bounded. The episode says others can no longer lightly despise Dong Yan after the emperor's acknowledgment, yet it does not show that he gains a defined legal status, an office, or durable immunity. Informal recognition should therefore be separated from formal appointment and from permanent protection.

## Key Claims

- A ruler's public address and treatment can make an ambiguous private relationship socially legible without issuing a formal title.
- Ceremonial submission can lower the political cost of granting an exceptional favor by reaffirming the ruler's superior authority.
- Clothing, seating, drinking, bodily gesture, and naming can carry status effects when performed before a court audience.
- Recognition can deter social disrespect while leaving legal category, office, succession, and long-term protection unresolved.
- The mechanism requires evidence of a visible acknowledgment act, not merely private affection or household patronage.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-271-xihan-mishi-wudi-yuemu-yu-yangzi-de-bulunlian-1-loeg6lrncynofm27ekpgtjkodrwf|Hanji 271]] explicitly interprets imperial acknowledgment as the reason other people can no longer dismiss Dong Yan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one popular-history summary and one court-favorite case. The episode interprets “主人翁” as legalization, but the supplied evidence establishes visible favor more clearly than a defined legal category. Exact dialogue, audience sequence, emotional reactions, and the breadth of public response remain source-scoped. Future cases should distinguish acknowledgment from office, ennoblement, marriage recognition, legal immunity, or durable succession rights.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish performative imperial recognition from formal appointment or title.
