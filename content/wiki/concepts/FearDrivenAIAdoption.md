# Fear-Driven AI Adoption / 恐懼驅動的AI採用

## Definition
Fear-driven AI adoption is a self-reinforcing transition in which organizations adopt, invest in, or prepare for AI partly because they fear competitors are already moving and that waiting will leave them structurally behind.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses Hollywood to separate technical inevitability from strategic coordination. Studios, technology companies, and creators may not know which current tools will survive, whether feature-length generation will work, or what audiences will accept. Yet each participant has reason to hedge against being late if others proceed.

When enough actors make the same defensive choice, preparation changes hiring, capital, workflow, and expectations. The future then looks inevitable partly because collective anxiety helped build it. This does not make adoption irrational: experimentation can be prudent under uncertainty. It does mean that “everyone is doing it” is evidence of competitive pressure, not proof of technical maturity or social desirability.

## Key Claims
- Competitive adoption can accelerate before products, business models, or long-term tools are settled.
- Defensive investment can be individually rational while collectively narrowing alternatives.
- Hiring, partnerships, internal platforms, and pilot workflows help produce the trajectory they are meant merely to hedge against.
- Public resistance and private preparation can coexist within the same industry or organization.
- Claims of inevitability can obscure present choices about labor, rights, craft, and acceptable use.

## Evidence
- Unsettled future - [[google-touzi-a24-ai-jiang-chengwei-haolaiwu-de-weilai-faaf14098cca4b99e1dcf1e6f71c3594]] says companies do not know what AI will ultimately bring or whether current tools will still matter in five years.
- Competitive anxiety - [[google-touzi-a24-ai-jiang-chengwei-haolaiwu-de-weilai-faaf14098cca4b99e1dcf1e6f71c3594]] describes Google, Disney, Netflix, A24, and other participants as fearing they will fall behind if others begin positioning first.
- Self-fulfilling coordination - [[google-touzi-a24-ai-jiang-chengwei-haolaiwu-de-weilai-faaf14098cca4b99e1dcf1e6f71c3594]] infers that industry-wide entry motivated by fear can itself make AI film feel unavoidable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not measure decision-makers' motives or prove that fear dominates expected productivity, creative opportunity, investor pressure, or genuine technical conviction. Adoption may remain reversible, uneven, or limited to support functions. The concept explains one acceleration mechanism and should not be used to declare either technological success or universal bad faith.
