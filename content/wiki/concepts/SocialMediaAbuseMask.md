# Social Media Abuse Mask / 社交媒体虐待遮蔽

Social media abuse mask / 社交媒体虐待遮蔽 is the pattern where a curated public feed makes an abusive or terrifying private life harder to see. In [[84-binglu-kuanghua-hao-guniang-ni-zhishi-wangle-yonggan-699443676]], [[AmyButcher|Amy Butcher / 艾米]] recognizes that her own [[Instagram]] showed gardens, bookshelves, handmade gifts, and a peaceful domestic life while she was privately afraid of Dave.

The episode contrasts Amy's feed with [[JoyWiebe|Joy "Joey" Wiebe / Joey]]'s road images. Joey's online presence is presented as sincere, but still partial: it shows strength, snow, trucks, and landscape before Amy learns the full history of marriage, violence, alcohol, and loss behind that strength. The source therefore treats social media as neither pure lie nor reliable evidence; it is a visibility filter.

This concept matters for [[WhyNotLeaveVictimBlaming]] because outsiders may use a person's visible happiness as evidence against their private danger. It also matters for [[PsychologicalAbuseRecognition]] because the victim may feel responsible for maintaining the image that later makes disclosure harder.

## Key Claims
- Curated domestic happiness can make abuse less credible to outsiders and more shameful to disclose.
- A sincere social feed can still omit the conditions that produced the visible courage or beauty.
- Public feminist, professional, or aesthetic identity does not immunize a person against private control.
- Social media evidence needs witnesses and context, not automatic belief or automatic dismissal.
