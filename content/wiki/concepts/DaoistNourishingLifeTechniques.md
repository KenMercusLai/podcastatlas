# Daoist Nourishing-Life Techniques / 道教养生术

[[shan-shang-shan-xia-jie-renjian-wushi-xiaoyao-ji-shenxian-lhjiguq74flhatdxnbrnzhjg-acp]] adds a contemporary Wudang version through [[LiChuang|李闯]]. The episode mentions varied practices at [[WudangMountain|武当山]] - recitation, sitting, internal alchemy, fasting, breathing, daoyin, martial practice, and even running-as-practice - while emphasizing that practitioners usually do not force a single method on others because cultivation is ultimately self-verified.

Daoist nourishing-life techniques / 道教养生术 capture the episode's account of 守一, 行气, 胎息, and 导引 in [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-jie-shangji-704051550]]. The source stresses that these practices are not presented merely as wellness routines; their larger aim is becoming an immortal, with ordinary health improvement treated as secondary.

The concept links body practice to [[FangshuSystematization]]. Breathing, visualization, internal unity, and movement are made meaningful through a cosmology of spirits, qi, and transcendence.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-171-zhangliang-jiao-ni-ruhe-mingli-shuangshou-1-lptgy7wxwsve8ljt79asetubin41|Hanji 171 part 1]] adds an early Han political use case through [[ZhangLiang|张良]]. The episode says Zhang Liang studies Daoist arts, meditates, reduces grain eating, and stays secluded after [[LiuBang|刘邦]] moves the capital to [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]]. Here nourishing-life language is not only about body or immortality; it helps stage a safe exit from founding power through [[TranscendentExitPoliticalCamouflage|假托神仙式权力退场]].

## Key Claims
- Health and longevity practices can have a religious goal beyond ordinary bodily maintenance.
- Body discipline becomes a route for holding spirit, breath, and cosmic order together.
- The source places practices such as Mawangdui daoyin images and Hua Tuo's five-animal exercises inside a longer Daoist cultivation frame.
- Hanji 171 part 1 adds that visible cultivation can also lower political exposure when a high-merit actor needs to look detached from power.
- The Wudang source adds a present-day pluralism: practice may appear as recitation, breath, sitting, internal alchemy, movement, martial exercise, or ordinary bodily discipline.
