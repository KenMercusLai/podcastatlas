# Selective Doctrine Enforcement

## Definition

Selective doctrine enforcement is the unequal application of an institution's teachings or sanctions to comparable members, making consistency, salience, power, and belonging part of the moral question rather than treating doctrine alone as a complete explanation.

## Current Synthesis

In [[the-standard-case-the-standard-case|The Standard Case]], [[JamesMartinJesuit]] compares the treatment of LGBT Catholics with the treatment of Catholics who use contraception, pursue in vitro fertilization, or fail obligations toward the poor. The comparison does not establish that every teaching or circumstance is identical. It asks why one departure can trigger dismissal or exclusion while other departures remain compatible with ordinary membership and employment.

Martin's baptismal-belonging argument shifts the baseline: LGBT Catholics are already members of the Church, not outsiders seeking discretionary admission. Enforcement therefore has to be judged through its pattern, practical effects, and treatment of whole persons as well as through the rule invoked in an individual case.

The concept joins consistency with accompaniment. Attention to prayer, friendship, service, suffering, and community prevents sexuality from becoming a totalizing category, while institutional comparison tests whether a sanction expresses a general norm or a selectively intensified burden.

## Key Claims

- Stating a doctrine does not explain why sanctions differ across members who depart from institutional teaching.
- Comparison across cases can reveal that salience, stigma, power, or visibility influences enforcement.
- Existing membership changes the question from discretionary welcome to the terms on which belonging is recognized or withdrawn.
- Whole-person attention resists reducing an individual to the characteristic targeted by enforcement.
- Consistency analysis can expose unequal treatment without independently settling the underlying doctrine or requiring identical sanctions for materially different cases.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Consistency does not require treating unlike roles, conduct, publicity, contracts, or institutional responsibilities as identical. The supplied summary does not provide particular employment cases, governing law, full Catholic doctrine, or the strongest institutional defense of differential enforcement. Comparison identifies a burden of justification; it does not by itself establish unlawful discrimination, doctrinal error, or the proper remedy.

## What Changed

- Established cross-teaching comparison as a test for unequal institutional sanctions.
- Added baptismal belonging and whole-person attention to the enforcement analysis.
- Preserved the distinction between identifying inconsistency and resolving doctrine or law.
