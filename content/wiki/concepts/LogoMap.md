# Logo Map

## Definition

A logo map is a simplified mental image of a territory that makes some places central while omitting, shrinking, displacing, or distorting others.

## Current Synthesis

The episode applies the idea to [[StatenIsland]], which is formally part of New York City but often absent from residents' imagined map or shown separately and out of scale. That symbolic geography places the borough “off screen” even when legal and physical connections remain intact.

The concept helps link representation to burden. Fresh Kills became a metaphor for putting unwanted city material where it would remain psychologically out of sight, while secession offered a new map with Staten Island at its own center. A logo map therefore does not merely describe cartographic error: it names a hierarchy of attention that can shape belonging, service expectations, and resentment.

Mental centrality is not proof of objective neglect, and redrawing a boundary cannot automatically repair infrastructure or recover a lost landscape. The concept is most useful alongside institutional and material evidence rather than as a self-sufficient explanation.

## Key Claims

- Mental maps simplify territory by privileging some places and marginalizing others.
- Formal inclusion does not guarantee symbolic visibility or experienced belonging.
- Cartographic displacement can express a broader hierarchy of attention and burden.
- A politically peripheral community may seek a map on which it becomes the center.
- Symbolic exclusion should be tested against material, institutional, and internal-distribution evidence.

## Evidence

### Staten Island as an omitted periphery

- [[the-staten-island-problem-part-2-a-new-map-the-staten-island-problem-part-2-a-new-map]] describes Staten Island as missing, separated, or distorted in imagined city maps and connects that invisibility to Fresh Kills and the desire for secession.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supplies one interpretive urban case rather than a comparative study of mental maps. Being omitted from a simplified image does not by itself establish fiscal deprivation, political powerlessness, or agreement within the omitted place. A new jurisdiction can reproduce its own center-periphery hierarchy, and symbolic visibility cannot substitute for service capacity, equal voting rights, or minority protection.

## What Changed

- Established the logo map as a mechanism linking imagined geography to political belonging.
- Bounded symbolic invisibility against claims requiring material or institutional evidence.
