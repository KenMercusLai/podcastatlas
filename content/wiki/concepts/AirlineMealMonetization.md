# Airline Meal Monetization

## Definition
Airline meal monetization is the strategy of turning onboard food from a bundled cost item into a priced option, member benefit, preference-data source, or loyalty signal.

## Current Synthesis
The new episode frames paid meals as a partial answer to why airlines keep investing in food even when passengers say price and baggage allowance matter more in purchase decisions. If a baseline meal remains free, optional paid meals can let airlines segment willingness to pay, test route-level preferences, and attach food to loyalty or membership systems.

The risk is fairness. If airlines push resources toward paid products and premium cabins while weakening economy baseline service, meal monetization can erode the same brand memory and future loyalty it is supposed to strengthen.

## Key Claims
- Paid meals can convert part of catering from a pure cost center into ancillary revenue.
- Meal choice data can help airlines understand route, cabin, and customer-segment preferences.
- The strategy works best when free baseline service remains credible and the paid offer feels incremental.
- Meal monetization sits between [[AirlineUnbundling]] and [[AirlineServiceDifferentiation]]: it can sell choice without making the base product feel stripped down.
- Economy-cabin fairness matters because economy passengers still provide large revenue and can become future loyal customers.

## Evidence
- Paid-meal launch: [[nan-yi-yingxiang-goupiao-juece-de-feijican-weihe-chengwei-hangsi-shengji-zhongdian-1009809273]] says [[AirChina]] and [[ChinaEasternAirlines]] were among Chinese carriers adding paid meal categories priced from RMB 19 to more than RMB 200.
- Cost-to-revenue logic: [[nan-yi-yingxiang-goupiao-juece-de-feijican-weihe-chengwei-hangsi-shengji-zhongdian-1009809273]] cites an industry estimate that paid meals can carry gross margins around 20-30%, above overall passenger-business margins.
- Loyalty and data: [[nan-yi-yingxiang-goupiao-juece-de-feijican-weihe-chengwei-hangsi-shengji-zhongdian-1009809273]] links meal choice to repeat purchase, recommendation, member rights, mileage redemption, and route-level taste data.
- Fairness boundary: [[nan-yi-yingxiang-goupiao-juece-de-feijican-weihe-chengwei-hangsi-shengji-zhongdian-1009809273]] notes the industry statement that domestic airlines had not cancelled free economy meals, framing paid food as an extra choice rather than a replacement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's margin estimate and paid-meal adoption details are media and industry claims, not audited unit economics. Paid meals can also backfire if passengers read them as degradation of included service rather than optional upgrade. Short-haul routes may have weaker meal willingness to pay than long-haul flights.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to capture meal-specific ancillary revenue without folding it fully into airline unbundling.
- Added member benefits and route preference data as monetization mechanisms.
- Added the economy-baseline fairness constraint.
