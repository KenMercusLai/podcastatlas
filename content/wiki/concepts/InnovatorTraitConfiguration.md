## Definition
Innovator trait configuration is [[MarkAndreessen|Marc Andreessen]]'s source-scoped heuristic that breakthrough builders often combine openness, conscientiousness, intelligence, disagreeableness, and enough emotional stability to sustain difficult work under rejection and uncertainty.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is best treated as a configuration rather than a founder checklist. Openness supplies unusual possibilities, conscientiousness sustains execution, disagreeableness permits norm resistance, intelligence supports difficult reasoning, and emotional stability makes conflict survivable. The same traits can also damage relationships, judgment, or organizations, and outcomes still depend on context, resources, teams, culture, and luck.

## Key Claims
- Innovation may depend on interacting traits whose value is conditional rather than universally positive.
- Disagreeableness can protect non-consensus judgment but can also become needless conflict or refusal to update.
- Conscientiousness converts ideas into sustained work, while openness without execution may remain novelty seeking.
- Emotional stability matters because rejection, uncertainty, public criticism, and repeated failure create prolonged stress.
- Trait profiles do not determine achievement; social context, opportunity, collaborators, and life choices shape what becomes possible.

## Evidence
- Proposed configuration: [[how-risk-taking-innovation-artificial-intelligence-transform-human-experience-marc-andreessen-scim7464698870]] records Andreessen naming openness, conscientiousness, disagreeableness, intelligence, and relatively low neuroticism as a recurring founder pattern.
- Context and cost: [[how-risk-taking-innovation-artificial-intelligence-transform-human-experience-marc-andreessen-scim7464698870]] also says traits are partly innate but not destiny, and connects extreme drive and risk tolerance to relationship strain, ostracism, and personal collapse.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The bounded source supplies investor observation rather than representative personality research. Successful founders are visible after selection, while failed people with similar traits and successful innovators with different profiles are less salient. The model may also understate teams, capital access, institutional privilege, timing, ethics, and the possibility that interpersonal restraint improves rather than inhibits innovation.

## What Changed
- Created a qualified configuration model from the episode's founder-personality discussion.
- Separated useful norm resistance from indiscriminate conflict and trait description from causal proof.
