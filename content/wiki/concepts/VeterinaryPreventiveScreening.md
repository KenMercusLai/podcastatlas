# Veterinary Preventive Screening / 宠物预防性体检

## Definition
Veterinary preventive screening is the examination and testing of an apparently well companion animal to identify otherwise hidden disease or establish a baseline before obvious symptoms appear.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes preventive screening from diagnostic investigation after vomiting, diarrhea, appetite change, or another complaint. The same blood, imaging, blood-pressure, or physical-examination components may appear in both settings, but their purpose and expected yield differ. Screening asks whether clinically important disease may be present despite ordinary behavior; diagnostic workup asks which explanation best fits an observed problem.

Value does not follow automatically from a larger or more expensive package. Animals may conceal illness and cannot narrate subtle changes, making systematic examination and selected tests useful, but equipment quality, handling stress, clinician sensitivity, species and age, prior findings, and a plan for follow-up all affect usefulness. An incidental finding can materially improve an individual outcome without proving that one protocol benefits every animal.

## Key Claims
- Preventive screening and symptom-led investigation can share tests while serving different clinical questions.
- Animals' limited symptom reporting and tendency to conceal illness create a rationale for periodic observation and selected testing.
- Physical examination, history, prior results, species, age, and risk context should shape test selection.
- Measurement conditions matter because fear and handling can distort blood pressure, heart rate, and other observations.
- A normal result can narrow current concern or establish a baseline, but it is time-bounded rather than a guarantee of future health.
- Incidental tumor detection demonstrates possible benefit, not a universal schedule or proof that maximal testing has positive net value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides clinician cases rather than comparative screening trials or a species-specific guideline. It does not establish which tests, intervals, ages, breeds, or risk thresholds optimize benefit, cost, false positives, stress, sedation, radiation, or downstream intervention. Normal results can miss early disease, while broad testing can create incidental findings and additional burden. Individual plans require qualified veterinary judgment.

## What Changed
- Established a veterinary-specific distinction between preventive screening and symptom-led diagnostic investigation.
- Made handling stress, clinician sensitivity, and follow-up part of screening quality.
- Preserved incidental early detection as possible benefit without inferring a universal package.
