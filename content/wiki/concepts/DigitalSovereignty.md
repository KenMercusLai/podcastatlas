# Digital Sovereignty

[[DigitalSovereignty]] is the claim that a country, institution, or organization needs meaningful control over its data, AI infrastructure, legal jurisdiction, and technology dependencies. In [[ep-47-the-ai-pioneer-who-decided-privacy-matters-more-than-hype]], [[JonathanSchaeffer]] uses Canada and Europe to argue that reliance on mostly U.S. AI companies creates a sovereignty problem when sensitive data, models, or compute must stay under local control.

The concept extends individual [[LocalPrivateAI]] into an institutional and national frame. A local product such as [[KindPrivateAI]] can keep one user's data off the internet, but sovereignty also depends on where infrastructure runs, which contracts govern it, who can access it, and whether policy can be enforced inside the relevant jurisdiction.

## Key Claims
- Digital sovereignty is not only data residency; it includes governance over AI tooling, infrastructure, legal agreements, and operational dependency.
- Cloud AI dependence can conflict with privacy, procurement, compliance, and national strategy when critical data is processed outside a trusted boundary.
- The concept connects to [[AIGovernanceAndCompliance]] because legal obligations are only enforceable when the deployment path is clear enough to audit and control.
- EP47 adds a Canadian and European version of the broader wiki theme that AI infrastructure dependence can become strategic dependence.
