# Livestream Persona Labor / 直播人设劳动

Livestream persona labor is the work of testing, performing, and revising a public self in response to audience attention, platform signals, and commercial trust needs. In [[ep247-chongqi-rensheng-dang-nongcun-zhongnian-nvxing-kaishi-zuo-zhubo-loz8q4e-2azwdzuqzxaj9b4i5dwj]], [[ZengXin|曾欣]] describes rural women streamers moving among beauty performance, "battle clothes," plain village-product credibility, field scenes, and wife-mother reliability.

The concept extends [[PlatformFeedbackLoop]] into embodied and gendered labor. A streamer is not only uploading content; she is learning what kind of woman the audience, platform, family, and buyers will reward. This can create [[FemaleSelfPossession|self-confidence]] through practice, but it can also reproduce [[FemaleBeautySelfSurveillance|beauty surveillance]] and family respectability tests.

## Key Claims
- Persona work begins before professionalization: makeup, clothing, camera angle, voice, and scripted stories are already labor.
- Rural-product selling can reward a plain "village woman" image because authenticity becomes part of product trust.
- Wife-and-mother performance can make goods feel safe, but it also folds commercial credibility back into domestic gender roles.
- Audience response changes the persona over time, so the public self is partly co-produced by comments, traffic, orders, and platform ranking.
- Persona switching gives streamers agency, but not total freedom; the available personas are constrained by gender, age, region, beauty norms, and buyer expectations.
