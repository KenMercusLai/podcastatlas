# 叶公好龙 / Ye Gong Hao Long

叶公好龙 / Ye Gong Hao Long enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-2-zanmen-dou-yuanwang-le-beifu-2000-duonian-heiguo-de-yegong-haolong-lh-h-gezpqtvnyq40zlqac8s-5e]] as the idiom that makes [[YeGong|叶公]] famous in a negative way. In common use, the story turns him into a person who claims to love dragons but panics when a real dragon appears, making him a symbol of performative or insincere liking.

The episode reverses that reception. It first presents Ye Gong as the official who correctly reads [[BaiGongSheng|白公胜]], suppresses the [[BaiGongShengRebellion|白公胜之乱]], restores [[ChuHuiwang|楚惠王]], and then withdraws from power. It then treats the idiom's source in [[LiuXiang|刘向]]'s [[XinXu|《新序》]] as a later moralizing anecdote whose cultural success damaged the memory of a capable historical figure.

This concept extends [[IdiomOriginSkepticism|典故来源辨伪]] from origin uncertainty to reputation distortion. A vivid story can be useful as moral instruction while still being a poor guide to the person named in it.

## Key Claims
- Idiom fame can overwrite historical biography.
- A moralizing anecdote should not automatically be treated as evidence about the named figure's real character.
- In this episode, Ye Gong's recorded political conduct cuts against the hypocrite image created by the idiom.
- Source criticism should ask what the story is trying to teach, not only whether the story is memorable.
