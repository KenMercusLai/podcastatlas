## Definition
中国足球外援政策 / Chinese football foreign-player policy is the roster-rule question of how many foreign players Chinese clubs may register and field, and how that interacts with salary caps, squad depth, injury risk, competitive quality, and domestic-player minutes.

## Current Synthesis
The sources favor a larger registration pool while retaining a lower matchday limit and salary discipline. The goal is not to reopen a golden-yuan spending race; it is to give clubs more injury tolerance, tactical options, affordable talent access, and continental squad depth while preserving real match opportunities for domestic players. Vol.227 adds the stronger but still unproven claim that better foreign-player competition can improve local development rather than automatically crowding it out.

## Key Claims
- A five-foreign-player registration limit makes clubs overly vulnerable to one or two injuries.
- Registering six while fielding five can improve squad stability without necessarily raising salary budgets.
- Mid-sized and smaller clubs could benefit by finding cheaper foreign players rather than only chasing high-priced names.
- The policy should be linked to expansion and tier quality so domestic-player opportunity is counted by real match minutes, not nominal roster size.
- Position and salary allocation matter: expensive foreign-player slots may be more valuable in central or core attacking roles than in every role equally.
- Continental competition makes registration depth more valuable because rotation and injury can otherwise expose a shallow squad.

## Evidence
- Injury tolerance: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] says "register five, play five" leaves relegation outcomes too exposed to foreign-player health.
- Salary-cap framing: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] discusses expanding foreign-player registration without expanding the salary space.
- Club examples: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] uses [[ShandongTaishan]] and Qingdao Hainiu examples to discuss role value and affordable foreign-player markets.
- Domestic-player linkage: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] argues that foreign-player rules, expansion, and real domestic-player minutes should be designed together.
- Competition-quality claim: [[vol-227-mubiao-yazhou-yaguan-yu-yayun-women-gai-renzhen-ma-ft-fengzhen-gkwrijiohmz4aubqtwtatc1u]] argues that affordable foreign players can raise physical, tactical, and competitive demands on local players rather than necessarily blocking development.
- Continental depth: [[vol-227-mubiao-yazhou-yaguan-yu-yayun-women-gai-renzhen-ma-ft-fengzhen-gkwrijiohmz4aubqtwtatc1u]] connects added foreign-player options to stronger rotation and AFC competition performance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide formal rule text, a league-wide injury dataset, causal evidence on domestic-player development, or a cost model. Liberalization depends on salary discipline, stronger [[ChineseLeagueOne]] quality, expansion, and real domestic-player minutes; otherwise it could still reduce local opportunity or restart harmful spending.
