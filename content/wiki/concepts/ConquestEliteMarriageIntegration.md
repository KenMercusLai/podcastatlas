# Conquest Elite Marriage Integration / 征服后联姻整合

## Definition
Conquest elite marriage integration is the use of a ruler's marriage to create a kinship bridge into the personnel and status network of a newly incorporated region.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1146 applies the mechanism to [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s marriage to [[LadyWuLiuBei|吴氏]] after the conquest of Yizhou. Lady Wu is [[WuYiLateHan|吴懿]]'s sister and the widow of Liu Mao, which gives the marriage connections to both a regional elite and the former ruling household. The episode therefore treats marriage as part of political consolidation rather than an isolated personal choice.

This pattern differs from [[PoliticalMarriageAllianceHedging|政治联姻式同盟对冲]]. The latter reinforces cooperation between still-distinct powers; conquest integration operates inside a new regime and seeks to reduce the distance between conqueror and incorporated elites. Neither mechanism guarantees consent, durable loyalty, or resolution of competing interests.

## Key Claims
- Conquest creates a personnel-integration problem after battlefield victory ends.
- Marriage can turn an incumbent elite connection into an affinal relationship with the new ruler.
- A widow's prior household ties can add political reach while also generating ritual or kinship objections.
- Historical precedent can be used to reframe a propriety concern as compatible with regime consolidation.
- The strategic value assigned to marriage does not recover the transferred woman's preferences or prove elite loyalty.

## Evidence
- Regional bridge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] connects Lady Wu through Wu Yi to the former Liu Zhang network.
- Former-household connection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] identifies Lady Wu as Liu Mao's widow and treats that history as politically relevant.
- Legitimacy argument: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1146-liubei-qu-guafu-beihou-de-suanji-taishenle-lv4if2rvouqruziv8pt-klrcasfp|Hanji 1146]] says Fa Zheng invokes Duke Wen of Jin to answer Liu Bei's propriety concern.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one ruler-centered podcast account. It does not establish Lady Wu's consent, Wu Yi's actual influence, the marriage negotiation, contemporary public response, or a causal effect on Yizhou stability. The Duke Wen story is a justificatory analogy, not a complete legal or ritual ruling. Kinship may create access while leaving factional, regional, and household conflicts intact.

## What Changed
- Established conquest-stage marriage integration as distinct from alliance hedging between autonomous powers.
- Made the missing evidence about Lady Wu's agency explicit.
