# Unemployment Stigma / 失业污名

Unemployment stigma is the process by which joblessness becomes evidence of defective character, ability, effort, or stability. In [[161-zuanzhe-wenping-shiye-wo-zenme-pa-chu-zhege-xianjing-933696202]], [[QinZong|秦总]] argues that the unemployed person may be blamed by employers, family, acquaintances, and eventually by themselves, even when the original job loss came from market conditions, organizational decisions, or opaque screening.

The concept connects social judgment to hiring mechanics. [[OpaqueHiringMarket]] lets employers and referrers imagine hidden reasons for unemployment, while [[LongTermUnemploymentPenalty]] makes time itself look like evidence. The result is a loop where the person has fewer chances precisely because the lack of chances is treated as suspicious.

[[ep257-bu-shangban-yi-chang-taoli-yu-xunzhao-de-fuza-maoxian-li-wflyd48dmxvgersphxfp-gnng]] adds the voluntary-gap and health-gap version. The source shows that even when time away from work comes from rest, burnout, treatment, or exploration, job seekers may still expect HR to read the gap as a defect, pushing them toward more respectable narratives such as entrepreneurship.

## Key Claims
- Stigma turns a temporary employment state into a moral diagnosis.
- Layoff and rejection narratives can manufacture blame even when the cause is organizational finance, management, or market weakness.
- Family members may reproduce the stigma through advice, criticism, or contradictory pressure to both lower and preserve status.
- Children and partners can absorb the stigma, treating a parent's unemployment as household failure rather than a temporary condition.
- [[UnemployedMutualAid]] counters stigma by making job-search difficulty visible as a shared condition, not a private defect.
- EP257 adds that [[ResumeGapStigma|resume-gap stigma]] can attach to illness treatment and voluntary pauses, not only to involuntary unemployment.
