# Sleep As Resistance / 睡眠作为抵抗

Sleep as resistance is [[ep258-women-ruhe-chongshi-shuiqian-dushu-lobyzxqk2oaov0v51cm355u8noix]]'s political and bodily extension of the bedtime-reading topic. The speakers move from Shenzhen's high-tech urban setting to the fact that intelligent systems, platforms, and productivity schedules still rely on human bodies that must stop, rest, and sleep.

The source links this to the book 24/7 and to the idea that sleep is time fully one's own. Unlike [[SleepAsDailyHealthAccount]], which emphasizes recovery inputs and next-day health, this concept emphasizes the limit that sleep places on capture: the person cannot be endlessly optimized, contacted, stimulated, or turned into productive output.

## Key Claims
- Sleep is not only passive shutdown; it is a bodily boundary against continuous productivity and stimulation.
- High-tech systems still depend on flesh, fatigue, repair, and downtime.
- Bedtime reading can become part of reclaiming a private nightly space, but only if it supports rest rather than extends work.
- Sleep exposes the limit of attention economies because the sleeping person stops responding.
- Protecting sleep is also protecting the conditions for attention, mood, and self-relation the next day.
