# Hong Kong Public-Resource Privatization / 香港公共資源私有化

## Definition
Hong Kong public-resource privatization is the source's frame for Tung-era land, transport, and housing-estate asset decisions that shifted public resources toward marketized or developer-led arrangements.

## Current Synthesis
The episode connects property-market management to public-resource politics. After property prices collapsed, [[TungCheeHwa|董建華]]'s administration is described as cancelling active land sales and moving to a developer-led application-list system. The same legacy is tied to [[MTRCorporation|MTR]] listing and selling estate malls to [[LinkREIT|Link REIT]], all of which the source treats as later grievance foundations.

## Key Claims
- Land-supply policy after the property crash is presented as increasing developer control over supply.
- MTR listing and estate-mall privatization are treated as public-resource marketization.
- These choices are framed as later sources of livelihood resentment in Hong Kong.
- The concept connects housing affordability to governance legitimacy rather than only to market cycles.

## Evidence
- Land policy: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] says Tung cancelled active land sales and used a developer-led application-list system.
- Infrastructure and estate assets: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] names MTR listing and sale of estate malls to Link REIT.
- Public grievance: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] treats these decisions as important sources of later public discontent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source sketches the policy legacy quickly. It does not model land revenue, fiscal constraints, MTR governance, estate-mall management, or alternative housing-supply counterfactuals.

## What Changed
- Created the public-resource concept for the Tung-era policy legacy.
