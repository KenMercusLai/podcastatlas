# Workplace Metric Gaming

## Definition

Workplace metric gaming is behavior that improves a measured score while weakening, bypassing, or merely simulating the underlying outcome the score was intended to represent.

## Current Synthesis

The source set now spans cashier speed, hospital waiting time, and coffee-store operations. In each case, a simple, consequential measure makes adaptation rational: an item can pass unscanned, a queue can move outside the measured window, or a drink can be scanned before completion. Additional monitoring may catch one tactic without repairing the staffing, timing, or incentive conflict that produced it.

The [[LuckinCoffee|瑞幸咖啡]] case also shows that workers can face multiple incompatible targets. Speed, customer satisfaction, hygiene, waste, early-scan detection, and regional rank all remain active while staffing falls. Gaming is therefore not always a worker seeking an easy advantage; it can be a local survival response to a system that demands mutually difficult outcomes without enough resources.

## Key Claims
- Metric gaming becomes more likely when a simple measure carries pay, status, discipline, ranking, or workload consequences.
- Gaming can be rational for workers even when it damages the organization's stated service goal.
- Multiple targets can conflict, making full compliance infeasible under the available time and staffing.
- Monitoring a known tactic can displace adaptation into another tactic without correcting the underlying constraint.
- Strong measurement needs outcome checks, exception review, qualitative judgment, and attention to hidden labor or queues.

## Evidence

### Narrow speed and waiting-time targets

- [[the-laws-of-the-office-revisited]] describes a cashier letting an unscanned item pass to protect items-per-minute performance and a British hospital moving bottlenecks outside the measured waiting window.

### Conflicting frontline metrics

- [[ai-guanli-ruixing-kafeidian-yuangong-weishenme-yuelaiyue-lei-594bf8ad2a57f9c85fe5c47c73d71461]] reports that staff could scan drinks early to protect timeliness, after which monitoring targeted early scanning while speed, satisfaction, hygiene, waste, and ranking pressure remained.
- [[ai-guanli-ruixing-kafeidian-yuangong-weishenme-yuelaiyue-lei-594bf8ad2a57f9c85fe5c47c73d71461]] also describes repeated cleaning and presentation work used to protect store data or inspection impressions under reduced staffing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The cases are illustrative rather than prevalence estimates, and the Luckin evidence comes from former employees without company records or response. Apparent gaming can sometimes be a legitimate workaround for a badly defined process, while a changed measure may genuinely improve service. The concept should direct attention to incentives, constraints, and real outcomes rather than presume dishonesty whenever measured behavior changes.
