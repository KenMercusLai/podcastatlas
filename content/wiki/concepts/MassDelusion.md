# Mass Delusion

## Definition

Mass delusion is the season-three trailer's umbrella label for situations in which a group becomes strongly convinced of a false belief while participants remain unable to recognize the error and difficult to reach through reminders, evidence, or education.

## Current Synthesis

[[introducing-revisionist-history-season-three|Introducing Revisionist History Season Three]] presents mass delusion as an investigative question rather than a settled mechanism. Its montage places disputed data, collective illness, institutional failure, and individual harm under one promotional theme, suggesting that false certainty can become consequential when it is socially reinforced or embedded in authority.

The useful current judgment is therefore narrow. The trailer identifies a family of problems involving collective conviction and resistance to correction, but it does not show that the previewed cases share one cause. “Mass delusion” should not collapse distinct phenomena such as misinformation, motivated reasoning, panic, sociogenic illness, bureaucratic incentives, or reasonable disagreement under uncertainty.

## Key Claims

- A false collective belief can be difficult for participants to recognize from inside the group.
- Repetition of evidence or education does not guarantee correction once a belief is socially or institutionally reinforced.
- Collective certainty can harm individuals when it shapes official judgment, public accusation, or crisis response.
- Similar-looking resistance to correction may arise through different psychological, social, informational, or institutional mechanisms.
- The trailer announces this problem family but does not provide enough evidence to validate a general causal theory.

## Evidence

### Announced problem and resistance to correction

- [[introducing-revisionist-history-season-three]] reports Gladwell naming mass delusion as the season's subject and describing affected people as unaware of their condition despite reminders or education.

### Breadth and consequence

- [[introducing-revisionist-history-season-three]] previews rejected data, poisoned schoolchildren, victims of mass hysteria, institutional failures, and a heroic responder, placing both collective belief and personal harm within the announced theme.

### Evidentiary boundary

- [[introducing-revisionist-history-season-three]] is a short promotional montage that withholds the cases' full arguments, sources, and conclusions, so it supports a research agenda rather than a unified explanation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The bounded source is a trailer summarized in a secondary document, not a transcript, clinical account, social-psychology study, or comparative investigation. It does not establish that any featured belief was false, measure resistance to correction, identify a transmission mechanism, or show that education generally fails. The term “delusion” also carries clinical associations that the trailer does not assess; it should not be used to diagnose individuals or to dismiss dissent merely because a belief is unpopular. Disagreement can reflect incomplete evidence, competing values, different incentives, or justified mistrust rather than collective irrationality.
