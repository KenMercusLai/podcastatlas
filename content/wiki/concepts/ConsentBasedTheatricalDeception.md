# Consent-Based Theatrical Deception

## Definition
Consent-based theatrical deception is a bounded performance agreement in which audiences authorize an entertainer to mislead perception or explanation for artistic experience without authorizing unrelated coercion, fraud, humiliation, unsafe risk, or exploitation.

## Current Synthesis
Wind describes the magician as having a “license to lie” because the audience knowingly enters a role-structured encounter with illusion. Spectators know the event is staged yet still seek a moment that feels real, impossible, or unexplained. Preserving mystery can therefore be part of the agreed artistic value rather than a failure to provide information.

A service-and-harm test further narrows that permission. Plants, editing, special effects, prior research, false danger, and other deceptive devices may be available theatrical tools, but technical availability does not settle their legitimacy. The performer should reduce real danger when simulation produces the same experience, avoid using belief to injure spectators' interests, and refuse the transition from entertainer to fraudulent spiritual authority. On this view, articulated by [[LiuQianMagician|Liu Qian]], secrecy is held for the spectator as well as against disclosure: revealing a method can erase a valued memory, and exposing another performer's working material can damage both colleague and audience.

The permission remains limited. Audience alignment cannot substitute for meaningful consent, and a ticket does not authorize any means whatsoever. Participation pressure, privacy, recording, labor, physical safety, third-party rights, and later use of information can all exceed the understood performance frame.

## Key Claims
- A magic audience knowingly authorizes a bounded form of perceptual and explanatory deception.
- The agreement permits mystery without requiring the performer to reveal the method afterward.
- Knowing an event is staged does not eliminate emotional surrender or the possibility that one moment feels real.
- Safe participation and non-adversarial presentation matter because intellectual challenge can otherwise feel humiliating.
- The ethical test includes purpose, harm, proportionality, and the difference between simulated and unnecessary real danger.
- Consent to a show is not blanket consent to coercion, exploitation, unsafe participation, privacy invasion, or belief-based fraud.
- Method secrecy can protect spectator memory and another performer's livelihood rather than merely performer status.

## Evidence
- Authorized role and staged possibility - [[what-magic-mind-reading-reveal-about-the-brain-asi-wind-scim6818902310]] records Wind's “license to lie” account and compares magic with staged forms whose known artifice can coexist with felt possibility.
- Mystery and safe surrender - [[what-magic-mind-reading-reveal-about-the-brain-asi-wind-scim6818902310]] links resistant spectators, intellectual challenge, safe mystery, and the irreversible effect of learning a secret.
- Service and non-harm - [[lkmbnvq3i5s8rvzs22fyi2xhnbte-lkmbnvq3i5s8rvzs22fyi2xhnbte]] has Liu distinguish deception used to provide a good experience from the same tools used to exploit belief or harm interests.
- Danger proportionality - [[lkmbnvq3i5s8rvzs22fyi2xhnbte-lkmbnvq3i5s8rvzs22fyi2xhnbte]] argues that a false bomb and a real bomb can create the same theatrical effect, so real lethal risk adds no necessary artistic value.
- Secrecy for audiences and peers - [[lkmbnvq3i5s8rvzs22fyi2xhnbte-lkmbnvq3i5s8rvzs22fyi2xhnbte]] describes withholding an explanation to preserve a participant's memory and refusing to teach another magician's livelihood-producing material.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Audience awareness does not resolve every ethical question. Participation pressure, embarrassment, age, accessibility, power differences, recording, physical risk, prior research, and use of audience data can exceed what a ticket or invitation reasonably authorizes. Both sources offer performers' ethical frameworks rather than a complete consent theory, and Liu's expansive list of possible tools should not be read as prior approval for a specific use.

## What Changed
- Added purpose and non-harm as tests separating theatrical deception from exploitative belief and fraud.
- Added proportionality: simulated danger is preferable when real danger contributes no distinct audience value.
- Reframed method secrecy as protection of spectator memory and peer livelihood as well as performer craft.
