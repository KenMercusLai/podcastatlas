# Postoperative Scar Management / 术后疤痕长期管理

## Definition
Postoperative scar management is the longitudinal coordination of wound-specific prevention, scar maturation monitoring, sustainable home care, surrounding-skin support, specialist escalation, and realistic psychological expectations after an incision or injury.

## Current Synthesis
Scar management begins with closure but continues for months or years. The source treats the visible result as the product of incision design, tissue alignment, mechanical tension, body site, movement, contamination or infection, personal biology, and aftercare. Because redness, vascular activity, firmness, and raised tissue can be part of maturation, an early appearance should not be mistaken for the final result or reduced to a vague “scar constitution.”

The practical model is a sustainable treatment ladder. Silicone gel or sheets form the home-care base after appropriate healing, with the choice driven by site, movement, visibility, comfort, cleaning, and the person's ability to continue. Surrounding skin also needs tolerable cleansing, moisturization, anti-irritation care, and ultraviolet protection. Persistent vascular redness, overgrowth, symptoms, or mature scars may justify clinician assessment for local corticosteroid injection, vascular laser treatment, or revision, but the episode does not provide a self-treatment algorithm.

The endpoint is both physical and psychological. Complete erasure is not promised; a successful result can be a scar that is hard to notice at ordinary social distance, causes manageable symptoms, and no longer consumes the patient's attention. Simpler plans that can be sustained may therefore outperform overloaded regimens that provoke irritation, burden, or abandonment.

## Key Claims
- Scar management is a lifecycle extending beyond surgery and initial wound closure.
- Prevention and early monitoring are generally more tractable than delayed correction of a mature problematic scar.
- Silicone gel and sheets are presented as foundational home-care options whose usefulness depends on site and adherence.
- Specialist treatments require assessment of scar color, vascularity, height, symptoms, maturity, and prior response.
- Surrounding-skin barrier tolerance and sun protection are part of scar care because irritation and pigmentation can undermine adherence and appearance.
- Sustainable simplicity is a clinical variable: an overcomplicated regimen can fail if the person cannot continue it.
- A realistic endpoint combines lower visibility, symptom control, and reduced psychological preoccupation rather than guaranteed disappearance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This framework is derived from one public-education episode centered on a plastic surgeon's clinical experience. Its intervention windows, follow-up intervals, treatment mechanisms, silicone-product comparisons, and escalation sequence are source-scoped rather than a guideline or comparative trial. Open, infected, painful, draining, rapidly changing, function-limiting, or diagnostically uncertain wounds and scars require qualified assessment; products, pressure, injection, laser, and revision are not interchangeable and may be inappropriate for a particular wound, site, skin condition, or healing stage.

## What Changed
- Created a longitudinal model joining closure, maturation, home care, skin-barrier support, escalation, and acceptance.
- Made adherence and comfort part of treatment effectiveness rather than secondary conveniences.
- Defined a realistic endpoint around social visibility, symptoms, and reduced anxiety rather than “no trace.”
