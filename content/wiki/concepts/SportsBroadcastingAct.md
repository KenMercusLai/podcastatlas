# Sports Broadcasting Act

The Sports Broadcasting Act is the legal and political mechanism in [[the-nfl-1]] that allowed professional sports leagues to sell pooled national television rights despite antitrust pressure. The episode presents it as crucial to [[PeteRozelle]]'s effort to centralize NFL media economics.

In the source's business architecture, the act made [[SportsMediaRights]] and [[LeagueFirstOperatingModel]] more durable. Instead of every team selling local rights separately, the [[NationalFootballLeague|NFL]] could package national inventory, share revenue, support weaker teams, and sell broadcasters a coherent product.

[[the-invention-invention]] adds a non-sports comparison through [[PatentPool|patent pools]]. Both branches ask when rival coordination can be socially useful, but the legal mechanisms differ: the Sports Broadcasting Act creates statutory permission for pooled media rights, while the [[MPEGPatentPool]] relies on [[EssentialPatents]], [[FRANDLicensing]], and [[USDepartmentOfJustice|DOJ]] review to avoid collusion.

## Key Claims
- Legal permission can be part of a league's business model, not just background regulation.
- Pooled media rights helped align teams around league growth.
- Political bargaining around sports can reshape commercial structure when antitrust law would otherwise constrain coordination.
- Patent-pool governance shows a parallel route where coordination is disciplined by scope, access, and licensing terms rather than a sports-specific statute.
