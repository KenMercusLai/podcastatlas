# Racialized Remittance Politics / 种族化汇款政治

Racialized remittance politics is the pattern where migrant remittances are interpreted differently depending on the migrant group's racial and national status. In [[168-yin-yu-xin-yibu-riben-laogong-yimin-de-kuayang-huikuan-shi-lmgfmmgkawoa-6xyucnm1ljebzwu]], Japanese and Italian migrants both send money home, but U.S. observers can treat Japanese remittances as a threat while normalizing similar behavior by other groups.

The concept becomes explicit through the [[DillinghamCommission|Dillingham Commission]], anti-Japanese organizing, school segregation, and West Coast fear after the Russo-Japanese War. Remittance is no longer only family support; it becomes a sign used in arguments about immigrant loyalty, wealth drain, and national danger.

## Key Claims
- A host society may treat outbound remittances as family duty for some migrants and as extraction or disloyalty for others.
- "Scientific" immigration investigation can reproduce racial assumptions when categories and questions are biased from the start.
- Remittance politics intensifies when migration overlaps with geopolitical fear and domestic labor competition.
- Financial behavior is interpreted through racial narrative, not only through economic effect.
