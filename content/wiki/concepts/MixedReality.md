# Mixed Reality / 混合现实

## Definition
Mixed reality is an interface category that combines virtual content with perception of and interaction with the physical environment, commonly through camera-passthrough headsets or future transparent augmented-reality displays.

## Current Synthesis
The complete evidence separates mixed reality's broad interface promise from its product form. The RayNeo source places current MR closer to immersive VR-style headsets than to ordinary daily glasses: cameras mediate the outside world, while weight, content, battery, comfort, and social friction limit all-day use. Zuckerberg uses a broader umbrella definition spanning virtual and augmented reality and argues that digital objects may become part of the experienced room.

The strongest use cases in the current evidence are embodied rehearsal and spatial interaction: exercise, dance, music instruction, surgical education, scientific visualization, games, creative design, and virtual work surfaces. This supports the idea of whole-body computing, but not the stronger claim that MR necessarily improves health, education, or access. Training transfer, physical safety, affordability, privacy, visual comfort, and the gap between a compelling demonstration and routine use remain decisive.

## Key Claims
- MR combines virtual objects or environments with awareness of the physical world.
- Current headset-style MR is more immersive and higher-friction than ordinary smart glasses.
- Embodied movement and spatial overlays distinguish MR from screen-bound computing.
- Rehearsal and instruction are plausible applications when virtual practice transfers to real-world performance.
- Future transparent glasses may lower friction but face harder optical, battery, compute, sensing, and social-design constraints.
- MR is a product and interface spectrum rather than one settled technical category.

## Evidence
- Headset and glasses distinction — [[no-221-leiniao-ceo-xinjishu-yuelaiyueduo-women-weishenme-hai-xuyao-yifu-zhineng-yanjing-gkwridon6uwmaoosszqset9vd]] contrasts camera-passthrough MR/VR devices with ordinary-glasses-like AI and AR products.
- Foundation-device role — [[no-221-leiniao-ceo-xinjishu-yuelaiyueduo-women-weishenme-hai-xuyao-yifu-zhineng-yanjing-gkwridon6uwmaoosszqset9vd]] treats [[VisionPro|Vision Pro]] as useful for testing eye tracking, gestures, spatial content, and applications without treating it as the final daily-wear form.
- Whole-body and spatial use — [[curing-all-human-diseases-the-future-of-health-technology-mark-zuckerberg-dr-priscilla-chan-scim6005948638]] describes boxing, dance, music instruction, surgery education, creative work, holographic screens, and digital objects embedded in a room.
- Form-factor tradeoff — [[curing-all-human-diseases-the-future-of-health-technology-mark-zuckerberg-dr-priscilla-chan-scim6005948638]] distinguishes more powerful VR headsets from more wearable but constrained future AR glasses.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not settle industry definitions, long-term adoption, training efficacy, injury risk, visual effects, privacy, or accessibility. One source is a founder account from an AI-glasses company and the other is a friendly interview with Meta's founder; both have product incentives. A cited training study is described in conversation but not independently evaluated here, and exercise engagement in a demonstration does not establish durable health outcomes.

## What Changed
- Added embodied exercise, rehearsal, education, creativity, and spatial-work use cases.
- Added the headset-versus-glasses compute and wearability tradeoff.
- Preserved adoption, efficacy, safety, and category-definition boundaries from the complete evidence.
