# Cable Network Resilience

Cable network resilience is the ability of [[UnderseaDataCables]] and connected terrestrial systems to keep traffic moving or recover quickly after cable damage. [[tech-20260820-tech-pod-128-tech-20260820-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[ErinMurphy]]'s explanation that operators can often reroute traffic through another fiber pair or another cable when damage occurs.

The episode's important qualification is probability versus consequence. Murphy describes sabotage risk as low, and says most cable cuts are ordinary damage, wear, or natural events; still, the effects can be significant if a damaged cable has few substitutes or connects important users.

## Key Claims
- More cables and route diversity make damage less visible to businesses, hospitals, governments, and ordinary users.
- Redundancy is practical because prevention cannot eliminate accidental cuts, natural damage, or sabotage risk.
- Operators often detect cable damage quickly, but user impact depends on spare capacity, route diversity, landing-point dependencies, and who the cable connects.
- Cable resilience is part of [[RegionalNetworkTopologyRisk]] because redundancy has to be planned at the level of geography, not only device or data-center uptime.
