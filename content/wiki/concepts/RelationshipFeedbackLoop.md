# Relationship Feedback Loop / 亲密关系反馈闭环

## Definition
Relationship feedback loop is the process by which expression, response, observation, and adjustment let people revise how they communicate and behave with each other.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.202 presents communication not as one perfect sentence but as a loop. People express something, receive feedback, observe whether the relationship feels safe or uncomfortable, and then adjust. This matters because intimacy often contains partial speech, hidden need, and defensive behavior; without feedback, one failed interpretation can harden into withdrawal.

The concept keeps both sides active. The speaker has to make needs more legible, and the listener has to respond and check understanding. Feedback is therefore a relationship practice, not a demand that one person decode everything. The Sexton episode adds a recurring-maintenance layer: a weekly walk-and-talk gives the loop a predictable setting for naming wins, moments of feeling loved, and possible improvements before conflict escalates.

## Key Claims
- Effective communication depends on response and revision, not just initial expression.
- People learn from how others respond and from how they themselves feel in the exchange.
- Feedback can reduce misinterpretation when indirect speech or shame makes needs unclear.
- A loop differs from guessing because it allows correction after imperfect understanding.
- Relationship growth requires repeated information exchange rather than one dramatic confession.
- Scheduled check-ins can lower the cost of feedback by making revision ordinary rather than crisis-driven.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Feedback loops require enough safety and good faith to work. They are not sufficient in abusive, coercive, or clinically dangerous situations where documentation, support, exit planning, or professional care may matter more than continued relational repair.
