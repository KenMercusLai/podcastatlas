# AI Research Automation Oversight

## Definition

AI research automation oversight is the testing, reporting, and institutional visibility needed to evaluate systems that automate AI research, including capable systems retained for internal lab use rather than released publicly.

## Current Synthesis

[[tech-20261124-1124-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261124-1124-mp-tech-pod-128]] identifies a gap between [[FrontierModelReleaseGovernance]] and the systems most relevant to [[RecursiveSelfImprovement]]. A lab may use an internal model to write research code, run experiments, generate new methods, or accelerate later models without planning to release that research system. Testing only planned releases can therefore miss the development loop policymakers are trying to understand.

The source proposes a layered response: benchmark whether advanced models can perform meaningful AI research, obtain confidential reporting on research automation and compute allocation, and compare the pace of automated capability work with automated safety work. This is a capacity-building proposal under uncertainty, not evidence that direct autonomous successor construction has occurred.

## Key Claims

- Release-linked testing does not cover every high-capability system used internally by an AI lab.
- Research-capability benchmarks can make part of the development loop more visible, but benchmark validity and gaming remain live problems.
- Operational reporting can reveal how widely research is automated and where compute is allocated.
- Capability automation and safety automation should be compared rather than assuming they advance together.
- Cyber-capability testing may supply institutional infrastructure for research-capability evaluation because the domains can overlap.
- Oversight of large-scale internal use may require authority beyond rules aimed at public model releases.

## Evidence

### Internal-system visibility and measurement

- [[tech-20261124-1124-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261124-1124-mp-tech-pod-128]] attributes to [[RachelSteratore|Rachel Steratore]] the proposal to test meaningful AI-research ability and confidentially report research automation, compute allocation, and the relative automation of capability and safety work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The source does not supply the cited executive order, METR report, benchmark design, legal authority, or lab reporting data.
- An AI-research benchmark can be narrow, leaked, gamed, or mistaken for open-ended research ability.
- Confidential reporting may protect proprietary and national-security information while reducing public accountability.
- Domestic reporting does not by itself cover foreign, open, distributed, or smaller-scale development loops.
- Internal research assistance is not equivalent to a system directly building its successor.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish oversight of internal research systems from release-only governance.
- Added research benchmarking, confidential reporting, compute allocation, and capability-versus-safety automation as a combined oversight frame.
