## Definition
Modern boundary hardening is the process by which states, markets, and empires turn overlapping spaces and populations into more exclusive territorial, legal, property, and identity categories.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's argument is historical rather than a simple contrast between a borderless past and a bordered present. Earlier governments could define and police boundaries, including along the Tumen and Yalu rivers, but their reach remained partial. Modern passports, citizenship, household registration, property rules, bureaucratic jurisdiction, and national classification deepen the consequences of belonging to one side.

[[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] connects this hardening to an interpretive “triangle” of imperialism, capitalism, and colonialism. Expanding capital and territorial competition reward exclusive claims over land, resources, labor, and people, even though ecology and lived relations continue to cross the resulting lines.

## Key Claims
- Modernity intensifies the depth and exclusivity of borders rather than inventing every border from nothing.
- Documents and registries convert ambiguous belonging into administratively consequential identity.
- Exclusive property and territorial claims make shared or overlapping control harder to sustain.
- Population settlement, taxation, and legal incorporation become instruments of territorial competition.
- Harder borders can provoke greater pressure when they conflict with ecology, livelihood, kinship, or inherited settlement.

## Evidence
- Documented identity: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] contrasts cross-border peoples with modern Chinese and Russian identity documents that divide them into separate national categories.
- Population as control: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] describes Qing, Korean, and Russian competition over settlement, taxation, and jurisdiction in the 1880s.
- Bureaucratic exclusion: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] recounts how a Korean citizenship document displaced a returning engineer from the position he previously held in China.
- Structural claim: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] links exclusive borders to capitalist accumulation, imperial expansion, and colonial ordering.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish one universal chronology for all borders, and earlier empires also used severe law and territorial demarcation. Modern documentation can enable rights and protection as well as exclusion. The concept names a tendency toward deeper, more exclusive classification, not a claim that every modern border is equally hard or illegitimate.
