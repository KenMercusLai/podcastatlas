# Scientist As Project Organizer / 科学家作为项目组织者

Scientist as project organizer is the source's account of how [[BigScienceInfrastructure|大科学基础设施]] changes the scientific role. In [[ep248-zai-gaoyuan-yu-dixia-tanxun-zhongguo-da-kexue-xiangmu-lidn7xxkcw4-66fw-y50fqqacwo]], [[MiaoQian|苗千]] says big-science chief scientists are no longer legible as solitary "个体户"; they also operate like project managers.

The concept does not demote scientific judgment. The source's point is that a chief scientist must first remain scientifically credible, then also coordinate engineering, personnel, data pipelines, schedules, institutional partners, and fiscal support. Large projects can last 30 years, outliving a single scientist's most productive research window and training multiple generations of graduate students along the way.

This frame also explains why basic-science facilities require public argument. A scientist-organizer must defend the value of [[WeakSignalDetection|weak-signal detection]], [[ExtremeEnvironmentPhysics|extreme environments]], and [[BigScienceTechnologySpillover|technology spillovers]] to funders and publics who cannot directly consume the research output.

## Key Claims
- Big science makes management a scientific responsibility rather than an external administrative afterthought.
- Long project duration changes career incentives, authorship, training, and credit.
- Funding advocacy becomes part of keeping the evidence system alive.
- Project organization is the human layer that connects instruments, sites, budgets, and shared scientific questions.
