# Mission-Aligned Revenue Model

## Definition
A mission-aligned revenue model funds an institution through sources and terms that support, or at least do not systematically redirect, the public purpose the institution is meant to serve.

## Current Synthesis
Wikipedia's institutional transition makes revenue architecture part of editorial governance. [[JimmyWales]] argues that advertising would reward page views, commercial topics, affluent markets, and advertiser-compatible priorities, while the mission required broad knowledge and access including in less lucrative contexts. Nonprofit status did not itself fund the servers; many small donations paired mission legitimacy with dispersed dependence, while reserves added continuity. The model is therefore alignment among purpose, payer base, editorial incentives, and operating resilience rather than a simple nonprofit-versus-business label.

## Key Claims
- Revenue sources shape which users, subjects, and metrics an organization is pressured to prioritize.
- Legal nonprofit status does not guarantee alignment; the actual payer base and restrictions still matter.
- Broad small-donor funding can reduce dependence on a few patrons, though it creates recurring fundraising and messaging pressures.
- Rejecting advertising can protect editorial priorities only if an adequate alternative revenue system exists.
- Reserves and cost discipline are part of mission protection because financial fragility weakens institutional choice.

## Evidence
- Incentive diagnosis - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] has Wales argue that organizations follow revenue and that advertising could privilege commercially valuable content and markets.
- Institutional choice - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] says the volunteer community favored nonprofit status and Wales viewed it as compatible with an enduring cultural contribution.
- Donor validation - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] reports nearly $30,000 raised in about two weeks after a server failure prompted an initial campaign.
- Financial continuity - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] describes distributed small donations and conservative reserves as supporting independence and stability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Donation funding can introduce its own distortions through campaign narratives, donor demographics, platform dependence, or concentration among nominally small gifts. Advertising does not automatically destroy editorial integrity, and nonprofit status does not eliminate governance failure. The episode supplies the founder's rationale and reported figures rather than a comparative audit of funding models or Wikimedia finances.

- Established a funding-governance concept linking payer structure, editorial priorities, and operating resilience.
