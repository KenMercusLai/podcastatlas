# Deaf Education And Belonging

Deaf education and belonging is the school-access concept extracted from [[88-bie-renshu-huizi-zai-ting-budao-hecai-de-shijie-li-huiquan-708510803]]. [[KeikoBoxer|惠子]] is sent through ordinary kindergarten and ordinary school because adults hope she can grow up like other children, but the episode shows that physical placement is not the same as communicative belonging.

The contrast with her sister's deaf-school route matters. 惠子 envies the possibility of lessons, friendships, and correction happening in a language environment she can actually use. When she later enters a deaf school herself, communication becomes smoother, but accumulated anger does not disappear automatically.

This concept therefore avoids a simple mainstream-school-versus-special-school answer. The source's durable point is that inclusion has to be judged by real communication, trust, teacher practice, peer life, and the student's ability to participate without constant guessing.

## Key Claims
- Placement in an ordinary classroom can still produce exclusion when teachers and peers do not make communication accessible.
- Specialized settings can restore belonging when they allow direct language, peer recognition, and less constant self-translation.
- Supportive adults such as [[WadaSachiko|和田幸子]] matter because belonging may begin with a refuge before it becomes classroom participation.
- Communication access can reduce isolation without instantly undoing earlier shame, rage, or distrust.
- The concept complements [[SubjectLedCare]] because educational support has to start from the student's actual communicative reality.
