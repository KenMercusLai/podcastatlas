# Tutoring Restriction Substitution Effect / 校外培训限制的替代效应

## Definition
The tutoring restriction substitution effect is the possibility that reducing visible or institutional tutoring supply moves competitive preparation into online, private, informal, or more expensive forms when selection incentives and family demand persist.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep180-feidianxing-beijing-mama-de-foxi-jiwa-women-jiujing-xiang-peiyang-zenyang-de-haizi-ckwridoewbb3abaaaadgpbgf|EP180]] argues that closing or constraining training institutions does not by itself eliminate the value families attach to school access and exam performance. Group courses may move online, one-to-one teaching may enter homes, and families with more money or networks may retain access through less visible channels.

The concept is a policy implementation warning, not a verdict against tutoring regulation. Supply restrictions may reduce marketing, excessive schedules, or some household spending, but distributional success depends on whether demand, enforcement, school provision, and access to substitutes change together.

## Key Claims
- Visible tutoring supply and underlying selection demand are distinct policy targets.
- Families may substitute online, home-based, one-to-one, or informal tutoring for closed group institutions.
- Scarcer private provision can raise price and make preparation more dependent on wealth or networks.
- Online substitution changes supervision and attention demands as well as delivery location.
- School staffing and instructional capacity affect whether families regard public provision as a credible substitute.
- Policy evaluation should measure total learning time, household cost, access distribution, child wellbeing, and school workload, not only institutional closures.

## Evidence
- Delivery substitution: [[ep180-feidianxing-beijing-mama-de-foxi-jiwa-women-jiujing-xiang-peiyang-zenyang-de-haizi-ckwridoewbb3abaaaadgpbgf|EP180]] describes subject tutoring moving online and expensive one-to-one teaching potentially moving into homes.
- Demand persistence: [[ep180-feidianxing-beijing-mama-de-foxi-jiwa-women-jiujing-xiang-peiyang-zenyang-de-haizi-ckwridoewbb3abaaaadgpbgf|EP180]] ties continued demand to unchanged selection standards and scarce high-quality educational resources.
- Unequal access risk: [[ep180-feidianxing-beijing-mama-de-foxi-jiwa-women-jiujing-xiang-peiyang-zenyang-de-haizi-ckwridoewbb3abaaaadgpbgf|EP180]] argues that hidden or one-to-one alternatives can become more expensive and therefore more dependent on household means.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode captures expectations during a 2021 policy moment rather than measured later outcomes. It does not establish the scale of substitution, enforcement response, price change, learning effect, or distribution across regions. Tutoring restrictions may still reduce exposure, advertising, or pressure for many families. Current empirical evidence is needed before treating substitution as dominant or nationwide.

## What Changed
- Created a distribution-sensitive framework for separating institutional closure from changes in tutoring demand and total access.
