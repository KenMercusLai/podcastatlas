# Reuse-First Rocket Design

Reuse-first rocket design is [[DongKai|董凯]]'s argument that reusable launch vehicles have to be designed for repeated flight from the beginning rather than retrofitted after a recovery attempt. In [[zhongting-zhuque-sanhao-fuzongshi-huojian-huishou-zhiyou-cheng-yu-bai-meiyou-zhongjiantai-s9e41-d9e11e3b-7c7e-4beb-b19f-6f90fff38714]], this is the logic behind [[Zhuque3|朱雀三号]]'s liquid-oxygen/methane propulsion and stainless-steel tank route.

The concept is tactical rather than fashionable. Dong does not say liquid oxygen/methane or stainless steel is inherently more advanced; he says those choices serve [[LandSpace|蓝箭航天]]'s target of large payload, high frequency, and low cost. Stainless steel can reduce external insulation and post-recovery maintenance work, while methane avoids some kerosene-engine soot-cleaning burden after repeated use.

## Key Claims
- Reuse is a system requirement, not a feature added after a disposable rocket succeeds.
- Material, propellant, engine, recovery, manufacturing, and refurbishment choices should be evaluated by their contribution to repeated flight.
- A route can be strategically correct without being universally "more advanced"; it is correct if it helps the chosen operational model close.
- Reuse-first design still has to be proven through recovery, inspection, turnaround, and reflying rather than through configuration alone.
