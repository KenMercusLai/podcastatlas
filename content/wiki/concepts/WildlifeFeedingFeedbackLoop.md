## Definition
Wildlife feeding feedback loop / 野生动物投喂反馈回路 is the process by which human food, intentional feeding, garbage, pet food, and social-media incentives teach wild animals to approach people, while animal approach then encourages more feeding, filming, complaints, injury risk, and management intervention.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] makes feeding the strongest causal thread across otherwise different animals. Tourists, volunteer feeders, short-video creators, residents feeding cats, and people giving bread to boars all alter animal expectations. Once animals learn that people, plastic bags, balconies, old neighborhoods, or park paths mean food, the problem stops being only an individual bad habit and becomes a public feedback system.

The loop is ethically tricky because feeding often begins as affection, guilt, or care. The episode's policy implication is that kindness without distance can become harm: it can damage animal diet, reduce fear, increase bites or grabs, amplify disease exposure, and create political pressure for relocation, sterilization, or killing.

## Key Claims
- Feeding changes animal behavior by reducing avoidance and linking people or urban objects to food.
- The same feeding act can be intentional, accidental, or indirect, as when cat food becomes raccoon dog food.
- Social-media attention can reinforce feeding by turning close contact into a filming opportunity.
- Feeding may feel compassionate but can increase later risks for both animals and people.
- Enforcement is hard when people define their action as helping cats, enjoying tourism, or making content rather than feeding wildlife.

## Evidence
- Behavioral learning: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] links macaque grabbing and boar responses to plastic-bag sounds with repeated food association.
- Indirect feeding: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] says residents may believe they are feeding stray cats while raccoon dogs, birds, yellow weasels, and hedgehogs also eat the food.
- Content incentives: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] describes tourists, volunteer monkey feeders, and short-video creators as separate feeding groups at Qianling Mountain.
- Harm from kindness: [[ep282-bu-wanmei-de-gongcun-dang-yesheng-dongwu-chengwei-chengshi-xin-jumin-lg31-8lajmed4k4mhg5iz7hh8r1a]] connects feeding to macaque bites, unhealthy raccoon dog diets, boar danger, and contagious-disease concern.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not claim feeding is the only driver of urban wildlife growth. It also names missing predators, greenbelts, old residential structures, garbage, species reproduction rates, and fragmented habitat. Controlled official feeding appears as a possible transitional tool, so the target is not food provision in the abstract but unmanaged public feeding that teaches risky closeness.

## What Changed
- Added feeding as a cross-species feedback mechanism joining macaques, boars, raccoon dogs, birds, and community animals.
- Clarified that some wildlife feeding is indirect and therefore harder to regulate than explicit hand-feeding.
