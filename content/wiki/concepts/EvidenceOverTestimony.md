## Definition
Evidence over testimony is a preference for independently checkable corroboration when evaluating claims that depend on perception, memory, confession, or institutional authority. It does not make physical traces infallible or eyewitnesses inherently dishonest.

## Current Synthesis
Sincere testimony may be distorted by fear, suggestion, translation, social pressure, or strategic incentives. Physical evidence can correct a narrative, but collection, interpretation, context, and uncertainty remain essential. Witch trials and contested criminal cases show the cost of treating approved accounts as proof; forensic science shows the cost of treating laboratory traces as automatic certainty. Fiction can illustrate the gap between legal judgment and intimate belief without serving as evidence about a real case.

*Free Brian Williams* extends the rule to autobiographical journalism. A false statement can require correction even when deliberate deception is unproven, and a plausible memory mechanism does not waive the speaker's duty to compare a consequential story with early reports, records, and independent witnesses. Listening seriously, checking details, and withholding unsupported moral certainty are compatible practices.

## Key Claims
- A confident or sincere recollection needs corroboration when the proposition is extraordinary or has serious consequences.
- Trace evidence is useful when its provenance, sampling, interpretation, and uncertainty are assessed alongside other evidence.
- An institution's acceptance of a vision, confession, or cultural explanation does not establish its factual truth.
- Protecting a victim and testing the accuracy of testimony are compatible obligations; source criticism must not become victim-blaming.
- A set of suspicious clues can warrant investigation without settling legal responsibility or omniscient truth.
- Inaccuracy, sincerity, and intent are separate findings; corroboration can establish the first without automatically deciding the other two.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
No simple hierarchy makes every physical object more reliable than every witness: contaminated samples, bad inference, weak methods, and overconfident juries can all fail. Corroboration is not an excuse to dismiss a vulnerable person's account or accept a defendant's unsupported cultural excuse. The Salem, Schmerler, forensic, Sagan, and Williams material is mediated through podcast or book discussion, and the Jacob case is fiction; specific historical, legal, and psychological details remain source-attributed rather than independently adjudicated here. Memory fallibility makes deliberate deception uncertain, not impossible.

## What Changed
- Extended the corroboration rule to consequential autobiographical journalism.
- Separated findings about inaccuracy, sincerity, and intent.
- Clarified that a plausible memory explanation does not cancel verification duties.
