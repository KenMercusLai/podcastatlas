# Long-Duration Bond Defense / 长久期债券防守配置

## Definition

Long-duration bond defense is a bounded allocation that uses rate-sensitive government bonds for potential gains and liquidity during recession or monetary easing while retaining coupon or maturity cash flow if the anticipated regime change does not arrive.

## Current Synthesis

The source separates three jobs that are often collapsed under the word “bond.” Intermediate-to-long Treasury ETFs provide liquid duration and possible capital gains; individual Treasuries provide contractual maturity value and cash flow; yen government or high-grade bonds match the host's living currency. The components are therefore selected by function rather than by yield ranking alone.

The defense is conditional, not safe in an absolute sense. Duration can generate substantial losses when yields rise, and foreign bonds add currency exposure. The structure remains defensive only because leverage is rejected, total weight is capped, entry is staged, and the holder has a fallback cash-flow use if recession and easing do not materialize.

## Key Claims

- Long-duration bonds can provide equity-deployment liquidity if recession and easing raise bond prices while weakening risk assets.
- Coupon and maturity cash flow can preserve a second portfolio job when the capital-gain scenario is delayed or absent.
- ETFs and individual bonds are not interchangeable because liquidity, duration, maturity repayment, and price path differ.
- Spending-currency matching can justify a lower-yield currency sleeve without requiring an additional exchange-rate forecast.
- Duration exposure must be capped by a portfolio loss budget and should not be made bond-like only in name through leverage.
- Staged purchases and evidence-based expansion acknowledge that the investor cannot identify the exact yield peak.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Long bonds can decline sharply under persistent inflation, higher policy rates, fiscal or issuance pressure, or a rising term premium. They may fail as an equity hedge when inflation drives both assets down, and foreign holders can lose through currency moves. Individual Treasuries still have opportunity cost and reinvestment considerations; ETF liquidity does not promise principal recovery at a fixed date. The source's allocation depends on Japanese living costs, multiple currencies, liquid assets, and personal loss tolerance.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish a risk-budgeted, multi-job bond sleeve from treating long bonds as cash or a universal hedge.
