# TCM Summer Pattern Differentiation / 中医夏季寒湿湿热辨别

## Definition
TCM summer pattern differentiation is [[MaoZheTCMDoctor|毛哲]]'s source-scoped framework for varying summer self-care according to cold-damp or damp-heat patterns, digestion, subjective constitution, environment, symptoms, and illness severity rather than applying one universal “dampness-removing” routine.

## Current Synthesis
The framework's most defensible behavioral contribution is anti-extremism: hot weather does not make deliberate overexposure to sun, total rejection of air conditioning, unrestricted cold food, forced sweating, one fruit pairing, or one recipe appropriate for everyone. The three episodes ask listeners to notice amount, tolerance, place, weather, exposure duration, symptoms, metabolic or allergy context, and the wider diet; to use ordinary cooling and hydration sensibly; and to place serious illness above lifestyle experimentation. The rain-exposure episode adds [[RainExposureFootCareBoundary|prompt rinsing, drying, and removal from prolonged wet footwear]] without requiring a cold-damp diagnosis. The litchi episode adds a useful distinction between food temperature and food meaning: chilling changes temperature, preservation, texture, and possibly intake behavior, but the source does not establish that it changes health effects or a clinically measurable “hot” property.

Its diagnostic and treatment content requires a firmer boundary. The sources map tongue coating, thirst, urine, bowel movements, sweating, limb temperature, fatigue, appetite, heaviness, mouth or throat discomfort, region, rain exposure, and eating history onto cold-damp, damp-heat, excess fire, deficiency fire, or organ-channel patterns, then assign foods, drinks, herbs, fruit pairings, peels, patent medicine, soaking, induced sweating, acupressure, and gua sha accordingly. These are TCM interpretations supplied by one podcast guest; the sources provide no validation, diagnostic-performance data, serving-limit evidence, doses, interaction review, or individualized examination. They therefore organize the episodes' claims without becoming established clinical rules.

## Key Claims
- Summer self-care should vary by individual tolerance, symptoms, place, weather, and severity rather than follow a universal seasonal rule.
- Fatigue, heaviness, sleepiness, poor appetite, and head fog are treated as broad “dampness” experiences in the source but do not independently distinguish a pattern or establish a medical diagnosis.
- The episode's cold-damp versus damp-heat distinction is a TCM classification, not a substitute for evidence-based assessment of dehydration, infection, heat illness, gastrointestinal disease, medication effects, sleep problems, or other causes.
- Moderate sun, comfortable air conditioning without direct exposure, hydration, and non-extreme food choices form the practical lifestyle layer, while herb, medicine, scraping, and induced-sweating claims require greater caution.
- After rain or standing-water exposure, prompt cleaning and drying are separable from claims that systemic cold-damp must be expelled through heat, sweating, food, ginger, or 藿香正气.
- Litchi amount, symptoms, allergy or diabetes context, and overall diet are more useful decision inputs than treating “上火,” sweetness, chilling, fruit pairings, or peel use as validated diagnostic or antidote rules.
- Severe or worsening heat-related symptoms override the self-care framework and require prompt qualified assessment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources supply no modern clinical evidence that their signs reliably identify cold-damp, damp-heat, excess fire, or deficiency fire; that summer air-conditioning or direct fan exposure causes the proposed mechanisms; that rain-related symptoms require sweating; that prior cold exposure explains most heat illness; or that litchi symptoms are diagnosed or neutralized through a hot/cold food taxonomy. They also do not establish the efficacy or safety of 藿香正气, induced sweating, foot soaking, ginger, lamb, porridge, litchi peel, fruit pairing, chrysanthemum, lotus leaf, mulberry leaf, mung bean, American ginseng, dried citrus peel, cardamom, coix seed, adzuki bean, winter melon, bitter melon, watermelon, sour-plum drink, acupressure, or gua sha for a particular listener. Serious heat illness, significant contamination, skin injury, vascular or metabolic risk, allergic reaction, or worsening symptoms must not be delayed by pattern differentiation or lifestyle experimentation; alcohol-containing medicines and illness-related impairment also require conservative driving decisions.

## What Changed
- Extended anti-extremism from cooling, sweating, and rain exposure to litchi portions, chilled foods, and summer meal temperature.
- Added allergy, diabetes, symptom persistence, and overall-diet context to seasonal food decisions.
- Expanded the evidence boundary to “上火,” excess/deficiency fire, fruit antidotes, and peel-based detoxification claims.
