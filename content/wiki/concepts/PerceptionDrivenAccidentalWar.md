# Perception-Driven Accidental War

## Definition

Perception-driven accidental war is escalation from an incident that no major actor initially intended to turn into war, but which hostile expectations, ambiguous commitments, military signaling, and weak crisis communication make difficult to contain.

## Current Synthesis

The episode distinguishes intent from control. States may prefer peace while public defense commitments, military operations, provocative gestures, and mutual historical ignorance narrow the room for de-escalation. A fatal aircraft collision is Xiang's concrete example: the physical incident matters, but the surrounding atmosphere determines whether leaders interpret it as accident, test, or attack.

The concept complements structural rivalry rather than replacing it. [[GreatPowerRivalryEscalationDynamics]] explains why the stakes are high; perception-driven escalation explains how a local event can activate those stakes before deliberate war plans exist.

## Key Claims

- Absence of aggressive intent does not guarantee crisis control.
- Strategic ambiguity can restrain commitments, while its erosion can harden expectations.
- Repeated military signaling changes how later accidents are interpreted.
- Historical and security-perception gaps can turn defensive behavior into apparent provocation.
- Crisis communication and reversible signals are core prevention capacity.

## Evidence

- Ambiguity erosion: [[508-xiang-lanxin-tan-guoji-guanxi-shiye-xia-de-zhongmeiou-jinxi-cong-sitanfo-xiaojituan-dao-junshi-kaiensizhuyi-lqrjnxctet30isfmgt-wyavdwffu]] argues that repeated public U.S. statements about defending Taiwan weakened earlier strategic ambiguity.
- Incident pathway: [[508-xiang-lanxin-tan-guoji-guanxi-shiye-xia-de-zhongmeiou-jinxi-cong-sitanfo-xiaojituan-dao-junshi-kaiensizhuyi-lqrjnxctet30isfmgt-wyavdwffu]] reports a simulation in which another fatal aircraft collision under severe tension greatly increased war risk.
- Historical mechanism: [[508-xiang-lanxin-tan-guoji-guanxi-shiye-xia-de-zhongmeiou-jinxi-cong-sitanfo-xiaojituan-dao-junshi-kaiensizhuyi-lqrjnxctet30isfmgt-wyavdwffu]] compares the pathway to Sarajevo while emphasizing mutual perception failure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The simulation is described but not documented, and the Sarajevo analogy can obscure important differences in nuclear deterrence, command systems, geography, and alliance structure. Strategic clarity may deter in some settings; the source does not prove that ambiguity is always safer.

## What Changed

- Created an escalation concept separating peaceful preference from reliable incident containment.
