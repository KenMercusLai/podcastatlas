## Definition

Coerced talent legitimacy / 胁迫征士合法性 is the pattern where a violent or insecure ruler tries to borrow legitimacy from respected talent while using threats, hostages, office pressure, or terror to force the appearance of elite consent.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 713 adds an early Shu variant before the later Dong Zhuo cluster. [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]] tries to force local scholars and officials into service through poison, family hostages, and title inducements. [[LiNieEasternHan|Li Nie]] dies rather than serve, [[QiaoXuanShuScholar|Qiao Xuan]] survives only because his son pays, [[WangHaoShuScholar|Wang Hao]] and [[WangJiaShuScholar|Wang Jia]] kill themselves under hostage pressure, Fei Yi, Ren Yong, and Feng Xin use illness or incapacity strategies, and [[WenQiYizhouEasternHan|Wen Qi]] refuses despite family hostages and secretly submits to Liu Xiu. The result is the opposite of legitimacy: forced recruitment becomes evidence against the regime.

Hanji 947 through Hanji 949 preserve the later Dong Zhuo form. [[DongZhuo|Dong Zhuo]] hears of [[CaiYong|Cai Yong]]'s reputation, treats illness refusal as defiance, threatens his family, and then promotes him rapidly once he appears. After the forced replacement of [[LiuBianHongnongWang|Liu Bian / Hongnong Wang]], Dong Zhuo also needs senior names such as [[LiuYuLateHan|Liu Yu]], [[YangBiao|Yang Biao]], and [[HuangWanLateHan|Huang Wan]] to make coercive power look like court order. Hanji 949 then expands the pattern through [[ZhouBiLateHan|Zhou Bi]], [[WuQiongLateHan|Wu Qiong]], [[ZhengTaiLateHan|Zheng Tai]], [[HeYongLateHan|He Yong]], [[XunShuangLateHan|Xun Shuang]], [[ChenJiLateHan|Chen Ji]], [[HanRongLateHan|Han Rong]], and [[ShentuPanLateHan|Shentu Pan]].

Across the sources, the decisive issue is not whether offices are staffed. Coercion can put respected people near power and can create the public surface of honors, recommendations, and promotions. It cannot make those signals prove willing elite support, and refusal by a respected figure exposes the gap between formal summons and credible legitimacy.

## Key Claims

- Violent rulers seek respected talent because force alone does not create durable legitimacy.
- Coerced summons can mimic honor while hiding family threat, poison threat, hostage pressure, or terror.
- Rapid promotion may package forced compliance as elite endorsement faster than trust can form.
- Offices staffed under threat lose evidentiary value because observers can read service as fear rather than consent.
- Refusal, illness claims, self-harm, ransom-mediated survival, and flight can expose the coercive basis of recruitment.
- The pattern sits between [[CourtOfficeCapture|court office capture]] and [[TalentAttractionThroughRespect|respect-based talent attraction]]: it uses appointment forms while failing the non-coercive consent test.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not claim coercive recruitment is always ineffective in staffing offices. Dong Zhuo secures Cai Yong's presence and high-profile appointments, and Gongsun Shu can sometimes extract money or temporary compliance pressure. The narrower claim is about legitimacy: once coercion is visible, appointment loses its value as proof of voluntary recognition. Hanji 713 also shows mixed survival paths, so martyrdom is not the only form of refusal under coercion.
