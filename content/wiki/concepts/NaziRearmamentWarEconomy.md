# Nazi Rearmament War Economy

## Definition

Nazi rearmament war economy names the episode’s account of an economic system in which military expansion, domestic recovery, national prestige, resource scarcity, and planned conquest became mutually dependent.

## Current Synthesis

[[AdolfHitler]] did not treat economic revival and rearmament as separable programs. Armaments restored visible national power, supported employment and industrial activity, and created patriotic identification among some workers and elites. Infrastructure such as the Autobahns also had military meaning, even though the episode, following Adam Tooze, rejects the claim that road building was the main solution to unemployment.

The system’s weakness was material. [[Germany]] depended on imported food and raw materials, lacked sufficient foreign exchange, and diverted resources from civilian consumption into arms. By late 1935, shortages of butter, fats, and meat exposed the cost. The regime’s answer was not a stable balance but deeper autarky, the Four Year Plan, and the expectation that territorial victory would supply future resources and wealth.

The concept therefore describes more than defense spending. Rearmament helped legitimize the regime while also creating pressures that made successful expansion increasingly important to the continuation of the economic and political project.

## Key Claims

- Rearmament joined military preparation to employment, industrial demand, national pride, and regime legitimacy.
- Autobahn construction had strategic and symbolic value but was not the primary cause of unemployment reduction in the source’s account.
- Import dependence and scarce food, raw materials, and foreign exchange limited the apparent recovery.
- Consumer shortages revealed the tradeoff between armaments and living standards.
- The regime expected later conquest and plunder to resolve pressures created by present spending.
- Foreign-policy victories helped sustain support by converting economic sacrifice into visible national restoration.

## Evidence

- Economic-military linkage: [[406-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-road-to-war-part-3-glt6776521114]] says Hitler linked revival to rearmament and viewed major infrastructure in military terms.
- Material constraint: [[406-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-road-to-war-part-3-glt6776521114]] identifies German dependence on imported food and raw materials and the diversion of scarce resources toward weapons.
- Consumer cost: [[406-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-road-to-war-part-3-glt6776521114]] reports late-1935 shortages of butter, fats, and meat while armament remained the priority.
- Political return: [[406-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-road-to-war-part-3-glt6776521114]] connects military production and foreign-policy success to worker pride, nationalist enthusiasm, and Hitler’s popularity.
- Expansionary endpoint: [[406-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-road-to-war-part-3-glt6776521114]] presents conquest as the expected future source of materials and wealth, followed in late 1936 by stronger autarky and Four Year Plan commitments.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not claim that rearmament alone caused every aspect of German recovery, that all infrastructure was purely military, or that every German supported the tradeoff. It also does not provide a complete fiscal, monetary, trade, labor, or industrial history. Exact employment effects, debt sustainability, import constraints, shortage severity, popular attitudes, and the degree to which war was economically inevitable remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established rearmament as both a source of domestic legitimacy and a generator of resource pressure.
- Distinguished visible recovery from a stable economic foundation.
- Connected the economic system’s continuation to anticipated conquest rather than treating war as an external later choice.
