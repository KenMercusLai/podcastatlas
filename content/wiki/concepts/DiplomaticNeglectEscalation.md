# Diplomatic Neglect Escalation

## Definition

Diplomatic neglect escalation is the pattern in which a seemingly small failure of response, recognition, or attention becomes a crisis amplifier because the neglected party treats the omission as a sign of contempt or threat.

## Current Synthesis

[[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] grounds the concept through [[TeodrosII]]'s unanswered letter to [[QueenVictoria]]. The crisis is not caused by correspondence alone, but the ignored letter becomes a symbolic wound inside a wider setting of missionary conflict, regional danger, personal instability, technology demands, and British imperial calculation.

## Key Claims

- Recognition failures matter most when the sender attaches political identity, religion, or status to the expected reply.
- A delayed answer can be interpreted as hostility even when the receiving bureaucracy treats it as neglect or low priority.
- Once people have been detained, the original omitted reply becomes harder to repair because every later gesture looks tactical.
- Diplomatic neglect can transform a symbolic grievance into material coercion when the aggrieved ruler has hostages or other leverage.

## Evidence

- Royal-recognition claim: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says Teodros sends Victoria a friendly letter asking for Christian friendship, but the letter is filed away and forgotten.
- Escalation claim: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says Cameron reports that he and others are chained and will not be released until Teodros receives a civil answer.
- Failed repair claim: [[705-queen-victorias-revenge-the-mad-emperor-of-abyssinia-part-1-glt8608697296]] says Victoria's reply and Rassam's mission briefly please Teodros, but subsequent detention of envoys shows that a late answer cannot by itself rebuild trust.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not reduce the crisis to the forgotten letter. Teodros's violence, missionary insults, rebellion, desire for guns and technicians, British imperial calculation, and regional geopolitics all contribute. The concept therefore names one escalation mechanism, not the whole cause.
