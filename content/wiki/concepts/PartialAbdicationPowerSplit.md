# Partial Abdication Power Split / 退位留权式权力分裂

Partial abdication power split / 退位留权式权力分裂 is the governance failure [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-66-zhongguo-lishishang-naxie-diwang-shi-bei-huohuo-e-si-de-1-lvl1uxjadiedqwad-skxrwf-usnq]] draws from [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]]'s "主父" arrangement. The incumbent yields the formal throne to [[ZhaoHe|赵何]], but keeps enough authority, military attention, and symbolic seniority that officials still have to ask what a command from the old ruler means.

The concept differs from ordinary succession disorder. Zhao Wuling Wang does designate and enthrone a successor, so the problem is not simple non-designation. The danger is that formal kingship, fatherly seniority, military authority, and old loyalty do not land in the same place after the handoff.

This split creates several downstream risks. The formal court begins to gather around Zhao He and [[FeiYi|肥义]], making Zhao Wuling Wang feel excluded. The displaced elder son [[ZhaoZhang|赵章]] receives territory and [[TianBuli|田不李]], making his grievance administratively usable. Fei Yi has to verify any claimed summons from the 主父 before letting the young king respond, which means command authenticity itself has become a political problem.

The episode's modern analogy is a chairman/general-manager split, but the source shows why that analogy fails under monarchical succession. A predecessor who still wants decisive control can become a rival center rather than a mentor, especially when the successor's ministers must choose whose risk to obey.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-66-zhongguo-lishishang-naxie-diwang-shi-bei-huohuo-e-si-de-2-ltsw2eilkegs5yjtrp6gkhrmsxsg]] shows the split turning lethal. Zhao Zhang can forge a summons because commands from the 主父 still have apparent force, while Fei Yi's verification rule works only at the cost of his own life. After Zhao Zhang fails, the court side does not restore Zhao Wuling Wang as protected father or legitimate former ruler; it treats him as a continuing threat whose survival might reopen the conflict.

The episode therefore closes the concept's causal chain: formal abdication without clean authority transfer creates rival summons; rival summons create a coup; coup suppression creates fear of later revenge; fear of revenge produces [[ResponsibilityAvoidantKilling|避责式杀害]].

## Key Claims
- Abdication can settle title while leaving decision rights unresolved.
- Retained symbolic authority can make forged or ambiguous commands dangerous.
- A formal successor's court can absorb ministers and leave the predecessor politically isolated.
- Compensating a displaced heir with territory and advisers can convert emotional grievance into organized capacity.
- A partial exit can become a re-entry attempt when the predecessor no longer accepts being outside the operating center.
- Once a partial-exit crisis becomes violent, the court that wins may still fear the symbolic authority of the former ruler enough to eliminate him indirectly.
- The risk overlaps with [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]] and [[DualExecutivePowerRisk|双首长权力风险]], but its trigger is a throne handoff that does not cleanly transfer real authority.
