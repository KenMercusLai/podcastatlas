# Revolutionary Atrocity Narrative Contest

## Definition

Revolutionary atrocity narrative contest is the struggle between accounts that normalize political violence as necessary self-defence and accounts that sensationalize it into evidence of an enemy's demonic or subhuman nature.

## Current Synthesis

The [[SeptemberMassacres]] generated both forms. Revolutionary newspapers and witnesses described an extreme duty or atrocious necessity, while counter-revolutionary stories around [[PrincesseDeLamballe|the Princesse de Lamballe]] layered cannibalism, sexual violence, and grotesque mutilation onto a documented murder. Historical judgment must preserve the real killing while testing each added detail rather than choosing between apologetic context and sensational myth.

## Key Claims

- Necessity narratives can convert atrocity into defensive duty and suppress the individuality of victims.
- Sensational atrocity stories can mobilize opposition while importing pre-existing sexual, gendered, or class hostility.
- Demonstrating exaggeration does not acquit perpetrators of the violence that remains documented.
- Contemporary records, later memoirs, propaganda, and historiography require different evidentiary weight.
- Moral condemnation and contextual explanation are compatible when uncertainty is stated rather than filled with partisan detail.

## Evidence

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] records contemporary descriptions of the killings as duty, appropriate extremity, indispensable sacrifice, and atrocious necessity.

### Lamballe's afterlife

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] treats her death and decapitation as established, the pike procession as plausible but uncertain, and many sexualized mutilation and cannibalism claims as unsupported or contradicted.

### Historiographical dispute

- [[544-the-french-revolution-the-september-massacres-part-1-glt8301042051]] contrasts contextual accounts of panic and self-defence with arguments that contextualization can become moral evasion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Propaganda can contain true details, and political bias alone is not evidence that a report is false.
- The surviving source note does not reproduce the full archival basis for every atrocity claim or rebuttal.
- Correcting lurid invention should not make methodical tribunal violence appear less severe.

## What Changed

- The wiki now separates the evidence problem around atrocity stories from the moral judgment of the underlying massacre.
