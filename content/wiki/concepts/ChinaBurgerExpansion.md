# China Burger Expansion / 中国汉堡开店潮

## Definition
China burger expansion / 中国汉堡开店潮 is the restaurant-market pattern where foreign burger chains, adjacent Chinese restaurant brands, and existing chain operators all test burgers as a repeatable meal occasion in China.

## Current Synthesis
The burger wave has two overlapping logics. One is value and meal completeness: burgers can be read as filling, familiar, and operationally standardizable when consumers are cautious. That makes the category attractive not only to Western burger chains but also to Haidilao, M Stand, and Pizza Hut China, which can reuse traffic, kitchens, supply routines, or adjacent brand permission.

The newer Five Guys episode adds a premium and cross-border layer. Burgers are not only an affordable-meal test; they are also a launch vehicle for U.S. casual-fast-food brands seeking China growth while their home market is pressured. This makes the current judgment more conditional: burger demand is real enough to attract expansion, but premium entrants must prove repeat purchase after first-store queues and social-media novelty fade.

## Key Claims
- Burger expansion is broader than foreign burger chains; hot-pot, coffee, and pizza operators are also testing the occasion.
- Existing restaurant infrastructure can reduce entry cost when a chain can use store-within-store operations, shared kitchens, or existing traffic.
- The category's appeal depends on price, fullness, meal occasion, and operational repeatability rather than novelty alone.
- Premium U.S. entrants add brand heat and mall launch value, but they also raise the repeat-purchase bar.
- Store plans and market-growth forecasts remain source-scoped until supported by realized store economics and consumer repeat behavior.

## Evidence
- Category breadth: [[zhongguo-yinglai-hanbao-kaidianchao-guochan-huangningmeng-jiage-huiluo-1006920543]] names Five Guys, Haidilao, M Stand, and Pizza Hut China as burger-market participants.
- Value and occasion evidence: [[zhongguo-yinglai-hanbao-kaidianchao-guochan-huangningmeng-jiage-huiluo-1006920543]] frames burgers as filling, relatively affordable, and meal-like in a cautious consumer environment.
- Infrastructure evidence: [[zhongguo-yinglai-hanbao-kaidianchao-guochan-huangningmeng-jiage-huiluo-1006920543]] says Pizza Hut China already had burgers in more than 200 stores and planned a 500-600 store year-end rollout, making store-within-store operations part of the wave.
- Premium entrant evidence: [[kafeidou-meishi-hanbao-zhaji-zhadui-laihua-kuaican-pinpai-weishenme-you-dingshang-zhongguo-shichang-1009244406]] describes Five Guys' Beijing expansion, casual-fast-food positioning, freshness claims, and much higher meal price.
- Repeat-risk evidence: [[kafeidou-meishi-hanbao-zhaji-zhadui-laihua-kuaican-pinpai-weishenme-you-dingshang-zhongguo-shichang-1009244406]] uses Shake Shack's earlier Shanghai queues and later slower expansion or weak-store closures to qualify queue-driven burger demand.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source-cited annualized market-growth projection, store targets, queues, and price figures are retained as source-scoped claims.

The burger wave includes both value-oriented and premium-positioned formats. Treating them as one category can hide different consumer expectations and unit economics.

Novelty queues are ambiguous evidence: they can signal consumer curiosity and mall traffic value without proving frequent repeat behavior.

## What Changed
- Added the premium U.S. fast-food layer from the Five Guys episode.
- Qualified the earlier value-oriented burger thesis with higher-price repeat-purchase risk.
- Linked the burger wave to shopping-center first-store traffic and U.S. fast-food home-market pressure.
