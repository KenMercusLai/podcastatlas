# Ecological Timekeeping

## Definition
Ecological timekeeping is the use of living or environmental processes as timekeepers, making growth, climate, and ecological conditions part of how time is marked.

## Current Synthesis
[[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] grounds the concept through [[CenturiesOfTheBristlecone]], where a bristlecone pine would keep a 5,000-year calendar. [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] extends that proposal into a municipal clock and the explicit principle of [[Chronodiversity]]. The point is not only a novel clock: it asks whether accuracy must mean invariant intervals or can also mean faithful registration of the conditions through which life persists.

## Key Claims
- Ecological timekeeping treats time as embodied in living growth rather than only measured by external units.
- A living calendar can intentionally drift from standardized calendars because ecological conditions are part of the record.
- The method makes climate and environmental change visible as temporal evidence.
- It reframes calendar authority as a philosophical and political question, not only a technical convention.
- Public clocks can carry a remote ecosystem's changing rate into civic experience.

## Evidence
### Bristlecone Calendar
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] says [[JonathanKeats]] proposed using a bristlecone pine's rings and growth as the basis for a 5,000-year calendar.
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] specifies future-dated markers arranged around a sapling according to estimated growth.

### Drift From Standard Time
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] says the calendar would fall out of sync with the Gregorian calendar, raising the question of which system should be considered right.
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] says the corresponding public clock may speed or slow relative to atomic time as tree growth responds to climate and carbon dioxide.

### Climate As Record
- [[salt-020260914-archive-keats-podcast]] frames bristlecone rings as reflecting climate and growth conditions rather than abstract interval alone.
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] connects the artwork to scientific evidence that growth limits vary by elevation and may shift from temperature to water as warming continues.

### Public Ecological Time
- [[02026-heggli-keats-csank-podcast-v2]] describes a municipal clock engineered by Phil Abernathy and Brittany Nicole Cox and a museum partnership intended to make the trees' time accessible in Reno.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide a finished conservation, calibration, or measurement protocol. Ecological timekeeping may be vulnerable to tree health, site management, climate stress, human intervention, and interpretation disputes. Variable clocks can complement standardized time by revealing environmental response, but the sources do not show that they can replace uniform standards for technical coordination.

## What Changed
- The living-calendar proposal now includes a public municipal-clock branch and explicit engineering collaborators.
- Chronodiversity and the precision-versus-accuracy distinction sharpen the concept's claim without turning it into replacement metrology.
