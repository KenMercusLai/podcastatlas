# Small-Circle Election Legitimacy / 小圈子選舉認受性

## Definition
Small-circle election legitimacy is the problem of public mandate raised when a leader is selected by a narrow elite electorate rather than by universal suffrage.

## Current Synthesis
The Tung source uses the 1996 chief-executive selection to make the mechanism concrete. [[TungCheeHwa|董建華]]'s 320-vote victory made him the first post-handover leader, but the episode treats the small electorate as part of a broader legitimacy deficit once economic and political crises arrived.

## Key Claims
- Narrow elite selection can create formal officeholding without broad public mandate.
- Small-circle selection fit Beijing's preference for a controllable, business-friendly handover arrangement.
- Crisis turns a narrow mandate into a governing vulnerability because public acceptance cannot be assumed.

## Evidence
- Election detail: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] says Tung won the 1996 small-circle election with 320 votes.
- Structural interpretation: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] links that selection model to executive-led governance without universal-suffrage legitimacy.
- Beijing preference: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] says Beijing valued Tung's business background and networks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives only Tung's selection as an example. It does not compare other small-electorate systems or later Hong Kong chief-executive elections.
