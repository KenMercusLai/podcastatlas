# Mission Constrained Product Development

## Definition
Mission constrained product development is the practice of turning a company's stated purpose into concrete product requirements, ingredient exclusions, safety consultations, or user constraints.

## Current Synthesis
The [[ThriveCausemetics]] case shows mission becoming more credible when it narrows product decisions. [[KarissaBodnar]] did not only tell a story about giving; she began with false lashes and adhesive meant to work for people with few or no natural lashes, consulted ophthalmologists and oncologists, and later described the mission as affecting formulation choices because some donated products go to cancer patients.

## Key Claims
- Mission becomes more operational when it changes what the product must safely do for a specific user.
- Healthcare-adjacent consumer products may need expert input even when they are not regulated like medical devices.
- Ingredient exclusions, use-case constraints, and underserved-user design can make purpose visible inside the product.
- Mission constraints do not remove the need for mainstream product performance.

## Evidence
- Underserved-user evidence: [[thrive-causemetics-karissa-bodnar-how-150-a-day-in-sales-turned-into-a-150-million-beauty-business-with-a-mission-be72ee5e-a5fd-4554-bd4d-fda64909e6f6]] says the initial Thrive lash product was designed to work whether users had natural lashes or not.
- Expert-input evidence: [[thrive-causemetics-karissa-bodnar-how-150-a-day-in-sales-turned-into-a-150-million-beauty-business-with-a-mission-be72ee5e-a5fd-4554-bd4d-fda64909e6f6]] says Bodnar worked with ophthalmologists and oncologists because intended users could have cancer or compromised immune systems.
- Performance qualification evidence: [[thrive-causemetics-karissa-bodnar-how-150-a-day-in-sales-turned-into-a-150-million-beauty-business-with-a-mission-be72ee5e-a5fd-4554-bd4d-fda64909e6f6]] says Bodnar identifies product, not mission, as the primary business driver.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is currently grounded in one source. The source does not establish clinical efficacy, medical-device status, or generalized rules for all mission-driven brands.
