# Frontier Model Peer Review

Frontier model peer review is [[ElonMusk]]'s proposal in [[an-interview-with-elon-musk-6a6212214fac21e67f9b8c8c]] that rival frontier AI companies should get a short early-access window before a powerful model is released. In the interview, he suggests a week or two of access so competitors can test for harmful behavior and recommend a pause if they find serious safety or security problems.

The idea extends [[AISafetyCoordination]] into a more concrete release-stage mechanism. It assumes that rival labs such as [[OpenAI]], [[Anthropic]], [[GoogleDeepMind]], and [[XAI|xAI]] have enough technical competence and competitive incentive to notice dangerous behavior that a releasing company might miss or underplay.

## Key Claims
- Peer review would happen before broad public release, making it part of [[FrontierModelReleaseGovernance]] rather than only post-release incident response.
- Rival companies may be motivated to keep each other honest, but they may also use safety objections strategically.
- The proposal still needs government backstops because peer review has no obvious enforcement power if a company ignores warnings.
- The approach is faster than formal lawmaking, but weaker than public regulation if tests, findings, and pause decisions remain private.
