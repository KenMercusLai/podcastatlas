# Internal Worth and Accountability

## Definition
Internal worth and accountability is Terry Real's source-scoped distinction between basic human worth, which does not have to be earned through performance, and moral evaluation of conduct, which still allows regret, correction, and repair.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that performance-based self-esteem makes criticism existential: admitting a mistake can feel like admitting worthlessness, so defense, counterattack, or shame collapse replaces learning. A more stable sense of worth permits a narrower judgment—“I behaved badly”—without turning it into a total identity verdict.

This does not mean unconditional approval of every action. Its practical value is precisely that worth and conduct can be separated: a person can remain humanly valuable while facing impact, asking what the other person needs, apologizing, and changing behavior. In this frame, loving firmness toward oneself is more compatible with responsibility than harshness that triggers concealment or collapse.

## Key Claims
- Basic human worth need not depend on status, productivity, dominance, or flawless performance.
- Performance-based esteem can make ordinary criticism feel like an identity threat.
- Global shame can produce denial, defensiveness, or collapse rather than proportionate responsibility.
- Stable worth allows a person to feel appropriately bad about harmful behavior without treating the whole self as bad.
- Accountability still requires attention to impact, repair, and changed conduct.
- Loving firmness is distinct from indulgence because it can preserve standards without using contempt as motivation.

## Evidence
- Inside-out worth: [[defining-healthy-masculinity-how-to-build-it-terry-real-scim2733623652]] presents worth as grounded in existence rather than performance.
- Shame and criticism: [[defining-healthy-masculinity-how-to-build-it-terry-real-scim2733623652]] links defensive responses to the fear that imperfection proves global inadequacy.
- Conduct-specific regret: [[defining-healthy-masculinity-how-to-build-it-terry-real-scim2733623652]] distinguishes feeling bad about behavior from collapsing into a bad-self identity.
- Repair orientation: [[defining-healthy-masculinity-how-to-build-it-terry-real-scim2733623652]] recommends looking beneath poor delivery for hurt or need and asking what repair would help.
- Firmness without harshness: [[defining-healthy-masculinity-how-to-build-it-terry-real-scim2733623652]] argues for loving firmness toward others and oneself.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a therapeutic model rather than a validated measure or universal account of self-esteem. Internal worth does not erase consequences, unequal power, repeated harm, or another person's right to set limits or leave. Looking beneath hostile delivery may help in ordinary conflict but is not a duty during abuse, coercion, or danger.
