# Rebel Founder Alienation / 起义首领人心疏离

Rebel founder alienation / 起义首领人心疏离 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-3-chensheng-zhisi-weishenme-dele-minxin-hanle-renxin-lnx4zs-vsi3zeoixnqskkpexmr3|Qinji 129-3]] draws from [[ChenSheng|陈胜]]'s final months. The source keeps Chen Sheng's founding importance intact: [[DazexiangUprising|大泽乡起义]] inspires the broader anti-Qin wave. But it argues that the same person loses intimate support after becoming king.

The alienation has several layers. Chen Sheng receives his father-in-law with the coldness of ordinary guest protocol, then cannot repair the insult. Old friends who mention his past become threats to royal dignity, and Chen Sheng kills some of them until others flee. Finally, he appoints [[ZhuFangZhangChu|诸坊]] and [[HuWuZhangChu|胡武]] to inspect and punish officials, turning fear into routine governance.

The episode's dramatic endpoint is [[ZhuangGu|庄谷]] killing Chen Sheng. The point is not that one driver alone explains the collapse, but that Chen Sheng has made closeness unsafe. The later revenge by [[LuChenQinRebel|吕臣]] shows that Chen Sheng's symbolic role still survives, while his personal regime has lost the trust needed to protect him.

## Key Claims
- Founding legitimacy can decay quickly if the founder treats new status as permission to humiliate intimates.
- A ruler who kills old friends to control reputation destroys the relationships that could preserve him in crisis.
- Fear-based inspection can make a rebel regime resemble the coercive system it rose against.
- Historical memory can separate symbolic initiating value from personal governing failure.
