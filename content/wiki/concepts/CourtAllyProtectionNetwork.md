## Definition
朝廷盟友保护网络 / Court Ally Protection Network is the pattern in which a politically exposed official survives accusation because credible allies with rank, procedural language, and ruler access move a defense through the court before a hostile channel hardens.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 818 supplies the current bounded case. [[WangGongTaiweiLateHan|王龚]] attacks eunuch power, eunuch-linked clients accuse him, and [[EmperorShunOfHan|汉顺帝]] orders him to appear and explain himself. The danger is immediate because the accusation is already inside a court procedure.

The protection network works because it is not only social popularity. [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] converts Wang Gong's danger into an institutional argument: a Three Excellency should not be hauled before lower judicial offices unless the charge is grave enough to justify that humiliation. [[LiangShangLateHan|梁商]] then carries that argument to Shun, whose cancellation of the inquiry turns status, relationship, and access into survival.

The concept is intentionally bounded. The same episode later shows Wang Gong abandoning an impeachment plan against [[ZhangFangEunuchLateHan|张防]] after relatives invoke [[YangZhenLateHan|杨震]], so one successful ally chain does not remove the underlying eunuch-retaliation risk.

## Key Claims
- Court friendships become protection only when allies have credibility, access, and willingness to act quickly.
- A procedural or status argument can slow a retaliatory accusation without requiring the accused official to win the whole factional conflict.
- The network protects a vulnerable decision point, not the official's long-term freedom from political danger.
- Effective protection combines relationship capital with institutional language that the ruler can accept.
- The same case qualifies heroic remonstrance: courage needs channels, and channels can still be too risky against entrenched palace power.

## Evidence
- Immediate exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-818-guanchang-weishenme-yao-duo-jiaoyou-neng-jiuming-liyziudpezaoa4l6pbsq0wk2qdjdr|Hanji 818]] says Wang Gong's anti-eunuch memorial is followed by an accusation from eunuch-linked clients and Shun's order to appear.
- Li Gu's conversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-818-guanchang-weishenme-yao-duo-jiaoyou-neng-jiuming-liyziudpezaoa4l6pbsq0wk2qdjdr|Hanji 818]] says Li Gu writes to Liang Shang and frames Wang Gong's treatment as improper humiliation of a Three Excellency.
- Liang Shang's access: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-818-guanchang-weishenme-yao-duo-jiaoyou-neng-jiuming-liyziudpezaoa4l6pbsq0wk2qdjdr|Hanji 818]] says Liang Shang presents the argument and Shun cancels the inquiry.
- Bounded protection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-818-guanchang-weishenme-yao-duo-jiaoyou-neng-jiuming-liyziudpezaoa4l6pbsq0wk2qdjdr|Hanji 818]] later has Wang Gong stop short of impeaching Zhang Fang after relatives cite Yang Zhen.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current evidence is one Shun-era court case. It does not prove that broad networking reliably saves officials, and the later Zhang Fang branch shows a limit: ally protection can prevent one humiliating inquiry while leaving structural eunuch retaliation dangerous enough to deter a second confrontation.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 818's Wang Gong, Li Gu, and Liang Shang rescue chain.
