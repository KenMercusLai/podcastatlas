# Place-Based Exhibition Curation / 在地化展览策展

## Definition
Place-based exhibition curation is the practice of making displayed objects legible through the local histories, landscapes, institutions, industries, and memories that produced or preserved them.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's key positive example is [[YiweiHangzhiExhibition|“意味杭之”]] at [[HangzhouMuseum|杭州博物馆]]. The exhibition does not merely attach a Hangzhou label to ancient books; it explains why Ming-Qing Hangzhou publishing, West Lake writing, local documents, and book-collection memory make these objects part of the city.

## Key Claims
- Place-based curation works when local context explains the object rather than decorating it.
- A small, focused exhibition can be stronger than a large survey when it lets visitors follow one grounded question.
- Local publishing, tourism writing, and collection history can turn book objects into city history.
- Site visits after an exhibition can deepen the same interpretive thread when they share place, memory, or material-culture concerns.

## Evidence
- Exhibition title and structure - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] explains the “一苇杭之” allusion and the four exhibition sections.
- Local publishing layer - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] says the final section treats Ming-Qing Hangzhou printing and narration as its own topic.
- Travel extension - [[246-hangzhou-bowuguan-yiwei-hangzhi-haojiu-mei-kan-guo-zheme-tuijian-de-zhanlan-le-8217911423-973793]] moves from ancient books to [[WenlanPavilion]], [[LiuhePagoda]], [[LeifengPagoda]], and other Hangzhou sites.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Place-based framing can become forced local branding if the local link does not explain the objects. This source praises the Hangzhou exhibition specifically because the hosts think the local layer is substantively connected.

## What Changed
- Created a place-based curation concept from the Hangzhou ancient-books exhibition.
