# Yangtze Fishing Ban Recovery

Yangtze fishing ban recovery is the ecological rebound described in [[socialist-mobility-a-telling-michigan-vote-6a74600ec3e1347e8b5bf216]]. [[GabrielCrossley]] says fish biomass in the [[YangtzeRiver|Yangtze River]] has risen by more than 200% since 2021, after [[China]] introduced a 10-year commercial fishing ban and broader pollution-control measures.

The concept is deliberately bounded. The source treats the recovery as real, but it also emphasizes continuing dams, pollution, extinct or probably extinct species, and the social damage done to former fishing communities.

## Key Claims
- A long commercial fishing ban can create measurable biomass recovery when enforcement is strong.
- Enforcement in the source includes river patrols, drones, surveillance cameras, and central political backing.
- Pollution-control measures, including moving chemical factories and changing city behavior, matter alongside the fishing ban.
- Recovery of aggregate fish biomass does not undo extinction of species such as [[ChinesePaddlefish]] or likely loss of the [[Baiji]].
- Dams such as [[ThreeGorgesDam]] remain conservation constraints because they interrupt fish movement and spawning.
