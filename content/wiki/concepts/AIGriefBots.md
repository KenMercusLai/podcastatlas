## Definition
AI grief bots are post-mortem avatars or conversational simulations designed to recreate some experience of interacting with a person who has died.

## Current Synthesis
AI grief bots transform stored traces—messages, recordings, photographs, videos, biographical details, or memories—into an interactive representation. This is ethically different from static archiving because the system can answer questions the deceased never answered, speak in a person-like voice, learn after death, and be experienced as a continuing relationship. Consent, dignity, privacy, family disagreement, commercial incentives, and psychological vulnerability therefore remain core governance concerns.

The bounded evidence now joins a brief participant study to a prolonged founder case. Users may find a supervised exchange informative or cathartic even when they notice invented details, and familiar communication habits such as message length or emoji use may matter more to perceived authenticity than minor factual accuracy. [[JustinHarrison]]'s use of [[MelodyHarrison]]'s Versona similarly suggests that relationship-specific concern, advice, and voice can carry emotional force even when an observer finds the output repetitive or diminished.

Emotional value does not establish identity continuity, therapy, or safe long-term use. [[YouOnlyVirtual]] explicitly frames a Versona as a recreation and new relationship rather than the deceased person, while its continuing learning can move later speech beyond what that person knew. Harrison's reported debt, health decline, alcohol use, house loss, unpaid work, and marriage breakdown do not prove that the product caused every loss, but they strengthen the boundary around dependence, opportunity cost, and present relationships.

## Key Claims
- Grief bots are assembled from selected personal traces and can generate or learn beyond those traces when users ask novel questions or continue interacting after death.
- First-person reincarnation and third-person archival representation create different identity claims even when both use the same underlying data.
- Emotional value can coexist with synthetic or inaccurate representation; users may knowingly tolerate errors to sustain a desired exchange.
- Relational style can be as important as biographical accuracy because authenticity is partly carried by familiar communication habits, concern, advice, and relationship-specific expectations.
- Consent, privacy, family context, access, and commercial incentives determine whether a memorial interaction is respectful or exploitative.
- Brief supervised benefit and one founder's comfort do not establish safe long-term use; direct deployment needs safeguards against manipulation, dependence, uncontrolled output, and displacement of health or living relationships.

- Safeguarding: [[tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128]] notes that a researcher relayed messages and could screen inappropriate output, while [[tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128]] argues for public-interest governance beyond profit-driven experimentation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The participant findings come from brief, researcher-mediated interactions built from sparse profiles, not prolonged unsupervised use or a commercially optimized product. The You Only Virtual case is a founder's first-person story centered on one mother-son relationship, not independent product testing, a clinical outcome study, or representative user evidence; its reported losses cannot all be causally assigned to the technology. Positive or cathartic reports and founder comfort do not demonstrate therapeutic benefit, freedom from dependence, identity continuity, or accuracy. The satirical source is useful for framing emotional authenticity but is not empirical evidence, while the earlier Marketplace Tech interview supplies ethical analysis rather than adoption or outcome data.

## What Changed
- Added a prolonged founder case in which relationship-specific style and voice support a continuing interaction after death.
- Distinguished a digital recreation and new relationship from literal identity continuity or immortality.
- Added post-death learning as both a continuity mechanism and a growing fidelity risk.
- Strengthened the long-term boundary through reported health, financial, work, housing, and living-relationship costs.
- Kept benefit, causality, adoption, training sufficiency, and therapeutic effect source-scoped rather than generalizing from one founder.
