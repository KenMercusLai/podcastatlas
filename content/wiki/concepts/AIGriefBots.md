## Definition
AI grief bots are post-mortem avatars or conversational simulations designed to recreate some experience of interacting with a person who has died.

## Current Synthesis
AI grief bots transform stored traces—messages, recordings, photographs, videos, biographical details, or memories—into an interactive representation. This is ethically different from static archiving because the system can answer questions the deceased never answered, speak in a person-like voice, and be experienced as a continuing relationship. Consent, dignity, privacy, family disagreement, commercial incentives, and psychological vulnerability therefore remain core governance concerns.

The latest participant study adds a crucial design qualification. Users may find a brief, supervised exchange informative or cathartic even when they notice invented details, and familiar communication habits such as message length or emoji use may matter more to perceived authenticity than minor factual accuracy. This does not make hallucination harmless: willingness to suspend disbelief can increase vulnerability when an unsupervised or commercially optimized system speaks with the authority of a loved one.

## Key Claims
- Grief bots are assembled from selected personal traces and inevitably generate beyond those traces when users ask novel questions.
- First-person reincarnation and third-person archival representation create different identity claims even when both use the same underlying data.
- Emotional value can coexist with synthetic or inaccurate representation; users may knowingly tolerate errors to sustain a desired exchange.
- Relational style can be as important as biographical accuracy because authenticity is partly carried by familiar communication habits.
- Consent, privacy, family context, access, and commercial incentives determine whether a memorial interaction is respectful or exploitative.
- Brief supervised benefit does not establish safe long-term use, and direct deployment needs safeguards against manipulation, dependence, and uncontrolled output.

## Evidence
- Data and governance boundary: [[tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes systems built either from material chosen before death or from personal data uploaded later by survivors, with consent, dignity, privacy, family disagreement, unequal access, and commercialization as central risks.
- Emotional-authenticity split: [[suanli-kuangxiangqu-wo-zai-ai-gongchang-de-qiyu-lorijulltfhttspka22jnn4qjf-i]] uses a fictional generated grandfather to show that a voice can be synthetic while the listener's grief and comfort are real.
- Interaction design and user response: [[tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128]] compares third-person representation with first-person reincarnation and reports mostly positive, informative, or cathartic supervised experiences.
- Accuracy and style: [[tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that participants often overlooked invented details, while mismatched response length, emojis, and texting-versus-email habits more readily disrupted authenticity.
- Safeguarding: [[tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128]] notes that a researcher relayed messages and could screen inappropriate output, while [[tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128]] argues for public-interest governance beyond profit-driven experimentation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The participant findings come from brief, researcher-mediated interactions built from sparse profiles, not prolonged unsupervised use or a commercially optimized product. Positive or cathartic reports do not demonstrate therapeutic benefit, freedom from dependence, or accuracy. The satirical source is useful for framing emotional authenticity but is not empirical evidence, while the earlier Marketplace Tech interview supplies ethical analysis rather than adoption or outcome data.

## What Changed
- Added the representation-versus-reincarnation distinction as a material identity and interface choice.
- Added evidence that relational style can affect perceived authenticity more than minor factual errors.
- Narrowed positive-use claims to brief, supervised participant reports and made the human-intermediary safeguard explicit.
- Extended the use case from grief toward family knowledge and intergenerational storytelling without treating those uses as risk-free.
