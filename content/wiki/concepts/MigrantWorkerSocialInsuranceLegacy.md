# Migrant Worker Social Insurance Legacy / 农民工社保遗留问题

Migrant worker social insurance legacy is [[ruci-dagong-30-nian-liushuixian-shang-de-miankong-yu-shijian-lodsuktwtcfxnhmzwum5iesbzbme]]'s account of how early informal hiring becomes a retirement problem decades later. [[ZhanTeacher|占老师]] describes first-generation workers reaching retirement age while facing missing contributions, vanished employers, employers unwilling to back-pay, borrowed identity documents, and later networked social-security records that expose old inconsistencies.

The concept connects worker history to [[PersonalPensionAccount|pension]] and social-protection design. It shows that [[ManufacturingJobQuality]] includes administrative durability: a job that paid cash and overtime in the 1990s can still leave workers vulnerable if identity, contribution, and employer records do not survive into old age.

## Key Claims
- Social insurance problems can be delayed rather than absent; they become visible when workers age.
- Early hiring workarounds around IDs, certificates, and local registration can become formal proof problems later.
- Worker advocacy and NGO attention can help create 补缴-style remedies, but those remedies still depend on evidence, employer status, and policy implementation.
- Retirement protection is part of job quality, not a separate welfare issue after the fact.
