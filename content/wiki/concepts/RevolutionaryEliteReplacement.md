# Revolutionary Elite Replacement

## Definition

Revolutionary elite replacement is the risk that overthrowing a ruling class changes who occupies power without ending hierarchy, allowing a new group to reproduce or intensify the domination it opposed.

## Current Synthesis

[[208-george-orwell-glt5823999588]] derives the pattern from [[AnimalFarm]]. The animals' rebellion removes an old ruler, but the pigs occupy the institutional and ideological vacuum and become a new elite. The Soviet allegory remains central, yet the episode treats the mechanism as portable across revolutions: emancipatory language does not itself prevent concentrated authority.

The concept therefore separates revolutionary purpose from revolutionary outcome. A movement can begin with genuine grievance and egalitarian promise while still producing a hierarchy that controls resources, doctrine, and memory. Preventing replacement requires attention to institutions and accountability, not only the moral identity of those taking power.

## Key Claims

- Removing an incumbent elite does not automatically remove the structures that sustain hierarchy.
- Revolutionary legitimacy can become a resource through which a new governing group protects itself.
- Ideological language can conceal continuity between the old domination and the new order.
- The pattern extends beyond a one-to-one Soviet allegory while retaining that history as its immediate reference.
- Institutional accountability matters because declared solidarity is not a sufficient constraint on power.

## Evidence

- Narrative mechanism: [[208-george-orwell-glt5823999588]] interprets the pigs in *Animal Farm* as a new and potentially worse elite filling the vacuum left by revolution.
- Generalization: [[208-george-orwell-glt5823999588]] explicitly treats the fable as a warning about revolutions, not only a coded portrait of the Soviet Union.
- Ordinary-versus-elite structure: [[208-george-orwell-glt5823999588]] describes the ordinary animals as the populist moral center against the pigs' consolidation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One literary fable cannot establish that every revolution produces elite replacement or that all post-revolutionary institutions are equivalent. The concept identifies a risk mechanism, not an inevitable law. Generalizing it should not erase the specific history of the Russian Revolution, Stalinism, or Orwell's democratic-socialist commitments.

## What Changed

- Established a general risk model from the episode's reading of *Animal Farm*.
