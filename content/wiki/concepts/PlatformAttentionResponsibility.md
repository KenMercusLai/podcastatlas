# Platform Attention Responsibility / 平台注意力责任

## Definition

Platform attention responsibility is the principle that a platform's obligations expand when it converts a large share of public time into advertising, commerce, content distribution, and behavioral influence while also setting the rules for creators and users.

## Current Synthesis

Time spent is not merely a popularity measure. In an integrated platform it can connect recommendation, entertainment, purchasing, advertising, production tools, and creator settlement. Users still exercise choice and may receive legitimate entertainment, convenience, or knowledge, but the platform has asymmetric control over objectives, defaults, ranking, data, monetization, and enforcement. Responsibility therefore concerns how attention is acquired and converted, whether creators can sustain production, how minors and vulnerable users are protected, and whether the platform's technical and coordinating capacity produces public value rather than only longer sessions and higher extraction.

## Key Claims

- Usage time becomes governance-relevant when it is a shared input to recommendation, advertising, commerce, and content monetization.
- User agency and platform power coexist; personalized demand signals do not erase the platform's control over objectives, defaults, data, and market rules.
- Free access can broaden participation while shifting payment pressure into advertising load, paid traffic, ranking dependence, and creator revenue sharing.
- Control of discovery, production tools, traffic purchasing, and settlement can weaken creator bargaining power and narrow viable content toward cheap, fast-converting formats.
- Responsibility should scale with infrastructural power and include sustainable ecosystem returns, youth protection, content governance, and public-value deployment of technology.
- Effective governance must avoid both no-accountability self-regulation and blunt intervention that destroys useful production and employment.

## Evidence

### Attention as a commercial system

- [[99e9bb96133f-99e9bb96133f]] connects viewing time to advertising, live commerce, local purchasing, free short-drama consumption, and creator payouts rather than treating time as an isolated audience metric.

### Asymmetric control and creator dependence

- [[99e9bb96133f-99e9bb96133f]] argues that a platform combining traffic entry, recommendation, AI production tools, paid distribution, and settlement can capture more value and leave creators with weaker bargaining power.

### Public and ecosystem obligations

- [[99e9bb96133f-99e9bb96133f]] links platform scale to youth protection, narrative influence, sustainable revenue sharing, employment, emergency coordination, and technology directed toward practical social needs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source rejects a simple manipulation model: people have always sought low-effort entertainment, work and life circumstances affect media use, recommendation responds partly to user behavior, and entertainment need not be educational to be worthwhile. The reported time-share figures are disputed and lack a complete methodology in the supplied digest. The episode offers a normative argument and industry interpretation, not causal evidence that one platform produces specific social outcomes or that one revenue-sharing rule is optimal.

## What Changed

- Established a focused framework connecting platform time share to commercial conversion, creator bargaining power, and public responsibility.
