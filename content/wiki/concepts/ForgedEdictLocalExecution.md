## Definition

伪诏地方杀官 / Forged Edict Local Execution is the failure mode in which private enemies fabricate an imperial command and local officers obey it quickly enough to execute or arrest the targets before central verification catches the fraud. Hanji 797 grounds the concept in the case that kills Yao Guang and nearly kills Feng Huan.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-797-gudai-zhongxiao-zhende-wufa-liangquan-ma-lru9shbwfwwr9lluhhd7-xjuall6|Hanji 797]] shows the mechanism after [[YaoGuangLateHan|Yao Guang]] and [[FengHuanLateHan|Feng Huan]] repeatedly expose bad actors. Their enemies do not simply denounce them through normal channels; they counterfeit the emperor's command and direct Liaodong commandery commandant Pang Fen to act.

Pang Fen executes Yao Guang and arrests Feng Huan. Feng Huan is ready to kill himself, but his son Feng Gun notices that the command's wording seems wrong and urges a petition to court. Only after this challenge does the court confirm the forgery and summon Pang Fen for punishment.

The concept therefore marks a difference from ordinary slander or forged orders for resource extraction. Here, the imperial document form directly mobilizes local coercion against officials. The case's danger lies in speed: once the order is treated as authentic, death can occur before authentication catches up.

## Key Claims

- The mechanism turns private revenge into public violence by borrowing the form of imperial command.
- Local obedience is the immediate transmission belt; the forged document harms because Pang Fen executes before verification.
- Textual anomaly detection matters because Feng Gun's attention to wording interrupts the command chain.
- Central correction arrives only after damage: Yao Guang is already dead when the fraud is exposed.
- The case shows that document authority requires verification routines, not only loyal obedience.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently source-bounded to Hanji 797. The source does not establish the full legal protocol for verifying edicts, Pang Fen's precise culpability standard, or the later punishment outcome. It also remains distinct from forged-edict succession struggles and private construction orders: the current pattern is specifically local execution and arrest under counterfeit imperial authority.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to capture a forged-command failure that is lethal before central correction.
- Distinguished the case from existing forged-edict pages focused on succession or private extraction.
