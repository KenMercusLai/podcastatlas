## Definition

刚直理想主义的政治风险 / Rigid Idealism Political Risk is the danger that moral consistency, strict principle, or willingness to sacrifice is treated as sufficient for effective action despite asymmetric power, blocked information, weak protection, missing discretion, or severe costs imposed on other people.

## Current Synthesis

The power-asymmetry branch is clearest through [[WangZhangWesternHan|王章]] and [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]]. Wang Zhang's refusal to become [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]]'s dependent and attempt to bypass a controlled memorial route establish real integrity, but Wang Feng controls appointments and documents while [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] is weak. Zhou's branch adds both role transfer and repeated-objective conflict: battlefield directness carries into opposition over succession, unmerited kin reward, and Xiongnu defector titles, while the emperor, dowager, and prince each control a different protection channel.

Hanji 242 part 1 makes the strategic test more precise. Zhou's positions contain intelligible public principles: established succession should not be casually reopened, titles should track merit, and rewarding betrayal may damage loyalty norms. Yet objections alone do not solve the ruler's competing problems of succession control, family pressure, and frontier inducement. A principle becomes politically brittle when it is repeatedly presented as a veto without coalition, timing, graduated fallback, or an alternative that addresses the other decision-maker's objective. This does not make accommodation morally superior or prove that Zhou had a safe path.

The discretion branch is clearest through [[ZhaoYuWesternHan|赵禹]]. Refusing hospitality and requests can protect impartiality, but strict textual legality, harshness toward self and others, and unwillingness to hear alternatives can make clean principle unfit for complex responsibility. The gendered-honor branch widens the cost test: consistency is especially suspect when institutions convert bodily suffering or death into proof of virtue.

The synthesis is not that compromise is always wiser, direct remonstrance is futile, or every political death is self-caused. Moral purpose and independence remain valuable, but neither removes the need to judge leverage, timing, role, institutional access, principal reliability, context, proportion, and who bears the cost. Zhou's prosecution especially warns against converting a hostile ruler, family misconduct, ambiguous objects, and expansive legal interpretation into a simple morality tale about stubbornness.

## Key Claims

- Integrity and political effectiveness are separate judgments.
- Directness becomes dangerous when the target controls appointments, information flow, and punishment channels.
- A weak or reversible principal cannot reliably protect a subordinate who acts on private encouragement.
- Strict independence can reduce favoritism while still producing bad judgment if context, listening, and proportion disappear.
- Household members and socially subordinate groups may bear costs that the praised actor or institution discounts.
- Strategic flexibility can preserve moral agency when an objection also addresses the sovereign's competing objective through timing, coalition, or an alternative policy.
- A trait rewarded in military command may become hazardous in court, but role mismatch does not erase coercion or prove that the victim caused the prosecution.

Integrity under asymmetric power:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-518-jiemi-xihan-zuihou-de-zhengchen-wangzhang-lg8zeudpelxedglsyqp_slo9piva|Hanji 518]] says Wang Zhang resists dependency, confronts powerful actors, and tries a sealed route while Wang Feng controls appointments and the ordinary memorial channel.

Weak protection and transferred household risk:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-518-jiemi-xihan-zuihou-de-zhengchen-wangzhang-lg8zeudpelxedglsyqp_slo9piva|Hanji 518]] presents Han Chengdi as unable to guarantee protection and Wang Zhang's wife as recognizing the household cost of confrontation.

Impartiality without discretion:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-347-1-gudai-shi-ruhe-yong-lijiao-pua-zhenjie-lienv-de-lt39fogfivnipellq6y2xl6ctrzq|Hanji 347-1]] says Zhao Yu cuts off requests to act independently, while Zhou Yafu treats his strict legal use and harshness as reasons not to give him major responsibility.

Suffering as moral proof:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-347-1-gudai-shi-ruhe-yong-lijiao-pua-zhenjie-lienv-de-lt39fogfivnipellq6y2xl6ctrzq|Hanji 347-1]] closes by criticizing chastity honor that converts widowhood, injury, disfigurement, or death into exemplary virtue.

Battlefield-to-court role mismatch:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-242-cong-chengxiang-dao-mianguan-zhouyafu-fanle-zhichang-daji-1-lu6ohybs3wq9p9z-rmzj-j5vjcrs|Hanji 242 part 1]] supplies three distinct policy disputes and shows Zhou losing protection across emperor, dowager, and prince channels before dismissal; the Xiongnu case also preserves the unresolved tradeoff between inducement and loyalty signaling.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-244-beiwu-yu-sao-sitong-zhibuyi-weisha-bubianjie-2-lvmhp96-dchl52uxpym-prof-pb1a|Hanji 244 part 2]] presents Zhou's directness across succession, military, enfeoffment, banquet, and investigation scenes as politically costly, while the same source also supplies external mechanisms of danger: damaged trust, a worker's report, ambiguous burial goods, and a hostile rebellion theory.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept rests on moralized podcast accounts and should not become a rule that frank speech, impartiality, self-sacrifice, or principled refusal is irrational. Wang Zhang's sealed memorial shows strategic adaptation, and Hanji 518 does not prove a safer route could have corrected Wang Feng's power. Zhao Yu's relationship distance can be an anti-corruption safeguard; Hanji 347-1 supplies no case-by-case demonstration that a particular legal decision was wrong. Hanji 242 part 1 does not show whether Zhou offered alternatives, how much room he had to build a coalition, or whether compromise would have preserved either policy or safety; Jingdi's frontier incentives also lack outcome evidence. Hanji 244 part 2 cannot isolate Zhou's personality from Jingdi's choices, court alliances, family misconduct, evidentiary ambiguity, or judicial coercion. The chastity branch is only an introduction and does not establish prevalence, enforcement mechanism, or individual agency.

## What Changed

- Added repeated-objective conflict: one ministerial principle can collide separately with succession control, family pressure, and frontier inducement.
- Added the need for an objection to address the decision-maker's competing objective without treating coalition or compromise as always available.
- Preserved Zhou's loyalty-and-merit reasoning as substantive rather than reducing his conduct to personality, while keeping policy outcomes unresolved.
