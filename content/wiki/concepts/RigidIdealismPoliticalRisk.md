## Definition

刚直理想主义的政治风险 / Rigid Idealism Political Risk is the danger that a morally serious actor treats integrity and willingness to sacrifice as sufficient for effective action despite asymmetric power, blocked information channels, weak protection, or severe costs imposed on dependants.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-518-jiemi-xihan-zuihou-de-zhengchen-wangzhang-lg8zeudpelxedglsyqp_slo9piva|Hanji 518]] builds the concept through [[WangZhangWesternHan|王章]]. His refusal to become [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]]'s dependent, earlier conflict with [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]], and attempt to bypass the controlled memorial route establish real integrity. Yet the source also makes effectiveness depend on conditions beyond courage: Wang Feng controls appointments and document flow, [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] is politically weak, and [[WangZhangWifeWesternHan|王章之妻]] recognizes that the household will bear the cost of a low-probability confrontation.

The synthesis is not that compromise is always wiser or that direct remonstrance is futile. It is that moral purpose does not remove the need to judge leverage, timing, institutional access, principal reliability, and transferred risk. A rigid actor may preserve personal consistency while losing the capacity to change the outcome.

## Key Claims

- Integrity and political effectiveness are separate judgments.
- Directness becomes especially dangerous when the target controls appointments, information flow, and punishment channels.
- A weak or reversible principal cannot reliably protect a subordinate who acts on private encouragement.
- Household members can perceive transferred political risk that the committed actor discounts.
- Strategic flexibility can preserve future agency without requiring moral surrender.

## Evidence

Integrity and repeated opposition:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-518-jiemi-xihan-zuihou-de-zhengchen-wangzhang-lg8zeudpelxedglsyqp_slo9piva|Hanji 518]] says Wang Zhang accepted Wang Feng's recommendations without becoming his dependent and had earlier lost office after confronting Shi Xian.

Asymmetric power and blocked channels:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-518-jiemi-xihan-zuihou-de-zhengchen-wangzhang-lg8zeudpelxedglsyqp_slo9piva|Hanji 518]] presents Wang Feng as able to veto a close imperial appointment, remove Liu Kang through eclipse politics, and control the ordinary Shangshu memorial route.

Transferred household risk:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-518-jiemi-xihan-zuihou-de-zhengchen-wangzhang-lg8zeudpelxedglsyqp_slo9piva|Hanji 518]] says Wang Zhang's wife first recalls him from despair during poverty and later invokes that same past to warn against an unwinnable confrontation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept rests here on one moralized podcast account and should not be generalized into a rule that frank speech or self-sacrifice is irrational. Wang Zhang's sealed memorial also shows strategic adaptation rather than pure rigidity, and the source does not establish that a safer method could have corrected Wang Feng's power. The later outcome is supplied by Hanji 519, not independently demonstrated by this source alone.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 518's explicit contrast between moral firmness and effective political judgment.
- Distinguished strategic flexibility from moral capitulation.
- Made household risk and controlled information channels part of the effectiveness test.
