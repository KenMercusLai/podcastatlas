# 诸侯王羞辱触发谋刺 / Vassal Humiliation Conspiracy

诸侯王羞辱触发谋刺 / vassal humiliation conspiracy is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-178-maodun-weihe-yao-fangguo-liubang-zhe-cai-shi-zhenxiang-2-loluh53iyrzo4ucf3ttzmn3-otkv|Hanji 178 part 2]] draws from [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s treatment of [[ZhangAo|张敖]] at [[Handan|邯郸]]. Zhang Ao receives Liu Bang with son-in-law deference, but Liu Bang's coarse insults make [[GuanGao|贯高]], [[ZhaoWuZhaoMinister|赵午]], and other Zhao ministers treat the scene as an attack on their king's status.

The concept is not ordinary assassination planning. The target is the emperor, but the emotional trigger is vassal-rank humiliation. Zhang Ao himself refuses the idea and insists that Zhao owes Liu Bang gratitude; the conspiracy forms because ministers reinterpret loyalty to their lord as requiring private violence against the overlord who humiliated him.

This sits beside [[RitualizedMinisterHumiliation|礼节化臣属羞辱]] and [[StatusInsultDefectionCascade|名分羞辱触发叛变]], but its mechanism is distinct. A ruler humiliates a dependent king, the king refuses rebellion, and subordinate ministers try to absorb both moral agency and punishment risk themselves.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-179-lishishang-heqin-zenme-laide-lslks-ovbomneornk4j-q6-ns0x1|Hanji 179]] carries the pattern from intent into attempted execution. Guan Gao and Zhao Wu hide assassins in the walls at 百人县, expecting Liu Bang to lodge there, but Liu Bang leaves after treating the place name as ominous. The concept therefore now includes a full sequence: insult, loyal refusal by Zhang Ao, private ministerial planning, and a failed ambush that keeps the ruler alive by contingency rather than repaired trust.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-1-li5u-dmsmb1lp4ny3rk02kaiyu4k|Hanji 180]] adds the legal-political aftermath. Once the plot is reported, the conspiracy no longer remains a hidden status response; it becomes the basis for arresting Zhang Ao and dismantling his Zhao kingship even though Guan Gao's testimony clears him personally. The sequence now ends with exoneration, demotion, and a Liu-family replacement through [[LiuRuyi|刘如意]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-2-lutkapdijrgpwfrz3saaowxtk-i2|Hanji 180 part 2]] qualifies the chain without erasing it. [[SimaGuang|司马光]] treats Liu Bang's arrogance as one cause of ministerial betrayal, but the host says insult cannot justify Guan Gao's decision to kill the ruler. The same episode also narrows the Zhang Ao consequence: the plot may explain the timing of his loss, while Liu Bang's broader different-surname king policy explains why the loss was likely.

## Key Claims
- Hierarchical insult can become politically dangerous when it is read as degrading not only a person but a vassal polity's standing.
- The humiliated lord may remain loyal while his ministers radicalize on his behalf.
- Attempts to protect the lord from responsibility can make private conspiracy more likely, not less.
- The episode treats etiquette as part of state security: crude ruler behavior can damage the political order that formal hierarchy is meant to stabilize.
- Hanji 179 adds that unrepaired status injury can survive the initial rebuke and become a near-executed field assassination attempt.
- Hanji 180 adds that even failed and disowned conspiracy can give the center a usable reason to reduce the dependent king's status.
- Hanji 180 part 2 adds that a status insult can be a real trigger without becoming moral justification, and that the later title removal may have a broader policy cause.
