# Boomless Cruise

## Definition
Boomless cruise is [[BoomSupersonic]]'s proposed operating technique for flying supersonically over land while using atmospheric refraction to prevent the aircraft's sonic boom from reaching the ground.

## Current Synthesis
The sources distinguish boomless cruise from an airframe that produces no boom or merely softens it. The aircraft still creates a normal-strength shock wave, but weather, altitude, speed, software, and engine control are used to keep the wave refracting upward. The earlier source explains why the capability emerged only after Boom's engine path changed; the newer source gives a claimed practical ceiling near Mach 1.3, roughly 50% faster than current airliners. This is a conditional operational envelope, not a universal promise of silent supersonic flight.

## Key Claims
- Boomless cruise redirects the boom through atmospheric cutoff rather than eliminating the shock wave at its source.
- Safe operation depends on measured conditions, altitude, speed, software calculation, and sufficient engine control.
- The claimed overland envelope reaches roughly Mach 1.3, below Overture's proposed overwater Mach 1.7 operation.
- Engine ownership matters because propulsion and flight profile must be jointly optimized for the cutoff regime.
- The capability weakens the rationale for a categorical speed ban only if it works reliably across operationally useful conditions.

## Evidence
- Capability origin and system dependencies: [[tsr-s5-blakescholl-v3-finalaudio-tsr-s5-blakescholl-v3-finalaudio]] connects atmospheric cutoff to weather, software, engine performance, and the post-[[RollsRoyce|Rolls-Royce]] engine path.
- Mechanism and speed claim: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-blake-scholl-why-plane-speed-stalled-supersonic-commercial-flight-revolutionizing-the-engine-42987238]] explicitly distinguishes redirection from softening and gives the approximate Mach 1.3 ceiling.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both explanations come from Boom's founder and do not supply independent operational data across routes, seasons, weather states, or dispatch constraints. A boom that misses the ground under modeled conditions is not equivalent to an aircraft that never produces one, and the transcript gives little treatment to off-nominal conditions, monitoring, certification, community acceptance, or other environmental effects.

## What Changed
- Clarified that the technique redirects a normal-strength boom rather than suppressing it at the aircraft.
- Added the claimed Mach 1.3 operating ceiling and explicit overwater/overland distinction.
- Strengthened the qualification that the capability is conditional on atmospheric and operational state.
