## Definition

权力欲自律 / Power Desire Self-Restraint is the pattern where holding any degree of authority requires active restraint against using it for private anger, status maximization, procedural domination, property coercion, truth suppression, or revenge. The concept treats power desire as especially dangerous because even small powers can be pushed to their limit when no internal check or external feedback stops them.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 752 now supplies the earliest Dou-family branch. [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]] and [[DouDuEasternHan|Dou Du]] gain favor and access through [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dou]] under [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]]. [[FifthLunEasternHan|Fifth Lun]] warns that this outer-relative access and guest network are dangerous. Dou Xian then forcefully takes [[QinshuiPrincessEasternHan|Qinshui Princess]]'s estate and tries to prevent the truth from being spoken when Zhangdi asks about it. This is power desire before full regency dominance: access becomes entitlement, property coercion, and information control.

Hanji 759 gives the explicit theme after Zhangdi's death. Empress Dowager Dou holds regency authority, Dou Xian and the Dou brothers gain near-body court access, and [[CuiYinEasternHan|Cui Yin]] warns Dou that outer relatives born into rank and favor often fall through arrogance. The warning fails in practice. [[DengBiaoEasternHan|Deng Biao]] becomes a formal shell for Dou's procedure, Dou channels old resentment into the killing of Han Yu's son, and Cui's fake-edict solution removes five royal princes' pressure.

The current synthesis is that power desire becomes dangerous before full tyranny appears. The signs are early: treating access as entitlement, using prestige to take property, turning process into cover, suppressing truth, answering injury with private revenge, and neutralizing correction instead of accepting it.

## Key Claims

- Power desire is dangerous because it pushes officeholders and protected relatives to extend authority beyond public purpose.
- Small or early powers can become abusive when the holder treats access, kinship, or procedural position as personal property.
- Outer-relative privilege is especially exposed because family access can outrun office discipline.
- Talent attraction and procedural skill can strengthen rather than restrain a dangerous powerholder.
- Private grievance becomes politically destructive when state or palace channels can be used to avenge it.
- Truth suppression and feedback removal are evidence that power desire has outrun self-restraint.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept remains bounded to Hanji 752 and Hanji 759 and should not be treated as a complete theory of political ethics. Hanji 759 also shows Dou Xian attracting real talent, and later source notes show that his campaign can succeed militarily, so power-desire failure does not imply total incapacity. Hanji 752 also records Zhangdi's partial response: the estate is returned and Dou is not entrusted with heavy responsibility under Zhangdi. The concept's narrower claim is that ability, rank, and partial correction do not remove the need to restrain private uses of power.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 752 as the earliest branch: Dou-family access, Fifth Lun's warning, Qinshui Princess estate coercion, and truth suppression.
- Reframed Hanji 759's explicit power-desire theme as an escalation from already visible Zhangdi-era behavior.
- Added property coercion and truth suppression to the concept's diagnostic signs.
