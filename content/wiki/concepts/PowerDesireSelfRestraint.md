## Definition

权力欲自律 / Power Desire Self-Restraint is the pattern where holding any degree of authority requires active restraint against using it for private anger, status maximization, procedural domination, or revenge. The concept treats power desire as especially dangerous because even small powers can be pushed to their limit when no internal check or external feedback stops them.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 759 defines the current concept through [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]]'s early rise after [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]]'s death. [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dowager Dou]] holds regency authority, Dou Xian and the Dou brothers gain near-body court access, and [[CuiYinEasternHan|Cui Yin]] warns Dou that outer relatives born into rank and favor often fall through arrogance.

The source then shows the warning failing in practice. [[DengBiaoEasternHan|Deng Biao]] becomes a formal shell for Dou's procedure, Dou channels old resentment into the killing of Han Yu's son, and Cui's fake-edict solution removes five royal princes' pressure. The episode generalizes from high office to ordinary settings: a person with even petty authority can be tempted to maximize it unless restrained.

The current synthesis is that power desire becomes dangerous before full tyranny appears. The signs are early: treating access as entitlement, turning process into cover, answering injury with private revenge, and neutralizing correction instead of accepting it.

## Key Claims

- Power desire is dangerous because it pushes officeholders to extend authority beyond public purpose.
- Small powers can become abusive when the holder treats them as personal assets.
- Outer-relative privilege is especially exposed because family access can outrun office discipline.
- Talent attraction and procedural skill can strengthen rather than restrain a dangerous powerholder.
- Private grievance becomes politically destructive when state or palace channels can be used to avenge it.
- Feedback suppression is itself evidence that power desire has outrun self-restraint.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in a single source note and should not be treated as a complete theory of political ethics. Hanji 759 also shows Dou Xian attracting real talent, and later source notes show that his campaign can succeed militarily, so power-desire failure does not imply total incapacity. The concept's claim is narrower: ability and achievement do not remove the need to restrain private uses of power.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 759's explicit power-desire theme.
- Bounded the concept to early Dou-family access, Cui Yin's warning, Deng Biao's procedural shell, private revenge, and feedback suppression.
