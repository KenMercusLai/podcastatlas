# Science-Based Tool Taxonomy

## Definition
A science-based tool taxonomy classifies practical levers by intervention type rather than treating “tools” as a synonym for tips, habits, or products. It can include actions, avoidances, nutrition, supplements, prescription drugs, brain-machine interfaces, and monitoring or intervention devices.

## Current Synthesis
The launch statement in [[scim5670648609-scim5670648609]] defines the practical scope of [[HubermanLab]] broadly while promising discussion of advantages and disadvantages. The useful distinction is between inclusion and endorsement: a category can be scientifically discussable without being safe, effective, appropriate, or self-administered for every person. Behavioral practices and avoidances sit beside progressively more context-dependent nutritional, supplemental, medical, and technological interventions.

## Key Claims
- Practical tools include both actions to take and exposures or behaviors to avoid.
- Nutrition, supplements, prescription drugs, and devices are distinct intervention classes with different evidence and supervision needs.
- Brain-machine interfaces and monitoring technologies belong to the scope of practical science discussion without automatically being mature consumer tools.
- Benefits and disadvantages should be evaluated together.
- Naming a tool category does not establish efficacy, safety, dose, indication, or individual suitability.

## Evidence
- Behavioral and avoidance scope: [[scim5670648609-scim5670648609]] includes practices affecting focus, sleep, learning, and plasticity as well as behaviors to avoid.
- Biological intervention scope: [[scim5670648609-scim5670648609]] separately names nutrition, supplementation, and prescription drugs.
- Technology scope: [[scim5670648609-scim5670648609]] includes brain-machine interfaces and devices that monitor or alter the nervous system or biology.
- Tradeoff requirement: [[scim5670648609-scim5670648609]] says tools will be discussed with their advantages and disadvantages.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies a show taxonomy, not an evidence hierarchy or clinical decision rule. It contains no study review, dosing guidance, contraindication analysis, or proof that every named category produces useful outcomes. Prescription drugs, supplements, invasive interfaces, and intervention devices require evidence and safety standards beyond their inclusion in a podcast's editorial scope.

## What Changed
- Created an umbrella taxonomy for the launch episode's unusually broad definition of practical tools.
- Made explicit that editorial inclusion, scientific plausibility, evidence strength, and personal suitability are separate judgments.
