## Definition
Jealousy as Political Violence is the pattern in which status anxiety turns another person's ability, legitimacy, or popularity into a perceived survival threat, making elimination seem rational to an insecure superior or rival.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 768 gives the current core case through [[AnGuoSouthernXiongnu|An Guo]] and [[ShiziChanyu|Shizi]]. Shizi's talent, lineage, Han court trust, and Xiongnu respect should have made him a stabilizing asset, but An Guo experienced that success as a direct threat. The resulting plot demonstrates how jealousy can convert normal hierarchy management into attempted political killing.

Hanji 576 adds a palace-memory version through [[FuTaihouWesternHan|傅太后]] and [[FengYuanWesternHan|冯媛]]. Feng Yuan's old bear-rescue courage and [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]'s affection do not threaten Fu Taihou in a direct military sense, but they remain a status wound. When [[ZhangYouWesternHan|张尤]] supplies a false accusation, jealousy becomes politically violent because Fu Taihou has enough authority to choose an investigator and punish the official who asks for review.

## Key Claims
- Jealousy becomes politically dangerous when ability and legitimacy accumulate in one subordinate or collateral figure.
- Insecure authority can mistake another person's usefulness for a threat to survival.
- External validation intensifies envy because it proves the rival's value to audiences outside the jealous ruler's control.
- Protecting the target often requires managing exposure, meetings, and access before open violence begins.
- Jealous plots can destroy the plotter when internal opponents and external overseers converge around the endangered target.
- Old status humiliation can remain politically dangerous long after the original comparison, especially when later office or dowager power creates a chance to act.

## Evidence
- Accumulated status threat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says Shizi was brave, strategic, trusted by earlier chanyus and the Han court, and respected by Southern Xiongnu people.
- Insecure ruler response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says An Guo envied Shizi and aligned with surrendered Northern Xiongnu who hated him.
- Exposure management: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says Huangfu Leng protected Shizi by keeping him from An Guo's court meetings.
- Plotter collapse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says An Guo's failed move against Shizi ended with An Guo killed by his uncle Xi and Shizi installed.
- Palace jealousy memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-576-dangxiong-jiu-huangdi-de-ta-weihe-bei-qingdi-bisi-ll3pgr_smwlebpgo1vlufhe6qll6|Hanji 576]] says Feng Yuan's bear-rescue courage and Yuandi's affection left Fu Taihou jealous.
- Later coercive opportunity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-576-dangxiong-jiu-huangdi-de-ta-weihe-bei-qingdi-bisi-ll3pgr_smwlebpgo1vlufhe6qll6|Hanji 576]] says Fu Taihou uses Zhang You's accusation and Shi Li's investigation against Feng Yuan, leading to suicide and family deaths.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept now rests on two cases but should still not be treated as a universal explanation for every succession conflict, palace accusation, or frontier intervention. Du Chong's complaint interception and the broader Southern Xiongnu succession context may have added strategic motives beyond jealousy alone. In Hanji 576, Zhang You's self-protection and Shi Li's career incentive also matter; jealousy explains why the accusation found a powerful sponsor, not every procedural step.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 576's palace-memory branch: old jealousy of Feng Yuan becomes lethal when a false accusation creates an opportunity.
- Preserved Hanji 768's frontier-succession branch through An Guo and Shizi.
- Linked jealousy to concrete political mechanics: access, factional allies, complaint control, investigator choice, and accusation sponsorship.
