## Definition
Jealousy as Political Violence is the pattern in which status anxiety turns another person's ability, legitimacy, or popularity into a perceived survival threat, making elimination seem rational to an insecure superior or rival.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 768 gives the current core case through [[AnGuoSouthernXiongnu|An Guo]] and [[ShiziChanyu|Shizi]]. Shizi's talent, lineage, Han court trust, and Xiongnu respect should have made him a stabilizing asset, but An Guo experienced that success as a direct threat. The resulting plot demonstrates how jealousy can convert normal hierarchy management into attempted political killing.

## Key Claims
- Jealousy becomes politically dangerous when ability and legitimacy accumulate in one subordinate or collateral figure.
- Insecure authority can mistake another person's usefulness for a threat to survival.
- External validation intensifies envy because it proves the rival's value to audiences outside the jealous ruler's control.
- Protecting the target often requires managing exposure, meetings, and access before open violence begins.
- Jealous plots can destroy the plotter when internal opponents and external overseers converge around the endangered target.

## Evidence
- Accumulated status threat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says Shizi was brave, strategic, trusted by earlier chanyus and the Han court, and respected by Southern Xiongnu people.
- Insecure ruler response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says An Guo envied Shizi and aligned with surrendered Northern Xiongnu who hated him.
- Exposure management: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says Huangfu Leng protected Shizi by keeping him from An Guo's court meetings.
- Plotter collapse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-768-lishi-gaosu-ni-jidu-shi-shaxin-lgk8qoxvs5ax8oikgecrxqvispna|Hanji 768]] says An Guo's failed move against Shizi ended with An Guo killed by his uncle Xi and Shizi installed.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept currently rests on one strong case, so it should not be treated as a universal explanation for every succession conflict or frontier intervention. Du Chong's complaint interception and the broader Southern Xiongnu succession context may have added strategic motives beyond jealousy alone.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 768's title-level claim and An Guo-Shizi case.
- Linked jealousy to concrete political mechanics: access, factional allies, complaint control, and succession replacement.
