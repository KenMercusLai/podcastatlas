# Joint Supplement and Treatment Boundary / 关节补剂与治疗边界

## Definition
Joint supplement and treatment boundary / 关节补剂与治疗边界 distinguishes nutrients and health products that may support intake or symptoms from interventions that diagnose, repair, or treat joint, cartilage, bone, and pain conditions.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-90-yundong-yixue-pengyou-ni-huaxue-ma-bugai-yao-chenzao-kemu-san-jinji-renqun-lvfameb1ztoxm5yx5e-ylsrekbsq]] applies this boundary to glucosamine, calcium, and bone broth. Glucosamine is discussed as a cartilage-matrix-related supplement that may offer limited symptom support in some contexts, not as a drug that regenerates cartilage cells or reverses established structural damage. Calcium advice is framed around gradual age-related loss rather than guaranteed restoration, while bone broth is rejected as a direct equivalent of bone or calcium replacement.

VOL.150 sharpens the route and subgroup boundaries. It treats a topical product marketed domestically as “glucosamine” as an especially weak claim when the original product description and plausible delivery to the joint do not match that identity. Oral glucosamine remains more qualified: the episode describes guideline-adjacent adjunct use and a large trial with no overall advantage but a possible combination signal in a more severe subgroup. That account does not establish universal benefit, disease modification, or cartilage restoration.

The reusable judgment is not whether a product sounds biologically related to a tissue. It is whether the labeled ingredient and route can plausibly reach the target, whether there is an actual nutritional or symptom need, what outcome was measured, how strong the evidence is, and whether pain or functional change requires assessment and established treatment instead of consumer self-care.

## Key Claims
- A nutrient being involved in cartilage or bone biology does not prove that oral supplementation rebuilds damaged tissue.
- Supplements and foods should not be presented as substitutes for diagnosis, indicated medication, rehabilitation, or surgery.
- Benefit, if any, can depend on baseline need, disease stage, symptom target, dose, and individual context.
- Pain that affects activity or daily life should be clinically assessed rather than masked through indefinite reliance on health products.
- Food resemblance is not treatment evidence: bone-derived food is not automatically a meaningful calcium or bone therapy.
- Route and relabeling matter: a topical massage product does not become joint-targeted glucosamine therapy because a reseller gives it that name.

## Evidence
- Glucosamine boundary: [[vol-90-yundong-yixue-pengyou-ni-huaxue-ma-bugai-yao-chenzao-kemu-san-jinji-renqun-lvfameb1ztoxm5yx5e-ylsrekbsq]] describes glucosamine as a supplement related to cartilage matrix while rejecting it as a fundamental repair for cartilage cells.
- Treatment escalation: [[vol-90-yundong-yixue-pengyou-ni-huaxue-ma-bugai-yao-chenzao-kemu-san-jinji-renqun-lvfameb1ztoxm5yx5e-ylsrekbsq]] says pain affecting life should prompt care and established treatment rather than reliance on glucosamine alone.
- Calcium and food claims: [[vol-90-yundong-yixue-pengyou-ni-huaxue-ma-bugai-yao-chenzao-kemu-san-jinji-renqun-lvfameb1ztoxm5yx5e-ylsrekbsq]] frames calcium loss as gradual and says bone broth mainly supplies fat rather than functioning as direct bone replacement.
- Topical and oral glucosamine split: [[vol-150-dawu-shiye-dabainiu-nmn-antang-huganpian-fumei-zhexie-baokuan-baojianpin-zhende-kaopu-ma-lhsvjvjkitjfjduycbp1mvrw4n2o|VOL.150]] rejects the relabeled topical route while preserving oral glucosamine as a disputed adjunct with no reported overall trial advantage and a possible subgroup combination signal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide a full systematic review of trials, topical pharmacokinetics, product formulation, guideline versions, deficiency testing, dietary intake, kidney risk, medication interactions, or comparative treatment outcomes. VOL.150's dose, guideline status, trial size, severity subgroup, combination result, and foreign-product description are speaker-reported. A subgroup signal does not establish a confirmed indication, and supplement decisions can require individualized nutritional and medical review.

## What Changed
- Added route-to-target and product-relabeling checks for topical glucosamine.
- Clarified that oral glucosamine's possible adjunct or subgroup symptom role does not establish universal efficacy, cartilage repair, or disease reversal.
