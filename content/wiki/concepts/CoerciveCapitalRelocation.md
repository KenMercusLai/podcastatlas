# Coercive Capital Relocation / 强制迁都

Coercive capital relocation / 强制迁都 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-951-tianjiang-hengcai-jiujing-shi-haoshi-haishi-huaishi-lovwgng2wrtfno-s9ttpybbhksvh|Hanji 951]] as the dark late-Han variant of [[StrategicCapitalRelocation|strategic capital relocation]]. [[DongZhuo|董卓]] can make a real military argument for moving [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] from [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] to [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]]: [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] has defensible approaches, stronger western support, and historical capital infrastructure.

The concept turns on the execution method. In the source, strategic geography does not remain separate from coercion: Dong Zhuo rejects consultation, removes or kills dissenting officials, confiscates wealthy households, executes people under accusations, and drives the population west under military force. The move therefore damages the political order it claims to protect.

## Key Claims

- A capital move can be strategically rational and still politically destructive if it is implemented through terror and extraction.
- Coercive relocation converts geography into domination: defensive terrain becomes a way to secure the powerholder, not the public.
- Forced population movement, confiscation, and elite punishment make the destination city only one part of the event; the origin city's social and ritual order is also attacked.
- The pattern differs from ordinary [[StrategicCapitalRelocation|strategic capital relocation]] because consent, institutional debate, and public costs are suppressed rather than managed.
- Hanji 951 ties this pattern to [[WarDisasterCompounding|war-disaster compounding]]: troops, migration, plunder, and civilian killing combine into a social disaster.
