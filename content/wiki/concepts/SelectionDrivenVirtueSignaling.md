# 选官驱动的德行表演 / Selection-Driven Virtue Signaling

## Definition

选官驱动的德行表演 describes a response to personnel incentives in which officials make the traits favored by selectors publicly visible—such as frugality, modesty, or plain appearance—because advancement and safety depend on appearing to satisfy the standard.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1087-simayi-zhuangbing-ju-caocao-yi-tingshuo-yao-bei-zhua-miao-song-likur-xfjqbk3kr-ew6ftvtwixq|Hanji 1087]] supplies the initial case. Once [[CuiYanLateHan|崔琰]] and [[MaoJie|毛玠]] are said to reject flashy reputation, falseness, and faction-building while rewarding substance and humility, elite conduct visibly shifts toward rough carts, unadorned appearance, and walking to court. The episode calls this “表演性节俭”: the behavior may improve public norms, yet the visible act cannot by itself distinguish sincere virtue from strategic compliance.

The concept therefore separates behavioral effect from moral truth. A selection rule can successfully make conduct more austere and socially imitable even when actors follow it because luxury has become risky. The same mechanism can also produce exaggeration, conformity, and enforcement vulnerability when a ruler values obedience more than the announced virtue.

## Key Claims

- Personnel criteria act as incentives before any formal punishment is applied.
- Visible frugality can be both socially consequential and privately strategic.
- A collective behavioral shift does not prove collective moral conversion.
- Rule-setters can govern indirectly by making selectors, promotion, and reputational risk reinforce one another.
- Virtue standards become politically dangerous when their application depends on discretionary loyalty tests.

## Evidence

- Incentive transmission: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1087-simayi-zhuangbing-ju-caocao-yi-tingshuo-yao-bei-zhua-miao-song-likur-xfjqbk3kr-ew6ftvtwixq|Hanji 1087]] links Cui Yan and Mao Jie's selection preferences to widespread adoption of rough carts, plain appearance, and walking.
- Behavioral ambiguity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1087-simayi-zhuangbing-ju-caocao-yi-tingshuo-yao-bei-zhua-miao-song-likur-xfjqbk3kr-ew6ftvtwixq|Hanji 1087]] explicitly interprets the pattern as performative because frugality becomes a safety and promotion asset.
- Power boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1087-simayi-zhuangbing-ju-caocao-yi-tingshuo-yao-bei-zhua-miao-song-likur-xfjqbk3kr-ew6ftvtwixq|Hanji 1087]] uses Cui Yan's death and Mao Jie's imprisonment to argue that moral criteria do not independently bind concentrated power.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one interpretive podcast episode. Plain conduct may reflect mixed motives, including sincere frugality, imitation, fear, career calculation, or real norm internalization; the source does not measure their relative weight. Its claim of a broad social transformation, its causal attribution to the selectors, and its reading of Cui Yan and Mao Jie's later punishment require corroboration from primary texts and wider scholarship.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the episode's link between official-selection rules and collective “performative frugality.”
