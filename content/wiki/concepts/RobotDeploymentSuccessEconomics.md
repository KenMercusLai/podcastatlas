# Robot Deployment Success Economics

## Definition
Robot deployment success economics is the commercial test that a robot must satisfy through high success rate, fast deployment, low adaptation cost, measurable ROI, and sustained customer payment.

## Current Synthesis
The Shizilukou Crossing episode reframes robotics commercialization around repeatable production value rather than demo impressiveness. [[HanZheng]] stresses high first-pass success and generalization without heavy post-training. [[WangQianZibianliang]] gives the cleanest business metric: customers keep paying and pay more. [[XuHuazhe]] separates scene-specific post-training revenue from the longer foundation-model ambition, while [[ShenYujun]] warns that deployment economics depend on full system design, not model performance alone.

## Key Claims
- Business robotics needs much higher success tolerance than many digital AI workflows because physical errors break objects, block work, or require human recovery.
- Repeat customer payment is stronger evidence than pilots, demos, funding announcements, or one-time sales.
- Deployment speed and replication across sites matter because post-training and integration hours can erase the economic advantage.
- ROI depends on the whole system: hardware form, whether a full humanoid is necessary, robustness, maintenance, cost, scene design, and customer workflow.
- Near-term cash flow may come from bounded deployments, while long-term upside depends on improving general task success.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide audited customer-retention data, deployment-hour distributions, or ROI calculations. It gives an operator consensus about what commercialization must prove, not proof that any one participant has solved it at scale.

## What Changed
- Added a success-economics concept that ties success rate, deployment replication, ROI, and customer renewal into one commercialization test.
- The current judgment distinguishes robot commercialization from digital AI because failed physical actions have higher visible cost.
