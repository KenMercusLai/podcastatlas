## Definition

Leader-worthiness assessment is the judgment process by which followers decide whether a not-yet-secure leader is worth joining. In the current evidence, the key standard is not present size alone but whether the leader can survive crisis, accept useful advice, recognize different kinds of talent, enforce credible discipline, and create room for followers to earn merit.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-670-juejing-qiusheng-liuxiu-xianru-shengsi-da-weiji-ltz1nciofev4cqjkod-p8mmwovhe|Hanji 670]] pushes the concept one step earlier than the Shanggu-Yuyang coalition. [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] is still fleeing [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] and reaches Xindu with limited force. [[RenGuangEasternHan|任光]] and [[PiTongEasternHan|邳彤]] are willing to receive him precisely before victory is visible; Pi Tong's advice then tests whether Liu Xiu can choose a risky but higher-upside strategy instead of being escorted back to Chang'an.

The same source broadens worth-following judgment beyond initial strategy. Ren Guang's proclamations and torch displays make weak numbers look like momentum, [[LiuZhiChangchengEasternHan|刘植]] and [[GengChunEasternHan|耿纯]] bring local armed commitment, and [[JiZunEasternHan|祭遵]]'s household-servant execution tests whether Liu Xiu will let discipline reach his own people. Liu Xiu becomes more worth backing because he accepts advice at both ends: staying in Hebei after Pi Tong's counsel and promoting Ji Zun after advisers explain the value of impartial enforcement.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] frames the question through [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] before Hebei has fully turned. [[DengYuEasternHan|邓禹]] answers Liu Xiu's worry over having only one commandery by saying that troubled people seek a wise ruler and that rise depends on 德才 rather than territory. [[KouXunEasternHan|寇恂]] and Min Ye then apply that standard politically: Handan's [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] claim is uncertain, while Liu Xiu's reputation for respectful treatment of worthies makes him the better long-term bet.

The pattern is not passive admiration. Kou Xun contacts [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]], [[WuHanEasternHan|吴汉]] uses information from a scholar and a forged proclamation to push Yuyang toward Liu Xiu, and [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]] helps connect Shanggu force to Liu Xiu. Liu Xiu then strengthens the assessment by rewarding the arriving commanders quickly, letting them keep original troops, and making alignment look like a chance to build shared merit.

The episode also makes receptivity part of the test. When the Julu siege stalls, [[GengChunEasternHan|耿纯]] argues that Liu Xiu should strike Handan while morale is high because Julu will submit after Wang Lang falls. Liu Xiu adopts the advice, so leader-worthiness includes the capacity to update strategy when a subordinate sees the better move.

## Key Claims

- Worth-following judgment can begin while the leader is still in flight, if local actors see a survivable base and a credible strategic opening.
- Worth-following leaders can be judged under weakness by reputation, virtue, capacity, and advice receptivity rather than only by present territory.
- Followers in a collapsing political field assess comparative futures: Kou Xun and Min Ye prefer Liu Xiu because Wang Lang's Handan claim looks uncertain.
- Allegiance often requires active brokers, not only private conviction; Kou Xun and Wu Han turn a judgment into a coalition.
- Reward design matters because Liu Xiu's quick appointments and troop-retention decisions make new followers believe they can build merit inside his order.
- Receptivity to strategic correction is part of leader-worthiness: Liu Xiu adopts Geng Chun's Handan-first advice after Julu stalls.
- Discipline credibility also matters: Liu Xiu strengthens his backing field when he accepts Ji Zun's enforcement against his own household.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept is based on two podcast source notes and should remain a compact Eastern Han pattern until more evidence accumulates. The episodes' workplace-leadership framing is interpretive, not a standalone historical proof. Hanji 670 also shows perception management through proclamations and torch displays, while Hanji 671 shows Wu Han using a forged proclamation; leader-worthiness can be advanced by questionable political technique. Finally, the sources do not deny the importance of force, reward, and timing; they argue that those factors become persuasive when joined to perceived survival capacity, virtue, competence, discipline, and opportunity.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 670 as the earlier Xindu survival, Pi Tong advice, local armed-support, and Ji Zun discipline layer.
- Preserved Hanji 671 as the explicit "what kind of leader is worth following" frame through Deng Yu, Kou Xun, Wu Han, and Geng Chun.
- Reframed the concept from reputation and merit opportunity alone into a broader pre-victory assessment of survival, advice receptivity, disciplined order, and costly follower commitment.
