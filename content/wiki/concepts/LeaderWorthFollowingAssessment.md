## Definition

Leader-worthiness assessment is the judgment process by which followers decide whether a not-yet-secure leader is worth joining. In the current evidence, the key standard is not present size alone but whether the leader has virtue and capacity, treats worthy people seriously, accepts useful advice, recognizes different kinds of talent, and creates credible room for followers to earn merit.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] frames the question through [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] before Hebei has fully turned. [[DengYuEasternHan|邓禹]] answers Liu Xiu's worry over having only one commandery by saying that troubled people seek a wise ruler and that rise depends on 德才 rather than territory. [[KouXunEasternHan|寇恂]] and Min Ye then apply that standard politically: Handan's [[WangLangHebeiClaimant|王郎]] claim is uncertain, while Liu Xiu's reputation for respectful treatment of worthies makes him the better long-term bet.

The pattern is not passive admiration. Kou Xun contacts [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]], [[WuHanEasternHan|吴汉]] uses information from a scholar and a forged proclamation to push Yuyang toward Liu Xiu, and [[GengYanEasternHan|耿弇]] helps connect Shanggu force to Liu Xiu. Liu Xiu then strengthens the assessment by rewarding the arriving commanders quickly, letting them keep original troops, and making alignment look like a chance to build shared merit.

The episode also makes receptivity part of the test. When the Julu siege stalls, [[GengChunEasternHan|耿纯]] argues that Liu Xiu should strike Handan while morale is high because Julu will submit after Wang Lang falls. Liu Xiu adopts the advice, so leader-worthiness includes the capacity to update strategy when a subordinate sees the better move.

## Key Claims

- Worth-following leaders can be judged under weakness by reputation, virtue, capacity, and advice receptivity rather than only by present territory.
- Followers in a collapsing political field assess comparative futures: Kou Xun and Min Ye prefer Liu Xiu because Wang Lang's Handan claim looks uncertain.
- Allegiance often requires active brokers, not only private conviction; Kou Xun and Wu Han turn a judgment into a coalition.
- Reward design matters because Liu Xiu's quick appointments and troop-retention decisions make new followers believe they can build merit inside his order.
- Receptivity to strategic correction is part of leader-worthiness: Liu Xiu adopts Geng Chun's Handan-first advice after Julu stalls.

## Evidence

- Territory versus 德才: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] says Deng Yu tells Liu Xiu that disorder makes people seek an able ruler and that rise depends on virtue and capacity rather than territory.
- Comparative allegiance judgment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] says Kou Xun and Min Ye advise Geng Kuang to support Liu Xiu because Handan's future is hard to trust and Liu Xiu treats worthies respectfully.
- Active brokerage: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] says Kou Xun arranges Shanggu-Yuyang troop support and Wu Han uses a forged proclamation to move Peng Chong from hesitation to action.
- Merit opportunity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] says Liu Xiu appoints arriving leaders as generals, lets them command original forces, and frames the moment as shared merit-making.
- Advice receptivity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-671-nide-lingdao-shifou-zhide-zhuisui-jinlai-duibi-xia-lrjwus-s14y1x2y4r3f-dvri1nv|Hanji 671]] says Liu Xiu adopts Geng Chun's recommendation to leave a force at Julu and strike Handan directly.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept is based on one podcast source note and should remain a compact Eastern Han pattern until more evidence accumulates. The episode's workplace-leadership framing is interpretive, not a standalone historical proof. Wu Han's forged proclamation also makes the attraction mechanism morally mixed: leader-worthiness can be advanced by questionable political technique. Finally, the source does not deny the importance of force, reward, and timing; it argues that those factors become persuasive when joined to perceived virtue, competence, and opportunity.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 671 to capture the episode's explicit "what kind of leader is worth following" frame.
- Distinguished pre-victory allegiance assessment from later post-victory amnesty and command-settlement patterns.
