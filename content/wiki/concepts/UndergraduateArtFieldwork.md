# Undergraduate Art Fieldwork / 本科艺术田野

## Definition
Undergraduate art fieldwork / 本科艺术田野 is EP280's model of making first-year art students leave the campus studio and learn from ordinary social sites before translating observation into art practice. It treats markets, neighborhoods, work routines, family conversations, body models, and community problems as evidence surfaces for artistic perception.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is not generic volunteer service or a requirement to make art socially useful. Its core is sequencing: students first meet people, notice things, record details, feel fear or rejection, and test assumptions before turning material into a handmade book, video, project, or exhibition. In the Guangzhou case, this sequencing helps students move away from ready-made themes and toward concrete perception, while still leaving professional training and institutional evaluation in place.

## Key Claims
- Art students need field contact because sensitivity and technique do not automatically produce questions grounded in reality.
- Fieldwork should begin with observation, conversation, and notes before students choose themes, messages, or polished outputs.
- Ordinary people and overlooked labor can become central artistic subjects when students stop treating them as background or props.
- Stranger interaction is itself a pedagogical challenge because many students have little practice handling refusal, awkwardness, and open-ended conversation.
- The field can return students to their own families, bodies, and memories, making external observation part of self-understanding.
- Art-fieldwork education has a hard boundary: it can open perception, but cannot replace technical training, grading systems, or employment preparation.

## Evidence
- Field contact need: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] says the reform responds to students who can handle concepts and assignments but struggle to open emotion and observe society.
- Observation before theme: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] contrasts PPT-style theme raising with field notes and the question of what students actually saw.
- Overlooked subjects: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] highlights cleaners, brush sellers, a grass-roots artist, body model Wang Xishou, dorm supervisors, factory work, square dancers, and handwritten community notices.
- Social fear: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] describes teacher-designed simulations, scripts, and language help because students feared approaching strangers.
- Self-return: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] shows students returning to parents, factory memories, and mothers' childbirth/body pain after external field encounters.
- Boundary: [[ep280-guangzhou-meiyuan-fangwenji-dang-meishusheng-kaishi-zuo-tianye-diaocha-lr4-jmpdo-oo7w8sdmcgcw-chqc9]] keeps the reform limited by later specialization, grading distribution, repeated practice, self-media work, and employment options.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supports fieldwork as a formative first-year intervention, not as a complete replacement for studio practice, design skill, calligraphy repetition, portfolio building, or career preparation. Its evidence is based on cases and reported reflections rather than controlled outcome data.
