# Remote Reporting Presence / 远程采访现场感

Remote reporting presence / 远程采访现场感 is [[ChengJing|程静]]'s method problem in [[xianchang-xianchang-2025-nianzhong-teji-sanlian-nvjizhe-jiemi-xinwen-beihou-de-ku-yu-guang-loz-sggelhx166n2me8v6d8k9a3c]]. Because international travel can be costly or impossible, the episode asks how a reporter can produce scene texture and original information through online search, phone calls, local media, translation, and long-distance source networks.

The Moscow terror-attack example shows the discipline involved. [[QinSi|秦思]] and Cheng Jing had to reconstruct existing reports and timelines before calling witnesses through Russian-speaking helpers, because translation consumes time and leaves fewer chances for broad discovery. The Gaza letters example adds a longer version through [[AishaGaza|Aisha]], where intermittent connectivity, fear for the source's safety, and months of conversation replace a single field visit.

## Key Claims
- Remote reporting is not desk summarization when it builds direct source contact, timelines, translation plans, and missing-fact questions.
- Translation makes preparation more important because interview time and emotional bandwidth are scarce.
- Long-distance reporting can create presence, but it also requires humility about what the reporter cannot see or fully feel.
- The method extends [[InterviewAsEmbodiedReporting|embodied reporting]] by showing how presence can be partially reconstructed under constraint.
