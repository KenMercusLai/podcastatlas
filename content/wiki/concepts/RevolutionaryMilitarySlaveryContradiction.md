# Revolutionary Military Slavery Contradiction

## Definition

Revolutionary military slavery contradiction is the tension between a liberty movement relying on Black military service and a revolutionary leadership and society that still preserved slavery.

## Current Synthesis

The sources make the contradiction visible from the beginning of the war through its aftermath. [[LordDunmoresProclamation|Lord Dunmore's proclamation]] offered freedom to enslaved people who left rebel enslavers for British service and alarmed white slaveholders before independence was declared. Black soldiers later served in an integrated Continental force, sometimes through emancipation-linked recruitment, while Black Loyalists pursued British freedom offers. At the same time Washington owned enslaved people, his household brought enslaved attendants to Valley Forge, and after Yorktown he ordered escapees returned to enslavers. Military emancipation and inclusion widened the liberty argument without resolving ownership, coercion, opportunism, or postwar vulnerability.

## Key Claims

- Black military service can expose the gap between revolutionary liberty language and actual freedom.
- Emancipation-linked recruitment can be pragmatic manpower policy and a moral pressure point at the same time.
- Integrated service does not erase slavery when commanders and households still own enslaved people.
- A leader's changing attitude toward slavery should be distinguished from completed emancipation.
- The military contradiction belongs beside, but is not identical to, Franklin's late abolitionist turn and Jefferson's unresolved plantation dependence.
- British freedom offers created real Black Loyalist agency without guaranteeing security from capture or re-enslavement.
- Emancipation policy can simultaneously enable freedom-seeking and serve an imperial military strategy.

## Evidence

- Rhode Island policy: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says Rhode Island bought the freedom of 117 enslaved men and sent them as soldiers to Valley Forge after struggling to meet recruitment quotas.
- Broader service: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says Massachusetts and Connecticut followed similar policies and that by August 1778 about 755 Black soldiers were in the army.
- Integrated setting: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says there were remarkably few recorded racial discipline problems in this integrated military setting.
- Washington household contradiction: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says Washington owned enslaved people, did not free them during his lifetime, and Martha Washington arrived with enslaved people from Mount Vernon.
- Attitude qualification: [[683-washington-hero-of-the-revolution-part-1-glt3752138067]] says the war appears to have affected Washington's attitude toward slavery.
- Loyalist service: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] discusses the Ethiopian Regiment and Harry Washington as evidence that British allegiance could offer an emancipation route.
- Early freedom offer: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] presents Dunmore's November 1775 proclamation as a service-linked offer that deeply alarmed slaveholding Virginia.
- Contemporary critique: [[348-the-boston-tea-party-part-2-glt5189620267]] records Samuel Johnson and Edmund Burke identifying the tension between colonial liberty claims and slaveholding.
- Yorktown aftermath: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] says Washington ordered escaped enslaved people rounded up and returned six days after the surrender.
- Abolitionist effect: [[350-the-triumph-of-george-washington-part-4-glt1619660676]] argues that the Revolution reinforced slavery in some respects while stimulating abolitionism in northern states and Britain.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not turn Black service into racial equality or British enlistment into guaranteed freedom. Dunmore's policy was directed at people enslaved by rebels and does not make him a universal abolitionist. The sources do not quantify all legal statuses, motives, captures, evacuations, or postwar outcomes, and they do not establish slavery defense as the Revolution's sole cause. The contradiction is distributive and temporal, not a claim that all Black participants experienced the same war.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept from Black Continental service to Black Loyalist emancipation routes and post-Yorktown re-enslavement.
- Added the distinction between abolitionist momentum and actual security or freedom.
- Added Dunmore's pre-independence freedom offer and contemporary British criticism of slaveholding liberty rhetoric.
