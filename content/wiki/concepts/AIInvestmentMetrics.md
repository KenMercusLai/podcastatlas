# AI Investment Metrics

## Definition
AI investment metrics are observable measures that test whether spending on models, compute, applications, and organizational change becomes useful demand, recurring revenue, customer outcomes, and financeable cash flow.

## Current Synthesis
The evidence replaces a single “AI bubble” verdict with a layered measurement chain. Capex creates models and capacity; better capability can increase useful token demand; usage can become subscriptions, contracts, or application revenue; and customer adoption should eventually appear as accepted work, profit, cost reduction, or strategic control. Raw DAU, token totals, ARR, or capex each capture only one stage and can be gamed or misread without task quality, unit cost, customer concentration, and downstream outcomes.

The newest source makes the framework operational for a later-cycle market: watch GPU rental prices for infrastructure demand, listed model/application-company revenue growth for commercialization, and infrastructure credit spreads plus free cash flow for financing resilience. These indicators do not produce an automatic trading rule, but their joint direction helps distinguish sentiment-led repricing from a deterioration in end demand and cash conversion.

## Key Claims
- Token usage is useful only when normalized by task completion, quality, hidden reasoning, repair work, and cost.
- Capex should be traced through utilization and customer demand rather than treated as proof of either confidence or waste.
- Contract liabilities, deferred revenue, subscriptions, and comparable ARR can show willingness to pay, but not terminal customer value.
- The decisive chain is input to output to result: compute and tokens must become accepted work, revenue growth, cost reduction, profit, or strategic control.
- Model-company revenue, application adoption, customer ROI, and infrastructure cash flow are distinct evidence levels.
- Credit spreads, interest cost, and free cash flow add financing discipline to operating metrics.
- A joint dashboard of GPU rental prices, downstream revenue growth, and infrastructure credit conditions is more informative than a single bubble indicator.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
No metric is self-interpreting. Cheaper compute can signal efficiency rather than weak demand; token growth can reflect waste; ARR can use inconsistent run-rate definitions; negative free cash flow can finance valuable capacity; and widening spreads can reflect macro conditions rather than AI fundamentals. Long-cycle significance does not establish a good entry price, and strong provider revenue does not prove that customers have earned a return.
