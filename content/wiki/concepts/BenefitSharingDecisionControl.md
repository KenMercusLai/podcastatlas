## Definition

分利共治与谋略独断 / benefit sharing with decision control is the political method of distributing offices, honors, or material advantage broadly enough to reduce opposition, while keeping sensitive strategy and final decision authority concentrated.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-2-jiemi-huoguang-chengwei-xihan-diyi-quanchen-de-xinji-fmmyov0ecqykq37zkphi40o52bc6|Hanji 394-2]] grounds the concept in [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]]'s early [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|Han Zhao]] regency. After Huo is warned that the Lu family failed partly because it excluded Liu royal kin from power, he appoints [[LiuBijiangWesternHan|刘碧江]] and [[LiuChangleWesternHan|刘长乐]] from the imperial clan. The host reads the move as a signal that cooperative actors can receive benefits even if they are not Huo's own faction.

The concept is not the same as general fairness. It is strategic distribution: the actor gives up enough visible benefit to prevent a large hostile coalition from forming, while preserving a smaller inner circle for planning. The source's use of [[ZengGuofan|曾国藩]]'s aphorism makes the tension explicit: interests can be shared, but strategy should be held by few. Huo's later career also qualifies the method because early stabilization does not remove the danger of becoming overmighty.

## Key Claims

- Benefit distribution can be a defensive act: offices and honors can turn potential opponents into neutral or cooperative actors.
- Sharing benefits differs from sharing final strategy; secrecy and concentrated decision authority remain part of the method.
- Including structurally important groups can answer legitimacy risk, especially when a regent might otherwise appear to exclude the ruling house.
- The method reduces the enemy field but does not eliminate jealousy, resentment, or later conflict when one actor still dominates actual power.
- The line between coalition maintenance and monopolized authority is unstable when benefit sharing depends on one central broker.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-2-jiemi-huoguang-chengwei-xihan-diyi-quanchen-de-xinji-fmmyov0ecqykq37zkphi40o52bc6|Hanji 394-2]] says Huo appoints Liu Bijiang and Liu Changle as Guanglu Dafu and gives Liu Bijiang an additional Changle Palace Guard Commandant post.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-2-jiemi-huoguang-chengwei-xihan-diyi-quanchen-de-xinji-fmmyov0ecqykq37zkphi40o52bc6|Hanji 394-2]] says the host interprets this as signaling that those willing to cooperate with Huo's regency can gain office and benefit.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-2-jiemi-huoguang-chengwei-xihan-diyi-quanchen-de-xinji-fmmyov0ecqykq37zkphi40o52bc6|Hanji 394-2]] uses the aphorism "利益可共而不可独，谋可寡而不可众" to distinguish shared benefits from concentrated planning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept is based on one short source. It should not be treated as proof that Huo Guang's entire regency was consensual or that benefit sharing prevents later crisis. The same episode closes by warning that Huo's position is "not emperor, yet like emperor," and later Huo-related pages already show slander, assassination plotting, deposition authority, delayed power return, family overreach, and purge. The Lu-family comparison and Zeng Guofan aphorism are the host's interpretive frame, not an independent primary-source claim about all regencies.
