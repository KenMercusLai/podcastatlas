# Constitutionalist Military Norm

## Definition
A constitutionalist military norm is the expectation that armed forces obey lawful civilian authority and do not decide political succession through force.

## Current Synthesis
The Chilean case in [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] shows both the value and fragility of such a norm. [[CarlosPrats]] could suppress the Tanquetazo and obstruct coup planning while he retained command, but professional tradition did not survive his political isolation, personal error, resignation, and replacement by [[AugustoPinochet]]. Institutional reputation therefore offered reassurance without guaranteeing behavior under polarized command pressure.

## Key Claims
- Constitutional restraint can depend heavily on command appointments and succession.
- A failed coup can reveal loyal capacity while teaching plotters how to organize a stronger attempt.
- Professional tradition is vulnerable when political polarization enters officer networks and families.
- Personal confidence in an officer is not a substitute for resilient institutional controls.
- Removing one constitutionalist commander can change feasible action even when formal rules remain unchanged.

## Evidence
- Active restraint: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] describes Prats suppressing the June revolt and being viewed as a coup obstacle.
- Norm fragility: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] follows reputational pressure, Prats's resignation, Pinochet's appointment, and the successful coup less than three weeks later.
- Misplaced reliance: [[371-the-1973-chilean-coup-general-pinochet-seizes-power-part-2-glt3740766328]] records Allende's circle trusting both Chile's non-intervention tradition and Pinochet's perceived loyalty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One episode cannot isolate how much causal weight belongs to command succession versus economic crisis, institutional conflict, foreign pressure, or deeper officer alignment. The concept does not imply that civilian legality was uncontested or that every officer shared the same motives.

## What Changed
- Created a command-succession model of how military non-intervention can fail.
- Added failed coups as learning events that can weaken rather than confirm democratic security.
