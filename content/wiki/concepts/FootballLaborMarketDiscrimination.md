# Football Labor-Market Discrimination

Football labor-market discrimination is the source's economic method for identifying prejudice in player and coach markets. [[22-zuqiu-jingjixue-duzhe-bubi-shi-qiumi-570874427]] explains that if teams with more underpaid Black players outperform equally paid comparison teams, the market is revealing that those players were undervalued.

The concept matters because it translates a moral claim into an observable market test. The episode also warns that discrimination can mutate: once Black players are more widely used, prejudice may reappear as praise for "natural" strength or speed while ignoring technique, tactical intelligence, effort, and leadership. The coaching pipeline adds another layer because managerial opportunities depend on trust, appearance, gender, race, and old networks, not only demonstrated competence.

## Key Claims
- Discrimination is economically wasteful when biased employers pass over productive workers.
- Salary, performance, and team composition can expose market mispricing even when prejudice is publicly denied.
- Bias can persist after hiring increases if the explanation for success remains stereotyped.
- Manager and coach selection may show discrimination more strongly than player selection because the criteria are less transparent.
