# Tactical Credibility Cost / 战术信用成本

## Definition
Tactical credibility cost is the price a political or military actor pays when a short-term trick spends personal, diplomatic, or institutional trust that would be useful later. The concept distinguishes reusable deception from moves that make future promises, distress signals, or alliance behavior harder to believe.

## Current Synthesis
The baseline contrast separates reusable battlefield deception from trust-spending entrapment. [[SunBin|孙膑]]'s [[DecreasingStovesDeception|减灶诱敌]] deceives the enemy, but it relies on a repeatable military structure: shape perception, force movement, and prepare the strike. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]]'s [[PeaceParleyEntrapment|和谈诱捕]] against [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]] brings larger immediate gain, but it uses old acquaintance, peace language, oath, and drinking as bait. That social form cannot be reused once known, and Wei later refuses Shang Yang when his own distress may be real.

The diplomatic and ruler-level branches show the same cost at larger scale. [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s Shangyu promise helps Qin break Chu's alliance with Qi, but later denial makes future promises easier to read as traps. [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]]'s Wuguan invitation captures [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]], but it damages meeting protocol itself by making invitation language look coercive.

The inverse branch shows a weaker actor managing credibility cost by exposing the stronger party's non-performance. Qin's promise of fifteen cities for [[HeShiBi|和氏璧]] creates a credibility test; when Qin enjoys the jade without naming the cities, [[LinXiangru|蔺相如]] turns that non-performance into the central fact and sends the jade back to [[ZhaoState|赵国]]. The credibility cost is imposed on the party that fails to perform, not on the envoy who manages the crisis.

The late-Han branch is smaller but structurally similar. [[YuanTan|袁谭]] secretly gives general seals to [[LyuKuangLateHan|吕旷]] and [[GaoXiangLateHan|高翔]] after they defect from [[YuanShang|袁尚]] to [[CaoCao|曹操]]. The immediate move aims to capture allegiance, but Cao discovers it and treats Yuan Tan as deceptive, using a marriage tie through [[CaoZhengLateHan|曹整]] to stabilize him without trusting him.

## Key Claims
- A trick can produce a major immediate advantage while still being strategically expensive.
- Reusable deception depends on repeatable structures, not on fooling the same people with the same social signal.
- A tactic that uses personal trust, diplomatic promise, or alliance conduct as bait can destroy future credibility.
- Battlefield deception and interpersonal trust-destruction are different cost categories.
- Later refuge, negotiation, or alliance cooperation can fail because earlier tactical deception made the actor unreadable.
- A weaker party can sometimes manage credibility cost by making the stronger party's non-performance visible.
- Small coalition maneuvers, such as Yuan Tan's seal-lure, can carry the same credibility cost as grand diplomatic traps.

## Evidence
- Reusable battlefield deception: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf|Zhouji 23]] presents Sun Bin's decreasing-stoves tactic as deception whose structure can still be learned and reused.
- Personal trust as bait: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf|Zhouji 23]] treats Shang Yang's peace-parley capture as a greater immediate win with a larger future credibility cost.
- Diplomatic promise cost: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-50-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-ba-chuhuaiwang-huyou-que-de-loxg3ylhmskvdm-axtco5y-acege|Zhouji 50]] shows Zhang Yi's Shangyu promise working against Chu while openly damaging promise credibility.
- Meeting-protocol damage: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-62-ji-er-kuang-ni-jiujiu-de-tui-a-lumch-ibj8m94abjh5nvr3qhiqff|Zhouji 62]] has Qin Zhaoxiangwang turn a meeting invitation into Chu Huaiwang's captivity and coercive territorial demand.
- Exposed non-performance: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-74-huanyuan-wanbi-guizhao-zhenxiang-lg1irvbbc1wp8oph2kmpatfoxebk|Zhouji 74]] shows Lin Xiangru shifting credibility cost to Qin by making Qin's failure to name the promised cities visible.
- Late-Han alliance maneuver: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1061-yuanshao-zhizi-he-shafu-chouren-jiecheng-qinjia-lnsnj6ipiyscw-vopnjjgoxpnt5r|Hanji 1061]] shows Yuan Tan's secret seals for Lyu Kuang and Gao Xiang producing Cao Cao's distrust even though Cao still uses Yuan Tan tactically.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not condemn all deception. Sun Bin's case remains evidence that deception can be strategically reusable when it manipulates battlefield perception without spending personal trust. The Yuan Tan branch is filtered through Hanji 1061's interpretive frame and does not prove Cao Cao's later decisions by itself; it shows that the seal-lure becomes credible evidence for distrust inside this episode.
