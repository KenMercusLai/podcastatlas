# Commercial Satellite Constellations

Commercial satellite constellations are networks of many smaller satellites used for services such as internet access, GPS, and Earth imaging. In [[tech-20260818-tech-pod-128-tech-20260818-tech-pod-128]], [[MichelleHanlon]] explains that the industry has moved away from relying on one very expensive satellite for decades and toward distributed networks where the failure of one or a few satellites is less damaging.

The concept links [[Starlink]] and [[SatelliteConnectivityCompetition]] to [[SpaceLaunchCapacityBottleneck]]. Many cheaper satellites can make services more resilient, but they also require repeated launch access, more launch scheduling, and more [[SpaceEconomyInfrastructure]] around pads, ranges, ports, licensing, and environmental review.

## Key Claims
- Distributed satellite networks reduce single-satellite failure risk by spreading service across many spacecraft.
- Smaller and cheaper satellites can increase replacement and refresh cadence, making launch access a recurring operating need.
- [[Starlink]] is the source's main example of constellation-driven launch demand.
- Constellation growth can expand internet, GPS, and imaging services while increasing pressure on launch pads, spaceports, and orbital governance.
- Constellation demand connects downstream connectivity markets to upstream rocket and launch-site capacity.
