# Professional Community Trust / 专业共同体信任

Professional community trust / 专业共同体信任 is [[SongFangjin|宋方金]]'s argument in [[271-tangchao-douyao-bucunzai-le-weishenme-weishilun-hui-zai-jinnian-da-baofa-lg7-wdrdw1b9h7lwa4vwkvmyb4un|Luanfanshu 271]] that modern knowledge depends on communities of verification. A non-specialist may not be able to independently verify a physicist's, mathematician's, historian's, or engineer's work, but can still ask whether the claim is answerable to a field with methods, criticism, records, and rival experts.

The concept is not blind faith in credentials. It sits between [[ScientificSkepticism]] and [[ExpertTrustRepair]]: trust must be earned by practices that make claims checkable, corrigible, and open to professional challenge. Its opposite is not healthy doubt, but the move in [[PseudoHistoryConspiracy|伪史论]] where all communities that could test a claim are dismissed as captured or fake.

The episode also ties the concept to media environment. In an "editor world," trust is partly routed through newspapers, books, and program makers; in a "search world," through active lookup; and in an "algorithm world," through feeds that may optimize relevance, conflict, or entertainment before reliability. Professional community trust therefore needs [[AlgorithmicMediaLiteracy]] as much as institutional respect.

## Key Claims
- Modern people cannot personally verify every technical or historical claim from first principles.
- Trust in a professional community is stronger when the community has methods, disagreement, correction, and public evidence.
- Rejecting every expert community can feel independent while actually leaving the person dependent on a narrower feed or charismatic explainer.
- Trust is degraded when professional communities communicate defensively, hide uncertainty, or fail to show how evidence is checked.
- Algorithmic feeds make trust harder because visibility can masquerade as credibility.
