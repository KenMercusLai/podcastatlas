# Trust Decision Quality

## Definition

Trust decision quality is the discipline of matching confidence and verification to relevant evidence, stakes, reversibility, and the consequences of being wrong.

## Current Synthesis

Trust is necessary for ordinary cooperation, so the framework does not recommend generalized suspicion. It instead targets predictable distortions in consequential decisions: moving too fast, preferring people who feel familiar, noticing desired signals while discounting red flags, allowing convenience to preserve a weak choice, and treating intuition as a verdict.

The operational tool is a trust pause. Before hiring, delegating, sharing confidential information, accepting a role, or entering a contract, the decision-maker asks what produced confidence, what they want to be true, which cues are merely familiar, what information is absent, and what lightweight verification would materially reduce uncertainty. The pause should be proportional; applying it indiscriminately to routine life would replace trust with paralysis.

## Key Claims

- Familiarity can feel like evidence of character or capability even when it is only resemblance, style, background, or presentation.
- Desired signals and convenience can keep a person committed to a choice after contradictory information appears.
- Intuition is useful as a prompt or input, but consequential trust should not rest on gut feeling alone.
- Verification should be proportional to stakes, reversibility, and potential harm rather than applied as constant suspicion.
- A deliberate pause can improve trust without requiring certainty or making distrust the default.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not establish that slower decisions are always better. Delay can itself impose costs, expertise can make some intuitive judgments informative, and excessive verification can damage relationships or make action impossible. The supplied summary also does not provide methods or effect sizes for the cited experiments and executive survey. The framework is therefore a decision aid, not a validated scoring model or substitute for domain-specific due diligence.

## What Changed

- Established a proportional trust-decision framework centered on signal awareness, convenience, tested intuition, and a deliberate pause.
