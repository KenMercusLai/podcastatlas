# Private Fief Expansion Through State War / 以国战扩私邑

Private fief expansion through state war / 以国战扩私邑 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-88-ni-zenme-kandai-fenghuangnan-1-lpuxkdq-rvnifuoneq212g53w5z5]] extracts from [[WeiRan|魏冉]]'s recommendation of [[KeqingZao|客卿灶 / 客卿造]]. The episode argues that the proposed attack on [[QiState|齐国]] is hard to justify as a direct [[QinState|秦国]] national-interest move because the target territory is distant from Qin and separated by Han and Wei space.

It becomes intelligible when read through Wei Ran's private base at [[Dingtao|陶 / 定陶]]. By seizing Gang and Shou near that area, Wei Ran can use Qin military power to enlarge or secure his own fief, while the state bears campaign cost and route risk.

The concept matters because it marks a different danger from simple corruption. A powerful minister can align private geography with public strategy closely enough that a war looks like state expansion while also serving a personal power base. In the episode, that is why Wei Ran's strength becomes a background problem for [[FanJu|范雎]]'s later anti-Wei-Ran political role.

## Key Claims
- State war can be redirected toward an officeholder's fief when campaign targets match private territorial interests.
- Distance, route burden, and weak state-level payoff are warning signs that a campaign may be serving a private power base.
- The pattern threatens ruler authority because a minister can accumulate territorial resources under the cover of public victory.
- Zhouji 88 uses this pattern to connect Wei Ran's power to Fan Ju's coming role in Qin court politics.
