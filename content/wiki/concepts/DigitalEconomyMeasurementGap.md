# Digital Economy Measurement Gap / 数字经济统计盲区

Digital economy measurement gap is [[133-quanqiu-hongguan-he-ziben-shichang-2025-nianzhong-pandian-zhongguo-de-sange-wencha-he-meiguo-de-sange-yuqicha-lhj-qcswiuqskvzn-f693vti6xsc]]'s claim that platform interaction, content payment, bullet-screen participation, virtual services, and other digital consumption can be hard for traditional GDP frameworks to capture. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] and [[Ricky]] contrast these activities with state-supported physical-goods consumption such as appliance subsidies.

The concept does not say official statistics are useless. It says production-era measurement and local-government assessment can under-recognize digital and service activity, which affects how policymakers interpret demand and where they choose to stimulate.

## Key Claims
- Digital consumption can create value through interaction, attention, entertainment, payment, and platform participation rather than only through physical output.
- A manufacturing-heavy statistics lens can make service and virtual consumption look smaller or less actionable than durable-goods purchases.
- Policy tools may over-favor subsidized physical goods if measured GDP and local assessment systems are built around tangible production and sales.
- The gap is especially important for [[ChinaMacroTemperatureGaps]] because weak traditional indicators can coexist with active but differently measured consumer activity.
- Measurement difficulty should not be confused with unlimited value; the source still leaves open whether digital and service consumption are large enough to move aggregate growth.
