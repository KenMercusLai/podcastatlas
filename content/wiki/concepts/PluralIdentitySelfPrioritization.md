# Plural Identity Self-Prioritization

## Definition

Plural identity self-prioritization is the claim that a person carries multiple identities and should retain meaningful agency to rank, foreground, background, and revise their importance over time.

## Current Synthesis

[[MalcolmGladwell]] connects this view to being born in England to an English father and Jamaican mother and then raised in Canada. Movement among family, national, racial, religious, and cultural settings made singular identity reduction feel descriptively inadequate.

The concept adds a procedural claim to ordinary recognition of multiplicity: outside institutions should not decide in advance which characteristic must explain the person. Gladwell also argues for a broad account of diversity that includes political viewpoint and other dimensions beyond a narrow fixed set. That expansion protects complexity, but it does not make all differences equivalent or remove the effects of discrimination, social categorization, and unequal power on which identities a person can safely background.

## Key Claims

- One person can simultaneously belong to several inherited, chosen, social, cultural, and political categories.
- The importance of an identity can change across settings and life stages without making it false.
- Institutions risk distortion when they reduce a person to the category outsiders find most salient.
- Diversity can include forms of difference beyond a fixed demographic checklist.
- Self-prioritization is constrained when law, prejudice, visibility, or material dependence makes an identity socially unavoidable.

## Evidence

- Biographical basis: [[malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor-malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor]] connects Gladwell's English birth, Jamaican maternal background, and Canadian upbringing to awareness of plural identity.
- Ranking agency: [[malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor-malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor]] reports his claim that individuals should decide which identities matter most and revise that ranking over time.
- Diversity scope: [[malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor-malcolm-on-no-small-endeavor]] records his objection to treating only a narrow set of characteristics as meaningful diversity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source offers a personal and normative position rather than a general model of identity formation. Freedom to rank identities is never complete: other people, institutions, bodily visibility, citizenship, economic dependence, and discrimination may impose a category regardless of personal preference. Including political viewpoint in diversity also does not mean every belief merits institutional protection independent of conduct, evidence, or harm.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded account of identity multiplicity that keeps personal agency and social constraint visible together.
