# Documentary Ethical Restraint / 纪录片伦理克制

## Definition
Documentary ethical restraint / 纪录片伦理克制 is the practice of letting facts, observed behavior, source boundaries, and possible harm constrain a documentary's interpretation instead of forcing subjects into a stronger narrative or moral position.

## Current Synthesis
The aging-documentary source contrasts 《前浪2》 with reality-show and short-video logic. Reality-show viewing is described as encouraging fast stance-taking and character judgment; short-video discourse as opinion-heavy and fact-light. Fan Shiguang instead frames documentary work as a slower discipline: a team may film for weeks, discover striking material, and still abandon a story if it cannot responsibly understand the person's inner world or if editing would overclaim.

The same boundary becomes more difficult in archives of mass violence. [[RithyPanh|Rithy Panh]] makes clay figures, testimony, propaganda, reenactment, missing images, and his own construction visible rather than presenting footage as self-explanatory. Inviting powerful perpetrators to stage themselves creates another risk: performance can expose impunity while also amplifying spectacle, complicating consent, and producing consequences a director cannot fully control.

Restraint is therefore not neutrality or non-intervention. It is an ethical boundary on selection, staging, interpretation, and release: filmmakers should keep construction and uncertainty visible, relate images to other evidence, avoid using vulnerable people as raw material for a clean thesis, and refuse to let complexity erase responsibility.

Closed-institution reporting makes participant safety part of documentary restraint. In [[TheAlabamaSolution|The Alabama Solution]], incarcerated people create much of the primary record, so restraint includes crediting their prior organizing, consulting them about representation, distinguishing conviction history from the abuse claim, and maintaining legal support when publication creates retaliation risk. Withholding some context can be ethical when it would invite irrelevant moral sorting, but selective disclosure must still be explained and cannot become a license to manipulate sympathy.

## Key Claims
- Documentary truth requires responsible interpretation of what footage can and cannot show; fact-centered work does not make editing viewpoint-free.
- Abandoning a strong story can be necessary when the subject's inner world is not knowable enough or the edit would impose an easy moral category.
- Subject feedback matters, but creators should not assume either fragility or approval without evidence.
- Reenactment can investigate memory when its construction is visible and tested against testimony, documents, places, and propaganda.
- Perpetrator access can expose continuing impunity while also risking renewed spectacle, manipulation, or harm beyond the filmmaker's control.
- Participant-generated evidence can redistribute documentary authority while increasing duties around consent, credit, security, and continuing protection.
- Context should be disclosed according to relevance, not merely because prejudicial biography is available.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources present filmmakers' accounts and podcast interpretation rather than independent audits of raw footage, editorial choices, consent procedures, compensation, security, platform constraints, or participant response. Visible construction and collaboration language improve epistemic honesty but do not by themselves establish factual accuracy, equal editorial power, or ethical adequacy. Historical-documentary and prison-abuse claims require corroboration beyond cinematic force, while protection efforts cannot eliminate retaliation risk.

## What Changed

- Added participant-generated evidence and shared authorship as sources of both authority and continuing duty.
- Added relevance-based contextual disclosure as an ethical editorial choice rather than a presumption that more biography is always better.
- Extended harm analysis through post-release retaliation risk and legal wellness support.
