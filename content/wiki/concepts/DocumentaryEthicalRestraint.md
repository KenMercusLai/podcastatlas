# Documentary Ethical Restraint / 纪录片伦理克制

## Definition
Documentary ethical restraint / 纪录片伦理克制 is the practice of letting facts, observed behavior, source boundaries, and possible harm constrain a documentary's interpretation instead of forcing subjects into a stronger narrative or moral position.

## Current Synthesis
The aging-documentary source contrasts 《前浪2》 with reality-show and short-video logic. Reality-show viewing is described as encouraging fast stance-taking and character judgment; short-video discourse as opinion-heavy and fact-light. Fan Shiguang instead frames documentary work as a slower discipline: a team may film for weeks, discover striking material, and still abandon a story if it cannot responsibly understand the person's inner world or if editing would overclaim.

The same boundary becomes more difficult in archives of mass violence. [[RithyPanh|Rithy Panh]] makes clay figures, testimony, propaganda, reenactment, missing images, and his own construction visible rather than presenting footage as self-explanatory. Inviting powerful perpetrators to stage themselves creates another risk: performance can expose impunity while also amplifying spectacle, complicating consent, and producing consequences a director cannot fully control.

Restraint is therefore not neutrality or non-intervention. It is an ethical boundary on selection, staging, interpretation, and release: filmmakers should keep construction and uncertainty visible, relate images to other evidence, avoid using vulnerable people as raw material for a clean thesis, and refuse to let complexity erase responsibility.

## Key Claims
- Documentary truth requires more than real footage; it also requires responsible interpretation of what the footage can and cannot show.
- Abandoning a strong story can be ethically necessary when the subject's inner world is not knowable enough.
- Restraint resists both sensational aging stories and easy moral sorting of family members, carers, notaries, and older adults.
- Fact-centered documentary work can counter opinion-first media environments without pretending that editing has no viewpoint.
- Subject feedback matters, but creators should not assume either fragility or approval without evidence.
- Reenactment can investigate memory when its construction is visible and tested against testimony, documents, places, and propaganda.
- Perpetrator access can expose continuing impunity while also risking renewed spectacle, manipulation, or harm beyond the filmmaker's control.

- Fact restraint - [[women-ruhe-laoqu-cong-qianlang2-kuijian-zhongguo-laonian-shenghuo-de-zhenshi-tujing-lkybur9dgim4vxxyjuq1eav4i-up]] records Fan Shiguang's claim that documentary work should not be led away by the creator's own expression.
- Anti-judgment structure - [[women-ruhe-laoqu-cong-qianlang2-kuijian-zhongguo-laonian-shenghuo-de-zhenshi-tujing-lkybur9dgim4vxxyjuq1eav4i-up]] contrasts the documentary's complexity with reality-show viewing habits that push audiences to choose the morally correct side.
- Subject response - [[women-ruhe-laoqu-cong-qianlang2-kuijian-zhongguo-laonian-shenghuo-de-zhenshi-tujing-lkybur9dgim4vxxyjuq1eav4i-up]] notes filmed participants who accepted or thanked the team, including one who said his life was his own to decide.
- Visible construction and missing images - [[20-shiji-zhuanzhi-shi-dianying-xian-qingsuan-yi-bufen-gkwriaiosoyxcck-awttzvx8]] describes [[RithyPanh|Rithy Panh]] placing clay figures, testimony, propaganda, and reenactment in explicit relation rather than disguising intervention.
- Perpetrator performance - [[20-shiji-zhuanzhi-shi-dianying-xian-qingsuan-yi-bufen-gkwriaiosoyxcck-awttzvx8]] treats *The Act of Killing* as both an exposure of impunity and an ethical problem around performance, consent, power, and uncontrolled consequences.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources present filmmakers' accounts and podcast interpretation rather than independent audits of raw footage, editorial choices, consent procedures, compensation, security, platform constraints, or participant response. Visible construction improves epistemic honesty but does not by itself establish factual accuracy or ethical adequacy. Historical-documentary claims also require corroboration beyond cinematic force.

## What Changed
- Expanded restraint from observational non-overclaiming to archival construction, reenactment, perpetrator performance, and political risk.
- Clarified that complexity and visible intervention do not require moral neutrality about state violence.
