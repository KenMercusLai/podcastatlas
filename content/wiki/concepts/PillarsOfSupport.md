# Pillars of Support

## Definition

Pillars of support are the workers, civil servants, businesses, political elites, media organizations, police, military units, and other constituencies whose continuing cooperation allows a ruler or political system to function.

## Current Synthesis

The concept rejects the idea that even a highly coercive ruler acts alone. Orders require implementation, revenue requires collection, narratives require transmission, and repression requires organizations willing to carry it out. [[NonviolentResistanceStrategy]] therefore aims less at changing a ruler's mind than at moving resistance through these relationships until important supporters defect, refuse, delay, or stop cooperating.

Serbia illustrates the end state when police ceased defending [[SlobodanMilosevic|Slobodan Milošević]]. Sudan illustrates a distributed pressure route in which strikes and stay-at-home action disrupted ordinary coordination while reducing direct exposure to security forces.

## Key Claims

- Political power depends on repeated cooperation from multiple institutions and social groups.
- Coercive capacity remains relational because police and security forces must continue obeying and acting.
- Broad movements have more personal and organizational ties through which to encourage defection.
- Withdrawal can occur through refusal, delay, strike, noncompliance, information loss, or neutrality.
- Fracture among key pillars can produce rapid collapse even when a ruler previously appeared secure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Institutional dependence does not imply that withdrawal is safe, easy, or sufficient. Regimes can replace personnel, divide constituencies, import resources, use surveillance, or impose severe collective punishment. The episode supplies illustrative cases rather than a general measure of which pillars matter most in every system.
