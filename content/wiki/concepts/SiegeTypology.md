# Siege Typology

Siege typology enters the wiki through [[elbows-up-again-a-us-canada-trade-flare-up-6a8c0fa3a89d26398ee7a5db]], where [[JeremyBlack]] distinguishes between totemic and utilitarian sieges. The segment is prompted by [[ChristopherNolan]]'s [[TheOdyssey]], but it moves from literary adaptation into military history and collective memory.

In the source, totemic sieges matter because of symbolic or civilisational meaning. [[Vienna]] in 1683 and the Alamo in 1836 are presented as examples where later memory and rallying power outweigh, or at least outlast, the immediate battlefield result. Utilitarian sieges matter because they directly shape campaigns, resources, and strategic outcomes; the segment names Tyre, Leningrad, and Malta in this category.

The concept complements [[HydraulicSiegeWarfare]]. Hydraulic warfare describes one material method of siege pressure, while siege typology asks what kind of historical importance a siege later carries: symbolic identity, strategic utility, or both.

## Key Claims
- Sieges can be turning points because they concentrate time, logistics, morale, and political symbolism in one place.
- A totemic siege can matter most through memory even when its military result is defeat.
- A utilitarian siege can matter most through material consequences, such as supply, casualties, territorial access, or campaign outcome.
- The same siege can acquire both symbolic and strategic meaning, so the categories are analytical tools rather than mutually exclusive boxes.
