# Efficiency Over Scale Expansion

Efficiency over scale expansion is the business pattern that links several segments in [[qishui-yinyue-chongji-tengxun-wangyi-tesila-deng-cheqi-zai-guonei-kaizhan-daguimo-zhaohui-1008396395]]. The episode repeatedly shows companies moving from traffic, stores, headcount, or network growth toward product proof, supply-chain control, channel discipline, and operating leverage.

The pattern appears differently by sector. [[OrientalSelection|东方甄选]] tries to turn anchor traffic into self-operated products; [[LiNing|Li-Ning]] cuts stores while testing categories and sponsorship depth; [[Atour|亚朵]] converts room experience into retail; [[Dreame|追觅]] pulls back a car project after fast hiring; and [[Beike|贝壳]] grows profit while store and agent counts stay roughly flat.

## Key Claims
- When a market matures, adding outlets, employees, products, or traffic can become less useful than improving conversion, retention, procurement, and organizational focus.
- Efficiency does not mean shrinking by default; Atour's retail growth and Oriental Selection's self-operated products show that new revenue can grow from better use of existing channels.
- The pattern is clearest when revenue and profit diverge: Beike's source-scoped revenue decline and profit increase make network productivity more important than gross scale.
- Fast expansion into adjacent categories can create option value, but Dreame's car contraction shows that capital, manufacturing, and financing gates can force a return to core businesses.
