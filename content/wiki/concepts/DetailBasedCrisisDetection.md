# Detail-Based Crisis Detection / 细节式危机识别

Detail-based crisis detection / 细节式危机识别 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-975-yanzhou-zhiluan-xunyu-ruhe-bang-caocao-shou-dabenying-ln0hh4jrokkwtqsmxmqhdjzcosdr|Hanji 975]] as the method [[XunYu|荀彧]] uses to catch the [[YanzhouLateHan|兖州]] rebellion before it fully becomes visible. The episode's point is not generic carefulness; it is that small mismatches in title, route, timing, and rear-area behavior can reveal a political break before formal declarations arrive.

The source case is [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]]'s envoy. Calling [[CaoCao|曹操]] "曹公" rather than "兖州牧" weakens Cao Cao's claim over Yanzhou in the sentence itself, while [[LyuBu|吕布]]'s movement from Henei toward Yanzhou does not match the claim that he is helping Cao Cao attack [[TaoQianLateHan|陶谦]] in [[Xuzhou|徐州]]. Xun Yu's response is operational: he recalls [[XiahouDun|夏侯惇]] to [[JuanchengLateHan|鄄城]] before the rear collapses.

This differs from [[BattlefieldInformationControl|战场信息控制]], which concerns shaping what others see. Detail-based crisis detection concerns noticing what the situation is already leaking. It overlaps with [[PowerEtiquetteReading|权力礼仪细读]] when titles and address expose status shifts, and with [[XingshiqingStrategicAssessment|行势情战略评估]] when small signs are checked against movement, incentives, and timing.

## Key Claims
- Small wording changes can carry political information when actors avoid naming a contested office or authority.
- Route direction and timing can expose whether a stated military purpose is plausible.
- Crisis detection becomes valuable only when it is converted into immediate action, such as recalling a stabilizing commander or securing a base.
- The method depends on fitting details together; no single clue is sufficient without the surrounding field of incentives and movement.
