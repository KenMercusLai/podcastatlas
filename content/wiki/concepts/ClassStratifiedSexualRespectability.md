# Class-Stratified Sexual Respectability

## Definition

Class-stratified sexual respectability is the unequal system by which public ideals of chastity, knowledge, marriage, discretion, and sexual availability apply differently according to gender and social position.

## Current Synthesis

Georgian and Victorian Britain cannot be understood through a single national level of permissiveness or repression. The episode places the strongest Victorian ideal of female asexuality and moral superiority among aspirational middle and upper-middle classes, while less documented working-class practices included informal unions, sex outside marriage, abandonment, and re-partnering across both periods.

Respectability was also asymmetric. Middle-class men could be expected to possess sexual knowledge that women were expected not to display; married men needed a veneer of discretion more than complete abstinence; and elite male sexual freedom could rely on poorer women being treated as available, disposable, or safely outside respectable society. Public morality thus organized hierarchy even when private behavior contradicted it.

## Key Claims

- Georgian license and Victorian repression are class-coded simplifications rather than complete period descriptions.
- Respectability operates as public performance as well as personal conduct.
- Sexual knowledge was gendered: men could be expected to know what respectable women were expected to conceal.
- Female sexual transgression carried a more total threat to social identity than comparable male behavior.
- Wealth and status could externalize the risks of male sexual freedom onto poorer women.
- Moral panic and scandal could function simultaneously as discipline and entertainment.

## Evidence

- Uneven period change: [[24-sex-in-the-city-glt9221135938]] distinguishes literate elite and middle-class ideology from less documented continuity in working-class relationships.
- Gendered knowledge and reputation: [[24-sex-in-the-city-glt9221135938]] contrasts male sexual knowledge with the ideal of female ignorance and the “fallen woman” consequence.
- Classed availability: [[24-sex-in-the-city-glt9221135938]] connects slumming and elite men's sexual access to the disposability assigned to working-class women.
- Public-private boundary: [[24-sex-in-the-city-glt9221135938]] describes openly fashionable courtesans, private mistresses, and the veneer expected of married men.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Class categories were internally diverse, ideals did not determine every life, and evidence survives unevenly. Continued working-class practices should not be romanticized as freedom, because abandonment, poverty, coercion, pregnancy, and stigma remained material risks. Nor does hypocrisy make norms unreal: reputational rules could shape marriage, work, housing, legal exposure, and survival even when widely breached.

## What Changed

- Established a framework joining period stereotypes to class-specific ideology, gendered knowledge, and unequal material consequences.
