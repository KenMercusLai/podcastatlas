# Elite Overproduction

## Definition

Elite overproduction is the condition in which a society creates more educated or credentialed aspirants to high-status roles than the available elite positions can absorb.

## Current Synthesis

The episode attributes the concept to [[PeterTurchin|Peter Turchin]] and applies it to imperial China. Examination participation and status expectations could expand even when civil-service posts did not, leaving many qualified or ambitious candidates blocked from the offices toward which the system directed them.

The concept qualifies simple stories of educational expansion. Greater access may widen opportunity, but if destination roles remain scarce it can also intensify competition, resentment, faction formation, and challenges to institutional legitimacy. The Hong Xiuquan case is illustrative but cannot carry the causal model alone.

## Key Claims

- Credential supply and elite-position supply can diverge.
- Selection systems shape expectations as well as allocate actual posts.
- Blocked aspiration can turn educational success into political pressure.
- Mobility and instability are compatible outcomes of the same institutional expansion.

## Evidence

- Chinese examination case: [[84-exams-glt6507641896]] says candidate numbers increased while civil-service openings did not keep pace.
- Political interpretation: [[84-exams-glt6507641896]] connects the mismatch to Turchin's concept and to potential destabilization.
- Biographical illustration: [[84-exams-glt6507641896]] places Hong Xiuquan's repeated failure within a later trajectory toward religious revolt.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not establish a threshold, measurement method, or general causal estimate. Examination failure cannot by itself explain the Taiping Rebellion, and educated non-officeholders may enter many productive roles outside the state. Position scarcity, family advantage, ideology, economic crisis, and political organization require separate analysis.

## What Changed

- Created a source-bounded synthesis linking credential expansion, scarce posts, and political pressure.
