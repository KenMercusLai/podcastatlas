# Colon Polyp Risk Stratification

## Definition
Colon polyp risk stratification is the source's distinction between lower-risk and higher-risk colorectal polyps based on pathology, size, dysplasia, and follow-up needs.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat a detected polyp as a risk question, not an automatic cancer verdict. Inflammatory or hyperplastic polyps and very small lesions are presented as lower risk than adenomatous, larger, morphologically concerning, or dysplastic lesions, while pathology, completeness of treatment, bleeding risk, and patient context govern surveillance. The clinical point is emotional as much as technical: pathology and risk tier should guide follow-up rather than generic panic or neglect.

VOL.158 adds a patient-level example in which a hyperplastic polyp is described as less concerning than an adenomatous polyp. It also connects risk interpretation to procedure logistics: very small lesions may be managed during colonoscopy, while larger lesions may require a different removal plan, bleeding control, clips, and temporary activity restrictions.

## Key Claims
- A polyp finding should be interpreted through pathology, size, morphology, and treatment completeness.
- Inflammatory, hyperplastic, or very small polyps are presented as lower risk after management.
- Adenomatous, larger, morphologically concerning, or dysplastic polyps may require different treatment and closer follow-up.
- Polyp removal can interrupt progression from precancerous lesion to cancer.
- Surveillance intervals should follow risk tier rather than annual repetition by default.

## Evidence
- Low-risk category: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] places inflammatory, hyperplastic, and small polyps in a lower-risk bucket after treatment.
- Higher-risk category: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] flags polyps larger than roughly 0.8 to 1 centimeter or with intraepithelial neoplasia as requiring closer follow-up.
- Prevention function: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] says finding and treating polyps can block precancerous progression.
- Patient-level pathology distinction: [[vol-158-gaojiacheng-zhe-changweijing-feizuo-bukema-ft-luffy-yixue-pindao-gaoguifm-lmifb8pnp9d-qs_ezkdzyijyutov|VOL.158]] describes a hyperplastic polyp as less concerning than an adenomatous polyp and says size, morphology, bleeding risk, removal method, and clipping can change management and short-term activity advice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources provide public education, not a pathology or surveillance protocol. Number of polyps, histology, size, morphology, completeness of removal, bleeding risk, bowel-prep quality, family history, genetic syndromes, inflammatory bowel disease, age, and clinician judgment can change treatment and surveillance.
