## Definition
Institutional exit escalation is the progression from temporary removal within an institution, through defensive withdrawal from a dangerous post, to a final break that creates an independent base of action.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1103-caocao-liangci-hui-laojia-zhen-shi-yinwei-guanchang-shiyi-ma-lq_e-te21mwhczyomeyxcem1brkz|Hanji 1103]] defines the pattern through three early [[CaoCao|曹操]] exits. His first return to Qiao is imposed by a court purge and marriage association; his second follows enforcement in Jinan and is interpreted as an attempt to leave before eunuch and local-power retaliation; his 189 CE flight from [[DongZhuo|董卓]] abandons the captured court and becomes viable only when [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]] and [[Chenliu|陈留]] provide protection and a mobilization base.

The sequence is not linear proof that Cao Cao always intended rebellion. Each exit preserves or expands future agency under a different threat. The concept therefore separates institutional exclusion, preventive resignation, and independent organization while showing how repeated exposure can make the next exit more structurally radical.

## Key Claims
- Dismissal by association is an imposed exit: the actor loses office because factional conflict reaches a family or patronage network.
- Preventive resignation is an anticipatory exit: the actor leaves while still holding office because enforcement has created credible retaliation risk.
- A final institutional break requires an outside option, not only disillusionment; trusted allies, geography, resources, and political protection make independence feasible.
- Re-entry between exits matters because it shows that early withdrawal did not yet equal rejection of the institution.
- Repeated exits can preserve agency while also accumulating knowledge, relationships, and reasons for a more durable break.

## Evidence
- Imposed exit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1103-caocao-liangci-hui-laojia-zhen-shi-yinwei-guanchang-shiyi-ma-lq_e-te21mwhczyomeyxcem1brkz|Hanji 1103]] links Cao Cao's first dismissal to the Song-empress purge and his family's marriage connection to Song Qi.
- Preventive exit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1103-caocao-liangci-hui-laojia-zhen-shi-yinwei-guanchang-shiyi-ma-lq_e-te21mwhczyomeyxcem1brkz|Hanji 1103]] interprets Cao Cao's Jinan resignation as leaving before eunuch and local-elite interests could retaliate.
- Independent-base exit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1103-caocao-liangci-hui-laojia-zhen-shi-yinwei-guanchang-shiyi-ma-lq_e-te21mwhczyomeyxcem1brkz|Hanji 1103]] makes Zhang Miao's friendship and Chenliu's location and commercial resources prerequisites for Cao Cao's 189 CE break.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source reconstructs motive from sequence and political context; it does not prove Cao Cao's private reasoning at every exit. The three cases also occur under late-Han elite conditions and should not be generalized into a universal career model without comparable evidence. This pattern differs from [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]], which concerns actors already trapped by accumulated power and enemies, and from [[ActivePoliticalDormancy|主动蛰伏式政治积累]], which requires evidence of capacity-building during the pause itself.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish Cao Cao's imposed dismissal, preventive resignation, and independent-base break.
