# Van Eyck Oil Layering

## Definition

Van Eyck oil layering is the episode's account of how translucent oil-based paint layers produce luminous color, apparent depth, reflected light, fine texture, and convincing material surfaces without claiming that [[JanVanEyck|Jan van Eyck]] invented oil painting.

## Current Synthesis

[[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] separates technical invention from transformative mastery. Oil media existed before Van Eyck, but his control of transparent layers made skies brighter, grass greener, gold appear metallic without relying only on gold leaf, and tiny figures or textures remain legible at extraordinary scale.

Technique and meaning reinforce each other. The increased illusion of presence supports portrait individualism, sacred immediacy in the [[GhentAltarpiece]], and the display of fabrics, jewelry, landscape, skin, and wealth within [[BurgundianCourtArt|Burgundian court culture]].

## Key Claims

- Van Eyck's significance is mastery and refinement, not invention of oil paint.
- Translucent layers allow light and color to accumulate into luminous depth.
- Fine control supports minute detail, texture, shadow, and three-dimensional presence.
- Material realism can serve religious, portrait, and patronage purposes simultaneously.
- Technical change gains historical force when joined to wealth, observation, and demand for individual presence.

## Evidence

- Priority boundary: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] explicitly says oil painting existed before Van Eyck.
- Layered luminosity: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] attributes shimmer, depth, brighter color, and convincing gold to transparent oil layers.
- Minute observation: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] points to tiny boatmen, fabrics, shadows, skin, and architectural or landscape detail.
- Cultural effect: [[271-belgium-historys-greatest-artist-glt1331750756]] links technical control to the contemporary impression of revolutionary realism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide conservation science, recipes, workshop comparison, or a complete chronology of oil-paint development. Its visual explanations are interpretive and should not be converted into a sole-cause theory of the Northern Renaissance.

## What Changed

- Created a technique concept that preserves the distinction between inventing oil paint and mastering layered oil effects.
