# Personal Context Knowledge / 个人上下文知识

## Definition
Personal context knowledge is a self-authored record of how information intersects with a person's experiences, states, changing beliefs, decisions, and consequences, rather than a generic collection of retrievable facts or quotations.

## Current Synthesis
As search and generation make public information easier to retrieve, archival value moves toward context that cannot be reconstructed reliably from the public corpus: why an idea mattered now, what it corrected, what action followed, and how body, mood, relationships, and circumstances shaped interpretation. Active recording matters because spending attention on an item is itself evidence of salience.

Accumulated context can support pattern recognition and [[AIAsPersonalMirror|AI reflection]], such as noticing a relationship between sleep, energy, anxiety, and interpretation of business news. Yet a record is selective rather than complete, and model memory does not share human forgetting or changing significance. Personal context is therefore evidence for reflection, not an exhaustive self or automatic authority over the person who produced it.

## Key Claims
- Generic information and isolated quotations lose archival value when they are cheaply retrievable.
- Records become more useful when they capture relevance, belief change, action, state, and outcome.
- Voluntary recording carries a salience signal that passive behavioral capture may lack.
- Longitudinal personal records can reveal recurring relationships among physiology, emotion, thought, and action.
- AI can reorganize such records into situated feedback but may misweight, omit, or over-retain context.
- Forgetting and changing importance are functional parts of human experience, not merely memory defects to eliminate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The reported user trend is not accompanied by sample size, method, or comparison data. Generic reference notes can remain valuable where provenance, exact wording, specialist detail, or offline access matters. Active records are still shaped by mood, self-presentation, omission, and survivorship, while passive data can sometimes reveal patterns that self-report misses. Record-grounded AI also raises privacy, retention, inference, and dependency risks that the episode does not evaluate systematically.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish personally situated records from generic knowledge collection.
- Made selective memory, changing salience, and privacy-relevant incompleteness explicit limits.
