# Windfall Decision Trap / 无故之利

Windfall decision trap / 无故之利 is the decision pattern highlighted in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-1-ljshdryvf9qgabtn-siontthzsok]]. [[FengTing|冯亭]] offers [[Shangdang|上党]]'s seventeen cities to [[ZhaoState|赵国]], making an exposed war burden look like a free territorial gain.

[[ZhaoBao|赵豹 / 平阳君]] gives the principle: a benefit with no apparent cause should be treated as potential disaster. The episode's modern gloss is that "free" can be the most expensive option when the recipient has not identified the obligation, enemy, or future action bundled into the gift.

The source also ties the case to "利令智昏." [[ZhaoDan|赵丹 / 赵孝成王]] can understand the abstract risk, but the emotional force of a seventeen-city offer leads him to reinterpret the event as evidence of his own virtue rather than as [[QinState|秦国]] pressure displaced onto Zhao.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-2-lrlulw0b6v0fb2xpr2i5ifem1h4n]] makes the trap operational. [[ZhaoBao|赵豹 / 平阳君]] diagnoses the offer as "嫁祸," while [[Pingyuanjun|平原君]] and [[ZhaoDan|赵丹 / 赵孝成王]] accept it anyway. The source also adds [[HistoricalDreamForeshadowing|dream foreshadowing]]: gold and jade appear in Zhao Dan's dream as treasure that signifies worry, matching the concept's core point that visible gain can hide future cost.

## Key Claims
- A windfall is dangerous when it transfers another actor's unresolved conflict to the recipient.
- The larger the visible gain, the easier it is for emotion and status hunger to outrun cost analysis.
- Political leaders can rationalize a trap by turning it into recognition of their virtue, prestige, or destiny.
- Strategic evaluation should ask who benefits if the recipient accepts, who will retaliate, and why the offer was available at all.
- The decision is no longer only a warning once the recipient acts; rewards and ceremonies can institutionalize the trap before the military consequence arrives.
