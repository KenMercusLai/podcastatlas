# Adventure-Game Narrative Structure / 冒险游戏式叙事结构

## Definition

Adventure-game narrative structure organizes a story as movement among bounded objectives where traversal, environmental clues, puzzles, escalating combat and acquired objects repeatedly unlock the next stage.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded 《云南虫谷》 discussions treat game language as a structural analogy rather than a claim that the novel literally follows software rules. In the approach, the protagonists cross a waterway where insects, python and predatory fish successively replace one another, acquire weapons from a tree-bound aircraft, defeat a vine-powered coffin, and obtain route information from a ritual register. In the valley, they locate an entrance, decode a temple mechanism, learn the gourd cave's ecology through murals and bodies, survive a boss-like encounter, and receive a copper box that contains both reward and the next problem. Progress is therefore not only geographic: each obstacle changes what the party knows, possesses and can do.

The analogy is strongest when stages remain causally connected. Earlier maps and feng-shui clues locate the route; local images foreshadow the creature; damage changes the battle state; and apparent victory releases an object that continues the serial plot. It is weaker when “boss,” “health bar” or “loot drop” becomes only a humorous label for spectacle, or when a new obstacle has no prepared relation to earlier observation and choice.

The 《精绝古城》 discussion adds vertical information progression. Animal, ordinary-human, giant-eye and queen levels do not primarily award equipment; they spatialize the fictional society's hierarchy and reveal increasingly consequential knowledge as the party descends. A tower-like structure can therefore organize interpretation and suspense even when it is not a literal sequence of combat encounters.

## Key Claims

- A level-like sequence joins travel, interpretation, action and information gain rather than reducing adventure to consecutive fights.
- Boss-like escalation becomes narratively useful when resistance, vulnerability and changed tactics are legible inside the encounter.
- Rewards can carry knowledge or activate the next danger, so apparent completion also functions as serial continuation.
- Threat replacement can create level-like escalation when one creature changes the environment for the next encounter rather than merely adding another fight.
- Tiered space can make status, information and danger legible as progressive stages even when combat is not the principal activity.
- Game language is an interpretive analogy; it does not by itself establish authorial borrowing, design intent or literary quality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on three episodes from one podcast's interpretation of two novels in one series, not comparative narratology or evidence of authorial intent. Travel quests, tiered buildings, riddles, monsters, treasure and serial cliffhangers predate video games, so resemblance does not establish direct influence. A game-like sequence can clarify causality and pacing, but it can also feel mechanical when obstacles are interchangeable, rewards are arbitrary, or characters become delivery systems for puzzles.

## What Changed

- Extended the structure backward from the valley entrance to the approach through waterway and forest.
- Added predator replacement, equipment gain and route information as forms of state change between stages.
- Added vertical information and status hierarchy as a noncombat form of stage progression.
