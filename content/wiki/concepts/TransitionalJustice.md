# Transitional Justice / 转型正义

## Definition

Transitional justice is the public effort to address state violence and systematic rights violations during or after major political change through truth, accountability, victim recognition and repair, memory, institutional reform, and decisions about punishment, amnesty, or reconciliation.

## Current Synthesis

[[20-shiji-zhuanzhi-shi-dianying-xian-qingsuan-yi-bufen-gkwriaiosoyxcck-awttzvx8]] presents transitional justice as a continuing political process rather than a one-time switch from dictatorship to democracy. Criminal trials can name perpetrators and establish a public record; archive opening can redistribute knowledge previously monopolized by security institutions; truth commissions can make testimony public; compensation and memorial practice can recognize victims; and cultural work can keep unresolved violence legible.

No mechanism is sufficient by itself. Germany's layered and delayed reckoning, Spain's transition through an initial pact of forgetting, Argentina's cycles of trial and obstruction, Chile's military-constrained transition, South Africa's conditional amnesty model, and Cambodia's delayed prosecutions all show that inherited power and social capacity shape what can be attempted. Peace may require compromise, but compromise can also postpone truth, recovery of remains, and responsibility.

## Key Claims

- Transitional justice combines legal, institutional, material, archival, and cultural work rather than reducing justice to criminal trials.
- Public acknowledgment by a state or institution differs from private regret because it establishes responsibility before the political community.
- Negotiated transitions inherit constraints from militaries, courts, bureaucracies, parties, wealth, and international power.
- Truth and reconciliation are distinct outcomes; disclosure does not guarantee healing, agreement, equality, or non-recurrence.
- Formal regime change remains incomplete when surveillance, impunity, patronage, inequality, or authoritarian habits persist.
- Accountability can begin before transition through evidence preservation, testimony, art, research, and public language.

### Cultural and pre-transition work

- [[20-shiji-zhuanzhi-shi-dianying-xian-qingsuan-yi-bufen-gkwriaiosoyxcck-awttzvx8]] treats film as a way to preserve missing histories and prepare the materials and imagination required for later accountability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a wide-ranging film podcast rather than a comparative legal or historical study. National pathways are compressed, some films are discussed briefly or without complete viewing, and claims about causal success, public opinion, healing, or recurrence require country-specific evidence. The episode's rejection of a universal model should not become a reason to treat all mechanisms as equally effective or all compromises as unavoidable.

## What Changed

- Created a comparative framework separating transition as an event from justice as a prolonged and reversible process.
- Added pre-transition evidence preservation and cultural memory as forms of preparatory justice.
