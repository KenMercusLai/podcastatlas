# Cycle Worldview / 周期世界观

Cycle worldview is [[176-niuyue-yinian-yige-beiguanzhuyizhe-de-huofa-725-shalong-shilu-lte-k8lw8e1txxw3nfxvo3wal8ah]]'s compact statement of [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]]'s investing and life philosophy: everything has cycles. The claim explains why he is drawn to [[AssetAllocation]], diversification, behavioral friction, and anti-linear extrapolation. If cycles are real, then neither boom nor despair should be treated as permanent.

The source also names the worldview's danger. A strong cycle view can drift toward nihilism if every effort feels temporary or pre-reversed by the next phase. David Weng's answer is to pair cycle humility with present-tense anchors: make content, meet listeners, keep learning, and do the concrete work at hand.

## Key Claims
- Cycles make all-in extrapolation dangerous because good and bad states both change.
- Diversification fits a cycle worldview because no asset, country, currency, or industry stays permanently dominant.
- The worldview supports patience and cooldown discipline, but it should not become an excuse for passivity.
- Present-tense work is the antidote to cycle nihilism: action remains meaningful even if every condition eventually changes.
- The concept clarifies why "investment is a vote for worldview" is not only a slogan; portfolio structure expresses a theory of time.
