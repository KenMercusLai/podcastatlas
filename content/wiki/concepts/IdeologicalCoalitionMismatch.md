# Ideological Coalition Mismatch

## Definition

Ideological coalition mismatch occurs when the policies or identity of a chosen leader conflict with the core expectations of the coalition that installed or sustains that leader.

## Current Synthesis

Mexican conservatives recruited a European Catholic monarch to reverse liberal disorder and protect a Church-centered social order, but [[FerdinandMaximilian]] was a genuine liberal. His decision to retain nationalized Church property and pursue reform satisfied neither side: sponsors felt betrayed, while republicans led by [[BenitoJuarez]] rejected the foreign-backed throne itself.

The case shows that moderation does not automatically create a center. When coalition partners disagree about the regime's purpose, compromise policies can subtract committed support faster than they add legitimacy.

## Key Claims

- Candidate prestige can conceal programmatic incompatibility during coalition formation.
- Sponsors may agree on removing an opponent without agreeing on the replacement regime's policy.
- A leader's sincere moderation can weaken rather than broaden support when legitimacy is already polarized.
- Coalition mismatch becomes existential when the regime also lacks independent military and fiscal capacity.

## Evidence

- Conservative invitation: [[163-the-last-emperor-of-mexico-glt3817272823]] says Mexican monarchists promoted a Catholic crown as a solution to liberal rule and instability.
- Liberal monarch: [[163-the-last-emperor-of-mexico-glt3817272823]] contrasts Maximilian's politics with the reactionary role his sponsors expected.
- Church-property conflict: [[163-the-last-emperor-of-mexico-glt3817272823]] identifies his confirmation of nationalization as a central conservative disappointment.
- Failed center: [[163-the-last-emperor-of-mexico-glt3817272823]] says attempts to win moderate liberals did not displace Juarez's republican resistance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not prove that ideological mismatch alone caused defeat. Foreign occupation, fiscal weakness, geography, military operations, United States pressure, and French withdrawal were independently important.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the contradiction between Maximilian's liberal program and his conservative route to power.
