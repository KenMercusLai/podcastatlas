# 1755 Lisbon Earthquake

## Definition

The 1755 Lisbon earthquake was a compound urban catastrophe of earthquake, tsunami, and fire whose destruction reshaped Lisbon and became a European reference point for debates about providence, suffering, reason, and state capacity.

## Current Synthesis

The episode treats the disaster as both intellectual shock and governing test. Lisbon's religious calendar, built environment, waterfront exposure, and fire combined to magnify destruction, while Voltaire used the event against easy philosophical optimism. [[MarquisDePombal|Pombal]] answered through emergency control and a planned grid reconstruction, making recovery an example of centralized Enlightenment administration without removing its coercive edge.

## Key Claims

- Earthquake, tsunami, and fire interacted rather than operating as separate disasters.
- Lisbon's wealth and prominence made the catastrophe a Europe-wide intellectual event.
- The disaster challenged confident providential and optimistic explanations of suffering.
- Emergency response joined practical relief to severe coercive control.
- Rebuilding the Baixa as a planned grid converted disaster recovery into state-led urban redesign.

## Evidence

- Compound-disaster evidence: [[229-portugal-gold-earthquakes-and-brazil-part-3-glt8317395817]] describes shaking, tsunami, church fires, and a wider firestorm on All Saints' Day.
- Intellectual-shock evidence: [[229-portugal-gold-earthquakes-and-brazil-part-3-glt8317395817]] connects the disaster to Voltaire's attack on philosophical optimism in *Candide*.
- State-capacity evidence: [[229-portugal-gold-earthquakes-and-brazil-part-3-glt8317395817]] links Pombal to burial, relief, anti-looting punishment, and planned reconstruction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Mortality and destruction estimates vary and are retained here only as episode-level claims. The source's contrast between an older city and rational Enlightenment rebuilding is analytically useful but compresses the social costs, engineering history, property politics, and longer recovery process.

## What Changed

- Established the earthquake as a compound disaster and state-capacity test.
- Connected its European philosophical afterlife to its material urban reconstruction.
