# 派系站队退出资本 / Factional Alignment Exit Capital

派系站队退出资本 / factional alignment exit capital enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1027-jingzhou-zhizhu-liubiao-weihe-nan-cheng-bazhu-ll-ebguqllsf-5ycvpdogihzn3lj|Hanji 1027]] as the episode's modern workplace frame before the [[LiuBiao|刘表]] branch. The host argues that as organizations grow, formal hierarchy becomes insufficient for understanding real power; employees also have to read informal reporting lines, resource channels, and factional expectations.

The concept's practical claim is not "always choose a side." If alignment becomes unavoidable, the source recommends matching the faction to one's professional capability, keeping independent output, and investing most effort in portable assets such as clients, technical skill, credentials, data-backed performance, and external networks. Exit capital is what prevents a person from becoming fully trapped by the side they temporarily need.

## Key Claims
- Factional politics often appears when organizations become too large for formal charts to capture real influence.
- Alignment is safer when backed by hard, portable professional value rather than only dependence on a patron.
- Independent output and external options reduce the cost of leaving a failed faction.
- Relationship maintenance is useful but should not consume the effort needed to build transferable capability.
- The source uses this workplace frame as a modern analogy for [[FenceSittingStrategicExposure|骑墙式战略暴露]], not as a separate historical claim.
