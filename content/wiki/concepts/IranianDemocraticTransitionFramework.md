## Definition

The Iranian democratic transition framework is [[RezaPahlavi|Reza Pahlavi]]'s proposed process for replacing the Islamic Republic while leaving Iran's permanent form of government to popular choice. In the source, it rests on territorial integrity, separation of religion and state, equality and rule of law, and a democratic constitutional process.

## Current Synthesis

The framework distinguishes transition leadership from permanent sovereignty. A temporary leader should connect domestic groups, the diaspora, existing state personnel, and outside supporters; maintain security; and create a credible constitutional choice without predetermining monarchy or republic. The ballot box, not foreign selection, hereditary claim, protest visibility, or transitional control, is supposed to confer final legitimacy.

Its central institutional tension is between rupture and continuity. Pahlavi says Iranians require a clean break from regime rule, while the [[IranProsperityProject|Iran Prosperity Project]] would retain or reintegrate personnel who did not commit atrocities. That may preserve state capacity, but the source does not explain the vetting, accountability, representation, or command arrangements needed to prevent either institutional collapse or disguised continuity.

The framework also joins political transition to economic expectation. [[ShervinPishevar|Shervin Pishevar]] and Pahlavi argue that secular democracy, diaspora participation, regional cooperation, and international investment could make post-regime Iran prosperous. Those benefits are a motivating vision rather than evidence that the transition path is secure or the forecasts will materialize.

## Key Claims

- Final constitutional authority should belong to Iranians through informed debate, voting, and ratification rather than to a transitional leader or foreign government.
- Territorial integrity, secular government, legal equality, and democratic procedure function as constraints on acceptable transition outcomes.
- Transitional leadership is legitimate only as stewardship toward a public decision, not as self-authorizing permanent rule.
- A clean break from authoritarian rule may still require selective administrative continuity to avoid state collapse.
- Economic reconstruction can support legitimacy, but investment forecasts do not substitute for security, justice, representation, and institutional design.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The framework is reconstructed from a favorable interview rather than from a transition mandate, election, plan text, or adversarial institutional review. Reported support from protests, universities, workers, diaspora groups, minorities, or military personnel does not establish majority consent. The episode does not specify representation rules, minority protections beyond general equality, transitional legal authority, atrocity-vetting standards, civilian control of armed bodies, treatment of separatist claims, dispute resolution, or safeguards against transitional entrenchment. Foreign military support may help remove coercive barriers while simultaneously weakening the domestic legitimacy the framework says is essential.
