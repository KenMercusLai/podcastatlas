# Battle of the Alma

## Definition

The Battle of the Alma was the Allied victory over Russian forces on 20 September 1854 during the opening invasion of Crimea in the [[CrimeanWar|Crimean War]].

## Current Synthesis

[[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] treats the Alma as a victory produced by uneven competence rather than clean high command. [[JacquesLeroyDeSaintArnaud|Saint-Arnaud]] was gravely ill, [[FitzRoySomerset1stBaronRaglan|Raglan]] issued ambiguous orders and moved beyond easy control of his army, and [[AlexanderSergeyevichMenshikov|Menshikov]] wrongly treated his left flank as impassable. French forces under Pierre Bosquet climbed the cliffs, while [[ColinCampbellBritishArmy|Colin Campbell]]'s disciplined Highland regiments used the Minié rifle effectively against Russian troops with inferior muskets.

The cavalry's non-use remained ambiguous. Raglan's restraint preserved a weakened Light Brigade on unsuitable ground and without the Heavy Brigade, but also prevented pursuit when the Russian retreat became a rout. The Allies then stayed to tend casualties rather than move rapidly on Sevastopol, allowing a tactical victory to lose much of its immediate strategic leverage.

## Key Claims

- Russian terrain confidence failed when French troops crossed a supposedly impassable flank.
- British infantry discipline and longer-ranged rifle fire materially contributed to victory.
- Command errors affected both coalitions, with Russian errors proving more damaging in the battle.
- Keeping the Light Brigade out of action may have preserved it while forfeiting pursuit.
- Failure to advance immediately on Sevastopol separated battlefield victory from strategic exploitation.

## Evidence

- Flank and firepower - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] links Bosquet's climb and Campbell's rifle-armed Highlanders to Russian defeat.
- Command friction - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] describes ambiguous British orders, Saint-Arnaud's illness, and Menshikov's overconfidence.
- Cavalry restraint - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] records Raglan's repeated refusal to commit or release the Light Brigade.
- Strategic aftermath - [[723-the-charge-of-the-light-brigade-crushing-the-russians-part-3-glt9985161615]] connects the pause after victory to Sevastopol's reinforcement and harbour obstruction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source's tactical sequence, weapon comparison, commanders' intentions, ground suitability, force figures, and counterfactual claim that Sevastopol could have been taken quickly remain episode-scoped. Preserving the cavalry and treating casualties were real considerations, so non-pursuit is not reducible to simple cowardice or incompetence.

## What Changed

- Established the Alma as a tactical victory whose incomplete exploitation helped transform a rapid operation into a siege.
