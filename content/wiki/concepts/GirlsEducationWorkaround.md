# Girls Education Workaround

Girls education workaround is the partial substitute described in [[afghanistan-five-years-on-our-correspondent-visits-6a7ee547049c692a6b3ee0a9]], where a Kabul madrasa provides some learning for secondary-age girls after the [[Taliban]] ban on girls' secondary education. The source treats the school as valuable to its students but far from a real replacement for public education and future opportunity.

The concept keeps two facts together. First, families and teachers may find narrow openings inside a restrictive system. Second, those openings can be expensive, limited, precarious and unable to restore university access, employment or public autonomy under [[TalibanHardlineRule]].

## Key Claims
- A workaround can preserve learning without ending the underlying ban.
- Private or religious schooling can exclude poorer girls even when it offers protection for those who can attend.
- Education restrictions are not only about classrooms; they also narrow work, movement, voice and imagined futures.
- Workarounds should be read as evidence of agency under constraint, not proof that the restriction has been solved.
