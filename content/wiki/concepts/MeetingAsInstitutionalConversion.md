# Meeting as Institutional Conversion / 会议的制度转化

## Definition

Meeting as institutional conversion is the process by which authority, selected participation, procedure, records, and implementation channels turn an idea or policy direction into durable organizational action.

## Current Synthesis

The comparison of the [[WannseeConference|Wannsee Conference]] and [[BaihuGuanConference|Baihu Guan Conference]] identifies a shared institutional form without implying moral equivalence. A meeting matters historically when it does more than exchange views: an authorized convener defines the problem, relevant actors coordinate or debate, a record stabilizes the result, and offices or legal channels carry it outward.

Wannsee shows the destructive extreme. Nazi racial ideology and mass killing preceded the meeting, but ministerial, police, diplomatic, transport, legal, economic, and occupation coordination made genocide more systematic. Baihu Guan shows a different state-making use: disputed classical interpretations were routed through scholars and officials, imperial review, written proceedings, and norms that the episode presents as politically and socially consequential.

## Key Claims

- A meeting gains institutional force from authorization, representation, procedure, documentation, and implementation together.
- Written minutes or compiled conclusions stabilize decisions beyond the people physically present.
- Specialized participants can convert a broad aim into interoperable tasks across offices and jurisdictions.
- Procedure is morally indeterminate: it can coordinate atrocity or organize legitimate disagreement and rule-making.
- Comparative mechanism does not imply equivalence of purpose, power, harm, or political system.

## Evidence

- Genocidal coordination: [[30-gaibian-lishi-de-guanjian-huiyi-wanhu-huiyi-vs-baihuguan-huiyi-ltuaquuhrwirgv2egihrk4vuo7cm]] presents Wannsee as cross-departmental coordination of an already developing extermination project.
- Administrative record: [[30-gaibian-lishi-de-guanjian-huiyi-wanhu-huiyi-vs-baihuguan-huiyi-ltuaquuhrwirgv2egihrk4vuo7cm]] connects Eichmann's minutes to later logistics and responsibility.
- Court consensus: [[30-gaibian-lishi-de-guanjian-huiyi-wanhu-huiyi-vs-baihuguan-huiyi-ltuaquuhrwirgv2egihrk4vuo7cm]] presents Baihu Guan as debate, reporting, imperial review, and compilation into authoritative doctrine.
- Long implementation chain: [[30-gaibian-lishi-de-guanjian-huiyi-wanhu-huiyi-vs-baihuguan-huiyi-ltuaquuhrwirgv2egihrk4vuo7cm]] traces meeting outcomes into bureaucratic execution, legal norms, political ethics, and social expectations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The paired cases do not establish that all meetings have causal force, that records accurately capture every decision, or that implementation follows mechanically from discussion. The episode's comparison is heuristic and source-scoped. Wannsee and Baihu Guan differ radically in purpose, coercion, victims, participants, political order, textual transmission, and moral status; only the bounded institutional mechanism is compared.

## What Changed

- Created a comparative concept linking meeting authority, participation, record, and implementation.
- Made moral non-equivalence an explicit boundary of institutional comparison.
