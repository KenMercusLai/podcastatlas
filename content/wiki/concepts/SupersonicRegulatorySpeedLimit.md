# Supersonic Regulatory Speed Limit

## Definition
Supersonic regulatory speed limit is the source-described U.S. rule that categorically restricted civilian supersonic flight over land rather than regulating the ground-level noise or impact that motivated the restriction.

## Current Synthesis
Across the sources, [[BlakeScholl]] argues that the 1973 rule froze experimentation by regulating speed as a proxy for sonic-boom harm. Boom's strategy pairs [[XB1SupersonicDemonstrator|XB-1]] proof with [[BoomlessCruise]] to support performance-based regulation. The newer interview reports rapid progress—an executive order and unanimous congressional steps—but also says the bill had not yet cleared the full Senate. The current judgment is therefore that Boom achieved meaningful policy momentum, not that every legal, administrative, and operational constraint has disappeared.

## Key Claims
- Proxy-based regulation can block low-impact technical approaches when it prohibits speed rather than measuring ground effect.
- A categorical ban restricted both commercial-airliner routes and smaller experimental or private-aircraft market paths.
- Visible technical proof can make regulatory reform more politically legible than abstract advocacy alone.
- Executive action, legislation, and agency implementation are different layers and should not be collapsed into one completed event.
- Bipartisan votes indicate coalition breadth but do not establish operational safety, community acceptance, or statutory finality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The policy chronology is Scholl's account and is time-sensitive. The transcript simultaneously describes the categorical ban as ended and legislation as unfinished, so the precise legal effect requires primary regulatory materials beyond this ingest. Technical feasibility under favorable atmospheric conditions does not itself settle certification, enforcement, off-nominal noise, environmental impact, or public acceptance.
