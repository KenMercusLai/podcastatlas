## Definition

分层验证式继位接管 / layered accession risk verification is the pattern in which an invited ruler does not treat an offer, a seal, or a single assurance as sufficient, but uses independent messengers, visible public procedure, ritual sequencing, physical site control, and trusted security appointments to convert a dangerous invitation into operational authority.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-2-luij-woqemjhmopkgrgnl9bclfwd|Hanji 207 part 2]] begins with competing threat models. [[ZhangWuWesternHan|张武]] sees founding veterans who have destroyed the Lü group and control Chang'an, so he recommends delay. [[SongChangWesternHan|宋昌]] sees Liu-family legitimacy, interlocking royal power, public settlement, and military allegiance that make ministerial self-coronation implausible. [[LiuHeng|刘恒]] does not resolve this disagreement through confidence alone; he accumulates distinct confirmations.

The verification chain moves from family and divination to political and physical checks. [[BoZhaoWesternHan|薄昭]] questions [[ZhouBo|周勃]], Song Chang inspects the ministerial reception, and Liu Heng proceeds only after both channels report sincerity. At the bridge, Song Chang refuses private business and Liu Heng postpones acceptance of the seals. At the Dai residence, [[ChenPing|陈平]] and the ministers publicly state their legitimacy case and the ritual refusals reach a ruler-minister orientation.

The final stage is operational rather than verbal. [[LiuHongYoungEmperorWesternHan|刘弘]] is removed, armed guards are made to stand down, Liu Heng enters the palace, Song Chang receives the capital armies, and Zhang Wu receives inner-palace security. The sequence does not eliminate coercion: rival young princes are then killed. It therefore describes how uncertainty is reduced and command is transferred, not a normative claim that the accession is lawful or humane.

## Key Claims

- Rival adviser assessments are useful when they expose different failure modes: invitation-as-trap and excessive-delay loss can both be tested.
- Independent confirmation is stronger than repeated reassurance from the same accession coalition.
- Refusing private negotiation and delaying receipt of symbols can protect the public legibility of an otherwise suspicious transfer.
- Ritual acceptance becomes credible only when paired with physical control of palace access and the incumbent command environment.
- Trusted appointments to army and palace-security posts convert formal enthronement into usable authority.
- Successful risk verification does not validate the purge, parentage claims, or killing that may accompany the transition.

## Evidence

Competing threat models:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-2-luij-woqemjhmopkgrgnl9bclfwd|Hanji 207 part 2]] contrasts Zhang Wu's warning about veteran control of Chang'an with Song Chang's argument from lineage, public settlement, royal structure, and military allegiance.

Independent confirmation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-2-luij-woqemjhmopkgrgnl9bclfwd|Hanji 207 part 2]] says Bo Zhao checks the ministers' intent through Zhou Bo and Song Chang later inspects the visible reception before Liu Heng advances.

Public and ritual boundary-setting:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-2-luij-woqemjhmopkgrgnl9bclfwd|Hanji 207 part 2]] says Song Chang rejects a private meeting, Liu Heng delays accepting the seals at the bridge, and the Dai-residence ceremony publicly converts invitation into accession.

Physical and command transfer:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-207-liubang-xiaoyizi-lvxu-bei-gunsha-2-luij-woqemjhmopkgrgnl9bclfwd|Hanji 207 part 2]] reports palace clearance, guard resistance and dispersal, Liu Heng's entry, and the appointment of Song Chang and Zhang Wu over the capital armies and palace security.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one structured podcast summary and should not be generalized to every accession. Divination may provide psychological permission but is not independent empirical verification. The public-procedure interpretation does not prove Liu Heng's private motives, and the survival of armed resistance at the palace gate shows that earlier assurances did not settle every command node. Finally, the same sequence ends in reported killings of displaced princes, so procedural caution for the incoming ruler must not be confused with due process for those removed.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the episode's full sequence of competing assessments, independent checks, public ritual, palace transfer, and trusted security appointments.
