## Definition
AI toy companionship is the design and governance of toys that use models, memory, voice, movement, or network access to create an ongoing social or emotional relationship, especially with children.

## Current Synthesis
AI toys are emotional products before they are productivity tools. Their value depends on continuity, timing, tone, restraint, and safe interaction rather than merely putting a chatbot in a physical shell. Speech can strengthen companionship, but a deliberately nonverbal toy may avoid uncanny or harmful responses.

Child-facing use creates the strictest boundary. A toy can displace parent-child conversation, misread household conflict, create dependence on artificial gentleness, or deliver unsafe guidance. Vol. 179 adds the policy layer: connected conversational toys need age-appropriate evaluation and market-access rules. The current judgment favors governed, testable access over either unregulated sale or an indefinite blanket ban.

## Key Claims
- AI toys create emotional value through memory, timing, tone, continuity, and physical presence.
- Speaking is only one route; nonverbal behavior can preserve companionship while reducing wrong or uncanny speech.
- Child-facing toys should not be designed as replacements for parents, friends, or real-world conflict learning.
- Network access expands both usefulness and exposure to unsafe advice, privacy loss, remote change, and service withdrawal.
- Age grading, safety testing, disclosure, parental controls, data limits, incident reporting, and market-access standards are more durable governance tools than treating every product identically.
- Anthropomorphic design and hidden technical infrastructure can deepen attachment, increasing the maker's responsibility for dependency and discontinuation.

## Evidence
### Emotional and interaction design
- [[ep253-baohuo-de-ai-haowu-daodi-shi-zhenxiang-haishi-zhishangshui-lgt0cdkotgnzjl0mu2tx41p9fw-4]] contrasts speech-centered and deliberately nonverbal toys and argues that memory and emotional timing matter more than generic chatbot access.

### Child-development boundary
- [[ep253-baohuo-de-ai-haowu-daodi-shi-zhenxiang-haishi-zhishangshui-lgt0cdkotgnzjl0mu2tx41p9fw-4]] warns that AI toys can displace family conversation, distort children's interpretation of conflict, or make artificial gentleness a substitute for real relationships.

### Market governance
- [[podcast179-v1-podcast179-v1]] reports a New York restriction on connected conversational AI toys and argues for age grading, safety testing, and market-entry rules rather than a permanent categorical ban.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Emotional attachment is not automatically harmful, and a well-designed toy may support play, comfort, or learning. The available sources provide product discussion and policy commentary rather than child-development trials, privacy audits, regulator text, or long-term family studies. The reported New York action and its scope remain episode-attributed, and no single age rule or test regime is established here as sufficient.

## What Changed
- Added connected-toy market governance as a distinct layer beyond product design.
- Added age grading and pre-market safety testing as possible alternatives to an indefinite blanket ban.
- Made network access and remote product change explicit sources of child-safety risk.
