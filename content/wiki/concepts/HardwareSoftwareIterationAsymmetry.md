# Hardware–Software Iteration Asymmetry / 软硬件迭代不对称

## Definition
Hardware–software iteration asymmetry is the difference between correcting a digital product after release and correcting a manufactured physical product whose architecture, tooling, supply chain, quality, safety, inventory, and customer delivery have already made the decision costly to reverse.

## Current Synthesis
The XPeng interview turns the familiar contrast into a decision rule: stop a wrong physical direction before delivery even when the write-off is painful, because discovering the mistake after delivery multiplies cost through users, quality, service, reputation, and replacement cycles. Software can often improve through frequent updates, while a vehicle needs a sufficiently strong baseline at launch. That changes the meaning of product excellence from endless pre-release perfection to "no fatal weakness plus a clear strength."

## Key Claims
- Physical-product errors become harder to reverse as design, sourcing, tooling, production, certification, and delivery accumulate.
- Software updates can improve behavior after release, but they cannot reliably repair every hardware, quality, ergonomic, or safety constraint.
- Killing a wrong project before delivery can be less damaging than protecting sunk cost until customers expose the failure.
- Hardware launch quality should be judged across the whole system, not only by a standout feature or software interface.
- The asymmetry makes direct fact-finding and early product judgment more valuable in capital-intensive companies.

## Evidence
- Direction-stop evidence: [[lnmpqiptzutrevnwr4ot5zrwxuak-lnmpqiptzutrevnwr4ot5zrwxuak]] records He Xiaopeng's decision to cancel an in-development vehicle and his view that he should have acted even more aggressively.
- Damage-timing evidence: [[lnmpqiptzutrevnwr4ot5zrwxuak-lnmpqiptzutrevnwr4ot5zrwxuak]] distinguishes the serious cost of stopping before formal delivery from the much larger damage of discovering the problem after customers receive the product.
- Product-standard evidence: [[lnmpqiptzutrevnwr4ot5zrwxuak-lnmpqiptzutrevnwr4ot5zrwxuak]] defines strong hardware as having no fatal weakness and at least one clear strength, rather than assuming later iteration will cure foundational defects.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The distinction is relative, not absolute. Hardware can be modular, repaired, recalled, or improved in later generations, while software failures can also cause irreversible safety, trust, legal, or data harm. Correction cost varies by product category, regulation, serviceability, inventory, and installed base. The current evidence is one automotive founder's retrospective account rather than comparative product-failure research.
