# Clinical Visit Symptom Tracking / 就诊症状记录

## Definition
Clinical visit symptom tracking is the practice of converting fluctuating lived experience into a concise, longitudinal record of symptoms, timing, function, treatment exposure, response, priorities, and warning signs for clinical review.

## Current Synthesis
The myasthenia-gravis episode treats visit preparation as evidence preservation. A diary, short home video, functional score, prior report, medication timeline, and comparison with personal best and worst states can show what a brief examination misses. The goal is not to narrate every detail or perform one's own diagnosis, but to make clinically relevant change visible: the leading problem, onset or worsening, affected daily action, intervention, response, and present priority.

Tracking also has limits. Excessive measurement can amplify anxiety, a score can miss context, and organized records cannot transfer diagnostic responsibility from clinicians to patients. Family participation can help when fatigue, distress, or cognitive load makes new information hard to retain, but it should preserve rather than overwrite the patient's own account.

## Key Claims
- Longitudinal evidence is especially valuable when symptoms fluctuate outside the clinic.
- A useful record prioritizes the main symptom, time course, functional effect, treatment exposure, response, and current question.
- Personal best and worst comparisons can be more informative than an isolated adjective such as “better” or “worse.”
- Diaries, videos, scores, and prior reports are complementary evidence rather than interchangeable proof.
- Family support can preserve observations and instructions when illness or distress limits real-time processing.
- Tracking should prompt timely escalation for warning signs rather than justify waiting for the next scheduled visit.
- Measurement supports shared judgment but does not replace examination, diagnosis, or clinician responsibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework comes from one disease-specific public-education episode. Different conditions require different instruments, observation windows, privacy choices, and urgent-care thresholds. Videos and family observations need consent and context; scores can create false precision; old records may not resolve a current problem. Clinicians and health systems still carry responsibility for adequate questioning, explanation, follow-up, and emergency routing.
