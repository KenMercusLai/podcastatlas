# Civic Relationships As Crisis Infrastructure

Civic relationships as crisis infrastructure is the pattern where long-term relationships with policymakers, regulators, and civic institutions become operational during a crisis. [[tsr-s5-ronconway-v5-tsr-s5-ronconway-v5]] adds the concept through [[RonConway]]'s claim that real relationships, not transactional lobbying, made calls get answered during the [[SiliconValleyBank]] weekend.

The concept extends [[FounderFriendlyInvestorSupport]] and [[FounderCrisisMediation]] beyond company-level crises. In this episode, founder-helping work moves into public-sector translation: explaining payroll risk, routing information to [[USTreasury|U.S. Treasury]], coordinating with congressional figures, and pushing urgency before global markets opened.

## Key Claims
- Crisis relationships are built before the emergency; they cannot be manufactured reliably during the emergency.
- Civic access can transmit operational facts quickly when formal processes are moving slowly.
- The same access can create legitimacy questions because public outcomes may depend on who can reach powerful people.
- Long-term relationships matter most when the message must be short, credible, and repeated across institutions.
