# Interstate Economic Warfare / 跨国经济战

## Definition
Interstate economic warfare uses demand, prices, trade access or resource flows to weaken another polity's productive capacity or finance, sometimes in preparation for armed conflict. A deliberate inducement-and-cutoff tactic differs from embargo or maritime seizure; the cases below span unlike eras and source traditions.

## Current Synthesis
The Guan Zhong stories depict a buyer drawing a rival away from food production, accumulating grain, then shutting trade. The Hengshan variant adds arms purchases and a subsequent attack. The Elizabethan notes instead describe [[Spain|Spanish]] silver-route vulnerability, [[England|English]] privateering and the use of captured treasure in diplomacy. They do not document an English-shipping embargo or quantify its impact. None establishes a universal quantitative model of economic war.

## Key Claims
- Temporarily attractive trade can redirect labor away from essential production before a buyer reverses terms.
- Grain reserves and border closure can turn market dependence into political coercion and migration pressure.
- The Hengshan arms-and-grain account makes economic pressure a prelude to, not replacement for, a military assault.
- Silver-route raiding and captured treasure work through transport and imperial finance rather than agricultural price manipulation.

## Evidence
- In the [[GuanZhong|管仲]] episode, [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]]'s [[QiState|齐国]] promotes [[LuState|鲁国]] and [[LiangState|梁国]] cloth, then drops demand after labor has moved from farms; high-priced [[ChuState|楚国]] deer purchases follow a similar attraction, while Qi stockpiles grain and closes borders. The speaker describes grain-price stress and flight toward Qi, but exact prices and totals are not independently checked. ## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The two Guan Zhong notes are one [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》解说]] series, with unverified quantities; the three Elizabethan notes are another narrative series. Raids, war finance and manipulated purchases are distinct mechanisms; an embargo would be another mechanism, but is not evidenced in this inventory. The 693 transfer-route discovery is a vulnerability assessment; 694 covers diplomatic use of treasure; 695's main concern is continental war. The old link to [[StateResourceMonopoly]] refers to Guan Zhong's domestic alternative to monopoly, not proof that a monopoly was itself this offensive tactic. [[LuxuryConsumptionEconomicCirculation]] concerns domestic employment, not intentional enemy deprivation.

## What Changed
- Split agricultural inducement from privateering and kept chronology and uncertainty visible.
- Distinguished civilian economic harm from the separate military sequel at Hengshan.
