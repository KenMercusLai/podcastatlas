# Interstate Economic Warfare / 跨国经济战

Interstate economic warfare / 跨国经济战 is the strategic frame [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-3-gudai-de-jingji-zhanzheng-neng-you-duo-kepa-liybgoz8lspypax6dhuke2js0pxf]] gives to [[GuanZhong|管仲]]'s attacks on Lu, Liang, and Chu. The episode's point is that a state can weaken rivals through prices, demand, trade access, grain reserves, and population movement before armies decide anything.

The source gives two cases. In the cloth case, [[QiState|齐国]] first manufactures demand for [[LuState|鲁国]] and [[LiangState|梁国]] cloth, then cuts off the market after rival labor has shifted away from farming. In the deer case, Qi pays high prices for [[ChuState|楚国]] live deer, encourages Chu people to leave the fields, quietly stockpiles grain, and then closes the border. In both stories, market attraction is followed by scarcity pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-4-weishenme-shuo-guanzhong-caishi-jingji-zhan-de-bizu-lrdleqozazh4-xdwas4karzgbmza]] adds the [[HengshanEconomicWarfare|Hengshan]] variant. Qi buys [[HengshanState|恒山国]] weapons at high prices, then grain at high prices, before closing passes and attacking. This extends the concept from consumer and hunting demand to strategic goods and staple food: a rival's apparent specialty becomes a vulnerability when the buyer can set prices, accumulate reserves, and choose when to stop.

This concept is adjacent to [[StrategicLuxurySabotage|strategic luxury sabotage]], but broader. The target does not need to become decadent; it only needs to reallocate labor and attention toward the buyer's artificially profitable demand. The decisive pressure then comes from food security, not from luxury itself.

## Key Claims
- Economic warfare can begin as apparently beneficial trade.
- A hostile buyer can change an opponent's production structure by making one activity temporarily more profitable than staple production.
- Grain reserves and border control turn a distorted market into political coercion.
- Population flight can be an economic-war outcome: people move toward the state where food and livelihood are more secure.
- The source treats economic war as an extension of state capacity, not as a substitute for it; Qi can exploit others because Guan Zhong has already strengthened Qi's internal administration and economy.
- The Hengshan case shows the military edge of the mechanism: economic distortion can prepare a direct attack instead of replacing war completely.
