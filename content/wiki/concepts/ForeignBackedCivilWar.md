# Foreign-Backed Civil War

Foreign-backed civil war is the pattern where domestic armed camps keep fighting partly because external patrons provide weapons, money, diplomatic cover, or political confidence. [[outsider-chance-ending-sudans-proxy-war-6a75b3695d765781a2b1b132]] grounds the concept in [[Sudan]], where the [[RapidSupportForces|RSF]] and [[SudaneseArmedForces|SAF]] are presented as local actors inside a wider regional competition.

The source says evidence points to significant [[UnitedArabEmirates|UAE]] backing for the RSF, while the UAE denies this. It also says the SAF receives support from [[SaudiArabia]], [[Egypt]], and especially [[Turkey]], whose drones are linked to recent battlefield gains.

The concept's diplomatic tension is that the same outside powers may be necessary for peace. The episode describes a Sudan "quad" of the [[UnitedStates]], Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE as a possible mediation channel, but their interests in the [[RedSea]], gold, arable land, and political influence make settlement difficult.

## Key Claims
- External backing can prevent either side from losing decisively, extending a war after battlefield momentum changes.
- Patron interests can be strategic, economic, and political at the same time.
- Outside pressure may be needed to push local partners toward ceasefire, but sponsorship can also reduce the credibility of that pressure.
- [[LowCostDroneWarfare]] makes foreign supply especially consequential when drones can shift battlefield momentum and hit civilian infrastructure.
