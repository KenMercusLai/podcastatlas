## Definition

选择性赦免与分批换防 is a crisis-control pattern that isolates leaders of collective defiance, preserves a return path for ordinary participants, and then separates or rotates the affected force in stages rather than attempting one abrupt purge.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1155 presents the pattern through [[ZhaoYanLateHan|赵俨]]'s handling of troops ordered from Guanzhong toward Hanzhong. Facing a force far larger than his immediate mounted escort, Zhao Yan first secures the loyalty of those around him, promises that only ringleaders will be punished, and allows other participants to return. When news of replacement troops renews fear, he reassures a selected group by retaining it temporarily, transfers the remainder, and later rotates out those initially kept after relief arrives.

The mechanism combines two time horizons. Selective accountability lowers the immediate cost of leaving the mutiny, while phased force separation reduces the longer-term risk of continuing to rely on a network whose cohesion has already proved politically dangerous. It is therefore neither blanket pardon nor indiscriminate punishment.

## Key Claims

- A collective disturbance can be de-escalated by distinguishing organizers from followers rather than assigning one liability to the whole group.
- Amnesty works as an exit route only when ordinary participants believe surrender will not expose them to the leaders' punishment.
- Immediate restoration of order does not by itself restore long-term command trust.
- Staged transfer can prevent a feared mass replacement from becoming the trigger for another coordinated revolt.
- Temporarily retaining a trusted subset can stabilize the transition, but later rotation may still be necessary to remove concentrated network risk.
- The pattern depends on credible communication, replacement capacity, and enough force or legitimacy to enforce the final separation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The evidence is one structured podcast summary of a historical episode, not a general test of mutiny policy. Exact troop totals, kinship ties, sequence, communications, pardon terms, reinforcements, and the claim that more than twenty thousand people were transferred remain source-scoped. Selective amnesty may fail when leaders cannot be identified, promises are not credible, replacement forces are unavailable, or participants expect delayed retaliation.

## What Changed

- Established selective accountability and staged force separation as a combined crisis-management pattern.
- Distinguished immediate return-to-order from the separate problem of rebuilding command trust.
