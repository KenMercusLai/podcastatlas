# Aggression Circuit Biology

## Definition
Aggression circuit biology is the source-scoped frame that aggressive behavior can arise from coordinated neural circuits and action patterns rather than from a single emotion, moral label, or generalized anger state.

## Current Synthesis
The aggression Essentials sources treat aggression as a structured process. Reactive, proactive, indirect, offensive, defensive, and predatory aggression differ by context and function, but all can be studied as behavior that depends on internal state, target cues, and neural action sequences. [[KonradLorenz]] supplies the fixed-action-pattern and pressure language, while [[WalterHess]], [[DavidAnderson]], and [[DaiYuLin]] supply the circuit evidence.

The key circuit node in the sources is the [[VentromedialHypothalamus]]. VMH neurons, especially estrogen-receptor-containing populations, can rapidly shift experimental animals toward attack, but fear-related VMH populations can interrupt offensive aggression and produce freezing. Downstream structures such as [[PeriaqueductalGray]] help route state signals into motor actions, posture, and pain-modulation support. This does not reduce aggression to one brain spot; it makes the practical claim more precise: pressure can build, circuits can be triggered, competing states can override each other, and interventions may lower the probability of expression before the full action sequence unfolds.

## Key Claims
- Aggression should be separated into reactive, proactive, and indirect forms because intent and context change interpretation.
- Aggression is not simply sadness amplified; the episode treats grief and aggression as distinct circuit processes.
- Fixed action patterns help explain aggression as a sequence that can begin, escalate, and end.
- VMH stimulation and optogenetic evidence show that small neuron populations can have large behavioral effects.
- Fear circuitry can override offensive aggression, showing that aggression circuits operate within a hierarchy of competing states.
- Downstream circuits translate VMH activation into biting, limb movement, attack patterns, and pain-modulation support.
- Circuit evidence explains behavioral probability and state transitions, not moral permission or complete human violence causation.

- VMH evidence - [[essentials-understanding-controlling-aggression-scim4757138073]] credits [[DavidAnderson]]'s lab with VMH necessity/sufficiency findings and describes [[DaiYuLin]]'s optogenetic stimulation examples.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources rely heavily on animal experiments and public neuroscience interpretation. They do not claim the VMH alone explains human criminal violence, abuse, war, online harassment, psychiatric diagnoses, or legal responsibility. Protective aggression, impulsive attack, deliberate harm, indirect shaming, predation, mating-related behavior, and defensive freezing remain contextually different even if some mechanisms overlap.
