# 旧敌忠义重释 / Former Enemy Loyalty Reframing

旧敌忠义重释 / former enemy loyalty reframing is the founding-order move [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-170-liubang-weisha-cong-luoyang-banjia-dao-changan-1-lpioq8izitba7uhbdybp7i03djdy|Hanji 170 part 1]] builds around [[JiBu|季布]]. A defeated officer's attacks on [[LiuBang|刘邦]] can be read as personal offense, but [[ZhuJiaLu|朱家]] persuades through [[XiahouYing|夏侯婴]] that the same conduct can be read as loyalty to [[XiangYu|项羽]].

The concept matters because the new ruler is choosing the moral vocabulary of the new regime. If loyal service to a former enemy remains punishable, capable defeated officers have reason to flee, hide, or join outside powers such as [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] or [[Nanyue|南越]]. If it is publicly pardoned and rewarded, the dynasty can claim to honor loyalty while also absorbing talent.

This differs from ordinary [[PardonAsStatePower|pardon]]. Clemency stops punishment; loyalty reframing turns the prior conduct into a usable virtue. It also differs from [[RetainerReciprocityEthic|士为知己者死]], because the focus is not the retainer's inner obligation but the victor's decision to reinterpret that obligation after regime change.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-170-liubang-weisha-cong-luoyang-banjia-dao-changan-2-lpqtexvxjrbrpjgbw5hrgmhl9apk|Hanji 170 part 2]] adds the negative boundary through [[DingGong|丁公]]. Ding Gong had helped Liu Bang by sparing him at Pengcheng, but Liu Bang later executes him as disloyal to [[XiangYu|项羽]]. The concept therefore does not mean all useful former enemies are forgiven. It requires the victor to distinguish loyal service to an old lord from conduct that can be framed as private betrayal of assigned duty.

## Key Claims
- A founder can reduce resistance by separating former enemy duty from personal enmity.
- Publicly rewarding a former enemy may signal that the new order honors loyalty even when loyalty once opposed it.
- The move is pragmatic as well as moral: it prevents talented fugitives from strengthening outside enemies.
- The concept belongs to [[BattlefieldVictoryToFoundingOrder|战场胜利到建国秩序]] because victory must be translated into norms for defeated personnel.
- Hanji 170 part 2 adds a boundary case: Ding Gong shows how a founder can punish former-enemy conduct when it is framed as dereliction rather than loyal service.
