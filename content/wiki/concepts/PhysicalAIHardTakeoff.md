# Physical AI Hard Takeoff

## Definition
Physical AI hard takeoff is the forecast that robots could enter a recursive physical-production loop by building robots, data centers, chip fabs, mines, refineries, and other infrastructure needed to scale AI and robotics.

## Current Synthesis
The All-In robotics special records this as 1X founder Bernt Bornich's most aggressive forecast, not as a settled wiki conclusion. It extends AI hard-takeoff language from software self-improvement into atoms: the decisive threshold would be robots becoming useful enough to expand the physical capacity that builds more robots and compute infrastructure.

## Key Claims
- The hard-takeoff claim depends on robots becoming capable enough to build and maintain the infrastructure of robotics and AI itself.
- A physical recursive loop is harder than software recursion because mining, refining, factories, chip fabs, and data centers require safety, materials, energy, logistics, and labor coordination.
- Home humanoid progress alone would not prove hard takeoff; the claim requires scaled industrial and infrastructure capability.
- Teleoperation, video pretraining, robot-control data, and deployment scale are possible precursors but not proof.
- Hurst's no-single-singularity view in the same episode is an internal qualification against treating hard takeoff as the only likely path.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is explicitly source-scoped. The same episode provides counterweight through Hurst's gradualist view and through the many deployment constraints named by all four robotics guests: control data, manipulation, safety, reliability, customer ROI, privacy, and supply-chain trust.
