# Air Cargo Value Density / 航空货运价值密度

## Definition
Air cargo value density is the idea that aviation's logistics importance comes from carrying high-value, time-sensitive goods whose economic value is disproportionate to their physical weight.

## Current Synthesis
The episode separates weight from value. Ma Xiao cites IATA statistics to argue that air freight represents only a small share of global cargo weight but a much larger share of cargo value, which makes aviation critical for goods where freshness, speed, inventory risk, or value concentration matter.

This concept helps connect airport hubs to supply chains. Hong Kong's cargo airport role, Memphis's FedEx-centered logistics position, and early Cathay advertising around fresh Australian oysters all show why aviation can matter even when most heavy bulk goods move by sea, rail, or road.

## Key Claims
- Air cargo should be evaluated by economic value and time sensitivity, not by tonnage alone.
- High-value and perishable goods make aviation strategically important despite its small physical share of freight.
- Cargo value can reinforce airport-hub cities by attracting logistics, manufacturing, and international trade flows.
- Air cargo and passenger networks can overlap but do not always have the same city logic.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The IATA figure is not independently verified in the wiki, and the source does not compare air cargo against carbon cost, price sensitivity, or sea/rail alternatives. Treat the concept as a value-density lens rather than a blanket argument for air freight.

## What Changed
- Created the cargo-value concept to distinguish aviation's economic role from tonnage share.
- Connected Hong Kong, Memphis, and Cathay examples under one logistics frame.
