# Age Verification Patchwork

Age verification patchwork is the fragmented legal environment where websites face different online age-check rules across U.S. states and countries. [[tech-20251226-1226-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251226-1226-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[DrewHarwell]]'s account of state laws, a [[SupremeCourt]] decision involving [[Texas]], and new rules in the [[UnitedKingdom|United Kingdom]] and [[Australia]].

The patchwork matters because the same website can face different obligations depending on a user's location. The source's [[Bluesky]] and [[Mississippi]] example shows one operational result: a platform may withdraw or block service rather than accept unclear liability or expensive compliance.

## Key Claims
- State-by-state age-verification laws can make web access depend on geography.
- Large fines or unclear liability can push platforms toward geoblocking, withdrawal, or conservative compliance.
- International rules in the UK and Australia make age assurance a cross-border platform-governance issue.
- Patchwork compliance increases pressure for federal rules, but federalization still leaves open who should verify age and hold liability.
- Moving checks from websites to app stores, devices, or operating systems may reduce repeated scans but concentrates control with platform companies such as [[Apple]] and [[Google]].
- The patchwork is related to [[StateAIRegulationPatchwork]] as a federalism problem, but it is distinct because the core burden is age assurance and access control rather than AI governance.
