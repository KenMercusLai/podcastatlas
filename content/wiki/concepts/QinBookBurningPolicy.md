# 秦代焚书令 / Qin Book-Burning Policy

秦代焚书令 / Qin book-burning policy is the 213 BCE measure [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-124-fenshu-kengru-wo-zai-bzhan-wei-qinshihuang-pingfan-ljnhwcouqu35rmvb-cnrzyzj2j6l]] presents after [[ChunyuYue|淳于越]] argues for restoring enfeoffment and [[LiSi|李斯]] answers that private classical criticism threatens unified rule. The source separates this policy from the later坑儒 incident.

The policy's target is not every book. In the source's summary, private non-Qin histories, [[Shijing|《诗经》]], [[Shangshu|《尚书》]], and other scholarly writings are to be burned under local official supervision, while official archives and practical books on medicine, divination, and agriculture/tree planting are excepted. People who want to study law are directed to officials, tying book destruction to [[OfficialLearningMonopoly|official learning monopoly]] rather than only to physical censorship.

The source treats the policy as political statecraft. It is meant to protect the [[CommanderyCountyAntiEnfeoffment|commandery-county settlement]], prevent "以古非今," and reduce the ability of private scholars to build rival interpretive communities. That links it to [[ImperialHomogenization|帝国整齐划一]] and [[LegalistRulerTechnique|法家君术]]: divergent texts and teachings become governability risks.

The same source also marks the policy's limits. Destroying six-state histories creates cultural loss, and harsh bans can produce concealment rather than compliance. [[KongFu|孔鲋]] hiding books becomes the example that coercive erasure can unintentionally strengthen preservation behavior among committed readers.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-2-lishishang-qinshihuang-weihe-yao-fenshu-kengru-lulaivzp1xqs71nbaltr-ygpjihw|Qinji 125-2]] reinforces the boundary by narrating the separate [[QinKengruIncident|秦代坑儒事件]]. The later punishment case arises from fangshi flight, criticism, and court interrogation rather than from Li Si's book policy, even though later memory often compresses both into "焚书坑儒."

## Key Claims
- The policy belongs to "焚书," not automatically to the separate坑儒 incident.
- It is triggered by an administrative-political dispute over enfeoffment versus direct rule.
- It targets private textual authority more than all practical knowledge.
- Official archives and practical technical books are treated differently from private histories and classical-political texts.
- Legal learning is not abolished; it is redirected to officials.
- The source frames the policy as rational from Qin's political-control perspective while still culturally destructive.
- Book burning can be self-defeating when it motivates careful hiding and later recovery.
- Qinji 125-2 confirms that this policy should not be used as direct evidence for the separate坑儒 punishment sequence.
