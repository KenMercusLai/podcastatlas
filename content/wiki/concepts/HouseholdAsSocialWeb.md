# Household As Social Web

Household as social web is a narrative structure where one family compound becomes a map of wider society. [[fanwai-15-banlatte-jiang-jinpingmei-la-erqie-haishi-luse-de-991508433]] uses [[JinPingMei|《金瓶梅》]] this way: [[XimenQing|西门庆]]'s home is not only domestic setting, but a node connected to wives, concubines, servants, business, loans, officials, neighbors, religious ceremonies, sworn brothers, and brokers.

The source's point is that the household turns private life into public structure. [[WuYueniang|吴月娘]]'s management, [[PanJinlian|潘金莲]] and [[WuDalang|武大郎]]'s shopfront marriage, [[WuSong|武松]]'s boundary warnings, and [[WangPo|王婆]]'s tea-shop mediation all show how money, gender, reputation, and access move through rooms, streets, and kinship. This also clarifies why the episode links [[JinPingMei|《金瓶梅》]] to [[HongLouMeng|《红楼梦》]]: one household can become a social world.

## Key Claims
- A household can be a literary model of society when it contains economic, gender, kinship, servant, neighbor, and official relations.
- Domestic scenes can carry political and commercial force without leaving the home or street.
- Household-network reading helps prevent character flattening: desire, cruelty, fear, duty, dependence, and status all move through positions in the web.
- The frame overlaps with [[UrbanEverydayFiction]] but focuses on the household as the organizing node.
