# Native Bee Ecological Pollination / 本土蜂生态授粉

Native bee ecological pollination is the source's frame for bees as local ecological actors rather than only honey-producing livestock. In [[ep261-mifeng-weiji-ruguo-shiqu-mifeng-shijie-jianghui-zenyang-lqp-cwcdxx1ziixb35ijmmvd6cv]], [[LiuChang|刘畅]] reports from [[Xishuangbanna|西双版纳]], where local bee diversity, rainforest plants, and cash-crop orchards make pollination a habitat relationship as much as a farm service.

The episode contrasts this with large-scale Italian honeybee management. Italian bees fit migratory, single-flower honey production and intensive beekeeper control, while Chinese honeybees and other local bees can use scattered mountain flowers and support surrounding vegetation. Stingless bees and giant honeybees add further variety: they may produce special honey or wild-harvest value, but overharvesting can remove the very pollinators that rare rainforest plants need.

## Key Claims
- Native bees can be less commercially scalable than managed Italian honeybees while still being more important for nearby ecological pollination.
- "Bee value" should not be measured only by honey volume; pollination of surrounding plants and crops is part of the value.
- Wild-honey collection becomes risky when harvesters remove whole nests or fail to leave enough for colony reproduction.
- Local cash crops can benefit from pollination services, but pesticide-heavy orchard management can damage the pollinators needed for yield.
- Species surveys matter because undercounted bee diversity makes conservation and agricultural planning less precise.
