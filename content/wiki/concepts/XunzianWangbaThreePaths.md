# Xunzian Wangba Three Paths / 荀子王霸三分法

Xunzian Wangba three paths / 荀子王霸三分法 is the framework [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-71-xunzide-xingbenelun-lnyfxoxauexhwbrwkdevs8m4mr2j]] draws from [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s Wangba chapter in [[XunziText|《荀子》]]. The episode presents state and throne as extremely powerful tools: used well, they bring order and honor; used badly, they make collapse more dangerous because the ruler cannot easily return to private safety.

The first path is rule through ritual and righteousness. A ruler who refuses unjust gain and innocent killing, and who aligns ministers, laws, and political goals with ritual order, can move toward kingly rule. The episode associates this with classical exemplars such as [[ShangTang|商汤]] and [[ZhouWuwang|周武王]].

The second path is rule through trust. Even if a ruler's moral order is not perfect, credible commands, reward and punishment, and interstate agreements can still generate hegemonic power. The third path is rule through profit, expedient deception, and broken trust; it makes subjects and ministers distrust each other, makes allies suspicious, and turns power into a route toward state ruin.

## Key Claims
- The framework refines [[WangdaoVsBadao|王道 versus 霸道]] by adding a collapse path beneath kingly and hegemonic rule.
- Trust is not treated as soft morality only; in the hegemonic path, it is a practical condition for military strength, administrative unity, and alliance reliability.
- Deceptive statecraft can win immediate advantage while destroying the social and diplomatic trust needed for survival.
- [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] is the source's practical warning: strong-state resources, conquest, and private power do not protect a ruler if they are not converted into durable order.
- The framework links [[ConfucianIdealGovernance|儒家理想治理]] to realistic state security rather than presenting it as only moral aspiration.
