## Definition

Reproductive body omen politics / 生殖身体祥瑞政治 is the pattern where fertility, menstruation, pregnancy, birth, or related body signs are translated into auspicious political evidence.

## Current Synthesis

The current page is grounded in one source. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] introduces the pattern through "子孙瑞," which the host reads as a euphemistic record of [[EmpressWangPingdi|汉平帝王皇后]]'s menstruation and fertility potential. [[WangMang|王莽]] then uses that sign to justify opening [[Ziwudao|子午道]].

The concept is adjacent to [[AuspiciousOmenPolitics|祥瑞政治]], but its focus is narrower. Instead of a river, stone, animal, or celestial sign, the omen is attached to a woman's reproductive body. That makes the political use double-edged: the body is treated as valuable for dynastic fertility, while the woman herself remains a sign-bearing instrument inside someone else's legitimacy project.

## Key Claims

- Female reproductive maturity can be recoded as public dynastic good fortune.
- Euphemism matters because it turns ordinary physiology into respectable court language.
- Political actors can use reproductive signs to authorize unrelated public projects.
- The sign-bearing woman may gain symbolic value without gaining political agency.
- The pattern overlaps with omen politics, marriage politics, and menstrual stigma.

## Evidence

- Fertility sign: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] reads "子孙瑞" as menstruation and reproductive maturity.
- Public project authorization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] says Wang Mang invokes the empress's sign while announcing the opening of Ziwudao.
- Agency limitation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-627-1-nvxing-biting-dute-shijiao-jingqi-yinyu-beihou-de-lishi-zhihui-lqomjfejmglm9hylkomktx6rtbtt|Hanji 627-1]] centers Wang Mang's use of the sign rather than any action by Empress Wang herself.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is a single-source concept. It should not yet be generalized into a complete theory of Chinese reproductive omen language without more examples.

The page treats "子孙瑞" through the episode's interpretation. It does not independently settle the philological range of the term across all Han sources.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 627-1 to capture the political use of menstruation and fertility language.
