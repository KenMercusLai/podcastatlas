# Black Death Historical Shock

## Definition

The Black Death historical shock is the exceptional fourteenth-century mortality event caused by *Yersinia pestis*, understood through its scale, speed, geographic reach, and the evidentiary uncertainty surrounding its boundaries.

## Current Synthesis

[[146-disease-vs-the-rise-of-civilisation-glt4238916512]] treats the Black Death as extraordinary even within a history that deliberately emphasizes less famous diarrheal, worm, tropical, and endemic diseases. The episode regards an older estimate of one-third to one-half mortality as broadly defensible but says detailed evidence often points toward roughly half the population across very large affected regions.

Genetics has widened the investigation beyond a Europe-only story. Evolutionary changes in the bacterium several generations before the Black Death may overlap with Mongol-era integration, while Arabic evidence makes Egypt unusually well documented. The eastern limits remain unresolved: scholars dispute whether thirteenth-century Chinese pestilences belonged to the same plague history, and the episode sees little evidence for fourteenth-century spread into India without explaining that apparent boundary.

## Key Claims

- Bubonic plague was exceptional in its combination of speed, extent, and mortality.
- Mortality near one half is plausible across large affected regions, though not uniformly established everywhere.
- Genetic evidence can reopen chronology and geography without by itself proving transmission routes.
- Arabic sources make the pandemic recoverable beyond Latin European evidence.
- Mongol expansion is a possible context for spread, not a settled single-cause explanation.
- Chinese inclusion remains contested and the apparent Indian exclusion remains unexplained.

## Evidence

- Mortality scale - [[146-disease-vs-the-rise-of-civilisation-glt4238916512]] reports detailed studies often converging closer to one-half mortality than one-third.
- Genetic chronology - [[146-disease-vs-the-rise-of-civilisation-glt4238916512]] places important bacterial evolutionary change several generations before the main fourteenth-century outbreak.
- Geographic boundary - [[146-disease-vs-the-rise-of-civilisation-glt4238916512]] contrasts rich Arabic evidence and Egypt's visibility with disputed Chinese attribution and weak evidence for Indian spread.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode summarizes rather than demonstrates mortality reconstruction or phylogenetic results. Regional losses varied, genetic branching does not identify a complete route, and chronological overlap with Mongol empires does not establish direct causation. Chinese pestilence identification remains disputed, and the proposed absence from India is an unresolved inference rather than a fully explained fact.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to preserve both the pandemic's exceptional scale and the uncertainty around its Asian geography.
