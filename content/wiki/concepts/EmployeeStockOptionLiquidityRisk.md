# Employee Stock Option Liquidity Risk / 员工期权流动性风险

Employee stock option liquidity risk is the gap between holding a valuable-looking option grant and being able to convert it into cash. [[qiquan-zhe-zhang-bing-weishenme-yuelaiyue-nanchi-le-1]] argues that this risk has become more important as more companies stay private longer, miss IPO windows, or avoid both failure and listing.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-inside-the-private-stock-market-boom-spacex-anthropic-openai-the-rise-of-secondaries-41564170]] adds the company-approved solution path. The panel argues that when companies such as [[SpaceX]] stay private for many years, organized [[PrivateCompanySecondaries]] can let employees diversify, buy homes, or turn long-vested equity into cash without forcing an IPO.

The source's point is not that [[EmployeeStockOptions]] are worthless. It is that their value depends on exit structure: IPO, acquisition, internal buyback, secondary sale, enforceable exercise rights, tax timing, and whether the company honors the plan. Without those, an employee may own a claim that looks like compensation but behaves like [[PaperWealthVsCashValue|paper wealth]].

## Key Claims
- Private-company options can be hard to sell even when the company's valuation appears high.
- Exercise and tax costs can make an option costly before the employee has any sale proceeds.
- Falling stock or equity value can make exercising economically irrational.
- Long-delayed listings create a different risk from startup failure: the company survives, but the employee still cannot monetize the grant.
- Repurchase rights, expiration windows, and disputes over grantor/employer identity can turn liquidity risk into governance risk.
- The worker-side decision should compare option value with cash salary, tax exposure, personal runway, and trust in the company.
- The All-In secondaries episode adds that structured liquidity programs can reduce the risk, but only if transfer permission, price discovery, tax cost, and sale access are practical for employees.
