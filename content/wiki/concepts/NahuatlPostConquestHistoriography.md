## Definition

Nahuatl post-conquest historiography is the recovery of Indigenous Mexican history through alphabetic Nahuatl writings produced after Spanish invasion, interpreted alongside pictorial records, archaeology, and historical linguistics.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents alphabetic writing as both a colonial disruption and an Indigenous instrument. Young Nahuatl-speaking scholars adopted Roman letters to record histories, prayers, stories, poetry, and religious speech in their own language, preserving perspectives rarely available for ancient conquered peoples.

These texts do not provide an unmediated pre-conquest archive: they were written after war, epidemic disease, conversion pressure, and institutional change. Their value lies instead in widening the evidence beyond Spanish retrospection and allowing Indigenous categories, remembered migrations, political judgments, grief, and survival strategies to reshape the questions historians ask.

## Key Claims

- Indigenous-language texts can materially revise narratives built primarily from conquerors' testimony.
- Alphabetic Nahuatl writing preserved multiple genres, not merely annals of conquest.
- Archaeology, linguistics, pictorial records, and written testimony constrain and complement one another.
- Post-conquest production makes the archive mediated without making it reducible to Spanish viewpoints.
- Writing history was itself a strategy for cultural survival after military defeat.

## Evidence

- Archive formation: [[51-aztecs-glt9590586130]] describes Nahuatl-speaking scholars using Roman letters for histories, prayers, stories, poems, and ritual utterances.
- Multi-method reconstruction: [[51-aztecs-glt9590586130]] combines written traditions with archaeology and historical linguistics in reconstructing migration and identity.
- Narrative correction: [[51-aztecs-glt9590586130]] uses the Indigenous-centered record to reject the gods and immediate-apocalypse traditions.
- Survival function: [[51-aztecs-glt9590586130]] presents the writing of histories as an effort to keep culture alive after failed military resistance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not identify individual manuscripts, authors, dates, transmission chains, or disagreements within the Nahuatl corpus. “Aztec voice” should not imply a single uniform perspective, and post-conquest alphabetic texts remain shaped by colonial settings, memory, genre, translation, and survival. The method broadens evidence; it does not remove the need for source criticism.

## What Changed

- Established post-conquest alphabetic Nahuatl writing as an Indigenous archive rather than a mere supplement to Spanish chronicles.
- Added cultural survival as both a subject of the archive and a reason for its creation.
