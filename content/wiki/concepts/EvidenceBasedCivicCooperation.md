# Evidence-Based Civic Cooperation

## Definition

Evidence-based civic cooperation is the practice of pursuing shared public work through fair inquiry, patient procedure, kindness toward participants, and willingness to revise beliefs when evidence conflicts with prior commitments.

## Current Synthesis

[[ZeldaFelchlin]]'s newsletter message and the episode's account of the [[ParentTeacherAssociation]] join epistemic and relational duties. Truth should not be twisted toward personal or factional purposes, but factual discipline alone is insufficient: inquiry must also be just and kind enough that people can continue participating across disagreement.

The PTA comparison makes the practice institutional. Local activities, school-health measures, national coordination, meeting procedure, and incremental reform are not politically trivial; they create habits and channels through which ordinary participants govern shared institutions. The contrast with the [[JohnBirchSociety]] is therefore not simply left versus right, but revisable cooperation versus a hidden-enemy model that treats disagreement as evidence of dishonesty or subversion.

## Key Claims

- Democratic truth-seeking requires both evidentiary discipline and fair treatment of other participants.
- Belief revision in response to unwelcome evidence is a civic strength, not merely a private intellectual virtue.
- Incremental local work can sustain national reform without requiring revolutionary certainty.
- Cooperation depends on procedures that keep disagreement inside a shared institution rather than recoding opponents as traitors.
- Ordinary association work can build democratic capacity even when its immediate subjects appear mundane.
- Civility cannot substitute for safety when harassment or coercion raises the cost of participation.

## Evidence

### Felchlin's truth standard

- [[john-birch-vs-the-pta-john-birch-vs-the-pta]] paraphrases Felchlin as urging just, rational, kind truth-seeking, resistance to distortion, and acceptance of facts that conflict with one's opinions.

### Institutional cooperation

- [[john-birch-vs-the-pta-john-birch-vs-the-pta]] presents local and national PTA coordination around school modernization, health, access, and meeting practice.

### Contrast under pressure

- [[john-birch-vs-the-pta-john-birch-vs-the-pta]] contrasts patient civic work with secrecy, revolutionary rhetoric, accusation, meeting attrition, and intimidation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode offers a normative contrast, not comparative evidence that PTA positions were always correct, inclusive, or effectively implemented. Calls for kindness and procedure can also protect an unjust status quo unless participants can challenge power and secure safety. The source itself notes that integration backlash affected both organizations' trajectories, so cooperative rhetoric should not erase conflict over race, authority, and unequal institutional access.

## What Changed

- Created a civic model combining evidence, kindness, procedure, and revisable belief.
- Added the qualification that civility requires participation safety and cannot settle substantive justice by itself.
