# Meditation Attention-Mode Selection

## Definition
Meditation attention-mode selection is a state-dependent framework for choosing inward-focused, outward-focused, or attention-switching practice according to the mode that is currently overused or difficult to access.

## Current Synthesis
[[how-meditation-works-science-based-effective-meditations-scim5642770846]] distinguishes interoception—attention to sensations and events at or within the body—from exteroception—attention to the environment. Eyes closed, breathing, and internal objects often favor inward attention; vision and external objects favor outward attention. The practical proposal is to assess the present bias and train flexibility, often by practicing the less automatic mode.

This is a selection heuristic, not a validated diagnostic test or rule that discomfort always means useful neuroplasticity. Attention varies with sleep, stress, setting, trauma, mood, and task. Practice should build voluntary range without intensifying excessive bodily vigilance, dissociation, panic, or other instability.

## Key Claims
- Meditation practices differ partly by where they place attention, so their effects should not be assumed interchangeable.
- Current attentional state can be more useful for practice selection than a permanent inward- or outward-focused identity label.
- Training the less available mode may expand attentional flexibility when challenge remains tolerable and functional.
- Eyes, posture, movement, breath, and the chosen object all influence whether attention turns inward or outward.
- More interoception is not automatically healthier; useful regulation includes the capacity to contact and leave internal sensation.

## Evidence
- Attention taxonomy - [[how-meditation-works-science-based-effective-meditations-scim5642770846]] defines interoceptive and exteroceptive poles and links eyes-closed versus eyes-open practice to different input balance.
- State-matched selection - [[how-meditation-works-science-based-effective-meditations-scim5642770846]] proposes checking the current bias and often training the less natural mode.
- Flexibility practice - [[how-meditation-works-science-based-effective-meditations-scim5642770846]] presents space-time bridging as an early source-scoped exercise for moving from internal sensation through progressively wider external scales and back.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish a validated assessment, comparative trial showing that opposite-bias practice is superior, or a universal relationship between friction and beneficial plasticity. Its continuum and space-time-bridging model are partly Huberman's synthesis. People with trauma, dissociation, panic, mania, psychosis, severe insomnia, or meditation-related deterioration may need modified or professionally supported practice rather than stronger exposure to a difficult mode.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate state-dependent practice selection from generic advice to meditate.
- Preserved the boundary that attentional flexibility, not maximal inward awareness, is the intended outcome.
