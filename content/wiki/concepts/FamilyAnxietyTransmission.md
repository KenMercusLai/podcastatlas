# Family Anxiety Transmission / 家庭焦虑传递

## Definition
Family anxiety transmission is the process by which household atmosphere, parental fear, unspoken pressure, caregiving conflict, and learned danger cues shape another person's anxiety before they can name the source.

## Current Synthesis
Episode 472 treats anxiety as relationally transmissible. Children may not understand family finances, marital conflict, health fear, or adult uncertainty, but they can live inside the emotional atmosphere and learn what is dangerous, important, or unspeakable.

The source extends this beyond childhood. Adult children and partners may absorb elder anxiety, death anxiety, parental helplessness, and a family's old shame or authority patterns. Naming anxiety too bluntly can threaten parental authority because it exposes the parent's felt inability to manage the situation.

## Key Claims
- Children can absorb family anxiety even when adults never explain what they are worried about.
- Unnamed household anxiety can become a normal background state rather than an identifiable emotion.
- Parents may react defensively when a child names their anxiety because it implies the adult cannot manage the situation.
- Fear can be learned through observation and conditioning, then generalized beyond the original object.
- Adult caregiving conflicts are easier to handle when current events, older resentments, and relationship roles are separated.
- Death anxiety and aging anxiety can become family problems when they appear as anger, avoidance, rigid health choices, or refusal to discuss care.
- Breaking transmission requires naming, support, and different action without treating family history as destiny.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode provides a clinical conversation framework, not a complete family-systems model or child-development study. Family anxiety should not be used to blame parents for every adult symptom, and eldercare or health decisions may involve medical, financial, legal, and safety constraints beyond emotion regulation.
