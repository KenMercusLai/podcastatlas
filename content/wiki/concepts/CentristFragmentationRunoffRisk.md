# Centrist Fragmentation Runoff Risk

## Definition

Centrist fragmentation runoff risk is the danger that several broadly compatible candidates divide first-round support so extensively that none reaches the decisive second round.

## Current Synthesis

In the source's [[France|French]] case, at least 15 potential candidates occupy the space between the Socialists and center-right Republicans while [[MarineLePen]] holds a large, stable plurality. The problem is not only candidate quantity: former senior officials struggle to claim renewal, candidates resist coordination, and a consensus figure may emerge too late for a [[TwoRoundPresidentialElection]].

## Key Claims

- A loyal plurality can gain disproportionate power when the opposing field splinters.
- Runoff access requires coordination before it requires majority persuasion.
- Establishment experience can weaken candidates who need to perform as political outsiders.
- Policy repositioning alone is insufficient without energy, direct voter contact, and simple communication.
- Late coordination may fail even if most candidates share the goal of blocking a populist victory.

## Evidence

- Field fragmentation: [[national-front-runner-who-can-beat-le-pen-6ab3960493749e4c0ba24e60]] reports at least 15 possible candidates across the broad center.
- Runoff exclusion: [[national-front-runner-who-can-beat-le-pen-6ab3960493749e4c0ba24e60]] identifies a possible Le Pen-Melenchon runoff as the sharpest consequence.
- Positioning limits: [[national-front-runner-who-can-beat-le-pen-6ab3960493749e4c0ba24e60]] says Philippe, Attal, and Retailleau struggle to look new, while Glucksmann's outsider profile has not made him the polling leader.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Early polls have failed before, candidate fields can consolidate, and the source does not prove that a single centrist candidate would defeat Le Pen. Coordination solves runoff access, not necessarily political purpose or majority appeal.
