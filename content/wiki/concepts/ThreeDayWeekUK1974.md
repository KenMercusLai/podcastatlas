# UK Three-Day Week of 1974

## Definition
The UK three-day week was the Heath government's emergency rationing of commercial electricity from New Year's Eve 1973 during the miners' overtime ban and wider energy crisis.

## Current Synthesis
[[417-britain-in-1974-state-of-emergency-part-1-glt6761791949]] traces the measure from the oil shock, rising coal leverage, and a November state of emergency to the cabinet decision to limit industrial, shop, and office power. It included reduced street lighting, office-temperature limits, a sports-floodlight ban, and early television closure. [[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] shows the political paradox: mild weather and successful adaptation limited disruption, weakening [[EdwardHeath|Edward Heath]]'s attempt to turn hardship into a mandate against union power.

## Key Claims
- The three-day week was planned rationing, not a period defined by random unexpected blackouts.
- It emerged from the interaction of the 1973 oil shock, coal dependence, and miners' industrial action.
- Government restrictions reached factories, shops, offices, street lighting, sport, and broadcasting.
- Businesses and households adapted more effectively than expected under relatively mild weather.
- Limited hardship reduced the political force of Heath's “who governs?” election framing.
- The measure converted a wage dispute into a visible test of governing authority.

## Evidence
- Escalation and design: [[417-britain-in-1974-state-of-emergency-part-1-glt6761791949]] connects the overtime ban, 13 November emergency, Barber's warning, and New Year's Eve restrictions.
- Lived experience: [[417-britain-in-1974-state-of-emergency-part-1-glt6761791949]] stresses orderly allocation, adaptation, and the difference between memory of power cuts and the three-day week itself.
- Electoral effect: [[418-britain-in-1974-the-crisis-election-part-2-glt9690615062]] says mild weather and lower-than-expected disruption weakened the government's case.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The two podcast sources emphasize political narrative and public mood rather than measuring output losses across sectors. Less disruption than forecast does not mean that restrictions imposed no economic or household cost.

## What Changed
- Created the missing link between the energy confrontation and February election.
- Distinguished scheduled restriction from popular memories of random blackout.
