# Working Towards the Führer

## Definition

Working towards the Führer is Ian Kershaw's formulation for subordinates and local activists anticipating [[AdolfHitler]]'s broad ideological wishes, competing to realize them, and radicalizing policy without requiring a detailed personal order for every action.

## Current Synthesis

The 1933–36 persecution sequence combines leader direction with decentralized initiative. Hitler's antisemitic worldview set the goal and political permission, while local SA members, party activists, towns, officials, and institutions tested boycotts, intimidation, signage, raids, exclusions, and violence. These actions shifted expectations and created pressure for national decisions.

The model rejects a false choice between a fully centralized command chain and autonomous popular disorder. Hitler could restrain embarrassing “individual actions” while preserving their ideological objective, then legalize selected demands through the [[NurembergLaws|Nuremberg Laws]]. Initiative from below and authorization from above were mutually reinforcing but did not carry equal power or responsibility.

## Key Claims

- Broad ideological direction can guide action without a detailed order for each measure.
- Competition among subordinate actors can drive cumulative radicalization.
- Local experimentation can prepare and pressure national legal consolidation.
- Tactical restraint of disorder need not mean rejection of the underlying goal.
- Central leadership responsibility remains decisive even when implementation is distributed.

## Evidence

- Local initiative: [[409-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-war-on-the-jews-part-6-glt9460111455]] describes continuing boycotts, intimidation, raids, signage, and the Gunzenhausen violence after the failed national boycott.
- Leader management: [[409-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-war-on-the-jews-part-6-glt9460111455]] recounts Hitler's August 1935 halt to individual actions amid backlash and diplomatic embarrassment.
- Legal consolidation: [[409-the-nazis-in-power-hitlers-war-on-the-jews-part-6-glt9460111455]] presents the Nuremberg Laws as a way to satisfy radical pressure and demands for order through systematic exclusion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is an interpretive model, not proof that every local act reflected a correctly inferred private instruction. It should not dissolve Hitler's agency into institutional momentum or portray all Germans as equally involved. Local actors, state offices, conservative elites, passive bystanders, opponents, and victims occupied sharply different positions.

## What Changed

- Established a leader-direction and subordinate-initiative model for the wiki's 1933–36 Nazi persecution sequence.
