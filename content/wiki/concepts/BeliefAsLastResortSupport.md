# Belief As Last-Resort Support / 信仰作为最后退路

Belief as last-resort support / 信仰作为最后退路 is the social reading of religious practice in [[shan-shang-shan-xia-jie-renjian-wushi-xiaoyao-ji-shenxian-lhjiguq74flhatdxnbrnzhjg-acp]]. [[LiChuang|李闯]] and the host discuss pilgrims, crying visitors, local gods, family protection rituals, and people who come to a Daoist temple after medical, financial, or social options have narrowed.

The concept does not verify supernatural efficacy. Its claim is sociological and ethical: when a person cannot afford care, cannot leave farm work, receives no diagnosis, or has no other place to take grief, [[ChineseFolkReligionLayering]] and temple hospitality can become a usable social form. This helps explain why [[ZhenwuDadi|真武大帝]], local deities, offerings, and ritual gestures can matter without requiring every practice to be reduced to superstition or policy failure.

## Key Claims
- Religious recourse often becomes visible where formal support is unavailable, unaffordable, or emotionally insufficient.
- Treating belief as last-resort support allows sympathy without converting the episode into a medical or theological proof.
- The source keeps a boundary between respecting practice and endorsing every claim made around it.
