## Definition

Command synchronization failure is the breakdown that occurs when commanders, support units, diplomatic agents, and the political center do not share or correctly update the same operational intent. Orders may remain formally valid after conditions change, a recommendation may be assigned to the wrong author or motive, or parallel commanders may pursue incompatible attack and surrender channels.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 163 shows synchronization failing across diplomacy and war. [[LiShiqi]] persuades [[TianGuangQiKing]] and [[QiState]] to submit, but [[HanXin]] continues under the older attack mandate after [[KuaiChe]] converts the missing stop order into a merit argument. Qi experiences the mismatch as deliberate betrayal and kills the envoy.

Hanji 377-1 adds a deployment variant. [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]] volunteers to lead infantry, [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] assigns [[LuBodeWesternHan|路博德]] as support, and Lu recommends a spring campaign because autumn favors [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] horses. Wudi reads the memorial as evidence that Li Ling is afraid and manipulating the channel. The support relationship disappears before battle because the center has corrupted its model of who wants what.

Hanji 366-2 adds a peer-command and negotiation variant. [[XunZhiWesternHan|荀彘]] and [[YangPuWesternHan|杨仆]] surround [[WangxianCity|王险城]] but fail to align attack dates; Yang Pu pursues surrender while Xun Zhi's own channel is rejected. [[GongsunSuiWesternHan|公孙遂]] resolves the visible split by arresting Yang Pu and merging the armies, but his execution and the generals' later punishments show that forced unity can restore tempo without validating the process that produced it.

Across all three cases, the procedural gap becomes dangerous because merit rivalry, distrust, status, or fear supplies an interpretation before verification does. Synchronization therefore requires more than transmitting an order: the center must confirm changed conditions, preserve the source and purpose of advice, reconcile parallel channels, define support roles, and make the updated plan binding across affected actors.

## Key Claims

- Operational alignment can fail through stale orders, motive misattribution, or incompatible parallel command and negotiation channels.
- Personal merit, status resistance, distrust, and fear can fill an information gap faster than verification.
- Support relationships and surrender channels are part of command intent; leaving them ambiguous changes battlefield and diplomatic risk.
- A forced merger can restore tempo while creating new legitimacy, morale, and accountability problems.
- Reliable synchronization requires source attribution, intent confirmation, role clarity, channel reconciliation, and a binding update across every affected actor.

## Evidence

- Outdated mandate after surrender: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-163-liubang-shouxia-moushi-ruhe-bei-hanxin-neijuan-cansi-lubinu6hoihu59knmlixmaryhnq7|Hanji 163]] says Li Shiqi secures Qi's submission while Han Xin remains authorized to attack and receives no binding stop order.
- Merit incentive inside ambiguity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-163-liubang-shouxia-moushi-ruhe-bei-hanxin-neijuan-cansi-lubinu6hoihu59knmlixmaryhnq7|Hanji 163]] says Kuai Che persuades Han Xin that waiting would let one envoy eclipse his military achievement.
- Misattributed memorial: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-377-1-liling-qingzhan-xiongnu-hanwudi-weihe-buyuan-ta-dang-qianfeng-lttki-nkmyzvt3q1wxomv0-hpmmk|Hanji 377-1]] says Lu Bode recommends waiting for spring, but Wudi concludes that Li Ling induced the request out of fear.
- Support-plan loss: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-377-1-liling-qingzhan-xiongnu-hanwudi-weihe-buyuan-ta-dang-qianfeng-lttki-nkmyzvt3q1wxomv0-hpmmk|Hanji 377-1]] says Wudi replaces the intended support arrangement with separate missions for Lu and Li.
- Parallel-channel conflict: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-366-2-hanwudi-henxin-zhansha-gongsunsui-ls1izjhow_j4ubgmm0lm8ruaurox|Hanji 366-2]] says Xun Zhi and Yang Pu do not meet agreed attack dates while Wiman Joseon distinguishes between their surrender channels.
- Coercive synchronization and review: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-366-2-hanwudi-henxin-zhansha-gongsunsui-ls1izjhow_j4ubgmm0lm8ruaurox|Hanji 366-2]] says Yang Pu's arrest creates unified command and a faster assault, but Gongsun Sui is killed and both generals later face severe judgment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

These sources do not prove that better synchronization alone would have produced success. Qi still faced Han military pressure; Li Ling's defeat also depends on force disparity, terrain, ammunition, betrayal, and Xiongnu action; and Wiman Joseon's collapse depends on elite defection and internal killing as well as command unity. In Hanji 366-2, Yang Pu's caution and separate surrender effort may be operationally intelligible even if missed attack dates are real, while Xun Zhi's accusation is not a neutral audit. Synchronization is therefore a risk condition and governance requirement, not a sufficient causal explanation for every outcome.

## What Changed

- Added the Wiman Joseon campaign as a peer-command case involving incompatible attack dates and surrender channels.
- Distinguished restored operational tempo from legitimate process: arrest and merger synchronize the army but do not settle accountability.
