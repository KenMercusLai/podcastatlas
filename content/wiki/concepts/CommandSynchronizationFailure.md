## Definition

Command synchronization failure is the breakdown that occurs when commanders, support units, diplomatic agents, and the political center do not share or correctly update the same operational intent. Orders may remain formally valid while field conditions change, or a message may be accurate in content but assigned to the wrong motive, producing incompatible action inside one side.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 163 shows synchronization failing across diplomatic and military channels. [[LiShiqi]] persuades [[TianGuangQiKing]] and [[QiState]] to submit, but [[HanXin]] continues under an older attack mandate after [[KuaiChe]] converts the missing stop order into a merit argument. The target experiences the mismatch as deliberate betrayal and kills the envoy.

Hanji 377-1 adds an internal deployment variant. [[LiLingWesternHan|李陵]] volunteers to lead infantry, [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] assigns [[LuBodeWesternHan|路博德]] as support, and Lu recommends a spring campaign because autumn favors [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] horses. Wudi reads the memorial not as Lu's position but as evidence that Li Ling is afraid and manipulating the channel. The result is not an obsolete order but a corrupted model of who wants what: Lu and Li receive separate missions, and the support relationship disappears before battle.

Across both cases, the procedural gap becomes dangerous because status incentives or distrust supply an interpretation before verification does. Synchronization therefore requires more than transmitting an order: the center must confirm changed conditions, preserve the source and purpose of advice, resolve role conflict, and make the updated plan binding across actors.

## Key Claims

- Operational alignment can fail because orders are not updated after conditions change or because the center misattributes who authored a recommendation and why.
- Personal merit, status resistance, and distrust can fill an information gap faster than verification, turning ambiguity into incompatible action.
- Support relationships are part of command intent; changing or dissolving them can alter risk even when the primary force still follows an explicit route order.
- External actors may treat unsynchronized diplomacy and attack as intentional deception, while internal forces may experience misread advice as abandonment or punishment.
- Reliable synchronization requires source attribution, intent confirmation, role clarity, and a binding update across every affected channel.

## Evidence

- Outdated mandate after surrender: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-163-liubang-shouxia-moushi-ruhe-bei-hanxin-neijuan-cansi-lubinu6hoihu59knmlixmaryhnq7|Hanji 163]] says Li Shiqi secures Qi's submission while Han Xin remains authorized to attack and receives no binding stop order.
- Merit incentive inside ambiguity: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-163-liubang-shouxia-moushi-ruhe-bei-hanxin-neijuan-cansi-lubinu6hoihu59knmlixmaryhnq7|Hanji 163]] says Kuai Che persuades Han Xin that waiting would let one envoy eclipse his military achievement.
- Misattributed memorial: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-377-1-liling-qingzhan-xiongnu-hanwudi-weihe-buyuan-ta-dang-qianfeng-lttki-nkmyzvt3q1wxomv0-hpmmk|Hanji 377-1]] says Lu Bode recommends waiting for spring, but Wudi concludes that Li Ling has regretted volunteering and induced Lu to write.
- Support-plan loss: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-377-1-liling-qingzhan-xiongnu-hanwudi-weihe-buyuan-ta-dang-qianfeng-lttki-nkmyzvt3q1wxomv0-hpmmk|Hanji 377-1]] says Wudi replaces the intended support arrangement with separate missions for Lu and Li.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

These sources do not prove that better synchronization alone would have produced military success. In Hanji 163, Han Xin may have retained broad campaign authority and the episode does not supply the full command record. In Hanji 377-1, Lu's seasonal caution may have been militarily sound, but the source also attributes status resistance to him; neither his private motive nor Wudi's complete evidence is independently established. Li Ling's later defeat also depends on force disparity, ammunition, terrain, betrayal, and Xiongnu action, so the missing support arrangement is a risk condition rather than a sufficient single cause.
