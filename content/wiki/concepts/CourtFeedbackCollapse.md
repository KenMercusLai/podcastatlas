# Court Feedback Collapse / 君臣反馈失灵

Court feedback collapse / 君臣反馈失灵 is the governance failure [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl|Zhouji 09]] draws from [[Zisi|子思]]'s criticism of [[WeiState|魏国]] politics. The episode says ministers echo the ruler whether he is right or wrong, so the ruler hears flattery rather than correction.

Zisi sharpens the point by extending it down the hierarchy: if the ruler believes no one can improve his plans and officials also assume they are always right, useful speech disappears at both levels. The state can then fail without noticing why, because the system filters out the very feedback that could correct it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] adds the administrative version through [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]]. [[JimoDafu|即墨大夫]] has a bad court reputation despite good local governance, while [[AyiDafu|阿邑大夫]] is praised by courtiers despite poor performance; the source uses [[IndependentOfficialAudit]] as the corrective mechanism when court talk has been captured.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] adds a remonstrance version of the same failure. Qi Wei Wang neglects government, ministers do not dare criticize him directly, and [[ChunyuKun|淳于髡]] has to use [[AllusiveRemonstrance|隐语进谏]] before the ruler can begin [[QiWeiwangPoliticalTurnaround|turning state action back on]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] adds a weaponized-warning version through [[JiangYi|江乙]]'s attack on [[ZhaoXixu|昭奚恤]]. The dog-at-the-well parable states a real feedback principle: a ruler is endangered if praise can arrive but defect reports are blocked. The episode marks the danger that true-sounding governance critique can still be used as factional maneuver.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-42-gei-mengchangjun-ti-yijian-de-houguo-lr1jp0tpiaboaca3j5-yvyhyn4vf]] adds a positive counter-technique through [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君 / 田文]]. [[GongsunXu|公孙须]] gives correct advice while carrying a private reward motive, and Mengchang Jun still uses the advice. The case becomes [[ContentOverMotiveRemonstrance|content-over-motive remonstrance]]: a court can lose feedback not only through fear and flattery, but also through rejecting every speaker whose motives are mixed.

## Key Claims
- A ruler's self-certainty can silence capable advisers before policy is tested.
- Ministerial flattery is dangerous because it disguises information failure as harmony.
- Feedback collapse spreads when lower officials also become immune to correction.
- Remonstrance has to be made to the ruler, not only voiced privately, if it is to function as governance.
- Reputation signals are dangerous when courtiers can buy, filter, or coordinate them away from ground truth.
- A correct diagnosis of blocked feedback can itself become politically suspect when deployed to remove a rival rather than repair the information system.
- Requiring motive purity from advisers can block useful correction just as effectively as fear or flattery.
