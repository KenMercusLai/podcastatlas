# Court Feedback Collapse / 君臣反馈失灵

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-927-zuo-shengyi-de-zuigao-jingjie-shi-shenme-lngfbjn6xui9k-9lp-ihdqkhpbpr|Hanji 927]] adds a late-Han root-cause warning before the inspection and palace-finance branches. [[FuXie|傅燮]] tells [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] that [[YellowTurbansLateHan|Yellow Turban]] disorder grows from internal court failure, especially eunuch power and unused loyal ministers, rather than from local rebels alone.

The collapse is in the conversion step. The warning reaches court, but [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]] sees it and later turns Fu Xie's Yellow Turban merit into unrewarded exposure. Accurate diagnosis is therefore present, yet the reward and interpretation channel punishes the official who supplied it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-928-zhichang-buke-mingshuo-song-dui-li-banshi-geng-shunli-lgup21lsa-u1ykaiyabcko2vhp0o|Hanji 928]] adds a late-Han field-report version before the Wang Yun and palace-finance branches. [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]]'s military progress against [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]] is strong, but [[ZuoFengLateHan|左丰]] returns from inspection with a report that makes siege preparation look like inaction.

The failure is not that no information reaches [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. Information reaches him through the wrong interpretive channel, and he acts on it by recalling Lu Zhi and appointing [[DongZhuo|董卓]]. This makes [[InspectionGiftGatekeeping|巡查送礼关口]] a compact form of feedback collapse: the ruler receives a report, but the report converts battlefield reality into a politically useful accusation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-931-rensheng-jiannan-chu-bianshi-xiuxin-shi-lpzmvxop8lmlwvxhoojzj5ok9qd6|Hanji 931]] adds a pre-palace-finance late-Han version through [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] and [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]]. Wang Yun reports letters linking Zhang Rang's retainers to the Yellow Turban side, and [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] becomes angry enough to scold Zhang Rang, but personal trust blocks deeper investigation.

This branch shows feedback collapse as inversion rather than simple non-reception. The warning reaches the ruler, yet the protected favorite survives and the reporter becomes the exposed actor through retaliatory imprisonment.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-932-weishenme-ren-yue-hao-yue-nan-dang-daguan-lvwzel782wcxs1lpt3rg2vmkdy29|Hanji 932]] adds a late-Han mixed-breakthrough layer. [[LuKangLateHan|陆康]]'s memorial against [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s added palace-construction burden reaches the court, but eunuch-side interpretation around [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]] and [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]] recodes it as disrespect and turns advice into a prison case.

The same source shows a partial repair through [[SimaZhiLateHan|司马直]]. His death memorial against `修宫` and appointment-fee abuses moves Emperor Ling to stop the palace-construction fee temporarily, but only after the normal feedback channel has already failed enough that correction requires self-destruction.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-934-sha-yiren-ze-tianxia-an-ni-sha-busha-ltar8hvdkqct8oizm3k22z38b1v8|Hanji 934]] adds a late-Han slander-and-partial-breakthrough layer. [[FuXie|傅燮]]'s fierce rebuttal of abandoning [[LiangzhouLateHan|凉州]] reaches [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] and works in the moment, but the same court soon accepts [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]] and [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]]'s accusations against [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]].

This branch shows feedback collapse as uneven rather than total. A ruler may accept one strategic correction while still letting palace favorites control the interpretation of another official's military record, refusal of bribery, and anti-corruption memorial.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-935-ren-huo-yi-kou-qi-nan-cheng-de-shi-ziji-lmy8tyrttv-gjvke7sdi6dx-oopw|Hanji 935]] adds a late-Han remonstrance-to-accusation version through [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]]. Liu Tao's memorial brings useful crisis information to [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] by linking [[ZhangJiaoLateHan|张角]]'s disorder, [[BianZhangLateHan|边章]], [[HanSui|韩遂]], and danger near [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], but the eunuch faction recodes the warning as proof of suspicious knowledge and possible rebel contact.

This branch shows feedback collapse before a total palace coup. Advice reaches the ruler, yet interpretation is captured by [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|宫廷接触垄断]], so the more specific and urgent the warning becomes, the easier it is to turn into evidence against the adviser.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-956-yongren-he-zhizhe-jiujing-chaju-zai-nali-lphee8cj-7gcmg-cynpiphjvauuf|Hanji 956]] supplies the pre-office-loss version of Han Fu's feedback failure. Multiple subordinates tell [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]] that [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]] has enough troops and grain to outlast [[YuanShao|袁绍]], and [[ZhaoFuLateHan|赵浮]] with [[ChengHuanLateHan|程奂]] physically demonstrate military capacity as they pass Yuan Shao's camp. Feedback exists, but Han Fu's fear and moralized self-comparison keep it from becoming policy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-957-ta-ceng-xiongba-yifang-weihe-ruonuo-dao-zai-cesuo-zijin-lnkhah144822mc4aa9bnezklgvoo|Hanji 957]] adds a fragile post-office version through [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]]. After losing [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]], Han Fu no longer has a trusted decision environment: [[ZhuHanLateHan|朱瀚]]'s revenge attack proves that old resentments can now reach his family, and a whispered exchange between [[YuanShao|袁绍]]'s envoy and [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]] becomes enough for Han Fu to conclude that death is imminent.

This is not a classic remonstrance case. Its value for the concept is the endpoint of feedback collapse: when office, personnel trust, and reliable interpretation are gone, a political actor may receive ordinary or ambiguous signals as fatal proof because no advisory channel remains strong enough to test them.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-975-yanzhou-zhiluan-xunyu-ruhe-bang-caocao-shou-dabenying-ln0hh4jrokkwtqsmxmqhdjzcosdr|Hanji 975]] adds a late-Han adviser-warning setup through [[TianFengLateHan|田丰]] and [[YuanShao|袁绍]]. Tian Feng identifies [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]]'s grain request as effective support for [[LyuBu|吕布]] during the [[YanzhouLateHan|兖州]] rebellion, while the host frames Yuan Shao as face-sensitive and hesitant.

This branch is a setup rather than a completed policy failure. Its value for the concept is the leader-side conversion problem: a court may have the person who sees the risk, but still face collapse if the ruler's character and decision process cannot turn warning into timely action.

Court feedback collapse / 君臣反馈失灵 is the governance failure [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl|Zhouji 09]] draws from [[Zisi|子思]]'s criticism of [[WeiState|魏国]] politics. The episode says ministers echo the ruler whether he is right or wrong, so the ruler hears flattery rather than correction.

Zisi sharpens the point by extending it down the hierarchy: if the ruler believes no one can improve his plans and officials also assume they are always right, useful speech disappears at both levels. The state can then fail without noticing why, because the system filters out the very feedback that could correct it.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] adds the administrative version through [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]]. [[JimoDafu|即墨大夫]] has a bad court reputation despite good local governance, while [[AyiDafu|阿邑大夫]] is praised by courtiers despite poor performance; the source uses [[IndependentOfficialAudit]] as the corrective mechanism when court talk has been captured.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] adds a remonstrance version of the same failure. Qi Wei Wang neglects government, ministers do not dare criticize him directly, and [[ChunyuKun|淳于髡]] has to use [[AllusiveRemonstrance|隐语进谏]] before the ruler can begin [[QiWeiwangPoliticalTurnaround|turning state action back on]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] adds a weaponized-warning version through [[JiangYi|江乙]]'s attack on [[ZhaoXixu|昭奚恤]]. The dog-at-the-well parable states a real feedback principle: a ruler is endangered if praise can arrive but defect reports are blocked. The episode marks the danger that true-sounding governance critique can still be used as factional maneuver.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-42-gei-mengchangjun-ti-yijian-de-houguo-lr1jp0tpiaboaca3j5-yvyhyn4vf]] adds a positive counter-technique through [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君 / 田文]]. [[GongsunXu|公孙须]] gives correct advice while carrying a private reward motive, and Mengchang Jun still uses the advice. The case becomes [[ContentOverMotiveRemonstrance|content-over-motive remonstrance]]: a court can lose feedback not only through fear and flattery, but also through rejecting every speaker whose motives are mixed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8]] adds the post-error version. [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] eventually knows that ignoring [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] on [[YanState|燕国]] was a mistake, but the episode stresses that regret without correction still leaves the governance failure in place. In this branch, feedback has technically arrived, but the ruler avoids the remedial action that would make it useful.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-50-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-ba-chuhuaiwang-huyou-que-de-loxg3ylhmskvdm-axtco5y-acege]] adds the ignored-hedge version. [[ChenZhenWarringStates|陈枕]] correctly warns [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] that [[QinState|秦国]] only values Chu because of the [[QiState|齐国]] alliance, and proposes verifying the [[Shangyu|商於]] land transfer before truly severing ties with Qi. Chu Huaiwang rejects both the preventive warning and the later damage-control advice, so the court hears the right correction twice without converting it into policy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac]] adds the court-capture sequel. The episode explains that [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] had already bribed senior Chu insiders, benefited from the exclusion of [[QuYuan|屈原]], and left Chen Zhen's advice isolated. Feedback fails before the formal war decision: by the time Chu Huaiwang reacts to the fake [[Shangyu|商於]] promise, the voices that might connect diplomacy, alliance repair, and military restraint are already weakened.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-53-qinhuiwang-yong-zhangyi-huan-dipi-lvmo2cwbafcg2gnxliuo18fewimk]] adds a negative complement rather than a clean repair. [[ZhengXiu|郑袖]]'s intervention helps prevent [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] from killing Zhang Yi, but the route is not principled open feedback; it is [[CourtBackchannelSurvival|宫廷后门自救]] driven by fear of Qin retaliation and by palace status anxiety around a possible Qin woman. The episode therefore distinguishes useful informal influence from a healthy information system.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-62-ji-er-kuang-ni-jiujiu-de-tui-a-lumch-ibj8m94abjh5nvr3qhiqff]] adds the end-state consequence for Chu Huaiwang. [[ZhaoJu|昭雎]] correctly warns him not to enter [[QinState|秦国]]'s meeting trap at [[Wuguan|武关]], but the warning loses to kinship optimism from [[MiLan|芈兰]] and the ruler's fear of angering Qin. After Qin detains him, the episode says Chu Huaiwang has already demoted or exiled upright remonstrants such as [[QuYuan|屈原]], so the court can preserve succession through [[MiHeng|芈横 / 楚顷襄王]] but cannot produce a serious rescue response.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-65-wo-chuanyue-qu-gei-langman-de-chuwang-nian-shou-shi-lvyd7pve-tfu4ixsqrbdvhm6g64j]] turns the Chu Huaiwang branch into retrospective diagnosis. The episode does not treat him as simply foolish; it emphasizes the court ecology around him, including [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s praise and gifts, [[ZhengXiu|郑袖]]'s palace manipulation, and close ministers who helped filter what the ruler heard. Feedback collapse here is an intimate-information problem as much as a formal remonstrance failure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-69-bikan-gudai-diwang-qipa-sifa-da-pandian-lukvjsktg-pgfjhizvypwmdmw-13]] adds the lethal-remonstrance version through [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]]. After Qi's conquest of [[SongState|宋国]], [[HuYanQiMinister|胡衍]] and [[ChenJuQiMinister|陈举]] warn against further overreach. Qi Min Wang executes them instead of using the warning, so feedback collapse is no longer only filtering, flattery, or ignored advice; the court now learns that correction can be punished with death.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-81-lishi-zaoqi-pua-jishi-lu-1-ljxbn4gznuohegzyee0-tudi9z-y]] adds the post-restoration suspicion version through [[QiXiangWang|齐襄王]]. An unnamed artisan briefly repairs the information environment by showing the king how to absorb [[TianDan|田单]]'s popular kindness as royal virtue, but the nine favored ministers later restore the king's suspicion by presenting [[DiaoBo|刁伯]]'s Chu mission as evidence of Tian Dan's independent power. Feedback collapses here because the ruler hears the interpretation that matches his insecurity.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-81-lishi-zaoqi-pua-jishi-lu-2-lpksu7523ut-skpmxxh9e-tcbz0k]] adds a stronger repair attempt. [[DiaoBo|刁伯]] speaks directly after returning from Chu, naming the king's treatment of [[TianDan|田单]] as state-destroying and forcing a comparison with rulers who honor great helpers. Qi Xiang Wang's execution of the nine favored ministers shows that feedback can break through even a captured court, but the episode still treats the repair as partial because the earlier suspicion has already changed Tian Dan's behavior.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-1-lishi-gan-dangmian-dingzhuang-qinshihuang-diyi-ren-lg0dssyicp387foiraohkv1x5aij]] adds a Qin personal-rule crisis version. After the [[LaoAi|嫪毐]] rebellion, [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] reportedly kills twenty-seven people for speaking on [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]]'s behalf, making correction itself a capital offense. [[MaoJiao|茅焦]]'s [[DeathDefyingRemonstrance|赴死式直谏]] briefly breaks the collapse by accepting death risk and redirecting the ruler toward the public consequence of killing kin and remonstrants.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-2-lishishang-qinshihuang-weihe-yao-fenshu-kengru-lulaivzp1xqs71nbaltr-ygpjihw]] adds the late-imperial Qin version. [[HouShengFangshi|侯生]] and [[LuSheng|卢生]] describe a court where doctoral officials are decorative, ministers cannot decide, prediction specialists risk death for failed forecasts, and everyone says only what [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]] wants to hear. The feedback failure then becomes part of the road to the [[QinKengruIncident|秦代坑儒事件]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-1-buxiang-cuanwei-de-taijian-bushi-ge-hao-jianchen-lh23xj9qxvubj5rlfa9fxmddgbiu]] adds the Hu Hai sequel. [[ZhaoGao|赵高]]'s flattery is the speech that works, while possible dissent from old ministers and princes is preemptively converted into a threat. [[YingJianglu|嬴将闾]]'s request to know his crime also fails as feedback: the envoy does not justify the charge because the order itself has replaced explanation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-2-lishishang-hejiu-neng-wu-duo-da-shi-zhuan-gei-jiugui-laogong-kan-zhe-ji-lvzxpoxclwdrr66bwejd-bga8dp]] adds the minister self-protection version through [[LiSi|李斯]]. When [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]] blames him for rebellion, Li Si could resign or diagnose the regime's destructive incentives. Instead, fear and office attachment lead him to offer a theory that confirms Hu Hai's wish to rule through pressure while enjoying power. Feedback fails because the senior minister gives the ruler a more elegant version of what the ruler already wants to hear.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-4-ruhe-dinglun-qinxiang-lisi-de-xiachang-ljnz0vflgr62hyktrg5cigz7c-7x]] adds the access-monopoly version. [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] persuades Hu Hai to stop appearing before senior officials, producing [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|宫廷接触垄断]]. Once access is filtered, [[LiSi|李斯]]'s remonstrance can be timed to feel like intrusion, his warning against Zhao Gao can be handed to Zhao Gao himself, and the joint advice of [[FengQuji|冯去疾]], Li Si, and [[FengJieQin|冯劫 / 冯杰]] can be reframed as proof that ministers have failed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-131-1-qinxiang-lisi-yisheng-jingming-que-zao-yaozhan-liupvalcghtas2ibfyjrtdz6ctg7]] adds the post-arrest version. Li Si still tries to send corrective information to [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]] by memorializing his service and denying rebellion, but [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] intercepts the document. The later imperial review also fails because fake reviews and torture have already trained Li Si to repeat a false confession. Feedback therefore collapses even when the ruler thinks he has checked the matter.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-138-wudu-qiannian-de-lishi-zhenxiang-zhaogao-bushi-taijian-2-lvtievjrclb1lxxxxr6k-q7kryto|Qinji 138 part 2]] gives the abandonment endpoint. At [[WangyiPalace|望夷宫]], Hu Hai asks why the remaining eunuch did not warn him earlier; the answer is that silence preserved the eunuch's life. In this branch, feedback collapse has become a survival norm: the ruler's past punishments teach attendants not to report reality, and when the coup arrives there is no advisory system left to defend him.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1007-cong-shentong-dao-fuwang-kongrong-zhenshi-mianmu-puguang-lkiljj4yvl5fhslrc9gcxudxxklt|Hanji 1007]] adds a late-Han local-command version through [[KongRong|孔融]] in [[BeihaiCommandery|北海郡]]. [[ZuoChengzu|左承祖]] gives a realistic correction by advising dependence on a stronger power, but Kong Rong kills him instead of using the warning. [[LiuYixun|刘义逊]] then leaves, so feedback collapse appears both as punished advice and as the loss of other people who now understand that correction is unsafe.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1018-ta-weihe-rang-caocao-jidan-sunce-weiju-liubei-wanxi-lqccdhrjmero-gt7vsaq2ejuhhw8|Hanji 1018]] adds a warlord-command version through [[LyuBu|吕布]] and [[GaoShun|高顺]]. Gao Shun gives practical warnings and remains loyal even after Lü Bu transfers his troops to [[WeiXuLateHan|魏续]], but Lü Bu still ignores the warning not to demand supplies from [[ZhangBaLateHan|张霸]] in person. Feedback has not disappeared from the organization; it fails because the leader cannot convert trusted correction into action.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-998-baima-jiangjun-gongsunzan-cong-sanqian-baima-dao-kunshou-gucheng-lp-1l2gvbsbfnyznutdghxwwvqna|Hanji 998]] adds a physical-isolation version through [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]]'s [[YijingFortress|易京]]. After retreating behind iron gates, he blocks ordinary access by guests, generals, advisers, and warriors; documents are hoisted by rope and orders are shouted through intermediaries. Feedback collapse here is architectural as well as psychological: the defensive system makes warning and command harder to move.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 928 adds that feedback can collapse through inspection translation: a report reaches the ruler but recodes front-line progress as culpable delay.
- Hanji 931 adds that a valid warning can invert into danger for the reporter when the ruler's trusted favorite controls the retaliation field.
- Hanji 932 adds that fiscal remonstrance can be recoded as personal disrespect, while an extreme death memorial may produce only temporary correction.
- Hanji 934 adds that feedback failure can be uneven: a ruler may accept one strategic warning while still letting favorites destroy a commander's anti-corruption report through slander.
- Hanji 935 adds that feedback can fail even when an urgent warning reaches the ruler, if palace intermediaries control the interpretation and turn knowledge into incriminating evidence.
- Hanji 956 adds that feedback can fail before office is lost: accurate resource and threat assessments may be present, but fear and self-abasing moral language can prevent them from becoming action.
- Hanji 957 adds a post-office endpoint: once trusted advisory channels and political protection collapse, ambiguous signals can be interpreted as fatal certainty.
- Hanji 975 adds a late-Han warning-conversion setup: an adviser may read the risk, but the ruler still has to overcome face, hesitation, and relationship pressure for feedback to become action.
- A ruler's self-certainty can silence capable advisers before policy is tested.
- Ministerial flattery is dangerous because it disguises information failure as harmony.
- Feedback collapse spreads when lower officials also become immune to correction.
- Remonstrance has to be made to the ruler, not only voiced privately, if it is to function as governance.
- Reputation signals are dangerous when courtiers can buy, filter, or coordinate them away from ground truth.
- A correct diagnosis of blocked feedback can itself become politically suspect when deployed to remove a rival rather than repair the information system.
- Requiring motive purity from advisers can block useful correction just as effectively as fear or flattery.
- Feedback also collapses after the fact when a leader admits error privately but refuses visible repair.
- A ruler can also collapse feedback by hearing a correct risk model but insisting on immediate gain, then rejecting containment advice after the loss is exposed.
- Feedback collapse can be prepared by hostile diplomacy before the formal decision point; advisers may be excluded before their warning is needed.
- Informal palace influence can sometimes prevent a bad decision, but it is not the same as durable feedback because it may work through private fear rather than shared correction.
- Feedback collapse can become abandonment: a ruler who has pushed away loyal correction may discover in crisis that the court can continue the state without saving him.
- Feedback collapse can also be created by validation hunger and intimate access: praise, favorites, and close ministers may shape perception before any formal policy debate begins.
- Feedback collapse becomes more dangerous when warning is not merely ignored but criminalized or killed, because later advisers learn that survival requires silence.
- A partial correction can fail if favorites later supply a more emotionally satisfying suspicion frame.
- Direct remonstrance can repair a captured information environment for a moment, but the target of earlier suspicion may continue acting as though the old danger remains.
- When a ruler kills remonstrants, later feedback may require extraordinary death-facing courage, which is a sign of institutional failure rather than a sustainable advice system.
- In Qinji 125-2, feedback collapse is tied to administrative overload and prediction risk: the ruler handles everything personally, while others survive by withholding unwelcome information.
- In Qinji 128-1, feedback collapse is tied to purge authority: the court no longer needs to answer a condemned prince's request for a charge.
- In Qinji 129-2, feedback collapse is tied to self-protective expertise: a frightened minister supplies theory that flatters the ruler's worst incentives.
- In Qinji 130-4, feedback collapse is tied to access capture: a court intermediary controls when and how advice reaches the ruler, so true warnings become emotionally or politically incriminating.
- In Qinji 131-1, feedback collapse reaches legal review: a memorial is intercepted, a confession is manufactured, and the ruler's check becomes confirmation of the intermediary's story.
- Hanji 1007 adds that killing one realistic adviser can collapse the wider advice environment by teaching other capable people to exit.
- Hanji 1018 adds that feedback can fail even when a loyal, competent adviser is visibly available, if the leader's pride and short-term appetite override correction.
- Hanji 1025 adds that feedback can fail by trust conversion: a leader may still have warning and loyal capacity, but suspicion toward ordinary officers turns them into a capture-and-surrender path.
- Hanji 998 adds that feedback can be collapsed by physical isolation, not only by flattery, punishment, or ignored advice.
