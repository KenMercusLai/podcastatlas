## Definition

Imperial death concealment / 秘不发丧式过渡控制 is the transition mechanism where a court hides or delays public notice that a ruler has died so succession, travel, security, or public order can be managed before the death becomes common knowledge.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg|Qinji 127-2]] gives the core danger case through [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s death on tour. [[LiSi|李斯]] fears immediate announcement away from [[Xianyang|咸阳]] could trigger princely disorder or popular unrest, so the traveling court keeps routine appearances: food continues to be sent in, officials still submit business, and the dead ruler remains associated with the travel vehicle. That secrecy creates room for [[ZhaoGao|赵高]], Li Si, and [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]] to control letters, seals, and commands before the public accession.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw|Qinji 127-3]] qualifies the same mechanism by preserving two branches. The received narrative keeps the hidden death, heat, odor concealment, fish, return route, and delayed announcement. The [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]] branch separates concealment from a forged post-death succession by suggesting that Hu Hai may have been selected in a deathbed consultation before Qin Shi Huang died. Under that version, concealment still controls timing and public announcement, but it is not identical to forged succession.

The direct Beixiang Hou case appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]]. After [[LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan|北乡侯刘裔]] dies, [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|阎太后]] conceals the death, summons royal descendants to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], closes palace gates, stations troops, and tries to finish a new selection before [[SunChengLateHan|孙程]]'s restoration coalition acts. This branch shows concealment becoming active succession control inside the capital itself, not only a travel-delay tactic.

A later late-Han contrast case appears after [[EmperorChongOfHan|汉冲帝]] dies. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]], [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠 / 梁太后]] considers delaying the announcement until summoned royal candidates reach Luoyang. [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] rejects the plan by invoking the Qin Shi Huang and Beixiang Hou precedents, and Liang Na announces the death that night. This shows death concealment as a proposal that can still be defeated by ritual, historical memory, and ministerial feedback before it becomes the governing mechanism.

## Key Claims

- Concealing a ruler's death can be framed as temporary stability management before it becomes succession manipulation.
- The method depends on routine performance: meals, reports, vehicles, attendants, documents, and silence preserve normality.
- A narrow information circle can stabilize an emergency while excluding possible veto actors.
- The most dangerous form appears when the same actors who hide the death can still issue commands as if the ruler personally authorized them.
- Source variants matter: death concealment can coexist with forged post-death succession, pre-death emergency heir choice, capital-side selection control, or an attempted delay that is rejected.
- Hanji 825 shows that a functioning remonstrance channel can stop concealment at the proposal stage.

## Evidence

- Qin routine performance: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg|Qinji 127-2]] says food, reports, travel, and limited disclosure preserve the appearance that Qin Shi Huang is alive during the return.
- Qin command-control danger: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg|Qinji 127-2]] links concealed death to control over letters and seals, forged succession commands, Fusu's suicide, and Hu Hai's accession.
- Variant branch: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw|Qinji 127-3]] preserves both the received concealed-death sequence and the Zhao Zheng Shu version in which Hu Hai may be selected before death.
- Timing versus mechanism distinction: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw|Qinji 127-3]] makes public-announcement delay analytically separate from whether the heir was chosen by forgery or emergency consultation.
- Beixiang Hou direct case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]] says Yan Taihou conceals Liu Yi's death, summons royal descendants, closes gates, stations troops, and raises security to preserve control over the next ruler choice.
- Capital-side failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]] shows concealment defeated by Sun Cheng's quicker palace coup, Li Run's coerced alignment, Feng Shi's noncommitment, and Guo Zhen's enforcement against Yan Jin.
- Late-Han proposal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] says Liang Na considers delaying Emperor Chong's death announcement until royal candidates arrive because Xu-Yang disorder makes the moment unstable.
- Late-Han refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] says Li Gu rejects the delay through historical precedent and Liang Na announces the death that night.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not treat every delayed announcement as a completed coup. Qinji 127-3 explicitly separates announcement timing from the heir-selection mechanism, Hanji 803 shows a completed concealment attempt that is defeated by a rival palace coup, and Hanji 825 shows a proposed delay that is blocked. The current evidence also remains source-note bounded: it covers Qin Shi Huang's tour crisis, Beixiang Hou's capital succession crisis, and Emperor Chong's late-Han succession context, not a comprehensive typology of imperial funerary secrecy.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 803 as the direct Beixiang Hou case where Yan Taihou conceals death to control succession inside Luoyang.
- Preserved Hanji 825 as a later late-Han contrast where Li Gu prevents concealment after Emperor Chong's death by invoking that precedent.
- Refined the concept to distinguish public-announcement timing from forged succession, emergency heir selection, capital-side selection control, and rejected delay.
