## Definition

Imperial death concealment / 秘不发丧式过渡控制 is the transition mechanism where a court hides or delays public notice that a ruler has died so travel, security, succession selection, or public order can be managed before the death becomes common knowledge.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg|Qinji 127-2]] gives the core danger case through [[QinShiHuang|Qin Shi Huang]]'s death on tour. [[LiSi|Li Si]] fears immediate announcement away from [[Xianyang|Xianyang]] could trigger princely disorder or popular unrest, so the traveling court keeps routine appearances: food continues to be sent in, officials still submit business, and the dead ruler remains associated with the travel vehicle. That secrecy creates room for [[ZhaoGao|Zhao Gao]], Li Si, and [[QinErshi|Hu Hai]] to control letters, seals, and commands before the public accession.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw|Qinji 127-3]] qualifies the same mechanism by preserving two branches. The received narrative keeps the hidden death, heat, odor concealment, fish, return route, and delayed announcement. The [[ZhaoZhengShu|Zhao Zheng Shu]] branch separates concealment from a forged post-death succession by suggesting that Hu Hai may have been selected in a deathbed consultation before Qin Shi Huang died. Under that version, concealment still controls timing and public announcement, but it is not identical to forged succession.

The Eastern-Han tour-death branch appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]]. After [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] dies near Ye County, [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|Yan Hou]], [[YanXianLateHan|Yan Xian]], and [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|Fan Feng]] hide the death, maintain the appearance of meals, medicine, and imperial rest, travel roughly six hundred li back to [[LuoyangLateHan|Luoyang]], and only then announce the death. The aim is not merely orderly travel: it prevents officials from rallying around [[EmperorShunOfHan|Liu Bao]] before the Yan side can install [[LiuYiBeixiangHouLateHan|Beixiang Hou]].

The direct Beixiang Hou death case appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]]. After Beixiang Hou dies, Yan Taihou conceals the death, summons royal descendants to Luoyang, closes palace gates, stations troops, and tries to finish a new selection before [[SunChengLateHan|Sun Cheng]]'s restoration coalition acts. This branch shows concealment becoming active succession control inside the capital itself, not only a travel-delay tactic.

A later late-Han contrast case appears after [[EmperorChongOfHan|Emperor Chong]] dies. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]], [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|Liang Na / Liang Taihou]] considers delaying the announcement until summoned royal candidates reach Luoyang. [[LiGuLateHan|Li Gu]] rejects the plan by invoking the Qin Shi Huang and Beixiang Hou precedents, and Liang Na announces the death that night. This shows death concealment as a proposal that can still be defeated by ritual, historical memory, and ministerial feedback before it becomes the governing mechanism.

## Key Claims

- Concealing a ruler's death can be framed as temporary stability management before it becomes succession manipulation.
- The method depends on routine performance: meals, reports, medicine, vehicles, attendants, documents, and silence preserve normality.
- A narrow information circle can stabilize an emergency while excluding possible veto actors.
- The most dangerous form appears when the same actors who hide the death can still issue commands, control gates, or choose successors as if the ruler's authority remains available.
- Source variants matter: death concealment can coexist with forged post-death succession, pre-death emergency heir choice, travel-return control, capital-side selection control, or an attempted delay that is rejected.
- Hanji 825 shows that a functioning remonstrance channel can stop concealment at the proposal stage.

## Evidence

- Qin routine performance: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg|Qinji 127-2]] says food, reports, travel, and limited disclosure preserve the appearance that Qin Shi Huang is alive during the return.
- Qin command-control danger: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg|Qinji 127-2]] links concealed death to control over letters and seals, forged succession commands, Fusu's suicide, and Hu Hai's accession.
- Variant branch: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw|Qinji 127-3]] preserves both the received concealed-death sequence and the Zhao Zheng Shu version in which Hu Hai may be selected before death.
- Eastern-Han tour return: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] says the Yan side hides Emperor An's death through food, medicine, and rest routines while returning to Luoyang.
- Eastern-Han succession control: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-802-andi-zhisi-donghan-wangchao-quanli-da-xipai-lgvne92e0jjzjnznzfkz7nvztnbj|Hanji 802]] says the concealed return lets Yan Taihou and Yan Xian announce the death from the palace, take dowager and military authority, and install Beixiang Hou before Liu Bao can be backed.
- Beixiang Hou direct case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-803-han-shundi-ruhe-zai-xuefengxueyu-zhong-dengshang-huangwei-lqvno7t-gcgxtbs3phuflr4ybe2s|Hanji 803]] says Yan Taihou conceals Liu Yi's death, summons royal descendants, closes gates, stations troops, and raises security to preserve control over the next ruler choice.
- Late-Han proposal and refusal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-825-lishi-gaosu-ni-dang-ni-meiyong-shi-renxing-zui-zisi-luv-8z9axuejnfhlkgsuxee3uked|Hanji 825]] says Liang Na considers delaying Emperor Chong's death announcement, but Li Gu rejects the delay through historical precedent and Liang Na announces the death that night.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not treat every delayed announcement as a completed coup. Qinji 127-3 explicitly separates announcement timing from the heir-selection mechanism, Hanji 802 shows a concealed return that succeeds long enough to install Beixiang Hou, Hanji 803 shows a completed concealment attempt defeated by a rival palace coup, and Hanji 825 shows a proposed delay that is blocked. The current evidence covers Qin Shi Huang's tour crisis, Emperor An's tour death, Beixiang Hou's capital succession crisis, and Emperor Chong's late-Han succession context, not a comprehensive typology of imperial funerary secrecy.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 802 as an Eastern-Han tour-death concealment case before Beixiang Hou's accession.
- Reframed the late-Han evidence as two Yan-side concealments: Emperor An's hidden return and Beixiang Hou's later hidden death.
- Preserved Hanji 803 as the capital-side Beixiang Hou case and Hanji 825 as the later rejected-delay contrast.
- Refined the concept to distinguish public-announcement timing from forged succession, emergency heir selection, travel-return control, capital-side selection control, and rejected delay.
