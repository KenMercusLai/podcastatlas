## Definition
Female religious life space is the limited room that religious roles, vows, rooms, and institutions can create for women inside a gender order that otherwise channels them toward marriage and family hierarchy.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's case is the Catholic 神女 or 守贞女 role. It could give some women a religious reason not to marry and a recognized form of service, but it was not simple freedom. The source stresses church hierarchy, moral discipline, family permission, room requirements, and economic cost. The concept therefore holds both sides: religious space can loosen one constraint while installing another.

## Key Claims
- Religious life can give women a legitimate reason to avoid or delay marriage.
- That space remains bounded by church hierarchy, family authority, rules of chastity, and moral supervision.
- Material thresholds matter because some systems require fees, rooms, or family resources.
- Female religious roles can support literacy and expression while also channeling that expression into approved language.
- The strongest reading keeps agency and discipline visible together.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is source-scoped to Chinese Catholic history in this episode. It should not be generalized to all religions, all women, or all forms of celibate religious life without additional sources.
