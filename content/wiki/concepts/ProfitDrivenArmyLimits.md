# Profit-Driven Army Limits / 功利型军队局限

Profit-driven army limits / 功利型军队局限 is the source's Xunzian critique of armies held together by recruitment, selection, rewards, punishments, and personal gain. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-3-xunzi-bingfa-li-cangzhe-renxing-de-mimi-lgfcp4hkruyhhrjguqhjbhllvwp43]] does not deny that such mechanisms work. It explicitly treats [[QinState|秦国]] as a powerful state whose harsh control, surveillance, reward, punishment, and upward channels produce real battlefield strength.

The limit is loyalty and stability. In the episode's rendering of [[Xunzi|荀子]], soldiers who fight mainly for gain are closer to hired labor or business partners than to a community ready to live and die for a trusted ruler. Xunzi labels this lower form "盗兵": it can expand and contract, win and lose, but it does not create the self-restraint, shared value, or trustworthy coordination of [[XunzianMilitaryLegitimacy|荀子仁义之师]].

The host extends the point to modern organizations by contrasting incentive-only groups with teams shaped by shared culture. The wiki keeps that as a source-scoped analogy: it is useful for understanding the episode's applied reading, but it is not a direct ancient-source claim.

## Key Claims
- Material incentives can produce high effort and battlefield capacity.
- Incentive alignment is not the same as legitimacy, trust, or durable loyalty.
- A state can be strong by reward and punishment while still falling below Xunzi's highest military standard.
- The "盗兵" label criticizes the basis of cohesion more than any single tactic.
- The modern team-culture analogy is interpretive and should not be back-projected as historical evidence.
