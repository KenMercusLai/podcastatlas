# China Divergent Technology Route

China divergent technology route is the source's fourth 2025 guess in [[vol-111-guanyu-2025-nian-de-si-ge-caixiang-lgaira5qw1fhic4qwihrimed6q9g]]. The host argues that Chinese firms may move further down technology paths that differ from overseas incumbents, sometimes because sanctions force substitution and sometimes because industry transitions let latecomers bypass old bottlenecks.

The concept has three source examples. In semiconductors, China is pushed toward domestic architecture, design, process, packaging, equipment, and materials alternatives inside the [[SemiconductorSupplyChain]], with [[NAURA|北方华创]] used as an equipment example. In vehicles, electrification and plug-in hybrids weaken some old combustion-engine and automatic-transmission advantages, creating [[LeadingIncumbentDisadvantage]]. In AI, [[DeepSeek]] is mentioned as an early signal that Chinese model work may also develop different route assumptions.

## Key Claims
- A divergent route can be more expensive and lower performing at first while still being strategically valuable if it avoids external chokepoints.
- The source treats semiconductor investment logic as partly driven by national will and industrial security, not only by near-term shareholder returns.
- In EVs, route divergence can be active rather than purely defensive because technical change alters which historical capabilities matter.
- The source's tone is ambivalent: route independence shows vitality, but it may also deepen bloc formation and technical-system separation.
