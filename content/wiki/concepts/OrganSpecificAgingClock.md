# Organ-Specific Aging Clock

## Definition
An organ-specific aging clock estimates whether the molecular or physiological pattern associated with a particular organ or cell type appears older or younger than expected for a person's chronological age.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded sources describe two uses of organ-specific clocks. A sleep study applies clocks across 23 body parts to find that sleep-duration associations differ by system, making a population-level U-shaped relationship more granular. A proteomic program assigns circulating proteins to organs, estimates an organ age gap, and reports that an older-appearing heart, kidney, or brain predicts later disease risk in the corresponding organ.

The combined value is localization: one whole-body biological-age number can hide divergent trajectories across organs and cell types. The combined limit is that an age-like score remains a model output. Association with later disease can support risk stratification, but it does not diagnose disease, prove what caused the score, identify the best intervention, or establish that lowering the score improves healthspan.

## Key Claims
- Organs and cell types can show different age-like patterns within the same person.
- Organ-level clocks can reveal heterogeneous associations that a whole-body score may obscure.
- A reported organ age gap may stratify future disease risk in the corresponding organ.
- Risk prediction does not by itself establish causation, diagnosis, treatment selection, or clinical benefit.
- Repeated measurement is potentially useful only when score reliability and outcome relevance are validated.

## Evidence
- Sleep heterogeneity: [[the-case-of-the-missing-totem-aung-san-suu-kyi-6a560fc4f96471dde4821d2f]] reports U-shaped sleep-duration patterns in nine of 23 organ or body-system clocks using [[UKBiobank]] data.
- Proteomic localization: [[restore-youthfulness-vitality-to-the-aging-brain-body-dr-tony-wyss-coray-scim5931542341]] describes assigning blood proteins to the brain, liver, lung, heart, kidney, and other organs.
- Prospective association: [[restore-youthfulness-vitality-to-the-aging-brain-body-dr-tony-wyss-coray-scim5931542341]] reports that organ age gaps predict later organ-matched disease risk.
- Proposed translation: [[restore-youthfulness-vitality-to-the-aging-brain-body-dr-tony-wyss-coray-scim5931542341]] describes [[VeroBiosciences]] using organ-age, clinical, and wearable signals in a measure-intervene-retest loop.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide complete model specifications, cohort composition, calibration, discrimination, subgroup performance, error bounds, intervention thresholds, or independent clinical validation. Sleep associations may reflect illness or other confounding rather than a causal effect of duration. Protein assignment to an organ can be imperfect because tissues share proteins and disease can alter production or clearance. A commercial or research clock should not replace symptoms, examination, established testing, or clinician judgment.
