# Organ-Specific Aging Clock

Organ-specific aging clock is the method described in [[the-case-of-the-missing-totem-aung-san-suu-kyi-6a560fc4f96471dde4821d2f]] for estimating whether a body part or system appears biologically older or younger than a person's chronological age. The episode names [[JunhaoWen]] as a lead author using these clocks to study sleep duration with [[UKBiobank]] data.

The concept matters because it makes sleep-health evidence more granular. Instead of asking only whether people who sleep more or less die sooner, the study looks at aging signals across 23 body parts and finds U-shaped sleep-duration patterns in nine of them. The source still keeps the method inside [[PopulationLevelHealthAdvice]] because causality is difficult.

## Key Claims
- Different organs or body systems may show different relationships to sleep duration.
- A biological-clock signal can suggest risk but does not automatically prove sleep caused the difference.
- The method can make population guidance more detailed without making individual prescriptions exact.
- Organ-level evidence reinforces the need to treat sleep as duration, quality, regularity, continuity, and context together.
