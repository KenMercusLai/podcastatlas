# Encircling Wei to Rescue Zhao / 围魏救赵

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-15-jiu-bu-chun-yinfa-de-shijie-dazhan-li-iojxthq8komhlhyumho3yvtem]] supplies the setup before the strategy is executed. [[WeiState|魏国]] surrounds [[Handan|邯郸]] after [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] reads [[ChuState|楚国]]'s attack on [[LuState|鲁国]] as a distraction. The episode names [[Jingshe|景舍]] as the Chu rescue commander and previews the later arrival of [[TianJi|田忌]] and [[SunBin|孙膑]].

Encircling Wei to rescue Zhao / 围魏救赵 is the strategy pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] draws from [[SunBin|孙膑]]'s advice to [[TianJi|田忌]]. [[WeiState|魏国]] is attacking [[ZhaoState|赵国]] at [[Handan|邯郸]], but Sun Bin rejects a direct rescue march and tells [[QiState|齐国]] to attack [[Daliang|大梁]] instead.

The concept's core is pressure displacement. A rescuer can change the war by threatening what the aggressor must protect, forcing the enemy to abandon its chosen battlefield and move into a prepared response. In the source, that move leads to [[GuilingBattle|桂陵之战]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-961-neihuang-zhizhan-caocao-chulu-fengmang-ljdlt6kiu8tg0qws-jlyej-yhgxr|Hanji 961]] adds a late-Han application through [[CaoCao|曹操]] and [[NeihuangBattleLateHan|内黄之战]]. When [[YuDuLateHan|于毒]] attacks [[DongWuyangLateHan|东武阳]], Cao Cao does not immediately march to the city. He threatens Yu Du's own base, forcing Yu Du to return and lifting the siege before Cao Cao advances to fight [[SuiGuLateHan|眭固]] and [[YufuluoChanyu|于扶罗]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-19-maling-zhi-zhan-lhbapt09x2ptdqx0uopkp8apbada]] extends the same logic into the [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]] setup. [[QiState|齐国]] rescues [[HanState|韩国]] by again moving toward [[Daliang|大梁]], which forces [[PangJuan|庞涓]] to leave the Han front. The new source adds [[DelayedRescueStatecraft|timing]] before the target shift and [[ForcedMarchOperationalRisk|forced-march risk]] after it.

## Key Claims
- The relevant target is not always the place where the ally is suffering.
- The setup matters: the strategy begins from Wei's siege of Handan, which the prior source frames as an opportunistic move after Chu is distracted.
- Attacking the enemy's must-save point can turn passive rescue into active control of tempo.
- The strategy depends on reading what the enemy cannot politically or militarily ignore.
- The source pairs the pattern with [[TianJiHorseRacing|田忌赛马]]: both accept local asymmetry in order to win the larger contest.
- Hanji 961 shows the pattern can operate at a smaller late-Han operational scale: threatening Yu Du's base relieves Dong Wuyang without first fighting at the city.
- The Maling setup shows the pattern can repeat with a different endangered ally while keeping the same must-save pressure point.
