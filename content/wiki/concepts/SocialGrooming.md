# Social Grooming / 社会性梳毛

Social grooming / 社会性梳毛 is the episode's frame for grooming as relationship work rather than only cleaning. In [[83-shumao-bagua-ji-yuyan-de-jinhua-renji-guanxi-dengbashu-zan-bu-fu-bu-xing-698132861]], the host explains that primates spend far more time grooming than hygiene alone would require; grooming relaxes the receiver, creates trust, and makes alliance investment visible.

The concept matters because grooming is costly in time. A participant can groom only a limited number of partners directly, so the practice can become a signal of preference, commitment, and mutual protection. [[LanguageAsSocialGrooming]] is the later human scaling move: talk can perform some of the same bonding function while including more people at once.

## Key Claims
- Grooming can signal trust because the receiver lowers vigilance and lets another individual close.
- Time spent grooming one partner cannot be spent on another, so the action can mark alliance choice.
- Social grooming connects emotion, bodily comfort, and coalition maintenance rather than separating care from strategy.
- It explains why apparently low-information interaction can still be socially important.
- The concept should be used carefully: adaptive plausibility is weaker than direct evidence about a given person or setting.
