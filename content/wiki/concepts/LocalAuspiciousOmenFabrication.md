## Definition

Local Auspicious Omen Fabrication / 地方祥瑞造作 is the pattern in which local officials or local reporting channels elevate rumor, joke, natural anomaly, or exaggeration into an auspicious-omen report for upward political value.

## Current Synthesis

Local auspicious omen fabrication now covers two incentive paths. One path starts below: a commandery-level joke, rumor, or natural anomaly can be upgraded into a report because auspicious signs flatter authority and may bring reward. Another path starts above: a superior's visible taste for auspicious or moralized indicators can make local officials infer what kinds of examples they should supply, even without an explicit order to fabricate.

The current synthesis is therefore not only "local officials lie upward." It is a reporting-field problem. A muddy pond can become a yellow-dragon possibility; an ordinary heque bird can become a divine-bird embarrassment; and exemplary indicators such as yielding fields, separate roads, lost objects left untouched, filial sons, or chaste women can become performable signs of order. Internal skepticism matters because clerks, officials, or memorial writers can interrupt the channel before the sign hardens into accepted political evidence.

The concept is a local-government complement to the broader [[AuspiciousOmenPolitics|祥瑞政治]] page. Instead of focusing only on court interpretation after an omen reaches the center, it highlights the fabrication or amplification channel: an ambiguous event, ordinary animal, joke, or superior's preference becomes politically useful because auspicious reporting can flatter authority, signal virtue, or protect reputation.

## Key Claims

- Local omen reports can originate from ambiguous, trivial, or joking observations rather than clear extraordinary events.
- The upward reporting channel creates incentives to convert ambiguity into auspicious language.
- Superior preferences can produce fabrication pressure even without explicit orders; subordinates infer what kind of examples will be rewarded.
- Local skepticism can exist inside the bureaucracy, so omen fabrication is not always uncontested.
- Fabricated or exaggerated auspicious signs can mask governance stress when paired with disasters, floods, and frontier unrest.

## Evidence

- Muddy-water origin: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-878-bu-shou-yueshu-de-quanli-hairen-buqian-lvevnahcxshgbz5zyqbaf36pa7nk|Hanji 878]] says the Ba Commandery yellow-dragon report begins from a joke about a pond's yellow muddy water.
- Reporting incentive: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-878-bu-shou-yueshu-de-quanli-hairen-buqian-lvevnahcxshgbz5zyqbaf36pa7nk|Hanji 878]] treats the case as a warning that local officials may report auspicious signs upward for advantage.
- Internal objection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-878-bu-shou-yueshu-de-quanli-hairen-buqian-lvevnahcxshgbz5zyqbaf36pa7nk|Hanji 878]] says a local clerk objects to reporting the supposed yellow dragon.
- Ordinary-bird origin: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-454-yi-zhi-niao-ganfan-yige-chengxiang-huangba-you-duo-can-lrjgft-s8oi3yt3lrnsh0_m20sxj|Hanji 454]] says Huang Ba mistakes Zhang Chang's household heque bird for a divine bird and considers reporting it as auspicious proof.
- Implied-preference pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-454-yi-zhi-niao-ganfan-yige-chengxiang-huangba-you-duo-can-lrjgft-s8oi3yt3lrnsh0_m20sxj|Hanji 454]] says Zhang Chang warns that Huang Ba's questions lead local officials to package exemplary moral achievements.
- Law-versus-display correction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-454-yi-zhi-niao-ganfan-yige-chengxiang-huangba-you-duo-can-lrjgft-s8oi3yt3lrnsh0_m20sxj|Hanji 454]] says Emperor Xuan accepts Zhang Chang's warning and instructs local officials through attendants.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is still source-limited and should not be treated as proof that every auspicious report is fabricated. Hanji 878 does not prove that the central court accepted the Ba Commandery report, and the local objection shows that omen fabrication could be resisted within ordinary administration. Hanji 454 adds a court-level inference problem rather than a completed local forgery: the danger is that local officials infer and perform the superior's preferred signs.
