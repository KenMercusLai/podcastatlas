## Definition

Hong Kong jiangshi cinema is a screen tradition that combines reanimated Chinese corpses and ritual specialists with martial action, comedy, horror, romance, and highly legible supernatural rules.

## Current Synthesis

In the bounded source, [[MrVampire1985|《僵尸先生》]] consolidates the genre by making mixed tones mutually supporting. A competent ritual master supplies authority, comic apprentices create error and improvisation, and props or bodily constraints turn supernatural danger into visible action. The resulting grammar is portable: later works can reuse the master figure, Qing-style corpse image, breath-holding suspense, rice, talismans, and rule discovery while changing the surrounding balance of comedy, romance, horror, or modernity.

The genre is therefore not a transparent survival of one folk tradition. It depends on [[CinematicFolkloreRecombination|selecting, recombining, and inventing]] motifs that work on screen. The episode's commercial history suggests that one successful film made this mixture reproducible across imitations, sequels, and television, although the precise box office, credit, and influence chain requires independent production evidence.

## Key Claims

- Genre coherence comes from repeatable scene rules rather than strict tonal purity.
- The master-and-apprentices structure joins authority, exposition, comedy, and plot-generating error.
- Ritual objects become action props when their functions are visually clear and testable.
- Breath-holding is a particularly portable device because viewers can bodily simulate the characters' constraint.
- Qing-style costume and other conventions may be products of cinematic repetition rather than universal folk rules.
- Commercial success can turn a locally assembled rule set into a wider genre template.

### Historical and commercial diffusion

- [[075-zhongyuanjie-teji-quhua-jiangshipian-zhenzheng-kaishan-zhizuo-linzhengying-jiangshixiansheng-lpcd2firlixh1qr-tnfkp0np0-jq]] reports a risky production, Taiwan-first release, strong Hong Kong box office, a subsequent film cycle, and later television renewal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One retrospective episode cannot establish where every convention originated or how directly later works copied this film. “Foundational” is a genre-history judgment, not proof that no earlier jiangshi film mattered. Folk practice, zhiguai literature, opera, action cinema, production improvisation, and later audience memory may contribute in different proportions.

- Separated reusable screen grammar from claims of authentic ritual continuity.
