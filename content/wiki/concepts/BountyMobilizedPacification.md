## Definition
Bounty-mobilized pacification is the pattern where the state uses public rewards, titles, and money to mobilize local actors alongside appointed commanders. It is not mere mercenary outsourcing: the reward works when credible command, local knowledge, and visible merit conversion align.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 819 and Hanji 826 now show two reward-backed pacification forms. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-819-2-ren-gong-bi-4-wanren-tade-quanmou-lingren-paianjuejue-lsslj3y-krnd8h-c8x5pgsqeinff|Hanji 819]] gives the frontier version: [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] proposes money, cloth, internal division, and heavy rewards for turning or killing hostile leaders in the Jiaozhi crisis, but embeds those incentives inside better appointments and reassurance.

Hanji 826 supplies the Xu-Yang anti-bandit version. Liang Na appoints Teng Fu and related commanders; Teng Fu then announces rewards that bring local participants into the suppression field. Xie An of Xiapi becomes the clearest case: after Xu Feng escapes and burns Dongcheng, Xie An leads kin and associates to kill him and is made Marquis of Pingxiang.

The concept sits between [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|因功赏罚]], [[RootCausePacification|查因安抚式治理]], and [[AppointmentQualityAsStateCapacity|任官适任性国家能力]]. Rewards must be credible enough to move local action, but pacification still depends on commander quality, verified service, and post-victory order rather than bounty money alone.

## Key Claims
- Rewards can extend state capacity by mobilizing local people with knowledge of terrain, fugitives, and kinship networks.
- Bounties work best when credible commanders create a field where local action can safely attach to public authority.
- Frontier reward systems can split hostile coalitions when money, cloth, and honors make defection safer than continued rebellion.
- Merit conversion must be visible: Xie An's marquisate matters because it proves that decisive local action will be recognized.
- Reward-backed suppression can reduce bandit networks, but it does not by itself solve every adjacent disorder node.
- The pattern is vulnerable if rewards become arbitrary, captured, or detached from real service.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current concept rests on two late-Han source notes. It should not be treated as a universal recipe for rebellion suppression, because reward systems can produce false reporting, bounty hunting, or postwar grievance if not tied to trustworthy command. Hanji 819 also shows that rewards alone are insufficient: Li Gu's plan needs fitted officials, temporary retrenchment, and surrender policy. Zhao Xu's execution for fearfulness and false merit reporting in Hanji 826 is a caution that campaign reward environments require verification.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 819's Jiaozhi frontier reward plan, including money, cloth, internal division, and leader-focused rewards.
- Reframed rewards as one instrument inside appointment quality and pacification strategy.
- Preserved Hanji 826's Xu-Yang local-ambush and visible marquisate layer.
