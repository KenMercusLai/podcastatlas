# Child Experiment Ethics

## Definition

Child experiment ethics governs research involving children who cannot independently authorize risk, with special attention to consent, necessity, developmental timing, delayed harm, identity, deception, and the difference between observation and imposed intervention.

## Current Synthesis

[[68-fengkuang-shiyan-shi-ai-zhe-gaisi-de-qiuzhiyu-664384549]] grounds the concept in [[WinthropKellogg|Winthrop Kellogg]]'s co-rearing of his son Donald with the chimpanzee Gua. The case became alarming when Donald's language development lagged and he imitated Gua's vocalizations. Both child and animal were placed in a design they could not authorize.

[[the-magic-wand-experiment-the-magic-wand-experiment|The Magic Wand Experiment]] makes the boundary explicit by imagining altered sex presentation, parental deception, social isolation, and lifelong screen assignment from birth. These designs clarify questions precisely because they suspend protections that responsible research must retain. A child's availability to a parent, researcher, or institution is not consent, and persuasive value does not justify turning development or identity into an imposed proof.

## Key Claims
- A child's availability to a researcher, parent, or institution is not consent.
- Developmental experiments create special ethical risk because harms may appear late or remain causally ambiguous.
- Children should not become proof objects for adult curiosity without strong protection and necessity.
- Ethical narration should avoid turning later tragedy into a causal claim when the source itself cannot support it.
- Deception, isolation, identity manipulation, and decades of assigned exposure intensify harm and the difficulty of meaningful withdrawal.
- Persuasive value for parents or policymakers does not override the child's interests.

## Evidence

### Co-rearing intervention

- [[68-fengkuang-shiyan-shi-ai-zhe-gaisi-de-qiuzhiyu-664384549]] presents the Kellogg child-chimpanzee experiment as scientifically memorable and ethically troubling.

### Impossible developmental controls

- [[the-magic-wand-experiment-the-magic-wand-experiment|The Magic Wand Experiment]] proposes deception, isolation, altered perceived sex, and lifetime screen assignment to reveal questions that cannot ethically be answered through those designs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Children can participate in ethically reviewed observational and interventional research when risk, assent, parental permission, necessity, monitoring, withdrawal, and expected benefit are appropriately handled. The concept does not reject pediatric research. The sources are structured podcast summaries rather than protocols, ethics records, or longitudinal outcome studies.
