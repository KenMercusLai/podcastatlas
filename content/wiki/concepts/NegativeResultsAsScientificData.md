# Negative Results As Scientific Data

## Definition
Negative results as scientific data is the principle that failed, null, or non-working experiments can improve cumulative knowledge when their methods, controls, and limits are recorded well enough to interpret.

## Current Synthesis
The Data Science With Sam source establishes the data-quality case: chemistry researchers and models learn a selectively successful record when failed reactions disappear, while biology requires caution because an apparent null may reflect technical failure. The Bhattacharya interview adds institutional supply: negative findings need a searchable venue and career credit, not only researcher willingness. Publishing more failure is useful only when documentation makes the result interpretable and the system distinguishes informative nulls from uninterpretable experiments.

## Key Claims
- Failed reactions can prevent duplicated work and improve model training data.
- Null or negative findings are evidence, but they need enough context to distinguish real absence from technical failure.
- [[PublicationBias]] can make the visible scientific record too successful and too clean.
- Negative data is useful for [[RetrosynthesisAI]] and [[BloodBrainBarrierPrediction]] because models need examples of non-working paths.
- Recording negative results supports [[ExperimentalFailureAsKnowledge]] only when researchers mark what the result did and did not establish.
- Searchability and career incentives determine whether negative findings become cumulative public knowledge or remain buried in laboratories.

## Evidence
- Chemistry and model training: [[data-ai-and-scientific-research-a-coffee-chat]] says omitted failed reactions distort the record available to chemists and retrosynthesis systems.
- Biological ambiguity: [[data-ai-and-scientific-research-a-coffee-chat]] distinguishes a true negative from a result caused by technical failure.
- Publication infrastructure: [[scim5646582763-scim5646582763]] records Bhattacharya's proposal for an NIH venue where negative and replication results can be published and searched.
- Career mechanism: [[scim5646582763-scim5646582763]] links publication of negative findings to broader efforts to make replication and cooperation professionally viable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is not a mandate to publish every failed attempt. Poorly controlled, undocumented, underpowered, or technically compromised work may add noise rather than knowledge. The proposed NIH venue is not outcome evidence, and neither source establishes the best editorial or metadata standards for making negative findings reusable.
