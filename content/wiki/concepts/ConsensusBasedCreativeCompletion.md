# Consensus-Based Creative Completion

## Definition

Consensus-based creative completion is [[RickRubin]]'s collaborative rule that substantive disagreement marks the present version as unfinished, so participants continue testing alternatives until everyone genuinely supports the result.

## Current Synthesis

[[from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with-malcolm-gladwell-from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with|From Broken Record]] presents consensus as a process test rather than polite harmony. Disagreement is permitted and made actionable: collaborators demonstrate competing versions, reduce authorship cues when possible, and judge the work instead of defending whose idea it was. Shared commitment to the strongest result gives another person's conviction that improvement remains possible procedural weight.

The principle is strongest when participants have real voice, a shared goal, and enough time and resources to iterate. It is not evidence that unanimity always produces better art. Unequal authority can turn consensus into coerced assent, vetoes can create endless delay, and some productions require an accountable final decision-maker.

## Key Claims

- Genuine collaborator disagreement is information about unresolved work rather than disloyalty.
- Concrete demonstrations make competing creative proposals easier to evaluate than abstract argument.
- Hiding authorship where practical can reduce ego defense and status bias.
- Consensus requires sincere support rather than silence, exhaustion, or majority rule mislabeled as agreement.
- A shared quality goal makes continued iteration more legitimate and useful.
- Time, power, labor, and decision-right constraints determine whether consensus remains workable.

## Evidence

- Unfinished-work rule - [[from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with-malcolm-gladwell-from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with]] records Rubin's agreement to keep working while any collaborator remains unhappy with the result.
- Group conviction - [[from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with-malcolm-gladwell-from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with]] presents a version supported by every band member as preferable to one accepted by only a majority.
- Demonstration over ownership - [[from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with-malcolm-gladwell-from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with]] recommends making alternatives audible and, where possible, concealing their authors.
- Shared goal - [[from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with-malcolm-gladwell-from-broken-record-rick-rubin-in-conversation-with]] conditions cooperation on mutual commitment to making the work as good as it can be.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode offers a practitioner rule, not comparative evidence that unanimity produces stronger work. Consensus can conceal power, reward the most persistent veto, dilute necessary risk, or become unaffordable under deadlines. Safety, legal responsibility, commissioned requirements, and role-specific expertise may require explicit authority rather than universal approval.

## What Changed

- Established a creative completion model based on explicit disagreement, concrete alternatives, and genuine shared support.
- Added power, deadline, veto, and accountable-authority limits to the practitioner rule.
