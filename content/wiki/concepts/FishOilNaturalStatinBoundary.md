# Fish Oil Natural Statin Boundary / 鱼油不是天然他汀

## Definition
Fish oil natural statin boundary is the warning that retail fish-oil supplements should not be marketed or used as natural substitutes for indication-based lipid-lowering drug treatment.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates medical-grade fish oil from ordinary retail fish-oil supplements. In the episode's China-facing framing, medical-grade fish oil is treated as a prescription medicine obtained through hospital channels, while supermarket or livestream fish oil is usually a health-product category. That makes "fish oil equals natural statin" a misleading compression of very different products and uses.

Fish oil can still be relevant as omega-3 gap-filling when someone does not regularly eat marine fish. But omega-3 products vary by structure, purity, molecular form, and DHA/EPA ratio, and cardiovascular or lipid treatment depends on indications and professional judgment. The source's main claim is therefore boundary-setting: naturalness and supplement status should not replace drug evidence, dose, indication, or clinician management.

## Key Claims
- Medical-grade fish oil and ordinary retail fish-oil supplements should not be treated as the same product class.
- Retail fish oil may help fill a dietary omega-3 gap, especially when marine fish intake is low.
- Omega-3 products vary by structure, purity, molecular form, and DHA/EPA ratio, so the category name alone is not enough.
- Calling fish oil a natural statin is misleading because statin use depends on medical indications and drug-level evidence.
- If a product is being used to treat disease, it belongs under stricter medical judgment rather than wellness marketing.
- Cardiovascular-risk, lipid, medication, and disease contexts should stay inside qualified care.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not evaluate fish-oil trials, statin guidelines, prescription omega-3 indications, or individual cardiovascular risk. The concept should be used only as a marketing and decision boundary: supplement gap-filling is not equivalent to disease treatment.

## What Changed
- Added a fish-oil boundary separating omega-3 supplementation from prescription lipid treatment.
- Captured the episode's warning that "natural statin" language converts a medical-indication question into a misleading supplement claim.
