# Emergency Succession Legitimation / 临终就近立储

Emergency succession legitimation / 临终就近立储 is the mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw|Qinji 127-3]] extracts from the [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]] account of [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s death. The pattern appears when a ruler is dying away from the capital, the more expected heir is distant, and ministers treat the nearby candidate as the least destabilizing choice.

The concept does not mean the chosen heir is morally or politically good. In the episode, [[QinErshi|胡亥 / 秦二世]] remains disastrous after accession. The claim is narrower: under emergency conditions, proximity, controllable timing, and continuity of command can become arguments for legitimacy even when later outcomes expose the choice as ruinous.

This concept is the counterweight to [[ForgedEdictSuccessionCoup|伪诏继位政变]]. The forged-edict account says [[ZhaoGao|赵高]], [[LiSi|李斯]], and Hu Hai simulate a dead ruler's will after the fact. The emergency-legitimation account says Qin Shi Huang, Li Si, and [[FengQuji|冯去疾]] may have made a live but constrained decision before death. The wiki keeps both source traditions visible rather than collapsing them into one settled sequence.

## Key Claims
- Distance can become politically decisive when a ruler dies or is dying away from the capital.
- A nearby heir may be treated as safer because he can be installed before news, troops, or rival ministers create a scramble.
- Emergency legitimation can be procedurally cleaner than forgery while still producing catastrophic rule.
- The mechanism depends on trusted ministers' participation; without them, proximity alone looks like seizure rather than succession.
- Source-critical uncertainty matters: the same event may read as emergency statecraft in one source and coup in another.
