# Evidence-Based Development Policy

## Definition
Evidence-based development policy uses measured effects, implementation detail, and revisable causal explanations to decide which poverty, education, health, and entrepreneurship interventions deserve support.

## Current Synthesis
The sources reject both intuition-only policy and experiment-only technocracy. Randomized evidence can show that plausible inputs fail, identify interventions that work, and reveal the mechanism that policy must address. Textbooks, additional teachers, and smaller classes may accomplish little when instruction remains above learners' usable level; reliable vaccine supply may remain insufficient when inconvenience and postponement dominate; and prices need not create use or value merely because a familiar story predicts they will.

Evidence still has to travel through politics, institutions, trust, financing, implementation capacity, and ethical relationships. YouWin's measured job effects did not protect the program from political discontinuity, while research conducted in Busia could generate global knowledge without guaranteeing local adoption. Good policy learning therefore connects [[RandomizedControlledTrials]] and [[InterventionMechanismLearning]] to institutional durability and [[DevelopmentResearchEthics]].

## Key Claims
- Measured outcomes can overturn intuitive claims about which interventions or incentives work.
- Failed input-only programs can redirect policy toward the operating mechanism that blocks results.
- Effective policy requires implementation detail, not only a favorable average treatment effect.
- Political continuity, financing, institutional relationships, and trust influence whether evidence becomes durable policy.
- Global knowledge gains do not guarantee local benefit, voice, capacity, or adoption.
- Evidence-based policy remains provisional because study framing and transfer across settings require judgment.

- Institutional continuity - [[piles-of-cash-and-a-town-of-solutions-in-kenya-nigeria-summer-school]] reports measurable YouWin effects alongside later program discontinuity.
- Local-benefit boundary - [[piles-of-cash-and-a-town-of-solutions-in-kenya-nigeria-summer-school]] distinguishes globally valuable research from direct policy gains for participating communities.
- Framing humility - [[putting-our-assumptions-to-the-test]] warns that experiments can answer a constrained or wrongly framed set of possibilities.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither episode provides the full study record needed to compare effect sizes, costs, uncertainty, implementation fidelity, or transferability. A result that contradicts one story does not by itself establish the replacement mechanism. Policy choices also incorporate rights, distribution, legitimacy, administrative burden, and opportunity cost; these cannot be reduced to a single experimental outcome.
