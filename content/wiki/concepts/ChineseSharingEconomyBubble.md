# Chinese Sharing-Economy Bubble

## Definition
The Chinese sharing-economy bubble is the 2010s wave in which companies bought assets, attached QR codes, collected deposits or rental fees, and used mobile apps to present the assets as shared infrastructure.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats the sharing-economy wave as an O2O subcycle rather than a single business model. [[Mobike]] and [[Ofo|OFO]] made the formula look universal because bikes were high-frequency, outdoor, visible, and politically aligned with low-carbon urban mobility. The same template was then stretched to power banks, cars, basketballs, gyms, sleep pods, wardrobes, mini KTV booths, massage chairs, umbrellas, and unmanned shelves.

The durable lesson is fit. Shared power banks had a better match between asset size, urgency, venue density, and standardization. Shared cars, umbrellas, wardrobes, gyms, and unmanned shelves exposed harder versions of theft, maintenance, sanitation, asset recovery, utilization, and operating-cost risk.

## Key Claims
- The shared-asset formula was simple enough to copy but not robust enough for every category.
- Deposits and high financing volumes could mask whether the rental loop produced healthy cash flow.
- Asset heaviness moved risk into procurement, maintenance, storage, recovery, write-downs, and supplier payments.
- Shared power banks are the episode's main survivor because the asset and use case fit venue-based rental economics.
- Failed sharing projects still clarified the boundary between genuine convenience and concept-driven asset deployment.

- Survivor logic - [[no-218-o2o-yu-gongxiangjingji-shuyu-danche-chongdianbao-shangmenmeijia-he-anmo-de-mohuan-saqian-niandai-1013073318]] identifies shared power banks as one of the few money-making categories, with [[JiedianPowerBank|街电]], 来电, 小电, and 怪兽 forming the remembered competitive set.
- Failure modes - [[no-218-o2o-yu-gongxiangjingji-shuyu-danche-chongdianbao-shangmenmeijia-he-anmo-de-mohuan-saqian-niandai-1013073318]] uses shared cars, basketballs, gyms, wardrobes, umbrellas, and unmanned shelves to show deposit complaints, theft, heat, sanitation, electricity, safety, and inventory problems.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a retrospective podcast and does not provide a complete financial audit of every company. Some shared formats may survive as facility amenities or platform features even if independent venture-backed versions fail.
