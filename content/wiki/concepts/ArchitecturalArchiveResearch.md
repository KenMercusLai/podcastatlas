# Architectural Archive Research / 建筑档案研究

## Definition
Architectural archive research is the source's method for reconstructing a building corpus through archival files, newspapers, advertisements, library materials, family lists, oral leads, field verification, and correction of mistaken attributions.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents Wu Feipeng's Lai An work as a case of bounded, evidence-sensitive urban research. A family list with question marks starts the search, but Wu treats Shanghai archives and period newspapers as stronger evidence, ultimately limiting confirmed Lai An works to 122. The method also includes correcting earlier mistakes, especially the Anna identity confusion.

## Key Claims
- Building attribution requires cross-checking archives, newspapers, lists, plaques, and on-site evidence.
- More claims are not automatically better; a confirmed count can be more valuable than an inflated list.
- Shanghai archives and newspapers may preserve richer local evidence than distant homeland archives.
- Published work remains revisable when later evidence exposes a name or identity confusion.
- Public plaques are downstream knowledge products, not substitutes for continued research.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This method does not eliminate uncertainty. Some apparently Lai An-like houses still cannot be proved, and wartime or postwar records for Anna and Lai An's final months remain incomplete.
