# Layered Feint Central Breakthrough / 多路佯动中央突破

Layered feint central breakthrough / 多路佯动中央突破 is the operational pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-148-1-hanxin-yupan-le-zhanghan-de-yupan-lodhpuxpossjno87gorjkfrtitbe|Hanji 148-1]] extracts from [[HanXin|韩信]]'s campaign against [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]]. The pattern is not simply "声东击西." It creates several visible side threats so the enemy believes he has read through the deception, then attacks through the center that the enemy has weakened while guarding against the feints.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-1-lishi-jizai-shenme-qingkuang-hui-fasheng-ren-chi-ren-canju-lrwz-prnxd8hzchagyrbywkux7lh|Hanji 155-1]] reopens the same tactical family from the [[WeiBao|魏豹]] campaign angle. Because the transcript only poses the "声东击西" question and has not yet narrated Han Xin's full operation against West Wei, this concept treats the episode as a pointer to the deception frame rather than as independent evidence for a new sub-pattern.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-2-zhuan-gei-ni-piqi-da-de-pengyou-weiwang-panhan-jing-shi-liubang-ai-maren-ls5nvunmhakvefd2oxn4zdyoob4l|Hanji 155-2]] resolves that pointer as a related but distinct [[RiverCrossingDeception|渡河欺敌]] case. Han Xin makes Wei Bao defend the obvious [[LinjinWarringStates|临晋]] / [[PubanWarringStates|蒲阪]] crossing while the real army crosses at [[XiayangChuHan|下阳]] and threatens [[Anyi|安邑]]. This is not the same "central breakthrough" geometry as Chen Cang, but it belongs to the same Han Xin repertoire of making an enemy defend the visible answer while the decisive route is elsewhere.

In the source case, [[GuanYing|灌婴]] moves by the Ziwu route, [[FanKuai|樊哙]] moves by the Qishan route, and [[CaoShen|曹参]] attacks Xiabian to make the Qishan-side signal stronger. Zhang Han is described as experienced and careful, but that competence becomes part of the trap: he thinks Han Xin's real aim must be behind the visible side pressure, so he concentrates on the flanks. Han Xin then sends the main force through [[ChenCang|陈仓]] and converts Zhang Han's anti-feint judgment into a central opening.

This concept is adjacent to [[BattlefieldInformationControl|战场信息控制]] and [[LureEnemyOutOfPosition|诱敌离位]], but it has a narrower structure: the enemy is not merely pulled forward or fed false information; the enemy is invited to feel superior for detecting the false move, and that second-order confidence becomes the vulnerability.

## Key Claims
- A feint can target an opponent's interpretive confidence, not only his physical position.
- Multiple side threats can make an enemy overcommit to defending against the deception he thinks he has solved.
- The breakthrough must have a prepared center; otherwise side feints only scatter one's own force.
- Competent opponents can be more vulnerable to layered deception when their experience makes them trust their diagnosis too soon.
- Hanji 155-1 confirms the "声东击西" frame as relevant to Han Xin's later Wei Bao problem, but not yet enough to expand the detailed campaign model.
- Hanji 155-2 shows the later Wei Bao case as a river-line variant: the defended obvious crossing becomes the feint, and the quieter crossing becomes the real route.
