# European Union AI Act

The European Union AI Act enters this wiki through [[tech-20260814-tech-pod-128-tech-20260814-tech-pod-128]] as the compliance backdrop for [[Anthropic]] adding invisible watermarks to [[Claude]]-generated text. [[MariaCurie|Maria Curi]] says the law requires AI labs to tell users when content is generated by artificial intelligence, making the act part of the wiki's [[AIContentProvenance]] and [[AIGovernanceAndCompliance]] branches.

In the source, the law matters less as a full regulatory code than as an example of extraterritorial product pressure. Anthropic applies the policy globally rather than only inside the [[EuropeanUnion|European Union]], showing how one jurisdiction's disclosure rule can shape default behavior for users elsewhere.

## Key Claims
- AI disclosure rules can push model providers to build provenance signals into products rather than relying only on user self-reporting.
- Global rollout can be easier or more reputationally consistent than maintaining separate EU and non-EU product behavior.
- Compliance-driven provenance still leaves interpretation problems when generated text, AI-edited human text, and human-only work overlap.
- The act functions as a regulator-side force behind [[AITextWatermarking]], not just as a privacy or market-access rule.
