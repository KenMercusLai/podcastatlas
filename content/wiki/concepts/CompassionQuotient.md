# Compassion Quotient

## Definition
Compassion Quotient, or CQ, is [[KailashSatyarthi]]'s proposed measure of how awareness, connectedness, genuine feeling, and action combine in a person's or institution's compassionate capacity.

## Current Synthesis
CQ reframes compassion as an innate but trainable capacity rather than a fixed personality trait or soft virtue. The source imagines it influencing personal relationships, employment choices, leadership, business, technology, and institutional accountability. Its practical promise is to reward concern translated into action; its unresolved problem is measurement, because the episode supplies dimensions and use cases but no validated instrument or protection against self-report, cultural, and incentive distortions.

## Key Claims
- Compassion can be strengthened through motivation and practice.
- Awareness, connectedness, feeling, and action are the proposed dimensions.
- Measurement should capture outward responsibility rather than sentiment alone.
- CQ is intended for both personal development and institutional comparison.
- A score becomes counterproductive if it rewards performance, compliance theater, or easily counted activity rather than reduced suffering and accountable relationships.

- Contrast with existing metrics - [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-23916f7db5fae55cf32448ade9833313-57996390-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-23916f7db5fae55cf32448ade9833313-57996390]] contrasts CQ with IQ, EQ, productivity, and GDP-centered success measures.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
No questionnaire, behavioral protocol, scoring rule, validity evidence, reliability evidence, cross-cultural study, or outcome evaluation is supplied. Self-report can confuse compassionate identity with conduct; institutional metrics invite gaming; and disagreement remains over whose suffering, action, and tradeoffs count. CQ should therefore be treated as a proposed framework and research agenda, not an established psychometric quantity.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the episode's four-component proposal.
- Separated trainability and accountability ambitions from unestablished psychometric validity.
- Added metric-gaming and cross-cultural interpretation as explicit boundaries.
