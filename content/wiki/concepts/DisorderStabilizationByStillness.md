# Disorder Stabilization by Stillness / 以静制乱式识别

## Definition

Disorder stabilization by stillness is a crisis-control pattern in which a commander reduces uncertainty by ordering uninvolved people to remain stationary, making active disruption easier to isolate.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1088 presents the pattern through [[ZhangLiao|张辽]]'s response to a night camp mutiny. Darkness, fire, fear, and uncertain allegiance make indiscriminate movement dangerous. Zhang Liao first rejects the assumption that the entire camp has rebelled, then tells nonparticipants to remain in place and holds the center with a small group. The order changes movement into information: stillness marks provisional nonparticipation, while continued disruptive action exposes the minority driving the revolt.

## Key Claims

- The tactic begins with a bounded diagnosis that a visible disturbance does not necessarily represent the whole organization.
- Restricting friendly movement reduces accidental escalation and mistaken identification under low visibility.
- A simple observable rule can turn chaotic behavior into usable information.
- Visible command presence helps stabilize the rule while the disruptive minority is isolated.
- The mechanism manages an immediate crisis; it does not by itself resolve the underlying integration or resource conflict.

## Evidence

- Night-camp response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1088-junying-yeluan-ta-dai-jishiren-jiu-zhenzhule-lu8ocnihz6arqllfcc3ujok6ujil|Hanji 1088]] says Zhang Liao orders uninvolved troops to stay still, stands in the camp center with several dozen men, and captures the leader after order returns.
- Organizational qualification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1088-junying-yeluan-ta-dai-jishiren-jiu-zhenzhule-lu8ocnihz6arqllfcc3ujok6ujil|Hanji 1088]] interprets the mutiny as tension following the receipt of Cao Hong's former troops, so the tactical solution sits above a deeper integration problem.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The pattern depends on enough command credibility for nonparticipants to obey and on disruption remaining a minority action. It could fail if most troops have defected, if communication cannot reach the camp, or if hostile actors imitate stillness. The episode's *Art of War* phrases are textually uncertain and are not used as verified quotations.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 1088's Zhang Liao night-mutiny case.
