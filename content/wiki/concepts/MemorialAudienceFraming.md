# Memorial Audience Framing

## Definition

Memorial audience framing is the way a heritage site's expected, visible, or economically important visitors shape which historical routes, victims, identities, and meanings receive emphasis.

## Current Synthesis

The Gorée episode argues that the [[HouseOfSlavesGoree|House of Slaves]] experience speaks especially to African American visitors seeking an ancestral place of mourning. That function is meaningful, but it can make the North American branch appear more representative of Atlantic slavery than it was and leave Brazilian and Caribbean destinations comparatively obscure.

Audience framing is not simply falsification. A community may need a legible point of connection to an erased past, and a museum must translate complex history into a visitable narrative. The problem emerges when emotional accessibility, purchasing power, and tourism design silently determine the apparent scale or center of the history.

## Key Claims

- Memorial narratives are shaped by the audiences who can travel, pay, and receive public attention.
- Diasporic visitors can create legitimate new meanings at a site beyond its original material function.
- A compelling visitor story can confuse one community's memory needs with the statistical center of a transnational system.
- The histories of Brazil, the Caribbean, and other destinations can be marginalized by a North American frame.
- Good interpretation should name the audience-specific perspective and reconnect the site to the wider system.

## Evidence

- Pilgrimage audience: [[272-senegal-the-door-of-no-return-glt8611353465]] links Gorée's rise to post-civil-rights African American mobility, ancestry seeking, *Roots*, celebrity visits, and tourism.
- Geographic imbalance: [[272-senegal-the-door-of-no-return-glt8611353465]] explicitly contrasts the museum's American emphasis with the greater scale of the Brazilian and Caribbean slave trades.
- Symbolic production: [[272-senegal-the-door-of-no-return-glt8611353465]] argues that visitors as well as documents give the doorway its durable meaning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode offers an interpretive claim rather than visitor surveys, museum scripts, revenue data, or a complete comparison of destination histories. An African American focus is not inherently exclusionary, and the source does not establish that every visitor receives the same narrative. The relative scale of national and regional slave trades remains source-scoped here.

## What Changed

- Established audience composition and travel access as forces shaping public-memory narratives.
- Added an explicit guardrail against making North American remembrance stand for the whole Atlantic slave trade.
