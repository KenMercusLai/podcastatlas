# Institutional Sectarian Discrimination

Institutional sectarian discrimination is the episode's frame for how ordinary state functions in [[NorthernIreland]] became organized around Protestant unionist dominance and Catholic exclusion. In [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]], the pattern includes [[Stormont]] boundaries, local ratepayer voting, housing allocation, employment disadvantage, Protestant-dominated courts and civil service, the [[RoyalUlsterConstabulary|RUC]], the [[BSpecials|B Specials]], and [[OrangeOrder]] overlap with unionist power.

The source's sharpest local case is [[Derry]], where a Catholic-majority city remained under Protestant control through ward design while Catholic families waited for housing. The concept explains why the later [[NorthernIrelandCivilRightsMovement]] could make housing, voting, policing, and boundaries part of one legitimacy claim rather than separate administrative complaints.

## Key Claims
- Discrimination is more destabilizing when housing, votes, jobs, policing, courts, and boundaries reinforce each other.
- Administrative decisions can become constitutional evidence when they determine who receives a home, a vote, or police protection.
- A state can be formally legal while many citizens experience it as structurally hostile.
