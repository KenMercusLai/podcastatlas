## Definition
Unrighteous merit reward is the governance problem that arises when an action helps the state against an enemy but violates a core moral or legal boundary, so rewarding the action can damage the meaning of official rank, punishment, and public legitimacy.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] frames [[ZiMiEasternHan|Zi Mi]] as useful to [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] because he kills the rebel [[PengChongEasternHan|Peng Chong]], but it refuses to treat usefulness as moral cleansing. Peng Chong's rebellion and Zi Mi's master-killing are both treated as crimes in different registers. The title "不义侯" therefore exposes a contradiction: the court names the act unrighteous while still converting it into hereditary honor.

The concept sharpens a broader distinction between rewarding outcome and preserving title meaning. A regime may pragmatically accept the collapse of a rebel enemy, but if it publicly rewards the method without separate legal accounting, it risks making rank look like a payment for any act that happens to help the winner.

## Key Claims
- Political usefulness does not automatically transform an unlawful act into legitimate merit.
- A reward title carries public meaning, so an internally contradictory title can advertise the regime's own discomfort.
- The problem is sharper when the rewarded act violates a relationship the legal order is supposed to protect, such as a servant killing a master.
- Labeling the reward "unrighteous" may acknowledge the problem but does not by itself repair the damage to rank credibility.
- The case distinguishes accepting the strategic benefit of enemy collapse from honoring the method that produced it.

## Evidence
- Politically useful but morally polluted violence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] records Zi Mi killing Peng Chong and sending the head to Luoyang, which helps Liu Xiu remove a rebel enemy.
- Contradictory public reward: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] emphasizes the "不义侯" title as an official reward that still names the act's unrighteous character.
- Legal-moral distinction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-693-panchu-liuxiu-de-pengchong-ruhe-yin-beipan-er-wang-lqler4fu9wqztsrgzsxhs1519nm]] argues that Peng Chong's rebellion and Zi Mi's killing should both be handled as crimes rather than folded into a single victor's reward logic.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The concept does not claim that states can never accept defectors, informants, or rebel-collapse opportunities.
- The current evidence is a single Guangwu-era episode and should be broadened before becoming a general rule for all imperial reward systems.
- The issue is specifically public honor without adequate moral accounting, not the mere tactical use of information from an enemy camp.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the Zi Mi "不义侯" case in Hanji 693.
