# Particularized Moral Discernment

## Definition

Particularized moral discernment is the practice of suspending prior ideological attachment long enough to examine a case's facts, motives, relationships, institutional context, lived consequences, and suffering before applying or revising a moral category.

## Current Synthesis

[[the-standard-case-the-standard-case|The Standard Case]] gives Jesuit casuistry its explicit comparative structure: narrate the disputed case, identify accepted standard cases, and ask which similarities and differences are morally controlling. Maritime insurance can resemble payment for protecting transported value more than an exploitative loan; [[AndyPettitte]]'s limited injury-recovery use can resemble [[TommyJohn]]'s restorative surgery more than [[BarryBonds]]'s transformative enhancement. The method treats purpose, duration, resulting capacity, risk, and effects on others as evidence to test rather than letting a single label decide the case.

[[descend-into-the-particular-descend-into-the-particular|Descend into the Particular]] develops the discipline of weakening prior attachment so inconvenient evidence and human experience can enter judgment. Ferguson demonstrates that findings at different levels can coexist: insufficient evidence to prosecute one officer does not negate evidence of predatory, racially harmful municipal policing. [[AngelNavarro]]'s death likewise shows that video, family memory, inferred intent, tactical danger, and differently situated suffering can matter without becoming equivalent.

The technology-and-doctrine test in [[dr-rock-s-taxonomy-dr-rock-s-taxonomy|Dr. Rock’s Taxonomy]] follows [[JohnRock]] from poor women's reproductive burdens to the question of whether the pill belongs with prohibited barriers or permitted rhythm. The speculative mTOR example similarly forces a choice between “contraception” and “fertility preservation.” Particularity therefore does more than complicate a verdict after the fact: it can reveal which features of a new case should control its moral classification.

The pastoral branch returns classification to relationship. [[JamesMartinJesuit]] uses comparison across Catholic teachings to ask whether LGBT Catholics face [[SelectiveDoctrineEnforcement]], while [[JamesKeenan]]'s account of omitted love makes nonresponse to suffering morally relevant. Accompaniment requires knowledge of whole lives and existing membership rather than reducing people to one disputed category.

Together the episodes defend neither permanent neutrality nor exemption from rules. They argue for disciplined attention before conclusion, explicit comparison among relevant cases, consistency across cases, and willingness to recognize when an inherited category hides important differences. The method remains vulnerable to rationalization, false equivalence, selective baselines, and uneven enforcement, so evidence, power, consequences, institutional role, and responsibility must constrain analogy.

## Key Claims

- Ideological allegiance can select supportive facts while making complicating facts or lived consequences psychologically inadmissible.
- Concrete attention can hold findings at different levels—individual liability and institutional legitimacy, present pregnancy prevention and future fertility preservation, doctrine and enforcement—without treating them as contradictions.
- Evidence constrains interpretation but does not make motive, moral meaning, or categorical fit self-evident.
- Listening to lived suffering and practical circumstances is part of moral judgment, not sentiment added after abstract analysis.
- Casuistic analogy can revise a taxonomy when a new case shares morally relevant features with a standard case.
- Suspending attachment is temporary discipline for better judgment, not permanent indecision or permission to ignore principles.
- Particularity should complicate categories without erasing rules or asymmetries of evidence, power, agency, risk, loss, and responsibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episodes offer a moral and interpretive method, not a validated decision procedure. “Both sides” attention can become false equivalence if it ignores unequal evidence, institutional power, causal responsibility, or preventable harm. Case-specific classification can become motivated hair-splitting when a preferred conclusion selects the baseline or analogy, while consistency arguments can flatten material differences among roles and conduct. Principles remain necessary for coherence, predictable treatment, and protection against biased exceptions. The accounts rely on structured podcast summaries rather than the complete reports, records, medical evidence, performance data, doctrinal arguments, or clinical literature relevant to their cases.

## What Changed

- Added the explicit standard-case method for building and revising a moral taxonomy.
- Extended classification to restorative versus transformative athletic intervention and competitor coercion.
- Added selective enforcement, existing membership, omitted love, and accompaniment as institutional and pastoral tests.
- Made baseline choice and consistency across sanctions explicit limits on casuistic reasoning.
