# Particularized Moral Discernment

## Definition

Particularized moral discernment is the practice of suspending prior ideological attachment long enough to examine the specific facts, motives, relationships, institutional context, and suffering within a case before reaching judgment.

## Current Synthesis

[[descend-into-the-particular-descend-into-the-particular|Descend into the Particular]] presents the practice through Jesuit casuistry and the image of a scale balanced at zero. A “disordered attachment” is not simply a bad desire; it is a commitment or aversion that prevents a person from hearing what others say and feel. The corrective is not moral neutrality forever but disciplined attention before conclusion.

Ferguson supplies the institutional test. Evidence insufficient to prosecute Darren Wilson and evidence of predatory, racially harmful municipal policing answer different questions and should not be forced into one partisan verdict. [[AngelNavarro]]'s death supplies the personal test: video, family memory, tactical danger, private distress, inferred intent, and officer trauma can all matter without becoming equivalent or dissolving responsibility.

## Key Claims

- Ideological allegiance can select supportive facts while making complicating facts psychologically inadmissible.
- Concrete attention can hold findings at different levels—individual liability and institutional legitimacy—without treating them as contradictions.
- Video and forensic reconstruction constrain interpretation but do not make motive or moral meaning self-evident.
- Listening to lived suffering is part of moral judgment, not an optional sentiment added after legal analysis.
- Suspending attachment is a temporary discipline for better judgment, not permanent indecision or refusal to use principles.
- Particularity should complicate categories without erasing asymmetries of power, agency, loss, and responsibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode offers a moral and interpretive method, not a validated decision procedure. “Both sides” attention can become false equivalence if it ignores unequal evidence, institutional power, causal responsibility, or preventable harm. Principles remain necessary for consistency and protection against biased case-by-case exceptions. The Ferguson and Navarro accounts also rely on a structured podcast summary rather than the full reports and records.

## What Changed

- Established disciplined suspension of ideological attachment as a precondition for hearing case-specific evidence.
- Distinguished simultaneous individual and institutional findings from contradiction.
- Limited compassionate complexity so it does not become false equivalence or moral evasion.
