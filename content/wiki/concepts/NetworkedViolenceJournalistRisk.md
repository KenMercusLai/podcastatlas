## Definition

Journalist risk in networked violence is the exposure created when armed groups produce and distribute their own social-media narratives, making reporters potential witnesses, intermediaries, symbols, hostages, or objects inside a violent performance.

## Current Synthesis

[[QilinPhotographer|麒麟]] contrasts traditional war reporting's limited and unreliable role conventions with gang violence in [[Haiti]], where armed actors can publish their own spectacle and retaliation. The reporter is no longer valuable only as an outside channel to an audience; the reporter's capture, presence, or image can itself become content.

This shifts risk assessment from front lines and accreditation toward distributed actors, uncertain rules, local movement networks, armed protection, digital amplification, and the possibility that attention increases rather than reduces danger. It also makes post-exposure psychological containment part of journalistic safety rather than an unrelated personal issue.

## Key Claims

- Armed groups' direct publishing capacity changes the journalist's role and threat profile.
- A press identity may offer less protection where authority is fragmented and violence is performative.
- Access depends on trusted local knowledge and can remain severely restricted even with armed support.
- Digital visibility can make the reporter an object of the story rather than a protected observer.
- Safety planning must include mobility, communication, extraction, and post-return trauma responses.

- Restricted movement: [[episode-24-jiefei-qiang-le-wode-luotuo-liangci-luotuo-yici-dou-mei-tongyi-yu-tanxianjia-qilin-liao-sheying-chuangshang-yu-renlei-de-xiaxian-b49a6090-cca1-451d-99f7-495f6d264992]] says Qilin spent roughly three or four months in Haiti, usually unable to move freely and dependent on a locally knowledgeable armed procurement person.
- Aftermath: [[episode-24-jiefei-qiang-le-wode-luotuo-liangci-luotuo-yici-dou-mei-tongyi-yu-tanxianjia-qilin-liao-sheying-chuangshang-yu-renlei-de-xiaxian-b49a6090-cca1-451d-99f7-495f6d264992]] links his return to trauma and deliberate concentration on aperture, shutter, and sensitivity as emotional containment during photography.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is one photographer's retrospective account and does not compare regions, organizations, assignments, or incident data. Traditional battlefields do not reliably protect journalists, and social media is not the sole cause of danger in Haiti. Armed accompaniment can reduce some threats while introducing escalation, dependency, and identification risks.
