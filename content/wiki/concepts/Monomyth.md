# Monomyth / 单一神话

[[Monomyth|Monomyth / 单一神话]] is the [[JosephCampbell|Joseph Campbell / 约瑟夫·坎贝尔]] claim that heroes from many cultures and periods often share a recognizable pattern. In [[29-qianmian-yingxiong-edipusi-zhengzai-xiaban-ji-ditie-590112891]], the pattern is summarized through departure, initiation, and return, then tested across fairy tales, Greek myth, Indian myth, Japanese myth, Irish myth, Chinese fiction, epics, religion, and modern popular culture.

The source treats the monomyth as useful but contested. Similar stories may come from shared psychic structures, but they may also come from flood memory, cultural transmission, historical contact, later retelling, or ordinary recurring human situations. The concept therefore sits between [[HeroJourneyNarrative]] as a narrative tool and [[MythAsHistoricalEvidence]] as an evidence problem.

## Key Claims
- Monomyth is strongest as a pattern-recognition tool for story structure.
- It becomes weak when structural resemblance is treated as proof of one origin or one universal meaning.
- The source keeps Campbell valuable by using his stages while preserving historical and interpretive caution.
