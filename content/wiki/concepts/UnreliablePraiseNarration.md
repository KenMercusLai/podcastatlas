# Unreliable Praise Narration / 赞美式不可靠叙述

Unreliable praise narration is a narrative pattern where a speaker praises a powerful figure so insistently that the praise begins to reveal the violence it is meant to cover. [[17-diyubian-zhongsheng-jie-ku-yi-nian-ru-mo-561780565]] adds the concept through [[HellScreen|《地狱变》]], where the first-person household voice repeatedly defends Horikawa while disclosing enough detail to make the defense suspect.

The device is different from simple lying. The narrator may genuinely belong to the lord's value system; that loyalty makes the story more revealing because ordinary service language turns cruelty into elegance, rumor into slander, and coercion into noble favor. The reader has to hear both what is said and what the social position allows the speaker not to say.

## Key Claims
- Praise can become incriminating when details contradict the speaker's declared evaluation.
- Servile narration can expose a whole social order, not only one unreliable individual.
- A reader must separate the narrator's official judgment from the events the narration preserves.
- The device connects narrative technique to power: authority can shape what counts as reasonable speech.
