# Public Health Geopolitics

Public health geopolitics is the pattern where outbreak response becomes a contest over territory, legitimacy, expertise, and state capacity. [[yi-fen-laizi-bingchuang-shang-de-xiangguan-shudan-598284093]] adds the concept through [[DongbeiBoyi|《东北博弈》]], where the 1910-1911 Northeast plague is tied to marmot hunting, railways, a weakened Qing state, and Japanese and Russian power competition.

The same episode extends the idea through [[PlagueNights|《瘟疫之夜》 / Nights of Plague]]. In fiction as in history, quarantine and medical command can become political acts when an empire is weak, foreign powers are watching, and public order is unstable.

## Key Claims
- Public-health authority can become a sovereignty signal: who investigates, commands, quarantines, and communicates matters politically.
- Railways, ports, borders, and imperial influence can turn local disease response into regional competition.
- Technical success does not float above politics; it can strengthen or weaken claims of competence and legitimacy.
- Fiction can expose the same pattern by compressing outbreak, empire, murder, rumor, and foreign pressure into one setting.
