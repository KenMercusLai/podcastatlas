# Stop-Loss Discipline

## Definition

Stop-loss discipline is a precommitted rule for reducing or closing exposure when price, time, behavior, liquidity, or thesis evidence invalidates the reason for holding, so that one adverse path does not destroy the investor's capital or ability to continue.

## Current Synthesis

Across the sources, a stop is not a universal percentage. Livermore-style trend trading uses price and behavior failure; Kelly-style repeated-game logic treats an entry as a probe; Hong Kong small-cap trading adds the risk that delay destroys exit liquidity; fixed-income-plus management ties exits to a portfolio drawdown budget and client holdability; and late-bull-market sources use trailing exits to preserve gains in positions already judged speculative.

EP95 sharpens the decision boundary. A voluntary stop is chosen before broker liquidation, while moving or cancelling it near the loss limit rewrites the original risk contract under emotional pressure. A later rebound cannot by itself prove that the change was sound, because [[TradingOutcomeBias|outcome bias]] confuses one favorable path with decision quality.

## Key Claims

- Stop rules should match the entry thesis, time horizon, liquidity, and position size.
- A stop can be based on price, elapsed time, expected behavior, or fundamental invalidation.
- Moving a stop to avoid realizing loss weakens the precommitment unless new evidence justifies a documented rule change.
- Larger or added positions require stricter control because more capital shares the same invalidation risk.
- Thin liquidity makes early exit capacity part of the stop, not merely the quoted price.
- Trailing exits can preserve prior gains, while take-profit can replenish a portfolio's future risk budget.
- A voluntary stop and broker-imposed liquidation are different: the latter means collateral rules have taken control.

## Evidence

### Trend, probe, and sizing discipline
- [[ep76-chuanyue-1940-wo-yu-gupiao-dazuoshou-lifumoer-de-zuihou-duihua-lr0fb5yfiwoxaamdwjjgh4rjeprg|EP76]] treats stops as protection when the market fails to confirm the thesis.
- [[e153-gushen-de-paiju-fuli-gongshi-kaili-gongshi-lmyvi9mnlaqib-baspjiehz3epqc|E153]] connects invalidation, fractional Kelly, leverage restraint, and repeated-game survival.

### Portfolio and client-risk budget
- [[suoyou-jingzhi-quxian-beihou-doushi-ren-zhengtai-fenbu-de-putongren-lsnrkqhtktjlmrscow2-mt2df0yq]] uses take-profit and stop-loss rules to keep fixed-income-plus products within a holdable drawdown range.

### Gain preservation and late-cycle entries
- [[157-ruhe-daizou-niushi-de-shengli-guoshi-lory40ilowkjfe-lt-hiwjdsdbq2|Episode 157]] uses a fall from the high as a stop-profit rule for assets already judged bubbly.
- [[171-weishenme-niushi-houqi-geng-rongyi-kuiqian-banniandu-touzi-zhang-fupan-lkkafvbea1ztxdwc0eempdinc4yk|Episode 171]] says FOMO or tip-driven positions need an explicit exit rule matched to their weaker entry basis.

### Liquidity-sensitive exit
- [[vol-104-putongren-ganggu-wanquan-shengcun-zhinan-chuantai-sandian-xiaban-lmzivgi6d3guv7wajyjw9cv9zz90|Vol.104]] argues that waiting for certainty in thin Hong Kong stocks can leave the investor competing with sellers in a market with little depth.

### Precommitment versus lucky recovery
- [[ep95-fx-zhanshi-jiuliumei-mama-kuidiao-de-qian-wo-yao-qinshou-zhuan-huilai-lgdurd85j7_cbimza4cegor-jfly|EP95]] distinguishes voluntary stop loss from liquidation and says a rebound does not validate cancelling or lowering a planned stop.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

A stop can create whipsaw, lock in a temporary loss, or be executed poorly in a gap or illiquid market. Long-horizon value positions and diversified client portfolios do not share the same invalidation rule as short-term leveraged trades. None of the sources establishes a universal 20%, 30%, technical, or time-based threshold. A rule should be revised when the strategy or evidence changes, but that revision should be explicit rather than an improvised refusal to accept loss.
