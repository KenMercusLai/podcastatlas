# Daoist Observing Sameness / 观同入道

## Definition
Daoist observing sameness / 观同入道 is [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-3-rang-ni-chushi-gengshang-yige-jingjie-laozi-jiao-ni-qiangu-zhihui-llaynmqfl8wfwo7ckkacdxommu9|Hanji 456-3]]'s practice of looking past surface difference toward the common root through which things become one, shapeless, nameless, and unpossessed in the mind.

## Current Synthesis
In the episode, Laozi moves [[Confucius|孔子]] from ethical dispute into the threshold of the Way by changing how he sees. Difference remains visible, but it is only the surface. Observing sameness turns heaven, earth, people, things, sun, moon, mountains, rivers, self, non-self, right, and wrong into a field where fixed distinctions loosen and the mind can enter the beginning of awakening.

## Key Claims
- Observing sameness is presented as an entry practice for the Way, not merely an intellectual comparison.
- The source distinguishes knowing differences from knowing the root.
- The practice reduces attachment to object/self and right/wrong divisions.
- The experience is described as unity without fixed form, character, or intention.
- Confucius's response shows a felt change: fame, profit, body, and ritual ambition temporarily lose their grip.

## Evidence
Difference versus root:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-3-rang-ni-chushi-gengshang-yige-jingjie-laozi-jiao-ni-qiangu-zhihui-llaynmqfl8wfwo7ckkacdxommu9|Hanji 456-3]] has Laozi say that knowing only what makes things different sees the surface, while knowing what they share reaches the root.

Unity and loosened distinctions:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-3-rang-ni-chushi-gengshang-yige-jingjie-laozi-jiao-ni-qiangu-zhihui-llaynmqfl8wfwo7ckkacdxommu9|Hanji 456-3]] says observing sameness makes things, self and things, and right and wrong return to one body.

Confucius's transformation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-3-rang-ni-chushi-gengshang-yige-jingjie-laozi-jiao-ni-qiangu-zhihui-llaynmqfl8wfwo7ckkacdxommu9|Hanji 456-3]] depicts Confucius looking back on body, honor, name, profit, renyi, and ritual transmission as suddenly lightened or worthless.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source records a spiritual-philosophical teaching scene rather than a settled historical event. Observing sameness should not erase concrete ethical, political, or bodily differences in every context; the episode's point is about non-attachment and root perception inside a Daoist cultivation frame.
