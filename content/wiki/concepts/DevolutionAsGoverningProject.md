# Devolution As Governing Project

Devolution as governing project is the idea that moving authority away from Westminster must change how decisions, services, and accountability work, not only where officials sit. In [[a-firm-andy-what-are-new-british-pms-plans-6a5deeca76f8ee25bb4bbf9c]], [[AndyBurnham]] makes devolution his clearest priority and proposes [[Number10North]] in [[Manchester]].

The episode treats the idea with sympathy and skepticism at the same time. Burnham's [[GreaterManchester]] mayoral record gives him credibility as a politician who understands local life, but the source warns that splitting the Downing Street operation between London and Manchester does not itself solve [[UnitedKingdom]]-wide problems in growth, borrowing, migration, health access, or public services.

## Key Claims
- Devolution becomes politically credible when it is tied to visible service improvement rather than only symbolism.
- Regional offices can signal changed priorities, but they can also look like favoritism if the national logic is unclear.
- A mayoral style based on local closeness may be hard to preserve under national media, fiscal, and foreign-policy pressure.
- Devolution can collide with [[PoliticalDeliveryGap]] when voters care more about doctors, dentists, growth, and tax burden than constitutional design.
