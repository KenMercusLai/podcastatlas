# International Control of Atomic Knowledge

## Definition

International control of atomic knowledge is the proposition that nuclear science and weapons cannot remain safely governed as a permanent secret monopoly of one state and therefore require some combination of openness, shared rules, and international authority.

## Current Synthesis

[[NielsBohr]] supplies the episode's clearest version of the idea. Before the bomb had been used, he argued that atomic knowledge should eventually be opened to the world in support of a more international political order. [[JRobertOppenheimer|Oppenheimer]] responded sympathetically, while Churchill's hostility demonstrates how directly the proposal challenged wartime secrecy and national advantage.

The concept is anticipatory rather than a worked policy. It identifies a structural problem: scientific knowledge can diffuse, national monopoly may be temporary, and secrecy can intensify rivalry. The source does not establish which institution, inspection system, enforcement mechanism, or disclosure schedule could make international control effective.

## Key Claims

- Atomic knowledge has international consequences that exceed the authority of any single laboratory or state.
- A temporary technical monopoly should not be assumed to create durable strategic control.
- Openness may support trust and shared governance but can also conflict with immediate wartime security.
- Scientists can identify governance implications without possessing authority to implement them.
- Internationalist principle is incomplete without institutions, verification, enforcement, and political consent.

## Evidence

- Early proposal: [[343-oppenheimer-the-father-of-the-atom-bomb-part-1-glt9202576882]] attributes to Bohr a wartime call for eventual openness around atomic knowledge.
- Scientific reception: [[343-oppenheimer-the-father-of-the-atom-bomb-part-1-glt9202576882]] presents Oppenheimer as receptive to the internationalist argument.
- Political resistance: [[343-oppenheimer-the-father-of-the-atom-bomb-part-1-glt9202576882]] contrasts that reception with Churchill's negative response.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source offers a brief narrative of Bohr's position, not a full policy design or diplomatic record. Sharing scientific knowledge does not automatically prevent arms races, guarantee compliance, or resolve conflicts among openness, verification, deterrence, and nonproliferation.

## What Changed

- Created the concept as the governance alternative raised inside the wartime bomb project before atomic monopoly had ended.
