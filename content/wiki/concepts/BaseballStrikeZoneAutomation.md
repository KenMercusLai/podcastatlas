# Baseball Strike-Zone Automation

Baseball strike-zone automation is the translation of a human-called, rulebook strike zone into a measured system using camera tracking, player-specific calibration, and review procedure. [[tech-20260330-0330-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260330-0330-mp-tech-pod-128]] introduces the concept through [[MajorLeagueBaseball]]'s [[AutomatedBallStrikeChallengeSystem|ABS]] and [[Hawkeye]] tracking.

The key design problem is that the source's rulebook strike zone is three-dimensional while ABS is described as two-dimensional. [[NolaAgha]] says MLB tested where to place that two-dimensional zone and found the middle of the plate most accurate; the episode also reports calibration at 53.5% and 27% of player height for the top and bottom of the zone.

## Key Claims
- Automation changes the strike zone by making measurement choices explicit.
- A measured zone can increase perceived fairness when fans already see tracking graphics and expect precision.
- Precision does not eliminate judgment because the system still needs challenge rules, calibration, failure handling, and human fallback.
- Umpire-accuracy data created by the system may become a training and evaluation resource.
