## Definition

刑法轻重与执行困境 / Penal Severity and Execution Tradeoff is the governance problem in which punishment must remain proportionate, credible, and enforceable while avoiding both deterrence collapse and hidden severity created by implementation.

## Current Synthesis

The tradeoff begins with deterrence and enforceability. [[LiangTongEasternHan|梁统]] argues that repeated commutation can make grave offenses appear negotiable; [[DuLinEasternHan|杜琳]] answers that dense and severe law can turn ordinary conduct into capital exposure and induce officials to evade enforcement. “适中” names the desired middle but does not operationalize it. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]]

The sanction-ladder and implementation dimensions complicate that middle. [[CaoCao|曹操]] reportedly wants a penalty between death and flogging, while [[ChenQun|陈群]] argues that nominally lighter flogging can kill more people than mutilation. Legal rank and experienced harm can therefore diverge. Yet a middle tier is not moderate merely because it avoids death: irreversible disability, castration, stigma, and exclusion also belong in the severity calculation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]]

## Key Claims

- Deterrence can erode when serious punishment is repeatedly reduced or treated as negotiable.
- Excessively dense or severe law can produce evasion, selective enforcement, and official shielding.
- A missing sanction tier can pressure courts toward penalties experienced as either too weak or too final.
- Formal sentence rank can diverge from mortality and disability under actual execution.
- “Moderate” punishment is a multidimensional design claim, not the name of a penalty.
- Shelved debates reveal durable problems without proving that any proposed reform would have worked.

## Evidence

### Deterrence and enforceability

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] reports Liang Tong's deterrence claim and Du Lin's warning that over-severe law creates non-enforcement and mutual shielding.

### Sanction-ladder gap

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] presents Cao Cao's search for a penalty between death and flogging for serious offenses.

### Legal label versus executed harm

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] reports Chen Ji and Chen Qun's argument that abolition followed by lethal flogging could be lighter in name but heavier in result.

### Non-decision

- Both [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] end with the reported proposal shelved rather than an outcome that could validate the design.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source supplies crime rates, sentencing records, mortality comparisons, or implementation outcomes sufficient to choose an optimal sanction. Liang Tong's deterrence claim, Du Lin's enforcement warning, and Chen Ji's mortality comparison remain attributed arguments. A punishment can preserve life while imposing permanent bodily and social harm, while a formally temporary beating can kill. The robust claim is therefore the need for multidimensional, implementation-aware design, not endorsement of harshness, leniency, or restored mutilation as such.

## What Changed

- Added the missing-middle problem between flogging and death.
- Added legal-label versus executed-harm divergence to the severity framework.
- Expanded moderation beyond deterrence and enforceability to mortality, disability, stigma, and return.
