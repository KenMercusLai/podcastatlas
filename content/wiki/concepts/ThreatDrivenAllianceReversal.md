# Threat-Driven Alliance Reversal / 威胁感驱动的同盟反转

## Definition
Threat-driven alliance reversal is the change from cooperation to attack or defection when one participant interprets an ally's growing capacity, demonstrated weapon, or likely next move as a future danger to its own survival.

## Current Synthesis
The evidence contains opposite directional forms. In the Liu Bei-Lü Bu sequence, the stronger host attacks a growing subordinate ally and pushes him toward Cao Cao. At Jinyang, weaker coalition members defect from the dominant leader after his successful water attack demonstrates how he could eliminate them next. In 219, Guan Yu's rise and the unresolved Jingzhou dispute make Sun Quan more receptive to Cao-side coordination against a nominal partner. The shared mechanism is prospective threat, not necessarily present treachery: actors reprice alignment around what another participant may soon be able and motivated to do.

## Key Claims
- An alliance can reverse before present betrayal when future capacity becomes threatening.
- Growth, territorial proximity, and demonstrated methods can all create the threat signal.
- Stronger actors may attack an emerging ally, weaker allies may defect preemptively from a dominant leader, and an external rival may exploit the repricing.
- Resource inducement and threat perception can occur in sequence but should remain analytically distinct.
- Short-term security moves can worsen the strategic field by giving the target stronger partners.
- Persuasion is most effective when it articulates a threat the listener already perceives.

## Evidence
- Protector-against-growing-ally form: after Liu Bei rebuilds more than ten thousand troops, Lü Bu attacks him; Liu Bei then receives Cao Cao's protection, troops, and grain. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1009-liubei-touben-caocao-xianxie-langru-hukou-llm0ds4krkptjdtb71hbusnpzbdc|Hanji 1009]]
- Distinct resource-driven prelude: Lü Bu had earlier taken Xiapi after Yuan Shu promised grain, so the first betrayal is better classified as inducement and scarcity rather than fear of Liu Bei's growth. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-999-sanguo-luanshi-renxing-you-duome-cuiruo-li8tz30elca7bnipei6dfqdxmmeu|Hanji 999]]
- Weaker-allies-against-dominant-leader form: Zhi Yao's water attack and state-destruction remark make Wei Ju and Han Hu fear sequential elimination; Zhang Mengtan then secures their defection. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-03-weiju-hanhu-wujiandao-lkiwf0zdnylmaqjgsix-dktxea0l|Zhouji 03]]
- Rival-enabled partner reversal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1168-zuoren-tai-ao-zhen-de-bi-zhaohuo-ma-lqufnweysvpas7ahznr3tpb9ajad|Hanji 1168]] says Sima Yi and Jiang Ji identify Guan Yu's rise as a threat to Sun Quan, while Jingzhou interests and the marriage insult make Cao-side inducement actionable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
All four notes come from one podcast series and narrate motive through historical storytelling. Troop figures, private fear, gestures, dialogue, letters, promises, and insults remain source-scoped. Alliance reversal can also come from grain, office, legitimacy, territory, or immediate payoff; this concept applies only where anticipated danger is central. Jinyang combines threat perception with coercion and self-preservation, the Liu Bei case combines it with an unstable relationship, and Hanji 1168 combines Guan Yu's rise with Jingzhou rivalry and Cao-side inducement. The immediate gain from attacking a partner does not settle the longer cost of weakening a coalition against a stronger rival.

## What Changed
- Added the 219 Sun–Liu rupture as a rival-enabled response to a partner's growing threat.
- Distinguished prospective danger from territorial grievance, insult, and inducement while preserving their interaction.
- Added the short-term security versus long-term coalition-cost qualification.
