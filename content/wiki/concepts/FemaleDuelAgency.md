# Female Duel Agency / 女性决斗主体性

Female duel agency is the episode's correction to treating women only as romantic causes or protected objects inside [[HonorDuelCulture|honor duel culture]]. In [[78-xifang-juedou-shi-chujian-ba-weile-rongyu-687731000]], women also challenge, fight, choose weapons, suffer wounds, and claim reputation directly.

[[JulieDAubigny|Julie d'Aubigny / Mademoiselle de Maupin]] is the strongest source case. The episode describes her as an opera performer and fighter whose duels make female self-command visible in a domain usually narrated through male insult and male courage. The point is not that duel violence becomes admirable when performed by women, but that agency in violent cultures can be historically broader than the official gender script admits.

## Key Claims
- Women in dueling culture can be combatants, not only stakes.
- Female dueling links bodily risk, public reputation, anger, and self-defense in ways that complicate tidy protection narratives.
- The practice can express [[FemaleSelfPossession]] while still belonging to a violent and ethically unstable honor system.
- Female duel stories should not be flattened into empowerment icons; they also raise the same legal and moral problems as male duels.
- The episode connects female duels to weapon design and medical treatment, showing that gender enters practical rules rather than only symbolic meaning.
