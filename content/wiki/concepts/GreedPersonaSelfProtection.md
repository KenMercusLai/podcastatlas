# Greed Persona Self-Protection / 贪财人设式自保

Greed persona self-protection / 贪财人设式自保 is the court-survival mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-2-xuezhedian-qinchao-wujiang-jingran-ruci-gaoqingshang-lsyw3eitcnzfexl1l6vfh2gbtaoy]] attaches to [[WangJianQin|王翦]]. After [[LiXinQin|李信]] and [[MengTian|蒙恬]] fail against [[ChuState|楚国]], [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] asks Wang Jian to return and gives him 600,000 troops, effectively entrusting him with the bulk of [[QinState|秦国]]'s armed force.

That amount of command creates political danger. In the episode's reading, Wang Jian repeatedly asks for fields and houses around [[Xianyang|咸阳]] because visible private appetite is less threatening than invisible ambition. If the ruler believes the commander wants property for descendants, the commander looks easier to satisfy and less likely to turn military authority into independent political power.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu]] keeps that persona attached to the actual 600,000-person command rather than treating it only as pre-campaign preparation. [[WangJianQin|王翦]] looks politically narrow at court while exercising unusually broad battlefield discretion through [[FortifiedAttritionPatience|坚壁耗敌式耐心]] against [[XiangYan|项燕]].

The concept differs from [[SelfPreservationAsStateStrategy|以自保包装国策]]. Zhang Yi survives by making his departure look strategically useful to a successor ruler; Wang Jian survives by making continued service look personally narrow and politically harmless. It also differs from [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]]: Wang Jian is not yet exiting power, but he is managing the danger created by entering a command whose scale could make exit or return unsafe later.

## Key Claims
- A high-capacity actor may need to manage not only performance but also how dangerous the performance looks to the ruler.
- Apparent greed can function as a loyalty signal when it suggests the actor's ambition is private, bounded, and purchasable.
- Self-diminishing presentation can protect military command, but it also depends on reading the ruler's suspicion accurately.
- The mechanism is especially relevant when a commander controls enough troops to make personal loyalty and institutional command hard to distinguish.
- This kind of harmlessness is performative rather than purely moral: the source presents Wang Jian as competent precisely because he knows what role to play.
- The battlefield payoff matters: Wang Jian's harmless court persona allows him to hold a very large army long enough for fortified attrition to work.
