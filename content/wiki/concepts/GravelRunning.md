# Gravel Running / 路野跑

## Definition
Gravel running / 路野跑 is a mixed-surface running format that combines road-running accessibility with trail-running variability across pavement, gravel, and improvised urban routes.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-274-chengwei-saluomeng-1013268300]] presents Gravel as both a product category and an event-design experiment. Cushioning and rebound remain close to road-training shoes while outsole design covers more surfaces; route-free events in Shanghai and Chengdu let runners plan their own path to a target. The result joins competition, city exploration, uncertainty, and outdoor identity without requiring a remote mountain course.

## Key Claims
- Mixed-surface design lowers the boundary between road running and trail running.
- Participant-chosen routes make navigation and exploration part of the event.
- Urban deployment can make trail-like variability more accessible to city runners.
- Brand-led naming, footwear, and events make Gravel a category-building exercise as well as a sport format.

## Evidence
- **Product form:** [[vol-274-chengwei-saluomeng-1013268300]] describes road-like midsole cushioning paired with outsoles for pavement and gravel.
- **Event form:** [[vol-274-chengwei-saluomeng-1013268300]] says Salomon held route-free Gravel events in Shanghai and Chengdu.
- **Category framing:** [[vol-274-chengwei-saluomeng-1013268300]] uses “有路能跑，没路也能跑” to describe movement across fixed-road and off-road boundaries.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The evidence is a brand-supported account and does not establish independent adoption, standardized rules, participation scale, or long-term durability.
- “Gravel” may be a marketing-led category label around practices runners already perform.
- Urban flexibility does not remove traffic, route-planning, weather, surface, or safety risks.

## What Changed
