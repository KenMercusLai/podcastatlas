## Definition

Desire governance fitness / 欲望治理适任性 is the principle that someone who cannot moderate desire, appetite, or ambition is a poor candidate to govern other people, because public authority can turn private wanting into self-serving rule.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-2-yisheng-zuoguo-sanwei-zhuhou-feizi-gudai-chaomei-nuoji-de-beiqing-rensheng-lt06-as48q9qlpt_-dfdx_jsidjy|Hanji 382-2]] creates the concept through the failed Zhao succession of [[LiuNaoziWesternHan|刘淖子]]. After [[LiuPengzuZhaoKingWesternHan|刘彭祖]] dies without a settled successor, [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] asks [[NuoJiWesternHan|淖姬]]'s brother about Liu Naozi. The decisive answer is that Liu Naozi has too many desires.

The episode turns that report into a governing test. Han Wudi is said to reject Liu Naozi because a person with excessive desire is not suited to rule a kingdom and manage its people. The contrast with [[LiuChangWushiHouWesternHan|武始侯刘昌]] is important: Liu Chang has no striking merit, but also no reported fault, and that low-risk profile is acceptable.

The host then generalizes the historical judgment into a management and life principle. Desire is not treated as evil in itself; it is an original human driver. The danger comes when material desire, sexual desire, power desire, or fame desire becomes excessive enough to make a person selfish and unable to govern fairly.

## Key Claims

- Desire can motivate action, so the concept is about moderation rather than total suppression.
- Excessive desire is politically dangerous because authority can convert private appetite into public harm.
- Succession review may prefer low-fault ordinariness over visible but desire-heavy candidacy.
- Character reports can function as governance-risk screens when an heir's direct record is thin.
- The same principle can be extended cautiously from royal succession to management and life conduct.
- The concept remains source-scoped until compared with other desire, succession, and office-selection cases.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not prove Liu Naozi's actual character beyond the reported judgment. It also does not show that Liu Chang later governed well; the concept's evidence is about selection logic, not outcome validation. Desire can be productive when aligned with duty and capacity, so this concept should be read alongside [[DesireCapacityFit|欲望与能力匹配]] rather than as a blanket rejection of ambition.

## What Changed
