## Definition

Concrete reciprocity over abstract loyalty / 具体互惠高于抽象忠诚 is an ethical ordering in which obligation is grounded first in how identifiable people have treated one another, especially friendship, aid, and non-betrayal, rather than in unconditional fidelity to dynasty, ethnicity, faction, or doctrine.

## Current Synthesis

The episode develops the contrast through [[WeiXiaobao|韦小宝]] and [[ChenJinnan|陈近南]]. Chen accepts repeated injury from the Zheng family because dynastic and patron loyalty remain binding independently of personal treatment. Wei cannot understand that submission. He protects Kangxi because Kangxi is his friend and aids Tiandihui because Chen and its members also hold concrete claims on him.

This ethic explains why Wei does not experience his double service as simple ideological contradiction. He applies a single rule across camps: do not betray particular people who have treated him as friends. The rule can humanize politics by resisting bloodline and orthodoxy, but it can also become partial, unstable, and ethically narrow when friends harm outsiders or obligations collide.

The later Kangxi-focused episode makes the asymmetry inside reciprocity explicit. Wei's discretion, warnings, practical service, and refusal to harm Kangxi support exceptional trust and pardon, while his refusal to attack Tiandihui marks a limit on imperial obligation. Kangxi's affection is reciprocal but not equal: as sovereign he can define the test, commission danger, and decide whether divided loyalty will be forgiven. Concrete reciprocity can therefore alter the exercise of power without dissolving [[SovereignFriendshipHierarchy|the hierarchy that contains it]].

## Key Claims

- Concrete treatment can create stronger felt obligation than shared identity or political doctrine.
- The same reciprocity rule can sustain ties across hostile factions.
- Non-betrayal of friends provides a real moral floor without becoming a complete public ethic.
- Personal loyalty remains ranked when several friends or patrons make incompatible claims.
- Abstract loyalty can sustain sacrifice beyond self-interest but also protect abusive institutions from moral revision.
- Reciprocity becomes ethically insufficient when the welfare or rights of non-friends disappear from judgment.
- Reciprocal affection inside hierarchy can change outcomes without equalizing authority, security, or exit rights.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is extracted from two literary discussions, not offered as a complete moral theory. Reciprocity can preserve friendship and constrain betrayal, but favoritism can also shield wrongdoing, exclude strangers, and prevent impartial judgment. Under sovereignty, pardon and affection remain dependent on the ruler's choice rather than equal rights. Abstract commitments to law, rights, institutions, or public welfare may protect people who have no personal relationship with the decision-maker. Wei's conduct also includes deception, corruption, and coercion, so person-centered loyalty should not be equated with general virtue.
