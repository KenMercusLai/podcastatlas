# 新附地区宽缓治理 / Newly Attached Region Leniency

## Definition
新附地区宽缓治理 / newly attached region leniency is the transitional governance pattern in which a power applies lighter taxes, looser legal enforcement, and local discretion to recently attached or remote regions until people have settled enough for more uniform rule.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1046 presents the pattern through [[HeKui|何夔]] at [[ChangguangCommanderyLateHan|长广郡]]. Cao Cao has strict laws and urgent levies, but He Kui argues that newly attached distant regions should not be treated like already-settled commanderies. The point is pragmatic rather than purely sentimental: if the state wants durable attachment, it must first reduce fear and burden enough for local life to stabilize.

The concept sits next to [[TaxationStabilityTradeoff|税赋与地方稳定权衡]]. Yang'an shows a concrete levy being returned to avoid disturbance; Changguang supplies a broader rule for newly attached regions where full central standardization should wait.

## Key Claims
- Newly attached or remote areas may need staged integration before uniform rules become practical.
- Light tax and flexible legal handling can be tools of consolidation, not signs of weak authority.
- Local officials need room to handle minor matters according to actual conditions during the transition.
- The policy depends on timing: leniency is presented as a bridge toward later rule, not permanent exemption from central order.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is currently grounded in one episode and should not be generalized into a universal policy rule. The source treats leniency as conditional on attachment stage, remoteness, and instability; it also assumes the central power eventually wants fuller law and tax integration.
