## Definition

度田问责 / Dutian Accountability is the Eastern Han governance pattern in which court-ordered land and household registration becomes a test of local fiscal truthfulness, elite privilege, official reliability, and implementation discipline. In the current wiki it is grounded in the 39 CE degree-field episodes under [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]].

## Current Synthesis

The concept currently describes a narrow but important mechanism: a technical fiscal survey becomes politically explosive once the center asks whether local officials can truthfully measure land controlled by powerful families without damaging ordinary people. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] supplies the implementation-abuse prelude: Liu Xiu orders careful land checks, but some inspectors and administrators disturb commoners, measure houses and villages, and favor local magnates.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-717-13-ge-zi-yinfa-de-diwang-zhinu-liz3jsbvw3snfapzsrqw917fqv7v|Hanji 717]] then shows the diagnostic and punishment mechanism through the Chenliu note that compares Yingchuan and Hongnong with Henan and Nanyang, [[EmperorMingOfHan|刘阳]]'s explanation of protected commandery interests, Liu Xiu's investigation of two-thousand-stone officials, and [[OuyangXiEasternHan|欧阳歙]]'s punishment.

The strongest current judgment is that degree-field accountability is less about measurement alone than about whether the court can identify where fiscal data has been bent by proximity to power. A good report is therefore not only accurate land data; it is evidence that local officials did not treat imperial-city ministers, imperial-hometown kin, or regional elites as untouchable.

## Key Claims

- Degree-field registration turns land measurement into a test of court capacity because hidden land directly affects taxation, corvee, and elite burden-sharing.
- The first implementation layer can harm commoners when officials turn measurement into disturbance while shielding powerful families.
- The Chenliu note shows that some commanderies could be used as comparison standards while Henan and Nanyang were suspect because of powerful court and kinship interests.
- Liu Yang's childhood interpretation matters because it identifies the political economy behind the paperwork before coercive questioning confirms it.
- Liu Xiu's response links investigation of records to inspection of senior local officials, especially two-thousand-stone administrators.
- Ouyang Xi's imprisonment and death show that a high-status classical official could become a punishment signal when degree-field falsification was tied to embezzlement.
- The episode qualifies later Guangwu-era fiscal punishments by showing the diagnostic stage before the broader 40 CE executions.

- Implementation-abuse prelude: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-716-luanshi-zhizhong-lvfa-you-duo-zhongyao-lqxuinbrl-rop8d9yxgwx9z4fr4i|Hanji 716]] says land checks lead some officials to disturb commoners, measure houses and villages, and favor local magnates.
- Data as political signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-717-13-ge-zi-yinfa-de-diwang-zhinu-liz3jsbvw3snfapzsrqw917fqv7v|Hanji 717]] centers the policy on reports from commanderies and the tiny note distinguishing Yingchuan/Hongnong from Henan/Nanyang.
- Protected comparison zones: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-717-13-ge-zi-yinfa-de-diwang-zhinu-liz3jsbvw3snfapzsrqw917fqv7v|Hanji 717]] has Liu Yang explain that Henan contains many close ministers and Nanyang contains many imperial hometown relatives, making both unreliable as neutral benchmarks.
- Official accountability: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-717-13-ge-zi-yinfa-de-diwang-zhinu-liz3jsbvw3snfapzsrqw917fqv7v|Hanji 717]] says Liu Xiu sends inspectors to examine two-thousand-stone officials for favoritism and hidden facts in degree-field work.
- High-status punishment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-717-13-ge-zi-yinfa-de-diwang-zhinu-liz3jsbvw3snfapzsrqw917fqv7v|Hanji 717]] records Ouyang Xi's imprisonment for inaccurate Runan degree-field work and major embezzlement, followed by death in prison despite petitions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on two adjacent source notes, so it should not yet be treated as a complete theory of Eastern Han land registration. Hanji 716 supports the implementation-abuse prelude but does not yet show Liu Xiu's corrective response; Hanji 717 supplies the later diagnostic and punishment layer. The sources strongly support the mechanism of accountability and protected comparison zones, but they do not independently prove the scale of hidden land in Henan or Nanyang. The host's inference that Liu Yang's answer worsened [[LiuQiangDonghaiKing|Liu Qiang]]'s succession position remains adjacent political interpretation rather than a core claim of this concept.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 716 as the implementation-abuse prelude: land checks disturb commoners and expose local-official favoritism before the Chenliu note.
- Preserved Hanji 717 as the diagnostic and punishment layer for protected comparison zones and Ouyang Xi's case.
- Reframed the concept from measurement accuracy alone to accuracy plus disciplined local execution.
