## Definition

Prestige-capacity gap names the failure to convert lineage, fame, learning, moral reputation, or social access into the practical abilities required by a political situation.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence develops the concept through [[KongRong|孔融]] in two settings. In [[BeihaiCommandery|北海郡]], lineage from [[Confucius|孔子]], literary fame, respect for [[ZhengXuan|郑玄]], and courteous reception of talent do not become troops, grain, alliance judgment, or tolerance for corrective advice. Under [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s concentrated power, the same cultural confidence does not become calibrated dissent or self-preservation: the source presents repeated satire as increasingly direct attacks on policy, military standing, household conduct, and political legitimacy.

The gap is not hypocrisy and does not imply that prestige has no value. It identifies a conversion failure. Cultural authority can legitimate choices and attract attention, but it becomes brittle when the holder cannot use advice, read coercive asymmetry, or choose forms of speech proportionate to the field.

## Key Claims

- Cultural prestige is a political resource, but it is not the same as state capacity.
- Talent reception fails if advisers are honored socially but excluded from real decision-making.
- Killing corrective advice turns a private capacity gap into an organizational warning signal.
- The gap also appears when moral or literary authority is mistaken for protection against concentrated coercive power.
- Public truth-telling can be substantively valuable while still revealing weak situational judgment in timing, framing, or risk control.
- The gap becomes clearest under pressure, when reputation can no longer substitute for troops, grain, allies, execution, or survival calibration.

## Evidence

- Governing conversion failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1007-cong-shentong-dao-fuwang-kongrong-zhenshi-mianmu-puguang-lkiljj4yvl5fhslrc9gcxudxxklt|Hanji 1007]] contrasts Kong Rong's lineage, literary standing, and ritual respect with shortages, isolation, the killing of [[ZuoChengzu|左承祖]], [[LiuYixun|刘义逊]]'s departure, and defeat by [[YuanTan|袁谭]].
- Power-reading and speech failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1089-shenwei-kongzi-ershishisun-kongrong-ruhe-chunu-caocao-lqz6drrrl4l4schcmtdkg2ofvo6i|Hanji 1089]] presents the liquor-ban retort, campaign mockery, and fabricated King Wu-Daji analogy as evidence that Kong Rong's learning and eloquence do not become calibrated action under Cao Cao's lethal dominance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not be used to collapse moral courage into incompetence or to justify repression after the fact. Hanji 1007 grounds a specific administrative and advice-tolerance failure; Hanji 1089 offers a stronger host judgment about arrogance and survival wisdom. The latter's exact anecdotes, wording, corrupt transcript names, and claims about motive remain source-scoped. A critic may be politically imprudent and still be correct about abuses, while a coercive ruler's ability to punish does not make that punishment legitimate.

## What Changed

- Hanji 1089 extends the concept from governing incapacity to failure to calibrate speech and cultural authority under coercive asymmetry.
- The synthesis now distinguishes the practical judgment question from the moral legitimacy of either criticism or repression.
