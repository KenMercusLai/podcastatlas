# Manufacturing Sales Shift / 制造业销售重心转移

## Definition

Manufacturing sales shift is the move from a market where expanding production is the central bottleneck toward one where recognition, product definition, channels, inventory turnover, trust, and demand creation become equally decisive.

## Current Synthesis

Factory successors make the shift visible when they enter through brand work, livestreaming, ecommerce, personal IP, design, and overseas sales. Their work is not ornamental: light-asset brands can outsource production while competing for attention and channels, leaving old manufacturers with execution strength but weak consumer control. [[ep255-chang-erdai-de-jicheng-zhi-zhan-nianqingren-jieban-hui-chongsu-qiye-ma-likvzmpgwxnetpaezxclsx4s-hkw]]

The longer Putian history reinforces the distinction: [[LuoChiFootwear|洛弛]] and [[WoteShoes|沃特鞋企]] could manufacture serious products yet struggled with patents, trademark protection, procurement scale, dealers, stores, marketing, receivables, financing, and expansion discipline. OEM/ODM remained the more durable reported revenue base, showing that “make a brand” is a capability and capital transformation, not a logo change. [[no-225-putian-xie-wangshi-gkwrijeowkkqa6hffgt1ih2w]]

## Key Claims

- Production competence remains necessary but is often insufficient for growth or survival.
- Ecommerce, livestreaming, exhibitions, personal accounts, overseas platforms, and dealer management are operating systems for demand, not merely promotion.
- Brand work requires product definition, intellectual property, procurement, channel control, inventory discipline, service, and working capital.
- Light-asset brands can capture attention and margin while outsourcing the factory layer.
- OEM strength can remain a rational moat; the shift does not imply every manufacturer should force a consumer-brand transition.
- Second-generation successors often enter through the new capability layers because they can add value without immediately replacing founder authority.

## Evidence

- **Successor entry routes:** [[ep255-chang-erdai-de-jicheng-zhi-zhan-nianqingren-jieban-hui-chongsu-qiye-ma-likvzmpgwxnetpaezxclsx4s-hkw]] presents ecommerce, short video, live selling, design, IP, and overseas sales as practical work for factory successors.
- **LuoChi capability costs:** [[no-225-putian-xie-wangshi-gkwrijeowkkqa6hffgt1ih2w]] links original-brand operation to small-order production, specialized materials, patents, trademarks, dealers, stores, and working capital.
- **Wote capital and channel risk:** [[no-225-putian-xie-wangshi-gkwrijeowkkqa6hffgt1ih2w]] links brand expansion to listing investment, dealer debt, bank withdrawal, shutdown, and later OEM retrenchment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The shift does not make manufacturing knowledge obsolete; quality, delivery, process, and cost remain the base of every route described.
- Successful OEM specialists may create more defensible value than weak consumer brands.
- Company outcomes, revenue shares, and causal accounts remain bounded to the episodes.

## What Changed

- Added patents, procurement scale, working capital, dealer receivables, and expansion discipline to the brand-capability problem.
- Added two cases where manufacturing strength did not produce a brand-led revenue model and OEM/ODM remained central.
