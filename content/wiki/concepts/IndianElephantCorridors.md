# Indian Elephant Corridors

## Definition
Indian elephant corridors are the shrinking habitat passages that let elephants move through fragmented forests, farms, villages, roads, railways, power lines, and mining zones in [[India]].

## Current Synthesis
In [[chainsaw-sputtering-mileis-experiment-falters-6aa1299999455f9a93f7de9b]], elephant corridors are a conservation problem where cultural reverence does not guarantee habitat protection. The source connects elephants' symbolic importance through [[Ganesha]] and ancient statecraft to a practical decline in corridors, rising human conflict, and the ecological work elephants do by dispersing seeds, shaping forests, fertilizing soil, opening canopies, sustaining insects, and keeping wetlands alive.

## Key Claims
- Reverence for elephants can coexist with habitat fragmentation and conflict.
- Infrastructure and extractive development can turn movement corridors into danger zones for both elephants and villagers.
- Corridor protection is ecosystem maintenance, not only species preservation.
- Legal or anti-poaching progress does not solve land-use pressure by itself.

## Evidence
- Population and habitat pressure: [[chainsaw-sputtering-mileis-experiment-falters-6aa1299999455f9a93f7de9b]] cites a [[WildlifeInstituteOfIndia]] survey showing fewer elephants than in 2017 and describes shrinking corridors.
- Conflict mechanics: [[chainsaw-sputtering-mileis-experiment-falters-6aa1299999455f9a93f7de9b]] says roads, railways, power lines, mining, farms, and villages push elephants into human contact.
- Ecological role: [[chainsaw-sputtering-mileis-experiment-falters-6aa1299999455f9a93f7de9b]] lists seed dispersal, forest shaping, soil fertilization, canopy opening, dung-supported insects, and wetland maintenance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source notes that poaching has fallen since the 1980s and that legal protections and anti-poaching institutions exist. Its criticism is that corridor protection and land-use conflict remain insufficient.
