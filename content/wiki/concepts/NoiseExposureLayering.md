# Noise Exposure Layering

## Definition
Noise exposure layering is the episode's frame for hearing risk that accumulates when environmental noise, headphone output, long duration, and user compensation by raising volume stack on top of one another.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats headphone safety as a combined exposure problem. In quiet conditions, a user may listen at a moderate volume. In subway, street, machinery, motorcycle, or other noisy settings, the same user may raise headphone volume to overcome background noise, turning environmental sound and earphone output into a layered stimulus.

The 60-60-60 rule is used as a rough operational boundary rather than a precise scientific law: limit continuous headphone use, keep device volume below a moderate fraction of maximum, and avoid adding headphone sound on top of already loud surroundings. The episode also extends the idea beyond headphones by noting that persistent background noise around housing or offices can matter for long-term hearing exposure.

## Key Claims
- Hearing exposure is shaped by the total acoustic field around the listener, not only by the headphone setting.
- Noisy settings can make safer-looking headphones riskier because users raise volume when speech or audio becomes hard to hear.
- Semi-in-ear, open-ear, and bone-conduction devices can preserve ventilation or environmental awareness but may invite higher volume in loud surroundings.
- The episode's 60-60-60 rule combines continuous-use time, percentage of maximum volume, and environmental noise as a practical safety heuristic.
- Sudden loud peaks matter because short high-energy events can produce different risk from steady moderate listening.
- Long-term background noise near homes, offices, transport, machinery, or roads can become a health-context issue, not only an annoyance.

## Evidence
- Volume compensation: [[vol-208-erji-ban-yongjiu-shengcun-zhinan-meitian-ting-jian-boke-nide-erduo-li-gongshang-haiyou-duoyuan-ljv6vmb1upj03uqjuglz6gjni-xb]] explains that street and subway noise can make semi-in-ear or open devices prompt users to raise volume.
- 60-60-60 heuristic: [[vol-208-erji-ban-yongjiu-shengcun-zhinan-meitian-ting-jian-boke-nide-erduo-li-gongshang-haiyou-duoyuan-ljv6vmb1upj03uqjuglz6gjni-xb]] names continuous use under 60 minutes, volume under 60 percent, and environmental noise around 60 dB as a practical boundary.
- Sudden peaks and source distance: [[vol-208-erji-ban-yongjiu-shengcun-zhinan-meitian-ting-jian-boke-nide-erduo-li-gongshang-haiyou-duoyuan-ljv6vmb1upj03uqjuglz6gjni-xb]] compares remote loud events with close headphone peaks and emphasizes concentrated energy at the ear.
- Environmental examples: [[vol-208-erji-ban-yongjiu-shengcun-zhinan-meitian-ting-jian-boke-nide-erduo-li-gongshang-haiyou-duoyuan-ljv6vmb1upj03uqjuglz6gjni-xb]] mentions machinery rooms, subway tunnel noise, motorcycle settings, high-road-adjacent housing, rental decisions, offices, and night-time sound checks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a formal occupational-noise standard, calibrated measurement method, or building-acoustics guide. The 60-60-60 rule is presented as a practical boundary with imperfect scientific precision, so it should not replace professional audiology, workplace-safety, or environmental-noise assessment.
