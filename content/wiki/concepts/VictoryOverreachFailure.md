# Victory Overreach Failure / 数胜必亡式胜利反噬

Victory overreach failure / 数胜必亡式胜利反噬 is the ruler-failure pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-69-bikan-gudai-diwang-qipa-sifa-da-pandian-lukvjsktg-pgfjhizvypwmdmw-13]] draws from [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] after the destruction of [[SongState|宋国]]. The episode quotes the older warning associated with [[LiKe|李克]] and [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]: repeated war exhausts the people, repeated victory makes the ruler arrogant, and the combination can destroy the state.

The source makes Qi Min Wang the active case. His victory over Song gives Qi extraordinary prestige and territory, but he treats that moment as permission to attack [[ChuState|楚国]], pressure the Three Jin, swallow East and West Zhou, and imagine himself as Son of Heaven. The strategic result is isolation: Qin attacks Qi, and Yan finds other states ready to join revenge because Qi has become intolerable.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-70-xihuan-huayang-zuosi-de-qiguo-junzhu-luuyasp5-nhnikdcozzaueosb-md]] shows the terminal phase of the same pattern. [[LeYi|乐毅]] defeats Qi in coalition, takes [[Linzi|临淄]], and then pursues because he believes Qi Min Wang's internal unpopularity has made the state brittle. Qi Min Wang's failed refuge in Wey, Zou, and Lu, followed by [[ZhuoChi|卓齿]]'s seizure and killing of him, makes overreach end as personal exposure as well as state defeat.

This is related to [[PostVictoryOppressionRisk|post-victory oppression risk]], but it is more statecraft-specific. The problem is not only that a victorious side mistreats others; it is that victory changes the ruler's risk perception and destroys the feedback, popular support, and alliance environment that made survival possible.

## Key Claims
- Victory can be destabilizing when it inflates ruler ambition faster than state capacity.
- Repeated military success can hide exhaustion among subjects and officials.
- Overreach after victory can turn former victims, rivals, and anxious neighbors into a common-enemy coalition.
- Killing remonstrants converts strategic overconfidence into [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|court feedback collapse]].
- The strongest moment in a ruler's reputation can become the opening of decline if it removes restraint.
- Once overreach destroys social and diplomatic protection, military defeat can become a refuge failure and bodily vulnerability for the ruler.
