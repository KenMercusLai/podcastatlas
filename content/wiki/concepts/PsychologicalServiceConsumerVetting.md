# Psychological Service Consumer Vetting / 心理服务消费甄别

## Definition
Psychological service consumer vetting / 心理服务消费甄别 is a layered way to assess psychology courses, counseling studios, online consultations, and related services through need, provider competence, training, supervision, method, accountability, emotional effects, promises, and referral capacity.

## Current Synthesis
The framework starts by separating a sales diagnosis from a clinical or personal need. A platform, influencer, or course can name a familiar feeling without proving disorder, causation, or treatment need. Persistent symptoms, functional impairment, physical contributors, crisis risk, and uncertainty may require qualified assessment rather than a purchase.

Provider review is similarly layered. Relevant education, sustained systematic training, supervised practice, experience, institutional setting, transparent fees, and a defined scope are stronger signals than celebrity, brand familiarity, warmth, or years in business alone. Trial sessions can help consumers inspect both the reasoning and the emotional direction: education that increases understanding differs from content designed to intensify hatred, grievance, fear, conflict, or a victim identity before selling rapid relief.

## Key Claims
- Decide what kind of help is needed before judging which seller or service is attractive.
- Evaluate relevant education, systematic training, experience, supervision, institutional accountability, fees, method, and referral practice together.
- Celebrity, brand recognition, longevity, testimonials, and a comfortable interaction are incomplete proxies for competence or effectiveness.
- Trial content should be judged partly by whether it builds understanding and agency or manufactures emotional destabilization and purchase urgency.
- Guarantees of fast personality change, effortless healing, universal efficacy, or permanent relief deserve heightened skepticism.
- Online and telephone services can widen access, but modality convenience does not remove competence, privacy, safety, or escalation requirements.
- The minimum ethical boundary is non-harm, including avoidance of delayed care, false labeling, financial extraction, and secondary psychological injury.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework does not establish that every online course, private studio, supportive listener, famous teacher, or branded institution is unsafe. Nor do credentials alone prove fit or effectiveness. The source supplies practical heuristics, not a complete licensing database, treatment-outcome comparison, legal standard, diagnostic protocol, or individualized referral decision.

## What Changed
- Created a layered consumer-vetting framework that joins need, competence, emotional effects, claims, modality, and escalation.
