## Definition

太学 / Taixue is the imperial classical-learning institution and late-Han scholar-student field that can function as education infrastructure, ritual site, official-selection pathway, textual standardization site, and public-opinion actor.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 810 now supplies the earliest current Taixue layer. It says Emperor An's neglect of classical learning leaves doctors no longer lecturing, students gone, buildings decayed, and the grounds used for vegetable plots, woodcutting, and grazing. Shun approves repairs, and the episode records a large physical rebuilding of Taixue. This makes institutional maintenance a prerequisite for the later selection and student-action layers.

Hanji 827 supplies the next current layer. In 146 CE the court expands Taixue by having commanderies and kingdoms recommend Mingjing students, allowing officials to send qualified sons, keeping them under a one-year study and examination cycle, and routing successful students toward office. Taixue is therefore an education-to-office pipeline only after the earlier layer has shown how badly the institution can decay.

The later sources broaden the institution's public role. Hanji 839 shows [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]] leading thousands of Taixue students to petition for [[ZhuMuLateHan|朱穆]]. Hanji 905 makes Taixue a public site of classical text standardization through the [[XipingStoneClassics|熹平石经]]. Hanji 908 treats imperial attendance at Taixue ritual as part of [[LiAsPoliticalOrder|礼制政治秩序]]. Hanji 910 and Hanji 911 then make Taixue the incumbent classical education-to-office field whose students and associated士人 resent [[HongduMenxue|鸿都门学]] because Hongdu creates a faster, ruler-favored appointment channel.

The current synthesis is mixed. Taixue can decay when rulers neglect its intellectual and physical maintenance; it can be repaired into a selection pipeline; it can defend public law and classical authority; and it can become a status field defending access to office. Those functions overlap but should not be treated as the same political role.

## Key Claims

- Taixue can physically and intellectually decay when imperial attention to classical learning collapses.
- Repairing buildings, lectures, and student presence is a precondition for later education-to-office use.
- Taixue can be expanded as an official-selection pipeline linking local recommendation, classical study, examination, and office.
- Taixue students can act collectively as a palace-facing public feedback force.
- Public classical text standardization makes Taixue a site where official knowledge can be checked.
- Ritual attendance at Taixue is treated as part of imperial role order, not merely education ceremony.
- Hongdu conflict shows Taixue as an incumbent status and appointment channel with defended interests.

## Evidence

- Institutional decay and repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-810-ta-pinge-manmen-beizhan-weihe-ye-yao-shaqi-lo8vw0jj0d6ir7yqbuh-5p9orzsc|Hanji 810]] says Emperor An's neglect leads to stopped lectures, departing students, decayed buildings, and non-educational use of the grounds before Shun approves rebuilding.
- Education-to-office pipeline: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] describes Taixue expansion through Mingjing recommendations, sons of officials, one-year study, examination, and post-study office placement.
- Collective petition role: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]] says Liu Tao leads thousands of Taixue students to defend Zhu Mu at the palace gate.
- Text standardization role: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-905-fugui-ruhe-xianzhong-qiu-lklxx5nquka2mtbllxivk0a7b-fu|Hanji 905]] says the Xiping Stone Classics are erected outside Taixue so students and copyists can compare classical texts against an official standard.
- Ritual-order role: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-908-nijing-chu-qiangzhe-xialu-chu-yongzhe-gudu-chu-zhizhe-lqih1dradhzi0mipxsaiedd52dlj|Hanji 908]] says Cai Yong criticizes neglect of the Taixue elder-honoring ceremony and Emperor Ling partially accepts by attending Taixue again.
- Incumbent selection-field role: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-910-shehui-shengcun-wo-quan-ni-duodian-chengfu-lp2fvuyb8ampzoeyr7znztnr0fgk|Hanji 910]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-911-gudai-gongwuyuan-ye-neng-bao-fenpei-ls3pxu9xlpuulsdailx8tcoylv9e|Hanji 911]] show Taixue and scholar-official networks opposing Hongdu because curriculum difference becomes appointment-channel competition.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Taixue should not be flattened into one political function. Hanji 810 presents an institution that can be neglected and physically decay; Hanji 827 presents a state-managed education and appointment pipeline; Hanji 839's student petition defends law and a punished official; and Hanji 910-911's Hongdu conflict also involves status and appointment scarcity. A successful pardon in Hanji 839 does not prove that Taixue can force systemic reform, and later qingyi and Party Prohibition material shows that scholar-student reputation pressure can itself become politically targetable.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 810 as the earliest current evidence for Taixue decay and repair under Shun.
- Reframed Hanji 827 as the next institutionalized selection-pipeline layer rather than the earliest Taixue layer overall.
- Preserved Hanji 839 as the first current student-petition layer.
- Preserved the later Xiping Stone Classics, Taixue ritual, and Hongdu conflict layers as distinct public and appointment-field functions.
