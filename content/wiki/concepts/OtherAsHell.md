# 他人即地狱 / Other As Hell

## Definition
他人即地狱 / Other As Hell is the condition in which other people become a source of spiritual confinement because damaged relations, mutual exposure, and dependence on external judgment trap the self inside someone else's gaze.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-395-2-sanren-beihou-de-renxing-gushi-lnxghfd6esj7jbir-m6qflth2ckp]] builds the concept through [[JeanPaulSartre|Jean-Paul Sartre / 萨特]]'s [[NoExit|No Exit / 《禁闭》]]. The episode stresses that the play's hell has no physical torture equipment. Garcin, Ines, and Estelle are punished by being unable to escape one another: each needs recognition, hides shame, wants someone who does not want them in the same way, and is continually exposed by the others' language and gaze.

The concept is not anti-social misanthropy. The episode explicitly narrows Sartre's phrase: other people become hell when relations have deteriorated and when a person relies too heavily on others' judgment. Its practical edge is freedom. A person cannot abolish the existence of other people, but can change conduct, reduce judgment-dependence, and refuse to remain wholly defined by a hostile relational room.

## Key Claims
- Other people become hell through a damaged relational structure, not merely through their existence.
- Mutual gaze, language, desire, and exposure can function as punishment without physical force.
- Dependence on other people's judgment turns social perception into confinement.
- The phrase should be read through the dramatic situation of [[NoExit|《禁闭》]] before becoming a free-floating slogan.
- Freedom requires changed behavior, not only private insight about the source of suffering.

## Evidence
- Relational hell: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-395-2-sanren-beihou-de-renxing-gushi-lnxghfd6esj7jbir-m6qflth2ckp]] says the room has no instruments or executioner, yet the three characters create mental and verbal torment for one another.
- Mutual exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-395-2-sanren-beihou-de-renxing-gushi-lnxghfd6esj7jbir-m6qflth2ckp]] summarizes how Garcin, Ines, and Estelle first conceal their lives and then reveal, attack, and judge one another.
- Judgment dependence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-395-2-sanren-beihou-de-renxing-gushi-lnxghfd6esj7jbir-m6qflth2ckp]] reports Sartre's clarification that many people live in hell because they rely too much on others' judgments.
- Freedom and behavior: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-395-2-sanren-beihou-de-renxing-gushi-lnxghfd6esj7jbir-m6qflth2ckp]] closes by connecting the play to freedom and the need to change one's own actions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not claim that all relationships are hellish or that other people should be avoided. It also does not offer a full scholarly reading of Sartre's philosophy; it is a short podcast explanation of one dramatic idea. The episode's date claim for the play is left source-scoped because the broader bibliography may differ.
