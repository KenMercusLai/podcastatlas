# Disaster Recovery Privatization

[[DisasterRecoveryPrivatization]] is the pattern where rebuilding after disaster becomes an opportunity to change property rules, development rights, or asset ownership. [[sand-heists-and-property-rights-in-the-caribbean-summer-school]] adds the concept through [[Barbuda]] after [[HurricaneIrma]].

In the source, [[GastonBrowne]] presents individual title as a recovery tool because households could use land as collateral. Barbudan critics see the same move as a transfer of collective island value: residents would get limited title to occupied plots while much of the remaining island could become easier for outside investors to develop.

## Key Claims
- Disaster can create a policy window where urgent rebuilding makes institutional change easier to justify.
- Privatization can be framed as empowerment through collateral while also reducing collective control over land.
- The conflict depends on who receives the residual development value, not only on whether homes can be rebuilt.
- Environmental review and public consent become especially important when the asset is both shelter and shared ecological resource.
