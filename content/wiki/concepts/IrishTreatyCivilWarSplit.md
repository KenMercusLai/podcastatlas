# Irish Treaty-Civil War Split

## Definition

The Irish treaty-civil war split is the transformation of a shared independence struggle into conflict over whether partial sovereignty, a royal oath, and partition were an acceptable settlement.

## Current Synthesis

The episode treats the Anglo-Irish Treaty as both a major transfer of power and an unstable compromise. Dominion status offered an Irish Free State but retained a crown relationship, while Northern Ireland could and did remain outside it. A seven-vote ratification margin divided Sinn Féin and the IRA; anti-treaty occupation of the Four Courts sought to reopen confrontation with Britain, but pressure after Henry Wilson's assassination helped produce a pro-treaty assault and civil war instead.

## Key Claims

- Agreements can end an interstate or anti-imperial war while relocating conflict inside the independence movement.
- Symbolic sovereignty questions such as an oath can carry practical coalition-breaking force.
- Partition makes the territorial definition of the new state part of the settlement's legitimacy crisis.
- Spoiler tactics intended to restart an external war can instead trigger coercion by the emerging state.
- Narrow formal ratification does not by itself create enough shared legitimacy to prevent civil conflict.

## Evidence

- **Compromise terms:** [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]] identifies dominion status, the oath, and Northern Ireland's opt-out as the settlement's central fault lines.
- **Movement fracture:** The source connects the narrow Dáil vote and de Valera's opposition to pro- and anti-treaty divisions in Sinn Féin and the IRA. [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]]
- **Coercive transition:** The Four Courts occupation, Wilson assassination, British pressure, pro-treaty assault, and subsequent guerrilla war form the episode's escalation chain. [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode is a compressed overview rather than a full causal history of the Irish Civil War. It does not establish that the treaty made war inevitable, and its account of intentions, British pressure, Belfast killings, house burnings, and casualty totals remains source-scoped. Irish political positions were more varied than a simple pro-/anti-treaty binary.

## What Changed

- Created a concept for the mechanism by which negotiated decolonization divided a revolutionary coalition and empowered a new state to coerce former allies.
