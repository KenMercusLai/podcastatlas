## Definition
Government model availability leverage is the ability of a state to interrupt or condition access to an AI model through export controls, licensing, procurement rules, or related restrictions.

## Current Synthesis
Model access can become a regulatory enforcement surface in its own right. The episode says earlier export controls made Anthropic's model unavailable online for more than two weeks to individuals, companies, and international allies. That turns a legal or political dispute into immediate operational exposure for both the provider and downstream users.

The same leverage can serve legitimate security or foreign-policy aims, so an interruption alone does not establish retaliation. Durable governance requires a clear legal basis, bounded scope, notice, review, and continuity planning for customers whose workflows depend on the model.

## Key Claims
- Access restrictions can impose business consequences before a final court or regulatory outcome.
- Effects can propagate beyond the target company to domestic customers, contractors, and allies.
- Model dependence makes continuity planning a customer-governance issue, not only a vendor-policy issue.
- Export controls can serve legitimate state aims while still creating due-process and proportionality questions.
- Availability events should be documented by scope, duration, legal basis, and affected user class.

- **Cross-border reach:** [[tech-20261030-1030-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261030-1030-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports effects on individuals, companies, and international allies rather than only a narrow government customer.
- **Continuing exposure:** [[tech-20261030-1030-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261030-1030-mp-tech-pod-128]] says renewed restrictions remain a disclosed risk during Anthropic's IPO process.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied summary does not include the export order, legal authority, affected jurisdictions, technical implementation, provider notice, or outage record. Its characterization of a governmental “kill switch” is interpretive. The evidence therefore establishes a reported access interruption, not the motive, legality, exact scope, or probability of recurrence.
