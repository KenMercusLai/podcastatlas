# Weaponized Reporting / 举报武器化

## Definition
Weaponized reporting is the use of complaint, moderation, or enforcement channels primarily to impose costs on disliked people, speech, or communities rather than to remedy a clearly evidenced rule violation.

## Current Synthesis
Reporting remains necessary for unlawful or harmful conduct. EP113 identifies a failure mode in which groups coordinate complaints, search old material, attach a discrediting label, and translate dislike into rule language. The mechanism exploits an asymmetry: filing or multiplying complaints can be cheap, while defending context, restoring access, or reviewing evidence can be costly.

The concept does not imply that every mass report is abusive or that contested content is lawful. The diagnostic question is whether evidence, proportionality, and a remedy connected to the alleged harm remain central, or whether enforcement has become a means of factional exclusion and collective punishment.

## Key Claims
- Legitimate reporting and factional punishment can use the same technical channel.
- Coordination can magnify complaint volume without improving evidentiary quality.
- Labels and decontextualized old statements can be assembled into a post-hoc enforcement rationale.
- Community infrastructure can bear costs far beyond the originating speaker or work.
- Procedural legitimacy requires evidence, proportionality, consistent rules, and some route for review or appeal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not independently verify the AO3 reporting chain, adjudicate the disputed work under applicable law or platform rules, or provide complaint and enforcement records. High complaint volume can sometimes reflect genuine widespread harm; motive cannot be inferred from scale alone.

## What Changed
- Created a governance distinction between evidence-led reporting and reporting used for factional exclusion.
