## Definition
Heir / claimant support threat perception is the succession-politics pattern in which a ruler interprets visible or rumored support for an heir, prince, or alternative claimant as a rival political center, then treats defense, proximity, or possible elevation of that figure as a danger to the ruler's own authority.

## Current Synthesis
[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] now supplies the pre-heir variant under [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]]. [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] delays returning power and retains [[LiuYiPingyuanWangLateHan|Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi]] in Luoyang after summoning royal sons. [[WangShengPalaceActorLateHan|Wang Sheng]] fears An may be deposed, and Wang Sheng, [[LiRunLateHan|Li Run]], and [[JiangJingLateHan|Jiang Jing]] slander Deng around that danger. After Deng dies, the alleged Liu Yi installation plan helps trigger the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]] purge, while Liu Yi survives by accepting demotion and withdrawing from public contact.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] gives the later crown-prince version. Palace accusation starts with Wang Sheng, Fan Feng, Yan Hou, Wang Nan, and Bing Ji, but the political meaning changes when ministers defend [[EmperorShunOfHan|Liu Bao]]. [[LaiLiLateHan|Lai Li]], [[HuanYanLateHan|Huan Yan]], and [[ZhangHaoRemonstratorLateHan|Zhang Hao]] argue that a ten-year-old should not bear full responsibility; after Liu Bao is demoted, more officials remonstrate at Hongdu Gate. The host reads this support as what frightens An: a child who can attract that much official sympathy now may become uncontrollable later.

In both variants, the ruler's answer converts succession anxiety into punishment. In Hanji 794, the feared claimant's surrounding network becomes the target and Liu Yi disappears into cautious withdrawal; in Hanji 801, loyal remonstrance on behalf of the heir is recoded as hidden motive and Lai Li is punished through office, family, and fief sanctions.

## Key Claims
- Succession threat can form around a potential claimant before that person has active political agency.
- Palace slander may start the case, but visible or rumored support makes the feared claimant look like a political center.
- Age, innocence, or non-action arguments lose force when the ruler's core concern shifts from guilt to future controllability.
- Threat perception recodes loyal remonstrance, proximity, or candidate retention as hidden factional motive.
- Punishing supporters or surrounding networks can make displacement more irreversible while exposing the ruler's own insecurity.

- Purge consequence and withdrawal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] says the alleged Liu Yi installation plan helps trigger Deng-family punishment and that Liu Yi survives by closing his doors after demotion.
- Palace-accusation trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says Wang Sheng, Fan Feng, Yan Hou, and others use Wang Nan and Bing Ji to build accusations against Liu Bao.
- Initial defense: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says Lai Li, Huan Yan, and Zhang Hao argue that Liu Bao is too young and uninformed to bear the alleged guilt.
- Broad support signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says more than ten officials gather at Hongdu Gate to say Liu Bao is blameless after his demotion.
- Criminalizing response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says the edict accuses the remonstrants of hidden motives and threatens punishment, after which Lai Li's family, office, and fief position are hit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The motive readings remain source-scoped. Hanji 794 does not prove that Deng actually intended to install Liu Yi, and Hanji 801 does not prove that Liu Bao had active factional intent. The concept should not erase palace slander, eunuch and empress interests, court compliance, or the difference between a collateral claimant and a crown prince; it names the shared moment when succession support or even candidate availability becomes a ruler-security problem.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 794 as an earlier alternative-claimant variant before Liu Bao's crown-prince deposition.
- Broadened the concept from visible support for a current heir to threat perception around heirs and collateral claimants.
- Preserved Hanji 801 as the core crown-prince case where loyal remonstrance is criminalized.
