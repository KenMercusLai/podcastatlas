## Definition

Heir support threat perception / 储君拥戴威胁感 is the succession-politics pattern in which a ruler interprets visible support for an heir as a rival political center, then treats defense of the heir as a danger to the ruler's own authority rather than as ordinary loyal correction.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] grounds the pattern in [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]]'s 124 CE deposition of [[EmperorShunOfHan|刘保]]. The case begins with palace accusation: Wang Sheng and [[FanFengEunuchLateHan|樊丰]] attack Liu Bao's wet nurse Wang Nan and kitchen supervisor Bing Ji; Fan Feng then works with [[EmpressDowagerYanLateHan|阎皇后]] to fabricate evidence against Liu Bao and his officers.

The political meaning changes when ministers defend Liu Bao. [[LaiLiLateHan|来历]], [[HuanYanLateHan|桓焉]], and [[ZhangHaoRemonstratorLateHan|张皓]] argue that a ten-year-old should not bear full responsibility and should receive loyal guidance. After Liu Bao is demoted, more than ten officials gather at Hongdu Gate to remonstrate that he is blameless. The host reads this support as what frightens An: a child who can attract that much official sympathy now may become uncontrollable later.

The ruler's answer converts succession advice into loyalty suspicion. An sends a Zhongchangshi edict that frames the father-son cut as public duty, accuses the remonstrants of noisy hidden motive, and threatens punishment. Once the support field itself becomes the problem, Lai Li's persistence no longer looks to the ruler like correction; it becomes defiance to be neutralized through office, family, and fief sanctions.

## Key Claims

- Heir support can become threatening before the heir has any active political agency of his own.
- Palace slander may start the case, but visible ministerial defense can harden the ruler's fear by revealing the heir's support base.
- Age and innocence arguments lose force when the ruler's core concern shifts from guilt to future controllability.
- Threat perception recodes loyal remonstrance as hidden factional motive.
- Punishing supporters can make the heir's displacement more irreversible while exposing the ruler's own insecurity.

## Evidence

- Palace-accusation trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says Wang Sheng, Fan Feng, Yan Hou, and others use Wang Nan and Bing Ji to build accusations against Liu Bao.
- Initial defense: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says Lai Li, Huan Yan, and Zhang Hao argue that Liu Bao is too young and uninformed to bear the alleged guilt.
- Broad support signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says more than ten officials gather at Hongdu Gate to say Liu Bao is blameless after his demotion.
- Ruler-security reading: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says the host interprets this official support for a ten-year-old prince as threatening to Emperor An's political safety.
- Criminalizing response: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-801-han-andi-weishenme-henxin-fei-taizi-lgnhbsqnp6vdigsmqfkxiqmx2vdy|Hanji 801]] says the edict accuses the remonstrants of hidden motives and threatens punishment, after which Lai Li's family, office, and fief position are hit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The motive reading is source-scoped. Hanji 801 clearly records slander, fabricated accusations, ministerial objection, formal deposition, threat edict, and Lai Li's punishment, but the specific claim that An fears Liu Bao's support base is the host's interpretation of those events. The concept should not erase the roles of Fan Feng, Yan Hou, Wang Sheng, Wang Nan, Bing Ji, or court compliance; it names the additional moment when the ruler begins treating support for the heir as a security problem.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 801's interpretation of Liu Bao's 124 CE deposition.
