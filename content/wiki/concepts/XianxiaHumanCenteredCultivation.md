## Definition

Human-centered xianxia cultivation is the principle that moral responsibility toward living beings must precede and constrain the pursuit of supernatural power, immortality, institutional glory, or ascension.

## Current Synthesis

In the bounded [[ChinesePaladin4|《仙剑奇侠传四》]] discussion, “先修人道，再修仙道” is not a rejection of fantasy, cultivation, or transcendence. It is a priority rule: power becomes illegitimate when its path requires treating disciples, demons, hosts, or civilians as expendable material.

The principle also changes what counts as loyalty and spiritual success. Qionghua's leaders pursue the visible form of ascension while abandoning the sect's professed humane purpose; Murong Ziying resists them and can therefore be interpreted as preserving rather than betraying that purpose. Yun Tianhe's effort to stop catastrophe, even at bodily cost, carries greater moral weight than successful ascent achieved through injury.

This human center is compatible with culturally specific mythic form. The episode argues that realms, swords, immortality, demons, ghosts, and divine judgment endure when they carry recognizable questions about friendship, love, responsibility, loss, and the use of power. “Human” here names an ethical standard of humane conduct, not a species privilege: nonhuman characters may embody it, while human institutions may violate it.

## Key Claims

- Cultivation power does not establish moral or spiritual worth by itself.
- Immortality or ascension loses legitimacy when pursued through coercion, sacrifice, or preventable harm to others.
- Institutional orthodoxy can preserve the language of virtue while abandoning its humane purpose.
- Resistance to corrupt leadership may express deeper loyalty to an institution's professed values.
- Xianxia's supernatural forms gain durable meaning when they stage recognizable human relationships, costs, and responsibilities.
- “Human-centered” judgment applies across species and factions rather than privileging human identity automatically.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is derived from one conversational interpretation of a fictional game, not a comprehensive history of xianxia, Daoist cultivation, Chinese philosophy, or player reception. The slogan can become empty if it is detached from who is harmed, what alternatives exist, and which consequences follow. Humane conduct does not require rejecting ambition, power, fantasy, or institutional membership, and resistance is not automatically righteous. Claims about the game's intended message, cultural representativeness, or causal influence on players remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established a priority rule placing humane responsibility before supernatural advancement.
- Clarified that the ethical category crosses human, demon, ghost, and sect boundaries rather than encoding species supremacy.
- Connected institutional loyalty to preservation of purpose rather than obedience to leaders alone.
