# Pet Insurance Coverage Boundary / 宠物保险保障边界

## Definition
The pet-insurance coverage boundary is the gap between a caregiver's expectation of broad veterinary protection and the actual eligibility, exclusions, caps, verification, and renewal rules of a policy.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents pet insurance as partial risk transfer rather than a substitute for savings or a counterpart to comprehensive human medical coverage. A preventive examination with no diagnosed disease may fall outside reimbursement, while illness coverage can still be limited by named conditions, age, per-event caps, cumulative caps, or renewal decisions after repeated claims.

The market also faces an identity and verification problem. If animals lack reliable unified identification, insurers may struggle to connect a claim to the correct animal and longitudinal record. Useful insurance therefore depends not only on premium and advertised coverage but on readable policy terms, stable identity, claim evidence, renewal conditions, and alignment between prevention incentives and illness payment.

## Key Claims
- Pet insurance generally transfers only part of veterinary financial risk.
- Preventive screening and diagnosed illness may be treated differently for reimbursement.
- Age limits, named-condition rules, per-event caps, cumulative caps, waiting periods, and renewal rules can materially narrow coverage.
- Repeated illness or claims can affect continued eligibility under some products.
- Reliable animal identity and longitudinal records matter for fraud control and fair claims handling.
- Insurance can still reduce common-disease losses for ordinary households even when it cannot absorb major or chronic costs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a clinician discussion, not a policy comparison, actuarial analysis, or current regulatory survey. Products and rules vary by insurer, jurisdiction, species, age, health history, enrollment date, network, deductible, coinsurance, exclusions, documentation, and renewal terms. Caregivers must read the operative contract; the page does not establish that any specific policy will pay or deny a claim.

## What Changed

- Preserved possible household value despite incomplete catastrophic protection.
