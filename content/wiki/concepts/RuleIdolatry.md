# Rule Idolatry

Rule idolatry is the pattern where a rule is treated as more real than the people, goals, and circumstances it was meant to serve. [[23-feima-pai-qiche-anquan-shi-di-yi-wei-de-572699943]] adds the concept through [[FeimaPaiQiche|《飞马牌汽车》]]: the public bus cannot open its doors because it has not reached a stop, so every emergency becomes secondary to preserving the form of compliance.

The concept differs from ordinary rule-following. Rules can protect against arbitrary power and prevent real accidents. Idolatry begins when the system loses the ability to interpret purpose, weigh harms, or create accountable exceptions.

## Key Claims
- A rule becomes idolatrous when its original safety or order purpose can no longer be questioned.
- Rule idolatry often lets officials avoid responsibility by claiming they merely obeyed procedure.
- The pattern can coexist with heavy activity: meetings, measurements, seals, associations, and devices may grow around the rule without resolving the underlying contradiction.
- In literature, rule idolatry makes bureaucracy funny and frightening at the same time because the local action can be rational while the whole system becomes absurd.
