# National Brand Narrative Backfire

National brand narrative backfire is the risk that patriotic brand rhetoric later turns against the company that benefited from it. In [[bu-xi-deng-e02-bi-quan-shan-beng-mei-guo-zheng-fu-guan-men-first-brands-po-chan-wa-ha-ha-feng-bo-lt-ghzgmiroo9a-odaih4xkhsulu]], the hosts say [[WahahaGroup|娃哈哈]] and [[ZongQinghou|宗庆后]] once used a national-brand frame effectively in conflict with [[Danone]], but later controversy around trademarks, [[HongshengSystem|红盛系]], and possible state-asset loss made public sentiment more dangerous for [[ZongFuli|宗馥莉]].

The concept extends [[NationalGoodsBranding]] by adding a feedback loop. National-goods language can mobilize customers, defend local industry, and distinguish a brand from foreign capital, but it also invites the public to judge ownership, control, loyalty, and asset transfer through moralized national language.

## Key Claims
- Patriotic consumption narratives can create a durable trust asset and a future scrutiny burden.
- A company that frames itself as a national brand may be judged more harshly when ownership, trademarks, or assets appear to move into private or family-controlled hands.
- Public opinion can shift from defending a brand against outsiders to accusing insiders of betraying the brand's public meaning.
- The concept does not say national-goods branding is always fake; it says the rhetoric changes the standards by which later conflicts are judged.
