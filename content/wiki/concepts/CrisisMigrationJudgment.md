## Definition

危局迁徙判断 / Crisis migration judgment is the decision to move a family or community before danger becomes locally obvious or socially accepted, using military geography, political shelter, timing, and the cost of immobility as one survival assessment.

## Current Synthesis

The Xun Yu case separates prediction from useful action. He reads [[YingchuanCommandery|颍川]] as exposed to approaching war, urges relatives and local people to leave, and moves his own family north when most residents remain attached to home or unconvinced. Later devastation gives the decision retrospective force, but the underlying skill is acting under uncertainty before consensus makes the choice easy.

Migration also changes political alignment. The move initially points toward [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]]'s protection, then passes through [[YuanShao|袁绍]]'s camp and ends with [[CaoCao|曹操]]. Family safety, access to a viable patron, and evaluation of how leaders use talent therefore belong to the same decision rather than to separate private and professional choices.

## Key Claims

- Crisis migration is a timing decision made before danger becomes common knowledge.
- Military geography, political shelter, and family responsibility must be assessed together.
- Attachment to place and social disbelief can delay departure after underlying conditions have changed.
- Acting on a warning is distinct from correctly describing the risk.
- Relocation can alter both physical safety and long-term political alignment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both sources are modern podcast summaries drawing on layered historical traditions, and their dramatic chronology should not substitute for direct textual comparison. Later devastation can create hindsight bias: a successful departure does not prove that every warning is sound or that migration is always safer. People who remain may face constraints absent from elite-family narratives, including land, dependents, transport, information, and lack of a receiving patron. The concept describes judgment under uncertainty, not a universal duty to leave.
