# Formula Feeding Stigma

Formula feeding stigma is the shame or failure feeling attached to using [[InfantFormula]] when breastfeeding is treated as the only morally acceptable path. In [[bobbie-laura-modi-how-a-baby-formula-startup-took-market-share-from-two-industry-giants-4488842b-74d3-40ed-8776-5831c5934e69]], [[LauraModi]] describes feeling ashamed when she had to buy formula late at night after struggling with breastfeeding and infection.

The concept matters because stigma can create a business and cultural opening without making the product anti-breastfeeding. [[Bobbie]]'s positioning depends on emotional permission: breast milk can remain valued while parents still need safe, high-quality alternatives and language that does not turn a feeding decision into a public judgment of motherhood.

## Key Claims
- Stigma can hide real demand because parents may use a product while feeling unable to discuss it openly.
- Reducing shame can be part of [[ConsumerBrandMoat]] when the product is a stigmatized necessity.
- The brand risk is overcorrecting into anti-breastfeeding claims; Bobbie's source framing instead treats feeding as multiple valid paths.
- Formula stigma connects commercial trust to policy work because insurance coverage, WIC access, and public discussion affect who can use alternatives without embarrassment or penalty.
