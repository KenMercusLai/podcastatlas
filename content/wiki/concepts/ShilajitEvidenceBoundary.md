# Shilajit Evidence Boundary

## Definition
Shilajit evidence boundary is a foundations-first rule for treating limited hormone, fertility, lipid, or cognitive claims about shilajit as low-priority and authenticity-sensitive rather than as established optimization guidance.

## Current Synthesis
The AMA describes some human evidence for small changes in testosterone, follicle-stimulating hormone, sperm measures, and possibly LDL-related measures, but places shilajit low in the intervention hierarchy. Product authenticity and dosing are uncertain, effects appear modest, and hormone effects that seem desirable in one context may be disruptive in another.

The practical synthesis is to establish sleep, nutrition, body composition, resistance training, cardiovascular training, and recovery before considering experimentation. Menstrual-cycle and fertility context matter because an FSH-active supplement is not neutral merely because it is sold as natural. Any trial should therefore be authenticated, cautious, and secondary to stronger foundations.

## Key Claims
- The source reports limited human evidence rather than a robust, general clinical case.
- Reported hormone and sperm effects appear small and context-dependent.
- Authenticity and dose uncertainty materially weaken practical confidence.
- Sleep, nutrition, body composition, training, and recovery should come first.
- FSH-related effects may matter differently for people with menstrual cycles or fertility concerns.
- Natural-product status does not remove endocrine or contamination risk.

## Evidence
- Evidence scope: [[ama-18-cold-therapy-advice-skin-health-tips-motivation-learning-strategies-more-scim1100391235]] reports small human signals for selected reproductive hormones, sperm quality, testosterone, and possibly LDL measures.
- Priority boundary: [[ama-18-cold-therapy-advice-skin-health-tips-motivation-learning-strategies-more-scim1100391235]] ranks sleep, body composition, nutrition, resistance training, cardiovascular training, and recovery above shilajit.
- Product uncertainty: [[ama-18-cold-therapy-advice-skin-health-tips-motivation-learning-strategies-more-scim1100391235]] notes fake products and uncertain dosing.
- Hormone qualification: [[ama-18-cold-therapy-advice-skin-health-tips-motivation-learning-strategies-more-scim1100391235]] warns that possible FSH stimulation may be relevant to menstrual-cycle and fertility context.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source note does not provide trial designs, effect sizes, product assays, adverse-event rates, interaction data, or long-term outcomes. It does not establish cognitive benefit, treatment of low testosterone, fertility benefit, or cardiovascular prevention. Pregnancy, menstrual irregularity, infertility, hormone-sensitive conditions, medication use, contamination risk, and abnormal laboratory results require appropriate clinical judgment.
