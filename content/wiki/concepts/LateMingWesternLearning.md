# Late-Ming Western Learning / 晚明西学东渐

Late-Ming Western learning is the knowledge-transfer frame opened by [[ep259-yu-miao-shifu-mantan-xu-guangqi-sibainian-qian-ruhe-rangren-biancongming-lkirczxnx8skbbfpmz5-m-mivs4e]]. The source follows [[XuGuangqi|徐光启]], [[MatteoRicci|利玛窦]], [[LiZhizao|李之藻]], maps, geometry, arithmetic, artillery, agriculture, and calendar science to show Western learning entering China through curiosity, translation, elite networks, and state needs.

The concept is not simple Westernization. The episode stresses that Xu and Li were admirable because they actively learned and tested new reasoning systems rather than merely admiring foreign things. At the same time, [[Jesuits|Jesuit]] transmission was never purely neutral: maps, calendars, astronomy, and elite access also served missionary strategy.

The concept becomes fragile after the Ming-Qing transition. [[JohannAdamSchallVonBell|汤若望]]'s calendar work survives institutionally for a time, but the [[YangGuangxian|杨光先]] controversy shows how foreignness, religion, imperial legitimacy, and cultural defensiveness could turn knowledge transmission into a political accusation.

## Key Claims
- Western learning arrived through people, objects, language, and institutions, not only through abstract ideas.
- Translation required Chinese scholars who wanted to learn the reasoning beneath the technique.
- Missionary strategy and scientific usefulness were intertwined, making the exchange productive and politically exposed.
- Cultural-confidence problems can turn epistemic questions into loyalty tests.
- The episode uses late-Ming Western learning as a historical mirror for contemporary chips, AI, and restricted knowledge circulation.
