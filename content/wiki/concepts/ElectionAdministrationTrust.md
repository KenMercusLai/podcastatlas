# Election Administration Trust

## Definition
Election administration trust is public confidence that vote collection, verification, counting, tabulation, and reporting are accurate, timely, and intelligible enough to be accepted as legitimate.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates accuracy from trust. Silver says California's count may be an accurate tally of votes cast while still calling the slow count and complex voting channels unacceptable because they invite suspicion. The reusable lesson is that legitimacy depends on both correct results and procedures that make cohort timing, mail ballots, ranked-choice tabulation, and verification easy to explain.

## Key Claims
- Slow vote counting can damage legitimacy even when there is no evidence that the count is fraudulent.
- Mail voting and late-arriving ballots create predictable blue or red shifts when different voter groups use different methods.
- Treating every ballot as an independent data point can produce false fraud inferences because voting methods sort voters into different cohorts.
- Voter ID can be reasonable in principle without explaining every disputed result.
- Ranked-choice voting can be defensible while still needing faster and simpler tabulation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source acknowledges some fraud cases in a decentralized election system, so the concept is not a claim that fraud is impossible. Its narrower judgment is that the discussed California/Los Angeles patterns do not supply evidence of fraud and that better administration would reduce needless suspicion.
