# Smart Glasses Bystander Privacy

Smart glasses bystander privacy is the problem that camera- and microphone-equipped glasses can record or infer information about people who did not choose to wear or buy the device. [[tech-20260109-0109-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260109-0109-mp-tech-pod-128]] grounds the concept through [[Meta]]'s updated [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Ray-Ban smart glasses]], whose stronger consumer demand keeps privacy advocates focused on recording notice and data collection.

The episode says the glasses have a small red indicator light when recording but questions whether bystanders can easily notice it. That makes the issue a concrete branch of [[ConsumerCameraSurveillance]] and [[ConsentBasedRecording]]: the owner receives the assistant benefit, while surrounding people absorb part of the sensing risk.

## Key Claims
- A recording indicator can improve notice without guaranteeing meaningful consent.
- Smart glasses make surveillance mobile and socially ambiguous because they look like normal eyewear.
- Two-party consent rules show that legal context can depend on location, but social trust may require clearer norms than the minimum law.
- Data collection by the platform matters separately from whether a bystander noticed a recording light.
- Strong consumer demand can intensify privacy scrutiny because more devices in public means more bystander exposure.
