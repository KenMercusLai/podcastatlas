# Real Exposure for Social Anxiety

## Definition
Real exposure for social anxiety is the episode's treatment principle that social fears change through safe, real-world interaction that tests feared predictions, rather than through simulated exposure alone or simple habituation to anxious feelings.

## Current Synthesis
[[NickEpley]] presents social anxiety as treatable when fear is maintained by inaccurate beliefs about how others will respond. Exposure works because reality supplies corrective evidence: a greeting is returned, a request is accepted, a stranger is warmer than expected, or an ambiguous cue turns out not to be rejection. The point is not to force risky intimacy but to start small enough that real social data can accumulate.

This frame connects clinical exposure therapy to everyday practice. The episode's examples include saying hello to familiar strangers, asking for help, making unusual requests, and treating beliefs about others as bets that may be wrong.

## Key Claims
- Social anxiety can improve when real interaction updates false predictions about rejection or negative evaluation.
- Simulated or pretend exposure is weaker than real exposure when the feared outcome concerns actual social response.
- Exposure is not merely numbing anxiety; it changes beliefs about the world and other people.
- Small, safe bids for contact are appropriate starting points.
- Boundaries, safety, and sensitivity to others' responses remain part of the practice.

## Evidence
- Treatment frame: [[how-to-overcome-social-anxiety-dr-nick-epley-scim7834121132]] has Epley describe social anxiety as treatable and discuss exposure therapy with reference to real interaction.
- Belief updating: [[how-to-overcome-social-anxiety-dr-nick-epley-scim7834121132]] says exposure works by changing beliefs about how others respond, not simply by numbing anxiety.
- Request practice: [[how-to-overcome-social-anxiety-dr-nick-epley-scim7834121132]] uses Jia Jiang's rejection project and Epley's lab review to show that unusual requests were accepted more often than expected.
- Small starts: [[how-to-overcome-social-anxiety-dr-nick-epley-scim7834121132]] recommends low-risk greetings and sensitivity to cues rather than pursuing interaction at any cost.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is public education, not individualized treatment guidance. Some social fears are tied to trauma, harassment, discrimination, unsafe environments, neurodivergence, or clinical severity that require professional support and different pacing. The source also acknowledges that friendliness can be misread and that physical risk is unevenly distributed.
