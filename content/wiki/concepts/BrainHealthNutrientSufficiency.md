# Brain-Health Nutrient Sufficiency

## Definition
Brain-health nutrient sufficiency is the source's nutrition frame that cognitive support starts with adequate energy, nutrient status, diet quality, and targeted testing before considering optimization or high-dose supplementation.

## Current Synthesis
Wood organizes nutrition around energy, nutrients, and dietary pattern. The source treats Mediterranean-style and MIND-style diets as useful partly because they improve whole-food quality and nutrient density, not because one named food or supplement explains brain health. The strongest practical claim is to avoid deficiency or insufficiency in nutrients tied to cognition and dementia risk.

The supplement discussion stays conservative. Vitamin D, iron, omega-3 status, B12, folate, homocysteine, and related B-vitamin interactions are testable targets; a basic multivitamin may help older adults by filling gaps; creatine has stronger safety evidence than many fashionable peptides. Benefits of pushing already adequate biomarkers higher remain uncertain.

## Key Claims
- Energy balance matters because chronic caloric restriction and chronic caloric excess are both associated in the source with smaller brain volume in population comparisons.
- Diet quality is treated as whole-food nutrient density rather than a single magic food.
- Vitamin D, iron, omega-3 status, B12, folate, and homocysteine are emphasized as practical testing targets when possible.
- Nutrient effects can interact, so single-nutrient trials may fail when a coupled pathway remains insufficient.
- Basic multivitamins may help some older adults by covering common gaps rather than by megadosing.
- Creatine is treated as a stronger supplement example than many peptides because of safety evidence and possible physical or cognitive benefits in some people.
- The evidence is strongest for raising the floor, not for optimizing already adequate levels.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is not nutrition or supplement advice. The source keeps biomarker interpretation, deficiency correction, concussion context, medication interactions, kidney disease, pregnancy, and other clinical conditions inside individualized medical judgment.
