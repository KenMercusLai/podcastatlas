# Reform Timing Trap

Reform timing trap is the source pattern where concessions that might have stabilized a system earlier arrive after trust has already collapsed or while the reformer lacks a trusted constituency. In [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]], [[TerenceONeill|Terence O'Neill]] begins as an economic modernizer, but class distance, uneven investment, and [[IanPaisley]]'s attack on the [[SeanLemass]] visit weaken his ability to sell compromise to hardline unionists.

In [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]], O'Neill accepts that Catholic grievances are real and that [[NorthernIreland]] cannot be governed by police power alone, but his limited reform package satisfies neither civil-rights activists nor hardline unionists.

The trap is intensified by rival clocks. [[NorthernIrelandCivilRightsAssociation|Civil-rights]] activists want visible change; [[WilliamCraig]] warns concessions could trigger loyalist uprising; [[HaroldWilson]] and [[JimCallaghan]] pressure [[Stormont]] from London; [[IanPaisley]] gains by portraying moderation as surrender.

## Key Claims
- Late reforms can confirm grievances for one side while confirming betrayal fears for the other.
- Moderate leaders may lose authority precisely because they are responsible for reforms that neither camp trusts.
- External pressure can force reform without rebuilding local legitimacy.
- A reformer can be too socially and symbolically detached from his own base to turn modernization into political authority.
