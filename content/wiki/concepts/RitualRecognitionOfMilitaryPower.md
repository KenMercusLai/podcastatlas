# Ritual Recognition of Military Power / 礼制承认军事权力

Ritual recognition of military power / 礼制承认军事权力 is the source's interpretation of [[ZhouXianwang|周显王]] giving [[FufuRobe|黻服]] to [[QinXianGong|秦献公]] after the [[ShimenBattle|Battle of Shimen]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]].

The point is that the weakened [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]] still has symbolic instruments. It may not control the interstate battlefield, but a ranked gift can convert military success into a publicly legible status claim. In the host's reading, Qin's conquest is not only successful; it is acknowledged as the work of a hegemonic power.

This concept extends the wiki's earlier Zhou legitimacy branch. [[ThreeJinVassalRecognition]] shows Zhou naming new post-Jin rulers, while [[KinshipLegitimacyDiplomacy]] shows Zhou trying to bind Qin through origin language. Here, ritual clothing performs a related function after Qin has already proved its power militarily.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] adds a stronger successor pattern through [[ZhouHegemonRecognition|王致伯于秦]]. Instead of only recognizing Qin's military prestige with clothing, [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]] now gives [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] a senior-lord status among the vassals, while [[GongziShaoguan|公子少官]]'s military presence at [[Fengze|逢泽]] keeps recognition tied to force.

## Key Claims
- Ritual signs can ratify battlefield outcomes even when the ritual center is politically weak.
- Recognition does not require full control; it can still shape how victory is narrated.
- Clothing, rank, and conquest belong to the same political language in the episode's reading.
- The concept connects Qin's rise to Zhou's surviving symbolic authority.
- Later Zhou recognition can move from symbolic rank objects to explicit hegemon status.
