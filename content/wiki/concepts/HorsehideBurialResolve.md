## Definition

马革裹尸式边将生死观 / horsehide burial resolve is the military ethos that treats death on a frontier campaign, even with the body returned in horsehide, as a more fitting end for a soldier than comfortable death away from service.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 714 foregrounds the idiom while narrating [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]]'s earlier Wudu victory over Qiang forces and his confidence under a false Qiang-alarm panic. In that episode, the phrase summarizes a border general's willingness to stake life on state service, but it is embedded in a broader portrait of disciplined judgment rather than only martial bravado.

Hanji 722 supplies the later, more direct Ma Yuan speech layer after his return from Jiaozhi. There Ma Yuan hears of renewed northern frontier pressure and frames proper military death as dying on the border and returning wrapped in horsehide. The concept therefore joins rhetoric and conduct: courage matters, but its strongest form in these sources is attached to repeated readiness for frontier service after prior campaigns.

## Key Claims

- The idiom is tied in the current wiki to Ma Yuan's frontier identity rather than to generic warrior romanticism.
- Horsehide-burial language treats the location and purpose of death as morally meaningful for a military man.
- The ethos depends on proven service: Hanji 714 links it back to Wudu/Qiang command, while Hanji 722 links it to renewed northern-campaign desire after Jiaozhi.
- The phrase should not erase judgment; Ma Yuan's calm handling of frontier panic shows that martial resolve can coexist with restraint.
- The concept remains dangerous if overgeneralized into automatic escalation, because later sources show frontier decisions still constrained by capacity and political cost.

## Evidence

- Idiom framing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] explains "马革裹尸" as a soldier's willingness to die in frontier service and associates it with Ma Yuan.
- Wudu command context: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-714-shi-shui-di-yi-ge-han-chu-nan-er-dang-ma-ge-guo-shi-lpkqxrl3dsok4ui0lij4c3x8zfpi|Hanji 714]] pairs the idiom with Ma Yuan's defeat of Qiang forces and his frontier-governance judgment.
- Later speech layer: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-722-rang-liuxiu-xiansi-de-toutongbing-zenme-huishi-logwccdsca7vm2zzssu8ubm8rwra|Hanji 722]] says Ma Yuan wants another northern campaign and states the ideal of dying on the frontier wrapped in horsehide.
- Repeated service: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-722-rang-liuxiu-xiansi-de-toutongbing-zenme-huishi-logwccdsca7vm2zzssu8ubm8rwra|Hanji 722]] places the statement after Ma Yuan's Jiaozhi return, making it a late-career resolve rather than a first campaign boast.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not be read as a universal approval of reckless campaigning. Hanji 714 itself pairs Ma Yuan's courage with delegated administration and refusal to overreact to rumors, and Liu Xiu's same-episode frontier edict allows officials to defend rather than pursue when capacity is insufficient. Hanji 722 records the clearer Ma Yuan speech, so Hanji 714's episode-title use is best treated as idiom framing rather than proof that the full phrase was uttered in the 36 CE Wudu scene.
