# Gynecological Exam Dignity / 妇科检查尊严

## Definition
Gynecological exam dignity is the principle that intimate medical need does not cancel explanation, consent, privacy, pain acknowledgment, communication, or the patient's right to refuse unnecessary observation.

## Current Synthesis
The Talk三联 source begins from experiences of postpartum follow-up, intimate examination, dismissive comments, and unwanted trainee observation. Its core distinction is care versus humiliation: an examination can be medically indicated while its delivery still undermines agency, privacy, and future willingness to seek care.

VOL.84 adds practical navigation. Abnormal discharge, itching, pain, pregnancy concerns, or other symptoms should prompt qualified assessment rather than shame-driven delay. Explaining routine questions, sample collection, possible discomfort, and the patient's ability to ask the clinician to slow down can reduce fear without pretending every examination will be painless.

## Key Claims
- Medical indication does not erase dignity, explanation, consent, privacy, or the right to refuse unnecessary observers.
- Fear, embarrassment, pain, and tension are clinically relevant patient information rather than evidence of immaturity.
- Clear explanation of routine history questions and examination steps can reduce shame and improve cooperation.
- Patients can communicate discomfort and ask clinicians to pause, slow down, or explain what is happening.
- Abnormal symptoms and pregnancy concerns should not be hidden or delayed because intimate care feels embarrassing.
- Disrespectful care can create later avoidance, so communication is part of safety rather than a cosmetic extra.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Communication and relaxation can reduce some distress but do not explain away pain or make every procedure optional. Symptoms, pregnancy status, examination purpose, urgency, safeguarding needs, local consent rules, and clinical setting determine appropriate care. This concept is not a screening schedule, diagnostic protocol, or substitute for qualified gynecological assessment.
