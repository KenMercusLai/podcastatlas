# Mercenary War / 雇佣兵战争

## Definition

The Mercenary War / 雇佣兵战争 is the post-First-Punic-War revolt in which [[Carthage]]'s inability to pay returning troops turned demobilization into organized rebellion, territorial struggle, and reciprocal atrocity.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents the conflict as a fiscal failure that became a sovereignty crisis. Carthage assembled unpaid troops without securing hostages or resolving arrears; Spendius and Matho then captured Gisco and the available treasure, reminted money, and behaved increasingly like a rival state. The revolt exposed the danger of a military system whose cohesion depended on cash after defeat had constricted revenue.

Escalation was not automatic. Gisco's mutilation and murder, followed by [[HamilcarBarca]]'s elephant executions, starvation siege, seizure of negotiators, and mass killing, progressively destroyed space for settlement. Syracuse and the [[RomanRepublic]] assisted Carthage from strategic self-interest, showing that outside powers could prefer a weakened but solvent Carthage to collapse. Rome's subsequent seizure of Sardinia then converted temporary assistance into a durable grievance.

## Key Claims

- Unpaid demobilization transformed military debt into collective armed leverage.
- Rebel seizure and reminting of treasure marked movement from mutiny toward proto-state behavior.
- Violence against envoys and prisoners narrowed the possibility of negotiated settlement.
- Hamilcar combined maneuver, blockade, deception, and exemplary killing to defeat a larger revolt.
- Syracuse and Rome supported Carthaginian survival because collapse threatened their own strategic or financial interests.
- The war left Hamilcar dominant while Rome's Sardinian intervention redirected recovery toward Iberia.

## Evidence

- Fiscal origin and organization: [[424-carthage-vs-rome-total-war-part-4-glt9312780357]] describes arrears, the unsecured encampment, Gisco's mission, rebel leaders, seized gold, and reminting.
- Escalatory atrocity: [[424-carthage-vs-rome-total-war-part-4-glt9312780357]] connects Gisco's death, elephant trampling, the Saw blockade, cannibalism, broken negotiation, crucifixion, and prisoner sacrifice.
- Outside intervention: [[424-carthage-vs-rome-total-war-part-4-glt9312780357]] attributes Syracusan and Roman aid to balance-of-power and indemnity interests.
- Aftermath: [[424-carthage-vs-rome-total-war-part-4-glt9312780357]] connects victory, Matho's punishment, Sardinia, and the Spanish turn.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's account is mediated through Polybius and dramatized in places through Flaubert's *Salambo*. Labels such as "mercenary," atrocity totals, motives, dialogue, cannibalism, executions, crucifixions, and the legal or moral status of Hamilcar's conduct remain source-scoped. A fiscal explanation is central but not exhaustive: Libyan participation, Carthaginian rule, leadership, and regional politics also require fuller evidence.

## What Changed

- Established unpaid demobilization as the bridge between external defeat and internal state crisis.
- Preserved reciprocal escalation rather than assigning the collapse of restraint to only one side.
