# Historical Dream Foreshadowing / 梦兆叙事伏笔

Historical dream foreshadowing / 梦兆叙事伏笔 is the historiographical pattern highlighted in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-2-lrlulw0b6v0fb2xpr2i5ifem1h4n]]. The episode says [[Shiji|《史记》]] records dreams in the Zhao lineage not as random curiosities, but as signs that organize later political rise, desire, disorder, and disaster.

The immediate case is [[ZhaoDan|赵丹 / 赵孝成王]] dreaming of mixed-color clothing, riding a flying dragon, falling before reaching heaven, and landing among heaps of gold and jade. The dream is interpreted as brokenness, force without food, and worry. Three days later, [[FengTing|冯亭]]'s envoy offers [[Shangdang|上党]], turning apparent treasure into the prelude to [[ChangpingBattle|长平之战]].

The episode then reads this as part of a four-dream structure in the Shiji Zhao lineage: [[ZhaoDun|赵盾]]'s sadness-then-joy dream around decline and recovery, [[ZhaoJianzi|赵简子]]'s dream travel to Juntian before later Zhao expansion, [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]]'s music-and-woman dream before affection-driven succession trouble, and Zhao Xiaocheng Wang's gold-jade dream before Shangdang and Changping.

This concept differs from [[DreamLogicNarrative]] because it is not about dreamlike plot motion in fiction. It is closer to [[CelestialOmenPoliticalResponsibility]] and [[ChronicleSourceGaps]]: the dream is a historical-text device that shows how old sources made political causality legible through signs, narrative placement, and retrospective patterning.

## Key Claims
- A recorded dream can function as narrative structure, not merely as supernatural anecdote.
- Dream interpretation in a historical text often compresses political diagnosis: desire, insufficiency, danger, and later disaster are made visible before the decision fully unfolds.
- The same dream can be treated as historical evidence in the source tradition and as literary foreshadowing in later reading.
- Source selection matters: when [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] omits a dream preserved by [[Shiji|《史记》]], it also changes the reader's sense of warning, fate, and moral framing.
