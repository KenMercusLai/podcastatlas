# Briggs v. Mullen

## Definition
Briggs v. Mullen is the court challenge named in [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] over DHS agents taking DNA from U.S. citizens during Operation Midway Blitz in Chicago.

## Current Synthesis
The episode frames Briggs v. Mullen as an early test of compelled DNA sampling outside the ordinary criminal-arrestee setting. Its importance for the wiki is that the plaintiffs are described as U.S. citizen protesters exercising First Amendment rights, so the case joins [[CompelledDNASamplingFourthAmendment]] with broader [[CivilLibertiesSurveillanceRisk]] around protest, detention, and future database searches.

## Key Claims
- The case is presented as the first court challenge known to Georgetown's center.
- The plaintiffs were U.S. citizens whose DNA was taken by DHS agents, not only immigration detainees.
- The protest context links DNA collection to speech and association risks, not only forensic identification.
- The case could clarify whether DHS sampling practices exceed the logic of [[MarylandVKing|Maryland v. King]].
