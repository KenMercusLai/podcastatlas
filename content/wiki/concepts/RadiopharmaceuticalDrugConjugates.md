# Radiopharmaceutical Drug Conjugates / RDC

## Definition
Radiopharmaceutical drug conjugates, or RDCs, are targeted cancer therapies that deliver radioactive payloads to selected tissue. In the wiki's current sources, they are best understood as a radiation version of ADC-like targeted delivery rather than as generic external-beam radiation.

## Current Synthesis
The current synthesis has two layers. The China biotech source introduces RDCs as a modality watch item whose value depends on targeting, isotope half-life, hospital distance, radiation controls, and clinical workflow. The All-In Actus pitch adds an investable platform version: a mini-protein carrier is claimed to carry radioactive payloads to known tumor markers, use imaging to verify tumor delivery, and create high upside if one program works. Together, the sources make RDCs a modality where chemistry, biology, logistics, clinical evidence, and investment risk cannot be separated.

## Key Claims
- RDCs can be understood as targeted radiotherapy: the payload is radioactive, while the delivery design determines where it concentrates.
- Carrier choice matters because antibodies, peptides, mini-proteins, or other binders create different tradeoffs around penetration, clearance, timing, and toxicity.
- Isotope supply, half-life, hospital proximity, radiation handling, and workflow are core capability tests rather than back-office details.
- Imaging can make early development more observable by showing whether a candidate reaches tumors, but it does not replace clinical efficacy or survival evidence.
- Platform-style RDC companies can have asymmetric upside, but no-revenue and no-product stages make valuation highly catalyst-dependent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not prove that any specific RDC platform will work clinically. Tumor targeting, payload choice, toxicity, manufacturing, isotope sourcing, dosing, patient selection, regulatory review, and commercial logistics can each block success. Imaging evidence can support a mechanism but still fall short of safety, efficacy, progression-free survival, overall survival, or reimbursement proof.
