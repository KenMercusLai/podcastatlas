# Animal Touch Consent

## Definition
Animal touch consent is a practical welfare test that offers brief contact, pauses, and uses the animal's voluntary re-initiation, approach, or disengagement as evidence about whether contact should continue.

## Current Synthesis
Human affection does not determine how touch feels to another species. A hug can be warmth to the person and restraint to the animal; even petting that is often welcomed can become unwanted because of location, duration, arousal, pain, or context. The consent test makes touch interactive rather than assumed.

The method is deliberately modest: touch briefly in an area the animal usually prefers, stop, and observe. Re-approach or solicitation supports continuing; withdrawal, freezing, avoidance, or failure to re-initiate supports giving space and considering stress or pain.

## Key Claims
- Benevolent human intent does not establish animal comfort.
- Pausing gives the animal an observable opportunity to choose proximity or distance.
- Consent must be renewed because preference changes with context, arousal, fatigue, and pain.
- Slow touch and a calm human state may support co-regulation for some animals, but response remains individual.

## Evidence
- Hugging and restraint - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] warns that many animals may experience a human hug as restraint.
- Pause-and-reinitiate test - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] recommends brief touch followed by observation of whether the animal requests more.
- State dependence - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] locates touch within pleasantness, arousal, safety, and possible human-animal co-regulation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Behavior is not verbal consent and can be ambiguous. Freezing is not necessarily enjoyment, conditioned tolerance is not necessarily preference, and sudden avoidance can indicate pain requiring veterinary attention. The test reduces presumption; it does not make humans infallible readers of animal experience.

## What Changed
- Created a practical choice-based boundary for affectionate handling.
- Connected touch interpretation to arousal, restraint, pain, and co-regulation.
