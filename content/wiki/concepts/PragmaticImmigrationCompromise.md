# Pragmatic Immigration Compromise

Pragmatic immigration compromise is [[RahmEmanuel|Rahm Emanuel]]'s source-scoped immigration frame in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-rahm-emanuel-trumps-foreign-policy-china-europes-decline-immigration-dsa-vs-democrats-42416310]]. He says the [[UnitedStates|United States]] must remain both a nation of laws and a nation of immigrants, which means pairing border enforcement and workplace enforcement with legal immigration and a workable settlement for people already inside the country.

The concept differs from a pure [[MeritBasedImmigrationFilter]]. Emanuel supports skilled immigration and foreign students, but he also endorses the [[DignityAct|Dignity Act]] as a legislative compromise. He criticizes Democrats for suggesting in the 2020 debate that illegal border-crossers should receive free healthcare, while also warning against losing America's immigration and university advantages.

The source uses [[Canada]] and [[Denmark]] as better comparison cases than [[Japan]] because Japan is much more homogeneous. That makes assimilation capacity, rule clarity, and labor-market contribution part of the same frame.

## Key Claims
- Border enforcement and immigration openness are treated as mutually necessary rather than mutually exclusive.
- Workplace enforcement matters because demand for unauthorized labor can undermine border law after entry.
- Skilled immigration and foreign-student retention are presented as American strategic advantages.
- The Dignity Act is treated as a passable compromise, not as Emanuel's ideal bill.
- Immigration policy has to account for assimilation and public trust as well as economic contribution.
