## Definition

强制边疆震慑 / Coercive Frontier Deterrence is the pattern where a court restores frontier order by forcing extradition, staging punishment before dependent rulers, and converting a local flight case into general rules against refuge or cross-border harboring.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-613-quanli-kuangchao-jiemi-wangmang-de-tiexue-shouwan-lgfzc3ypqizddsmd6mvaasb1dxsd|Hanji 613]] grounds the concept in the 2 CE handling of [[GougouCheshiKing|车师后王沽沟]] and [[TangdouRuoqiangKing|若羌去胡来王唐斗]]. Both rulers flee to [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] after different failures in Han frontier management: one after detention over a road project, the other after failed rescue and blocked pass entry.

The Han court does not treat the chanyu's report or plea for leniency as enough. It demands the two men, assembles Western Regions rulers and troops, publicly executes them, and then issues four rules forbidding Xiongnu from receiving Han people, Wusun people, Wuhuan people, and seal-bearing Western Regions or other state figures. The mechanism is deterrence through visible punishment plus jurisdictional boundary setting.

The concept stays qualified because deterrence has a trust cost. The episode says the policy makes Western Regions states dissatisfied and afraid, so the same act that clarifies Han coercive capacity also weakens voluntary confidence in Han protection.

## Key Claims

- Frontier deterrence can operate through public theater as much as through battlefield victory.
- Extradition pressure turns a refuge case into a test of hierarchy between the court and a frontier counterpart.
- Executing dependent rulers before assembled peers makes punishment communicative, not only retributive.
- Anti-harboring rules convert individual flight into a general border-control regime.
- The same policy can deter flight while damaging dependent-polity trust.

- Public punishment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-613-quanli-kuangchao-jiemi-wangmang-de-tiexue-shouwan-lgfzc3ypqizddsmd6mvaasb1dxsd|Hanji 613]] says Western Regions kings and troops are assembled before the beheading.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is currently a one-source concept. The source does not prove whether the deterrence succeeded beyond the immediate case, nor does it give the viewpoint of every Western Regions state. The concept should be used when punishment, diplomatic pressure, and general anti-harboring rules are joined; ordinary frontier law enforcement, military victory, or symbolic demotion alone should use narrower concepts such as [[SymbolicStatusDowngradeBackfire|象征性名分降格反噬]] or [[UnsupportedFrontierIntervention|无支撑边疆干预]].
