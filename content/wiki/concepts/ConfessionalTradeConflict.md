# Confessional Trade Conflict

Confessional trade conflict is the episode's pattern where religious division and commercial competition reinforce each other. In [[14-wushi-weilian-dahanghai-shidai-de-riben-he-xifang-555771285]], Catholic [[Spain]] and [[Portugal]] already have Asian trade and [[Jesuits|Jesuit]] influence, while Protestant [[England]] and the [[Netherlands]] try to break into the same routes.

[[693-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-englands-greatest-hero-part-3-glt5069845329]] adds an Atlantic and Pacific prelude through [[FrancisDrake]]. The episode treats Drake's Protestant formation, [[SanJuanDeUlua]] grievance, raids on [[NombreDeDios]], and [[GoldenHind]] voyage as a case where religious rivalry, trade exclusion, slaving, piracy, and imperial competition are fused rather than separate.

This matters in Japan because information about Europeans is not neutral. Jesuits can describe English and Dutch survivors as pirates, while [[WilliamAdams|William Adams / 三浦按针]] has to explain the Reformation-era split and wars to [[TokugawaIeyasu|Tokugawa Ieyasu]]. The source therefore treats European religious conflict as a practical diplomatic fact inside [[TokugawaForeignTradeAmbivalence]].

## Key Claims
- In the source, religion is not separate from shipping, trade permission, and accusation.
- Protestant/Catholic rivalry travels into Japanese political judgment through interpreters and missionaries.
- Adams's usefulness partly comes from explaining why Iberian Catholic claims about him are interested claims.
- Confessional conflict helps explain why trade openings can close when rulers fear foreign religious allegiance.
- The Drake branch shows the same Protestant/Catholic trade rivalry before Japan enters the frame: Atlantic slaving, Spanish monopoly, and English raiding all operate inside confessional hostility.
