[[HighTempleRite|高庙礼]] is the ritual frame used in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p|Qinji 121]] to explain Qin Shi Huang's first post-unification journey away from [[Xianyang]]. The episode reads the westward route through [[LongxiCommandery]] and [[BeidiCommandery]] as movement toward Qin old lands and [[YongchengQin]], where a ruler could report major achievement to ancestral temples.

The episode uses [[ZhouWuwang]] as a precedent: after major victory, the ruler announces accomplishment before the ancestral line. This makes the journey political and ritual rather than a generic inspection tour.

## Key Claims
- [[HighTempleRite]] makes imperial travel a form of retrospective legitimation: conquest is reported to ancestors.
- The rite helps explain why Qin Shi Huang went west after unification rather than first touring newly conquered eastern territories.
- In the episode, the rite links Qin's old homeland to the new imperial order and complements later cosmic symbolism at [[QinJimiao]].
- This is an interpretive explanation of the terse itinerary, not an explicit full ritual description in the chronicle line.

## Connections
- [[QinShiHuang]] — ruler whose westward route is interpreted through the rite.
- [[YongchengQin]] — old Qin ritual center and likely destination behind the interpretation.
- [[LongxiCommandery]] and [[BeidiCommandery]] — route markers indicating movement through Qin old territory.
- [[ZhouWuwang]] — precedent for achievement-reporting at an ancestral temple.
- [[QinState]] — old Qin political identity carried into empire.
- [[QinJimiao]] — later ritual architecture that shifts legitimacy from ancestral report to cosmic staging.
