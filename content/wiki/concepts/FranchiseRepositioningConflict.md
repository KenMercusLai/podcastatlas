# Franchise Repositioning Conflict / 加盟品牌升级冲突

## Definition
Franchise repositioning conflict occurs when a chain raises price, store, product, or marketing ambition while franchisees bear much of the upgrade cost and the existing network still reflects the older promise.

## Current Synthesis
[[LuckyCoffee|幸运咖]] illustrates a three-way conflict: dense low-price rollout can depress store economics, repositioning into a higher price band adds refurbishment and marketing cost, and [[MixueBingcheng|蜜雪冰城]]'s own coffee rollout can overlap with the original low-price proposition. The system must therefore coordinate brand direction, franchisee returns, store density, and portfolio boundaries rather than treating an image upgrade as a communications exercise.

## Key Claims
- Brand repositioning changes franchisee economics before it proves stronger demand.
- Dense rollout can make a chain's own stores the nearest competitors.
- Celebrity and image investment raise the fixed cost of an uncertain price transition.
- Parent-group category overlap can blur positioning and redirect traffic within the portfolio.
- Slower openings may protect unit economics but can also expose dependence on continued footprint growth.

## Evidence
- Network pressure - [[yingweida-qidong-dui-huangrenxun-de-diaocha-xiaofei-pinpai-jiti-kuajie-chongwu-shengyi-gkwrijiox3zoafmb9at4mvyn]] says weaker demand and lower delivery subsidies reduced some store sales after rapid densification.
- Upgrade burden - [[yingweida-qidong-dui-huangrenxun-de-diaocha-xiaofei-pinpai-jiti-kuajie-chongwu-shengyi-gkwrijiox3zoafmb9at4mvyn]] reports a move toward a 10–16 RMB price band and celebrity endorsement, with franchisees not necessarily willing to absorb higher costs.
- Portfolio overlap - [[yingweida-qidong-dui-huangrenxun-de-diaocha-xiaofei-pinpai-jiti-kuajie-chongwu-shengyi-gkwrijiox3zoafmb9at4mvyn]] says Mixue's growing fresh-coffee offer overlaps with Lucky Coffee's former positioning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives no store cohort data, closure count, franchisee survey, same-store sales, unit economics, refurbishment budget, or evidence that parent-brand coffee is a direct substitute in every market. Higher prices and better stores could improve margins or attract new occasions if execution and product quality support the change.

## What Changed
- Created a system-level account of brand upgrading under franchisee and portfolio constraints.
- Added internal category overlap to the usual density-versus-price repositioning problem.
