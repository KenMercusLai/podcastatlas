# Rule-Breaking Abdication / 破坏规则式禅让

Rule-breaking abdication / 破坏规则式禅让 is the succession failure [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-47-yanwangkuai-de-lixiangzhuyi-lhgl6rqkv9nonzxc9xqquzzlz5pr]] draws from [[YanWangKuai|燕王哙]]'s transfer of authority to [[ZiZhi|子之]]. The ruler treats the state as if it can be yielded to a minister for moral reputation, but the episode argues that a Warring States throne is not a private object that can be gifted outside the accepted succession order.

The concept differs from [[PartialAbdicationPowerSplit|退位留权式权力分裂]]. In Zhao Wuling Wang's case, the danger comes from yielding formal title while retaining too much authority. In Yan Wang Kuai's case, the danger comes from crossing a legitimacy boundary: the state is moved toward a non-dynastic minister whose right to rule does not follow the expected line.

The mechanism is gradual. [[SuDai|苏代]] first pushes Yan Wang Kuai to prove trust in Zi Zhi. [[LuMaoshou|鹿毛寿]] then uses Yao-Xu You and Yu-Boyi-Qi stories to recode power transfer as ancient virtue. The final step is not merely symbolic abdication language; Yan Wang Kuai gives Zi Zhi appointment authority over senior officials, which makes people, offices, and finances follow the minister.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-48-junwang-weisha-buneng-chuanwei-zaixiang-lnwhljnn888mzlj7let2ptn1pcdm]] gives the consequence branch. After [[ZiZhi|子之]] has held practical power for three years, [[YanState|燕国]] falls into disorder, [[ShiBei|市被]] and the crown prince fail to restore the old line, and [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] can send [[ZhangZiQiGeneral|张子]] into Yan under the language of restored ruler-minister and father-son order. The same source turns the case into [[LiAsPoliticalOrder|礼制政治秩序]]: breaking the role rule does not merely offend etiquette, it makes command, succession, and political rescue claims unstable.

The source's point is not that Yan Wang Kuai has no moral qualities. It says precisely the opposite: benevolence, frugality, and care for the people do not protect a ruler who cannot distinguish personal virtue from political procedure. Moralized humility becomes destructive when it dissolves the rules that make authority recognizable to ministers, heirs, and the state.

## Key Claims
- A ruler can damage succession not only by failing to choose an heir, but by choosing outside the rule that makes the heir politically legible.
- Ancient abdication stories can become dangerous when used as live Warring States procedure rather than moral myth or retrospective ideal.
- Public refusal can increase a usurper's prestige when the surrounding authority transfer continues underneath it.
- Appointment power is the operational core of the transfer: whoever selects senior officials begins to own the governing machine.
- Once the succession boundary breaks, later actors can claim to restore order by force, but the same broken legitimacy gives outside states an opening.
- The failure connects [[MoralReputationPoliticalCapital|道德名望政治资本]] with [[GoodIntentionsPoliticalLimits]]: sincere or performed virtue can still break institutions.
