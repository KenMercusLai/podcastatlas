# Tuntian System / 屯田制

## Definition

屯田制 / the tuntian system is state-directed agricultural logistics that organizes land, soldiers or displaced and surrendered population, offices, production, storage, territorial consolidation, and military supply under a repeatable administrative structure. It is not simply soldiers farming; it converts frontier or wartime insecurity into more durable provisioning and control capacity.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 329 adds the earliest current Western Han branch after the Mobei campaign. The episode says Han digs canals, develops fields, appoints agricultural officials, and stations fifty to sixty thousand soldiers from 朔方郡 toward Lingju, using local production and settlement to consolidate pressure on former Xiongnu territory. This broadens the system beyond the later Cao-side response to displaced population: frontier屯田 can join garrison supply, infrastructure, administration, and territorial control.

The later-Han evidence separates strategic prehistory from formal implementation. Hanji 968 connects the 192 CE Qingzhou surrender to [[DefeatedPopulationConversion|降众资源化]]: selected fighters become [[QingzhouSoldiers|青州兵]], while other people are directed toward farming and supply. [[MaoJie|毛玠]] then joins imperial legitimacy to deep farming and stockpiling. Hanji 979 adds [[ZaoZhi|枣祗]]'s earlier emergency organization at [[DongALateHan|东阿]], showing an administrative orientation before a dedicated屯田 apparatus exists.

Hanji 1008 provides the clearest formal implementation in 196 CE. Zao Zhi proposes the policy, [[CaoCao|曹操]] appoints him屯田都尉 and [[RenJun|任峻]]典农中郎将, and production around [[Xudu|许都]] becomes a repeatable grain base. Hanji 976 retrospectively places this logistics layer inside the broader Cao-side adviser and state architecture.

Hanji 1104 strengthens the strategic judgment but compresses the chronology, describing屯田 as beginning after the 192 CE Qingzhou surrender and turning a large displaced population into production and soldiers. The current synthesis treats that as the population and strategy phase, while retaining 196 CE as the source set's specific institutional implementation point. Hanji 974's claim that earlier land clearance and屯田 pressure contributed to Yanzhou elite resistance remains a source-scoped causal hypothesis.

## Key Claims

- Tuntian converts insecure people, abandoned land, and military demand into organized production rather than relying only on requisition or plunder.
- Frontier屯田 can combine canals, garrison settlement, agricultural offices, supply, and territorial consolidation, not only postwar refugee reorganization.
- The Qingzhou surrender supplies a population base and strategic prehistory, while the Xudu appointments provide the clearest formal administrative start.
- Offices and named administrators matter because durable food security requires repeatable authority, storage, and distribution.
- Grain production creates strategic freedom by reducing immediate campaign dependence on seized supplies.
- The system belongs to a wider architecture of legitimacy, advisers, local defense, and population incorporation.
- Agricultural state capacity can provoke distributional or frontier conflict when land clearance, settlement, or control threatens established interests.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source set does not support one origin story or a single institutional design. Hanji 329 presents an earlier Western Han frontier practice but gives limited office, land-tenure, labor, storage, and distribution detail; its force size, geography, and “蚕食” interpretation remain source-scoped. Within the Cao-side branch, Hanji 1104's 192 CE formulation is best read as strategic and demographic prehistory, while Hanji 1008 gives the narrower 196 CE formal implementation. Hanji 974's elite-resistance claim may describe land-control conflict without proving a mature屯田 bureaucracy at that earlier point. The reported million斛 harvest, modern weight conversion, supported-population estimate, and “million refugees” language remain source-scoped. The system's coercive effects on soldiers, displaced people, frontier populations, and landholders are underdescribed by sources that emphasize state success.

## What Changed

- Distinguished the 192 CE Qingzhou population-conversion phase from the 196 CE formal Xudu implementation.
- Added the earlier Western Han frontier branch and broadened the concept from refugee conversion to garrisoned territorial consolidation.
- Added Hanji 1104's stronger claim that agricultural logistics, not initial wealth alone, created durable northern-war capacity.
- Migrated the page to synthesis-v1 and made land-conflict and numerical limits explicit.
