# YuanDan Festival Layering / 元旦节日层累

## Definition

YuanDan festival layering is the accumulation and reassignment of New Year meanings across mythical rulership, official calendars, names, sacrifices, harvest, food, protective objects, poetry, domestic reunion, and modern holiday administration.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-plus-zaiye-bujian-2022-qing-duo-guanzhao-2023-fjhgtqac9r-1rpjh6oubmspqywue|Hanji 150 PLUS]] moves from [[YaoShunYuLegend|尧舜禹传说]] and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s calendar standardization into textual, food, plant, doorway, poetic, and blessing practices. [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq|怪东西 episode 11]] sharpens the naming boundary: 过年 and year-turning rites are much older than the modern use of 春节 for lunar New Year, while Gregorian 元旦 and lunar 春节 became an administrative pair only in the early Republic.

Together the sources show continuity through repeated reassembly rather than one fixed package. Calendar authority defines when the year begins; agriculture and sacrifice supply harvest, ancestor, and renewal frames; households materialize the turn through food, money, plants, doors, greetings, and reunion; governments and later speakers rename or reinterpret the same temporal threshold.

## Key Claims

- A recognizable year-turn can persist while its calendar, public name, ritual explanation, and household practice change.
- Mythic origin stories are traditional frames rather than secure chronology for a complete festival.
- Calendar standardization determines public rhythm and ritual timing as well as astronomical calculation.
- The Taichu reform is an important anchor for the first-month year beginning, but the source record includes earlier and later alternatives.
- Modern 春节 naming should not be projected unchanged into ancient references to spring or New Year.
- Food, plants, protective objects, money, poetry, and greetings make official time socially tangible.

## Evidence

- Myth, calendar, and texts - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-plus-zaiye-bujian-2022-qing-duo-guanzhao-2023-fjhgtqac9r-1rpjh6oubmspqywue]] connects Yao-Shun-Yu tradition, Han Wudi, *Jin Shu*, *Jingchu Suishi Ji*, *Simin Yueling*, and Lu You.
- Household materiality - [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-150-plus-zaiye-bujian-2022-qing-duo-guanzhao-2023-fjhgtqac9r-1rpjh6oubmspqywue]] records pepper-cypress wine, peach decoction, eggs, soup cakes, New Year cakes, and peach charms.
- Festival duration and agricultural roots - [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq]] treats the festival as a season from late-year preparation through Lantern Festival and links year-end celebration to harvest and sacrifice.
- Name reassignment - [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq]] distinguishes ancient uses of 春节 from the early-Republic naming of lunar New Year as 春节 alongside Gregorian 元旦.
- Gift layer - [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq]] adds 厌胜钱 and later monetary 压岁钱 as changing protective and familial practices.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both sources are compact podcast overviews rather than complete histories of Chinese calendars, terminology, regional customs, numismatics, or ritual. The earliest use of 元旦, the 1914 naming date, the exact persistence of Qing terminology, the 厌胜钱-to-压岁钱 path, and the scale and timing of monetary diffusion remain source-scoped. The concept does not imply that every region or household observed the same sequence or attached the same meanings.

## What Changed

- Distinguished the long history of 过年 from the comparatively recent official pairing of 元旦 and 春节.
- Added harvest, sacrifice, festival-season duration, and changing New Year money to the existing food and protective-symbol layers.
- Qualified the Taichu anchor by noting that year beginnings changed before and after it.
