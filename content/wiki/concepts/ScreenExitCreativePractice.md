# Screen Exit Creative Practice / 离开屏幕的创作实践

## Definition
Screen exit creative practice is a deliberate move from digital product surfaces toward physical spaces, objects, handwork, retail, markets, or craft as a way to recover contact with material work and personal meaning.

## Current Synthesis
In [[anyway-195-anyway-195]], the screen exit appears through [[UXCoffee|UX Coffee]]'s shift from digital-product media toward a bookstore, market stalls, goods, ceramics, glassware, and future maker/craft interviews. The move is not anti-digital nostalgia. It is a response to changing life priorities, AI-era design uncertainty, and the desire for an outlet where work has material resistance and cultural texture.

The concept also adds a cost boundary. Physical projects cannot be completed only with late-night individual effort: they require supplier schedules, daytime market work, logistics, inventory, storage, and interpersonal coordination.

## Key Claims
- Leaving the screen can be a creative outlet for designers and podcasters whose digital-work motivation has changed.
- Physical practice makes material constraints visible through sourcing, packing, display, storage, and market presence.
- The move can preserve continuity with earlier design interests because curation, object selection, and spatial experience are still design work.
- Offline practice can create cultural translation through books, goods, and crafts rather than only through media explanation.
- The source frames screen exit as a privileged but concrete option, enabled by tech-work income, time, and accumulated interests.

## Evidence
- Project evidence: [[anyway-195-anyway-195]] describes UX Coffee preparing a physical bookstore and doing repeated offline market stalls.
- Labor evidence: [[anyway-195-anyway-195]] contrasts podcast production that can happen late at night with physical work requiring supplier communication and weekend daytime stalls.
- Continuity evidence: [[anyway-195-anyway-195]] says the hosts' inner interests have not changed completely; they have shifted from digital products toward industrial design, crafts, and physical culture.
- Privilege evidence: [[anyway-195-anyway-195]] notes that big-tech employment, stock, time, and economic space make the bookstore experiment possible.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Screen exit is not universally available. The source explicitly recognizes that time, income, location, and financial cushion condition whether someone can treat a physical project as an outlet. It also does not show whether the offline project will become sustainable beyond the initial experiment.
