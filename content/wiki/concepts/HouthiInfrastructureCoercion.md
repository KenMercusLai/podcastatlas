# Houthi Infrastructure Coercion

## Definition

Houthi infrastructure coercion is the use of drones and missiles against airports, aircraft, energy facilities, and other high-visibility assets to impose economic, political, and reputational costs on [[SaudiArabia]] without requiring decisive territorial victory in [[Yemen]].

## Current Synthesis

[[saudi-under-siege-houthis-attack-airport-cc6579d3f0d15fe71826fc6b995c02cc]] presents infrastructure attack as a strategic substitute for battlefield breakthrough. Strikes can cancel flights, complicate international events, expose air-defence limits, and challenge the stability narrative required by [[SaudiVision2030]]. [[dunes-and-gloom-mbss-annus-horribilis-0a44ecf1bba6689b3b1e02af9ca13b2c]] adds weeks of attacks, closure of Riyadh's airport, a broader reported death toll, and cancellation of the Future Investment Initiative. The result is leverage through accumulated disruption and uncertainty rather than proof of decisive military victory.

## Key Claims

- Infrastructure attacks can generate leverage even when the ground war remains stalemated.
- Airports are unusually consequential targets because physical damage, passenger safety, airline confidence, and international visibility compound one another.
- Energy and transport attacks connect the Yemen war directly to Saudi diversification and investment goals.
- Improved Houthi drones and ballistic missiles make defence more difficult and widen the target set.
- Saudi response options remain constrained by fragmented allies, limited outside help, and costly negotiating demands.
- Coercion can weaken development credibility while simultaneously rallying domestic opinion around defense.

## Evidence

- Aviation disruption: [[saudi-under-siege-houthis-attack-airport-cc6579d3f0d15fe71826fc6b995c02cc]] reports repeated Riyadh airport attacks, three deaths, destruction of a Saudia aircraft, and hundreds of cancelled foreign-airline flights.
- Development pressure: [[saudi-under-siege-houthis-attack-airport-cc6579d3f0d15fe71826fc6b995c02cc]] links the attacks to disruption of the World Petroleum Congress and uncertainty around the Future Investment Initiative.
- Wider target set: [[saudi-under-siege-houthis-attack-airport-cc6579d3f0d15fe71826fc6b995c02cc]] also identifies Saudi Aramco facilities and Dammam airport as targets.
- Accumulated urban and event disruption: [[dunes-and-gloom-mbss-annus-horribilis-0a44ecf1bba6689b3b1e02af9ca13b2c]] reports weeks of attacks, airport closure, roughly 20 deaths in Riyadh, and cancellation of the Future Investment Initiative.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not establish that infrastructure coercion will force Saudi capitulation, permanently defeat air defences, produce a Houthi territorial victory, or threaten [[MohammedBinSalman|MBS]]'s rule. The earlier account reports three deaths in a specific airport strike while the later one reports roughly 20 deaths in Riyadh across a broader period; this likely reflects scope or timing but remains unreconciled. Continued attacks could also harden Saudi policy and increase domestic support for defense.

## What Changed

- Added accumulated urban disruption and cancellation of the Future Investment Initiative.
- Preserved differing casualty scopes rather than forcing a single total.
- Added rally-around-defense effects as a limit on coercive political impact.
