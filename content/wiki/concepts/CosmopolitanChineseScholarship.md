## Definition
Cosmopolitan Chinese scholarship is inquiry that uses international methods, comparison, institutions, and conversation to understand Chinese questions without treating either foreign knowledge or Chinese tradition as a closed authority.

## Current Synthesis
The two sources show worldliness as method and formation rather than status display. The modern-institutions episode follows scholars who combine overseas training, foreign collaborators, instruments, fieldwork, and global scholarly standards with Chinese land, buildings, language, archaeology, and textual inheritance. The Xu Zhuoyun episode adds a displaced historian's version: maps, world war, Taiwan, Chicago, Pittsburgh, Hong Kong, and mainland research networks produce a reciprocal method of seeing China from the world and the world from China.

## Key Claims
- International collaboration and education are valuable when they sharpen evidence, comparison, and local institutional capacity.
- Chinese historical and scholarly inheritance remains active when tested through fieldwork, archaeology, social science, and comparative questions.
- World perspective does not require abandoning cultural location; it can make inherited assumptions more visible and revisable.
- Migration and political rupture can widen intellectual networks while imposing real loss, precarity, and exclusion.
- Cosmopolitan method depends on continued access to sources, languages, colleagues, and current information rather than a one-time foreign credential.

- Tradition-and-method evidence: [[19-huangye-shang-de-dashi-wo-bei-yu-luanshi-qiu-yanjiu-ben-wei-niliu-zhi-wangju-565412437]] connects Xu Xiake, Yinxu, dialect fieldwork, buildings, and modern disciplines without opposing Chinese inheritance to global science.

- Transregional network evidence: [[503-wuxi-taibei-pizibao-fengjunwen-huiyi-shidai-jubian-zhong-de-lishixuejia-xuzhuoyun-llhgtepdszpyv65xkizxvaquicu]] traces scholarship through Taiwan, Hong Kong, Japan, North America, and renewed mainland archaeological contact despite Cold War division.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
International training can reproduce prestige hierarchy or shallow borrowing, and cultural identification can become exclusionary if treated as a fixed essence. The sources support reciprocal comparison and grounded evidence, not a claim that mobility automatically produces better scholarship or that one scholar represents all overseas Chinese intellectual experience.
