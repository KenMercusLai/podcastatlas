# Islamism

## Definition

Islamism is a political ideology that seeks to organize government, law, or public life around claimed Islamic principles. It is distinct from Islam as a religion, from Muslim identity, and from ordinary social conservatism.

## Current Synthesis

[[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] presents Islamism as a broad ideological family rather than a synonym for terrorism. Jihadists form a violent extreme, while other Islamists pursue influence through campaigning, protest, community representation, schools, mosques, local government, or [[IslamistEntryism]]. The episode argues that authoritarian, anti-pluralist, anti-integration, or unequal-rights commitments can remain politically consequential even when the actors reject violence.

The same source insists on a dual boundary: criticism of Islamism is not hostility toward Muslims, and concern about anti-Muslim bigotry does not require treating every political claim made in Islam's name as representative or immune from scrutiny.

## Key Claims

- Islamism concerns political and social rule, not Muslim belief or identity as such.
- Violence is one possible tactic within Islamism, not the category's defining feature.
- Non-violent Islamist activity can still conflict with pluralism, free expression, integration, or equal rights.
- Ideological sympathy, social conservatism, and organized movement membership are different phenomena.
- Muslims who are secular, female, or members of minority sects may face pressure from Islamist actors.

## Evidence

- Ideological definition: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] defines Islamism through efforts to make Islamic principles guide government, society, and law.
- Tactical range: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] distinguishes jihadist violence from lobbying, protest, and entryism.
- Liberal-democratic tension: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] associates the ideology's harder forms with opposition to pluralism, free expression, equal rights, and integration.
- Intra-Muslim harm: [[islamism-in-britain-what-is-the-threat-6aa9164ffca3d5c77b4b383a]] identifies secular Bangladeshis, minority sects, and women using family-dispute bodies as vulnerable groups.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Islamism is internally diverse, and the source does not supply a complete typology of its movements or theology. Polling support for Islamic principles in public life does not by itself prove authoritarian intent, entryist coordination, or membership in an Islamist organization. The source's numerical estimates and British cases should not be generalized automatically to Muslims elsewhere.

## What Changed

- Initial concept created with an explicit religion-ideology boundary and a violent/non-violent tactics distinction.
