# Soviet-Afghan War

## Definition
The Soviet-Afghan War was the conflict initiated by the [[SovietUnion|Soviet Union]]'s December 1979 intervention to replace Hafizullah Amin and sustain a friendly communist government against a widening Afghan insurgency.

## Current Synthesis
[[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] rejects a picture of automatic Soviet expansion. Soviet leaders recognized the risk of a Vietnam-like commitment and had warned Afghanistan's communist rulers that coercive transformation could provoke mass resistance, yet they invaded when the alternatives appeared worse.

The war then exposed the gap between holding cities and governing the countryside. Mujahideen groups used religion to coordinate resistance across some ethnic and tribal divisions and received money, weapons, volunteers, and political support through the [[UnitedStates]], [[Pakistan]], and Saudi Arabia. The resulting conflict caused mass Afghan death and displacement, fed transnational militant networks involving [[OsamaBinLaden|Osama bin Laden]], and left the state vulnerable to civil war and the later rise of the [[Taliban]].

## Key Claims
- Afghan communist factionalism and repression helped create the crisis before Soviet entry.
- Soviet leaders understood major intervention to be dangerous but chose it as a perceived lesser evil.
- Urban and military control did not produce durable control of rural society.
- External sponsorship internationalized and prolonged the insurgency.
- Religious identity supplied a coalition language against an officially atheist foreign occupier.
- The war's institutional and social damage continued after Soviet withdrawal.

## Evidence
- Decision for war: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] describes Soviet warnings, the failed poisoning of Amin, and the plan to install a friendlier government and withdraw.
- Military pattern: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] compares Soviet city control and search-and-destroy operations with difficulty in the countryside.
- External network and aftermath: [[87-afghanistan-part-1-glt7464087190]] connects American, Pakistani, and Saudi support to the mujahideen and later Taliban emergence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is a short survey, not a full military, social, or diplomatic history. Its funding totals, casualty estimates, account of pre-invasion support, and comparison with Vietnam remain source-scoped, while "mujahideen" should not be treated as one unified organization or ideology.

## What Changed
- Added the Soviet war as the modern hinge between communist state crisis, internationalized insurgency, civil war, and Taliban formation.
