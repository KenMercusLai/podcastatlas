# Counterfeit Authentication Arms Race / 仿冒鉴定军备竞赛

## Definition

A counterfeit authentication arms race is the adversarial cycle in which inspectors discover product or provenance signals, counterfeiters learn those signals, and both the object and its surrounding proof system are repeatedly modified.

## Current Synthesis

[[no-225-putian-xie-wangshi-gkwrijeowkkqa6hffgt1ih2w]] shows that authenticity cannot be reduced to a static visual checklist. Production variation can make genuine goods differ, highly similar counterfeits can defeat image review, and publicizing a detection detail can teach producers what to change next.

The attack surface also extends beyond the shoe. Sellers can splice genuine and counterfeit photos, seek inside knowledge, attempt bribery, or reproduce certificates, tags, packaging, webpages, QR codes, and tracking histories. A trust system therefore has to protect the inspection process, evidence chain, personnel, and digital verification surface as well as the physical product.

## Key Claims

- Visible product details lose discriminating power when counterfeiters can observe authentication criteria.
- Batch and factory variation in genuine goods creates uncertainty that simple binary checklists handle poorly.
- Image-only authentication is more exposed to substitution and collage than controlled physical inspection.
- Certificates, QR codes, packaging, and logistics records are themselves counterfeit targets.
- Trust requires secure process design, uncertainty reporting, staff integrity, and provenance controls rather than confidence theater.

## Evidence

- **Product-level adaptation:** [[no-225-putian-xie-wangshi-gkwrijeowkkqa6hffgt1ih2w]] describes a highly convincing shoe batch initially missed until a box-stamp detail was identified.
- **Inspection uncertainty:** [[no-225-putian-xie-wangshi-gkwrijeowkkqa6hffgt1ih2w]] notes genuine batch variation and platforms returning items as unable to authenticate.
- **Trust-layer attacks:** [[no-225-putian-xie-wangshi-gkwrijeowkkqa6hffgt1ih2w]] reports image splicing, attempted bribery, forged platform pages, reusable QR results, and large seizures of fake authentication materials.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode supplies selected cases, not measured false-positive or false-negative rates across platforms.
- Physical inspection can improve evidence quality but is not presented as infallible.
- Specific authentication criteria should not be generalized across brands, factories, product years, or batches.

## What Changed

- Added authentication as a dynamic adversarial system rather than a one-time expert judgment.
- Expanded the object of authentication from the shoe to its provenance, packaging, certificate, webpage, QR code, and personnel chain.
