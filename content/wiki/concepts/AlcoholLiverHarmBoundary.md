# Alcohol Liver Harm Boundary

## Definition
The alcohol liver harm boundary separates practices that may slow absorption or reduce dehydration and discomfort from the stronger claim that they prevent alcohol-related liver harm.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's clearest recommendation is to avoid or minimize alcohol because ethanol and acetaldehyde exposure is harmful. Food before drinking, slower pacing, avoiding mixed drinks, carbohydrate afterward, and water are discussed only as personal or symptom-oriented harm-reduction ideas. They do not convert drinking into liver protection.

## Key Claims
- Alcohol exposure is treated as harmful rather than health-neutral for the liver.
- Eating before drinking or slowing the pace may change absorption and subjective intoxication without canceling exposure.
- Water can address dehydration-related discomfort but is not an antidote to liver injury.
- Carbohydrate after drinking may support energy intake in context, but it does not make alcohol safe.
- Personal drinking rituals should not be generalized into medical prevention advice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish safe quantities, compare beverages, or provide treatment for alcohol use disorder or poisoning. The proposed yogurt “protective film,” food choices, pacing, and recovery practices are source-scoped personal experience or inference, not verified hepatology guidance.

## What Changed
- Initial synthesis records the difference between discomfort reduction and liver protection.
- The source's personal drinking practices remain explicitly non-generalizable.
