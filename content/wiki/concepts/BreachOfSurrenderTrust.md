## Definition

受降失信 / breach of surrender trust is the frontier-governance failure where an authority accepts, invites, or uses surrender contact and then kills or betrays the surrendering side, producing a tactical gain that damages later credibility.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 757 creates the concept through [[ZhangYuEasternHan|Zhang Yu]] and [[MiWuQiangLeader|Mi Wu]]. After Mi Wu is defeated, he sends an interpreter to request surrender and comes to Linqiang with his leaders. Zhang Yu then holds a banquet, poisons the wine, uses ambush troops, kills Mi Wu and many Qiang chiefs, and turns Mi Wu's head into revenge at [[FuYuEasternHan|Fu Yu]]'s grave.

Hanji 713 adds a city-scale conquest case. After [[YanCenEasternHan|Yan Cen]] offers [[Chengdu]] in surrender, [[WuHanEasternHan|Wu Han]] kills [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]]'s household and clan, destroys Yan Cen's clan, and allows looting and burning. [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]]'s rebuke makes the breach explicit: the city had surrendered and the officials and civilians had already submitted, so post-surrender plunder violates the protection expectation attached to surrender.

The immediate result is not pacification. [[MiTangQiangLeader|Mi Tang]] responds after his father's death by ending feuds with other groups, intermarrying, exchanging hostages, and concentrating a stronger rebellion in the large and small Yu valleys. Hanji 760 then supplies the later diagnostic layer through [[DengXunEasternHan|Deng Xun]], who treats prior bad faith as a cause of Qiang rebellion and chooses protective trust-building with threatened Hu groups instead.

Hanji 1137 adds an internal-regime aftereffect. [[WeiKangLateHan|韦康]] opens Ji and surrenders to [[MaChao|马超]], but Ma Chao reportedly kills him. The officials incorporated into the new order do not become reliable subordinates: [[YangFuLateHan|杨阜]] uses nominal service and funeral leave to recruit [[JiangXuLateHan|姜叙]], while outside forces and city insiders prepare a coordinated revolt. The case shows surrender betrayal converting administrative incorporation into concealed opposition.

## Key Claims

- Surrender contact carries a credibility obligation even in a violent frontier setting.
- The same obligation applies to surrendered cities and submitted civilians, not only to frontier envoys or banquet guests.
- Betraying surrender can produce revenge, concealed opposition, casualties, and short-term intimidation without producing durable settlement.
- Bad-faith settlement can make former rival groups cooperate because trust in the state falls below trust among themselves.
- Imperial condemnation can mark the breach, but without recorded sanction it does not by itself prove full institutional repair.
- Later pacification must account for earlier breaches; trust-building begins from a damaged baseline.
- This concept is distinct from false surrender by rebels: the breach here is committed by the receiving authority.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not say force is never used after surrender, nor does it reconstruct every legal norm around surrender. It applies to source patterns where surrender contact, banquet hospitality, or submitted-city status become tools or opportunities for killing. Hanji 757 reports immediate military success against remnants, so the qualification concerns durable credibility rather than casualties. Hanji 713 reports Liu Xiu's anger but not formal punishment for Wu Han or Liu Shang, proving the norm more clearly than the sanction mechanism. Hanji 1137 is a structured popular-history summary; Wei Kang's authority, surrender terms, exact killing circumstances, and the causal weight of the breach in the later revolt require broader verification.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 713 as a city-scale surrender breach: Yan Cen offers Chengdu, but Wu Han destroys households and permits sack until Liu Xiu rebukes the violation.
- Preserved Hanji 757's Zhang Yu banquet killing and Hanji 760's later Deng Xun trust-repair contrast.
- Added Wei Kang's reported killing as a late-Han case where incorporated officials become covert opponents.
- Extended the aftereffect from external coalition formation to an inside-outside conspiracy within an occupied city.
