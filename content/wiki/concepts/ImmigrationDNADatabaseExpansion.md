# Immigration DNA Database Expansion

## Definition
Immigration DNA database expansion is the shift in which DNA collected from immigration or federal detainees is sent into criminal-policing databases such as [[CODIS]].

## Current Synthesis
The Marketplace Tech episode turns immigration detention into a data-infrastructure issue. [[USDepartmentOfHomelandSecurity|DHS]] and [[USImmigrationAndCustomsEnforcement|ICE]] are not only detaining people; through post-2020 DNA collection, they can create profiles that persist in an FBI-linked database and are searched later by policing agencies. That makes detention a gateway into long-term criminal-investigation infrastructure even when the person was not arrested on criminal probable cause.

## Key Claims
- Detention can become a DNA database intake path rather than only a temporary custody event.
- The post-2020 DHS ramp-up materially changes CODIS composition because millions of detainee profiles enter a database built mostly through criminal policing.
- Retention and removal rules are central because temporary detention can create long-lived database exposure.
- The expansion joins immigration enforcement with criminal-policing search infrastructure.
- Child-sampling records raise a separate implementation-risk signal when agency age thresholds are unclear or not followed.

## Evidence
- Intake-path shift - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] says a 2020 policy change let DHS ramp up detainee DNA collection and send profiles to the FBI.
- Scale change - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports nearly a million collections last year and about 3.3 million DHS additions to CODIS in recent years.
- Database composition - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] says federally detained profiles rose from 0.21% of CODIS and are projected by Georgetown to reach one-third by 2030.
- Implementation boundary - [[tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260831-0831-mp-tech-pod-128]] says records indicate sampling of children below a stated 14-year threshold.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives Georgetown's analysis and critique without a detailed agency defense. The exact legal status, expungement mechanics, projection assumptions, and reasons for below-threshold child samples remain unresolved.

## What Changed
- Initial synthesis defines DHS/ICE DNA collection as a database-expansion pattern that links immigration detention to future policing.
