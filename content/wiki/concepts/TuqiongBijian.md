# 图穷匕见 / Tuqiong Bijian

图穷匕见 / Tuqiong Bijian is the idiom-origin moment highlighted in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-2-qinwang-danan-busi-zhihou-ganle-jian-ni-buzhidao-de-shi-lidfjb6-nggubw8vpz5fvnfacrqu]]. As [[JingKe|荆轲]] presents the [[DuKangMap|督亢地图]] to [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]], the scroll reaches its end and the hidden dagger appears.

The concept is useful because it marks the instant when an [[AssassinationAccessCredential|access credential]] changes function. Before the reveal, the map supports [[YanState|燕国]]'s staged surrender script; after the reveal, it becomes the delivery mechanism for a close-range attack inside the Qin court.

For the wiki, Tuqiong Bijian sits at the boundary between deception and exposure. It is not just a phrase meaning "the hidden intention is revealed"; in this source it is also a physical sequence where diplomatic performance, concealed weapon design, and [[WeakStateAssassinationStrategy|weak-state assassination strategy]] meet in one gesture.

## Key Claims
- The idiom is attached to Jing Ke's map presentation and dagger reveal.
- The map works first as political evidence and then as weapon concealment.
- The moment shows that access devices can succeed tactically while still leaving the actual strike vulnerable to timing, grip, distance, and team failure.
