# Sovereign Infrastructure Interdependence

## Definition
Sovereign infrastructure interdependence is the condition in which a state or company seeks control over data, models, and compute while remaining materially dependent on foreign cloud platforms, capital, chips, software, or networks.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes political demands for technological sovereignty from the practical ability to decouple. Ownership labels can conceal an international dependency stack, so sovereignty is better assessed by substitutability, control, jurisdiction, financing, and operational continuity than by company nationality alone.

## Key Claims
- Sovereignty is a layered capability rather than a label attached to a firm or data center.
- Cloud, capital, models, hardware, and software can create cross-border dependence even when data residency is local.
- Deep technical coupling makes digital decoupling harder than switching a commodity supplier.
- Diversification and coordinated transition are more plausible than immediate technological separation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source's percentage claim about Mistral's U.S. composition is rhetorical and unverified.
- Interdependence does not make sovereignty meaningless; jurisdiction, portability, local capacity, redundancy, and exit rights can still change exposure.
- Strategic diversification may be costly and incomplete without being futile.

## What Changed
- Established an infrastructure-level qualification to simple national-sovereignty and decoupling claims.
