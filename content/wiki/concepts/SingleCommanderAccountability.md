## Definition

Single-commander accountability is a command design in which one person holds final operational authority while other senior figures retain explicit supporting roles, so disagreement does not leave action without a recognized decision owner.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1169-2026-gai-zenme-shou-zhu-zishen-de-fuqi-lu-3dpvj0nbqhise2aqx-a3zi3kp|Hanji 1169]] grounds the concept in [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]]'s objection to sharing equal command with [[SunJiaoLateHan|孙皎]]. Lyu Meng invokes the earlier Zhou Yu-Cheng Pu conflict to argue that two senior commanders with unclear final authority can turn status disagreement into operational delay. [[SunQuan|孙权]] responds by giving Lyu Meng command and placing Sun Jiao in support.

The synthesis is narrower than “one leader is always better.” The mechanism depends on role clarity, competence, information flow, and real accountability. A sole commander who suppresses correction or lacks local knowledge can create a different bottleneck; the useful design is final decision ownership combined with defined subordinate authority and usable dissent.

## Key Claims

- Equal status without a final decision rule can convert disagreement into delay or fragmented execution.
- Final authority and responsibility should be located together rather than split between a nominal leader and an operational actor.
- Supporting senior commanders still need defined authority, information access, and a path to raise objections.
- Historical conflict can be used as organizational evidence without proving that every co-command arrangement will fail.
- Unity of command does not substitute for competence, logistics, intelligence, or review.

## Evidence

### Final authority with a supporting commander
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1169-2026-gai-zenme-shou-zhu-zishen-de-fuqi-lu-3dpvj0nbqhise2aqx-a3zi3kp|Hanji 1169]] says Lyu Meng opposes equal co-command, cites the Zhou Yu-Cheng Pu precedent, and receives final command while Sun Jiao is assigned to assist.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supplies one popular-history account, not a comparative study of command structures. Co-command can work when jurisdiction, escalation rules, and shared intent are explicit; single command can fail through overload, bad judgment, information suppression, or unreviewable authority. The reported dialogue, precedent, titles, and exact division of responsibilities remain source-scoped.
