## Definition

分隔战区支援风险 / fragmented-theater support risk is the vulnerability created when a polity's forces and key territories are separated by distance, terrain, or narrow transport routes, so local commands can face multiple threats faster than the wider system can reinforce or coordinate them.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1165-zhangji-zhuan-didiao-caishi-dingji-shengcun-zhihui-lr7x5qltfi_ikktqabowt1garphl|Hanji 1165]] applies the pattern to Liu Bei's apparent 219 high point. Yizhou, Hanzhong, Jingzhou, and the Fangling–Shangyong extension enlarge the political map, but they do not form an equally fast support network. Jingzhou relies heavily on water connection to Yizhou, while [[GuanYu|关羽]] faces both Cao Cao and Sun Quan.

The risk is not territorial size by itself. It arises when threat tempo exceeds communication, movement, reserve, and alliance reliability. Strong local commanders such as [[WeiYan|魏延]] can add autonomy and resilience, but they do not remove the need for routes, reserves, shared priorities, and timely reinforcement.

## Key Claims

- Territorial gain can increase exposure faster than it increases usable support capacity.
- Separated theaters require transport routes, delegated authority, reserves, and shared strategic priorities to function as one system.
- A commander facing threats on more than one frontier may be locally strong yet systemically isolated.
- Political high points can therefore conceal declining recoverability when rapid reinforcement is difficult.

Two-front exposure:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1165-zhangji-zhuan-didiao-caishi-dingji-shengcun-zhihui-lr7x5qltfi_ikktqabowt1garphl|Hanji 1165]] emphasizes Guan Yu's simultaneous exposure to Cao Cao and Sun Quan and the difficulty of rapid relief.

Expansion pressure:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1165-zhangji-zhuan-didiao-caishi-dingji-shengcun-zhihui-lr7x5qltfi_ikktqabowt1garphl|Hanji 1165]] places the Fangling–Shangyong advance beside, rather than after resolving, the existing multi-theater support problem.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source provides a strategic interpretation, not route-time calculations, force tables, order logs, or a counterfactual showing that faster reinforcement would have changed later events. Distributed positions can create depth, multiple axes, and local initiative when logistics and command are adequate. The concept therefore identifies a conditional risk rather than proving that territorial expansion was inherently mistaken or that geography alone caused Guan Yu's later defeat.

## What Changed

- Created a concept separating visible territorial reach from actual reinforcement capacity.
- Added two-front exposure and recovery time as mechanisms of fragmented-theater risk.
