# T-Cell Engagers

T-cell engagers, or TCEs, are explained in [[vol-117-shengwu-yiyao-de-2025-chaodi-zhongguo-yanfa-jiaolv-he-xinwang-jiwei-lmhral0rmq6tohiqdwsgmfapnyn7]] as a class of bispecific-like drugs that connect tumor cells or disease-relevant cells to T cells. The episode treats them as a possible lower-cost, lower-intensity alternative to [[CARTCellTherapy|CAR-T]], not as a full replacement.

The source's main insight is spillover. CAR-T's logic of redirecting immune cells creates interest in TCEs, especially in autoimmune diseases where clearing abnormal B-cell populations may be useful. The same immune activation that creates efficacy can also create toxicity if engagement is too broad or non-specific.

[[156-shengwu-yiyao-de-2026-dang-shichang-bu-zai-wei-bd-zaodong-zhongguo-yaoqi-de-xingchen-dahai-cai-ganggang-zhankai-lil-ugrzq8uvzviq3f8i-wm9ilup]] adds the direct competition case. In multiple myeloma, the episode says [[JohnsonAndJohnson|Johnson & Johnson]]'s TCE and combination data can pressure [[LegendBiotech|Legend Biotech]]'s CAR-T expectations, showing that TCEs can be commercially disruptive even if they are not a universal CAR-T replacement.

## Key Claims
- TCEs are cheaper and easier to administer than patient-specific CAR-T in the source's account.
- The episode expects weaker effects than CAR-T, so the tradeoff is access and cost versus potency.
- Autoimmune disease interest comes from the possibility of clearing pathological B cells without full CAR-T manufacturing.
- Non-specific immune activation remains a safety risk, connecting the concept to [[CytokineReleaseSyndrome]].
- Episode 156 adds that TCEs can compete with CAR-T in specific disease-area portfolios when data, convenience, and partner strategy line up.
