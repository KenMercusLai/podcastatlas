# 襄王有意，神女无心 / Xiangwang Youyi, Shennv Wuxin

襄王有意，神女无心 / Xiangwang Youyi, Shennv Wuxin is the one-sided desire motif [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-2-lt1nwraiuuvpy2tzo-zwaoqsajzx]] uses to resolve its title question about a "著名舔狗." The episode traces the phrase to the [[SongYu|宋玉]] and [[WushanGoddess|巫山神女]] tradition around 《神女赋》.

The motif depends on contrast. [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] receives a dream encounter with the goddess in the Gaotang story, but [[MiHeng|楚顷襄王]] cannot reproduce his father's experience by desire alone. He imitates the setting, waits for the goddess, and receives only a dreamlike refusal. The phrase therefore becomes a literary way to describe unreciprocated affection or projection.

For the wiki's Chu branch, the concept matters because it shifts Mi Heng's memory from pure political failure to cultural afterlife. The same ruler previously associated with hostage rupture, Qin pressure, loss of the capital region, and late [[ZhuangXin|庄辛]] repair is also preserved as "襄王" in a love-and-dream idiom. The episode's modern joking frame should remain source-scoped; the durable knowledge claim is about how political biography, myth, and literary reception can collapse into a short idiom.

## Key Claims
- The phrase names asymmetric desire: one party is invested, while the other remains unavailable or uninterested.
- In this source, the asymmetry is staged through failed repetition; Chu Qingxiang Wang wants the dream pattern that had belonged to Chu Huaiwang.
- The motif links political memory to literary reception: Mi Heng's remembered name survives not only through state failure but through Song Yu's fu tradition.
- The episode's "舔狗" language is a modern comic gloss on the ancient motif, not a technical historical category.
