# Customer Reactivation

Customer reactivation is the growth pattern where a stalled or paused business starts by returning to people who already bought, used, referred, or showed meaningful interest. In [[advice-line-with-curt-richardson-of-otterbox-e94bf774-de11-4434-843b-a551309a6773]], [[GildedCoachTeas]] uses this frame after [[MarissaValenzuela]] takes a one-year hiatus and asks how to regain momentum.

[[GuyRaz]] argues that many businesses overfocus on acquisition while neglecting retention, especially when paid acquisition has become expensive. In the Gilded Coach case, reactivation can mean identifying repeat customers, contacting them directly, offering samples or incentives, testing new blends, and learning which fairy-tale stories or tea-party bundles still create [[CustomerPull]].

## Key Claims
- A pause does not reset a company to zero if prior buyers can still be reached and learned from.
- Reactivation is stronger when it produces behavior: repeat purchase, feedback, sharing, event interest, or use-case expansion.
- Existing customers can help a founder separate the product people remember from the brand story the founder intended.
- Reactivation can be cheaper and more informative than broad customer acquisition when the brand's current message is still being rebuilt.
- For story-led products, reactivation should test whether the story still makes the product easier to buy, gift, or use.
