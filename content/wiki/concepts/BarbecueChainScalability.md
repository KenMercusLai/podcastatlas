# Barbecue Chain Scalability / 烧烤连锁规模化

## Definition

Barbecue-chain scalability is the ability of a barbecue restaurant format to reproduce local demand, food quality, labor execution, supply, store utilization, and capital returns across many locations without allowing expansion speed to destroy the product or economics.

## Current Synthesis

[[gaojiesi-huoguo-liansuo-neng-kai-shang-qianjia-tongyang-zuo-jucan-shengyi-de-shaokao-pinpai-weihe-kuozhang-nan-1023993593]] argues that the gap between large hotpot and barbecue chains is cumulative rather than attributable to one missing tactic. Barbecue begins with a smaller market split among skewers, grilled meat, local tastes, store types, and price bands. A high-spend mall format proven in Beijing or Shanghai can therefore fail in lower-density markets that prefer cheaper street stores and trust familiar local operators.

The production system also travels poorly. Central preparation cannot eliminate cutting, skewering, marinating, grill timing, heat control, and final-service variation. Moving the work to customers can damage quality, machines accept only some ingredients and shapes, and trained staff create a labor and supervision burden. Franchising can supply capital and local operators, but it expands the number of points at which sourcing and cooking can drift; direct operation reduces some drift while concentrating financial risk at headquarters.

Finally, scale depends on how often an asset can earn. Dinner and late-night concentration, group-dining orientation, weak lunch demand, rapid delivery-quality loss, limited solo use, and seasonal demand reduce utilization. Thousand-store expansion is therefore a joint test of addressable demand, process modularity, local proof, quality governance, time-of-day breadth, and capital—not simply brand awareness or total restaurant-market size.

## Key Claims

- **Addressable demand is narrower than category totals imply.** Barbecue divides by food form, region, price, location, and local habit, limiting the market available to one standardized format.
- **Local proof does not transfer automatically.** Mall rents and higher spending can work in dense first-tier cities while failing against lower-priced street stores and trusted local competitors elsewhere.
- **Final cooking remains a replication bottleneck.** Cutting, skewering, marinating, heat, timing, seasoning, and service introduce variation after centralized procurement.
- **Ownership structure redistributes rather than removes risk.** Franchising accelerates rollout and shares capital burden; direct operation increases control while concentrating fixed cost and management demands.
- **Narrow occasions depress store utilization.** Dinner, late night, groups, warm-weather demand, and poor delivery performance leave fewer earning windows than more flexible formats.
- **Scale is an interacting system.** Improving automation or marketing alone cannot compensate when demand fit, food quality, labor, seasonality, and store economics remain misaligned.

## Evidence

- **Market and format segmentation:** [[gaojiesi-huoguo-liansuo-neng-kai-shang-qianjia-tongyang-zuo-jucan-shengyi-de-shaokao-pinpai-weihe-kuozhang-nan-1023993593]] reports a 2025 barbecue market around 40% of hotpot's size and notes that barbecue itself includes multiple formats and regional price expectations.
- **Geographic transfer:** [[gaojiesi-huoguo-liansuo-neng-kai-shang-qianjia-tongyang-zuo-jucan-shengyi-de-shaokao-pinpai-weihe-kuozhang-nan-1023993593]] says nearly 60% of barbecue stores were in third-tier or lower cities by February 2026, while [[HenjiuyiqianYangrouchuan|很久以前羊肉串]] remained concentrated in Beijing and Shanghai with a higher-spend mall model.
- **Process replication:** [[gaojiesi-huoguo-liansuo-neng-kai-shang-qianjia-tongyang-zuo-jucan-shengyi-de-shaokao-pinpai-weihe-kuozhang-nan-1023993593]] contrasts hotpot's customer-completed cooking with barbecue's cutting, skewering, marinating, machine limits, and trained grilling.
- **Control and capital:** [[gaojiesi-huoguo-liansuo-neng-kai-shang-qianjia-tongyang-zuo-jucan-shengyi-de-shaokao-pinpai-weihe-kuozhang-nan-1023993593]] frames franchising as faster but harder to govern and direct operation as more controllable but more demanding in rent, fit-out, labor, and headquarters capital.
- **Utilization:** [[gaojiesi-huoguo-liansuo-neng-kai-shang-qianjia-tongyang-zuo-jucan-shengyi-de-shaokao-pinpai-weihe-kuozhang-nan-1023993593]] reports weak lunch, unsuccessful delivery, limited solo-dining development, and demand concentrated in June through August.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The framework explains constraints, not an impossibility theorem. Automation, narrower menus, regional formats, smaller footprints, mixed menus, improved packaging, or franchise governance could change the result.
- Hotpot is a comparison case, not a perfect control: category size, cooking responsibility, meal temperature, delivery suitability, and existing supply ecosystems differ.
- Market size and store-distribution figures do not establish profitability, and the source provides no store cohorts, closure rates, cash returns, franchise contracts, or audited margins.
- Beijing and Shanghai concentration may reflect deliberate strategy as well as inability to expand; the source does not separate those explanations.

## What Changed

- Established a multi-constraint framework for why barbecue chains may scale more slowly than hotpot chains.
- Joined local-market fit and cooking-process variation to ownership, capital, and asset-utilization constraints.
- Preserved the possibility that operating innovation can relax individual constraints without assuming it resolves the whole system.
