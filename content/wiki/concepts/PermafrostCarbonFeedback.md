# Permafrost Carbon Feedback

## Definition
Permafrost carbon feedback is the warming-amplification process in which thaw exposes long-frozen organic material to decomposition, releasing carbon that can contribute to further warming and additional thaw.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ce411815c1cca7882f5b93d662620544-51879597-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ce411815c1cca7882f5b93d662620544-51879597]] uses [[ChrisHiemstra]]'s Alaskan permafrost tunnel to make the mechanism concrete. Preserved plant, animal, and fungal material becomes available for decomposition after thaw, so a consequence of warming can also become an amplifier.

The episode's larger contribution is communicative. The process is geographically distant from many audiences, gradual at human attention scales, and difficult to experience directly, making it a case for [[WarningReceptionFramework]] as well as a climate mechanism.

## Key Claims
- Permafrost preserves organic carbon while frozen.
- Thaw allows biological decomposition that can release carbon to the atmosphere.
- Added atmospheric carbon can intensify warming and further thaw.
- The feedback framing matters because it makes permafrost an active climate-system response, not only a passive casualty.
- Geographic distance, gradual change, and scientific distrust make the warning difficult to communicate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source summary does not distinguish carbon dioxide from methane pathways, time scales, regional variability, abrupt-thaw processes, or model uncertainty.
- Its claim that permafrost stores roughly twice the carbon currently in the atmosphere requires verification against current primary assessments.
- A feedback can amplify warming without implying a single inevitable threshold or making every local thaw observation attributable to one cause.
