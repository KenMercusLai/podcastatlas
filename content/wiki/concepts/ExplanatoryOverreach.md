# Explanatory Overreach

## Definition
Explanatory overreach occurs when the coherence, simplicity, breadth, emotional satisfaction, or identity value of an account is treated as stronger evidence than its observations, mechanism, exceptions, and rivals justify.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-d08500083dda91b814b053f30bd7b557-87666361-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-d08500083dda91b814b053f30bd7b557-87666361|The Story of Stories]] presents explanation as indispensable and risky. People prefer accounts that unite many observations with few exceptions, but a noisy world may offer only partial regularities. Overreach begins when virtues become vices: a candidate interview story displaces broader evidence, a stereotype survives through exception-making, evidence order changes a jury's preferred narrative, or random events are joined under one intentional actor.

Uncertainty and low perceived control can intensify the demand for pattern and intention. [[ConspiracyTheoryPatternSeeking]] is a strong case of over-explanation, but the concept is broader: political, interpersonal, legal, and hiring judgments can also protect an attractive model. Correction requires specifying mechanism, tracking exceptions, asking what would change the account, and constructing alternatives.

## Key Claims
- Explanatory appeal and evidential strength must be assessed separately.
- Broad, simple accounts can be useful, but exception-free order can discard real partial patterns or invent false total ones.
- Identity and prior belief can absorb conflicting evidence by classifying it as error or exception.
- Narrative ordering can influence judgment even when underlying evidence is unchanged.
- Uncertainty and low control can increase pattern perception and intentional hidden-cause explanations.
- Detailed mechanism and rival-explanation tests are practical safeguards.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Simple explanations are not inherently false, real conspiracies occur, and an exception does not automatically invalidate a useful probabilistic rule. The episode's claims are compressed summaries without full methods. The concept should not dismiss an unpopular account by label; the test is evidence, mechanism, alternatives, scope, and falsifiability.
