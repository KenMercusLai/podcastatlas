# Repeated-Contact Dating

## Definition
Repeated-contact dating is the practice of creating romantic opportunity through recurring social settings where people see one another's behavior over time rather than relying only on stranger approaches, profiles, or one-off interview-like dates.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents repeated contact as the practical counterweight to app and stranger dating. Parties, hikes, shows, sports, barbecues, labs, churches, running clubs, improv classes, friend groups, and other recurring settings let people observe how someone treats others, handles responsibility, and participates in ordinary life.

Repeated contact matters because attraction often starts uncertain and middling, then accumulates through moments. It gives [[IdiosyncraticAttraction]], [[SharedRelationshipNarrative]], and [[PerceivedSimilarityInRelationships]] more room to form than a profile or first-date checklist can provide.

## Key Claims
- Most relationships need not begin with overwhelming first-impression attraction; middling uncertainty can change through shared experience.
- Group and activity settings reveal behavior that ordinary dates or apps can hide.
- Recurring groups reduce the difficulty of approaching strangers by making interest emerge inside a known social context.
- Repeated contact can support safer dating because friends, groups, and public activity provide more information than isolated persuasion.
- The approach complements [[FamiliarSpaceDating]] but is broader than bringing a date to one known venue.

## Evidence
- Slow accumulation: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] says most relationships do not begin with an overwhelmingly positive first impression and that attraction often accumulates gradually.
- Better environments: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] discusses parties, hikes, shows, sports, barbecues, labs, and other contexts where behavior can be observed.
- Stranger boundary: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] argues that approaching strangers romantically or sexually is difficult because humans did not evolve around large numbers of strangers.
- Recurring pool design: [[science-of-attraction-compatibility-romance-dr-paul-eastwick-scim9984287085]] recommends churches, running clubs, improv classes, and other recurring groups that repeatedly put people near others.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Repeated contact is not a guaranteed dating protocol. The source does not compare the success rates of particular clubs, apps, parties, or activities, and Eastwick explicitly leaves several practical strategy questions unresolved. Recurring groups can also create gossip, awkwardness, or pressure if participants treat them only as dating funnels.
