# Mandatory Treatment Homelessness Policy

Mandatory treatment homelessness policy is the approach [[SpencerPratt|Spencer Pratt]] advocates in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-spencer-pratt-on-fixing-la-wildfires-homelessness-corruption-the-fight-to-take-it-back-41236810]]. Pratt frames [[LosAngeles|Los Angeles]] homelessness primarily as a drug and treatment problem and calls for mandatory treatment, separated facilities for different populations, and large treatment campuses away from dense neighborhoods.

The concept is source-scoped and contested. It contrasts with [[SROLossAndHomelessness]], where a prior housing-policy source emphasizes the disappearance of cheap rooming-house supply as a homelessness driver. The wiki should preserve both causal frames until more sources connect or adjudicate housing supply, addiction, mental health, enforcement, and civil-liberties tradeoffs.

## Key Claims
- Pratt argues that allowing open drug use and disorder is not compassion when people remain untreated and neighborhoods deteriorate.
- The source treats treatment capacity, legal compulsion, facility separation, and public-order enforcement as linked parts of one policy.
- The approach depends on state capacity: treatment beds, staffing, legal authority, transport, accountability, and relapse management all have to exist.
- The episode does not examine objections from civil-liberties, housing-first, disability-rights, or treatment-provider perspectives.
