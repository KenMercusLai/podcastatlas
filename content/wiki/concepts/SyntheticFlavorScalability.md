# Synthetic Flavor Scalability / 合成风味可规模化

## Definition
Synthetic flavor scalability is the ability to build repeatable sensory profiles from essential oils, extracts, powders, compound seasonings, aroma chemicals, or biologically produced compounds that can be manufactured more consistently than the fresh ingredient alone.

## Current Synthesis
Flavor scale and ingredient scale are different. The black-truffle case shows how a scarce, seasonal aroma can travel through stable oil, powder, or seasoning systems; the [[Flavorman]] case shows the compositional layer beneath that result. A flavorist may combine dozens of components, including individually unpleasant notes, to choose a particular version of a fruit or invent a profile that has no direct natural original. Better analysis, extraction, and biological production expand the palette, but scalability does not establish authenticity, nutritional value, safety at unspecified doses, or consumer preference.

## Key Claims
- A sensory identity can scale even when the fresh ingredient is scarce, fragile, variable, or expensive.
- Repeatability across processing, transport, and storage can matter as much as raw-material cost.
- Recognizable flavors are designed interpretations assembled from components rather than perfect copies of one universal natural target.
- Dissonant aroma notes can add depth when balanced within a larger formula.
- Analytical, extraction, and biological-production advances can enable both closer imitation and wholly invented flavor profiles.
- Scalable flavor expands product variety while increasing the importance of accurate ingredient and flavor language.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Scalability does not establish equivalence to a fresh ingredient, ingredient authenticity, nutritional value, or preference. The sources do not provide controlled sensory comparisons, compound concentrations, or safety methods. “Synthetic” should not be treated as synonymous with unsafe or deceptive; trust depends on formulation, permitted use, labeling, and the claim being made.

## What Changed
- Expanded the concept from scarce-ingredient substitution to component-level flavor construction.
- Added flavorist interpretation and dissonant-note balancing as mechanisms of repeatable sensory design.
- Added analytical, extraction, and biological production as sources of a widening flavor palette.
