# Sexual History Evidence Interpretation

## Definition

Sexual history evidence interpretation is the contextual reading of diaries, letters, memoirs, fiction, pornography, legal proceedings, and scandal records as evidence that can reveal intimate life while also performing fantasy, status, shame, desire, or commercial appeal for an audience.

## Current Synthesis

The sexual past is neither inaccessible nor transparently recorded. Personal writings can approach beliefs, bodies, fear, and relationships unusually closely, yet even first-person accounts are selected and performed. Male-authored “whores' memoirs,” published adultery trials, erotic literature, and celebrity stories may describe real social worlds while reorganizing women's experience as entertainment.

The method therefore works at claim level: identify who produced the account, for whom, in what genre, under what incentives, and which parts concern practice, fantasy, reputation, law, or moral prescription. A gap between public rhetoric and behavior is itself historical evidence, but it cannot be measured by treating either side as a complete population record.

## Key Claims

- Intimate evidence can be unusually revealing without being unperformed or complete.
- Authorship, audience, genre, and commercial purpose shape how sexual experience is narrated.
- Prescriptive morality describes norms and anxieties more directly than it describes universal behavior.
- Legal scandal can preserve detail while selecting exceptional, prosecutable, or entertaining cases.
- Male-mediated accounts require special care when reconstructing women's experience and agency.
- Class and literacy determine whose sexual lives enter the archive and in what form.

## Evidence

- Personal and performed evidence: [[24-sex-in-the-city-glt9221135938]] presents diaries, memoirs, and letters as access to inner life while warning that sexual self-reporting can be performance.
- Gendered authorship: [[24-sex-in-the-city-glt9221135938]] contrasts rare female-authored testimony with male-voiced prostitution memoirs shaped for titillation and male readership.
- Public spectacle: [[24-sex-in-the-city-glt9221135938]] describes adultery trials and their published transcripts as moral controversy that also functioned as entertainment.
- Archive inequality: [[24-sex-in-the-city-glt9221135938]] warns that changes among literate classes are easier to observe than continuity or variation among the less documented majority.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Source performance does not make every intimate claim false, and mediated evidence can still preserve otherwise inaccessible social detail. Conversely, a vivid diary, memoir, or trial transcript is not automatically representative. The episode supplies a methodological orientation rather than a comprehensive archival protocol, and its examples require comparison with specialist legal, demographic, material, and medical scholarship.

- Established a sexual-history-specific extension of source criticism centered on intimacy, performance, gendered authorship, and archive inequality.
