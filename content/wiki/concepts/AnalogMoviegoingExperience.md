## Definition
Analog moviegoing experience is the event value created when physical film projection, venue history, communal attendance, and visible technical craft make a screening feel materially different from home digital viewing.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that analog moviegoing is not only nostalgia for older technology. Projected film becomes valuable because viewers cannot reproduce the same film stock, scale, booth labor, sound, and historic-theater atmosphere on TVs, computers, or phones.

The Alex Theatre case also shows how analog experience can be designed. Visible projection-booth windows turn technical labor into part of the event, while younger interest in learning projection and collecting tapes, LPs, and eight-tracks suggests a broader desire for tactile and shared media experiences. The source keeps that generational claim suggestive rather than settled.

## Key Claims
- Analog moviegoing creates event value by making the viewing format hard to reproduce at home.
- Historic theaters can act as living cultural infrastructure because generations of audience use become part of the experience.
- Visible projection labor can deepen audience attachment by showing the craft behind the image.
- Younger interest in analog media may reflect a search for physical connection, not only retro taste.
- The market can respond to analog demand when a venue combines scarce format access with a strong release-window film.

## Evidence
- Non-reproducible viewing - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] says Williams frames film projection as an event because audiences cannot reproduce it on home screens.
- Venue memory - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] records Williams's description of historic theaters as living museums shaped by generations of public entertainment.
- Craft visibility - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] says the Alex Theatre added booth windows so patrons can see projectionists and projectors working.
- Younger analog interest - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] notes apprenticeship emails, a young projectionist, a patron wanting to learn projection, and interest in tapes, LPs, and eight-tracks.
- Market response - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] links The Odyssey's 70mm demand to the Alex Theatre's retrofit and reported high-grossing performance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is strongest on one theater's practical and emotional case. It does not quantify national analog-media demand, prove a generational trend, or show whether 70mm demand persists after a Nolan-scale event.
