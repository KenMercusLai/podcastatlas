# Public Voting and Electoral Coercion

## Definition

Public voting and electoral coercion describes the mechanism by which openly declared votes let candidates, patrons, employers, neighbours, and crowds observe political choices and reward, pressure, or punish voters accordingly.

## Current Synthesis

In the source's pre-1872 elections, voters declared choices orally and publicly. That visibility linked campaigning practices such as treating, tally organization, landlord control, crowd intimidation, and abduction to a credible means of verifying whether pressure worked.

The secret ballot matters here as an institutional change in observability. It did not remove patronage, persuasion, or violence, but it weakened the enforcement loop by making a voter's actual choice harder for coercive actors to confirm.

## Key Claims

- Public oral voting made individual political choices observable.
- Observability increased the practical value of bribery, treating, patronage, and threats.
- Group arrival under tally captains combined participation with surveillance and partisan display.
- Social dependence could make a formally valid vote substantively unfree.
- Secret voting weakened coercion by separating promised support from verifiable choice.
- Institutional privacy reduced one mechanism of abuse without ending electoral intimidation.

## Evidence

- Vote visibility: [[463-mad-elections-part-1-glt4220133865]] says voters announced choices orally in public.
- Organized dependence: [[463-mad-elections-part-1-glt4220133865]] describes treating, landlord-created faggot voters, and tallies led by captains in candidate colours.
- Direct coercion: [[463-mad-elections-part-1-glt4220133865]] recounts blocked or intimidated voters at Coventry and kidnapped Conservative voters held and intoxicated in Bolton.
- Privacy reform: [[463-mad-elections-part-1-glt4220133865]] links the 1872 secret ballot to declining opportunities for verified bribery and coercion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not isolate the secret ballot's causal effect from franchise expansion, policing, party organization, social change, or legal enforcement. Treating could function as expected hospitality and partisan ritual as well as purchase, while public participation could express community membership even when it exposed voters to pressure.

## What Changed

- Created the concept around vote observability as an enforcement mechanism for electoral pressure.
