## Definition

Local official tenure stability is the governance principle that provincial, commandery, and county officials need enough continuous time in office for policy effects, public welfare, and administrative character to become observable before promotion, demotion, or transfer.

## Current Synthesis

The concept's earliest current reform program centers on [[ZuoXiongLateHan|左雄]]'s argument that local officials who rotate too quickly become short-term, reputation-seeking, greedy, or violent, while lawful officials can be misread as incapable because their effects are not instantly visible. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-811-gudai-huangdi-jing-kao-chouqian-xuan-huanghou-lghfih9xcrjyrvdlb4obdxvwhl1y|Hanji 811]], his proposal is to retain and promote good local officials, prevent unjustified departures, distinguish real from fake performance, and stop low-level offices from extracting administrative costs from the people.

A later institutional formulation appears through [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]]: governors and inspectors should not be moved too frequently, and local officials need at least a three-year term plus longer observation before the court can judge ability, record, and change over time. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-913-kan-dong-saiweng-shima-wu-tou-rensheng-fuhuo-lkfbrwtgsjma85bzugqonzk3hy4g|Hanji 913]] confirms the same logic under [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], though the ruler does not act.

A vacancy-before-tenure qualification also matters. [[CaiYong|蔡邕]]'s complaint in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-906-lihai-de-ren-weishenme-xiangshou-chongtu-lvc3wedrifdzdu-yv1qhocl8uss2|Hanji 906]] is not that [[YouzhouLateHan|幽州]] and [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]] officials rotate too quickly, but that overbroad [[SanhuLawAppointmentConstraint|三互法任官限制]] leaves inspector posts hard to fill at all. Tenure stability presupposes timely appointment of capable officials before multi-year evaluation can even begin.

## Key Claims

- Stable tenure gives local policy enough time to show results beyond immediate reputation, coercive display, or patronage timing.
- Tenure stability requires truthful inspection; otherwise longer terms can simply protect officials who manufacture reputation.
- Promotion and dismissal need an observation period that can test actual ability, public benefit, and change over time.
- Office continuity can fail before tenure starts when appointment constraints leave key provincial posts vacant.
- The repair is politically fragile when rulers or court factions refuse to implement tenure and evaluation reforms.

## Evidence

- Churn creates bad incentives: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-811-gudai-huangdi-jing-kao-chouqian-xuan-huanghou-lghfih9xcrjyrvdlb4obdxvwhl1y|Hanji 811]] says Zuo Xiong criticizes frequent local-official transfers for producing short-termism, greed, violence, and false quick results.
- Retention and promotion of good officials: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-811-gudai-huangdi-jing-kao-chouqian-xuan-huanghou-lghfih9xcrjyrvdlb4obdxvwhl1y|Hanji 811]] says Zuo wants capable taishou, xiang, county magistrates, and county heads who love the people to be promoted and kept in office rather than casually moved.
- Inspection truth as a condition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-811-gudai-huangdi-jing-kao-chouqian-xuan-huanghou-lghfih9xcrjyrvdlb4obdxvwhl1y|Hanji 811]] says Shun orders rules to distinguish real and false performance after Zuo criticizes inspectors who chase visible results without investigating.
- Multi-year evaluation proposal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-913-kan-dong-saiweng-shima-wu-tou-rensheng-fuhuo-lkfbrwtgsjma85bzugqonzk3hy4g|Hanji 913]] says Lu Zhi proposes that governors and inspectors not rotate too frequently and that local officials need at least three years plus longer observation for credible judgment.
- Vacancy-before-tenure qualification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-906-lihai-de-ren-weishenme-xiangshou-chongtu-lvc3wedrifdzdu-yv1qhocl8uss2|Hanji 906]] says Cai Yong's Sanhu-law complaint concerns posts that cannot be filled, showing that stable tenure depends on the appointment pipeline first.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Hanji 811 records imperial approval but also eunuch resistance, so it does not prove stable tenure was fully implemented.
- Hanji 913 records Lu Zhi's proposal under Emperor Ling but also shows the ruler ignoring the memorial, making the concept an unrealized repair in that layer.
- Hanji 906 qualifies the concept by showing that tenure stability cannot operate if formal constraints prevent key offices from being filled.
- Stable tenure alone can protect mediocrity or corruption unless paired with [[IndependentOfficialAudit]] and accountable appointment channels.
