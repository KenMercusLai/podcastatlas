# Landscape Fire Prevention

Landscape fire prevention is the sustained work of reducing fire risk before ignition or while a fire is still small. In [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]], [[OliverMorton]] says the ingredients are mostly known: landscape management, behavior controls, fire breaks, rapid detection, modeling, aircraft, drones, and best-practice sharing.

The source's point is that prevention is less a mystery than a commitment problem. As [[ExtremeWildfire]] becomes more common, the effort needed to manage vegetation, regulate risky behavior, fund detection, coordinate crews, and share lessons becomes harder to sustain. That makes the concept adjacent to [[DisasterResponseStateCapacity]] and [[FireTechClimateResilience]] rather than only to ecology.

## Key Claims
- Fire prevention depends on long-term maintenance, not only emergency heroics.
- Satellites can help detect fires quickly enough for early response, but detection still has to connect to crews and institutions.
- Fire spread modeling is useful but inherently difficult because fires are complex physical and ecological systems.
- Richer regions may add drones or electric aircraft, while poorer regions may need lower-cost governance and practice-sharing mechanisms.
