# Hostage-Ransom Deterrence / 劫质禁赎威慑

Hostage-ransom deterrence is the public-order rule pattern created from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-915-shishang-meiyou-zhenzheng-de-kouwu-zhehua-dui-ma-lgxcodjxnaozvyegxqurgzji5gus|Hanji 915]]'s account of [[QiaoXuanLateHan|桥玄]]. When Qiao's youngest son is abducted and ransom is demanded, Qiao refuses payment and later memorializes a ban on buying hostages back with private wealth.

The logic is severe: if hostage-takers can reliably convert elite affection into money, the crime becomes scalable. The state therefore removes the payoff and punishes private ransom payment, even though that choice can sacrifice the immediate hostage. The episode says the policy sharply reduced or ended this form of kidnapping; the wiki keeps that effect claim source-scoped.

[[LiuXiu|刘秀]]'s precedent involving [[YinLihua|阴丽华]]'s mother and brother gives the concept a higher-status comparison. The deterrence signal only works if powerful families are not exempt from the rule they impose on others.

## Key Claims
- Ransom can create future hostage-taking incentives when criminals learn that family attachment reliably produces payment.
- A no-ransom rule works only if high-status households do not buy their own exceptions.
- The rule is ethically costly because protecting future victims can require accepting the death of the current hostage.
- The episode presents [[QiaoXuanLateHan|桥玄]]'s case and [[LiuXiu|刘秀]]'s precedent as credibility signals, not as general advice for every hostage situation.
- The source's claim that kidnapping disappeared after the rule is recorded as source-scoped and should be checked by later sources before becoming a broad historical finding.
