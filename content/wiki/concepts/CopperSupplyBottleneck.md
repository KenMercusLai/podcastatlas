# Copper Supply Bottleneck

Copper supply bottleneck is the source-scoped constraint [[DanDreyfus|Dan Dreyfus]] emphasizes in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-dan-dreyfus-americas-critical-minerals-crisis-is-here-41594225]]. He treats copper as the "king of metals" because power generation, transmission, data centers, electric vehicles, solar, wind, military systems, and industrial rebuilding all draw on the same material base.

The argument matters because it ties AI deployment to [[CriticalMineralsGeopolitics]] and [[DataCenterPowerBottleneck]]. Dreyfus says a one-gigawatt AI factory would require 50,000 tons of copper, and that building many such facilities would absorb supply at a scale comparable to recent annual global copper-supply growth. Those figures are recorded as source claims rather than independently verified wiki facts.

## Key Claims
- Copper demand can rise from several directions at once: AI factories, grid hardening, EVs, solar, wind, power plants, defense, and reindustrialization.
- Mine development is slow, with Dreyfus describing seven-to-12-year copper mine timelines and aging ore grades.
- Recycling helps but is not presented as enough to satisfy the source's demand-growth scenario.
- Investors should distinguish durable copper scarcity from companies that may be technologically bypassed by substitution, redesign, or new extraction methods.
