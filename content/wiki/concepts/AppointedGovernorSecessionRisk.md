# 受任地方长官割据风险 / Appointed Governor Secession Risk

Appointed governor secession risk / 受任地方长官割据风险 is the mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-52-chenzhuang-panqin-duanqi-wan-chifan-fangxia-wan-ma-niang-ls13ixmbhflepjgpelxgu6idjqa2]] extracts from [[ChenZhuangWarringStates|陈庄]]'s rebellion. [[QinState|秦国]] conquers [[ShuStateWarringStates|蜀国]], lowers the old ruler to [[ShuHouNingTong|蜀侯宁通]], and appoints Chen Zhuang as chancellor. That delegated arrangement gives Qin a way to govern the new region, but it also gives one official a local power base.

The risk rises when three conditions stack together. The region is hard for the center to enter, so rebellion looks defensible; the region is productive, so independent control looks rewarding; and the appointee's status lets him imagine moving from manager to ruler. In the episode, [[BaShu|巴蜀]]'s terrain and grain value make that stack visible.

The concept differs from ordinary local resistance. Chen Zhuang is not presented as the conquered population reacting to abuse; he is a Qin-appointed insider trying to capture the territory he was entrusted to govern. That makes the case a useful counterpart to [[SichuanPostConquestResistance|川蜀征服后反抗]], where the wiki's later Song branch emphasizes conquest violence, tax burdens, and monopoly extraction.

## Key Claims
- Indirect governance can solve short-term control while concentrating local power in one appointee.
- Resource-rich geography raises rebellion incentives because the local office becomes a potential throne.
- Difficult terrain can make the appointee overestimate the center's inability to intervene.
- The central state still matters: Chen Zhuang's failure shows that secession risk becomes reality only if the appointee's estimate of central reach is correct.
