## Definition

Palace Accusation Verification / 宫廷指控核验 is the governance pattern where a ruler, regent, or senior court official slows a high-risk court or palace accusation, checks behavior and evidentiary fit, and avoids broad coercive interrogation when the likely cost is implicating innocent people.

## Current Synthesis

The positive Deng-regency version is preventive. In the jewel theft case, [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]] avoids dragnet questioning, personally inspects the relevant workers, and narrows the case until the thief confesses. In the Ji Cheng wugu case, she doubts accusations that look clear on the surface because they do not fit Ji Cheng's behavior or position near [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]], and repeated checking exposes a frame-up.

The Chu-prison material supplies the negative and repair version. [[EmperorMingOfHan|Mingdi]] initially lets the [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|Liu Ying]] treason case expand through torture and implication. Verification comes late: [[LuXuEasternHan|Lu Xu]] supplies conduct and family evidence, [[JianLangEasternHan|Jian Lang]] tests whether accusers can describe the people they name, and [[YuanAnEasternHan|Yuan An]] later reviews case files for actual support.

Hanji 576 adds a Western Han failure case. [[ZhangYouWesternHan|张尤]]'s self-protective report against [[FengYuanWesternHan|冯媛]] is not stopped by the first no-result inquiry because [[FuTaihouWesternHan|傅太后]] changes investigators and [[ShiLiWesternHan|史立]] expands the accusation through coercion. [[SunBaoWesternHan|孙宝]] attempts after-damage verification by asking to reopen the case, but the political sponsor is strong enough to have him jailed before other officials secure only his release. The concept therefore covers preventive anti-dragnet verification, late repair, and the failure mode where power blocks repair.

Hanji 572 adds a related Western Han evidence problem rather than a successful verification model. [[XieGuangWesternHan|谢光]]'s report on [[XuMeirenWesternHan|徐美人]]'s child uses a sealed box, restricted burial, [[JiWuWesternHan|纪武]]'s testimony, and [[WuQiuzunWesternHan|武秋尊]]'s fear-filled warning to reconstruct a palace accusation. The host's doubt about why Ji Wu was told the box contained a child corpse shows that verification must test not only witness statements but also whether the claimed secrecy procedure makes sense.

## Key Claims

- Court and palace accusations are dangerous because proximity, rivalry, ruler anger, mourning, hidden-harm claims, and treason language can turn weak evidence into punishment quickly.
- Verification includes behavioral fit, motive plausibility, concrete observation, chain-of-custody logic, and independent checks, not only surface confession or testimony.
- Avoiding dragnet interrogation can be an active justice strategy when broad coercion is likely to produce innocent victims.
- When verification comes late, it can still reduce harm, but it cannot undo deaths, exile, imprisonment, or stigma already produced.
- Wugu, treason, sealed-object, and secret-handling accusations require special caution because they can be hard to disprove, easy to weaponize, and prone to witness or chain-of-custody problems.
- The pattern strengthens court legitimacy when restraint produces a visible correction rather than a wider case.
- After-damage review can itself become punishable when reopening a case threatens the powerful figure who benefited from the accusation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept began as a Deng-regency page and should still emphasize restraint before coercion when possible. Hanji 740 broadens it carefully: the Chu-prison evidence shows late verification after an accusation cascade, not a fully successful procedure. Hanji 576 adds an even weaker repair outcome: Sun Bao survives, but Feng Yuan's case is not truly reversed. Hanji 572 supplies an evidentiary puzzle rather than a corrective success. Personal observation, appearance testing, file review, chain-of-custody testing, and reopening requests are examples of independent checks in these sources, not a complete legal code.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 572 as a sealed-box and fear-limited-reporting problem, where the issue is whether the accusation procedure itself makes evidentiary sense.
- Preserved Hanji 576's failed repair branch, Hanji 740's late-repair branch, and Hanji 777's preventive anti-dragnet branch.
