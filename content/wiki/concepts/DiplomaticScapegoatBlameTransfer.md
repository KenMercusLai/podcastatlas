## Definition

外交替罪式责任转嫁 / diplomatic scapegoat blame transfer is the crisis tactic in which a ruler sacrifices or punishes a subordinate to absorb a foreign counterpart's anger, converting a policy dispute into the visible removal of a blamed official.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-649-guanchang-li-de-renqing-shigu-you-duo-xianshi-ljcwds5n-xdxjo-ewt2arrgf9ib|Hanji 649]] grounds the concept in [[WangMang|王莽]]'s handling of a [[Xiongnu|Xiongnu]] dispute. After Xiongnu demands the return of the chanyu's son's body, Wang Mang fears that sending the corpse directly could provoke anger against Xin envoys. He therefore kills Chen Qing, the former general associated with the earlier execution proposal, under other charges before sending envoys, treasure, titles, and the corpse.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-554-chengnianren-de-dingji-zilv-kezhi-yuwang-lo3brdufkiaks3mfsqsruxprqdcn|Hanji 554]] supplies an earlier, nonlethal Western Han variant. [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] and [[WangGenWesternHan|王根]] keep a land-demand probe deniable by having [[XiahouFanWesternHan|夏侯藩]] speak in his own name to [[WuzhuliuChanyu|乌珠留单于]]. After Xiahou Fan presents the demand as imperial will and the chanyu reports it back, Chengdi answers that Xiahou Fan falsely invoked imperial command and moves him away from the Xiongnu channel. The subordinate is not killed, but he absorbs the diplomatic embarrassment created by a risky policy channel.

The concept therefore includes both lethal and administrative forms. The tactic may lower immediate diplomatic risk by presenting a punished or disowned responsible party, yet it also shows subordinates that policy failure can be made personal after the fact. Hanji 649's symbolic naming demand and continued Xiongnu raids show that scapegoating does not necessarily solve the underlying frontier relationship; Hanji 554 shows that disavowal can preserve imperial face while leaving the original desire and misjudgment exposed.

## Key Claims

- Scapegoating can make diplomatic repair legible by giving the offended side a named punished actor.
- The tactic shifts responsibility downward even when the original policy belonged to the ruler or regime.
- Killing or punishing a subordinate can protect envoys in the short term while damaging internal trust.
- Material gifts and ritual language can accompany scapegoating without producing real strategic settlement.
- The concept requires a foreign-facing blame-transfer function; ordinary court purge or domestic blame avoidance is not enough.
- The scapegoated subordinate may be killed, punished, transferred, or publicly disowned; the defining feature is foreign-facing responsibility absorption.

- Subordinate sacrifice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-649-guanchang-li-de-renqing-shigu-you-duo-xianshi-ljcwds5n-xdxjo-ewt2arrgf9ib|Hanji 649]] says Wang Mang has Chen Qing killed under other charges because Chen Qing had proposed the earlier execution.
- Ritual and gift repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-649-guanchang-li-de-renqing-shigu-you-duo-xianshi-ljcwds5n-xdxjo-ewt2arrgf9ib|Hanji 649]] says Wang Mang sends envoys, treasure, corpse return, and title language to Xiongnu.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources give Han/Xin-side narratives and host interpretation; they do not provide independent Xiongnu deliberation records. Chen Qing's broader biography, the exact legal charges, Xiahou Fan's full intent, and Chengdi's inner motive remain source-scoped. The concept should not be used for every diplomatic concession, only where blame is deliberately personalized through a sacrificial, transferred, or disowned official.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 554 as a nonlethal Western Han variant: Chengdi disowns Xiahou Fan's imperial-command claim after a deniable land probe fails.
- Preserved Hanji 649's Chen Qing branch as the lethal Wang Mang-era variant.
