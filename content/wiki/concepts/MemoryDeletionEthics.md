## Definition
Memory deletion ethics is the question of whether painful memory may be blocked, erased, or suspended, and who has authority to decide when relief would also change identity, responsibility, evidence, or relationship repair.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence now has two branches. The science-fiction branch uses veterans and war memory to ask whether trauma relief can be separated from grief, loved ones, public history, and collective accountability. The relationship branch uses an infidelity thought experiment to ask whether a couple that wants to continue should delete the betrayal memory or preserve it as warning, responsibility, and leverage.

Across both branches, pain relief is ethically serious but incomplete. Forgetting may be humane when a memory traps someone in suffering, yet deletion becomes dangerous when another person or institution decides what should be remembered, when accountability is erased with the pain, or when the remaining relationship depends on an unreal reset rather than repair.

## Key Claims
- Pain relief matters, but it cannot by itself answer who controls memory.
- Forgetting may protect a person from trauma while also erasing loved ones, evidence, responsibility, or repair signals.
- Collective, partner, or institutional decisions over memory can undermine private agency.
- Memory can function as accountability when the pain was caused by war, political greed, betrayal, or another responsible actor.
- Deleting a relationship wound can make continued life easier, but it may also remove the warning or moral work needed to prevent repetition.

## Evidence
- Trauma and agency: [[166-youyou-changfeng-wenrou-de-huanxiang-zhi-ge-fuping-jiaolv-de-gouhe-948043059]] develops the concept through [[MemoryDayStory|《记忆日》]], where veterans use a shielding tool to avoid living continuously with war memories.
- Loved ones and history: [[166-youyou-changfeng-wenrou-de-huanxiang-zhi-ge-fuping-jiaolv-de-gouhe-948043059]] treats forgetting as morally mixed because war memory may include a dead spouse, public commemoration, and responsibility for what caused the suffering.
- Decision authority: [[166-youyou-changfeng-wenrou-de-huanxiang-zhi-ge-fuping-jiaolv-de-gouhe-948043059]] states the boundary that no one should make another person's pain, memory, or death decision for them.
- Infidelity repair: [[ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur-ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur]] has panelists split over whether deleting both partners' memories of infidelity is rational relief or an evasion of responsibility.
- Warning and accountability: [[ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur-ln6gnhmsx1xo3p2n8z2powciqnur]] has Xuanzong prefer to keep betrayal memory as a warning if she is betrayed, while Luo Yonghao, Liu Hongwei, and Wang Jiye lean toward deletion when both people want to continue and the betrayer is deeply remorseful.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both source cases are hypothetical or literary rather than clinical evidence about real memory interventions. The science-fiction case centers war trauma and public memory, while the Luo Yonghao panel centers relationship repair and comic debate. Neither source should be read as medical, legal, or couple-counseling advice.
