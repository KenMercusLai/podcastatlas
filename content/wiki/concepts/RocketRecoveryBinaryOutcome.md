# Rocket Recovery Binary Outcome

Rocket recovery binary outcome is [[DongKai|董凯]]'s strict engineering standard in [[zhongting-zhuque-sanhao-fuzongshi-huojian-huishou-zhiyou-cheng-yu-bai-meiyou-zhongjiantai-s9e41-d9e11e3b-7c7e-4beb-b19f-6f90fff38714]]. For [[Zhuque3|朱雀三号]], he says recovery succeeds only if the first stage stands at the target site and remains still for one minute; otherwise the recovery portion fails.

The concept separates mission scoring from learning value. The 2025 first flight could be called basically successful because orbit insertion met its standard, but the booster did not land stably, so recovery itself remained a failure. At the same time, the flight still produced real data on re-entry ignition, aerodynamic descent, thermal conditions, and landing ignition, making it useful for later iteration.

## Key Claims
- A reusable-booster recovery should not be treated as partly successful when the vehicle does not land and remain stable.
- Binary outcome standards can coexist with engineering learning from failed tests.
- Clear success criteria prevent public narratives such as "almost recovered" from hiding the terminal-control work still required.
- The standard matters for [[ReusableRocketEconomics]] because only repeated, stable recovery can move from test achievement toward reliable transport infrastructure.
