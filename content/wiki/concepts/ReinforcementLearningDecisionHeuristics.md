# Reinforcement-Learning Decision Heuristics

## Definition
Reinforcement-learning decision heuristics are analogies that translate exploration versus exploitation, policy-value consistency, and discounted future reward into practical rules for learning and action under uncertainty.

## Current Synthesis
In the source, exploration means deliberately retaining time and randomness for unfamiliar paths instead of optimizing every current task. Policy-value consistency means testing stated beliefs through action and revising them from observed consequences. Discounting means valuing long horizons without assigning so much weight to noisy distant outcomes that present action becomes impossible.

These are reflective heuristics, not a claim that human lives can be optimized with a literal RL objective. Their value lies in making tradeoffs explicit and preserving feedback; their danger lies in flattening moral commitments, relationships, and structural constraints into reward metaphors.

## Key Claims
- Some apparently inefficient exploration is necessary to escape local optima.
- “90-point” execution can preserve capacity for experiments with uncertain upside.
- Values become more reliable when action exposes them to real feedback.
- Long-termism needs a practical discount on distant, noisy predictions.
- Entrepreneurship can provide rapid and integrated feedback for revising beliefs.

### Policy and value
- [[lkbt2upqw6eoxm0yjc1zklr8wlgc-lkbt2upqw6eoxm0yjc1zklr8wlgc]] connects “knowing and doing” to acting, collecting experience, and updating one's judgments.

### Discounted horizons
- [[lkbt2upqw6eoxm0yjc1zklr8wlgc-lkbt2upqw6eoxm0yjc1zklr8wlgc]] uses the discount factor to reject both short-termism and paralysis caused by over-weighting a distant uncertain future.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source offers one entrepreneur's philosophy rather than controlled evidence that these heuristics improve life outcomes.
- Human goals are plural, socially embedded, and not reducible to a single reward function.
- Exploration capacity depends on resources and obligations; a “90-point” rule is not equally available to everyone.
- Rapid feedback can reward short-term signals and still mislead long-term judgment.

## What Changed
- Established a bounded analogy between RL concepts and personal or entrepreneurial decision practice.
