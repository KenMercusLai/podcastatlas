## Definition
Vision as a decision filter is the practice of describing a desired future state concretely enough that present choices about product, channel, partnerships, hiring, and scale can be evaluated by whether they move the organization toward it.

## Current Synthesis
Tara Bosch's retrospective lesson is not that founders should predict a fixed five-year plan. It is that a vague mission gives little guidance when tactics proliferate. A concrete description of what customers can do, how the category changes, and what role the company plays can preserve direction while experiments remain flexible. Romanticize Collective illustrates the distinction: making offline experiences broadly accessible may be the destination even if the box is only one temporary vehicle.

## Key Claims
- A destination becomes strategically useful when it describes observable change rather than only aspiration.
- Vision should constrain and rank experiments without predetermining every tactic or product format.
- Channel and scale decisions become easier to compare when founders can state what future capability each choice builds.
- A company can preserve its mission while replacing a vehicle that fails customer or operating evidence.
- Long-range clarity does not eliminate uncertainty, market feedback, or the need to revise assumptions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- A vivid vision can harden into narrative commitment, causing founders to discount disconfirming evidence or over-scale prematurely.
- Five-year specificity can create false precision in volatile markets; the useful object is a revisable future state, not a guaranteed forecast.
- The source offers retrospective advice rather than comparative evidence that vision clarity caused better outcomes.

## What Changed
- Established a distinction between stable destination, revisable assumptions, and replaceable product vehicles.
