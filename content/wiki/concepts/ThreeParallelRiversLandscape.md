# Three Parallel Rivers Landscape / 三江并流地景

## Definition
Three Parallel Rivers landscape is the episode's frame for understanding northwest Yunnan as a compressed geography where the Nujiang, Lancang, and Jinsha rivers run close together through steep mountains while leading toward different wider water systems.

## Current Synthesis
In [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]], 三江并流 is more than a map fact or UNESCO label. The hosts treat it as a bodily geography lesson: being inside [[NujiangGorge|Nujiang Gorge]] makes the region's short horizontal distances, huge vertical relief, biodiversity, weather shifts, and cultural complexity feel almost excessive.

## Key Claims
- The region's significance comes from natural beauty, geological process, ecological evolution, and biodiversity together.
- Map proximity can hide severe travel difficulty because parallel rivers are separated by mountains and deep valleys.
- Direct travel changes the hosts' sense of geographic scale from abstract knowledge into bodily pressure.
- The landscape helps explain why Yunnan cannot be summarized as one simple ecological or cultural zone.
- The same terrain that produces scenic awe also shapes roads, settlement, religion, food, and tourism logistics.

## Evidence
- World-heritage frame: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] says the Three Parallel Rivers area entered the UNESCO World Natural Heritage list in 2003 and meets four natural-heritage criteria.
- Parallel difference: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] explains that the Nujiang, Lancang, and Jinsha rivers run near one another but feed different downstream systems or directions.
- Embodied scale: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] shifts from map knowledge to the hosts' sense of being overwhelmed inside a canyon.
- Cultural geography: [[245-nujiang-chunri-mijing-chutan-8221838951-646453]] links the same route to Lisu, Nu, Dulong, Tibetan, and Han communities, sacred mountains, churches, temples, and local myths.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a technical geography or conservation survey. Its strongest evidence is travel interpretation and host reflection grounded in the Nujiang route.
