# Exam-Driven Science Education / 应试化科学教育

Exam-driven science education / 应试化科学教育 is the reform failure mode named by [[ep262-gaokao-guohou-zhishi-guiling-women-xuyao-zenyang-de-kexue-jiaoyu-ls8ecqcdpe9d5ioytyca3jqdf-3r]]. The source says science education has gained policy status, including stronger primary-school science requirements, but that status can make science another subject for worksheets, memorization, and score pressure.

The problem is not that scientific knowledge should be avoided. The episode's criticism is that exams can detach knowledge from observation, inquiry, life use, and concept-building. A student can learn to answer questions and still lose the ability to ask what evidence would matter, how a system works, or how to apply a principle in a new scene.

The source also treats exam-driven science as an inequality amplifier. Schools in Beijing, Shanghai, or long-running reform contexts may have flexible spaces, labs, teacher collaboration, and assessment experience. Ordinary schools in high-pressure regions may face larger classes, less specialized teacher capacity, and stronger incentives to drill.

## Key Claims
- Raising science's status can backfire if the only available status language is tests and rankings.
- Inquiry, experiment, and project learning are fragile under heavy middle-school and gaokao pressure.
- Evaluation of scientific thinking is harder than scoring factual recall, which makes schools fall back to measurable worksheets.
- Exam-driven science can widen regional gaps because reform-ready schools adapt first.
- Good science education needs aligned curriculum, teacher capacity, space, and assessment rather than isolated "interesting" lessons.
