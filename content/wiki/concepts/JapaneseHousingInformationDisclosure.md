# Japanese Housing Information Disclosure / 日本住宅信息披露

Japanese housing information disclosure is the dense document and reporting layer described in [[131-wo-zai-riben-mai-le-yi-tao-zizhu-fang-llofkgaj-hyajksrpkoylakuocxz]]. After signing, the buyer receives important-matters materials covering land history, legal restrictions, utilities, disaster information, surrounding plans, urban-planning limits, construction progress, handover steps, registration, and specialist contacts.

The source frames this disclosure as both protection and bureaucracy. It gives the buyer more visibility into land, building, public infrastructure, and future neighborhood change, but it also reflects a legal culture where institutions document responsibilities carefully and procedures can feel slow, repetitive, and paper-heavy.

[[kyiv-brawl-ukraines-top-general-fired-6a609aec4fac21e67f1be165]] adds a stigma-disclosure extension through [[JikobukkenIncidentProperty|jiko bukken]]. Death-associated homes are not only hidden folklore; once mapped, narrated, certified, or disclosed, their past becomes part of the housing information set and can produce a [[HousingStigmaDiscount]].

## Key Claims
- Housing information is not only about the unit; it includes land, surrounding planning, utilities, disaster routes, and legal restrictions.
- Construction reports and handover checklists reduce uncertainty during the wait between reservation and key delivery.
- Disclosure helps buyers compare future light, road, railway, and building-height risks before purchase.
- The same documentation culture can become rigid because paper mail, electronic forms, passwords, bank notices, and specialist procedures remain fragmented.
- Disclosure can protect buyers and tenants while also making non-physical stigma searchable and price-relevant.
