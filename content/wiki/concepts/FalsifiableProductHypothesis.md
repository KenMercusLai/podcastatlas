# Falsifiable Product Hypothesis / 可证伪产品假设

Falsifiable product hypothesis is the practice of turning founder fear, conviction, or product belief into a claim that reality can support or overturn. In [[ep119-duihua-liu-kefan-yong-try-catch-finally-gei-duli-zuo-chanpin-de-neihao-xie-ge-chuli-liucheng-ludjc3ab-jbwpci6tpaajtffsblx]], [[LiuKefan|刘可凡]] uses the `catch` step of [[TryCatchFinallySelfManagement]] to move from vague anxiety such as "nobody will watch" or "the direction is wrong" toward observable hypotheses.

The source's examples include setting a time window and deciding what would count as evidence: revenue, followers, usage, user feedback, cost reduction, or another concrete metric. This is close to [[ValidatedLearning]], but Liu emphasizes the psychological benefit for solo builders: when a claim is falsifiable, a bad result becomes information about the theory instead of proof that the person is fundamentally failing.

## Key Claims
- A useful hypothesis names both the expected change and the evidence that would disconfirm it.
- The metric does not have to be revenue, but it should match the question being tested.
- Validation is not only an external market process; it is also a way to reduce internal friction by replacing vague fear with a bounded experiment.
- Failed hypotheses should produce a review of assumptions, ignored variables, and next tests rather than self-denial or unfalsifiable persistence.
- Unfalsifiable theories are dangerous because every bad result can be explained away as insufficient effort or incomplete faith.
