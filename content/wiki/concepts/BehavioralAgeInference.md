# Behavioral Age Inference

Behavioral age inference is the use of a user's activity patterns to estimate whether they are a child or an adult. In [[tech-20251226-1226-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251226-1226-mp-tech-pod-128]], [[DrewHarwell]] says companies such as [[Google]] and [[OpenAI]] are talking about inferring age from signals like follows, searches, and watched videos.

The method can look less intrusive than scanning a driver's license, but it depends on platforms analyzing ordinary behavior to classify users. That makes it an age-assurance version of [[PlatformDataRegulation]] and [[CivilLibertiesSurveillanceRisk]]: the system may reduce explicit ID collection while deepening the sense that online activity is continuously interpreted.

## Key Claims
- Behavioral signals can support age estimation without asking every user for an official ID.
- The method depends on platform-scale data about interests, searches, relationships, and media consumption.
- Classification errors near age thresholds can affect access, safety settings, or account treatment.
- Behavioral age inference can accelerate the decline of informal online anonymity because users learn their activity is being scanned to infer identity traits.
- The method shifts responsibility toward large account or platform operators that already hold broad behavioral data.
