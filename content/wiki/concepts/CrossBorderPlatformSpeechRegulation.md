# Cross-Border Platform Speech Regulation

Cross-border platform speech regulation is the conflict that emerges when one jurisdiction's content rules affect users, companies, or speech norms outside that jurisdiction. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-under-secretary-of-state-sarah-b-rogers-on-dismantling-the-censorship-industrial-complex-39828360]] adds the concept through [[SarahBRogers|Sarah B. Rogers]]'s critique of the [[UKOnlineSafetyAct|UK Online Safety Act]] and [[EuropeanUnionDigitalServicesAct|EU Digital Services Act]].

The episode's frame is not ordinary local enforcement. Rogers says the [[UnitedKingdom]] and [[EuropeanUnion]] can make their own domestic speech rules, but that fining American platforms for carrying speech lawful under U.S. norms can turn foreign platform law into a conflict with American foreign-policy interests and [[PlatformFirstAmendmentDefense]] principles.

## Key Claims
- Platform markets are transnational, so domestic moderation laws can spill into foreign users and companies.
- High fines and vague standards can induce over-removal even when a government does not directly order a specific post removed.
- Geofencing may not fully solve the conflict if regulators continue to pursue sites or companies that limit local access.
- Cross-border enforcement can create a "lowest common denominator" pressure if platforms harmonize around the most restrictive jurisdiction.
- The source frames foreign fines against American tech companies as a potential de facto censorship tariff.
