# Trust Reciprocity Loop

## Definition
A trust reciprocity loop is the episode's account of how one person's trust or mistrust changes another person's incentives and behavior, making social expectations partly self-reinforcing.

## Current Synthesis
Trust is not only a private belief measured before interaction. Monitoring, threatening, spying, withholding, or approaching someone as presumptively selfish can reduce cooperation and evoke defensiveness. Conversely, bounded trust can invite reciprocity and reveal cooperative behavior that distance alone could never observe.

The loop does not prove that trust always works. It shows why passive guardedness is not a neutral test: the test itself changes the relationship and narrows available evidence. A better approach is graduated exposure—small, reversible acts of reliance or disclosure, attention to the counterpart's response, and updating in either direction.

## Key Claims
- Social expectations can influence counterpart behavior rather than merely predict it.
- Persistent guarding reduces access to evidence that could disconfirm mistrust.
- Trust can elicit trustworthy behavior through reciprocity, but the effect is conditional rather than guaranteed.
- Competitive and collaborative environments can train different levels of trust and trustworthiness over time.
- Graduated, reversible social experiments are more informative than indiscriminate vulnerability.

## Evidence
- Interpersonal feedback: [[how-to-cultivate-a-positive-growth-oriented-mindset-dr-jamil-zaki-scim9746981563]] links monitoring, threats, and guardedness with more selfish responses from others.
- Experimental model: [[how-to-cultivate-a-positive-growth-oriented-mindset-dr-jamil-zaki-scim9746981563]] uses the trust game to illustrate underestimation of trustworthy behavior.
- Environmental shaping: [[how-to-cultivate-a-positive-growth-oriented-mindset-dr-jamil-zaki-scim9746981563]] contrasts collaborative ocean fishing with more competitive lake fishing in the Brazil example.
- Practical test: [[how-to-cultivate-a-positive-growth-oriented-mindset-dr-jamil-zaki-scim9746981563]] recommends calculated leaps of faith and recording the resulting evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Reciprocity is probabilistic, not a safety guarantee. Power imbalance, abuse, fraud, coercion, and high-cost exposure can make vulnerability inappropriate, and one positive encounter does not establish durable trustworthiness. The fishing-village and trust-game findings are presented conversationally and should remain bounded to the study descriptions in the source.
