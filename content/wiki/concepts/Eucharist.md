# Eucharist

## Definition

The Eucharist is the Christian bread-and-wine rite grounded in Jesus's words at the [[LastSupper]] and repeated as remembrance, participation, and communion.

## Current Synthesis

In the source, the rite carries a meal beyond its original moment by making bread and wine recurring signs of Jesus's body, blood, covenant, sacrifice, and the disciples' connection to him. The episode distinguishes rather than resolves denominational interpretations: Catholic teaching is summarized through real transformation, while some Protestant traditions are described as emphasizing symbol or spiritual participation. The durable common point is ritual continuity, not doctrinal uniformity.

## Key Claims

- The rite derives its central bread-and-wine symbolism from the Last Supper tradition.
- Repetition turns a finite event into communal remembrance across generations.
- Body and blood language connects meal, covenant, sacrifice, and Passion.
- Christian traditions disagree about presence, transformation, symbolism, and participation.

## Evidence

- Ritual origin and continuity: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] connects Jesus's bread-and-wine words to more than two millennia of practice.
- Doctrinal plurality: [[24-dongxifang-de-fanju-zuihou-de-wancan-yu-lanting-yaji-lraekm-bgowhg1wxysfshcwoz5xp]] contrasts Catholic and selected Protestant interpretations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode offers only a compressed overview of Eucharistic theology and Protestant diversity. It does not establish a complete denominational taxonomy, adjudicate doctrinal truth, or trace the full liturgical history. [[GrailEucharisticTheology]] concerns a later medieval literary-sacramental development rather than the entire Eucharistic tradition.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded synthesis separating ritual continuity from doctrinal uniformity.
