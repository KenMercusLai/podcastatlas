# Early Modern Japan-Europe Contact

Early modern Japan-Europe contact is the source's frame for seeing [[WilliamAdams|William Adams / 三浦按针]] as one node in a wider field rather than as a lone exotic exception. In [[14-wushi-weilian-dahanghai-shidai-de-riben-he-xifang-555771285]], contact includes Iberian Catholic missions, English and Dutch maritime rivalry, Japanese Christian daimyo, Japanese envoys to Spain and Rome, shipwreck diplomacy, trade permissions, cultural shock, and later restricted Dutch access.

The concept matters because the episode repeatedly avoids first-contact simplification. Japan was not closed to all foreigners before Adams, and Europe was not wholly ignorant of Japan. The historically interesting pattern is that trade, religion, war, translation, fantasy, hygiene judgment, punishment shock, and political risk all travel together.

## Key Claims
- Contact is a network, not a single arrival: missionaries, sailors, daimyo, envoys, merchants, and rulers all carry information.
- [[ConfessionalTradeConflict]] means European internal conflict becomes part of Japanese decision-making.
- [[TokugawaForeignTradeAmbivalence]] turns possible exchange into a governed risk problem.
- [[SakokuDutchWindow]] is presented as a later narrowing of contact, not the absence of all contact.
