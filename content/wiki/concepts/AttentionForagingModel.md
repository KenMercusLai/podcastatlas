# Attention Foraging Model

## Definition
The attention foraging model treats focus as allocation among competing information patches, with switching shaped by current returns, expected alternatives, salience, goals, and environmental richness.

## Current Synthesis
The episode extends the marginal value theorem from physical foraging to websites, feeds, devices, and internal thought. When alternatives are numerous and immediately available, the brain can lower its threshold for leaving the current task; even a nearby phone can function as a latent option. Focus difficulty is therefore partly an environment-design problem, not necessarily evidence of personal deficiency.

## Key Claims
- Attention amplifies selected information while suppressing competing inputs.
- Staying or switching depends partly on the perceived return from the current information source relative to alternatives.
- Rich, high-arousal digital environments can train faster switching and shorter stays.
- A nearby device may consume background processing because it represents an accessible alternative.
- Removing options, reducing external input, or using a visual-focus warm-up may support sustained attention.

## Evidence
- Foraging analogy - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] applies the marginal value theorem to websites and social media.
- Latent option load - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] describes poorer working memory when a phone is nearby and interprets the device as part of the brain's opportunity calculation.
- Environmental intervention - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] recommends removing competing devices and discusses reduced-input and visual-focus practices.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish that every attention problem is environmental or that phone removal treats ADHD, schizophrenia, autism-related attention differences, or other clinical conditions. Visual warm-ups and closed-eye focus practices are discussed as plausible behavioral tools, not universal treatments.

- Created a foraging-based account of attention switching and environmental focus design.
