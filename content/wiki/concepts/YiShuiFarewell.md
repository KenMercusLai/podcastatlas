# Yi Shui Farewell / 易水送别

Yi Shui Farewell / 易水送别 is the departure scene recorded in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-3-jingke-ciqin-weihe-jiujiu-bu-xingdong-zai-deng-shui-liihe-kgwigu3bggxmyofgvotged]] after [[JingKe|荆轲]] decides to leave for [[QinState|秦国]]. [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] and close retainers see him off at the Yi River wearing white mourning clothes, marking the mission as a likely death rather than an ordinary embassy.

The scene combines image, sound, and political emotion. [[GaoJianli|高渐离]] strikes the zhu, Jing Ke sings in a tragic mode before turning to a more forceful tone, and the farewell becomes a cultural memory of a mission that expects no return. The episode emphasizes that later readers remember the scene because it compresses doomed state resistance into a few vivid signs: white dress, cold river, music, song, and departure without looking back.

In the wiki, Yi Shui Farewell sits at the boundary between [[WeakStateAssassinationStrategy|weak-state assassination]] and memory-making. It does not make the strategy sound reliable, but it gives the failed mission a durable moral and aesthetic form.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-2-qinwang-danan-busi-zhihou-ganle-jian-ni-buzhidao-de-shi-lidfjb6-nggubw8vpz5fvnfacrqu]] extends that memory beyond the river scene. After Jing Ke dies, the episode follows his reputation into [[TaoYuanming|陶渊明]]'s poem on Jing Ke and [[GongZizhen|龚自珍]]'s later response to Tao. The farewell therefore becomes one point in a longer literary chain that remembers failed action as moral intensity rather than as a successful strategy.

## Key Claims
- Ritualized farewell can frame a political mission as already belonging to death and memory.
- Music, dress, place, and verse turn a risky operation into a remembered public image.
- The scene's force comes from the gap between strategic desperation and personal resolve.
- Its later cultural afterlife can dignify the participants without proving that the underlying strategy was sound.
- Qinji 115-2 shows the afterlife of the scene continuing through later poetry, where Jing Ke's failure remains available as a symbol of侠义 and doomed resolve.
