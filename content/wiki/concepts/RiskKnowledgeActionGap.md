# Risk Knowledge-Action Gap / 风险知识行动鸿沟

## Definition

The risk knowledge-action gap is the difference between knowing or being warned that an action may cause harm and actually changing behavior, policy, or deployment in response.

## Current Synthesis

[[Prometheus]] warns [[Epimetheus]] not to accept a gift from [[Zeus]], but the warning does not prevent acceptance of [[Pandora]]. That mythic case distinguishes information from action at the level of a warned recipient: evidence can identify danger without defeating attraction, short-term benefit, distrust, organizational incentives, or inability to coordinate.

The historical evidence adds an institutional case through [[HaiLuBook|《海录》]]. [[XieQinggao|谢清高]] recorded British colonial reach, troops and firearms, Bengal opium production, China-bound supply, and steam navigation before the Opium War. Yet scattered observation, oral and textual mediation, inherited categories, uneven circulation, and weak routes into bureaucratic decision-making prevented those facts from becoming a shared threat model or coordinated response. Risk failure can therefore occur even when no single decision-maker receives one clear warning at one clear moment.

Risk governance must therefore do more than publish a forecast. It must connect evidence to decision rights, credible communication, incentives, alternatives, enforcement, and the capacity to stop or modify action.

## Key Claims

- Advance warning is necessary in many cases but rarely sufficient for prevention.
- Desire, interest, trust, and immediate benefit can override acknowledged long-term danger.
- Scientific uncertainty does not eliminate responsibility to act on credible risk.
- Institutions need pathways that translate evidence into authority, incentives, and execution.
- Failure to act should not automatically be misdescribed as failure to know.
- Knowledge must circulate, become legible inside prevailing categories, reach decision authority, and survive organizational incentives before it can guide collective action.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Not acting on a warning can be rational when evidence is weak, costs are disproportionate, or alternatives carry greater risks. The concept therefore requires calibrated evidence and explicit tradeoffs; it should not turn every disputed forecast into a command. The *Hai Lu* case is reconstructed from a later podcast summary, and showing that information existed does not establish who read it, trusted it, understood it, possessed authority to act, or could overcome other fiscal, military, and political constraints.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept from individual warning failure to institutional failures of circulation, interpretation, authority, and execution.
- Added a historical warning inventory in which overseas information existed without becoming a coherent strategic model.
