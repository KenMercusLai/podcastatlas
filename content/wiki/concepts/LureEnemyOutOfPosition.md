# Lure Enemy Out of Position / 诱敌离位

## Definition
Lure Enemy Out of Position / 诱敌离位 is the tactic of using bait, false signals, exposed targets, concessions, or incentives to make an opponent leave a safer formation, posture, route, base, or force distribution before the prepared strike lands.

## Current Synthesis
The current synthesis treats the pattern as a position-control tactic that scales from battlefield formation to campaign design. Early sources show exposed small forces, false weakness, feigned retreat, decreasing stoves, and frontier livestock bait drawing commanders into pursuit or encirclement. Hanji 1038 adds the named [[SunCe|孙策]] case of 调虎离山: a strong enemy is not only drawn down a road, but pulled away from his [[RootBasePriority|root base]] so the rear can be seized. Hanji 1023 adds a retreat-path version: Cao Cao appears to escape from [[AnzhongLateHan|安重]], drawing Zhang Xiu and Liu Biao's blocking force out of position before a prepared ambush. Hanji 1043 adds a relief-operation version at [[BaimaReliefLateHan|White Horse]], where a feigned Yellow River crossing and rear threat pull Yuan Shao's troops west while Cao's light force strikes the actual siege point.

## Key Claims
- A strong enemy can be made weaker by changing its position, formation, operating condition, base security, or force distribution before the decisive strike.
- Bait has strategic value only when paired with a prepared attack, ambush, encirclement, rear-base seizure, or rapid relief strike.
- The false signal can be visible weakness, concession, decreasing camp stoves, exposed people or livestock, feigned retreat, gifts, promised gain, or a threatened move against the rear.
- Opponent psychology matters: contempt, greed, desire for visible merit, overconfidence, or defensive concern can make the lure persuasive.
- The pattern scales from tactical pursuit to campaign-level displacement, political misreading, and diversionary relief.
- Hanji 1038 adds a base-emptying version in which the target is lured away from the rear rather than merely out of battle formation.
- Hanji 1023 and Hanji 1043 add escape/diversion variants in which the enemy is pulled away from a terrain advantage or away from the true relief target.

## Evidence
- Foundational position-control pattern: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr|Zhouji 16]] presents Sun Bin's answer to a stalemate as exposing a weaker force and attacking once the enemy leaves its strong position; [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-19-maling-zhi-zhan-lhbapt09x2ptdqx0uopkp8apbada|Zhouji 19]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-20-pangjuan-si-ci-shu-xia-ls-8-3jhz5q1c5ca11mhdzmz4wg4|Zhouji 20]] extend the same logic through Maling pursuit and decreasing stoves.
- False weakness and concession signals: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-175-bikan-liubang-de-huangjia-liyi-ke-los9hsomndnhyqpxafstgyquomzf|Hanji 175]] uses Modu's concessions to Donghu, [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-177-beikun-baidengshan-liubang-mingxuan-yixian-ljdmxf6bej8ju0gmvm9cp48gy0on|Hanji 177]] uses weak-looking Xiongnu displays before Baideng, and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-925-tuoxie-bushi-rensong-xuehui-tuoxie-lu-geng-changliang-limafqskgblrwetestyh39w-hzm4|Hanji 925]] uses Huangfu Song's weak visible posture before the Changshe fire attack.
- Terrain, pursuit, and contempt: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-2-xiangyu-touhao-dajiang-zenme-shu-gei-hanxin-de-lhe-lmb1zrzipbongg02gmnmb4l|Hanji 164-2]] shows Long Ju abandoning a safer posture to chase Han Xin into the Weishui trap, while [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-105-zhanzheng-huayang-zhaoguo-limu-zui-hui-shua-ljtqdg46a8o13zzsjv-p-fpol5oh|Qinji 105]] shows Li Mu building Xiongnu overconfidence over years before exposing people and livestock.
- Base-emptying campaign pattern: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1038-sanshiliu-ji-zhi-diaohu-lishan-sunce-zhiqu-wancheng-lhomk8hfdidl61degwbqtj2xkj9z|Hanji 1038]] explicitly labels Sun Ce's Lujiang operation 调虎离山: gifts and deferential language draw [[LiuXunLateHan|刘勋]] toward Shangliao, while Sun Ce and [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] take [[WanChengLujiang|皖城]].
- False escape from blocked retreat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1023-jiemi-caocao-yu-zhangxiu-de-bingfa-boyi-lphbnmfdu0x5w7zjra7k0o8cyel4|Hanji 1023]] shows Cao Cao opening a narrow night path at Anzhong and making withdrawal look like flight so Zhang Xiu and Liu Biao commit to pursuit before the ambush lands from front and rear.
- Diversionary relief at White Horse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1043-yuanshao-tongma-caocao-jing-zhihaole-tade-piantouteng-ltibjj6xizsrynpgr9oyf-q3ydna|Hanji 1043]] says Cao Cao first pretends to cross the Yellow River and attack Yuan Shao's rear, drawing Yuan forces west, then moves light troops rapidly to White Horse before Yan Liang can prepare.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Not every retreat, gift, concession, or diversion is a lure. The pattern requires a false or selectively displayed opening, a predictable enemy misread, and a prepared exploitation plan. Hanji 1023 also shows that a lure can coexist with real danger: Cao Cao's retreat route is genuinely under pressure, but the displayed escape path is still used to manage enemy movement. Hanji 1043 shows a lure that divides force rather than drawing all of it into a trap, so the concept should include redistribution and timing gaps as well as direct ambush. The modern phrase 调虎离山 is used source-scoped here; older episodes may show the same strategic logic without naming it that way.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 1043 as a diversionary relief variant at White Horse.
- Extended the concept beyond base-emptying and false escape to include pulling enemy reserves away from the real siege target.
- Retained Hanji 1023's blocked-retreat variant while making the shared position-control logic explicit.
