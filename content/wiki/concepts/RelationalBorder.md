## Definition
A relational border is a boundary understood as a changing zone of separation, contact, movement, identity, and negotiation rather than only as a fixed cartographic line.

## Current Synthesis
[[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] develops the concept across scales. Xita's limits differ between residents, visitors, and historians because a place is made through memory and connection. The Tumen and Yalu river lines can remain geographically stable for centuries while laws, sovereignty, identity, and everyday crossing change around them.

The concept does not deny boundaries. It distinguishes the line from the social system that gives the line meaning. Qing and Korean restrictions, disaster migration, shallow rivers, limited patrols, family journeys, competing state claims, and cross-border peoples show that border order is always incomplete and negotiated in practice.

## Key Claims
- A border divides, but it also creates contact and communication.
- Physical continuity of a line does not imply continuity of its political or identity meaning.
- Local memory and outside comparison can produce different but informative spatial boundaries.
- Law and patrol establish real constraints without eliminating survival movement, ecology, kinship, or strategic identity choice.
- Borderland detail reveals the gap between a state's clean map and lived social relations.

## Evidence
- Urban boundary: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] uses disagreement over Xita's limits to show that lived place depends on memory and relationships.
- Historical continuity: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] says the Tumen and Yalu river boundaries persisted while their institutional meaning changed.
- Porous enforcement: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] describes crossing for food, cultivation, timber, ginseng, family, and evasion despite severe prohibitions.
- Identity negotiation: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] shows people using baptism, citizenship claims, or documents to move among jurisdictions while states competed to classify them.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not show that borders are imaginary or harmless. States can punish crossing, documentation can determine life chances, and boundaries can provide jurisdiction and protection. The narrower claim is that no line exhausts the ecological, social, and historical relations around it.
