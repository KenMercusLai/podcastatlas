# Performance Authenticity Tradeoff / 表演真实性权衡

## Definition
Performance authenticity tradeoff is the tension between presenting a performance as live and real, delivering the polished experience audiences expect, and disclosing the prerecorded, edited, or substituted process that actually produces it.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-354-wanru-taitannike-maochongzhe-zonghezheng-dabaofa-xiangei-caihua-pingping-dan-yexin-bobo-de-ren-gkwrirwokgwgauo05wtfda-h]] develops the concept through [[SoundsLikeTitanicBook|《宛如泰坦尼克》]]. The touring ensemble supplied visual liveness while prerecorded music supplied flawless sound. The emotional outcome was not necessarily fake: listeners felt comfort, applauded, bought recordings, and supported public television or charities. But those real effects do not make the hidden production process irrelevant.

The deeper tradeoff is co-produced. Performers and organizers can conceal substitution, while audiences demand the prestige of liveness and punish the mistakes that make live performance real. Visual spectacle encourages people to "listen with their eyes," so stage presence can certify sound the performers did not make. Authenticity therefore cannot be judged from emotional payoff or polish alone; it also requires attention to representation, disclosure, consent, responsibility, and tolerance for human error.

## Key Claims
- A performance can produce genuine emotion through a materially misrepresented process.
- Live appearance and flawless sound are different goods, even when audiences are encouraged to treat them as one.
- Audience intolerance of mistakes can reward prerecording, lip-syncing, and other substitutes for risky live performance.
- Visual presence can overpower auditory evidence and make a performance feel authentic because it looks authentic.
- Explaining why organizers, workers, and audiences sustain the arrangement does not erase responsibility for concealment.
- Greater tolerance for bounded imperfection can protect real performance from being optimized out.

## Evidence
- Real effect and concealed process: [[vol-354-wanru-taitannike-maochongzhe-zonghezheng-dabaofa-xiangei-caihua-pingping-dan-yexin-bobo-de-ren-gkwrirwokgwgauo05wtfda-h]] reports genuine audience comfort, applause, purchases, and fundraising around musicians miming to prerecorded sound.
- Perfection pressure: [[vol-354-wanru-taitannike-maochongzhe-zonghezheng-dabaofa-xiangei-caihua-pingping-dan-yexin-bobo-de-ren-gkwrirwokgwgauo05wtfda-h]] uses prerecorded inauguration music and lip-sync examples to argue that audiences ask for liveness while rejecting live error.
- Visual certification: [[vol-354-wanru-taitannike-maochongzhe-zonghezheng-dabaofa-xiangei-caihua-pingping-dan-yexin-bobo-de-ren-gkwrirwokgwgauo05wtfda-h]] describes audiences as "listening with their eyes" and treating stage image as evidence of musical production.
- Ethical boundary: [[vol-354-wanru-taitannike-maochongzhe-zonghezheng-dabaofa-xiangei-caihua-pingping-dan-yexin-bobo-de-ren-gkwrirwokgwgauo05wtfda-h]] repeatedly separates understanding the system from declaring its deception harmless.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Prerecording, amplification, editing, and theatrical convention are not automatically deceptive. Genre, safety, broadcast constraints, accessibility, and explicit audience expectations can make mediation legitimate. This source does not establish how much the ensemble's audiences knew, what disclosures were made, or when emotional benefit would outweigh consumer-consent concerns. The concept is an ethical map, not a universal ban on production technique.
