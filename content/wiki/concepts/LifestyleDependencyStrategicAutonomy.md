## Definition

生活方式依赖与战略自主 / Lifestyle dependency and strategic autonomy is the source-grounded idea that imported goods can alter elite tastes, everyday practice, ecological adaptation, and military readiness in ways that create political leverage for the supplying power. Autonomy therefore depends not only on arms or treaties but also on whether essential habits remain locally supportable.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-217-kan-fuhei-hanwendi-ruhe-wansi-yimudi-liuchang-2-ljmhwnxkjcegqfevo8hmvoblrcye|Hanji 217]] attributes this logic to [[ZhonghangYue|中行说]]. Because Han has a much larger population and production base, he argues, it need not conquer Xiongnu directly if a small flow of attractive goods can reshape preferences and produce dependence. Silk and prepared foods become strategic not because foreign goods are inherently harmful, but because habitual reliance may let the supplier influence the recipient cheaply.

The source tests the claim through environmental fit. Silk is presented as visually attractive but fragile in brush and less protective against cold than felt or fur; Han foods are contrasted with milk and cheese as steppe nourishment. The demonstrations connect custom to terrain and fighting capacity, but they are advocacy rather than controlled evidence. The strongest synthesis is conditional: imported consumption becomes a strategic vulnerability when it displaces locally sustainable practices, concentrates supply leverage, or weakens capabilities needed in the recipient's environment.

The episode also shows that material autonomy alone is incomplete. Zhonghang Yue reportedly pairs consumption advice with record keeping, population and livestock counts, and assertive diplomatic forms. Strategic autonomy therefore joins an adapted material base to administrative legibility and symbolic parity.

## Key Claims

- Consumption preferences can become a channel of external influence when a supplier controls goods that recipients come to treat as necessary.
- The strategic issue is dependence and substitution, not foreign origin by itself.
- Clothing and food should be judged partly by ecological fit and their effects on resilience and operational capacity.
- A larger state may gain leverage through relatively cheap exports if they reshape the habits of a smaller counterpart.
- Material self-reliance is stronger when paired with administrative knowledge and diplomatic confidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source offers strategic advocacy, not comparative economic or military measurement. Imported goods can supplement rather than displace local capacity, exchange can create mutual dependence, and preference change does not automatically cause political control or weaker combat performance. The clothing and food demonstrations may select favorable use cases, while rejection of useful imports can itself impose costs. The concept should therefore be applied through supply concentration, substitutability, ecological fit, and capability effects rather than as a general argument for autarky or cultural purity.

## What Changed

- Established the concept from the episode's joined argument about goods, custom, ecology, administration, and power.
- Narrowed the claim from blanket rejection of imports to conditional vulnerability through dependence and capability displacement.
