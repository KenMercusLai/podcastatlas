# Postprandial Glycemic Stability / 餐后血糖稳定

## Definition
Postprandial glycemic stability is a meal-level framework for moderating rapid glucose rises and falls through food form, portion, cooking, meal order, regular timing, and appropriate movement without eliminating carbohydrate.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats a meal as a system rather than ranking one ingredient in isolation. Soft, highly gelatinized starches such as very soft noodles, rice noodles, porridge, or boiled wrappers may be absorbed faster, while sauces, cooking oil, sugar-fat combinations, and actual portion can matter as much as whether the staple is rice or wheat. Whole fruit differs from juice because chewing, fiber, intact structure, and the number of fruits compressed into a drink change delivery.

The practical pattern is to eat vegetables first, then protein-rich foods, and staple foods later; keep meals regular; avoid routinely using a feast-fast cycle as compensation; and add light activity after digestion has begun rather than immediate intense exercise. The rice evidence adds that grain color is an unreliable shortcut: brown rice retains bran, fiber, and micronutrients, but amylose content, variety, parboiling, and cooling can change glycemic response enough that some prepared white rice may produce a smaller rise than ordinary brown rice. These tactics aim to reduce repeated sharp swings and make portions easier to regulate, not to guarantee a single “perfect” glucose curve.

## Key Claims
- Glycemic response depends on food structure, cooking softness, portion, oil, sauce, and the rest of the meal, not only the staple-food label.
- Vegetables and protein before staple foods may increase satiety and moderate the meal's glucose response.
- Whole fruit and juice are metabolically different eating contexts because juicing disrupts structure and can concentrate a larger dose.
- Regular meals are preferable to repeated feast-fast compensation that creates large intake swings.
- Light post-meal movement can support glucose handling, while immediate high-intensity exercise is not a universal default.
- The goal is a sustainable dietary pattern with less extreme fluctuation, not carbohydrate elimination or constant device surveillance.
- Brown-versus-white color is not a sufficient glycemic ranking because amylose content, variety, processing, preparation, and cooling can outweigh bran status in a specific meal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This framework comes from structured podcast summaries, not direct review of controlled feeding studies or clinical guidelines. The rice episode reports both a large observational review and a smaller randomized-trial review, but the underlying papers, interventions, rice varieties, follow-up, adherence, and effect estimates were not inspected here. Individual responses vary with diabetes status, medication, pregnancy, age, digestive conditions, activity, meal size, and baseline glucose. Rankings of rice, noodles, dumpling wrappers, porridge, and other foods are contextual rather than universal, and reducing visible glucose variation does not by itself establish complete metabolic health or long-term disease prevention.

## What Changed
- Replaced the simple brown-versus-white hierarchy with an evidence-qualified comparison.
- Added amylose, variety, parboiling, and cooling as rice-specific preparation variables.
- Preserved the distinction between short-term glycemic response and long-term diabetes outcomes.
