# Human Error Without Negligence

## Definition

Human error without negligence is a consequential mistake produced by ordinary limits of perception, motor control, memory, or stress even when the person is competent, responsible, and not acting recklessly.

## Current Synthesis

[[blame-game-blame-game|Blame Game]] uses [[MarkSaylor|Mark Saylor's]] crash to resist a false moral binary. If the driver pressed the accelerator while intending to brake, that would be a causal human error; it would not by itself show disregard for safety, dishonesty, or defective character. [[DickSchmidt]]'s account of impulse variability supplies the mechanism by which sincere intention and bodily action can separate.

The distinction matters for safety because refusing to name human error can make prevention worse. Advice that assumes the driver is already on the brake may reinforce a wrong-pedal event. A better response can acknowledge fallibility, tell a driver to lift off, verify foot position, and deliberately reapply the brake, while still investigating vehicle design and institutional responsibility.

## Key Claims

- Causal responsibility for an action does not automatically establish moral or legal negligence.
- Competent people can execute a movement different from the movement they consciously intend.
- Panic and unfamiliar geometry can produce error without prior recklessness.
- Shame-avoiding explanations can obstruct practical safeguards if they make ordinary error unspeakable.
- Human-centered prevention should anticipate recoverable mistakes rather than rely on perfect performance.

## Evidence

### Intention–action gap

- [[blame-game-blame-game]] uses Schmidt's impulse-variability account to explain sincere but incorrect pedal application.

### Moral distinction

- [[blame-game-blame-game]] explicitly presents Saylor as non-reckless and argues that the proposed mistake could happen to ordinary responsible people under extreme stress.

### Prevention consequence

- [[blame-game-blame-game]] criticizes safety advice that presumes correct pedal placement and recommends verifying the foot's position before deliberate braking.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The distinction does not absolve every driver, manufacturer, regulator, or media institution, and it is not a case-specific legal conclusion. Negligence depends on duties, foreseeability, precautions, and evidence that the supplied summary does not provide. Human error can coexist with poor interface design, mechanical failure, weak training, misleading guidance, or institutional misconduct.

## What Changed

- Created a moral and safety distinction between ordinary action error and reckless behavior.
