## Definition

Political plurality and the shared world is the Arendtian claim that politics should sustain a common world among people with distinct standpoints rather than eliminate disagreement through one allegedly final political truth.

## Current Synthesis

Factual reality and expert knowledge remain indispensable, but public judgment also involves interpretation, priority, experience, and tradeoffs among plural people. The Connecticut citizens' assembly adds a concrete institutional case: a demographically composed group can learn complex material, develop fairer speaking procedures, sustain ideological disagreement, and form civic relationships. Its post-vote conflict also shows that a shared deliberative world is fragile when drafting, publication, or implementation remains controlled by outside political gatekeepers.

## Key Claims

- Factual truth is indispensable and should not be collapsed into mere opinion.
- Political judgment remains plural because people encounter a common world from different positions.
- Claims to final political truth can become tyrannical when they disqualify competing judgments in advance.
- Expertise can inform politics without replacing the agency and judgment of citizens.
- A common world is built through continued speech, listening, procedure, action, and relationship rather than uniform belief.
- Institutional promises and implementation pathways matter because deliberative trust can be lost after consensus is reached.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Plurality does not license denial of established facts or excuse deliberate deception.
- One citizens' assembly demonstrates feasibility, not comparative superiority or durable policy impact.
- Some conflicts concern coercion, rights, or immediate safety and cannot be resolved merely by preserving conversation.
- Expert curriculum design, facilitation, selection, and drafting retain power inside participatory institutions.
- Friendship and civic affection do not substitute for transparent wording, kept promises, or accountable implementation.

## What Changed

- Added a concrete citizens' assembly case to the earlier philosophical account.
- Distinguished expert input from expert political rule through observed fiscal learning.
- Added post-deliberation gatekeeping as a threat to the shared world and participant trust.
