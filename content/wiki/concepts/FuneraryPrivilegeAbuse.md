## Definition

丧葬名分僭越 / Funerary Privilege Abuse is the pattern where a politically connected household uses burial goods, garments, or rites above its rank to convert private power into public status, making enforcement of ritual hierarchy a politically dangerous law case.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 839 gives the current source case through [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]]'s father. Zhao Zhong, a powerful eunuch, allegedly has his father buried in garments reserved for emperors or kings. [[ZhuMuLateHan|朱穆]], serving as [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]] inspector, orders the commandery administrator to verify the violation; officials open the tomb and expose the corpse.

The enforcement problem is not only ritual. Once the tomb is opened, [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] becomes angry and Zhu is sentenced, while [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]] and Taixue petitioners argue that eunuch relatives are using imperial proximity to oppress local society. The concept therefore sits between status-symbol abuse and palace-protected legal reversal.

## Key Claims

- Burial goods can function as public rank claims, not only as private family mourning.
- Misusing imperial garments extends palace privilege into the status of an unelected kin household.
- Enforcing ritual hierarchy against a palace-connected family can become more dangerous than enforcing ordinary local law.
- Tomb-opening verification creates political shock because it visibly reverses the offender's attempted status display.
- The violation becomes a feedback case when petitioners link it to broader eunuch-family abuse.

## Evidence

- Rank violation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]] says Zhao Zhong's father was buried wearing garments reserved for the emperor and royal lords.
- Enforcement action: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]] says Zhu Mu orders officials to verify the matter by opening the tomb.
- Political reversal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]] says Emperor Huan becomes angry and Zhu is sent to the廷尉 process and sentenced to hard labor.
- Public accusation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-839-ni-neng-zuodao-wenxin-wukui-ma-lqvlswubf4ak5qfgzjf-owigneua|Hanji 839]] says Liu Tao's petition treats the case as evidence that eunuch relatives are damaging law and harming commoners.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is not every expensive funeral or every burial-status dispute. It requires rank-signaling burial privilege plus political protection. Hanji 839 gives a single source case, and the exact legal category and procedural details around the tomb opening remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 839's Zhao Zhong father burial branch.
- Distinguished funerary privilege abuse from imperial burial legitimacy disputes among royal or empress-dowager actors.
