# 清代谣言政治 / Qing Rumor Politics

清代谣言政治 / Qing rumor politics names the source's account of rumor as a political force in the [[ZengJing|曾静]] case. In [[11-yongzheng-wangchao-zhi-dayi-juemi-shijingqian-bi-xia-de-qiangu-qi-an-547265513]], rumors about [[YongzhengEmperor|雍正]]'s succession, family violence, greed, sexuality, and Manchu rule mattered because they could bind personal scandal to state legitimacy.

The episode argues that rumor was not defeated by simple explanation or suppression. Yongzheng thought public refutation through [[DaiYiJueMiLu|《大义觉迷录》]] could make falsehood collapse, while [[QianlongEmperor|乾隆]] later thought destroying the book could restore order. Both strategies could backfire: publication preserved the rumor's details, and suppression suggested that the text contained dangerous truth.

## Key Claims
- Rumor can become politically serious when it attaches private scandal to legitimacy.
- Authoritarian control can push dissent into more indirect, fantastical, and unverifiable forms.
- Official rebuttal may carry the rumor farther by repeating the accusation.
- Suppression can increase curiosity and suspicion when the public already distrusts official explanation.
