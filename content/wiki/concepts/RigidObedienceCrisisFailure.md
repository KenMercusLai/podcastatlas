# Rigid Obedience Crisis Failure / 危机僵化奉诏

Rigid obedience crisis failure / 危机僵化奉诏 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-966-meiyou-yexin-budong-quanmou-jiu-bie-dang-lingdao-lldn3hi1gl5v3axvklwakgf6vx3u|Hanji 966]]. It names the failure mode in which an actor obeys a formal command without reassessing whether the command channel has been captured, whether obedience removes practical leverage, and whether the result still serves the authority the command claims to represent.

The source case is [[SongYiLateHan|宋翼]]. [[WangHongLateHan|王宏]] reads [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and [[GuoSiLateHan|郭汜]]'s summons as a trap because outside armed officials are the only remaining deterrent protecting [[WangYunLateHan|王允]]. Song Yi instead says the imperial summons cannot be resisted. That response treats [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] as absolute while ignoring the coercive conditions around [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s court.

The concept differs from ordinary loyalty. Loyalty can require risk, but this pattern appears when the actor refuses to distinguish the ruler's long-term safety from a command produced under enemy pressure. In Hanji 966, obedience eliminates the outside shield, making Wang Yun, Song Yi, Wang Hong, and their families easier to kill.

## Key Claims

- Formal authority must be interpreted inside its coercive setting during a crisis.
- Obeying a captured command can betray the practical interest the command name invokes.
- Correct crisis judgment may require preserving leverage outside the official ritual channel.
- Rigid obedience is especially dangerous when disobedience and rebellion are treated as the same category even after the command channel has been seized.
