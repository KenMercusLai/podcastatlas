# Synthetic Content Training Feedback

## Definition
Synthetic content training feedback is the risk that generated text, images, audio, or video enter the public information environment and later become input to models that reproduce or amplify their errors, sameness, or low-quality patterns.

## Current Synthesis
The [[TomorrowToday]] discussion extends “garbage in, garbage out” from a single model response into a media-system loop. Cheap generation can flood feeds with plausible but low-quality material; if provenance and filtering are weak, later models and people may treat that material as ordinary evidence or creative precedent.

The risk has two layers. Information errors can be repeated and gain apparent corroboration through volume, while stylistic abundance can make content more derivative and harder to trust. The episode predicts eventual legal and social backlash, but does not establish how much current training data is synthetic, which model pipelines admit it, or whether curation, provenance, deduplication, and evaluation can control the loop.

## Key Claims
- Cheap generation can increase the volume of low-quality or erroneous public material.
- Synthetic material can affect later systems if it enters collection pipelines without reliable provenance or quality controls.
- Repetition can make an error look widely corroborated even when multiple outputs share the same origin.
- The loop threatens information quality and creative diversity, not only factual accuracy.
- Provenance, dataset curation, filtering, and human evaluation are possible controls rather than proof of inevitable collapse.
- Public or legal backlash is a source forecast, not a demonstrated stage of a universal cycle.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode provides a conceptual risk, not measurements of dataset composition, model degradation, or public response. Synthetic data can also be deliberately generated, filtered, labeled, and validated for useful training. The danger depends on provenance loss, task, collection method, quality controls, and whether independent human or primary-source evidence remains available.
