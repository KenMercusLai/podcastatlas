# Prospective Memory

## Definition
Prospective memory is remembering to do something in the future, especially when a planned action must survive distraction, routine, stress, or a changed context.

## Current Synthesis
The Castel episode treats prospective memory as the future-facing side of ordinary memory. It appears in small planning tasks, such as remembering an escape route for a hotel room, and in high-stakes failures, such as routine taking over under stress or emergency devices not being used when the moment arrives.

The practical synthesis is cue design plus rehearsal. Merely knowing what should be done is weaker than walking the route, building a reliable reminder, practicing the action under realistic conditions, or changing the environment so the future intention has a trigger.

## Key Claims
- Remembering a future action is a distinct problem from remembering past facts.
- High arousal can push people toward trained or habitual routines, even when the situation requires a different action.
- Active rehearsal can make a future action more available than passive exposure to instructions or maps.
- Everyday prospective memory should sometimes be forgotten and updated when circumstances change.
- Older-adult safety depends partly on whether reminders, devices, and environments work at the moment of need.
