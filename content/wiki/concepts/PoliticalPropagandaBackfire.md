# Political Propaganda Backfire / 政治宣传反噬

Political propaganda backfire / 政治宣传反噬 is the episode's mechanism for a state message preserving or strengthening what it tries to defeat. In [[11-yongzheng-wangchao-zhi-dayi-juemi-shijingqian-bi-xia-de-qiangu-qi-an-547265513]], [[YongzhengEmperor|雍正]] published [[DaiYiJueMiLu|《大义觉迷录》]] to show that [[ZengJing|曾静]] had been corrected and that rumors about the emperor were false.

The backfire came from attention and memory. Readers could become more interested in the accusations than in the rebuttal, officials could use the text to denounce enemies, and [[QianlongEmperor|乾隆]]'s later suppression could be read as proof that the book contained dangerous content. The episode's general lesson is that propaganda must account for how audiences remember scandal, not only for what the official text says.

## Key Claims
- Rebuttal can spread an accusation by making it official enough to read.
- Political texts can be used by local actors for purposes the center did not intend.
- Suppression after publication can make the previous message look suspicious.
- Propaganda backfire is especially likely when [[QingRumorPolitics|rumor]] and [[PersonalRuleLegitimacyCrisis|personal legitimacy]] are already entangled.
