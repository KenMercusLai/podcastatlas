# Bicycle Component Stack Power

Bicycle component stack power is [[no-199-zixingche-200-nian-973489465]]'s explanation of why [[Shimano]] can capture high profit in a fragmented bicycle value chain. The source treats Shimano's moat as a system: cold forging, gear teeth, derailleurs, chains, cables, tolerances, indexed shifting, patents, and component matching reinforce each other.

The concept matters because it separates visible brand ownership from control over the performance-critical stack. Many finished-bicycle makers assemble frames and parts, but if the drivetrain and shifting experience depend on a supplier's matched system, that supplier can shape margins, delivery timing, and product positioning.

## Key Claims
- A component supplier can own the value chain if its parts work best as a matched system.
- Manufacturing process knowledge and system tolerances can be harder to copy than the visible product shape.
- Patents matter most when combined with tacit process data, tooling, and ecosystem acceptance.
- The E-bike transition may change the stack by moving value toward motors, batteries, software, sensors, and tuning.
