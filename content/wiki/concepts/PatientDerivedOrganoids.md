# Patient-Derived Organoids

Patient-derived organoids are the source's proposed pretesting layer for personalized longevity interventions. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bryan-johnson-i-just-took-the-most-powerful-dose-of-dmt-in-the-world-heres-what-it-was-like-40626165]], [[BryanJohnson|Bryan Johnson]] says his team has created induced pluripotent stem cells from his blood and is developing organoids such as heart, liver, and lung models.

The episode frames organoids as a way to test molecules, doses, side effects, and combinations before trying interventions in Johnson's own body. That connects the concept to [[PersonalizedMolecularMedicine]] and [[AnimalTestingSubstitution]], but the wiki should still keep the validation boundary visible: organoid response can reduce uncertainty without becoming a complete substitute for whole-human biology.

## Key Claims
- Patient-derived organoids can make preclinical testing more personally relevant than generic cell lines.
- The source presents organoids as a risk-reduction step before self-experimentation, not as proof of safety.
- Organoid testing fits the broader shift from generalized protocols toward individualized intervention design.
- The main constraint is translation: organoid results still need [[ClinicalDevelopmentCapability|clinical development capability]] and human monitoring.
