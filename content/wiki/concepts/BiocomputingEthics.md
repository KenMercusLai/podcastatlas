# Biocomputing Ethics

## Definition
Biocomputing ethics is the set of questions raised when living biological neural tissue becomes part of a machine, research platform, or possible computing product.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats ethics as a live boundary rather than an afterthought. [[LenaCurtis]] says there is no specific legislation for biocomputing yet, which she considers understandable because the field remains early. The ethical issue is not only safety; living neurons in machines blur familiar categories of tool, organism, human tissue, and computer, making public acceptance and philosophical framing part of the technology's adoption path.

## Key Claims
- Biocomputing lacks specific legislation in the episode's account.
- The ethical question is sharpened by living human neurons being used inside machines.
- FinalSpark has reached out to philosophers to encourage ethical and philosophical work.
- Public acceptance may matter alongside technical progress.
- Existing human-machine examples such as prosthetics and pacemakers do not fully settle the boundary question for living neural processors.

## Evidence
### Regulatory gap
- [[ep-37-neurons-future-of-ai-processing]] says Curtis sees no current legislation specific to biocomputing.

### Philosophical outreach
- [[ep-37-neurons-future-of-ai-processing]] says FinalSpark reached out to philosophers to encourage work on biocomputing ethics and philosophy.

### Human-machine boundary
- [[ep-37-neurons-future-of-ai-processing]] discusses smartphones, prosthetics, pacemakers, and robotic systems as background for the more difficult question of living neurons in machines.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source does not resolve whether current tissue, organoid, animal-research, medical-device, or AI regulations should apply.
- The episode raises public acceptance and philosophical questions but does not provide a detailed ethical framework.
- The maturity of the technology affects the urgency and specificity of regulatory design.

## What Changed
- Created an ethics concept for living-neuron computing and machine-boundary questions.
