## Definition

边疆将领擅断干预 / Unauthorized Frontier Command Intervention is the pattern where a frontier official uses local force, delegated office, or controlled reporting to alter an allied frontier polity's leadership, complaint channel, or submission order without sufficient central authorization, creating legality and legitimacy problems beyond the immediate tactical result.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 427 now adds a Western Han boundary case through [[FengFengshiWesternHan|冯奉世]] at [[ShacheKingdomWesternHan|莎车国]]. Feng's action succeeds: he bypasses slow Chang'an authorization, falsely commands Western Regions cavalry to assemble, and forces Shache nobles to remove Hutuzheng. But the court response also marks the authorization problem. [[XiaoWangzhiWesternHan|萧望之]] warns [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] that marquisate reward would teach envoys to launch frontier wars on their own, so Feng is promoted without being enfeoffed. This makes Hanji 427 a controlled-success case rather than a collapse case.

Hanji 769 supplies a later related case around Southern Xiongnu chanyu An Guo. The dispute begins as a complaint against Du Chong, but Du uses his office to block An Guo's memorial and joins Zhu Hui in countercharging An Guo with rebellion. An Guo flees and is killed by his uncle; once the false-report chain is exposed, the court summons Zhu Hui and Du Chong, jails them, and executes them. This case is not a formal chanyu installation, but it belongs here because controlled reporting and local force distort the court's handling of an allied leader.

Hanji 820 supplies the earlier formal leadership-coercion case. During the 140 CE [[SouthernXiongnu|Southern Xiongnu]] revolt, [[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan|Chen Gui]] acts in the Xiongnu command role after [[LiangBingLateHan|Liang Bing]] is recalled for illness. Although [[XiuliChanyu|Xiuli]] is presented as uninvolved in [[WusiSouthernXiongnu|Wusi]] and [[CheNiuChanyu|Che Niu]]'s plot, Chen Gui forces Xiuli and the Left Wise King into suicide and wants to move close chanyu kin inward. The court later jails and removes him, and the surrounding narrative shows the intervention worsening surrender and succession conditions.

Hanji 917 supplies the later repeated case through [[ZhangXiuProtectorOfXiongnu|Zhang Xiu]]. As protector of Xiongnu, Zhang clashes with [[HuzhengChanyu|Huzheng]], kills him without prior court authorization, and installs [[QiangquChanyu|Qiangqu]] as Southern Xiongnu chanyu. The court punishes Zhang Xiu, showing that a frontier outcome may look useful and still violate the authority structure that gives allied rule legitimacy.

## Key Claims

- Frontier officials can create facts on the ground faster than the center can approve or legitimate them.
- Frontier officials can also distort the center's field of vision by intercepting complaints and submitting countercharges.
- Local discretion becomes politically dangerous when it changes chanyu succession rather than only battlefield tactics.
- Even successful unauthorized mobilization can require capped reward so tactical success does not become general precedent.
- A tactically assertive intervention can damage surrender prospects if loyal or neutral actors no longer trust Han promises.
- Court punishment in both cases marks the boundary between delegated frontier command and unauthorized regime alteration.
- The pattern differs from frontier-population backlash: here the trigger is unilateral Han-official intervention, not primarily requisition or supply pressure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

These cases do not prove that the center is always better informed than frontier commanders. Hanji 427 shows the strongest counterweight: slow authorization can be dangerous in emergencies, and field officials may see conditions the capital cannot. The concept applies when local action rewrites leadership legitimacy, blocks a complaint channel, fabricates or controls the ruler's information, or coerces submitted actors without clear mandate; it should not be used to condemn every battlefield decision made before formal approval arrives.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 427 as a Western Han controlled-success boundary case: Feng Fengshi's unauthorized mobilization works tactically but is rewarded below marquisate to avoid precedent.
- Preserved Hanji 769 as a complaint-blocking and false-reporting case around Southern Xiongnu chanyu An Guo.
- Preserved Hanji 820 and Hanji 917 as the formal leadership-coercion and unauthorized installation cases.
