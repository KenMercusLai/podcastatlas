# 边疆将领擅断干预 / Unauthorized Frontier Command Intervention

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-917-ruhe-xunsu-zhaodao-shengzhi-jiaxin-de-pojudian-lk2zt5c3onvui9a4ppbowshy66tg|Hanji 917]] introduces unauthorized frontier command intervention through [[ZhangXiuProtectorOfXiongnu|张脩]]. As protector of Xiongnu, Zhang clashes with [[HuzhengChanyu|呼征]], kills him without prior court authorization, and installs [[QiangquChanyu|羌渠]] as [[SouthernXiongnu|南匈奴]] chanyu.

The concept names the gap between frontier discretion and central legitimacy. A field official may have local force and immediate knowledge, but succession intervention in an allied frontier polity can exceed delegated authority and create a court-level legality problem.

## Key Claims
- Frontier officials can create facts on the ground faster than the center can approve them.
- Local command discretion becomes politically dangerous when it changes leadership succession rather than only battlefield tactics.
- The Han court's punishment of Zhang Xiu shows that an outcome may be strategically convenient and still legally unauthorized.
- The pattern differs from [[FrontierConscriptionBackfire|边疆征发反噬]]: here the trigger is unilateral Han-official intervention, not frontier-population backlash against troop requisition.
