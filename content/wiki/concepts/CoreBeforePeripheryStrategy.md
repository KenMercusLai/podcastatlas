## Definition

Core-before-periphery strategy / 先内后外式战略顺序 is the decision rule that a ruler or commander should secure closer, easier, more decisive, or capacity-enabling problems before spending scarce attention on exposed peripheral targets whose defeat would not yet stabilize the center.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 680 adds a commander-level capacity case before the later Guangwu sequencing examples. [[DengYuEasternHan|邓禹]] refuses his generals' call for an immediate [[ChangAnHanCapital|Chang'an]] attack because attached people are numerous but battle-ready troops are few, supply and transport are not ready, and [[ChimeiRebellion|赤眉]] is still temporarily flush with food and goods. He instead moves north to gather provisions, rest, train, and wait for Chimei exhaustion.

Hanji 688 broadens the concept from "near before far" into sequencing by decisive pressure and capacity windows. [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] tells [[ZhuFuEasternHan|朱浮]] that he had expected the [[ChimeiRebellion|赤眉]] to exhaust food and move east, and the source presents that judgment as timing rather than passivity. The same episode has Liu Xiu judge [[PengChongEasternHan|彭宠]] as externally connected but internally exposed, while also noting that Han supply limits require waiting for the wheat harvest.

Hanji 688 also explains why Peng Chong is not the most decisive immediate rival. The host treats Liu Yong as more politically important because a Liu-surnamed claimant can make a stronger imperial-legitimacy claim than Peng Chong's exposed Yan field. The result is a priority hierarchy: handle Chimei through food-pressure timing and surrender management, keep Peng Chong under watch while his position strains, and prioritize threats or capacity conditions that matter more to the core.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-690-jiaoyou-bushen-you-duo-kepa-kankan-tianrong-de-jingli-lpbms8pebzdo2ldiwho1cba81njk|Hanji 690]] gives the concept its clearest remonstrance form through [[FuZhanEasternHan|Fu Zhan]]. Liu Xiu plans to personally attack Peng Chong in Yuyang, but Fu Zhan argues that nearer regions such as Yan, Yu, Qing, and Yi still have unresolved disorder. Peng Chong remains a legitimate target, but a personal imperial campaign against a distant rebel would misallocate attention while easier internal disorder remains unsettled.

Hanji 1135 adds a cost-bearing late-Han case. [[CaoCao|曹操]] is close to extending the western pursuit of [[MaChao|马超]] when Tian Yin and Su Bo rebel in Hejian near his Hebei and Ye political base. He returns east despite [[YangFuLateHan|杨阜]]'s warning that Ma Chao will recover if Liangzhou is left weak. The source therefore sharpens the rule: core-first sequencing can be strategically coherent without being costless, and an accurate warning about the deferred frontier does not prove the priority choice was mistaken.

## Key Claims

- Strategic priority depends on distance, difficulty, contribution to central stability, and current capacity, not only on whether an enemy is guilty or visible.
- A legitimate target can still be the wrong immediate target.
- Personal attention from the ruler is a scarce resource and should not be pulled toward peripheral campaigns too early.
- Nearer domestic disorder, stronger legitimacy threats, or supply windows can outrank a conspicuous rebel at the edge.
- Waiting can be active strategy when the enemy's food, geography, or alliance structure is expected to worsen.
- A commander can be right to delay even after winning submissions if the force is not yet battle-ready and logistics remain weak.
- Protecting the political center can justify abandoning a nearly completed peripheral campaign, but the deferred enemy may recover and impose a measurable territorial cost.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is grounded in four popular-history campaign-sequencing notes, not a universal rule that peripheral threats should always wait. A frontier enemy can become urgent if it threatens the core or closes future options. Hanji 680 shows Deng Yu using this logic well before later sources show him making worse Chimei decisions, so the concept describes a situational judgment rather than a permanent character trait. Hanji 688 includes the host's strategic interpretation of Liu Yong's greater priority, so Liu Yong remains source-scoped until more evidence warrants a standalone page. Hanji 690 works because domestic regions are still unsettled and Yuyang is comparatively remote. Hanji 1135 supplies the strongest qualification: the choice protects Cao Cao's political center but permits Ma Chao's recovery, and the source does not establish the counterfactual costs of staying west while Hejian remained in revolt.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 1135 as a political-center security case rather than another simple near-before-far example.
- Made the frontier cost explicit: a coherent priority choice can still allow a deferred enemy to recover.
- Distinguished the accuracy of Yang Fu's warning from the separate question of whether Cao Cao ranked the risks correctly.
- Preserved timing, legitimacy, capacity, distance, and ruler-attention as the concept's wider variables.
