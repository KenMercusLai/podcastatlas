# Football Manager Myth Deflation

Football manager myth deflation is [[22-zuqiu-jingjixue-duzhe-bubi-shi-qiumi-570874427]]'s challenge to the fan and media habit of treating most coaches as decisive geniuses or obvious failures. The episode summarizes the book's claim that most coaches do not show statistically significant effects on team winning, especially when they take over after bad results and benefit from natural regression or squad quality.

The concept does not deny that any coach can matter. [[ArseneWenger|Arsene Wenger / 温格]] remains a rare source example of a coach whose long-term institutional effect was plausible. The point is that public narratives often overassign causality to the visible person at the press conference, while modern football outcomes also depend on scouting, finance, wages, player quality, data staff, medical teams, and ownership.

## Key Claims
- Coach evaluation is vulnerable to regression-to-the-mean stories after a firing or appointment.
- Media visibility can make managers look more causally central than they are.
- Public relations, emotional leadership, and press conferences may shape reputation even when the win-rate effect is limited.
- Discrimination can be sharper in coaching than playing because selection criteria are opaque and risk perceptions are socially loaded.
