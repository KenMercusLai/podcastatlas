## Definition

上古五刑 / Ancient Five Punishments is the source's compact taxonomy of 墨, 劓, 刖, 宫, and 大辟: visible marking, nose-cutting, foot or knee mutilation, castration, and a broad category of capital punishment.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1139-jiemi-gudai-rouxing-yige-bi-yige-xiaren-lgnj-8smphspf8nud-2o3xh_lggk|Hanji 1139]] presents the five not merely as increasing physical pain but as different attacks on social identity and future capacity. 墨 makes punishment visibly portable through stigma; 劓 joins disfigurement to bodily impairment; 刖 damages mobility and work; 宫 attacks reproduction, dignity, and psychological survival; 大辟 ends life and can extend political communication through public or posthumous treatment.

The taxonomy is analytically useful because it separates mortality from other irreversible harms. It is historically compressed, however: names, techniques, severity, and legal use varied across periods, and 大辟 denotes a capital category rather than one stable execution method.

## Key Claims

- The five punishments impose distinct combinations of bodily injury, social marking, incapacity, humiliation, and death.
- Visible marking can make a sentence continue through everyday recognition and exclusion.
- Mutilation changes future mobility, work, reproduction, and social return even when it preserves life.
- Capital punishment can add corpse treatment and political warning to life deprivation.
- A compact taxonomy should not be mistaken for one unchanged institution across all ancient periods.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page rests on one structured podcast summary rather than legal manuscripts or a specialist history. The five-part scheme compresses institutional change and may blur distinctions among dynasties, statutes, and later narrative examples. The exact punishment suffered by each named person, its mechanics, and the source tradition require independent verification. The episode's reference to harm against women is important but too compressed here to support a full comparative institution.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded taxonomy connecting each punishment to a distinct form of irreversible harm.
- Kept institutional continuity, mechanics, and named examples explicitly source-scoped.
