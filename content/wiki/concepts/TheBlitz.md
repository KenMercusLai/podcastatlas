# The Blitz

## Definition

The Blitz was the sustained German bombing campaign against British cities, industry, transport, and supply systems from September 1940 into May 1941 after the Luftwaffe failed to defeat the [[RoyalAirForce]] in the [[BattleOfBritain]].

## Current Synthesis

[[623-the-nazis-at-war-churchills-finest-hour-part-4-glt7746266438]] presents the Blitz as both destructive urban warfare and a strategic substitution. After airfield attacks and mass raids failed to eliminate Fighter Command or create the conditions for [[OperationSeaLion]], German bombing increasingly targeted London, Coventry, docks, factories, railways, fuel, and other urban systems.

The campaign imposed severe civilian and infrastructural costs but did not produce the political collapse Hitler expected. The episode's judgment is that retaliation, anger, and shared danger hardened British resistance, making spectacular destruction a poor substitute for a coherent method of forcing Britain out of the war.

## Key Claims

- The campaign followed the Luftwaffe's failure to destroy British air defence or enable invasion.
- Bombing combined morale pressure with attacks on industry, docks, railways, supply chains, and fuel.
- London and Coventry became major sites of destruction across an eight-month campaign.
- Urban devastation did not compel British surrender.
- The episode judges the campaign strategically counterproductive because it strengthened anger and resolve.

## Evidence

- Strategic substitution: [[623-the-nazis-at-war-churchills-finest-hour-part-4-glt7746266438]] places sustained city bombing after the failure to achieve a decisive air result against the RAF.
- Target system: [[623-the-nazis-at-war-churchills-finest-hour-part-4-glt7746266438]] identifies morale, factories, docks, railways, supply chains, and fuel depots as targets.
- Political result: [[623-the-nazis-at-war-churchills-finest-hour-part-4-glt7746266438]] argues that bombing strengthened resistance rather than producing surrender.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a strategic narrative rather than a social history of civilian experience or a quantitative bombing study. British morale was not uniform, and failure to force surrender does not mean that deaths, homelessness, disruption, industrial damage, fear, and unequal exposure were minor. The exact balance among morale effects, production losses, targeting choices, civil defence, retaliation, and German operational constraints remains source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created the concept as destructive urban warfare that substituted for, but could not repair, the failure to defeat British air defence.
