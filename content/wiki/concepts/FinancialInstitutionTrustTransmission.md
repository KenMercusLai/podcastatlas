# Financial Institution Trust Transmission / 金融机构信任传导

## Definition

Financial institution trust transmission occurs when the reputation, premises, staff, interface, or regulatory appearance of a bank, insurer, asset manager, or wealth platform makes a separate product, account, or underlying asset feel safer or more suitable than its legal and economic structure warrants.

## Current Synthesis

The historical source shows a trusted commercial bank using its retail network to distribute stocks, bonds, and foreign government debt. The modern private-credit analogy asks how insurers, asset managers, and wealth channels can move household savings into illiquid, leveraged, manager-valued, or redemption-constrained assets.

Episode 187 adds a more granular consumer mechanism. A transaction can occur in a bank-like interface or through an employee and still involve an unauthorized flying order, a different receiving account, a product outside deposit insurance, or an issuer whose obligations are not those of the trusted institution. Trust therefore must be decomposed: where a product is encountered does not settle who issued it, who received the money, who owes repayment, or what protection applies.

## Key Claims

- **Channel reputation does not prove suitability.** Familiar institutions can distribute or appear adjacent to assets the buyer does not understand.
- **Interface and premises can obscure legal separation.** A branch, employee, logo, or app screen may not make the institution the issuer or obligor.
- **Internal incentives can conflict with customer protection.** Distribution capacity, commissions, or unauthorized sales can override underwriting concerns.
- **Trust transmission is strongest when risk is visually quiet.** Stable marks, modest yields, and normal early payments can reduce scrutiny before a discontinuous loss.
- **Protection must be matched to the exact claim.** Deposit insurance, guarantees, custody, and regulatory recourse apply to defined legal relationships, not general familiarity.
- **Verification requires a role map.** Issuer, seller, manager, recipient, borrower, custodian, and guarantor should be checked independently.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Institutional reputation is not meaningless. Regulated firms may have stronger controls, capital, complaint handling, and remediation capacity than informal promoters. The claim is narrower: reputation cannot replace product-level verification. Episode 170's current-cycle mappings are watchlist analogies, and episode 187's village-bank and flying-order examples remain source-scoped rather than independent legal findings.

## What Changed

- Added interface, employee, branch, account, and deposit-protection separation to the trust-transmission mechanism.
- Clarified that modest yield and professional presentation can make risk quieter without making it smaller.
- Turned verification into an explicit map of legal and economic roles.
