# Luxury Consumption As Economic Circulation / 奢侈消费的经济循环

Luxury consumption as economic circulation / 奢侈消费的经济循环 is the economic idea [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-1-qiongshe-jichi-de-zushiye-guanzhong-lmeeh8t6dobgzrkoouato9vhjjuj]] extracts from [[GuanZhong|管仲]] and [[GuanziText|《管子》]]. The episode's reading is that rich people's luxury spending can become poor people's income when it requires craft, service, and material labor.

The examples are intentionally excessive: carved firewood and painted eggshells are wasteful if judged only as utility, but useful in the source's economic frame because they pay people to produce, decorate, transport, and serve. The host translates this into a demand-side claim: consumption by people with surplus wealth can pull money into circulation and create livelihood opportunities.

The concept is not the same as [[StrategicLuxurySabotage|奢侈诱导式削弱]]. In the Su Qin branch, luxury is a covert way to weaken Qi for Yan's benefit. In the Guan Zhong branch, luxury is described as domestic circulation and employment, even though the source still asks whether economic cleverness can become durable political order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-3-gudai-de-jingji-zhanzheng-neng-you-duo-kepa-liybgoz8lspypax6dhuke2js0pxf]] extends the domestic side through [[PeopleFirstEconomicStatecraft|people-first economic statecraft]]. The episode says reasonable consumption can stimulate production and internal demand, but it also shows the hostile mirror image: externally manufactured demand for cloth or deer can become [[DemandInducedAgrarianDisruption|demand-induced agrarian disruption]] when it pulls a rival away from farming.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-4-weishenme-shuo-guanzhong-caishi-jingji-zhan-de-bizu-lrdleqozazh4-xdwas4karzgbmza]] restates the same idea more directly as employment and redistribution through spending. The episode again uses carved firewood and painted eggs as deliberately wasteful examples, arguing that rich people's labor-intensive consumption can give poorer people income and keep wealth moving through society.

## Key Claims
- Luxury can be read economically as labor demand rather than only morally as waste.
- The distribution mechanism is indirect: rich people's surplus spending becomes poorer people's earning opportunity through craft and service work.
- The episode treats material livelihood as connected to ritual and shame through Guan Zhong's "仓廪实" frame.
- The policy remains limited inside the episode's Xunzian hierarchy: economic circulation and state strength do not automatically produce ritual-righteous governance.
- The concept should be kept distinct from hostile luxury inducement, where another state encourages extravagance to drain resources.
