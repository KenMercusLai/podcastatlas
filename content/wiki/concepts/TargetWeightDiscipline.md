# Target Weight Discipline / 目标权重纪律

[[ep239-he-daka-liaoliao-jinjia-you-shuang-ruo-zhuo-biao-le-putongren-hai-neng-shangche-ma-lgcy2trgzt7expvr5bcybybrummq]] adds the gold-strategic-bottom version. [[WangLixin|王立新]] says [[GoldPortfolioProducts|黄金家组合产品]] may keep at least 5% gold, raise the sleeve when the market view supports it, and still allow partial profit realization without clearing the whole gold position; the target weight is tied to role, not to whether the investor feels regret after a price move.

Target weight discipline is the [[AssetAllocation]] behavior rule added by [[166-putongren-neng-cong-jigou-touzizhe-shenshang-xuedao-shenme-chuantai-touzi-abc-lpg8zl95kbvjvxsjr8cpb8ogkeg6]]. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] argues that ordinary investors should think more like institutions in terms of portfolio percentage, asset role, and allocation logic, rather than anchoring on cost basis, today's profit and loss, or whether a single fund has recovered.

The episode's gold example makes the rule concrete: a neutral weight might be 5%, an overweight 10%, and a low weight 3%, with the adjustment driven by the thesis and portfolio role rather than by the original purchase price. This extends [[PositionSizing]] from single-trade size into allocation maintenance and gives [[BehavioralInvestingBiases]] a practical countermeasure.

## Key Claims
- Cost basis is psychologically vivid but usually weak as an investment decision rule.
- Target weights force the investor to name whether an asset is a hedge, income source, growth exposure, experiment, or short-term tactical position.
- Overweight and underweight should be expressed as percentages inside the whole portfolio, not as emotional reactions to price moves.
- A changed thesis can justify rebalancing; a changed floating profit alone should not automatically decide the trade.
- Written target weights make later review easier because the investor can compare behavior against the planned role.
- EP239 adds that a gold target weight can be embedded in a professionally managed product, but the investor still has to understand whether that sleeve is strategic or tactical.
