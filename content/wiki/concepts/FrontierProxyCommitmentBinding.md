## Definition

边疆代理承诺绑定 / frontier proxy commitment binding is a frontier strategy in which a central official or commander makes a dependent, submitting, or allied outside actor prove alignment through costly action against a rival power, so future neutrality or reconciliation becomes harder.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 725 now gives an earlier Liaodong form through [[JiTongEasternHan|Ji Tong]] and [[PianHeXianbei|Pian He]]. Ji Tong first uses gifts and trust to pull Pian He toward Han service, then changes the relationship from rewarded submission to repeated proof by telling Pian He to attack the [[Xiongnu|Xiongnu]] and bring heads for reward. The point is not only battlefield force; the proxy's own violence creates evidence of alignment and a reason for the rival side to treat him as an enemy.

Hanji 807 supplies a later and more institutional Western Regions version through [[BanYongLateHan|Ban Yong]]'s handling of [[CheshiHouKingdomLateHan|车师后王国]]. Ban Yong installs Jiatenu, organizes allied regional forces against Northern Xiongnu, and has Jiatenu personally execute a captured Xiongnu royal kinsman. That act turns Cheshi from a passive beneficiary of Han support into an exposed participant in anti-Xiongnu violence. Across the two cases, proxy alignment is treated as a commitment problem, not only a diplomacy or military-force problem.

## Key Claims

- Frontier control can work through outside actors whose own forces reduce the center's direct exposure.
- Rewards and recognition become stronger when the proxy must prove loyalty through action against a rival power.
- Enemy-directed violence can convert a reversible submission into a public commitment that is harder to unwind.
- Personal or symbolic acts of violence can be used to create enmity between the proxy and the rival side.
- The pattern can stabilize alignment but transfers retaliation risk onto the proxy or dependent polity.
- The concept is narrower than recognition-backed diplomacy because it emphasizes costly proof, not only title, submission, or negotiated status.

## Evidence

- Rewarded submission: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-725-donghan-mingjiang-jitong-yao-baojiaweiguo-jiu-ba-diren-da-paxia-lq06wntw8k5230zmlncquykara9y|Hanji 725]] says Ji Tong gives Pian He goods, treats him generously, and asks him to draw other outside groups toward Han submission.
- Costly proof: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-725-donghan-mingjiang-jitong-yao-baojiaweiguo-jiu-ba-diren-da-paxia-lq06wntw8k5230zmlncquykara9y|Hanji 725]] says Ji Tong tells Pian He to attack the Xiongnu and bring heads as proof of loyalty, after which Pian He repeatedly returns with enemy heads for reward.
- Local kingship and coalition action: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-807-banchao-zhizi-weihe-hui-hanyuan-ruyu-liiwgi77g2on1z2vnwaf1-wvph2d|Hanji 807]] says Ban Yong installs Jiatenu as king of Cheshi Hou and organizes Western Regions forces to attack Northern Xiongnu.
- Irreversible participation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-807-banchao-zhizi-weihe-hui-hanyuan-ruyu-liiwgi77g2on1z2vnwaf1-wvph2d|Hanji 807]] says Ban Yong has Jiatenu execute a captured Xiongnu royal kinsman, and the host reads this as making reconciliation harder.
- Retaliation exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-807-banchao-zhizi-weihe-hui-hanyuan-ruyu-liiwgi77g2on1z2vnwaf1-wvph2d|Hanji 807]] says Northern Xiongnu later attacks Cheshi Hou before Han assistance drives the pressure back.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept now rests on two source notes but remains evidence-bounded. Hanji 725 does not prove Pian He's inner motives or the long-term settlement behind his repeated attacks, and Hanji 807 supports the strategic reading through the host's interpretation without proving Cheshi's internal willingness or Jiatenu's own political calculus. The tactic should also not be romanticized: binding a proxy through violence can create security alignment while transferring retaliation risk onto the local actor.

## What Changed

- Recast the concept from a single Ban Yong/Cheshi example into a broader Eastern Han frontier pattern.
- Added Ji Tong and Pian He's Liaodong case as an earlier reward-backed proxy version.
- Clarified that the shared mechanism is costly proof against a rival power rather than local kingship alone.
