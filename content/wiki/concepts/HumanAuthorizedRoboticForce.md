# Human-Authorized Robotic Force

## Definition

Human-authorized robotic force is a use-of-force model in which a drone or robot may sense, navigate, communicate, or deliver an intervention, but people retain the decision, approval, supervision, and accountability for any force.

## Current Synthesis

The Axon case distinguishes autonomous movement from autonomous coercion. Drones or robots may arrive first, transmit information, and reduce human exposure, while any force remains subject to a human operator, real-time supervisor, and potentially multiple independent authorization keys. The claimed benefit is comparison with current armed entries, where one stressed officer may make an irreversible decision with limited information.

Human authorization is a necessary boundary, not a complete legitimacy test. Remote operators can still misperceive, automation can shape options, multiple approvers can share the same bad information, and non-lethal weapons can cause injury or expand the frequency of coercion. Law, necessity, proportionality, technical reliability, public authorization, incident review, and meaningful abort capability remain essential.

## Key Claims

- Autonomous navigation and sensing should not imply autonomous authority to use force.
- Remote systems can create time and information before a dangerous human entry.
- Multi-person approval can distribute responsibility beyond one officer under stress.
- Non-lethal intent does not eliminate injury, misuse, escalation, or rights risk.
- Recorded operation can improve review without proving the intervention was justified.
- Public legitimacy requires law, disclosed policy, independent oversight, and post-incident accountability.

- **Operational sequence:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] predicts drones and robots arriving before responders to provide situational awareness.
- **Force boundary:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] records Smith's rejection of autonomous use-of-force decisions.
- **Authorization design:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] proposes real-time supervisory approval and multi-key authorization even for a non-lethal weapon.
- **Comparative benefit claim:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] argues that remote, recorded intervention could replace some armed entries and individual split-second decisions.
- **Governance dispute:** [[allin-intv-ricksmith-ch-allin-intv-ricksmith-ch]] records ethics-board resignations over the Taser-equipped drone project and Smith's call for independent civilian review.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not provide deployment evidence showing that robotic intervention reduces deaths, injuries, bias, escalation, or officer risk. The proposal remains controversial even with human control, and the advisers' resignations show that process safeguards do not settle whether a system should exist. “Non-lethal” describes intent or category, not guaranteed outcome.

## What Changed

- Established a force-specific distinction between autonomous mobility and retained human coercive authority.
