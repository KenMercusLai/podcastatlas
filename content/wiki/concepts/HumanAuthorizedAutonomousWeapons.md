# Human-Authorized Autonomous Weapons

Human-authorized autonomous weapons is the governance pattern where AI and autonomy support sensing, classification, navigation, targeting context, or mission execution, while humans set mission intent and authorization thresholds. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-saronic-founders-autonomous-warships-chinas-230x-advantage-swarms-of-robot-ships-42334230]] adds the concept through [[DinoMavroukis]]'s comments on autonomous naval systems.

The source says there is an autonomous weapon-system standard that Dino calls 3009 and frames the policy boundary as one where AI can help distinguish friendly, enemy, combatant, and non-combatant categories, but people still make decisions. The wiki keeps this as a source-scoped governance claim rather than an external legal determination.

## Key Claims
- Human authorization is presented as a design and policy boundary, not just a public-relations phrase.
- AI can support perception and classification, but the source says mission intent and approval thresholds remain human-set.
- The boundary may become harder under high-intensity conflict, which is why the source's confidence should remain source-scoped.
- The concept extends [[HumanJudgmentUnderAI]] into naval and defense autonomy.
