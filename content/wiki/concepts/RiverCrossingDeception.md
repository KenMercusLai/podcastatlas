## Definition

River crossing deception / 渡河欺敌 is the use of managed signals, concealed routes, timing, and engineering to make an opponent defend the wrong crossing, enter a dangerous water line, or react too late to a completed passage.

## Current Synthesis

The three cases show that river deception is not one trick. Against [[WeiBao|魏豹]], [[HanXin|韩信]] displays crossing preparations near Linjin and Pujin while actually crossing at Xiayang with improvised floats, making the prepared defense irrelevant. At the [[WeishuiBattle|潍水之战]], Han Xin dams the river, crosses, feigns retreat, and releases water after [[LongJuChuGeneral|龙且]] pursues, turning the river from obstacle into a timed weapon.

The [[WeinanBattleLateHan|渭南之战]] adds a campaign-scale bypass. [[CaoCao|曹操]] fixes the coalition at Tong Pass through visible pressure while [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] and [[ZhuLingLateHan|朱灵]] secure the Pujin route. The main army's contested crossing and later protected movement show the concept's logistical boundary: surprise gains access, but rearguard action, corridor protection, supply, and fortification are needed to keep access usable.

## Key Claims

- A visible crossing threat can concentrate defense at the obvious point while the real passage occurs elsewhere.
- Deception requires a workable mobility solution, such as an alternate ferry, improvised floats, secured approaches, or controlled water flow.
- Rivers can be manipulated as timed weapons, not only bypassed as obstacles.
- The tactical payoff is positional shock: a successful hidden crossing makes a strong static defense irrelevant or forces late redeployment.
- A concealed advance is incomplete until the army can protect the main crossing, movement corridor, supply flow, and new position.
- The concept differs from attacking a force in mid-crossing because it concerns management of one's own route and the enemy's perception.

Wrong crossing defended:

Water-line pursuit trap:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-2-xiangyu-touhao-dajiang-zenme-shu-gei-hanxin-de-lhe-lmb1zrzipbongg02gmnmb4l|Hanji 164-2]] says Han Xin uses an upstream dam, a crossing, and a false retreat to draw Long Ju forward before releasing water and splitting the Chu force.

Campaign-scale bypass and consolidation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1121-tongguan-shengsi-jie-caocao-weile-ying-yishen-fanxian-lvfxitrmo0wq9hl1fmzzuagsobbl|Hanji 1121]] says Cao Cao maintains pressure at Tong Pass while Xu Huang and Zhu Ling take Pujin, then protects the main movement through rearguard action, defended corridors, a Wei River crossing, and fortification.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

These podcast summaries do not independently establish exact routes, engineering methods, timing, troop totals, or authorship of each plan. Similar surface actions may belong to different mechanisms: [[CrossingVulnerabilityStrike|半渡击敌]] attacks an enemy during its crossing, while this concept manages one's own crossing or the opponent's perception of the water line. Hanji 1121 also shows that a successful concealed route does not eliminate crossing vulnerability; Cao Cao's personal danger demonstrates the exposure created while the main force is still moving.

## What Changed

- Expanded the concept from two Han Xin battlefield cases to a campaign-scale Tong Pass/Pujin bypass.
- Distinguished initial access through deception from the logistical work needed to preserve that access.
- Added contested main crossing, corridor protection, and fortification as limits on what surprise alone can accomplish.
- Migrated the page to the synthesis-v1 contract.
