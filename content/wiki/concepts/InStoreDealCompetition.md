# In-Store Deal Competition / 到店团购竞争

## Definition

In-store deal competition is rivalry among local-commerce platforms for discovery, evaluation, voucher purchase, payment, and redemption when the customer travels to the merchant and the platform does not perform delivery.

## Current Synthesis

The absence of platform delivery makes in-store deals economically different from food delivery: the platform can compete for a potentially higher-margin transaction without paying for the last mile. When the user has already selected the merchant, comparable vouchers can make price and checkout friction decisive. That gives [[Doushenshen|抖省省]] a route to use [[Douyin]] traffic and subsidies against [[Meituan]] without first reproducing a delivery fleet.

The market is not price alone. [[DazhongDianping|大众点评]] contributes accumulated review and decision content, while Meituan contributes merchant breadth, store matching, transaction habit, and redemption experience. The durable competitive system therefore joins price with intent, merchant supply, trust, payment, anti-fraud, and reliable redemption.

## Key Claims

- Removing platform delivery makes in-store vouchers a different cost and margin structure from delivered orders.
- Once a merchant and package are chosen, a cheaper credible voucher can shift the final transaction away from the discovery platform.
- Purposeful search and near-term use should produce a stronger redemption loop than impulse coupon buying, though the episode's rate claims remain unverified.
- Merchant breadth, especially among small independent stores, is a supply-side defense against traffic and subsidy advantages.
- Reviews and ranking help discovery only while users trust their independence and the platform can retain the checkout.
- Subsidies require anti-fraud controls and cannot by themselves establish normal-price loyalty or durable market share.

## Evidence

- Cost structure and price - [[ep149-ep149]] contrasts customer-performed in-store fulfillment with delivery and argues that identical-store comparison makes price unusually salient.
- Intent and redemption - [[ep149-ep149]] contrasts content-stimulated coupon purchase with purposeful search, simpler order retrieval, and near-term redemption.
- Incumbent defenses - [[ep149-ep149]] attributes decision value to Dianping reviews and stronger merchant coverage and store matching to Meituan.
- Competitive limits - [[ep149-ep149]] discusses subsidy fatigue, fraud controls, fragmented Alibaba entrances, and the resource strain of multi-front competition.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not independently verify daily active users, margins, redemption rates, personalized discounts, merchant coverage, or subsidy economics. Low price can acquire transactions without establishing repeat use after subsidies. Review content can remain valuable even when checkout leaks, and merchant coverage may be expensive but not impossible for a well-funded challenger to reproduce.

## What Changed

- Created a framework separating in-store deal competition from delivery competition.
- Joined price and traffic advantages to merchant supply, trust, payment, redemption, and anti-fraud constraints.
