# Dilemma Actions

## Definition

Dilemma actions are protest tactics designed so that several plausible official responses impose political, reputational, or operational costs on the authority.

## Current Synthesis

[[Otpor]]'s “Dinar for Change” action made the logic visible. Allowing passersby to strike a barrel bearing [[SlobodanMilosevic|Slobodan Milošević]]'s image normalized public ridicule; arresting participants risked advertising repression; removing or arresting the barrel made police look absurd. Recruiting grandparents created a related dilemma by widening participation while activating a norm against publicly beating elderly people.

The mechanism combines tactical design with audience interpretation. Humor can weaken an autocrat's performed invincibility, but an action works only if observers recognize the asymmetry and if organizers manage the safety, accessibility, and escalation risks.

## Key Claims

- A dilemma action evaluates success across multiple opponent responses rather than betting on one predicted reaction.
- Humor can reduce fear and make rigid authority appear vulnerable or absurd.
- Participant identity can change the reputational cost of repression.
- Simple, repeatable actions can widen participation beyond trained activists.
- The tactic supports rather than replaces organization, discipline, and broader political strategy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Authorities can ignore, censor, infiltrate, violently suppress, or reframe a tactic, and participants may bear severe risks despite clever design. Social taboos do not reliably constrain repression. The Serbian examples illustrate a mechanism but do not establish universal transferability.
