# Ableist Cure Pressure / 健全中心主义治愈压力

## Definition
Ableist cure pressure / 健全中心主义治愈压力 is the episode's frame for the social force that treats disabled, autistic, or developmentally delayed children as errors to be corrected before their lives can be valued.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that cure pressure is not only an individual parent's wish. It is produced by an achievement culture in which speech timing, school performance, employment, competitiveness, and "normal" development become the standard for whether a child is imagined to have a future. Under that pressure, parents may pursue risky interventions, scientists may overtrust a repair narrative, and society may underestimate the life satisfaction and agency of disabled people themselves.

The concept does not reject all treatment. It separates interventions that reduce a person's real suffering or expand communication from interventions driven by stigma, professional ambition, or the discomfort of nondisabled observers.

## Key Claims
- Cure pressure begins when disability is interpreted as a failed version of normality rather than a life requiring support and accommodation.
- Family desperation is socially produced as well as personal, because schooling, labor, status, and public language can make disabled futures appear closed.
- Scientific and medical actors can share ableist assumptions when they undervalue disabled people's own judgments of quality of life.
- Ethical care asks whose suffering is being addressed: the person's distress, the family's fear, or society's intolerance.
- The alternative to cure pressure is not inaction, but [[AutismSupportSystems]], [[AutismEarlyIntervention]], accessibility, and respect for personhood.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not be used to dismiss all medical treatment, pain relief, communication support, or developmental intervention. The source's boundary is about repair-first normalizing pressure and unjustified risk, not about refusing help that the person needs.
