## Definition

分层式地方武力拆解 / layered local elite demobilization is the sequence of separating a local power network's leaders, fighters, and families so that no single home-based bloc retains enough social and military capacity to resist the center.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]] grounds the concept in [[LiangXiLateHan|梁习]]'s settlement of [[BingzhouLateHan|并州]]. The sequence begins softly: local strongmen are treated with courtesy and some receive office. That step draws leaders away from independent home bases and converts status into a state-mediated benefit.

The next layers target capacity rather than title. Local young men are recruited into the army and divided among different commanders, preventing them from remaining a single locally controlled force. Their families are then moved to [[YeLateHan|邺城]], weakening the fighters' home network while bringing relatives under closer central supervision. Armed suppression is reserved for refusal. The method therefore combines incorporation, fragmentation, relocation, and exemplary force.

The source credits the sequence with rapid order, submission, taxation, agriculture, and stricter law, but those outcomes do not remove its coercive character. The concept names an administrative mechanism, not a moral endorsement: state capacity rises because local capacity is deliberately decomposed.

## Key Claims

- Local elite power is a network of leaders, armed followers, families, and place-based ties rather than a single officeholder.
- Courtesy and appointment can separate leaders from an independent local base before direct coercion begins.
- Recruiting and dispersing fighting-age men prevents their reuse as a unified local force.
- Relocating families weakens local reproduction of power and can create a de facto compliance guarantee.
- Sequential measures reduce the chance that every layer of a local network resists at once.
- Armed punishment remains the enforcement backstop, making the method coercive despite its administrative surface.
- Apparent post-settlement order should be evaluated alongside displacement, killing, and the loss of local autonomy.

## Evidence

- Leader incorporation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]] says Liang Xi honors local strongmen and recommends some for office.
- Fighter dispersal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]] says local youths are recruited and divided among different commanders during expeditions.
- Household removal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]] reports tens of thousands of relatives relocated to Ye.
- Coercive backstop and outcome: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1073-huguan-zhi-zhan-caocao-de-tucheng-jihua-haican-le-shui-lspli7p63ouktn6k0zbgvggj59di|Hanji 1073]] reports attacks on refusal, mass surrender, frontier submission, and restored administrative order.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one podcast account and does not establish that every step followed a single prior blueprint or that the reported order was durable and broadly accepted. Exact movement and casualty totals require independent textual verification. Relocated households may have served several functions—supervision, population concentration, supply, or hostage-like leverage—and the source does not let the wiki reduce them to only one.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 1073's complete leader-fighter-family settlement sequence.
