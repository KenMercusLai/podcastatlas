# Sectarian Security Dilemma

Sectarian security dilemma is the source pattern where each community's defensive fear or preparation is read by the other as offensive threat. In [[697-the-troubles-murder-in-belfast-part-1-glt4877520736]], the dynamic begins before street fighting: Protestants are a majority inside [[NorthernIreland]] but a minority on the island of [[Ireland]], while Catholics experience state institutions as hostile through [[InstitutionalSectarianDiscrimination]].

In [[698-the-troubles-the-brink-of-civil-war-part-2-glt2308408988]], the dilemma becomes operational. Catholics in [[Derry]] build barricades and prepare petrol bombs because they fear loyalist and [[RoyalUlsterConstabulary|RUC]] violence, while Protestants interpret those preparations and [[JackLynch]]'s comments as signs of insurrection or Irish intervention. The same dynamic appears in [[Belfast]], where loyalists feared attack from the Falls Road while Catholics feared attack from the Shankill Road.

## Key Claims
- Fear can make preparation rational locally while destabilizing the wider system.
- Political rhetoric from outside the immediate conflict can alter threat perception inside it.
- Once trust collapses, neutral separation becomes hard because any armed force is quickly assigned a side.
- Demographic fear and institutional exclusion can create the dilemma before visible barricades or weapons appear.
