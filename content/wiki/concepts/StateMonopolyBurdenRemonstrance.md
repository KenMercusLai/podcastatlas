# State Monopoly Burden Remonstrance / 官营垄断扰民进谏

## Definition

State monopoly burden remonstrance is the argument that direct state operation of lucrative resources can burden producers, corrupt official incentives, and damage public trust even when it promises treasury gain. In Hanji 753, [[ZhuHuiZhangEraEasternHan|Zhu Hui]] applies this critique to Zhang Lin's proposal for official salt monopoly and restoration of the Han Wudi junshu system.

## Current Synthesis

The source does not present Zhu Hui as rejecting fiscal capacity itself. His objection is institutional: if officials operate as merchants, the state imports market-seeking behavior into administration while salt producers experience the policy as impoverishing extraction.

The remonstrance also tests imperial tolerance. [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Zhangdi]] first reacts angrily when Zhu Hui and the Shangshu officials refuse, but then releases them, says opposition is welcome, and rewards Zhu Hui after he refuses to sign an infeasible policy.

## Key Claims

- A fiscally attractive monopoly can be administratively damaging if it turns officials into profit-seeking operators.
- Producer resentment is a governance cost, not merely a private complaint.
- Remonstrance against monopoly is about public burden and office incentives, not only about policy preference.
- Imperial acceptance of dissent is part of the policy outcome because a ruler must tolerate correction before bad extraction hardens into law.
- The concept connects resource policy to ministerial duty: signing a known-infeasible measure is itself a breach of office.

## Evidence

- Monopoly proposal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] introduces Zhang Lin's proposal to restore official salt monopoly and the junshu system.
- Incentive critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] has Zhu Hui argue that officials would become merchant-like under such a system.
- Public-burden critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] links the policy to salt producers' resentment at being impoverished.
- Ministerial duty and imperial tolerance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-753-banchao-beigao-heizhuang-lt-sksxrz30sawit81eqbeonhzv|Hanji 753]] shows Zhu Hui refusing to sign and Zhangdi later accepting and rewarding the refusal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The current evidence is a remonstrance case, not a full fiscal comparison between monopoly and non-monopoly systems.
- Zhu Hui's critique does not prove that every state monopoly is harmful; it identifies the burden and incentive risks at stake.
- The Han Wudi precedent remains source-scoped here and should not be expanded without additional sources.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 753's Zhu Hui remonstrance against salt monopoly and junshu restoration.
