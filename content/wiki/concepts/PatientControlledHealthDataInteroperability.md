# Patient-Controlled Health Data Interoperability

## Definition

Patient-controlled health data interoperability is the ability for people to retrieve, move, combine, and authorize use of their medical records across providers, payers, devices, and software without proprietary lock-in.

## Current Synthesis

The sources treat legal ownership without practical access as incomplete. [[MehmetOz|Mehmet Oz]] says a 600-company pledge is intended to make records and tools more interoperable, while the hosts argue that AI can help translate unstructured records into usable context. [[DiogoRau]] adds a patient-agency argument: people should be able to see their underlying health data directly and then choose how to share it rather than being required to receive provider interpretation first.

The durable judgment is narrower than either aspiration. Portability and direct visibility can improve continuity, patient preparation, self-understanding, and clinician tools, but only when identity, consent, provenance, privacy, security, correction, comprehension, and clinical responsibility travel with the data. Patient access does not imply that every raw signal is self-explanatory or that professional interpretation is unnecessary.

## Key Claims

- Formal patient ownership has limited value when records remain difficult to retrieve, export, or combine.
- Interoperability can reduce repeated intake and give clinicians a more complete longitudinal view.
- AI can help structure messy records, but model readability does not prove clinical accuracy or authorization to act.
- Standards and participation matter across hospitals, pharmacies, payers, devices, and consumer tools; one app cannot create interoperability alone.
- Patient control requires usable consent, revocation, correction, provenance, and access logs, not only downloadable files.
- Direct patient visibility and optional professional interpretation can coexist; access need not be delayed merely because interpretation is valuable.

## Evidence

- **Access and portability:** [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-dr-mehmet-oz-on-fixing-american-healthcare-fraud-live-from-davos-39860555]] says records are legally patient-owned but often trapped in proprietary systems or hard to access.
- **Ecosystem coordination:** [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-dr-mehmet-oz-on-fixing-american-healthcare-fraud-live-from-davos-39860555]] attributes a pledge on interoperability, transparency, and patient-facing tools to roughly 600 companies.
- **AI usability layer:** [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-dr-mehmet-oz-on-fixing-american-healthcare-fraud-live-from-davos-39860555]] argues that AI may make unstructured records easier for patients and clinicians to interpret.
- **Direct patient visibility:** [[the-future-of-healthcare-technology-with-eli-lilly-and-companys-diogo-rau-the-future-of-healthcare-technology-with-eli]] argues that patients should be able to see underlying health data and decide whether to share it with providers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The Oz episode does not identify the pledge's signatories, technical standards, compliance measures, adoption rate, or patient outcomes. The Rau interview does not specify data types, access interfaces, consent architecture, correction workflows, or how raw continuous measurements should be contextualized. Easier movement also expands the attack surface for sensitive data, and patient access does not remove HIPAA, consent, data-quality, bias, comprehension, or medical-liability constraints. AI-generated summaries can omit, distort, or over-prioritize details and still require review against the original record.

## What Changed

- Established an initial synthesis that separates formal ownership from usable portability and governed reuse.
- Added direct patient visibility as distinct from, and compatible with, professional interpretation.
