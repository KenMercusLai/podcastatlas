## Definition
帝国整齐划一 is the podcast's interpretation of Qin post-conquest efforts to make populations, standards, movement, local power and sanctioned learning answer to a central order.

## Current Synthesis
Direct [[CommanderyCountyAntiEnfeoffment]] rule by the [[QinState]] requires more than a map: material standards, disarmed rivals, managed elites, roads and state-controlled instruction support command transmission. Tours can dramatize reach but cannot alone establish administrative compliance. Hanji 280 adds a long-run hypothesis: inherited standardization and shared writing can remain available to later reunifiers even after dynastic collapse. That is a path-dependence claim, not proof of permanent uniformity or a timeless “unity gene.”

## Key Claims
- Appointed local rule and verified measures turn formerly separate territories into a centrally legible fiscal and legal space.
- Collecting weapons and relocating local elites target armed capacity and inherited networks, at social cost.
- Roads structure imperial travel and traffic hierarchy, though precise specifications are the host's reconstruction.
- Touring makes the center visible in former rival territory but is a performance, not proof of daily control.
- Removing walls and levees and restricting private scholarly authority aim to weaken alternative local bases of power, each with distinct costs.
- Hanji 280 interprets Qin-Han standardization and shared script as infrastructure later regimes could reuse when reconstructing large-scale rule.

## Evidence
- Institutions and coercion: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-4-bing-liuguo-hou-qinshihuang-jiao-ni-ruhe-shou-jiangshan-lgwsd0c3wkl-cphma8sm3ftkowwr]] reports 36 commanderies, Qin-standard weights and measures with government references and annual checks, captured weapons cast into twelve figures at [[Xianyang]], and 120,000 powerful households forcibly relocated. [[PostConquestDisarmament]] and [[EliteRelocationControl]] make resistance harder; copied six-state palaces north of the Wei River display possession. The host notes limited transitional accommodation and six-state resentment, not a frictionless integration.
- Movement: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p]] describes [[QinChidao]] tamped roads, lane hierarchy, permissioned crossings and planting. This physical [[QinAdministrativeStandardization]] makes imperial mobility repeatable, but the design details and cosmic [[QinJimiao]]/[[Taiyi]] associations are host-level reconstructions.
- Visibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-1-jiemi-qinshihuang-fengchan-weishenme-xuan-taishan-lkcyayuqrtsgy-k4bhdvy-l-lapo]] places [[QinShiHuang]] in former six-state land in 219 BCE: prepared routes, inscriptions and a disputed [[Taishan]] fengshan sequence make [[ImperialTourPoliticalTheater]] and [[FengshanRitualLegitimacy]] possible public assertions. Sacred legitimacy is related to but not interchangeable with uniform local administration.
- Local capacity: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8]] has a post-[[Jieshi]] order to remove large city walls and levees. The presenter reads it as [[StrongTrunkWeakBranchCentralization]]—disabling local fortification and water-control autonomy—while noting smaller defenses survived; the hydraulic effects are not independently measured.
- Authorized learning: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-124-fenshu-kengru-wo-zai-bzhan-wei-qinshihuang-pingfan-ljnhwcouqu35rmvb-cnrzyzj2j6l]] places [[ChunyuYue]]'s enfeoffment argument against [[LiSi]]'s 213 BCE [[QinBookBurningPolicy]]. Private non-Qin histories, [[Shijing]] and [[Shangshu]] were targeted while official copies, medicine, divination and farming texts were exempt. [[OfficialLearningMonopoly]] aimed to stop using older norms to criticize rule; the host also describes lost six-state history, hidden books by [[KongFu]] and the order's counterproductive coercion. This does not establish the separate later kengru incident.
- Long-run reuse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-280-heshuo-dajie-weiqing-ruhe-yizhan-fenghou-3-li8xxfweqgks9iadx-vynsuak52e|Hanji 280]] argues that standardized institutions and a shared written tradition helped make later Chinese reunification imaginable and administratively feasible, while offering Rome as a contrast.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
A title of “homogenization” describes ambition and instrument, not successful erasure of regional variation. Much of the program is read retrospectively by one history show; standards, roads, staged presence, defensive infrastructure and intellectual controls work through different mechanisms. [[ConquestCommanderyTransition]] and the episode's limited concessions caution against a pure sudden-uniformity story. Violence, labor and cultural destruction are substantive consequences, not efficiency footnotes. Hanji 280's claims of continuous linguistic unity, a Chinese “unity gene,” and Roman non-unity are broad comparative interpretations; the narrower synthesis is that institutional and written templates can create path dependence without determining outcomes.

## What Changed
- Added the qualified hypothesis that standardization and shared writing can outlast a dynasty as reusable reunification infrastructure.
- Separated institutional path dependence from claims of civilizational essence or achieved cultural uniformity.
