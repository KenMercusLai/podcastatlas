# Cocaine Route Displacement

Cocaine route displacement is the source's mechanism for how enforcement pressure changes trafficking geography. In [[taking-the-shine-off-albedo-and-global-temperatures-6a730fdaca067b295bab9760]], [[OriOgunbiyi]] says European crackdowns on direct shipments from South America have pushed cartels to route more cocaine through [[WestAfrica]].

The pattern repeats inside the region. The episode says increased scrutiny of shipments from [[SierraLeone]] pushed traffickers toward other countries, including [[Liberia]] and [[Ghana]], while [[PauloSilva]] says scrutiny of cargo from places such as [[Brazil]] and Suriname created more points of departure.

## Key Claims
- Interdiction can shift routes without ending the underlying trade when demand and profit remain high.
- More departure points make checks harder and increase the value of intelligence, port screening, and maritime response.
- Displacement can spread corruption pressure across weaker institutions instead of concentrating it in one known corridor.
