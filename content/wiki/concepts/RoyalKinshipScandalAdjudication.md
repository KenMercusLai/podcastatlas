## Definition

宗室亲属丑闻审理 / royal kinship scandal adjudication is the court pattern where an accusation against imperial kin must balance public law, kinship taboo, royal-house dignity, evidence reliability, and political consequences for ruler-kin trust.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 545 grounds the concept through [[LiuLiWesternHan|梁王刘立]]'s case. A report that begins with lawlessness and hostile speech against [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s outer-relative family expands into an alleged relationship with [[LiuYuanziWesternHan|刘原子]], Liu Li's aunt. Because officials frame the accusation as "禽兽行" and request execution, the court must decide whether royal status makes punishment more necessary or procedure more careful.

[[GuYongWesternHan|谷永]] supplies the concept's key procedural counterweight. He does not say royal kin should be outside law; he argues that a politically explosive private accusation should not be treated as proved simply because the first investigation failed to confirm the original charge. His memorial uses ritual privacy, emotional plausibility, age difference, royal access to women, and Liu Yuanzi's likely shame to question whether coerced or confused testimony has turned an uncertain matter into public disgrace.

The concept therefore sits between evidence skepticism and royal-house management. It asks how a court can discipline a prince without letting an expanded case, sexual taboo, or prosecutorial eagerness destroy trust among imperial relatives.

## Key Claims

- Royal kinship does not remove legal exposure, but it raises the political cost of public accusation and punishment.
- A case that expands from speech or political offense into private sexual scandal needs especially careful evidentiary review.
- Kinship taboo can make punishment seem urgent while also making false or coerced accusation more damaging.
- Ritual privacy and royal-house dignity function as procedural concerns, not merely as coverups.
- A ruler may preserve authority by ordering careful review or suppressing an unreliable case rather than by staging maximum punishment.

- Case expansion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-545-xihan-huangzu-jingtian-dagua-lvdplebjdtlav7kywaxvevmbtx6x|Hanji 545]] says Liu Li is first investigated for hostile speech and lawlessness, then accused of adultery with Liu Yuanzi.
- Capital charge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-545-xihan-huangzu-jingtian-dagua-lvdplebjdtlav7kywaxvevmbtx6x|Hanji 545]] says officials request execution under the "禽兽行" frame.
- Privacy argument: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-545-xihan-huangzu-jingtian-dagua-lvdplebjdtlav7kywaxvevmbtx6x|Hanji 545]] says Gu Yong begins from ritual screen-and-boundary language to argue against exposing inner royal matters rashly.
- Plausibility challenge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-545-xihan-huangzu-jingtian-dagua-lvdplebjdtlav7kywaxvevmbtx6x|Hanji 545]] says Gu Yong questions the age gap, Liu Li's incentives, and Liu Yuanzi's willingness to expose disgrace.
- Review remedy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-545-xihan-huangzu-jingtian-dagua-lvdplebjdtlav7kywaxvevmbtx6x|Hanji 545]] says Gu Yong proposes pardon or a respected Tingwei-led reexamination, and Chengdi ultimately suppresses the case.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is grounded in one Western Han source note and should not be treated as a complete doctrine of Han royal criminal procedure. Hanji 545 does not prove the accusation false, reconstruct the full testimony, define "禽兽行" in statutory detail, or show how ordinary non-royal defendants would have been treated. The source does show that Gu Yong treats procedure, plausibility, privacy, and royal trust as inseparable in a politically explosive case.
