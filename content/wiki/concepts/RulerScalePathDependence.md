## Definition

Ruler-scale path dependence is a governance failure where a leader carries habits from an earlier, smaller role into a larger authority environment even after the problem scale has changed. The issue is not experience itself; it is the unexamined transfer of local routines, old symbols, familiar control mechanisms, or household preferences into imperial, state, or organization-wide decisions that require different feedback and delegation.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-700-lishi-gaosu-ni-bie-rang-renzhi-piancha-zuai-nide-chenggong-lhgt6dyzgc4s-ost2nbzdhfyzofv|Hanji 700]] grounds the concept through [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]]. The episode presents him as a Shu-based emperor-claimant who keeps thinking from the scale of an old Qingshui county magistrate: he renames counties and offices, governs too minutely, and pursues control through visible administrative tinkering after his claimed authority has become much larger.

The same source ties that mismatch to strategic and symbolic judgment. [[JingHanEasternHan|Jing Han]] warns that Shu's chance lies in disrupting [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] before Liu Xiu can concentrate against the west, but Gongsun Shu retreats after internal objections. He then leans on fuming legitimacy, ceremonial display, and early rewards to his sons, which make the regime look self-authorizing and family-centered before the wider contest is settled.

The concept therefore sits between cognitive bias and institutional design. Old experience can become useful only if the leader asks whether the old task, authority, information flow, and error cost still match the current role. When those dimensions change, repeating what once felt natural can create policy disorder, adviser frustration, public burden, and elite resentment.

## Key Claims

- Past governing experience can become a bias when the leader treats a changed scale as if it were the old job with more status.
- Administrative detail can signal diligence at local scale but become micromanagement, churn, or burden at imperial scale.
- Symbolic legitimacy is especially brittle when it substitutes for resolving military, fiscal, and elite-trust problems.
- Adviser systems fail when a ruler can understand a strategic window but cannot sustain commitment after family or insider resistance.
- Household-first rewards damage public authority when followers are still bearing war cost and the outcome remains undecided.
- The pattern is transferable to organizations because founder or executive habits can outlive the stage where those habits fit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently source-scoped to Hanji 700. The episode's "cognitive bias" reading is an interpretation of Gongsun Shu's behavior, not an independently measured psychological diagnosis. Jing Han's offensive plan may have been strategically coherent, but the source does not prove it would have succeeded against Liu Xiu. The iron-money and administrative details should be treated as episode-bounded governance evidence rather than a complete economic history of Shu. The closing company analogy is incomplete in the provided transcript, so it supports only a cautious organization-governance extension.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 700 to distinguish ruler-scale path dependence from generic institutional path dependence.
- Grounded the concept in Gongsun Shu's local-official habits, iron money, administrative minuteness, strategic wavering, and household-first rewards.
