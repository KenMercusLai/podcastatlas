## Definition
End-user-purchased workwear is required or customary professional clothing that is used inside an employer's institution but selected and paid for by the individual worker, shifting product choice and channel power toward the wearer.

## Current Synthesis
The FIGS source shows why an apparently business-to-business category can behave like a consumer market. Hospital administrators govern the workplace, yet the episode says clinicians usually bought their own scrubs. Selling to administrators therefore targeted a party with limited product desire, while selling directly to clinicians aligned purchaser, wearer, feedback source, and advocate. Dense workplaces then made each worn product a demonstration to colleagues, allowing word of mouth to compound.

## Key Claims
- Institutional use does not imply institutional purchasing; buyer, wearer, approver, and beneficiary must be mapped separately.
- When the wearer is also the purchaser, fit, identity, comfort, and daily function can matter more than procurement standardization alone.
- Direct feedback becomes more reliable when it comes from the person performing the work in the garment.
- Workplace density can turn visible use into repeated peer demonstration and lower-cost acquisition.
- The model remains constrained by employer dress codes, credentialing, safety rules, reimbursement, and workers' ability to pay.

## Evidence
- **Purchaser mapping:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] reports Trina's finding that 85% of healthcare professionals bought their own uniforms even though incumbent manufacturers emphasized distributors.
- **Failed institutional route:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] says direct attempts to sell large quantities to hospitals and clinics produced little traction.
- **User-channel fit:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] describes direct sidewalk sales and online relationships with clinicians.
- **Peer acquisition:** [[figs-trina-spear-and-heather-hasson-the-2-5-billion-scrubs-brand-that-investors-said-would-never-work-vmp6448511514]] identifies workplace word of mouth as FIGS's largest acquisition driver.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The self-purchase percentage and acquisition ranking are source-scoped and may vary by country, employer, role, union agreement, and time. Direct-to-worker sales can improve choice while also normalizing the transfer of essential-equipment cost from institutions to workers. The concept should not collapse regulated protective equipment into ordinary apparel.

## What Changed
- Added a buyer-user mapping for professional clothing used in institutions but purchased by workers.
- Added cost-transfer and workplace-policy limits to the direct-to-consumer opportunity.
