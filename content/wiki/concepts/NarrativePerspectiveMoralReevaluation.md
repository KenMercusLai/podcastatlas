## Definition

Narrative-perspective moral reevaluation is a change in audience judgment produced when stable traits are realigned with a different focal character, conflict, or set of consequences. What once read as wit, loyalty, or justified caution may read as manipulation, partiality, or prejudice when experienced from the receiving side.

## Current Synthesis

[[HuangRong|黄蓉]] provides the initial case. In 《射雕英雄传》, her schemes usually advance her bond with 郭靖 or frustrate characters positioned against them, so cleverness and partiality remain pleasurable. In [[ReturnOfTheCondorHeroes|《神雕侠侣》]], the reader follows 杨过, and Huang Rong's suspicion becomes an obstacle imposed on a neglected young protagonist.

Perspective shift does not make all moral evaluation arbitrary. The useful analysis separates initial evidence, later evidence, role obligations, harm, and willingness to revise. Huang Rong has reasons for early caution, but repeated rescues weaken later assumptions that Yang Guo will harm the Guo family. Likewise, a mother's impulse to protect Guo Fu explains partiality without resolving the injustice done to Yang Guo.

The framework therefore joins reception analysis to evidence-sensitive judgment: narrative alignment explains why a flaw becomes visible, while conduct and consequences determine whether the revised criticism is warranted.

## Key Claims

- Stable traits can receive opposite moral labels when their beneficiaries and targets change.
- Focalization influences sympathy but does not by itself establish innocence or guilt.
- A fair reevaluation separates contextual reasons for caution from failure to update after contrary evidence.
- Role changes can raise the stakes of an existing trait without requiring a personality reversal.
- Prior idealization magnifies disappointment when a formerly admired character causes visible harm.
- Explanation by family loyalty, age, or narrative position should not become automatic exoneration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

A perspective shift may expose previously hidden harm, but it can also manufacture sympathy through selective information. Readers need not abandon earlier judgments merely because focalization changes. In the Huang Rong case, the source is a podcast interpretation rather than a systematic comparison of every scene or edition, and the hosts disagree over sincerity, motive, and the lasting effect of her conversation with Yang Guo.
