# Narrative Frame and Authorial Distance

## Definition

Narrative frame and authorial distance describe the interpretive separation created when a work places a character's account inside another narrator's telling rather than presenting that character as the author's direct voice.

## Current Synthesis

In [[HeartOfDarkness|Heart of Darkness]], an outer narrator aboard the Nellie reports Marlow's Congo story. This structure makes it unsafe to equate every statement by Marlow with [[JosephConrad]]'s own position and turns listening, memory, distortion, and judgment into parts of the work. However, as the episode stresses, formal distance is not a complete defense against [[ChinuaAchebe]]'s critique: the author still constructs the total representational world in which African voices are limited.

## Key Claims

- A framed speaker is not automatically a transparent proxy for the author.
- Nested narration makes mediation and uncertainty part of a text's meaning.
- Author-speaker separation can support criticism of a narrator's assumptions.
- The same separation cannot by itself absolve a work's wider patterns of omission or stereotype.

## Evidence

- Formal design: [[541-heart-of-darkness-fear-and-loathing-in-the-congo-glt5482142786]] explains that Marlow's story is narrated by another listener on the Thames.
- Interpretive defense: [[541-heart-of-darkness-fear-and-loathing-in-the-congo-glt5482142786]] presents the argument that Marlow is not simply Conrad and that Conrad's structural position is closer to a listener.
- Limit of the defense: [[541-heart-of-darkness-fear-and-loathing-in-the-congo-glt5482142786]] warns that narrator-author distance can too easily become a way to excuse any troubling representation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Distance must be demonstrated by the work's structure and effects, not merely asserted whenever a narrator says something objectionable. Even when irony is present, the full text remains an authored arrangement of available voices, silences, and images.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to preserve both the interpretive value and the ethical limit of Conrad's framed narration.
