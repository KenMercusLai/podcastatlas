# Censorship Industrial Complex

Censorship industrial complex is the episode's term for a network of governments, regulators, NGOs, platforms, advertisers, payment providers, and other intermediaries that can suppress speech indirectly. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-under-secretary-of-state-sarah-b-rogers-on-dismantling-the-censorship-industrial-complex-39828360]], [[SarahBRogers|Sarah B. Rogers]] argues that this network tries to obtain moderation outcomes that would be constitutionally harder for government to demand directly.

The source's strongest examples are disinformation NGOs, the [[CenterForCounteringDigitalHate]], and the [[EuropeanUnionDigitalServicesAct|EU Digital Services Act]]'s [[TrustedFlaggerSystem|trusted flagger]] model. The episode also connects the concept to debanking, demonetization, advertiser pressure, and platform labels when those tools move beyond counterspeech into service denial.

## Key Claims
- Disinformation definitions can become expansive enough to include true but politically inconvenient claims.
- NGOs can be legitimate critics when they publish evidence, but become more coercive when they get privileged regulator or platform access.
- Governments may use informal channels or intermediaries to create pressure while avoiding direct speech prohibitions.
- Platform labels are not automatically transparent; their legitimacy depends on who applies them, what evidence they expose, and whether users can inspect the underlying claim.
- The concept overlaps with [[IndirectRegulatoryCoercion]] when government leverage is the source of pressure and with [[IntermediarySpeechPressure]] when private service providers carry it out.
