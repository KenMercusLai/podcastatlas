# Multi-Manager Investment System / 多基金经理制

## Definition
A [[MultiManagerInvestmentSystem|multi-manager investment system / 多基金经理制]] divides a portfolio into independently managed sleeves, combines differentiated return streams, shares research infrastructure, and holds each manager accountable without forcing a single committee consensus.

## Current Synthesis
[[lossad1trayjdp11pbjiywrxy43o|The episode]] presents the system as a third route between one star manager and committee compromise. Independent sleeves preserve strong but conflicting judgments; portfolio-level diversification reduces dependence on any one manager remaining right; shared research and contribution attribution make distributed skill more reusable. The label alone is insufficient: a nominal group with one real decision-maker and several assistants does not create independent return streams, while a genuinely distributed structure can still deteriorate into duplication, internal competition, high turnover, and disputes over credit unless information sharing and incentives are designed together.

## Key Claims
- Independent sleeves preserve disagreement that committees may average into mediocre consensus.
- Combining less-correlated manager return streams can improve risk-adjusted results and reduce severe bad-year outcomes.
- The system converts some individual skill into organizational capability, reducing star-manager succession and departure risk.
- Decision attribution is necessary because good outcomes can follow bad decisions and bad outcomes can follow disciplined processes.
- Shared research works only when contributors receive credit and recipients can trust that recommendations reflect durable judgment rather than rapid trading.
- Capacity benefits are secondary to the deeper goal of keeping a product's behavior from depending on one person's permanent correctness.

## Evidence
### Independent judgment and diversification
- [[lossad1trayjdp11pbjiywrxy43o]] describes separate manager sleeves and the aggregation of relatively differentiated return streams.

### Attribution and incentives
- [[lossad1trayjdp11pbjiywrxy43o]] reports substantial evaluation infrastructure and examples of rewarding research that creates value for other managers.

### Organizational resilience
- [[lossad1trayjdp11pbjiywrxy43o]] links the system's origin to the failures of committee compromise and single-manager product drift.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Diversification among managers can vanish when their research inputs, benchmarks, incentives, or crowded positions converge.
- Multiple sleeves can introduce duplicated holdings, internal conflict, slow coordination, and contested contribution measurement.
- A long evaluation period can protect thoughtful contrarians or merely delay recognition of persistent underperformance.
- The source does not provide audited sleeve-level attribution, comparative performance, fees, turnover, or implementation details sufficient to establish causal superiority.

## What Changed
- Distinguished independent sleeve management from nominal co-management.
- Framed the system as organizational risk diversification rather than only a capacity-expansion technique.
- Added attribution, research-credit, and turnover incentives as required supporting mechanisms.
