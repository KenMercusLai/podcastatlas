# 桌下炸弹悬念 / Bomb-Under-Table Suspense

Bomb-under-table suspense / 桌下炸弹悬念 is the Hitchcockian information-design rule explained in [[27-xiqukeke-yizai-nin-zhuoxia-anzhi-zhadan-582370829]]. A sudden explosion produces shock, but an audience that knows a bomb is present while characters continue ordinary conversation experiences sustained anxiety.

The source emphasizes variants rather than only the simplest version. Suspense changes depending on whether one character knows, whether another character behaves strangely, whether the knower refuses to speak, and whether the audience has more information than the people on screen. This makes the device a close neighbor of [[NarrativeMisdirection]]: both techniques manage what the audience knows, when they know it, and which interpretation feels urgent.

## Key Claims
- Suspense is often stronger before danger resolves than at the instant of impact.
- The device depends on timing and information distribution, not only on the size of the threat.
- Audience knowledge can become an emotional burden when characters are ignorant, distracted, or unable to act.
- The same principle can work without a literal bomb whenever a story lets the audience wait with known danger.
