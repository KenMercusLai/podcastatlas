# Undefined War Objectives

## Definition
Undefined war objectives are the strategic failure that arises when a government initiates or continues military action without stable goals, success criteria, public rationale, or an exit strategy tied to political outcomes.

## Current Synthesis
Both sources use Trump's Iran war as the case but illuminate different parts of the failure. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-josh-shapiro-on-trump-iran-war-chaos-israels-failure-the-economy-and-2028-race-40774740]] calls it a war of choice and argues that the administration never defined whether the goal was nuclear delay, regime change, deterrence, support for Israel, or something else.

[[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-graham-allison-on-the-global-realignment-iran-china-israel-greenland-40373425]] shows how leaders can partly fill that vacuum after the fact. [[GrahamAllison]] says Trump could declare that Iranian leadership, military capacity, nuclear capability, and power projection had been destroyed or degraded, but this narrative flexibility does not answer who governs afterward, whether Israel keeps fighting, or how second-order costs are contained. A list of damaged targets can therefore supply a victory claim without supplying a political objective.

## Key Claims
- A war of choice requires especially clear public objectives.
- Undefined goals make exit strategy and success measurement impossible.
- Inconsistent administration explanations can signal that policy is being improvised after escalation.
- A flexible victory narrative can facilitate exit while concealing that no stable political end state was defined.
- Military action should be judged by American security and economic interests, not by another country's preferences.
- Strategic ambiguity magnifies material and second-order costs because leaders cannot say which risks are necessary to the mission.

## Evidence
- War-of-choice critique: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-josh-shapiro-on-trump-iran-war-chaos-israels-failure-the-economy-and-2028-race-40774740]] records Shapiro calling the Iran war a war of choice and saying Trump never defined the objectives.
- Inconsistent rationale: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-josh-shapiro-on-trump-iran-war-chaos-israels-failure-the-economy-and-2028-race-40774740]] records Shapiro citing shifting explanations around Netanyahu, nuclear capability, and regime change.
- American-interest standard: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-josh-shapiro-on-trump-iran-war-chaos-israels-failure-the-economy-and-2028-race-40774740]] has Shapiro argue America should not be led around by any other nation and should act according to U.S. national-security interests.
- Post hoc victory criteria: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-graham-allison-on-the-global-realignment-iran-china-israel-greenland-40373425]] says Trump retained room to declare victory through claims of destroyed or degraded Iranian capabilities.
- Exit conflict and aftermath: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-graham-allison-on-the-global-realignment-iran-china-israel-greenland-40373425]] distinguishes Trump's possible desire to stop from Netanyahu's possible preference to continue until regime destruction and lists civil war, fragmentation, and regional spillovers as unresolved outcomes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source provides the administration's full legal or strategic case. Shapiro offers an opposition politician's critique, while Allison interprets shifting messages during an unfolding war. Leaders sometimes preserve deliberate ambiguity for bargaining or coalition management, and a limited degradation mission need not specify a complete replacement regime. The sources nevertheless leave unresolved how military outputs connect to an attainable political end state.

## What Changed
- Added the distinction between target-destruction metrics and a political end state.
- Added post hoc victory narration as both a possible exit device and a sign of unresolved objectives.
