# Viral Food Ingredient Demand / 社交媒体爆款食材需求

## Definition
Viral food ingredient demand is the rapid expansion of an ingredient from an established use into many product categories after visually legible social-media content, branded launches, novelty, and existing consumer associations reinforce one another.

## Current Synthesis
The pistachio case shows virality amplifying rather than inventing demand. The nut already had snack, Mediterranean-diet, plant-protein, and lower-guilt positioning. Dubai chocolate supplied a compact, highly visual format in which green pistachio filling was easy to recognize, taste on camera, imitate, and extend.

Once the format attracted attention, chocolate makers and foodservice brands could translate it into confectionery, drinks, ice cream, desserts, and spreads. This creates a feedback loop between content and procurement: launches generate more visibility, while concentrated ingredient buying can tighten availability and raise costs for unrelated buyers. The effect is strongest when the ingredient is visually distinctive, familiar enough to try, novel in the new format, and hard to replace without changing the promise.

## Key Claims
- Viral food demand often builds on an existing taste and health narrative rather than starting from zero.
- Visual distinctiveness makes an ingredient easier to recognize across short-form content and derivative products.
- Cross-category launches convert a single viral format into broader procurement pressure.
- Social-media attention and brand imitation can reinforce each other faster than agricultural supply can respond.
- The viral item need not become a durable brand for the underlying ingredient market to feel the shock.
- Substitution is easiest in background formulations and hardest when the ingredient names and visually defines the product.

## Evidence
- Pre-existing demand base: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] describes pistachio as an established snack and health-positioned plant-protein product before Dubai chocolate.
- Social acceleration: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] attributes rapid Dubai-chocolate attention to a late-2023 TikTok tasting video and reports more than 30,000 orders for FIX.
- Category diffusion: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] reports growth in pistachio-related launches and use across drinks, ice cream, desserts, chocolate, and paste.
- Procurement effect: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] says large confectionery purchases reduced supply available to other buyers and contributed to price pressure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not isolate social media from climate losses, inventory, retailer pricing, longer-running health demand, or general product innovation. Launch growth does not prove repeat purchase, and a viral tasting video does not establish the durability of the originating brand. The reported order count and category-growth figures remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Established a demand framework in which social virality multiplies an existing ingredient narrative across categories.
- Added procurement crowd-out as the bridge between online attention and upstream commodity pressure.
