## Definition
史书体例意识 is the reader's awareness that historical texts organize evidence differently: by chronology, dynasty, annals, biography, tables, treatises, or ruler-instruction sequence, and that those forms change what the reader notices.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 591-2 makes form awareness a practical entry skill for reading Chinese history. The episode does not ask listeners to master every textual tradition first; instead, it builds a simple map: Shiji models wide biographical-historical synthesis, Han Shu and Hou Hanshu continue dynastic official history, Sanguozhi is concise and structurally incomplete compared with fuller histories, Zuo Zhuan is a narrative-rich chronological classic, and Zizhi Tongjian selects long chronological material for "资治" use. Knowing the form helps readers understand why one text preserves drama, another institutional coverage, and another compact governance lessons.

## Key Claims
- Historical form shapes interpretation because chronology, biography, dynasty, and treatise structures foreground different kinds of causality.
- A reader can use form awareness without becoming trapped in formalism; the point is to know what a source tends to include or omit.
- Zizhi Tongjian becomes clearer when read as a chronological statecraft compilation rather than as a neutral pile of events.
- Sanguozhi's concise structure is itself a reading clue because missing tables and treatises limit what kinds of institutional questions it directly answers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Form awareness should not become a shortcut that replaces source reading. The episode itself argues against "dead reading": readers still have to enter the people, events, and interpretive conflicts inside the text.
