# Religious Practice as Spiritual Technology

## Definition
Religious practice as spiritual technology is [[DavidDeSteno]]'s framework for studying prayer, meditation, ritual, gratitude, mourning, community worship, mortality contemplation, and surrender as inherited methods that may shape minds, bodies, relationships, and conduct independently of whether science can settle their metaphysical claims.

## Current Synthesis
The framework begins with a jurisdictional boundary. God's existence is not an experimentally manipulable question, whereas respiration during prayer, behavior after gratitude, connection during coordinated ritual, grief after mourning customs, and long-run health associations can be studied. This permits serious empirical investigation without treating measurable benefit as proof of theology or lack of proof as disproof of God.

Practice is the active unit. The episode says nominal God-belief without participation does not show the same health pattern and notes that some people participate deeply in religious traditions without literal belief. Meaning, bodily form, repetition, community, and expectations can combine rather than reduce to one mechanism. The “technology” metaphor remains morally neutral: the same capacity to coordinate hearts and minds can support compassion, recovery, and belonging or punishment, aggression, charisma, and exploitation.

## Key Claims
- Science can investigate consequences and mechanisms of religious practice without claiming authority over God's existence.
- Repeated participation is a more plausible active ingredient than religious identity or verbal belief alone.
- Religious practices can combine semantic meaning, breathing, movement, attention, memory, emotion, community, and moral expectations.
- Private practices and collective practices may work through partly different pathways.
- Health and flourishing associations do not by themselves establish causal effects or doctrinal truth.
- Spiritual technologies are directionally neutral and can be used for prosocial coordination or manipulation and harm.

## Evidence
- Testability boundary: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] separates experiments on consequences from metaphysical proof or disproof.
- Practice-belief distinction: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] says belief without practice does not show the same health benefits and notes practice without literal belief in several traditions.
- Mechanism range: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] discusses prayer breathing, meditation, synchrony, gratitude, grief ritual, community, surrender, and mortality contemplation.
- Misuse boundary: [[science-health-benefits-of-belief-in-god-religion-dr-david-desteno-scim1533978257]] connects threat and charismatic authority to punitive religious interpretation, aggression, exploitation, and cultic harm.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The technology metaphor can understate theology, history, obligation, identity, and sacred meaning for practitioners. It can also encourage selective extraction without cultural competence. Reported longitudinal associations remain vulnerable to selection and confounding, while experimental findings about components do not establish that whole traditions or every practitioner will benefit. Psychological usefulness neither proves a supernatural claim nor makes a harmful institution benign.

## What Changed
- Created a framework separating metaphysical truth claims from empirically testable practices and outcomes.
