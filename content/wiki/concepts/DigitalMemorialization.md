## Definition
Digital memorialization is the preservation or representation of people, memories, testimony, traditions, and family knowledge through digital systems after death.

## Current Synthesis
Digital memorialization ranges from static files and testimony archives to interactive systems that answer questions or speak in a person-like style. The move from preservation to generation changes the ethical object: the system no longer only keeps a trace but can invent continuity, infer missing details, and become socially present to survivors. The legitimacy of that move depends on consent, purpose, audience, privacy, access, maintenance, and whether the representation can be mistaken for the deceased person's own judgment.

The strongest constructive case is broader than acute grief. Deliberately preserved stories, traditions, recipes, and testimony can support family and public memory across generations. Yet the same interaction can be emotionally compelling despite factual errors, which means memorial value cannot be evaluated only through technical realism. Provenance, uncertainty, style, deletion, family disagreement, and safeguards against monetized dependence remain part of the memorial design itself.

## Key Claims
- Digital preservation becomes interactive memorialization when a system generates answers or a person-like presence from stored traces.
- Intergenerational stories, traditions, recipes, and survivor testimony can be legitimate memorial purposes when consent and context are clear.
- Not every trace should be preserved indefinitely, made searchable, or converted into a speaking representation.
- Generated memorials can create comfort while also inventing details, shifting a family narrative, or implying authority the deceased never gave.
- Paid services can distribute memory unequally and create incentives to maximize engagement with grief.
- Public-interest institutions, family governance, provenance, uncertainty signals, and deletion options can help bound those risks.

- Intergenerational value: [[tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128]] identifies consented family memory and historically meaningful survivor testimony as promising uses, while [[tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds family traditions, storytelling, recipes, and inherited knowledge.
- Interactive-presence boundary: [[suanli-kuangxiangqu-wo-zai-ai-gongchang-de-qiyu-lorijulltfhttspka22jnn4qjf-i]] depicts a generated grandfather producing real emotion, and [[tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128]] distinguishes static preservation from post-mortem avatars.
- Invented continuity: [[tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261012-1012-mp-tech-pod-128]] shows that sparse profiles force generative ghosts to fill gaps and that participants may accept inaccuracies to sustain conversation.
- Governance and access: [[tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260318-0318-mp-tech-pod-128]] raises preservation burdens, unequal paid access, account deletion, family disagreement, and the need for public institutions or NGOs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish that interactive memorials improve grief outcomes or accurately preserve a person's beliefs. One source is satirical, one is an ethics interview, and the participant study is brief and supervised. Family-story retrieval may be less identity-intensive than first-person reincarnation, but it can still reproduce inaccuracies or contested family memories. Static archives, deletion, and noninteractive remembrance remain valid alternatives.

## What Changed
- Expanded the constructive use case from preservation and testimony to traditions, recipes, storytelling, and family knowledge.
- Added evidence that users may tolerate invented detail, making uncertainty and provenance central memorial-design issues.
- Distinguished researcher-mediated interaction from unsupervised commercial memorial products.
- Clarified that familiar style can create memorial presence even when biographical accuracy is incomplete.
