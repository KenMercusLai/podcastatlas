# Festival Gift-Custom Evolution / 节庆赠礼习俗流变

## Definition

Festival gift-custom evolution is the process by which offerings, protective objects, cash, food, and imagined gift-bringers acquire new recipients and meanings as ritual, political hierarchy, monetary systems, family life, moral education, literature, and commerce change.

## Current Synthesis

[[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq|怪东西 episode 11]] compares two non-equivalent histories through a shared mechanism. In the Chinese branch, protective 厌胜钱 and New Year warding are proposed as one uncertain ancestry of 压岁钱; later monetization makes ordinary currency a practical family gift, and modern debate turns children's cash into a question of consumption, parental control, and financial learning. In the Christmas branch, gifts move among sacred narrative, hierarchical offering, child-centered domestic practice, regional gift-bringers, Dickensian charity, and commercial exchange.

Continuity therefore lies less in an unchanged object than in recurring social work: gifts mark protection, blessing, rank, relationship, generosity, obligation, and festive joy. New media and economic forms can materially change a custom while retaining or reinterpreting some of those functions.

## Key Claims

- A shared festive purpose does not prove uninterrupted descent of a specific object, name, or practice.
- Gift recipients can shift from gods or rulers toward kin, dependents, children, employees, and people in need.
- Monetization changes both the form and scale of giving, turning symbolic money-like objects into spendable cash and financial-socialization opportunities.
- Literature, film, and popular imagery can stabilize or redirect expectations about generosity, food, family, and gift-bringers.
- Charity and commerce can expand together rather than operating as simple opposites.
- A changing custom can retain genuine attachment and social meaning without being timeless or uniform.

## Evidence

- Protective object to cash - [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq]] distinguishes non-circulating 厌胜钱 from later spendable New Year money and treats the connection as qualified rather than proven.
- Money and education - [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq]] follows modern concern about children's cash into proposals for parental guidance, saving, spending, and record-keeping.
- Hierarchy to domesticity - [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq]] contrasts earlier gifts to rulers with later gifts among family members and especially to children.
- Narrative and social ethics - [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq]] uses [[AChristmasCarol|*A Christmas Carol*]] to connect Christmas giving with compassion, employer responsibility, charity, and moral conversion.
- Hybrid gift-bringer - [[11-yasuiqian-yu-shengdan-liwu-de-qiyuan-luf0cjj9qquztmuhmzrghb-9dneq]] joins [[SantaClaus|Santa Claus]] to Saint Nicholas, Dutch-American literature, Father Christmas, and a nineteenth-century domestic setting.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The single source is a conversational comparative history rather than a complete numismatic, linguistic, religious, economic, or folklore study. The proposed 厌胜钱 etymology, diffusion dates, Reformation chronology, literary causation, and Santa genealogy remain source-scoped. Similar functions across Spring Festival and Christmas do not establish shared origin or institutional equivalence, and regional, class, denominational, and family variation remains substantial.

## What Changed

- Established a comparative mechanism for tracking changes in gift object, recipient, meaning, and economic setting.
- Preserved the difference between functional continuity and a proven line of descent.
