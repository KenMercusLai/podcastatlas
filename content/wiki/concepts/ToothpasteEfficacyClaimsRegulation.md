# Toothpaste Efficacy-Claims Regulation

## Definition
Toothpaste efficacy-claims regulation is the boundary that permits supported product-benefit language while barring labels from expressly or implicitly presenting toothpaste as medical treatment.

## Current Synthesis
The episode frames the proposed Chinese classification requirements as a compliance and evidence filter in a mature market. Stronger limits may make wording, substantiation, R&D, and label review more important, but they do not automatically prove that market concentration will rise.

## Key Claims
- Implied medical meaning can matter as much as explicit treatment language.
- Mature categories can compete through claims even when basic product penetration is saturated.
- Compliance capability and evidence investment may become a competitive resource.
- Smaller firms can face disproportionate adjustment cost without concentration being a necessary outcome.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source does not reproduce the rule text, classifications, enforcement standard, transition provisions, or permitted efficacy vocabulary.
- The forecast of greater industry concentration is an inference rather than a demonstrated regulatory outcome.

## What Changed
