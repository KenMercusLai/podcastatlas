# Promotion Position Fit / 升职位置匹配

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-917-ruhe-xunsu-zhaodao-shengzhi-jiaxin-de-pojudian-lk2zt5c3onvui9a4ppbowshy66tg|Hanji 917]] opens with promotion position fit: the problem that hard work may not lead to promotion if the role is not on a visible advancement path. The episode's career frame gives three tests: read the real superior's demand, find a more valuable position, and move with the larger organizational trend.

The concept is adjacent to [[PromotionExpectationManagement]], but it asks an earlier question. Promotion expectation management makes a case legible once there is a plausible case; promotion position fit asks whether the work itself is strategically valuable enough, visible enough, and sponsor-relevant enough to become a case at all.

The historical analogy remains source-scoped. The episode uses late-Han court placement and demand-reading as a workplace lesson, but the concept should not collapse imperial office politics into a direct modern HR rule.

## Key Claims
- Advancement may depend on role placement and organizational demand as much as individual effort.
- A promotion case is stronger when the employee's work solves the actual decision-maker's problem rather than only completing assigned tasks.
- Valuable positions usually sit where the organization is investing attention, resources, and future authority.
- Position fit does not replace [[UpwardManagement|upward management]]; it gives upward management a more promising surface to work on.
- The source's history-to-workplace lesson is interpretive and should be used as a heuristic, not as a literal institutional equivalence.
