# Urban-Rural Transition Generation / 城乡过渡一代

## Definition

The Urban-Rural Transition Generation describes people whose education, work, care, and aging span village and city systems without producing secure belonging or support in either one.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents two related positions: younger educated migrants may reach large cities yet remain blocked by credentials, housing costs, unstable work, and long commutes; older people may spend working years in factories or construction and return to farming with weak pensions and declining physical capacity. The frame treats urbanization as a prolonged transition with uneven costs rather than an instantaneous move from rural to urban life.

## Key Claims

- Education can enable migration without guaranteeing stable urban employment or belonging.
- Housing and commuting costs can turn nominal opportunity into repeated displacement and exhaustion.
- Older returnees may lose access to city labor before receiving adequate rural income or care support.
- Village children and elders bear transition costs when working-age adults migrate.
- Divergent age groups share incomplete institutional absorption but should not be treated as one uniform experience.

## Evidence

- Younger migrants: [[98b809db-0025-4671-9ca4-195f6e0c722f-98b809db-0025-4671-9ca4-195f6e0c722f]] follows credential screening, three moves in six months, long commutes, unemployment, and departure from Beijing.
- Older returnees: [[98b809db-0025-4671-9ca4-195f6e0c722f-98b809db-0025-4671-9ca4-195f6e0c722f]] describes people who moved through farming, industrialization, migrant labor, and late-life return while losing access to strenuous work.
- Care consequences: [[98b809db-0025-4671-9ca4-195f6e0c722f-98b809db-0025-4671-9ca4-195f6e0c722f]] links working-age absence to villages dominated by elders and children.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is a synthesis of two creators' experiences and field observations, not a demographic category validated across regions. Urban migrants, returnees, farmers, and families vary by hukou, assets, gender, locality, industry, and public services. The source does not establish prevalence or causal weight.

## What Changed

- Established a generational frame for incomplete belonging and support across rural and urban systems.
