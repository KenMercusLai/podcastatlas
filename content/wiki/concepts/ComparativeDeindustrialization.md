## Definition
Comparative deindustrialization interprets the loss of industrial employment and capacity across regions while comparing what institutions, services, populations, and cultural forms persist after decline.

## Current Synthesis
The episode places Northeast China beside the US Rust Belt, older European industrial regions, the former Soviet sphere, and Hokkaido. The comparison shifts attention from an allegedly unique regional failure toward a global transition in which finance and technology can prosper while industrial cities lose work and population.

Difference matters as much as similarity. The source argues that many Northeast cities retained enough supermarkets, health care, security, housing, and public infrastructure to remain habitable even after severe layoffs and out-migration. Cultural relocalization also followed economic change: urban modern culture became less visible while ruralized symbols such as er ren zhuan were increasingly made to stand for the whole region.

## Key Claims
- Industrial decline should be compared across regions rather than explained only as local cultural failure.
- Growth in finance and technology can coexist with concentrated losses in older industrial places.
- Employment loss, population movement, public-service continuity, and housing affordability are distinct dimensions.
- Retained urban services can create adaptation paths even where property demand and industrial work collapse.
- Deindustrialization can reshape how outsiders and residents symbolize a region's culture.

## Evidence
- Global comparison: [[cong-dongbei-dao-dongya-lishi-shi-yige-guocheng-ren-yeshi-lgolueqq4aiwj4bg5jk-im4qhzc5]] compares Northeast China with US, European, Soviet, and Japanese industrial regions.
- Institutional persistence: [[cong-dongbei-dao-dongya-lishi-shi-yige-guocheng-ren-yeshi-lgolueqq4aiwj4bg5jk-im4qhzc5]] contrasts Northeast urban continuity with more deeply abandoned districts in Detroit and Baltimore.
- Cultural change: [[cong-dongbei-dao-dongya-lishi-shi-yige-guocheng-ren-yeshi-lgolueqq4aiwj4bg5jk-im4qhzc5]] links the post-1990s industrial downturn to a stronger ruralized representation of Northeast culture.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode offers an interpretive comparison rather than standardized city-level measures. “Public services remained” can conceal differences in quality, accessibility, fiscal sustainability, and neighborhood coverage, while low housing prices may signal opportunity and continuing contraction at the same time.

## What Changed
- Created a comparative frame that preserves both shared industrial decline and Northeast institutional differences.
