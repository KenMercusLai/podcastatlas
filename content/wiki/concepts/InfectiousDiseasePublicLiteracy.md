# Infectious Disease Public Literacy

Infectious disease public literacy is the ordinary-reader knowledge frame added by [[yi-fen-laizi-bingchuang-shang-de-xiangguan-shudan-598284093]]. The episode uses [[ZhangWenhongShuoChuanran|《张文宏说传染》]] as the simplest entry: people need usable concepts for pathogens, routes of transmission, daily prevention, symptoms, medical care, and the difference between personal experience and general disease rules.

The concept sits between individual self-care and public systems. A person can hydrate, rest, eat, monitor symptoms, and avoid casual medication, but the larger problem still depends on professional medicine, trustable information, surveillance, water, sanitation, and social cooperation.

## Key Claims
- Basic infectious-disease literacy should be low-threshold enough for non-specialists.
- Personal symptom stories can help people feel less alone, but they should not be treated as universal disease courses.
- Practical literacy includes prevention, when to seek care, what not to overclaim, and why expert advice may change early in a new disease.
- Public literacy is weaker when health information is either over-mystified or flattened into search-result fragments.
