## Definition
Evidence-bound historical revision revisits an inherited account by testing the timing, provenance, material traces, motives, and alternatives behind it, while distinguishing a plausible reconstruction from a settled event.

## Current Synthesis
An archive shaped by political victors or literary memory deserves scrutiny, not automatic reversal. The bounded sources move from a detective novel's critique of Richard III's reputation and public resistance to Cao Cao tomb evidence, through Song founding and succession narratives, to a late-Chu genealogical reconstruction. They also define a negative boundary: denying an interlocking network of records and material evidence simply because it resists a preferred theory is pseudohistory. The method grades source-dependent hypotheses and preserves contradictions rather than manufacturing certainty.

## Key Claims
- Historical revision begins with source timing, provenance, motive, material evidence, and competing accounts rather than the desire to overturn a familiar verdict.
- A political winner's influence on the record warrants criticism but does not establish the opposite story; suspicious patterns still need graded confidence.
- Gaps in a compressed chronicle can motivate a reconstruction without turning the reconstruction into the only permissible reading.
- Genealogical variants and succession traditions may change the contradiction map, but a later account must retain earlier incompatible claims explicitly.
- Public acceptance of a revision is a separate question from the strength of its evidence, especially where fiction has shaped collective memory.
- Wholesale denial of mutually constraining documents, archaeology, and cross-cultural traces abandons the evidentiary burden rather than strengthening revision.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
《时间的女儿》 is a literary argument, not independent proof that Richard III was innocent. The Song upper and lower episodes are one series, as are Qinji 118-1 and 118-2; their cumulative detail is not four independent confirmations. The latter Qinji account disputes a previously transmitted childless-Mi-Wan premise and varies the earlier Xiang Yan sequence; neither tradition should be silently harmonized. Changping Jun's identity and Zhao Kuangyin's death remain source-scoped reconstructions. Criticism of official history is not proof that every unofficial version is true.
