# DEA Exemption Process

The DEA exemption process is the formal route discussed in [[a-trip-to-the-magic-mushroom-megachurch]] for a church seeking permission to use controlled substances as religious sacrament. The source says the [[DrugEnforcementAdministration|DEA]] process exists, but can be slow and may require applicants to pause illegal substance use during review.

[[JohnRapp]] frames the process as a legal catch-22. A church needs an exemption for fuller protection under the [[ControlledSubstancesAct]], but a denied application may leave regulators with detailed evidence about practices the church itself described.

## Key Claims
- Exemption is different from decriminalization, tolerance, or a church's own assertion of religious freedom.
- The process can reduce risk, but it can also create exposure if applicants document conduct that remains illegal.
- Applicants may need to show security, handling protocols, theology, rituals, leadership, and anti-diversion safeguards.
- Churches that avoid applying may still face raids, seizures, or later litigation.
