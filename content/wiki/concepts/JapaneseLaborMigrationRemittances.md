# Japanese Labor Migration Remittances / 日本劳工移民汇款

Japanese labor migration remittances are the episode 168 frame for reading overseas Japanese workers through wages, family support, state oversight, and financial infrastructure. [[168-yin-yu-xin-yibu-riben-laogong-yimin-de-kuayang-huikuan-shi-lmgfmmgkawoa-6xyucnm1ljebzwu]] starts from Japanese workers in [[Hawaii]] and the U.S. West Coast, then follows the money back to Japanese households, village officials, ministries, consulates, and [[YokohamaSpecieBank|Yokohama Specie Bank]].

The concept matters because it shifts migration history from identity alone into a material operating system. Workers left for higher wages, families waited for support, officials tried to preserve national reputation, banks turned small transfers into foreign-exchange business, and U.S. observers sometimes converted the same family transfers into [[RacializedRemittancePolitics]].

## Key Claims
- Remittances reveal the economic reason labor migration was undertaken and sustained.
- Migrant money connected overseas work to household survival, public buildings, status, and return-home imagination.
- Japan's system became unusually legible because officials and banks standardized petitions, lists, deposits, and transfer records.
- Remittance history has to be read together with labor exploitation, family pressure, national image, and immigration restriction.
