# Taylorist Factory Time / 泰勒制工厂时间

Taylorist factory time is [[ruci-dagong-30-nian-liushuixian-shang-de-miankong-yu-shijian-lodsuktwtcfxnhmzwum5iesbzbme]]'s account of how factory discipline reorganizes the body. [[ZhanTeacher|占老师]] contrasts agricultural time, which followed seasons and weather, with manufacturing time, where machines, workshops, shifts, and cost recovery push production toward continuous operation.

The source makes the discipline concrete: 12-hour shifts, roughly 11 hours of standing work, short scheduled breaks, detailed process decomposition, line managers, and later QR/SAP-style tracking make time and movement into production variables. This deepens [[ManufacturingJobQuality]] by showing how wages and overtime can coexist with fatigue, narrow control, and [[FactoryDisciplineAndSensoryNumbing|sensory numbing]].

## Key Claims
- Factory time is not only a work schedule; it is a way of training bodies to obey production rhythm.
- Taylorist decomposition can raise efficiency and quality while reducing the worker's control over movement and meaning.
- Digital systems intensify time discipline by embedding tracking into tools, process steps, packaging, and customer visibility.
- Flexible manufacturing changes the order pattern but does not automatically release workers from time pressure.
