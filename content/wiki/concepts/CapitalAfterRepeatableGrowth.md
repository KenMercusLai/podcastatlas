# Capital After Repeatable Growth

## Definition
Capital after repeatable growth is the principle that founders should identify a predictable use for outside money before accepting return-seeking investment.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded evidence frames outside investment as neither inherently good nor bad. It becomes more defensible when a company knows which product, channel, or operating system can convert additional money into repeatable growth, or when capital responsibly reduces founder risk that is impairing judgment. The anniversary retrospective adds a second gate: even repeatable growth can be the wrong target if the investor's required scale and timing do not match the company and life the founder wants.

## Key Claims
- Accepting the first outside dollar changes the company's stakeholder obligations and expected outcomes.
- Capital should scale an understood engine more often than substitute for discovering one.
- Investor fit matters when margin pressure could weaken a mission or material standard.
- Bootstrapping protects control but may slow category capture.
- Reducing excessive founder household risk can be a legitimate use of financing.

## Evidence
- Governance threshold - [[advice-line-with-scott-tannen-of-boll-branch-and-jamie-siminoff-of-ring-2025-e64c6623-10d1-4222-85d6-22eeab736221]] records [[JamieSiminoff]]'s warning that investors may expect multiples of their money back.
- Repeatability gate - [[advice-line-with-scott-tannen-of-boll-branch-and-jamie-siminoff-of-ring-2025-e64c6623-10d1-4222-85d6-22eeab736221]] has [[GuyRaz]] advise [[QForQuinn]] to find a predictable growth channel before raising.
- Personal-risk qualification - [[advice-line-with-scott-tannen-of-boll-branch-and-jamie-siminoff-of-ring-2025-e64c6623-10d1-4222-85d6-22eeab736221]] says investment helped [[ScottTannen]] reduce personal debt pressure at [[BollAndBranch|Boll & Branch]].
- Ambition and investor fit - [[10-years-of-how-i-built-this-a-decade-of-innovation-risk-and-reinvention-fdd4f8d2-9920-427e-ae2a-ae2d44768a31]] contrasts Serena & Lily's changing investor mandates with Title Nine's bootstrapped preference for steady profitability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Waiting can forfeit share in a fast-moving category, and not every growth engine can be proven cheaply. Capital-intensive businesses may need funding before repeatability is fully visible. The sources do not model financing terms, competitor behavior, or the relative cost of debt and equity.

## What Changed
- Established a sequencing rule that joins repeatable growth evidence, founder control, investor fit, and personal-risk limits.
- Added the requirement that investor return expectations match the founder's desired company and life.
