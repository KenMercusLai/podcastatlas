# Seafood Cold-Chain Packaging

Seafood cold-chain packaging is the fish-shipping constraint introduced in [[tech-20251224-1224-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251224-1224-mp-tech-pod-128]]. The episode says polystyrene containers are used to keep fish chilled during transport, which makes packaging part of the product's logistics infrastructure rather than a disposable afterthought.

The source uses [[WildSource]] and [[ChrisSanito]] at [[KodiakHarbour]] to show why replacement materials have to be judged economically. A mycelium cooler may reduce plastic-waste risk, but it still has to compete on weight, insulation, durability, and cost because shipping-box changes can affect seafood margins.

## Key Claims
- Seafood shipping packaging must preserve temperature, protect the product, and stay light enough for transport economics.
- Polystyrene's environmental downside is hard to address because its operational advantages are real.
- [[BiodegradableFoamPackaging]] can matter most in cold-chain settings when it reduces waste without weakening temperature control or durability.
- The episode leaves open whether [[MyceliumInsulation]] can scale beyond early testing in Alaska's fishing industry.
