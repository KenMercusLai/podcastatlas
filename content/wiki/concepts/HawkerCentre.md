# Hawker Centre / 食阁

A hawker centre, called 食阁 or 熟食中心 in [[vol-270-da-shi-dai-liu-zai-le-ta-de-shi-dai-1006063835]], is the Singapore-origin shared foodservice infrastructure behind the episode's food-court history. The source says Singapore moved from many street hawkers in the 1960s toward registered, licensed, centralized spaces that provided stalls, water, sanitation, toilets, and shared seating.

The episode treats the early hawker centre as urban governance and daily-life infrastructure before it became a mall business model. Its commercial importance is that shared facilities let small vendors avoid the full cost of standalone stores, keep prices lower, and focus on food production while the operator or state handles the space around them.

## Key Claims
- Shared seats, utilities, sanitation, and licences can turn fragmented street vending into a more governable food system.
- The model lowers both vendor capital needs and consumer search costs.
- Public food infrastructure can later be commercialized by private mall operators.
- The food-court business model inherits both sides of the origin: small-vendor affordability and centralized control.
