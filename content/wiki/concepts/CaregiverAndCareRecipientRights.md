# Caregiver and Care Recipient Rights / 照顧雙方權利

## Definition
Caregiver and care recipient rights / 照顧雙方權利 is the principle that long-term care must preserve the cared-for person's agency, role, and dignity while also protecting the caregiver's rest, health, work, safety, and right to shared public responsibility.

## Current Synthesis
The source describes good care through a double prohibition against deprivation. A person with dementia should not lose every choice merely because assistance is needed; communication and support should preserve participation in ordinary life wherever capacity remains. At the same time, family duty should not consume one caregiver's sleep, livelihood, health, relationships, and independent future.

The current judgment is that these rights are interdependent. A rested, supported caregiver is better able to preserve the other person's agency, while care organized around participation rather than total substitution can reduce unnecessary dependency and resistance. Neither side's humanity should be made conditional on unlimited family sacrifice.

## Key Claims
- Dependence on care does not erase the recipient's remaining preferences, abilities, biography, or social role.
- Support should enable shared participation in daily tasks when possible instead of automatically replacing the person.
- Family caregivers retain rights to sleep, health, work, relationships, safety, and an independent life.
- Asking the state and professionals to share care is an exercise of rights, not abandonment of duty.
- Protecting one side by erasing the other produces an unstable and ethically incomplete care arrangement.
- Life-extension decisions in irreversible decline require dignity, quality-of-life, capacity, and preference questions rather than filial duration alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page is grounded in one journalism episode and does not resolve legal decision authority, clinical capacity, emergency safety, guardianship, or end-of-life standards. Participation must fit actual ability and risk; caregiver boundaries do not license neglect, and recipient autonomy does not require one unsupported relative to provide unlimited care. Medical and legal decisions need qualified, jurisdiction-specific guidance.

## What Changed
- Established a two-sided rights frame for long-term care.
- Connected supported participation with caregiver sustainability.
- Preserved life-extension and filial-duty questions as unresolved rather than prescribing an answer.
