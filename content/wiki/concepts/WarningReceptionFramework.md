# Warning Reception Framework

## Definition
Warning reception is the process by which message clarity, messenger credibility, threat proximity, audience identity, institutional incentives, and feasible response options shape whether an evidence-based warning receives attention and changes action.

## Current Synthesis
[[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ce411815c1cca7882f5b93d662620544-51879597-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-ce411815c1cca7882f5b93d662620544-51879597]] contrasts [[CassandraMythology|Cassandra]] with [[AndrewNatsios]]. Cassandra is accurate but cryptic, institutionally weak, temporally distant, and threatening to her listeners' identities. Natsios combines projections and satellite evidence with plain language, insider trust, shared values, and politically intelligible actions. The synthesis is not that persuasion should replace evidence; it is that evidence reaches decisions through social and institutional channels.

The permafrost and Challenger cases extend the framework. Gradual, remote, hard-to-see dangers struggle for attention, while a near-term technical warning can still lose when schedules, authority, and organizational momentum favor proceeding.

## Key Claims
- Forecast accuracy and warning effectiveness are different achievements.
- Clear causal explanation is especially important when the messenger sees several steps beyond the audience.
- In-group trust and recognized authority affect access and attention but do not determine truth.
- Distant, gradual, or invisible threats are harder to prioritize than imminent, visible harms.
- Identity-threatening information can be rejected because accepting it would destabilize status, strategy, or self-conception.
- Warnings travel further when they offer feasible action within institutional constraints, though compatibility can also blunt necessary change.
- Decision systems need protected escalation and stop authority so reception does not depend only on a messenger's social fit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- A credible insider can be wrong, while an obscure outsider can be right; reception cues are not truth tests.
- Tailoring a warning to existing values may improve attention while understating structural change or excluding affected outsiders.
- Leaders face real attention, resource, and uncertainty constraints, so failure to act does not by itself prove irrational denial.
- The mythological, humanitarian, climate, and aerospace cases are compressed comparisons, not controlled evidence that isolates each mechanism.

## What Changed
- Established a unified framework separating forecast correctness from the social and institutional conditions of action.
