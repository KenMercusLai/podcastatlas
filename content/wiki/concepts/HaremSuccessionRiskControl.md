## Definition

Harem succession risk control / 后宫继承风险控制 is the attempt to manage ruler desire, consort hierarchy, and childbearing as public political risks before they produce jealousy, rival heirs, or factional disorder.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-504-nanren-haose-zenmeban-kexi-guren-zhezhao-ni-xuebuhui-lsevoh__1vxdtzqhlzq6_an3xdq6|Hanji 504]] creates the concept through [[DuQinWesternHan|杜钦]]'s advice to [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]]. Du Qin does not treat [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s desire as merely private morality. He argues that if the palace has no limiting rule, the ruler can keep adding women, the empress and consorts can become suspicious or ambitious, and childlessness can turn sexual favor into succession instability.

The Xiongnu branch in the same source supplies a comparative counterpart. [[HuhanyeChanyuWesternHan|呼韩邪单于]] faces a consort-and-son problem of his own: the favored younger son and the older politically credible candidate imply different risks. His settlement names [[FuzhuleiRuodiChanyu|雕陶莫皋]] successor while requiring later transfer to 且莫车, showing another way consort rank, age, affection, and stability have to be made into a visible order.

## Key Claims

- Ruler sexuality becomes a state problem when childbearing, palace hierarchy, and heir recognition depend on it.
- A harem rule can be proposed as preventive governance, not only as moral restraint.
- Early timing matters: regulation is easier before favorites, jealousies, and succession expectations become entrenched.
- Consort hierarchy can generate rival stability arguments rather than one obvious heir answer.
- A planned transfer arrangement can try to balance age, maternal status, and political acceptance, but its durability remains source-scoped.

## Evidence

- Preventive timing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-504-nanren-haose-zenmeban-kexi-guren-zhezhao-ni-xuebuhui-lsevoh__1vxdtzqhlzq6_an3xdq6|Hanji 504]] says Du Qin urges action while Chengdi is young, childless, and not yet publicly criticized for closeness to consorts.
- Rule as limit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-504-nanren-haose-zenmeban-kexi-guren-zhezhao-ni-xuebuhui-lsevoh__1vxdtzqhlzq6_an3xdq6|Hanji 504]] presents the "nine wives" rule as a way to provide heirs without indefinite harem expansion.
- Succession danger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-504-nanren-haose-zenmeban-kexi-guren-zhezhao-ni-xuebuhui-lsevoh__1vxdtzqhlzq6_an3xdq6|Hanji 504]] says Du Qin worries about empress suspicion, consort ambition, and stories where favor disrupts heir order.
- Xiongnu comparative case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-504-nanren-haose-zenmeban-kexi-guren-zhezhao-ni-xuebuhui-lsevoh__1vxdtzqhlzq6_an3xdq6|Hanji 504]] says Huhanye chooses Diaotao Mogao and arranges future transfer to Qiemo Che to stabilize the Xiongnu succession.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not prove the nine-wives proposal was institutionally feasible in Han law; the dowager rejects it precisely because Han precedent is lacking. The source also does not show whether Huhanye's planned transfer succeeded in the long run. This concept should not absorb every harem, marriage, or favorite case: it names cases where consort access is explicitly tied to heir production and political stability.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 504's paired Chengdi and Huhanye cases.
