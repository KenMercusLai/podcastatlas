# Public Moral Performance / 公共道德表演

[[PublicMoralPerformance|Public moral performance / 公共道德表演]] is the pattern [[12-man-dang-milan-kundela-yi-ju-zhengjing-meiyou-549335675]] extracts from [[SlowKundera|《慢》 / Slowness]]. In the episode's account, televised charity, camera presence, moral rivalry, and "dancer" behavior turn goodness into a visible stage where reputation can matter as much as help.

The concept does not say that public causes are false. Vincent's objection inside the episode matters: if suffering needs public attention, then rejecting performance altogether may become another kind of escape. The Kundera problem is sharper: moral visibility can attract aid, but it can also reward vanity, ignorance, and competitive posturing.

## Key Claims
- Public morality becomes unstable when compassion, media incentives, and personal reputation become inseparable.
- Accusing others of performance can itself become a performance of cleverness or superiority.
- The strongest test is whether the performer still knows the concrete person, place, and harm they claim to defend.
