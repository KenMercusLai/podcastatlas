## Definition
AI data center site selection is the choice of where to place AI compute facilities based on energy access, cooling conditions, regulatory certainty, political durability, latency, local consent, and cost.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents site selection as a strategic layer of the AI boom rather than a simple search for cold weather. Patagonia and Finland are attractive because cool climates and energy availability can reduce operating pressure, but the viable site also needs policy stability, tax and regulatory terms, energy-efficiency rules, and enough local permission to avoid backlash. The U.S. segment adds the counterpoint: domestic data centers may be close to users and model customers, but they face hyperlocal fights over electricity capacity and whether companies pay for what they consume.

## Key Claims
- Cold climate can make a region attractive for AI data centers, but it is not sufficient by itself.
- Energy access, energy price, and grid capacity are central to AI infrastructure siting.
- Regulatory certainty and tax incentives can compete with physical climate advantages.
- Political risk matters when incentives depend on future governments or elections.
- Local opposition and cost-allocation disputes can slow or redirect data-center buildout.
- Latency and workload requirements may limit how much overseas capacity can substitute for U.S. data centers.
