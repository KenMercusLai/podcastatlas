# Fitness Assessment Testing Week / 年度体能测试周

## Definition
A fitness assessment testing week is the episode's annual protocol for running the full battery of assessments for the [[NinePhysiologicalAdaptations]], spread across about three days and ordered so that non-fatiguing tests come first and maximal tests come last.

## Current Synthesis
Galpin frames the testing week as the exercise equivalent of annual blood work: run it roughly once a year, at the start of the year or whenever training changes, and start with 48 hours of rest and no hard exercise the day before because acute exercise changes inflammation and water storage.

Ordering carries the design. Body composition and [[FatFreeMassIndex]] go first because they are non-fatiguing, movement and skill work needs freshness, and skill, maximum strength, and power belong at the beginning of a day while fatiguing tests belong at the end. Power, strength, and muscular endurance can share one day, anaerobic and long-duration work can be paired or separated, and maximal aerobic testing generally needs its own day. The point of the week is prioritization rather than a complete scorecard, since the goal is to clear severe [[FitnessPerformanceAnchors|performance anchors]] and then optimize the one or two capacities that matter to the person.

## Key Claims
- The full battery is meant to be repeated roughly once a year rather than monitored continuously in every capacity.
- Non-fatiguing measurements such as body composition come first, and maximal or fatiguing tests come last.
- Rest and recent training load have to be controlled because acute exercise affects inflammation and fluid retention.
- Skill, strength, and power need freshness, so they are scheduled early in a day, while anaerobic work and maximal aerobic tests need separation.
- Standardized warm-ups and retest conditions are what make comparisons across years meaningful.

## Evidence
- Annual framing: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] compares the testing week to going to a physician for annual blood work and places it at the start of the year or when training changes.
- Order and rest: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] recommends 48 hours of rest, no hard exercise the day before, non-fatiguing tests first, skill, maximum strength, and power at the beginning of a day, and fatiguing tests last.
- Three-day split: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] treats three days as probably best and two days as technically possible, pairing power, strength, and muscular endurance while giving maximal aerobic capacity its own day.
- Retest conditions: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] says to standardize the warm-up if the plan is to retest and compare results, and not to test to maximum without comfort in the technique.
- Prioritization: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] says the aim is to eliminate severe performance anchors rather than be optimal in all nine areas.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The testing week is a public-education protocol, not a validated measurement standard, and the episode itself predicts it will feel like a substantial amount of work rather than a week off training. Conditions such as cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled blood pressure, joint or back pain, pregnancy, frailty, injury history, or unfamiliar maximal movements should change which tests are appropriate, and several tests are explicitly described as scaling imperfectly with body size or modality.
