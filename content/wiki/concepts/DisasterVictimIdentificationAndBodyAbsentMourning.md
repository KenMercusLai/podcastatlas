# Disaster Victim Identification and Body-Absent Mourning

## Definition
Disaster victim identification and body-absent mourning joins the forensic work of recording and matching remains with the social and religious work families perform when a body is missing, unidentified, or temporarily buried.

## Current Synthesis
The Nepal flood episode shows that body recovery is both infrastructure and grief care. Long-distance river transport, decomposition, limited mortuary space, and epidemic concerns led authorities to document and temporarily bury unidentified remains while retaining samples for later DNA matching. Families could not wait indefinitely for forensic certainty, so some used kush-grass figures in Hindu funeral rites, creating a symbolic body through which mourning could proceed.

## Key Claims
- Mass-fatality response requires traceable documentation and sample preservation before temporary burial.
- Identification delay can become a second harm because families remain between hope, legal uncertainty, and ritual obligation.
- Symbolic funerals are adaptive social practices, not evidence that identification and return of remains no longer matter.
- Mortuary capacity, public health, forensic systems, and culturally competent family communication belong to disaster preparedness.

## Evidence
- Temporary burial protocol: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] says authorities photographed bodies and preserved teeth, hair, and other material before temporary burial for later DNA matching.
- Geographic dispersal: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] says remains were found far downstream, including hundreds near Chitwan.
- Body-absent ritual: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] describes Govinda's family making a kush-grass figure so funeral rites could proceed without his recovered body.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide a complete identification protocol, legal framework, identification rate, or long-term account of whether families recovered remains. Its claims about epidemic risk and national first-use of temporary burial remain source-scoped.
