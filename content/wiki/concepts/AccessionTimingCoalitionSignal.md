## Definition

称帝时机与拥戴信号 / accession timing coalition signal is the founding-stage pattern where a claimant's imperial title becomes acceptable only after military position, follower expectation, title clarity, elite counsel, and symbolic warrant converge.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 676 frames [[LiuXiu|刘秀]]'s accession as a repeated-pressure sequence rather than a single burst of ambition. After [[FengYiEasternHan|冯异]] and [[KouXunEasternHan|寇恂]] break Zhu Wei's forces and Luoyang panics, commanders use the victory report to urge Liu Xiu to take the imperial title. Ma Wu argues that a settled title clarifies who is being fought and what political order the army serves.

Liu Xiu initially refuses. The pressure then shifts from congratulations to coalition-management logic: [[GengChunEasternHan|耿纯]] warns that followers have left homes and kin because they expect to attach themselves to a dragon and phoenix, so repeated delay may make them disperse. Feng Yi adds the strategic diagnosis that the Gengshi regime must fail and that Han restoration now rests on Liu Xiu.

The final signal is symbolic. [[QiangHuaEasternHan|强华]] arrives from Guanzhong with the 赤伏符, and the commanders use it as mandate evidence. The concept therefore treats accession timing as a conversion point: military success becomes founding order only when enough followers, advisers, and signs make refusal more destabilizing than acceptance.

## Key Claims

- Accession pressure can intensify after victories because commanders need a named political object for continued war.
- Title refusal may be prudent early, but repeated refusal can begin to threaten coalition cohesion.
- Followers' private ambition and public mandate language can reinforce each other during a founding transition.
- Strategic diagnosis matters: Feng Yi's claim that Gengshi will fail turns accession from personal promotion into restoration duty.
- Prophetic signs do not work alone; in Hanji 676 the 赤伏符 arrives after military, organizational, and advisory pressure already exists.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not imply that every accession should happen as soon as commanders request it. Hanji 676 still shows Liu Xiu refusing more than once, which suggests caution and timing control before acceptance. The source is a podcast summary with ASR-sensitive title and place forms, so exact wording of Ma Wu's, Geng Chun's, Feng Yi's, and Qiang Hua's interventions remains source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 676 to capture the convergence of military victory, follower cohesion, title clarity, strategic diagnosis, and prophetic warrant in Liu Xiu's accession.
