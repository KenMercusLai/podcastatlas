## Definition
AI dubbing is the use of AI voice, translation, timing, and lip-sync systems to reproduce, alter, or assist dubbed media performance across languages.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki now has two AI-dubbing layers. [[e234-weilai-shipai-dianying-hai-cunzai-ma-yu-daoyan-luchuan-liaoliao-ai-gei-yingshiren-de-kongju-yu-ziyou-b2be7093-3366-4ee2-8a7a-625f06206ae5]] defines the craft boundary through [[HuangYing]]: strong dubbing depends on context, breath, pauses, imperfection, and scene-aware emotional judgment. [[tech-20260911-tech-pod-128-tech-20260911-tech-pod-128]] adds the platform-product layer through [[Amazon]] Prime Video's AI-generated lip syncing, where improved access for international viewers is weighed against labor, disclosure, quality, and cost-cutting pressure.

## Key Claims
- Lip sync and clear pronunciation are necessary but not sufficient for strong dubbing.
- Translation and word choice must fit character, rhythm, mouth shape, and scene context.
- Human voice acting includes subconscious timing, cooperative emotional judgment, stress, hesitation, and useful vocal imperfection.
- Platform AI dubbing can improve access to international media but can also intensify labor and compensation concerns.
- Human-in-the-loop assurances and disclosure matter more as generated dubbing becomes less visibly artificial.
- Cost pressure may push wider adoption even when early output quality remains mixed.
