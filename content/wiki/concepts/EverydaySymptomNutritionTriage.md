# Everyday Symptom Nutrition Triage / 日常困扰营养分诊

## Definition
Everyday symptom nutrition triage is a four-part framework for common health concerns: assess severity and plausible causes, strengthen food and lifestyle foundations, test products against the actual problem, and escalate persistent or serious symptoms to qualified care.

## Current Synthesis
The source applies the same reasoning to hair loss, constipation, acne, insomnia, and weight loss without claiming that they share one cause. Each problem can reflect several interacting factors, so a familiar food, supplement, or consumer product should not become the diagnosis. Nutrition and routine can support prevention or improvement, especially when inadequate intake, added sugar, inactivity, irregular sleep, or unsustainable restriction is relevant, but supportive care is not a substitute for treating disease.

The framework also explains why product debates need context. Black sesame can contribute nutrients without directly turning hair black; probiotics require an appropriate strain, viable product, and a reason to use them; melatonin fits circadian disruption better than every form of arousal-driven insomnia; and a low-calorie meal replacement can create an energy deficit while remaining nutritionally incomplete. The practical judgment is therefore not “natural versus artificial” or “works versus useless,” but problem fit, evidence, dose, duration, side effects, and whether the symptom has crossed a clinical threshold.

## Key Claims
- Similar-looking symptoms can have nutritional, behavioral, hormonal, psychological, medication-related, structural, or disease causes, so mechanism and severity should precede product choice.
- Balanced meals, adequate protein and energy, plant variety, fiber, ordinary movement, hydration, and regular sleep are background supports rather than guaranteed treatments.
- One favorable ingredient or biological mechanism does not prove that a food or supplement will resolve a multifactorial symptom.
- Product usefulness is conditional on the actual problem, formulation, dose, duration, quality, adverse effects, and user context.
- “Detox,” miracle-food, rapid-loss, and universal-supplement narratives often monetize anxiety by compressing a complex problem into one purchase.
- Persistent, severe, widespread, painful, function-limiting, or otherwise concerning symptoms belong in qualified clinical assessment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This framework is derived from one structured podcast summary, not a clinical guideline or systematic review. It does not establish diagnostic criteria, treatment thresholds, probiotic duration, supplement doses, optimal macronutrient ratios, acne diets, insomnia treatment, or safe weight-loss targets. Some mechanistic and product claims in the episode are contested, formulation-specific, or too broad to generalize; individual symptoms may require dermatology, gastroenterology, sleep medicine, mental-health, endocrinology, or other professional assessment.

## What Changed

- Distinguished foundational support from symptom-specific diagnosis and treatment.
- Made problem-product fit and anxiety-driven marketing explicit parts of everyday nutrition judgment.
