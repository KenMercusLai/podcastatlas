## Definition

Correctable leadership error / 犯错能改的领导力 is the principle that leadership quality after a mistake depends on recognizing the fault, changing course, and repairing consequences rather than preserving an image of infallibility.

## Current Synthesis

The concept begins with [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]]'s distinction between understandable error and excuse-making. After [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] regrets ignoring advice about [[YanState|燕国]], [[ChenJiaWarringStates|陈甲]] invokes [[ZhouGong|周公]] to show that even exemplary figures can err. Mencius accepts the premise but rejects its use as cover: the decisive question is whether the known fault produces concrete repair.

[[XiangYu|项羽]] supplies a negative endpoint. At [[DongchengChuHan|东城]] and [[WujiangChuHan|乌江]], he can still prove tactical excellence, but “Heaven will destroy me” protects dignity while leaving the political, strategic, alliance, and morale failures undiagnosed. Real strength becomes a [[TacticalSelfVindicationTrap|战术自证陷阱]] when it is used to prove personal worth instead of revise the error model.

The Liu Ying road dispute adds an institutional face-saving case. [[LiuYing|刘盈]] is initially willing to remove a road after [[ShusunTong|叔孙通]] identifies a ritual violation, but Shusun Tong reportedly says a ruler should not visibly acknowledge error and proposes a new ancestral temple instead. [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s criticism sharpens the concept: a remedy can still be weakened when the court encodes the premise that the ruler must always appear right. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-198-jiu-qu-waishengnv-zhe-wei-huangdi-shenshang-shier-bu-jiandan-lkgewcb8avfq_qy0jpqkdfm5t6wi|Hanji 198]]

The combined judgment separates correction from self-abasement. Public humiliation is not required, and an adviser may need to preserve a workable acceptance path. But the leader or institution must still name the relevant fault accurately enough to reverse it, learn from it, and avoid converting reputation management into a second policy cost.

## Key Claims

- Even highly capable or exemplary leaders can make serious errors; infallibility is not a credible governance standard.
- Regret, embarrassment, and private shame do not substitute for visible reversal or practical repair.
- Rationalization preserves self-image by moving causation outside the leader, leaving the failed decision model unchanged.
- Tactical or technical success can obstruct learning when it is used to vindicate the person instead of diagnose the system.
- Face-saving can help advice land, but it becomes anti-correction when the court must deny that any fault occurred.
- Correction requires enough truthful diagnosis to change future action, even when the public form remains tactful.

## Evidence

Error followed by excuse rather than repair:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8|Zhouji 49]] says Qi Xuan Wang is ashamed after Yan rebels but does not adopt Mencius's concrete program of release, consultation, repair, and withdrawal.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8|Zhouji 49]] uses Mencius's Zhou Gong exchange to distinguish correcting a mistake from inventing reasons that preserve the ruler's self-image.

Self-vindication after strategic failure:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-168-bawangbieji-qian-fashengle-shenme-2-liqgict85wh-bf4fn2bytygmk-ca|Hanji 168 part 2]] shows Xiang Yu proving battlefield ability at Dongcheng while attributing total defeat to Heaven rather than revising his political and strategic judgment.

Institutionalized rulerly infallibility:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-198-jiu-qu-waishengnv-zhe-wei-huangdi-shenshang-shier-bu-jiandan-lkgewcb8avfq_qy0jpqkdfm5t6wi|Hanji 198]] says Shusun Tong redirects Liu Ying from demolishing a mistaken road to building another temple because visible admission of error is treated as impermissible.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-198-jiu-qu-waishengnv-zhe-wei-huangdi-shenshang-shier-bu-jiandan-lkgewcb8avfq_qy0jpqkdfm5t6wi|Hanji 198]] presents Sima Guang's contrary judgment that sage worth lies in correcting known faults rather than concealing them.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Not every indirect or face-saving correction is dishonest. Advice delivered upward may need tact, and Hanji 198's substitute temple still addresses the ritual route problem. The narrower warning is about the premise of infallibility: if an organization cannot accurately name why a decision was wrong, it may repair the immediate symptom while preserving the decision habit that produced it. The sources do not establish exact quotations, private mental states, or the full cost of the temple alternative.

## What Changed

- Added Sima Guang's explicit critique of rulerly infallibility as an institutional version of the concept.
- Distinguished tactful acceptance paths from denial that a fault occurred.
