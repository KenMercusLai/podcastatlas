# Correctable Leadership Error / 犯错能改的领导力

Correctable leadership error / 犯错能改的领导力 is the leadership lesson [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8]] draws from [[ChenJiaWarringStates|陈甲]]'s exchange with [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]]. After [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] regrets not following Mencius's advice on [[YanState|燕国]], Chen Jia asks whether even [[ZhouGong|周公]] made mistakes. Mencius answers that understandable errors can happen, but the political test is whether the actor corrects them or hides behind excuses.

The concept is narrower than general accountability. It focuses on the moment after a leader already knows a decision failed. Regret, embarrassment, and private shame do not repair policy; correction requires visible reversal, remedial action, and a willingness to abandon the story that the leader was right all along.

This links ancient court politics to modern management in the episode. The host says many leaders avoid admitting mistakes because it costs face, seems unnecessary, or weakens an image of omnipotence. The lesson is not performative self-blame; it is operational repair before the loss of trust becomes harder to reverse.

## Key Claims
- Even excellent leaders can make understandable mistakes.
- The decisive distinction is between correcting an error and rationalizing it.
- Private remorse does not substitute for policy repair.
- Face-saving can turn a correctable mistake into a trust failure.
- A leader who must always appear right loses access to the practical benefits of advice, review, and correction.
