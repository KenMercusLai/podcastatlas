# Strange Tale Social Critique

Strange tale social critique is the reading frame generated by [[28-liaozhai-zhiyi-buwei-junwang-chang-zange-pianxiang-cangsheng-shuo-guihua-587843930]] for [[LiaozhaiZhiyi|《聊斋志异》]]. The source argues that [[PuSongling|蒲松龄]]'s ghosts, foxes, corpses, and marvels often make worldly violence more visible: war leaves corpse piles, officials fail to protect victims, courts can be useless, and exam success can expose examiner blindness rather than literary merit.

The concept is not the claim that every tale is coded politics. It is narrower: supernatural narrative can make social critique survivable and vivid when direct institutional accusation would be dangerous, dull, or too narrow. In this source, the title's "不为君王唱赞歌" becomes an interpretive guide because the collection's moral energy turns toward common suffering, ordinary friendship, retribution, mercy, and corrupt or blind institutions.

## Key Claims
- Strange tales can expose human harm by making ghosts and monsters less frightening than war, lawsuits, exam gates, or social cruelty.
- Indirect critique does not require one hidden allegorical key; pressure can sit in setting, aftermath, missing justice, and repeated moral contrast.
- The source separates moral didacticism from loyalist praise: a text can teach filial piety, faithfulness, mercy, and retribution without celebrating rulers.
- Official failure is a recurring pressure point: when law or office cannot protect the wronged, foxes, ghosts, dream logic, or private chivalry may become the only available narrative justice.
- Examination satire belongs to the same frame because the institution decides value while being shown as sensory, moral, or intellectual failure.
