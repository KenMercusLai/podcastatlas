# Biodegradable Foam Packaging

Biodegradable foam packaging is the material-substitution problem added by [[tech-20251224-1224-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251224-1224-mp-tech-pod-128]]. The episode uses [[MyceliumInsulation]] as a candidate replacement for polystyrene foam, especially in shipping containers that need insulation, low weight, and durability.

The concept is not simply "green packaging." The episode's practical claim is that foam persists because it performs well for fragile or temperature-sensitive logistics, so a biodegradable replacement must meet the same operational constraints while lowering plastic-waste harm.

## Key Claims
- Foam packaging is hard to replace because lightness, insulation, protection, and price all matter at once.
- A biodegradable substitute has stronger environmental appeal if it can avoid persistent plastic waste after disposal or ocean exposure.
- Biological binding through mycelium is one route, but the source does not settle cost, manufacturing scale, certification, or long-term durability.
- In seafood shipping, the packaging material is part of the cold-chain system rather than a cosmetic wrapper.
