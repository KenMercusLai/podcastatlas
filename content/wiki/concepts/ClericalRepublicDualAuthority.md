# Clerical-Republican Dual Authority

## Definition

Clerical-republican dual authority is a state design in which elected and administrative institutions operate beneath religious offices that define constitutional boundaries, claim superior legitimacy, and can restrict ordinary political competition.

## Current Synthesis

[[637-revolution-in-iran-rise-of-the-ayatollah-part-2-glt7243579163]] supplies the founding sequence. [[RuhollahKhomeini]]'s doctrine of *velayat-e faqih* made clerical guardianship the basis of the new order even as [[MehdiBazargan]] promised a constituent assembly, constitution, elections, and republican government. The episode therefore treats elections and parliament as real institutional forms whose permissible outcomes were constrained by an authority able to identify opposition with opposition to Islam.

[[ep251-yilang-kunju-sishinian-zhanlue-xuanze-de-daijia-yu-xianshi-lp8vyonyugtd2ozymgof-mim9zkd]] extends the design into the contemporary state. It credits Khomeini with joining clerical authority to technical bureaucracy and argues that later practical decision-making depended on interaction among religious leadership, technocrats, and the [[IslamicRevolutionaryGuardCorps]]. Dual authority is therefore not simply two parallel governments: it is an unequal institutional arrangement whose administrative capacity, elections, coercive organizations, and religious legitimacy interact.

## Key Claims

- Republican offices can remain consequential without possessing final constitutional authority.
- Clerical guardianship converts theological interpretation into a boundary on political competition.
- Technical administration can coexist with revolutionary religious supremacy rather than displacing it.
- Institutional duality can provide continuity while also narrowing reform and accountability.
- The balance among clerics, elected officials, technocrats, and coercive institutions can change without abolishing the underlying hierarchy.

## Evidence

### Founding doctrine and institutional promises

- [[637-revolution-in-iran-rise-of-the-ayatollah-part-2-glt7243579163]] connects *velayat-e faqih*, Khomeini's Revolutionary Council, Bazargan's appointment, democratic promises, Sharia, and limits on opposition.

### Administrative and coercive continuity

- [[ep251-yilang-kunju-sishinian-zhanlue-xuanze-de-daijia-yu-xianshi-lp8vyonyugtd2ozymgof-mim9zkd]] connects Khomeini's founding design to later interaction among clerical leadership, technocratic officials, and IRGC command continuity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The two podcast sources offer a high-level institutional interpretation rather than a complete constitutional, legal, electoral, or administrative history. The concept does not imply that elections, factions, parliament, presidents, bureaucracy, street politics, or public opinion are meaningless. Nor does it prove that Khomeini alone designed every institution or that the balance among clerical, military, and administrative actors has remained fixed. Claims about authorship, hierarchy in particular decisions, and long-run causal effects remain source-scoped.
