# Qin Eastward Pressure / 秦国东进压力

Qin eastward pressure / 秦国东进压力 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]] uses to explain why [[WeiState|魏国]] and [[HanState|韩国]] meet at Zaiyang in 366 BCE despite recent Three Jin conflict. Qin defeats their joint force at Luoyang Jinjiao, then [[QinXianGong|秦献公]] wins the [[ShimenBattle|Battle of Shimen]] in 364 BCE.

The concept marks a change in pressure direction. Earlier Zhouji episodes show the Three Jin states fighting each other, dividing older orders, and exploiting succession crises. This source adds a western pressure that can force those rivals into tactical coordination while still exposing their military vulnerability.

The 363 BCE Shaoliang notice keeps the pressure continuous. Qin attacks [[WeiState|魏国]] again, [[ZhaoState|赵国]] sends aid, and the conflict appears to continue beyond the episode's endpoint.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-2-llzzalowv5vmbvgd6l-jujltkbyf]] extends the Shaoliang branch into 362 BCE. [[WeiState|魏国]] first defeats [[HanState|韩国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] at Hui, then Qin defeats Wei at [[Shaoliang|少梁]] and captures [[GongshuWeiChancellor|公孙措 / 公叔痤]]. The sequence sharpens the concept: Qin pressure is real, but it does not automatically make the Three Jin states strategically coherent.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] extends the pressure into 358 BCE. Qin defeats [[HanState|韩国]] at West Mountain, and [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] responds by sending [[LongJia|龙甲]] to build western defenses, making [[WeiGreatWallAntiQinDefense|魏长城防秦]] a material expression of the same pattern.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-15-jiu-bu-chun-yinfa-de-shijie-dazhan-li-iojxthq8komhlhyumho3yvtem]] extends the pressure into the 355-354 BCE Qin-Wei sequence. [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] and [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] meet at Duping, but the episode infers failure from the following year's war: Qin defeats Wei at Yuanli and occupies [[Shaoliang|少梁]]. The source also ties the fighting to [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]], making pressure both territorial and incentive-driven.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] extends the pressure into 352-351 BCE. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] attacks [[WeiState|魏国]], pressures [[Anyi|安邑]] and [[Guyang|固阳]], and makes Wei repair other fronts by returning [[Handan|邯郸]] to [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and joining the [[ZhangshuiAlliance|漳水之盟]]. The source also treats Wei-Qi peace-seeking around [[XianglingWarringStates|襄陵]] as part of the same Qin-driven rebalancing.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] adds the recognition consequence. After years of visible Qin pressure and reform, [[ZhouRoyalHouse|周王室]] confers hegemon status on [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] through [[ZhouHegemonRecognition|王致伯于秦]], while [[GongziShaoguan|公子少官]] brings Qin troops to [[Fengze|逢泽]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] makes the strategic rationale explicit in 340 BCE. [[ShangYang|公孙鞅 / 商鞅]] tells Qin Xiao Gong that Qin and Wei are mutually threatening neighbors and that Wei's recent defeats create a chance to occupy western advantage, force Wei eastward, and prepare broader control of the lords. The campaign then turns pressure into commander capture through [[PeaceParleyEntrapment|和谈诱捕]] against [[GongziAng|公子卬 / 公子昂]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]] gives the territorial result. After Qin breaks Wei, [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] cedes Hexi and moves from [[Anyi|安邑]] to [[Daliang|大梁]], making the pressure no longer only a border threat but a capital-geography change for Wei.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-27-weiguan-weihuiwang-he-qiweiwang-shi-za-shangye-hupeng-lminlxhrm5bethub9kyy-8blimrb]] extends the same pressure onto [[HanState|韩国]] in 335 BCE. [[QinState|秦国]] takes [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]], which the episode describes as a long-targeted strategic place comparable to Wei's Hexi. The notice shows Qin pressure continuing while the eastern states are also managing the post-[[MalingBattle|马陵之战]] diplomatic order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-57-gudai-fa-dushi-haiyou-zhe-zuoyong-lj6wi61osaytowgmdp8dtkrwb-si]] adds a later Yiyang branch and a source-layer tension. In 308 BCE, [[QinWuwang|秦武王]] sends [[GanMao|甘茂]] to secure [[WeiState|魏国]] support for attacking [[HanState|韩国]], but Gan Mao warns that [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]] is a large, distant target and that court accusations will erode the king's trust if the siege runs long. The resulting [[OathBoundCommandTrust|Xirang oath]] turns Qin eastward pressure into a problem of sustaining ruler commitment as well as winning territory; it should be read alongside, not over, the 335 BCE Yiyang notice until the wiki has a clearer reconciliation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-58-zhaowulingwang-ruhe-dazao-qiangguo-lq9mgpib0whh0w-jkv0kxcafbnnz]] completes that later Yiyang branch. Qin Wuwang initially wavers under pressure from [[Chulizi|樗里疾 / 樗里子]] and the Gongsun faction, but Gan Mao's appeal to the Xirang oath produces reinforcements and the capture of Yiyang. The episode then shows Qin's momentum interrupted by Qin Wuwang's sudden death, shifting the pressure pattern into succession under [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-59-yichao-junwang-yichaochen-ljq-qkueix0rnzfekzuadicce1bh]] adds the administrative and political aftermath of that capture. Qin Zhaoxiang Wang sends [[XiangShou|向寿]] to regularize Yiyang, accepts [[GanMao|甘茂]]'s advice to return [[WusuiWarringStates|武遂]] to [[HanState|韩国]], and then faces both Gan Mao's flight and renewed [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] by [[ChuState|楚国]], [[QiState|齐国]], and Han. Qin pressure therefore remains strong enough to hold and administer Yiyang, but the succession transition creates friction inside Qin and openings outside Qin.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-60-zhongguo-di-yi-wei-bachi-chaozheng-de-taihou-jingshi-ta-ltazis9qo0dwvwmzxikk8mmpvosc]] adds a pressure-and-reversal case in 303-302 BCE. Under [[XuanTaihou|芈八子 / 宣太后]]'s Qin-Chu alignment, Qin attacks [[WeiState|魏国]] and takes [[PubanWarringStates|蒲阪]], [[YangjinWarringStates|阳晋]], and [[FenglingWarringStates|封陵]], then attacks [[HanState|韩国]] and retakes [[WusuiWarringStates|武遂]]. After [[MiHeng|芈横]] kills a Qin official and the Qin-Chu tie breaks, Qin repairs relations with Han and Wei at [[LinjinWarringStates|临晋]] and returns Puban to Wei. Qin eastward pressure is therefore strong but not linear: land seizure can be followed quickly by diplomatic return when the target of pressure changes.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-67-zhanguo-mieba-baiqi-de-junshi-caineng-jiujing-ruhe-ljw0lltowxd4qz75v-wcnlkwqskv]] pushes the pattern into 294-290 BCE. Qin defeats Wei at Xiecheng, [[BaiQi|白起]] breaks the Han-Wei coalition at [[YiqueBattle|伊阙之战]], Qin receives or takes Han and Wei land, and [[MangMao|芒卯]] appears in a Wei rescue story precisely because Qin's pressure makes even dubious persuasion useful.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-68-jingtan-songkangwang-huangyinwudu-de-gouxue-lishi-1-lss3e9g45jyndnvmsexwibwce5oo]] adds the next pressure-and-status turn. After Wei and Han cede land, Qin sends [[BaiQi|白起]] and [[SimaCuo|司马错]] against [[WeiState|魏国]] again and takes dozens of towns, proving that concession does not stop the pressure cycle. The same episode then has [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] test western/eastern emperor titles with [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]], so eastward pressure now includes a status experiment as well as continued attack on Wei and [[Anyi|安邑]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-73-lishishang-ziyou-lianai-de-kaichuangzhe-shi-shui-lkumfsfialqgdl6qpb3c-hnz7h9u]] adds a 284 BCE pressure case after the anti-Qi coalition. Qin receives wealth when [[LeYi|乐毅]] sends distant allies home, but the episode says Qin is not satisfied with treasure. Qin attacks [[WeiState|魏国]], takes [[AnchengWarringStates|安城]], and reaches toward [[Daliang|大梁]], making eastward pressure reappear as a follow-on to [[PostCoalitionSpoilsImbalance|post-coalition spoils imbalance]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0]] adds the Zhao-front continuation. After [[ZhaoHe|赵惠文王赵何]] refuses Qin's proposal to attack [[QiState|齐国]], [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] attacks Zhao, takes cities, attacks again in 281 BCE, and captures [[ShichengWarringStates|石城]]. The source then previews [[Mianchi|渑池]], showing eastward pressure moving from battlefield punishment into a risky diplomatic meeting.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-77-lianpo-yu-linxiangru-shishang-zui-zhuming-de-yidui-hao-jiyou-lmelnhhtfuzezvmmsuw4yf10plsb]] completes that meeting and adds another Zhao-front battle notice. In 280 BCE, [[BaiQi|白起]] defeats Zhao and kills twenty thousand troops; in the following Mianchi scene, Qin's pressure does not turn into Zhao humiliation because [[LianPo|廉颇]] prepares against hostage leverage and [[LinXiangru|蔺相如]] forces [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] into a parity gesture.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-83-zhuhou-bailan-jing-chengle-jingdian-ji-zhong-ji-ljp7nbqnrctr5mw6i9u982abmx-1]] adds a pressure-through-rescue case. [[WeiState|魏国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]] attack [[HanState|韩国]] at [[HuayangBattle|华阳之战]], but Han makes rescue a Qin interest through [[ThreatenedDefectionLeverage|投敌威胁式求援]]. Qin's rapid intervention under [[WeiRan|魏冉]] and [[BaiQi|白起]] punishes both attackers and leaves Wei debating another land cession, extending eastward pressure through both battlefield defeat and [[LandForPeaceExhaustionTrap|抱薪救火式割地求和]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-84-fangzhi-qinguo-miechu-jing-yong-zhe-zhao-llgh12eistejijsl6yildzkixocm]] reframes the same pressure against southern overextension. [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] does not deny Qin's strength; he argues that a direct follow-on campaign against [[ChuState|楚国]] would make Qin depend on [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] as exposed corridor states, while a Qin-Chu repair would let Qin keep pressing Han, Wei, and [[QiState|齐国]] in a safer sequence. Eastward pressure therefore becomes the alternative to an overextended Chu campaign.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-87-xialu-xiangfeng-yongzhe-sheng-zhongyu-zhaodao-chuchu-le-loppr83sdq5hhesmqihckwmhpl2h]] adds a failed pressure case on the Zhao front. Qin attacks [[EyuBattle|阏与 / 燕宇之战]] over an incomplete hostage-and-land settlement, but [[ZhaoShe|赵奢]] turns distance, narrow roads, a Qin spy's report, and the northern mountain into a Zhao victory. The concept therefore now includes a limit case: Qin's pressure can be tactically checked when the defender controls information and timing better.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-88-ni-zenme-kandai-fenghuangnan-2-lpzyziww177yvvjksmuioa-p-1qi]] adds a strategic-clarification case through [[FanJu|范雎]]. Qin pressure is not useful simply because Qin can project force; Fan Ju argues that pressure must be sequenced by distance and retainable land. [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]] therefore narrows the pressure pattern: manage distant states such as [[QiState|齐国]] diplomatically, then attack neighboring states where each gain can become Qin territory.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-89-qinwang-jing-bei-ta-dui-mabaonan-ljugedx8qxhtuzd1ja-xiwxgse19]] shows that clarified pressure continuing. Qin fails in a Zhao-front attack under [[HuShangHuyang|胡伤 / 胡阳]], then sends [[QinWangWan|武大夫绾]] against [[WeiState|魏国]] to take Huai and later takes Xingqiu nearby. The same episode complicates the pressure pattern through [[QinDaotaizi|秦悼太子]]'s death as a hostage in Wei and through [[QinFourNoblesCourtPower|秦国四贵权力结构]], showing that outward pressure depends on both succession calculation and internal authority.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-1-lmwf0q0-3hxb6d4hro8fdmc3zdxp]] extends the pressure into 264-263 BCE against [[HanState|韩国]]. [[BaiQi|白起]] takes cities, seizes [[HanNanyangWarringStates|韩国南阳]], and attacks [[TaihangRoad|太行道]], while [[FanJu|范雎]]'s strategic logic treats Han as a neighboring state whose internal routes can be cut. Qin eastward pressure here is not only border seizure but [[TransportCorridorSeverance|交通线切割]], creating the isolated [[Shangdang|上党]] problem that can pull [[ZhaoState|赵国]] into the next stage.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-1-ljshdryvf9qgabtn-siontthzsok]] follows that pull into the next stage. [[FengTing|冯亭]] offers Shangdang to Zhao not because Qin has stopped being dangerous, but because Qin pressure has made Han unable to hold the territory. The result is pressure by displacement: Zhao must decide whether a seventeen-city windfall is worth inheriting Qin's attack.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-2-lrlulw0b6v0fb2xpr2i5ifem1h4n]] shows why [[ZhaoBao|赵豹 / 平阳君]] thinks the inheritance is too dangerous. The fuller [[Shiji|《史记》]] layer points to Qin's cattle-assisted farming, water transport, and reward incentives, making Qin pressure an institutional-capacity problem rather than only battlefield momentum. Zhao's acceptance of Shangdang therefore pits a windfall decision against Qin's farming, logistics, and [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|military-merit]] mobilization.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-1-lq4wvkd8qx3q7zji6fnnkxi3qcl1]] shows Qin pressure reaching its most dangerous Zhao-facing moment and then pausing. After [[ChangpingBattle|长平之战]], [[BaiQi|白起]] uses terror signaling to weaken Zhao morale while Qin continues pressure through [[WangHe|王龁]] against [[WuanWarringStates|武安]] and [[Pilao|皮牢]] and through [[SimaGeng|司马梗]] against [[TaiyuanWarringStates|太原]] and [[Shangdang|上党]]. Yet pressure converts into settlement rather than conquest after [[SuDai|苏代]] makes [[FanJu|范雎]] fear Bai Qi's post-victory elevation, and Qin accepts [[Yuanyong|垣雍]] plus six Zhao cities.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-103-1-simaqian-kanzhong-de-xinlingjun-suan-shi-xiaoren-ma-lvty1pdnibvqpk0m16kkxzw4t-ck]] carries the pattern into 247 BCE under [[QinZhuangxiangwang|秦庄襄王]]. Qin attacks unsubmitted [[Shangdang|上党]] cities and establishes [[TaiyuanWarringStates|太原]] commandery, while [[MengAo|蒙敖]] takes Wei territory and forces [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]] to recall [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]]. The same source adds a city-and-water-control layer: [[GuanchengWarringStates|管城]], [[Xingze|荥泽]], and [[YingyangWarringStates|荥阳]] make [[Daliang|大梁]] vulnerable even after Xinlingjun's five-state victory checks Qin in the field.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-103-2-simaqiande-idol-weihe-siyu-jiuse-lvmqg1ojmka9pqagfhgvo4albpjx]] adds the non-battle continuation. After Xinlingjun's coalition victory, Qin turns to [[CounterintelligenceRumorWedge|反间流言楔入]] to separate him from Wei Anxi Wang, and the successful removal of Xinlingjun weakens Wei's best anti-Qin asset. The same source also notes that Qin's Taiyuan consolidation is not smooth: after Qin Zhuangxiang Wang dies and [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] succeeds, Jinyang rebels.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-31-hezong-lianheng-weiou-qin-lncpgmxrojc0y1meg9a3ul-o9nih]] shows Qin pressure interrupting diplomacy itself. As [[SuQin|苏秦]] prepares to turn Yan backing into a broader [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] project, Qin attacks [[WeiState|魏国]], defeats more than forty thousand troops, captures [[LongJia|龙甲]], and takes Diaoyin. The episode treats this as the military shock that forces Su Qin to manage the Qin front by sending [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] west.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-34-qinguo-zongli-ruhe-wanzhuan-liuguo-lsenzwp0kr5wscb0btvuzmkibriq]] extends the pressure from battlefield shock into cumulative territorial extraction. Qin takes or receives [[Yinjin|殷晋]], [[Shaoliang|少梁]], [[HexiWarringStates|河西]], [[Jiaoyi|焦邑]], [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]], [[PuyangWarringStates|蒲阳]], and finally [[Shangjun|上郡]]'s fifteen counties, while also using [[GongsunYan|犀首公孙衍]] to disrupt the eastern coalition environment. The pattern now includes force, cession, and [[StrategicConcessionDiplomacy|欲擒故纵式让利外交]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-35-lishishang-diyi-qibing-jiaofu-shi-ta-lnxgnu1czkl-ftfb3gq9kzxwnxdk]] complicates the eastward-pressure pattern by showing the rear frontier that Qin must manage while looking east. [[YiquState|义渠]] is strong enough that Qin cannot ignore it, but the episode still presents Hexi and Wei as Qin's central strategic concern. Qin's partial victory over Yiqu and later return of Jiaoyi and Quwo to Wei show pressure operating alongside [[QinFrontierBufferManagement|frontier buffer management]] and tactical restraint.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-38-gao-jianzhi-de-zhangyi-bu-yiban-lobhcpz679psy54k0iduccqkfcrn]] renews the pressure after [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] enters Wei. When [[WeiState|魏国]] does not accept Zhang Yi's plan to model service to Qin for the other lords, [[QinState|秦国]] attacks and takes [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]] and [[PingzhouWarringStates|平州]]. The episode also adds a strategic-use-of-disorder version: Zhang Yi tells [[QinWuwang|秦武王]] that Qi-Wei conflict can give Qin a window against [[HanState|韩国]] and the Zhou royal domain.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-39-lishishang-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-nudui-qiangdi-de-lv2gc2wtcxuhugfgixk7rbbhpa6x]] adds the personnel-pressure version. [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] keeps [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] bound through [[GraceDebtLoyaltyPressure|恩赏负债式忠诚压力]], and Zhang Yi's Wei-court position then becomes a way to keep Qin's pressure active through factional struggle and secret alignment as well as direct attack.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-46-ma-ni-shi-ge-erbaiwu-de-lishi-laiyuan-lkz5hiamaojtvanllbunecy3g3ff]] adds a 317 BCE pursuit version. After the failed anti-Qin attack at [[HanguPass|函谷关]], Qin follows [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] east under [[Chulizi|樗里子]], wins at [[XiuyuBattle|修鱼之战]], and continues to [[ZhuozeBattle|浊泽]]. This makes eastward pressure visible as deep pursuit into the central plains, not only stepwise border seizure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-48-junwang-weisha-buneng-chuanwei-zaixiang-lnwhljnn888mzlj7let2ptn1pcdm]] adds a 314 BCE pressure cluster. Qin's attack on [[YiquState|义渠]] and capture of twenty-five cities show rear-frontier instability still affecting expansion; its Quwo notice keeps [[QuwoWarringStates|曲沃]] in the pressure vocabulary with a source-scoped Wey wording ambiguity; and its victory over [[HanState|韩国]] at [[AnmenWarringStates|岸门]] forces Han to send crown prince [[HanXiangwang|韩仓 / 韩襄王]] as a hostage. The pattern is therefore not a single eastward front but a rolling mix of rear pacification, border seizure, battlefield victory, and hostage-peace extraction.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-50-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-ba-chuhuaiwang-huyou-que-de-loxg3ylhmskvdm-axtco5y-acege]] broadens the pressure into a coordinated 313 BCE military-diplomatic sequence. [[Chulizi|樗里子]] attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]], while [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] sends [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] to break [[ChuState|楚国]] from [[QiState|齐国]] before moving against Qi. Qin pressure therefore works through both field operations and alliance surgery.

[[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac]] shows the military result of that alliance surgery. Qin joins with Qi to defeat Chu at [[DanyangBattle|丹阳之战]], captures [[Hanzhong|汉中]], then faces Chu Huaiwang's advance through [[Wuguan|武关]] toward [[LantianBattle|蓝田之战]] while [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] pressure Chu from behind. Qin pressure now reaches Chu as territorial seizure, alliance reversal, and multi-front coercion.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-105-zhanzheng-huayang-zhaoguo-limu-zui-hui-shua-ljtqdg46a8o13zzsjv-p-fpol5oh]] adds a famine-year continuation. In 244 BCE, [[QinState|秦国]] is hungry but still sends [[MengAo|蒙敖]] against [[HanState|韩国]], taking twelve cities. The source uses the notice to argue that not every war is a pure drain: against a weak nearby target, campaigning can also seize supplies and extend pressure.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-106-jingren-faxian-xinlingjun-de-feizhengchang-siwang-lsj3kd2tb4tqtuvhwvsdwkh-h5el]] adds the locust-and-epidemic continuation. Qin suffers "蝗、疫" in 243 BCE and mobilizes grain through [[RankSaleDisasterFinance|鬻爵赈灾融资]], yet in 242 BCE still sends [[MengAo|蒙敖]] against [[WeiState|魏国]], takes twenty cities, and creates [[DongCommandery|东郡]]. The pressure pattern now includes administrative conversion after disaster, not only battlefield persistence during famine.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-107-zuihou-yici-hezong-faqin-fa-le-ge-jimo-lrzq1qks7z-oromd-5f-dgqjwjhw]] adds the failed-coalition continuation. In 241 BCE the eastern states make their last anti-Qin [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] attempt, but the source's competing traditions both leave Qin without meaningful loss. Qin then continues territorial pressure around [[DongCommandery|东郡]] and forces [[ChuState|楚国]] to rethink capital geography by moving to [[ShouchunWarringStates|寿春]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-1-lishi-gan-dangmian-dingzhuang-qinshihuang-diyi-ren-lg0dssyicp387foiraohkv1x5aij]] adds the Qinji 108-1 continuation. [[YangDuanhe|杨端和]] continues the pressure against [[WeiState|魏国]], and the source says Qin's boundary now meets [[QiState|齐国]]. This pushes the concept from repeated Wei-front pressure into coalition geography: Qin's eastward line is now read as making the remaining [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] option structurally harder, not merely as another city-taking campaign.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-111-1-ruoda-de-zhaoguo-jing-wang-yu-yige-jinv-zhi-shou-lhrqb8-swlkuyoydgjyn8ldnlkzc]] moves the pressure to Zhao in 236 BCE. Qin does not need to manufacture the whole opening: [[ZhaoState|赵国]] attacks [[YanState|燕国]], Yan warns Qin that Zhao may become too large, and Qin sends [[WangJianQin|王翦]], [[HuanYiQin|桓齮]], and [[YangDuanhe|杨端和]] against Zhao. Eastward pressure now includes opportunistic timing against distracted rivals as well as direct city-taking.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-113-limu-ruhe-yong-yiji-zhili-zu-qinguo-tongyi-luymqpcolsvyz5miodit3wdlx0ob]] marks the pressure pattern turning into unification war. [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]] concede land in 231 BCE, Han is destroyed in 230 BCE, and Qin strikes disaster-weakened [[ZhaoState|赵国]] in 229 BCE. The source also preserves an important limit: pressure alone does not solve [[LiMu|李牧]], so Qin compounds the timing with rumor, bribery, and [[BattlefieldCommanderReplacementRisk|enemy commander replacement]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-2-yanguo-cisha-qinshihuang-weihe-hui-xuan-jingke-lsewjrmgup1yanf8ipn-i0ykqwjx]] shows the pressure pattern changing Yan's internal options after Zhao's fall. With [[WangJianQin|王翦]] positioned forward and Wei too weak to matter, [[JuWu|居武]] can still imagine coalition repair, but [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] behaves as though Qin pressure has already shortened the clock enough to justify [[WeakStateAssassinationStrategy|assassination]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-1-jingke-ciqin-sihu-you-dianer-caoshuai-le-lgz1adsn1t8jjiaobmtfedpr-ixn]] adds the counterfactual limit to that assassination turn. The episode argues that [[JingKe|荆轲]]'s success might have shaken the Qin court, using [[QinWuwang|秦武王]]'s sudden death as analogy, but would probably not have reversed the broader [[QinState|秦国]] trend. Eastward pressure has now become institutional momentum: [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] can target Ying Zheng's body, but not easily undo Qin's accumulated military, administrative, and coalition-breaking advantage.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-116-shishang-zuican-taizi-bei-fuwang-qinshou-zhansha-lthruqxtd4iqofdxzvim58ph8rlk]] adds the post-assassination payoff. Qin's pressure no longer only makes Yan consider assassination; after the attempt fails, [[WangJianQin|王翦]] captures Ji, [[LiXinQin|李信]] pursues [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] toward Liaodong, and Yan's killing of Dan cannot stop the campaign. The source then pivots the same pressure logic south as [[WangBenQin|王贲]] attacks [[ChuState|楚国]] and Ying Zheng prepares a larger Chu operation.

## Key Claims
- Qin's eastern expansion pushes Wei, Han, and Zhao to respond across older rivalries.
- Qin pressure can coexist with Three Jin infighting; shared danger does not guarantee stable alignment.
- A coalition does not prove strategic coherence; Wei and Han can cooperate and still lose.
- Qin's advantage is presented as already visible before later Qin institutional and territorial expansion becomes dominant.
- The pattern connects battlefield pressure to legitimacy because Qin's victory at Shimen receives Zhou ritual recognition.
- Failed diplomacy can be part of the pressure pattern: a meeting may mark attempted management of conflict rather than resolution if war follows immediately.
- Qin pressure can force Wei to trade away recent gains and reduce exposure to other rivals before the western front worsens.
- Qin pressure can also become status: the weakened Zhou court may formally recognize the rising power after its military and administrative strength has become visible.
- The 340 BCE attack shows Qin choosing timing after Wei's eastern defeat and treating western terrain as the bridge from border war to future hegemony.
- The Hexi cession and capital move show Qin pressure reshaping Wei's strategic geography, not just winning one campaign.
- The 335 BCE Yiyang notice shows Qin pressure also cutting into Han while Qi and Wei are rearranging the eastern title hierarchy.
- The Zhouji 31 Wei attack shows Qin pressure as a timing threat to hezong: even a persuasive coalition plan can fail if Qin moves before the alliance exists.
- The Zhouji 34 sequence shows Qin pressure compounding across years: repeated smaller gains and a calculated concession can reshape Wei's territory without one decisive conquest.
- Zhouji 35 shows that eastward pressure depends on rear-frontier stability: Yiqu can be contained or partially absorbed, but not ignored.
- Zhouji 38 shows pressure continuing through Zhang Yi's ambiguous Wei role: Qin can attack Wei directly while also treating Qi-Wei disorder as an opening.
- Zhouji 39 shows pressure working through office and obligation: Qin's influence can persist inside Wei through Zhang Yi's debt to Qin Huiwen Wang.
- Zhouji 46 shows pressure as post-coalition pursuit: once Hangu Pass defense succeeds, Qin can punish Han and Wei beyond the pass.
- Zhouji 48 shows pressure as synchronized with rear-frontier management and hostage diplomacy, not only city-taking along the central-plains front.
- Zhouji 50 shows pressure as theater management: Qin can strike Zhao directly while using Zhang Yi to isolate Chu from Qi before a separate campaign.
- Zhouji 51 shows Qin pressure turning a successful alliance rupture into territorial seizure and multi-front coercion against Chu.
- Zhouji 57 keeps Yiyang central but adds a trust-management layer: Qin pressure against Han can require prior ruler-commander commitment because distance, delay, and factional accusation threaten the campaign.
- Zhouji 58 shows that commitment converting into operational reinforcement, while Qin Wuwang's death adds succession exposure to an otherwise successful pressure campaign.
- Zhouji 59 shows the post-capture layer: administration and strategic concession can follow conquest, but succession disorder can expose ministers and revive anti-Qin coalition behavior.
- Zhouji 60 shows pressure being redirected by court faction and alliance rupture: Qin can attack Han/Wei during the Qin-Chu warm phase, then return Puban to Wei when Qin prepares to turn against Chu.
- Zhouji 67 shows pressure becoming annihilating and extractive: Qin can defeat a Han-Wei coalition, force land transfers, and make Wei value morally ambiguous persuaders as emergency assets.
- Zhouji 68 part 1 shows pressure persisting after concessions and expanding into status politics: Qin tests emperor language while continuing to attack Wei and Anyi.
- Zhouji 73 shows Qin pressure after a coalition victory against someone else: compensation in wealth does not prevent Qin from seeking land and balance against Wei.
- Zhouji 76 shows Qin pressure against Zhao after refusal: Qin turns a failed joint-war request into city-taking attacks and then a meeting invitation at Mianchi.
- Zhouji 77 shows the pressure continuing through Bai Qi's Zhao campaign, but also shows the limit of pressure in a diplomatic room when Zhao prepares succession safeguards and Lin Xiangru prevents one-sided humiliation.
- Zhouji 83 shows that Qin pressure can arrive as rescue: a weak state's appeal can give Qin a reason to strike its rivals and deepen their dependence afterward.
- Zhouji 84 shows Qin pressure as a sequence choice: Huang Xie persuades Qin to avoid overextension against Chu and instead use Qin-Chu repair to continue pressure toward Han, Wei, and Qi.
- Zhouji 87 shows Qin pressure can fail at the tactical level: Zhao She converts apparent delay, spy deception, and a high-ground race into relief of Eyu.
- Zhouji 88 part 2 shows Qin pressure becoming programmatic: force should be aimed at nearby, holdable territory rather than distant campaigns that enrich intermediaries or ministers.
- Zhouji 89 shows the program in motion against Wei while adding the internal constraint: succession hostages and court-power blocs can complicate the use of external force.
- Zhouji 92 shows pressure as internal route severance: Qin can split Han's geography and make Shangdang unstable before the next Qin-Zhao escalation.
- Zhouji 93 shows pressure as crisis transfer: after Shangdang is isolated, Feng Ting's offer to Zhao turns Qin's Han campaign into a Zhao decision trap.
- Zhouji 93 part 2 adds Zhao Bao's institutional diagnosis: Qin pressure is backed by agricultural, transport, and reward systems that make retaliation more credible.
- Zhouji 95 shows pressure as post-victory extraction and pause: Qin can keep attacking after Changping, but intra-Qin ministerial fear turns possible Zhao conquest into land cessions and rest.
- Qinji 103-1 shows pressure becoming commandery consolidation and capital-environment threat: Qin can lose a battle to a coalition and still leave Wei exposed through nearby cities, routes, and water points.
- Qinji 103-2 shows pressure continuing through information warfare and succession instability: Qin can remove a successful enemy commander through slander while its own new commandery seat faces revolt.
- Qinji 104 shows pressure becoming infrastructural: [[HanState|韩国]] tries to drain Qin labor through [[ZhengGuoCanal|郑国渠]], but completion strengthens [[QinState|秦国]]'s Guanzhong base and makes later eastward campaigns easier to sustain.
- Qinji 105 shows pressure continuing despite Qin famine: a limited campaign against weakened Han can be framed as resource-seizing expansion rather than only consumption.
- Qinji 113 shows pressure becoming state destruction, while still relying on [[DisasterExploitingConquestTiming|disaster timing]] and [[CounterintelligenceRumorWedge|counterintelligence rumor]] to remove Zhao's best commander before the conquest can proceed smoothly.
- Qinji 106 shows pressure resuming after locust and epidemic shock: grain mobilization and commandery creation keep Qin expansion moving despite disaster.
- Qinji 107 shows pressure surviving the final hezong attempt: coalition failure is followed by further Qin territorial gains and Chu's defensive capital relocation.
- Qinji 108-1 shows pressure closing coalition geography: Qin's advance through Wei to the Qi border makes hezong less viable as a spatial arrangement, even before later conquest notices.
- Qinji 111-1 shows pressure exploiting weak-state mutual predation: Zhao's attack on Yan gives Qin the timing and diplomatic rationale to strike Zhao.
- Qinji 114-2 shows pressure changing enemy decision horizons: Qin does not need to attack immediately for Yan's court to abandon slow coalition repair and reach for assassination.
- Qinji 115-1 shows pressure becoming hard to reverse through ruler-killing alone: the source treats Qin's conquest capacity as larger than Ying Zheng's personal survival.
- Qinji 116 shows pressure converting failed assassination into capital capture, failed appeasement, and a pivot from Yan toward Chu.
