# Polycentric Urban Structure and Job-Housing Balance / 多中心城市结构与职住平衡

## Definition

Polycentric urban structure and job-housing balance coordinate multiple activity centers, housing, employment, services, and transport so that a city does not force most daily movement through one dominant core or between distant single-use zones.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents center hierarchies as an implementation tool rather than a universally superior shape. Shenzhen's linear geography, Hong Kong's separated clusters, and the concentric forms of Beijing and other cities show that terrain, history, scale, and transport determine what can work. Within those constraints, distributed centers and mixed functions can reduce tidal commuting and make more daily needs reachable near home.

The standard is functional, not geometric. A nominal secondary center may fail if jobs, schools, hospitals, and cultural facilities remain concentrated elsewhere, while dense urban villages can produce practical job-housing proximity without appearing formally planned.

## Key Claims

- Single-function districts create sharp day-night occupancy cycles and concentrated commuting demand.
- Secondary centers can distribute employment, commerce, talent, and public services away from one overloaded core.
- Job-housing balance depends on affordable housing and useful services, not only spatial distance.
- Terrain, water, transport, history, population, and economic organization constrain feasible city forms.
- No linear, clustered, concentric, or grid structure is universally best.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode supplies illustrative comparisons rather than travel data, accessibility measures, land-market analysis, or causal evaluation of specific centers. Polycentric plans can reproduce inequality or create longer cross-subcenter trips if housing, jobs, and high-quality services do not actually co-locate.

## What Changed

- Created a functional framework joining center hierarchy, mixed use, affordability, and commuting rather than ranking city shapes abstractly.
