## Definition

救灾临机裁量 / emergency relief discretion is the use of unauthorized or exceptional official action when delay would deepen immediate mass harm, with legitimacy depending on public purpose, disclosure, proportionality, and willingness to accept review rather than on necessity as a blank cheque.

## Current Synthesis

The current case is [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]]'s reported journey through Henan after he is dispatched to inspect a fire elsewhere. Finding people devastated by flood and drought, he uses an imperial command he does not possess to distribute relief. He then reports the act, returns his official tally, and offers to answer for the offense; [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] accepts the judgment and does not punish him in the episode's account.

The case separates accountable emergency discretion from concealed lawlessness. Ji does not claim that good intent places him beyond law, nor does he use the emergency for private benefit. He acts against immediate suffering, makes the breach visible, and leaves the ruler to judge it afterward. The synthesis is therefore conditional: urgency can justify acting before authorization only when harm, remedy, scope, and accountability remain tightly joined.

## Key Claims

- Immediate mass suffering can make waiting for ordinary authorization itself a consequential decision.
- Emergency discretion is strongest when the act is limited to relief of the visible harm rather than used to enlarge general power.
- Disclosure and acceptance of review distinguish accountable discretion from covert personal rule.
- Public purpose does not erase the procedural breach; it supplies a reason the breach may be excused after scrutiny.
- A ruler's willingness to evaluate outcome, motive, and proportionality preserves space for useful initiative in emergencies.

## Evidence

Urgent harm and bounded action:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-1-lp-pohns6ymvfpytkih9xkhwcoaw|Hanji 257 part 1]] says Ji An encounters severe flood-and-drought suffering in Henan and uses an unauthorized imperial command to provide relief.

Disclosure and accountability:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-1-lp-pohns6ymvfpytkih9xkhwcoaw|Hanji 257 part 1]] says Ji reports the act on return, surrenders his tally, and offers to accept punishment, while Wudi declines to prosecute him.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on one popular-history episode and does not establish the exact legal authority, relief instrument, number of victims, available alternatives, or whether the reported act is complete. A favorable result does not make all unauthorized action legitimate, and claimed urgency can be manipulated. The case supports a narrow pattern requiring visible emergency, bounded relief, prompt disclosure, and review; it does not support permanent exception, secret coercion, private gain, or retroactive invention of necessity.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Ji An's reported relief action and post-action submission to review.
- Defined emergency initiative through both urgency and accountability rather than benevolent motive alone.
