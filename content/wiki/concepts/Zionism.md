# Zionism

## Definition
Zionism is the modern political project of establishing and sustaining Jewish national self-determination in the historic land of Israel/Palestine; the episode analyzes it critically as a demographic and territorial state project rather than treating it as identical to Judaism or Jewish identity.

## Current Synthesis
The source places Zionism within European nationalism and antisemitism, arguing that Jewish persecution explains the appeal of sovereign safety but does not settle the rights of Palestinians already living on the land. Its strongest boundary is categorical: Jews are internally diverse, many Jews historically opposed Zionism, and criticism of Zionist policy is not permission for antisemitic generalization.

## Key Claims
- Zionism is a political ideology and state-building project, not a synonym for Jewish people or Judaism.
- European persecution and the Holocaust explain insecurity without automatically legitimizing dispossession.
- Maintaining a Jewish demographic majority is presented as central to the state project.
- Jewish communities differ by region, class, religious position, and relationship to Israel.
- Anti-Zionism can become antisemitic when it turns policy critique into collective claims about Jews.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page currently reflects one explicitly critical episode. It does not yet synthesize Zionism's competing liberal, labor, revisionist, religious, cultural, or post-Holocaust traditions, nor the full range of Jewish and Palestinian scholarship.
