## Definition

临终辅政梯队安排 / deathbed ministerial succession planning is the attempt by a dying ruler to preserve regime continuity by naming not one permanent successor-minister but a staged bench whose members fit different tasks, weaknesses, and future political crises.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-193-liubang-zhisi-yiyan-baohanchao-sibainian-jiangshan-1-loppqvgdajwnfwjx4cd-v9itdji7|Hanji 193 part 1]] supplies the current case. When [[LuZhi|吕雉]] asks whom to use after [[XiaoHe|萧何]], [[LiuBang|刘邦]] names [[CaoShen|曹参]], then [[WangLingHanMinister|王陵]] with [[ChenPing|陈平]] as a complement, and finally [[ZhouBo|周勃]] as the person likely to secure the Liu house.

The sequence is useful because it does not treat political talent as one scale. Cao Shen represents continuity after Xiao He; Wang Ling's firmness is paired with Chen Ping's intelligence; Zhou Bo's plain reliability and military standing are reserved for a later security problem. The plan therefore resembles a staged portfolio of role fit, but its success still depends on later actors, coalitions, and circumstances that a deathbed forecast cannot control.

## Key Claims

- A ruler can plan ministerial succession as a sequence rather than as a single appointment.
- Complementary weaknesses matter: one official's firmness, intelligence, administrative continuity, or military credibility may require another official's support.
- The political stage changes the relevant role, so the best immediate successor need not be the person best suited to a later regime-security crisis.
- A deathbed recommendation can preserve a shared personnel map without binding later courts mechanically.
- Later events may make a forecast look prophetic, but hindsight does not prove that the recommendation alone caused dynastic survival.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one popular-history episode's rendering of a famous deathbed exchange. It does not establish the exact dialogue, prove that every named transition unfolded because of Liu Bang's recommendation, or show that he foresaw the full Lu-clan crisis. Cao Shen's later continuity, Wang Ling and Chen Ping's interaction, and Zhou Bo's restoration role were shaped by institutions and coalitions beyond this one personnel forecast. The title's four-century claim is therefore treated as retrospective rhetoric rather than demonstrated causation.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 193 part 1's staged ministerial sequence.
- Separated immediate administrative succession from later dynastic-security roles.
- Qualified prophetic hindsight by emphasizing implementation and coalition dependence.
