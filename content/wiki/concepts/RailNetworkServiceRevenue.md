# Rail Network Service Revenue / 铁路路网服务收入

## Definition

Rail network service revenue is income earned when other railway transport operators pay to run trains over a company's track and associated network infrastructure.

## Current Synthesis

The [[JinghuHighSpeedRail|京沪高铁]] case separates passenger demand from infrastructure monetization. A dense corridor can support its own ticket business while also functioning as a scarce trunk-line platform used by trains operated by other railway companies. In the episode's account, this access-and-service layer produces more revenue than Beijing-Shanghai passenger fares, so profitability depends on network position and traffic carried for the wider system as well as endpoint demand.

## Key Claims

- A railway company can monetize track access and network services independently of tickets sold on its own trains.
- Trunk-line position matters because traffic from multiple operators can share the same scarce corridor.
- Strong passenger demand and network-service revenue are complementary rather than competing explanations of profitability.
- Revenue composition is necessary for comparing a railway infrastructure company with airlines or passenger-service operators.

## Evidence

### Infrastructure access exceeds direct passenger revenue

- [[jinghu-gaotie-qiansanjidu-jingzhuan-baiyi-ruixing-cheng-xinjiapo-dierda-kafei-pinpai-gkwriw4ownzzaervcqt1rzsr]] reports that passenger revenue was less than 40% of Jinghu High-Speed Railway's prior-year total and that services paid by other railway operators formed the main revenue source.

### Corridor demand still supports the platform

- [[jinghu-gaotie-qiansanjidu-jingzhuan-baiyi-ruixing-cheng-xinjiapo-dierda-kafei-pinpai-gkwriw4ownzzaervcqt1rzsr]] links the line's reported profit to its connection of the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei and Yangtze River Delta regions while qualifying a passenger-volume-only explanation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a short business-news digest and does not reproduce audited segment definitions, access-price rules, cost allocation, train-path capacity, related-party terms, or cash-flow data. Revenue leadership does not by itself establish margin contribution, competitive neutrality, regulatory independence, or the value of the infrastructure under a different national network design.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish trunk-line access economics from direct passenger-ticket economics.
