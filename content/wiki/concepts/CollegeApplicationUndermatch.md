# College Application Undermatch

## Definition

College application undermatch is the pattern in which students with academic records compatible with selective institutions do not apply to them, even when admission and aid could make those institutions educationally or financially plausible.

## Current Synthesis

In [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember|Carlos Doesn’t Remember]], [[CarolineHoxby]] and [[ChristopherAvery]] test the belief that selective colleges had already found nearly all plausible low-income high achievers. Their linked test and contextual data are reported to reveal roughly 35,000 students per year above a score and income threshold, many outside familiar recruiting networks and many not applying to selective colleges.

The episode rejects a simple information-only diagnosis. Application decisions arise after years of uneven preparation, confidence, counseling, geography, family obligation, and institutional familiarity. Some selective colleges may cost less after aid than local alternatives, but a lower net price does not make the path legible or usable. [[TalentFragilityUnderDisadvantage]] further implies that observed undermatching excludes students lost before testing.

## Key Claims

- High achievement does not reliably produce applications to institutions matched to that achievement.
- Selective-college recruitment can mistake weak application flow for a scarcity of qualified low-income students.
- Sticker price, net price, perceived belonging, information, counseling, geography, and family constraints can point in different directions.
- Application-stage interventions reach only students whose educational trajectory has survived to that point.
- Test-based counts are lower bounds on potential because they exclude non-testers and earlier attrition.

## Evidence

### Overlooked pool

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] reports Hoxby and Avery's estimate of roughly 35,000 high-scoring students from families earning less than $40,000 annually.

### Nonapplication despite aid

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] says many did not apply to selective colleges even when those colleges could cost less than nearby alternatives.

### Institutional assumption

- [[carlos-doesn-t-remember-carlos-doesn-t-remember]] describes admissions officers believing that conventional recruiting and counseling networks had already saturated the pool.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Academic score compatibility does not establish that a selective college is the best choice for every student. Family proximity, culture, disability support, program fit, work, care obligations, risk tolerance, and preference can make a less selective option rational. The source does not provide the underlying study's definitions, outcomes, later persistence data, or current replication, so the numerical estimate remains episode-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established nonapplication as distinct from absence of ability or inability to pay.
- Added pipeline survival as a limit on application-stage measurement.
- Preserved legitimate reasons a student may choose a less selective institution.
