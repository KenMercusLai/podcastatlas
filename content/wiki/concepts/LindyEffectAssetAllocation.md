# Lindy Effect Asset Allocation / 林迪效应资产配置

Lindy effect asset allocation is [[161-quanqiu-hongguan-he-ziben-shichang-2026-yijidu-fupan-yu-zhanwang-lgzjnsgkur1mcrmkqqxsvxhzb0bm]]'s ultra-long-horizon filter for judging assets and institutions under high randomness. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] introduces the Lindy effect as the idea that some non-natural things gain expected durability from having already survived for a long time, then applies it to religion, double-entry bookkeeping, gold, and core land.

The source does not turn age into an automatic buy signal. Its use is comparative: assets that have survived many political, monetary, technological, and social regimes have passed tests that newer assets have not yet faced. This makes the concept a companion to [[GoldAsCurrencySpareTire]], [[NewOrderAssetPricing]], and [[InvestmentRiskManagement]], especially when markets are trying to price war, inflation, AI, private credit, and institutional trust at the same time.

In the episode, the Lindy filter supports patience and sizing more than prediction. It helps explain why gold may remain a portfolio diversifier even after a sharp move, why core land can carry different uncertainty properties from young financial products, and why newer assets should face a higher burden of proof before they occupy permanent-capital roles.

## Key Claims
- Longevity can be evidence that an asset, system, or institution has survived varied historical stress, but it does not remove valuation, liquidity, or sizing risk.
- The filter is most useful when the investor is comparing permanent-capital roles, not when trying to time a quarter's price move.
- Gold's long monetary history can justify a hedge sleeve without justifying leverage or all-in positioning.
- Newer assets and narratives need stronger evidence because they have not yet crossed enough regimes to prove durability.
- Lindy logic complements [[PortfolioSuitability]]: an asset can be historically durable and still unsuitable if the holder cannot tolerate its drawdowns, liquidity, or income profile.
