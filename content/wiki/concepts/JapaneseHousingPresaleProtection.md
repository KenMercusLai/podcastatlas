# Japanese Housing Presale Protection / 日本住宅预售保全

Japanese housing presale protection is [[131-wo-zai-riben-mai-le-yi-tao-zizhu-fang-llofkgaj-hyajksrpkoylakuocxz]]'s account of why buying an unfinished new-build condo in [[Japan]] does not expose the buyer to the same full-prepayment risk as some Chinese presale housing. The host says he paid a 10% deposit when reserving the unit, while the remaining 90% and the bank loan were paid to the developer at handover.

The source says Japanese law generally restricts presale of commodity housing unless the developer has approval and deposit-protection measures. The result is not risk-free, but the financing sequence gives the buyer and bank more protection because the developer does not receive the whole sale price long before completion.

## Key Claims
- Presale exists in Japan, but the source frames it as more tightly staged than a buyer-financed construction model.
- Deposit protection and handover-timed final payment reduce unfinished-delivery exposure.
- Mortgage disbursement timing matters because the lending bank pays the developer only when the collateral and handover process are ready.
- This protects buyers without removing ordinary risks around developer quality, contract terms, completion delay, interest rates, and personal cash flow.
