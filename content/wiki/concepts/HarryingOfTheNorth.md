# Harrying of the North

## Definition

The Harrying of the North is William's 1069-1070 campaign of destructive repression against northern resistance, presented by the episode as the Norman Conquest's clearest case of violence against food systems and civilian survival.

## Current Synthesis

The episode treats the Harrying as an escalation from conquest to collective devastation. Repeated rebellion helps explain why William hardens his policy, but does not excuse the destruction of supplies and the starvation of guilty and innocent alike; Orderic Vitalis's condemnation is used to preserve that moral distinction.

## Key Claims

- The campaign targeted the material basis of resistance through destruction, looting, and attacks on food supplies.
- Its effects extended beyond armed opponents to civilians exposed to famine and displacement.
- Rebellion explains the strategic context without making indiscriminate suffering morally neutral.
- The campaign is central to English memory of the conquest as trauma rather than orderly succession.

## Evidence

- Destructive mechanism - [[557-1066-the-norman-conquest-part-4-glt5156716826]] describes destruction, looting, and attacks on food supplies in 1069-1070.
- Moral judgment - [[557-1066-the-norman-conquest-part-4-glt5156716826]] cites Orderic Vitalis condemning the slow starvation of innocent and guilty alike.
- Consolidation context - [[557-1066-the-norman-conquest-part-4-glt5156716826]] places the campaign within William's increasingly harsh response to recurring rebellion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's specific mortality and livestock-loss estimates remain source-scoped, and the page does not infer that William began the invasion with a settled plan to eliminate the English elite. Strategic context is preserved without treating it as moral vindication.

## What Changed

- Created the Harrying as the main famine-producing repression case in the Norman consolidation branch.
