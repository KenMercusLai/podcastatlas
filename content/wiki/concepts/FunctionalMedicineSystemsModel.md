# Functional Medicine Systems Model

## Definition
The functional medicine systems model is [[MarkHyman]]'s framework for interpreting symptoms and diagnoses as outputs of interacting gut, immune, mitochondrial, endocrine, metabolic, environmental, behavioral, and social systems.

## Current Synthesis
The model's useful core is multi-causality. One diagnosis can arise from several combinations of diet, infection, microbiome disruption, toxins, allergens, stress, trauma, sleep loss, nutrient gaps, or inactivity, while one disrupted input can contribute to several diagnoses. That makes the framework a map for forming and testing hypotheses rather than proof that every chronic illness has one hidden root cause.

The episode contrasts this approach with specialty-by-symptom fragmentation and laboratory reductionism. Its strongest synthesis is compatible with ordinary complex-systems reasoning: clinicians need both mechanistic detail and a view of interacting inputs. Its weakest edge appears when a broad systems story moves from plausible connection to causal certainty on the basis of a single case, anecdote, or incomplete evidence.

## Key Claims
- Chronic diseases can be multi-causal, and similar diagnoses can arise from different combinations of inputs.
- A change in one input can affect several systems, so interventions and adverse effects may be distributed rather than local.
- The exposome, behavior, and social context can modify biological function alongside inherited risk.
- Symptom clusters may justify cross-system hypotheses instead of automatic separation into unrelated problems.
- The framework is most credible when hypotheses are measured and revised rather than treated as a universal root-cause explanation.

## Evidence
- Network framing - [[how-to-improve-your-vitality-heal-from-disease-dr-mark-hyman-scim9845833582]] describes the body as a network, system, and ecosystem rather than isolated organs and diagnoses.
- Multi-causality - [[how-to-improve-your-vitality-heal-from-disease-dr-mark-hyman-scim9845833582]] names diet, microbes, toxins, infection, trauma, stress, sleep, and nutrient status as interacting candidate causes.
- Cross-diagnosis case - [[how-to-improve-your-vitality-heal-from-disease-dr-mark-hyman-scim9845833582]] reports one patient with inflammatory, metabolic, gastrointestinal, pain, and mood diagnoses improving after a combined diet-and-supplement intervention.
- Complex intervention logic - [[how-to-improve-your-vitality-heal-from-disease-dr-mark-hyman-scim9845833582]] argues that changing sleep, food, movement, nutrients, or medication can alter several biological systems at once.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
A framework's breadth can make it difficult to falsify. The source does not independently establish that every named exposure caused the conditions discussed, and its multi-component case cannot identify which component produced which outcome. Systems thinking should complement, not replace, diagnosis, controlled evidence, specialist care, or urgent treatment. “Root cause” is therefore a hypothesis discipline, not a guarantee of one discoverable cause or cure.
