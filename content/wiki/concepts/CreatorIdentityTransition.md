# Creator Identity Transition / 创作者身份转型

## Definition
Creator identity transition is the friction that appears when a person moves from an old professional role into a public creator role, especially when visibility, audience contact, platform metrics, and business expectations redefine how others treat them.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses Tianzhen's backstage-to-front-stage experience to show that creator transition is not only a career move. The work changes how other people address, schedule, touch, photograph, and evaluate the person. A former operator may understand public attention technically while still feeling discomfort when the attention lands on her own body and daily interactions.

The transition is workable when the person separates old self-image from the concrete value others receive. Tianzhen's later interpretation of photos, handshakes, and live appearances is pragmatic: if the action is easy for her and meaningful to the other person, rejecting it only to defend an old identity may be unnecessary.

## Key Claims
- Moving from behind-the-scenes work to public creator work can make ordinary production treatment feel identity-threatening.
- Audience contact has value beyond information transfer because it makes the creator feel real to listeners or viewers.
- Public visibility can be reinterpreted as service or emotional value rather than only vanity or celebrity treatment.
- Creator identity transition links career strategy to bodily and social habits, not only to content format.
- The same person may need to update internal self-image before new business or media roles become comfortable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not say every creator should accept unlimited access or parasocial demand. It identifies a transition friction and a limited reframing, while boundaries around privacy, fatigue, and safety remain outside this source.
