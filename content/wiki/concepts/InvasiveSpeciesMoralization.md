# Invasive Species Moralization / 外來種道德化

## Definition
Invasive species moralization is the public-discourse pattern where alien or invasive status is treated as evidence that a species is morally bad, making hatred feel like conservation judgment.

## Current Synthesis
The Duanwen myna episode treats this as a failure of environmental education and public conversation. Alien species can create real ecological, economic, health, or safety harms, but their origin does not itself prove harm in every local setting or justify casual cruelty.

The Taiwan nightjar livestream made the pattern visible: emotional attachment to one native bird family, a visible introduced myna, and simplified education about alien species combined to produce calls for private killing. The source's stronger claim is that conservation management should remain evidence-based and ethically accountable even when lethal removal is warranted.

## Key Claims
- Alien-species status can become a moral label when ecological definitions are detached from evidence and context.
- Emotional animal stories can intensify moralization by making one species a victim and another species a villain.
- Hatred-based language can lower ethical standards around killing, especially when private actors treat removal as revenge or entertainment.
- Better public education should preserve the distinction between origin, proven harm, management necessity, and responsibility for death.

## Evidence
- Livestream moralization: [[wailai-bage-yeying-zhibo-yu-taiwan-wailai-wuzhong-nanti-db02e7aa04c129b49b1f36f750155b42]] reports public anger and threatened killing after the common myna pecked the nightjar egg.
- Education simplification: [[wailai-bage-yeying-zhibo-yu-taiwan-wailai-wuzhong-nanti-db02e7aa04c129b49b1f36f750155b42]] says native/alien identification materials can leave people with a crude enemy distinction.
- Ethical boundary: [[wailai-bage-yeying-zhibo-yu-taiwan-wailai-wuzhong-nanti-db02e7aa04c129b49b1f36f750155b42]] argues that even necessary killing should be done with clear reasons and responsibility rather than hatred.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not deny invasive-species harm or the legitimacy of removal. The source explicitly accepts that alien invasive species are a major biodiversity threat in many cases and that professional removal can be necessary.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the Taiwan introduced-myna controversy.
