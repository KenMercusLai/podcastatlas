# Positive Guidance in Early Childhood / 幼儿正向引导

## Definition

Positive guidance in early childhood is the practice of giving a child a concrete action to perform, recognizing the emotion or aim behind the behavior, and explaining rules and consequences instead of relying mainly on prohibition, labels, or unexplained obedience.

## Current Synthesis

[[ep94-meiyou-nage-haizi-tiansheng-jiushi-xionghaizi-liaoliao-zhongmei-de-zaoqi-jiaoyu-xia-wkgmbv3qpbud6lata67q4kiompu513|EP94]] makes the method operational through language. “Walk indoors” tells a young child what to do, whereas “do not run” may leave the prohibited action as the clearest available image. Adults can then explain likely consequences in language the child can gradually understand through repetition, visual cues, and lived experience.

The approach is not passive or permissive. Immediate danger justifies immediate adult intervention; a controllable risk permits more time for instruction and consequence explanation. In both cases, the long-term aim is understanding and usable self-regulation rather than proof that the adult can win a confrontation.

## Key Claims

- Actionable affirmative instructions are often more usable than prohibitions that name only the unwanted behavior.
- Emotion recognition should precede or accompany correction without making every behavior acceptable.
- Explanation helps rules become intelligible rather than arbitrary, even when full verbal understanding develops gradually.
- Safety determines how quickly the adult must act, while explanation remains part of later learning.
- Specific acknowledgment of safe, helpful, or courteous conduct teaches more than global praise.
- Calm adult language supports regulation better than escalating labels such as “naughty” or “rebellious.”

## Evidence

- Replacement action - [[ep94-meiyou-nage-haizi-tiansheng-jiushi-xionghaizi-liaoliao-zhongmei-de-zaoqi-jiaoyu-xia-wkgmbv3qpbud6lata67q4kiompu513|EP94]] contrasts “不要跑” with a concrete instruction to walk indoors and contrasts “不要爬” with placing feet on the ground.
- Emotion and consequence - [[ep94-meiyou-nage-haizi-tiansheng-jiushi-xionghaizi-liaoliao-zhongmei-de-zaoqi-jiaoyu-xia-wkgmbv3qpbud6lata67q4kiompu513|EP94]] recommends acknowledging sadness or anger, then explaining injury, medical care, or lost play time as possible consequences.
- Gradual comprehension - [[ep94-meiyou-nage-haizi-tiansheng-jiushi-xionghaizi-liaoliao-zhongmei-de-zaoqi-jiaoyu-xia-wkgmbv3qpbud6lata67q4kiompu513|EP94]] uses repeated language, visual countdowns, and preparation for school holidays to show understanding forming over time.
- Specific acknowledgment - [[ep94-meiyou-nage-haizi-tiansheng-jiushi-xionghaizi-liaoliao-zhongmei-de-zaoqi-jiaoyu-xia-wkgmbv3qpbud6lata67q4kiompu513|EP94]] recommends naming gratitude, helping, and following safety guidance rather than saying only “真棒.”

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supplies practitioner examples, not comparative evidence that one script works across every age, language profile, neurotype, family, or culture. Positive wording should not delay physical protection in urgent danger, become a demand that distressed adults remain perfectly calm, or substitute for developmental, behavioral, or clinical assessment when concerns are persistent or severe.

## What Changed

- Created a concrete early-childhood guidance frame joining affirmative instruction, emotion recognition, explanation, and safety sequencing.
