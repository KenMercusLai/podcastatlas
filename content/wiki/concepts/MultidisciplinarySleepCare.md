# Multidisciplinary Sleep Care / 多学科睡眠照护

## Definition
Multidisciplinary sleep care is a cause-directed model that routes sleep complaints across relevant specialties and modalities instead of treating every difficulty sleeping as the same disorder or assuming one drug, procedure, or behavioral technique can cover all causes.

## Current Synthesis
The source places anesthesiology sleep clinics beside respiratory medicine, neurology, psychiatry, psychology, and behavioral care. The route depends on the driver: pain may require analgesic and cause-directed assessment; sleep apnea requires breathing-focused evaluation; anxiety and hyperarousal may benefit from psychological or behavioral support; routine and caffeine can require daily-timing changes.

Anesthesiology contributes a pain, sedation, and autonomic-intervention perspective, including source-scoped discussion of analgesic or sedating medicines and stellate ganglion block. Mindfulness, behavioral therapy, and environmental support broaden the pathway, but the episode does not establish that every modality is evidence-equivalent or suitable for every sleep complaint.

The psychiatric episode adds a layered assessment before routing. Acute stress, light, temperature, food, and schedule may be modifiable contributors; persistent low mood, medication questions, limb movement, seizures or brain injury, and dementia-related sleep-maintenance loss can require psychiatric, neurological, sleep-medicine, or caregiving pathways. Polysomnography may characterize brain activity, breathing, movement, and sleep stages when indicated, while dementia care may prioritize fall and wandering prevention plus caregiver rotation rather than assuming medication can eliminate every waking.

The later psychiatry-psychotherapy episode makes modality choice explicitly collaborative. Organic disease should redirect attention to the underlying condition; psychologically maintained insomnia may benefit from CBT-I or other psychotherapy; and medication or clinician-delivered physical treatment can be considered after assessment. This expands the routing map without making the episode's rTMS course length, home devices, imaging list, or hypnosis claims universal standards.

## Key Claims
- Sleep difficulty is a symptom domain with multiple possible causes and care routes; pain, breathing disorders, neurological conditions, psychological distress, medication, caffeine, and routine should not be collapsed into one pathway.
- Anesthesiology can contribute pain-management and procedure expertise to selected sleep complaints.
- Behavioral and psychological approaches can complement medical assessment when arousal, stress, or learned patterns contribute.
- Polysomnography can support selected differential diagnosis, while indirect wearable estimates are not equivalent clinical measurements; in dementia-related waking, environmental safety and caregiver support may remain essential even when complete symptom control is unrealistic.
- Multi-modal care does not mean that every intervention has equal evidence or universal applicability.
- Persistent impairment or red-flag symptoms require cause-directed professional assessment.
- Shared decisions should distinguish treatment modality from treatment sequence: non-drug care may be preferred first when appropriate, but severity, cause, risk, access, prior response, and patient preference can change the route.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episodes are not comparative reviews of specialties or treatments. Their stellate-ganglion-block, sedative, mindfulness, behavioral, polysomnography, dementia, lavender, medication, environmental, hypnosis, rTMS, imaging, and home-device claims are source-scoped and do not establish indications, efficacy, durability, protocol length, or comparative safety. Natural sleep and anesthesia are explicitly not equivalent, a comfortable sedated-procedure experience is not evidence that anesthesia replaces restorative sleep, and a wearable's inferred stages are not a clinical diagnosis.

## What Changed
- Added collaborative routing among underlying-disease care, CBT-I or psychotherapy, physical treatment, and medication.
- Kept rTMS course length, imaging lists, and home-device claims source-scoped rather than treating modalities as evidence-equivalent.
