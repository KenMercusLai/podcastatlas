## Definition

东汉宫廷女性教育 / Eastern Han Female Court Education is the source-scoped pattern where inner-court and elite learning for women becomes tied to formal instruction, classical-historical texts, and court authority rather than only household training.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 775 creates the concept through [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Deng Sui]] and [[BanZhaoLateHan|Ban Zhao]]. Deng's own childhood learning is used as the personal formation layer: she is portrayed as reading classical texts early and carrying that cultivated restraint into palace life. The episode then extends the point institutionally by saying [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] set up North Palace women's study and invited Ban Zhao to teach palace women.

Hanji 705-6 extends the concept from palace schooling to textual transmission around the court. Ban Zhao is described as teaching `Han Shu` after the work is completed or made transmissible, taking [[MaRongLateHan|Ma Rong]] as a student, and opening instruction so more people can read the text. This does not turn the page into a general history of education, but it strengthens the narrower pattern: learned women can become named teachers and interpreters of canonical-historical knowledge.

The current synthesis remains bounded. Hanji 775 frames the curriculum broadly, naming classics, astronomy, and arithmetic, while Hanji 705-6 adds `Han Shu` interpretation and the source-scoped "first female teacher" claim. Together they show female learning as part of palace governance, family discipline, text circulation, and political capacity, but not as evidence for ordinary women's educational access.

## Key Claims

- Women's learning is presented as formation for court conduct, not only ornament or leisure.
- Imperial and dowager sponsorship can turn elite female education from household practice into an inner-court institution.
- Ban Zhao's teaching role gives the school a named intellectual mediator rather than leaving it as anonymous palace instruction.
- The curriculum is source-described as broader than literary polish, including classics, astronomy, and arithmetic.
- `Han Shu` teaching extends the evidence from palace women to source-scoped public textual transmission, including Ma Rong as a student.
- The evidence supports a bounded elite and court-adjacent education pattern, not a general claim about education access for ordinary women.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on two source notes. Hanji 775's claim that the school was the first of its kind in China or the world and Hanji 705-6's claim that Ban Zhao was the first female teacher are preserved as source-scoped rather than accepted as comparative education history. The page also does not imply broad female literacy across Eastern Han society; the evidence is palace-centered, elite, and text-transmission focused.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 705-6 as the `Han Shu` transmission layer, including Ma Rong as a source-scoped student and the bounded female-teacher claim.
- Preserved Hanji 775 as the North Palace women's study, Deng Sui learning profile, and Ban Zhao court-teaching layer.
