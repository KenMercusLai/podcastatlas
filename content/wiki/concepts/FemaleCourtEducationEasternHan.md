## Definition

东汉宫廷女性教育 / Eastern Han Female Court Education is the source-scoped pattern where inner-court and elite women receive formal instruction in classics and practical knowledge through imperial or dowager-backed institutions rather than only through household training.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 775 creates the concept through [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Deng Sui]] and [[BanZhaoLateHan|Ban Zhao]]. Deng's own childhood learning is used as the personal formation layer: she is portrayed as reading classical texts early and carrying that cultivated restraint into palace life. The episode then extends the point institutionally by saying [[EmperorHeOfHan|Emperor He]] set up North Palace women's study and invited Ban Zhao to teach palace women.

The source frames the curriculum broadly, naming classics, astronomy, and arithmetic. Deng's later influence is described as expanding the impact of women's schooling and encouraging unmarried high-ritual households' daughters to study. In the current wiki, the safest synthesis is not a universal history of girls' education, but a bounded court pattern: female learning can become part of palace governance, family discipline, and political capacity.

## Key Claims

- Women's learning is presented as formation for court conduct, not only ornament or leisure.
- Imperial and dowager sponsorship can turn elite female education from household practice into an inner-court institution.
- Ban Zhao's teaching role gives the school a named intellectual mediator rather than leaving it as anonymous palace instruction.
- The curriculum is source-described as broader than literary polish, including classics, astronomy, and arithmetic.
- The evidence supports a bounded elite court-education pattern, not a general claim about education access for ordinary women.

## Evidence

- Deng's formative learning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-775-gan-xiang-mingyun-shuo-no-bukuishi-you-tounao-de-nvzhumu-lh0erdh890ooow6dn0wvf1fbzedf|Hanji 775]] portrays Deng as studying from childhood and later carrying learning, restraint, and etiquette into palace life.
- Institutional teaching: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-775-gan-xiang-mingyun-shuo-no-bukuishi-you-tounao-de-nvzhumu-lh0erdh890ooow6dn0wvf1fbzedf|Hanji 775]] says Emperor He established North Palace women's study and invited Ban Zhao to teach palace women.
- Curriculum and expansion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-775-gan-xiang-mingyun-shuo-no-bukuishi-you-tounao-de-nvzhumu-lh0erdh890ooow6dn0wvf1fbzedf|Hanji 775]] names classics, astronomy, and arithmetic, and says Deng later developed the influence of women's schooling.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one source note. The episode's claim that the school was the first of its kind in China or the world is preserved as source-scoped rather than accepted as comparative education history. The page also does not imply broad female literacy across Eastern Han society; the evidence is palace-centered and elite.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 775's North Palace women's study, Deng Sui's learning profile, and Ban Zhao's court-teaching role.
