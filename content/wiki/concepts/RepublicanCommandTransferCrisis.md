# Republican Command Transfer Crisis / 共和军事指挥权转移危机

## Definition

Republican command transfer crisis / 共和军事指挥权转移危机 is the late-republic failure mode in which a formally assigned military command becomes a political prize that assemblies, tribunes, street force, rival generals, and soldiers fight to reassign or resist.

## Current Synthesis

Episode 702 grounds the concept in the 88 BC struggle over the war against [[MithridatesVI]]. Because [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]] is consul, the command is assigned to him. The command promises glory and wealth, so [[GaiusMarius]] wants it despite age and declining military centrality. He allies with [[PubliusSulpiciusRufus]], whose citizenship bill and street support create the political machinery for reassignment.

The crisis escalates because legal form and coercion become inseparable. Sulpicius's bill sparks rioting, Sulla is forced into humiliating concessions, a plebiscite removes him from the eastern command, and an officer arrives at his camp to take over six legions. Episode 703 resolves the cliff edge: Sulla does not accept the reassignment, his soldiers follow him toward Rome, and the [[FirstMarchOnRome|first march on Rome]] makes command transfer a cause of armed seizure rather than only a contested vote.

The concept now also includes the aftershock. Sulla tries to secure Italy by transferring [[GnaeusPompeiusStrabo]]'s legions to Pompeius Rufus, but the soldiers kill the replacement commander and Strabo keeps the army. Command transfer has therefore failed in both directions: Sulla refuses Marius's takeover of his command, and Strabo's troops prevent Sulla's preferred transfer of their command.

## Key Claims

- Foreign commands become dangerous when they promise personal glory, wealth, and future political leverage.
- Consular assignment is not secure when tribunes and assemblies can be mobilized to transfer command.
- Street violence can make formally legal decisions coercive in practice.
- Command transfer becomes explosive when the officer losing command still has soldiers in camp.
- Soldiers' expectation of campaign reward can make them resist legal reassignment.
- The boundary between republican procedure and military rebellion breaks once a command loser can march on Rome.
- Failed command transfer can also create independent army brokers, as Strabo's retained legions show.

## Evidence

- Valuable eastern command: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] says Roman elites saw the Mithridatic war as an opportunity for glory and wealth.
- Initial assignment: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] says Sulla received the command because he was consul.
- Political bargain: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] says Marius backed Sulpicius's citizenship policy while Sulpicius backed transferring the command to Marius.
- Coercive procedure: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] describes rioting, Sulla's refuge in Marius's house, and forced acceptance of Sulpicius's legislation.
- Legion threshold: [[702-roman-civil-war-the-barbarian-invasion-part-2-glt3336491089]] ends with a messenger arriving at Sulla's camp to remove him from command and take control of his six legions.
- Armed refusal: [[703-roman-civil-war-marching-on-the-senate-part-3-glt3125075511]] says Sulla speaks to his soldiers, they urge him to lead them to Rome, and he marches despite senior-officer desertion.
- Second transfer failure: [[703-roman-civil-war-marching-on-the-senate-part-3-glt3125075511]] says Sulla's attempt to transfer Strabo's legions ends with Pompeius Rufus killed and Strabo retaining the army.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not say command reassignment is inherently illegitimate. Its late-republic danger appears when reassignment combines with street intimidation, huge campaign rewards, preexisting personal command loyalty, and political actors willing to treat opponents as public enemies. Episode 703 resolves the first march but still stops before Sulla's return and final civil-war settlement.

## What Changed

- Moved the concept from a threshold before Sulla's choice to a failure mode that produces the first march on Rome.
- Added soldier reward expectations, senior-officer desertion, and Strabo's failed reassignment as evidence that command could no longer be transferred cleanly by law.
