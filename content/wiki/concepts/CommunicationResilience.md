# Communication Resilience

## Definition

Communication resilience is the capacity to preserve a useful flow of information when a primary channel is damaged, denied, monitored, congested, or technically unavailable.

## Current Synthesis

[[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] shows resilience through channel diversity rather than technological sophistication alone. [[CarrierPigeonCommunication]] could cross siege lines, battlefields, occupied territory, or open water when runners, wires, or radios were unsafe or had failed. Its limitations were real, but its different failure mode made it useful alongside newer systems.

The central lesson is conditional: an old or low-bandwidth method can be strategically valuable when it does not share the primary system's dependencies. Resilience therefore depends on preparation, trained operators, maintained assets, clear message priorities, and integration into decision processes—not merely on possessing a backup technology.

## Key Claims

- Redundancy is strongest when backup channels fail differently from the primary channel.
- A low-bandwidth channel can preserve high-value messages even when it cannot replace normal traffic.
- Fallback capacity must be trained, positioned, maintained, and incorporated before disruption.
- Communication usefulness depends on the whole operating system, including senders, receivers, authentication, routing, and response.
- Historical technologies can remain relevant when modern systems acquire new shared vulnerabilities.

## Evidence

- Battlefield fallback: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] reports a First World War commander preferring pigeons if only one front-line method could remain.
- Rescue fallback: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] describes RAF aircraft carrying pigeons in case ditching and radio failure cut crews off.
- Intelligence fallback: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] shows Operation Columba moving reports from occupied Europe without an electronic transmitter in the field.
- Modern vulnerability: [[216-pigeons-glt2770080110]] links reported Chinese military pigeon training to concern about cyber disruption of electronic communications.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode offers suggestive historical cases, not a comparative reliability study or a current defense recommendation. A backup can add logistics, security risks, false confidence, and maintenance cost. Whether pigeons or any other legacy method are useful depends on the threat model, geography, payload, latency, reliability, and alternatives.

## What Changed

- Established communication resilience as diversity of failure modes supported by operational preparation.
