# Dynastic Claim National Mobilization

## Definition

Dynastic claim national mobilization is the source's pattern for converting a ruler's inherited or legal claim into a broader political cause that can recruit institutional backing and national identity.

## Current Synthesis

The episode grounds this concept in [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]]'s 1337 turn against [[PhilipVIOfFrance|Philip VI]]. Edward's claim to [[France]] through [[IsabellaOfFrance|Isabella]] is dynastic, but the war case he presents is not only personal. He goes to Parliament, secures peer backing, and issues a public manifesto tying Philip's actions to Scotland, coastal raids, and the unlawful stripping of [[Aquitaine]].

The concept matters because it links emerging English identity to medieval monarchy without treating England as a fully modern nation-state. The source says England is closer to national consolidation than France, yet the mobilization still runs through royal rights, Parliament, war funding, and legal grievance.

## Key Claims

- A dynastic claim becomes more powerful when attached to wider institutional and public grievances.
- Edward III initially does not go to war over the succession alone, showing that claim possession is not the same as mobilization.
- Parliamentary backing turns the dispute from private royal ambition into an English political commitment.
- The manifesto links France, Scotland, coastal raids, and Aquitaine into one public case.
- English national consolidation makes Edward's framing more plausible without making the polity fully modern.
- The concept depends on layering succession, territory, alliance pressure, and identity rather than isolating one cause.

## Evidence

- Initial restraint: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says Edward III does not immediately go to war over the French succession.
- Parliamentary backing: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says Edward goes to Parliament and secures the backing of the peers of England.
- Manifesto: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says Edward's 28 August 1337 manifesto accuses Philip of backing Scots, coastal raids, and unlawful confiscation.
- Identity setting: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] says England is closer to a nation-state than France while preserving the category boundary.
- Causal layering: [[318-hundred-years-war-a-game-of-thrones-part-1-glt5584156008]] places succession beside Gascony, Scotland, trade, and French power.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not claim fully modern nationalism in 1337. It also does not show popular participation in detail beyond parliamentary backing and manifesto framing, so the concept should remain narrower than mass nationalism.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to describe Edward III's move from dynastic grievance to institutional and English-cause framing.
