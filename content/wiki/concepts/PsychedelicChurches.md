# Psychedelic Churches

Psychedelic churches are religious organizations that use psychoactive substances such as [[PsilocybinMushrooms]] or [[DMT]] as sacrament. In [[a-trip-to-the-magic-mushroom-megachurch]], [[PlanetMoney]] treats them as a growing U.S. movement operating between religious freedom claims, drug prohibition, harm reduction, and business-like access models.

The source's key example is [[ZydorChurch]], but [[JohnRapp]] estimates that there are more than 300 psychedelic religious organizations in the [[UnitedStates]]. Their shared problem is institutional legibility: they need theology, ritual, leadership, texts, and safety practices strong enough to support [[ReligiousControlledSubstanceExemption]] while still facing the [[ControlledSubstancesAct]].

## Key Claims
- Religious language changes the legal and social framing of drug access, but it does not automatically create legality.
- A church's size, revenue, initiation process, security, and product range can make sincerity harder for outsiders to judge.
- Legal advice often turns private visions and loose communities into bylaws, canons, holidays, codes of ethics, and ritual systems.
- Harm-reduction arguments can coexist with religious claims, but they do not by themselves settle whether an organization is a church.
