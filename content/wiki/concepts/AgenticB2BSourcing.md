# Agentic B2B Sourcing

## Definition
Agentic B2B sourcing is the use of governed agents to turn a product need or idea into research, specifications, supplier selection, cost comparison, communication, transaction, logistics, and outcome feedback.

## Current Synthesis
The workflow is more demanding than consumer product search because commercial commitments depend on supplier capability, specifications, certificates, hidden charges, tariffs, freight, landed cost, delivery timing, payment, and after-sales support. The agent must produce usable artifacts and state changes, not merely plausible recommendations, while preserving decision traces so later manufacturing and sales outcomes can improve earlier choices.

E255 extends E231's sourcing chain into a merchant operating loop. Product research and supplier coordination connect to shipping, multi-platform listing, pricing, customer response, profit reporting, replenishment, and dispute handling. This broadening makes model intelligence only one layer: tools, permissions, persistent state, proprietary trade context, regional integrations, routing, verification, and human approval jointly determine whether the workflow works.

## Key Claims
- Rough product ideas must become supplier-ready specifications, designs, and comparison criteria.
- Supplier choice should use landed cost, validity, capability, timing, compliance, and fraud signals rather than headline price.
- Long sourcing and sales cycles require persistent state, new-information handling, and feedback across weeks or months.
- Real-task evaluation must verify completed commercial outcomes and report autonomous performance separately from assisted usefulness.
- Human review remains necessary for supplier selection, payment, feasibility, margin, inventory, advertising, and brand-risk decisions.
- The workflow becomes an operating loop when sourcing data connects to listing, sales, service, replenishment, and the next product cycle.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources describe Alibaba-linked products through the responsible executive, so speed, adoption, benchmark, and merchant-outcome claims lack independent controls in the supplied material. Not every business uses the same platforms, objectives, suppliers, or definition of quality. A workflow that accelerates routine comparison may still fail on tacit product taste, unusual manufacturing constraints, legal accountability, negotiation relationships, or long-run demand.

## What Changed
- Extended sourcing from procurement into a multi-platform merchant operating loop.
- Added the model–harness–context architecture and regional adaptation requirement.
- Added outcome-based ecommerce evaluation and the autonomous-versus-assisted distinction.
- Added model routing, affordability, and explicit human approval for consequential actions.
