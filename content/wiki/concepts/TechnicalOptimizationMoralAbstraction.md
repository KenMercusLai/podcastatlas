# Technical Optimization as Moral Abstraction

## Definition

Technical optimization as moral abstraction is the separation of a system's measurable performance variables from the human meaning of the outcome being optimized, especially when neutral engineering language makes destructive capability easier to improve than to judge.

## Current Synthesis

[[the-bomber-mafia-the-bomber-mafia]] first supplies a command-and-doctrine case. Abort rates, strike photographs, anti-aircraft probabilities, straight-run duration, bomb patterns, range, altitude, and payload made bombing performance legible and improvable. Those measures supported real learning, but LeMay's description of a tightly grouped factory strike as beautiful illustrates how technical and aesthetic success can narrow attention to what the attack means beyond the aiming point.

[[may-the-best-firebomb-win-may-the-best-firebomb-win]] extends the structure into weapons research. Gel consistency, globule size, adhesion, dispersal, temperature, altitude, impact, burn duration, replica construction, and fire classification created a disciplined experimental program for napalm. Each variable answered a legitimate technical question, but together they optimized the destruction of homes and the people likely to be inside them.

Abstraction is not the same as ignorance or proof of malign intent. Controlled measurement can reveal whether a device works and can support accountable comparison. The ethical failure occurs when success on the proxy becomes sufficient authorization for use, or when the experimental frame hides that the target category has shifted from infrastructure to an urban population.

## Key Claims

- Measurement can make destructive work feel administratively neutral without changing its human consequence.
- A proxy can be technically valid while remaining morally incomplete.
- Realistic testing reduces uncertainty about capability but may increase pressure to use what has been proven effective.
- Optimization requires renewed ethical review when the practical target expands from materiel to homes or populations.
- Scientific rigor and moral responsibility are compatible obligations, not substitutes for one another.
- Accuracy can reduce some harm while still leaving target choice, coercive purpose, and downstream civilian effects morally unresolved.

## Evidence

### Laboratory optimization

- [[may-the-best-firebomb-win-may-the-best-firebomb-win]] describes researchers adjusting gel consistency and measuring the size and distribution of dispersed globules to improve adhesion and burning.

### Command measurement and aesthetic distance

- [[the-bomber-mafia-the-bomber-mafia]] describes LeMay using abort rates, strike photographs, firing probabilities, and a personally led straight approach to improve results, then remembering a precise bomb pattern as beautiful.

### Realistic target testing

- [[may-the-best-firebomb-win-may-the-best-firebomb-win]] describes accurate Japanese and German residential replicas, categorical fire scoring, and repeated rebuilding to compare incendiary designs.

### Capability-to-doctrine pressure

- [[may-the-best-firebomb-win-may-the-best-firebomb-win]] frames napalm's demonstrated effectiveness as a temptation for precision-bombing advocates because the weapon made mass urban destruction practical.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Technical language does not itself prove moral evasion, and greater accuracy may reduce unintended harm. Rigorous testing can expose limits, prevent unreliable claims, or support safer design. The bounded sources do not reconstruct participants' private reasoning, the full authorization chain, enemy military use of urban industry, or every wartime alternative. The concept identifies a risk in decision structure, not a complete explanation of individual culpability.

## What Changed

- Added bombing accuracy and aesthetic response as an earlier form of abstraction before napalm optimization.
- Distinguished reduced circular error from complete judgment of target, coercive purpose, and downstream harm.
