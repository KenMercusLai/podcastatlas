# Conflict Information Verification / 冲突信息核验

## Definition
Conflict information verification is the practice of separating observation, testimony, official messaging, circulating rumor, and unresolved disagreement when war conditions prevent complete independent confirmation.

## Current Synthesis
The source describes a shift from information scarcity to signal-selection pressure. Official propaganda, counter-propaganda, platform imagery, rumors, and mutually hostile accounts can all be abundant while access remains dangerous and partial.

Responsible reporting does not solve this by declaring every claim equally true or by forcing one definitive version. It identifies who believes or asserts what, protects vulnerable sources, records what was directly observed, and states where corroboration ends. Deleting images that could endanger interviewees can be an ethical evidence decision even when it reduces the reporter's archive.

## Key Claims
- Information abundance can increase rather than reduce verification difficulty.
- Direct observation, testimony, rumor, and official claims require distinct treatment.
- Contradictory social groups may each accuse foreign reporting of distortion while offering incompatible accounts.
- Source protection can require withholding or deleting material.
- Explicit uncertainty is more responsible than unsupported certainty or indiscriminate equivalence.

## Evidence
- Conflicting claims - [[lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord-lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord]] describes unverifiable and contradictory Iranian claims, with liberal and conservative groups offering opposing versions of the country's reality.
- Surveillance and protection - [[lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord-lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord]] recounts device inspection and deletion of images to reduce risk to interviewees.
- Signal overload - [[lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord-lkuvt7driaody6cy5pq-bj517ord]] says years of Russia-Ukraine war produced dense streams of propaganda, counter-messaging, battlefield images, and texts, shifting the task toward selection and interpretation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Transparent uncertainty does not remove the duty to verify what can be checked, nor does it make all sides equally credible. The source offers a reporting ethic, not a substitute for forensic, historical, regional, or open-source investigation.
