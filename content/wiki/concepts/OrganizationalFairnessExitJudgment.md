## Definition

组织公平感与退出判断 / Organizational Fairness and Exit Judgment is a workplace decision frame that distinguishes felt unfairness, the organization's evaluation logic, the worker's remaining development path, and the manner of exit.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-367-2-jiao-ni-yizhao-yingdui-zhichang-zhong-de-bugong-lmphtxnfn76galepblj-x4ikc3gl|Hanji 367-2]] starts from recognizable grievances: credit theft, unreciprocated help, favoritism, exclusion, hidden rules, and rewards disconnected from visible effort. It argues that personal hurt alone cannot explain why an organization rewards or retains someone; the worker also needs to ask what the organization treats as useful to its development.

That perspective is diagnostic, not a command to submit. If a role still offers meaningful development and the costs are tolerable, continued work may be rational even when the environment is imperfect. If the environment is structurally dark, health-damaging, and offers no credible development, controlled exit is preferable to chronic anger or a destructive final confrontation. The concept therefore joins organizational perspective with personal boundaries and future optionality.

## Key Claims

- Felt unfairness is important evidence, but it is not a complete model of how an organization allocates recognition, opportunity, or protection.
- Understanding organizational incentives can clarify a bad outcome without proving that the outcome is ethical, lawful, or worthy of acceptance.
- Staying is conditional on remaining development value and tolerable personal cost, not on loyalty to the organization at any price.
- Exit becomes rational when structural conditions appear unchangeable, future growth has disappeared, or continued exposure is damaging health and agency.
- A controlled departure protects reputation, relationships, and future options better than an impulsive attempt to punish the organization on the way out.

Conditional continuation:

Controlled exit:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-367-2-jiao-ni-yizhao-yingdui-zhichang-zhong-de-bugong-lmphtxnfn76galepblj-x4ikc3gl|Hanji 367-2]] recommends leaving when development is absent, but rejects a provocative “甩锅” departure and uses “分手见人品” to emphasize future relational consequences.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page rests on one short advice segment rather than comparative workplace research. “老板思维” can become a rhetorical tool for excusing favoritism, exploitation, discrimination, retaliation, or illegality if organizational usefulness is treated as the only standard. Some harms require documentation, support, formal complaint, legal advice, or urgent exit rather than patient perspective-taking. Conversely, one disappointing evaluation does not by itself prove structural corruption or eliminate the value of feedback and repair.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 367-2's distinction among personal grievance, organizational logic, development value, and exit conduct.
- Limited “老板思维” to a diagnostic perspective rather than treating organizational interest as a moral definition of fairness.
- Made health, future growth, and relationship-preserving departure explicit decision conditions.
