## Definition

轮台罪己诏政策转向 / Luntai self-criticism policy turn is the source's nameable pattern in which [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] publicly converts late-life remorse over war, levies, and failed frontier schemes into a stated turn toward restrained governance, agriculture, horse recovery, and popular rest.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence comes from Hanji 389-2. The episode treats the Luntai edict as an accountability text with administrative consequences. Han Wudi does not merely express regret; he refuses a per-head levy for frontier expenses, rejects the distant Luntai屯田 plan, dismisses an assassination scheme against the Xiongnu chanyu, and orders officials to stop harshness, encourage farming, rebuild horse resources, and submit local plans for frontier supply.

The turn is made legible by failure. [[LiGuangliWesternHan|李广利]]'s expedition is framed as a decision shaped by misread Xiongnu signals, optimistic courtiers, fangshi and astrology talk, and later disastrous casualties. Against that background, the edict becomes a reversal from prestige-driven expedition and covert violence toward state repair. [[ZhaoGuoWesternHan|赵过]] then supplies the practical recovery branch: agricultural technique, tools, and labor-saving cultivation help embody the court's new priority.

## Key Claims

- The Luntai edict joins self-blame to concrete policy refusal, especially extra levies and distant屯田.
- The source frames late Han Wudi remorse as a response to war cost, labor burden, and intelligence failure rather than only personal emotion.
- Rejecting the chanyu-assassination plan marks a boundary between strategic rivalry and morally degrading covert violence.
- Agricultural promotion and horse-resource rebuilding show recovery policy rather than pacifist disarmament.
- Zhao Guo's farming-tech role illustrates the post-turn value of practical livelihood talent.
- The episode uses the turn to reassess Han Wudi as both deeply damaging and capable of unusually public responsibility-taking.

## Evidence

Policy refusal:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-389-2-lishi-jiemi-shui-shi-xie-jiantaoshu-zuiduo-de-wang-lvnamm5dza3s9ao8ofcshhiynsu1|Hanji 389-2]] says Han Wudi rejects an added thirty-coin levy and the proposal to send people to Luntai to farm, fortify, and maintain distant posts.

Failure background:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-389-2-lishi-jiemi-shui-shi-xie-jiantaoshu-zuiduo-de-wang-lvnamm5dza3s9ao8ofcshhiynsu1|Hanji 389-2]] says Li Guangli's expedition followed Xiongnu provocation, detained Han envoys, auspicious readings by officials and specialists, and later turned into defeat, death, capture, and flight.

Moral boundary:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-389-2-lishi-jiemi-shui-shi-xie-jiantaoshu-zuiduo-de-wang-lvnamm5dza3s9ao8ofcshhiynsu1|Hanji 389-2]] says Han Wudi rejects recruiting prisoners to escort envoys and assassinate the chanyu, calling such conduct beneath the standards even of Spring-Autumn hegemons and also impractical under Xiongnu searches.

Recovery program:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-389-2-lishi-jiemi-shui-shi-xie-jiantaoshu-zuiduo-de-wang-lvnamm5dza3s9ao8ofcshhiynsu1|Hanji 389-2]] says the edict orders a stop to harmful, wasteful projects, forbids harsh treatment of the people, cancels unauthorized tax increases, encourages agriculture, and asks commandery and kingdom officials to plan horse breeding and frontier supplies.

Practical talent:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-389-2-lishi-jiemi-shui-shi-xie-jiantaoshu-zuiduo-de-wang-lvnamm5dza3s9ao8ofcshhiynsu1|Hanji 389-2]] says Zhao Guo becomes sousu duwei and spreads farming methods, tools, and daitian-style technique that help people get more harvest with less effort.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page is bounded to one podcast source. It records the episode's synthesis of the Luntai edict rather than independently verifying the original text, the exact expedition chronology, casualty scale, or whether every later harmful policy truly stopped. The source praises Han Wudi's courage in admitting fault, but that praise coexists with other wiki pages' evidence of severe war, fiscal extraction, commercial damage, and displacement under his reign.
