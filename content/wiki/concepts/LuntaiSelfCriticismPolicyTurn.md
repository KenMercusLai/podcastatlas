## Definition

轮台罪己诏政策转向 / Luntai self-criticism policy turn is the source's nameable pattern in which [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] publicly converts late-life remorse over immortality-seeking waste, war, levies, and failed frontier schemes into a stated turn toward restrained governance, agriculture, horse recovery, and popular rest.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence comes from Hanji 389-1 and Hanji 389-2. Hanji 389-1 supplies the prelude: after failed sea-searching, sacrifice, and public self-reproach, Han Wudi accepts [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]]'s advice to dismiss ineffective fangshi and later says immortal claims are empty. The policy turn therefore begins not only with frontier retrenchment but also with rejecting sacred promises that had justified wasteful imperial projects.

Hanji 389-2 then treats the Luntai edict as an accountability text with administrative consequences. Han Wudi does not merely express regret; he refuses a per-head levy for frontier expenses, rejects the distant Luntai屯田 plan, dismisses an assassination scheme against the Xiongnu chanyu, and orders officials to stop harshness, encourage farming, rebuild horse resources, and submit local plans for frontier supply.

The turn is made legible by failure. [[LiGuangliWesternHan|李广利]]'s expedition is framed as a decision shaped by misread Xiongnu signals, optimistic courtiers, fangshi and astrology talk, and later disastrous casualties. Against that background, the edict becomes a reversal from prestige-driven expedition and covert violence toward state repair. [[ZhaoGuoWesternHan|赵过]] then supplies the practical recovery branch: agricultural technique, tools, and labor-saving cultivation help embody the court's new priority.

## Key Claims

- The Luntai edict joins self-blame to concrete policy refusal, especially extra levies and distant屯田.
- The turn begins with a break from ineffective immortality-seeking and fangshi authority before it becomes a frontier and agricultural recovery program.
- The source frames late Han Wudi remorse as a response to war cost, labor burden, and intelligence failure rather than only personal emotion.
- Rejecting the chanyu-assassination plan marks a boundary between strategic rivalry and morally degrading covert violence.
- Agricultural promotion and horse-resource rebuilding show recovery policy rather than pacifist disarmament.
- Zhao Guo's farming-tech role illustrates the post-turn value of practical livelihood talent.
- The episode uses the turn to reassess Han Wudi as both deeply damaging and capable of unusually public responsibility-taking.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page is bounded to two podcast sources. It records their synthesis of the Luntai turn rather than independently verifying the original text, the exact expedition chronology, casualty scale, fangshi identities, or whether every later harmful policy truly stopped. The sources praise Han Wudi's courage in admitting fault and breaking with fangshi, but that praise coexists with other wiki pages' evidence of severe war, fiscal extraction, commercial damage, and displacement under his reign.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 389-1's prelude, where Tian Qianqiu's anti-fangshi advice and Han Wudi's rejection of immortality claims make the Luntai turn begin as a break with wasteful sacred promises.
- Preserved Hanji 389-2's account of the Luntai edict as self-criticism plus policy reversal toward recovery.
