# Presbyopia Correction and Fitting / 老花矫正与验配

## Definition
Presbyopia correction and fitting is the episode's approach to age-related loss of near accommodation through refraction, habitual working distance, lens design, trial wear, and adaptation rather than an off-the-shelf age estimate alone.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes presbyopia from myopia. Nearsightedness can make one uncorrected near distance clearer because the focal point is already close, but it does not prevent age-related decline in accommodation. A myopic person may therefore remove distance glasses or hold material at a particular distance while still having presbyopia.

Correction is task- and person-specific. Reading glasses need testing against prescription, preferred reading distance, clarity, comfort, and dizziness. Progressive lenses combine distance and near zones in one lens, but their placement and power change make accurate fitting, trial wear, and adaptation especially important. The goal is usable vision across real tasks, not merely matching a nominal age to a generic lens power.

## Key Claims
- Presbyopia reflects age-related reduction in accommodation and eventually affects myopic and non-myopic people.
- Myopia may make a particular uncorrected near distance clearer without preventing presbyopia.
- Reading-glass power should be matched to refraction, task distance, clarity, and comfort rather than age alone.
- Progressive lenses distribute distance and near correction across zones and require accurate fitting.
- Trial wear and adaptation matter because a technically plausible prescription may still cause blur or dizziness in use.

- Reading-glass fitting - [[vol-38-yanke-dangdai-nianqingren-de-zhe-12-ge-yanbu-wenti-ni-zhong-le-jige-lphaxiiov6oe9fii1zotrzxygyor|VOL.38]] recommends refraction and testing at the user's habitual reading distance rather than casual purchase by age.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not compare bifocals, occupational lenses, multifocal contacts, monovision, surgery, anisometropia, binocular-vision conditions, or detailed fitting measurements. New near blur, headache, double vision, rapid refractive change, or unequal vision should not be assumed to be ordinary presbyopia without examination. Lens design and adaptation vary by prescription, task, anatomy, and individual tolerance.
