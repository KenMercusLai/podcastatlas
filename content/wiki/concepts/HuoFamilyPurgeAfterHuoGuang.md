## Definition

霍氏灭族与权力退场失败 / Huo family purge after Huo Guang is the court-politics pattern where a meritorious regent's household remains too close to coercive authority after the ruler can govern personally, turning inherited merit into ruler suspicion, public resentment, conspiracy risk, and eventually excessive collective punishment.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-425-huoguang-sihou-canzao-miemen-simaguang-weisha-fenfenbuping-lmz7bdfy8anhkuq24l-f2ulkolbu|Hanji 425]] creates the concept through [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s judgment on [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] and [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]. The source refuses a simple villain story. Huo Guang's loyalty and founding service remain real, but Sima Guang says authority belongs to the ruler and should have been returned once Xuan was mature enough to rule.

The failure has two sides. Huo Guang's side is overstay and family discipline: the Huo household keeps power, offices, and military access while resentment grows. Xuan's side is delayed settlement: he could have rewarded the Huo descendants with wealth, titles, and court audience while removing them from real command, but instead lets them remain powerful until fear hardens into conspiracy. The final punishment is therefore both intelligible and excessive. The Huo conspirators can deserve destruction, while the total extinction of Huo Guang's descendants still appears刻薄寡恩 because it erases the sacrificial line of a loyal minister.

## Key Claims

- A regent's public merit becomes politically unstable when the household continues to hold ruler-like authority after the ruler can govern personally.
- Timely power return is a family-preservation strategy, not only a constitutional nicety.
- Rulers can worsen overmighty-family risk by leaving descendants in command positions instead of converting merit into safe titles, stipends, and ritual honor.
- Collective punishment may answer real conspiracy while still violating proportional gratitude toward a meritorious ancestor.
- Sima Guang's judgment allocates responsibility across the ministerial household and the ruler rather than treating purge as self-evidently just.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one episode's presentation of Sima Guang's commentary. It does not prove that every Huo family member was innocent, nor does it deny that the named Huo conspirators bore responsibility for their actions. Its narrow claim is about failure of timely power exit, safe reward conversion, and proportional punishment after meritorious regency service.
