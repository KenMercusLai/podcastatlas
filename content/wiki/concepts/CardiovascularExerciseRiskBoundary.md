# Cardiovascular Exercise Risk Boundary / 心血管运动风险边界

## Definition
Cardiovascular exercise risk boundary / 心血管运动风险边界 is the distinction between ordinary exercise intensity guidance and situations where heart disease, blood pressure, cardiopulmonary reserve, autonomic recovery, symptoms, or high-performance goals make professional assessment and an individualized exercise prescription more appropriate.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-211-yue-yundong-yue-kualian-zhengxing-yisheng-yu-xinxueguan-yisheng-de-jianshen-qumei-zhinan-lsp1xlzq1iapc-qale-mzutigv0u]] develops the boundary around a common wearable-era habit: watching heart rate during training. The episode accepts heart rate as an accessible signal, but it warns that the same heart-rate number can mean different physiological load for people with different resting blood pressure, organic heart disease, training history, sleep, and autonomic state.

The practical synthesis is not "do not exercise"; it is to match intensity to risk. Ordinary exercisers can use heart rate as one cue, but long-term high-intensity participants, people chasing performance, people with hypertension or known heart disease, and people whose symptoms or recovery look abnormal may need cardiopulmonary exercise evaluation and a clinician-guided exercise prescription.

VOL.51 adds the post-infection boundary. Fever resolution or a negative test does not prove that heart-rate response, breathing, or exercise capacity has normalized. VOL.43 adds a general progression lesson through an older hypertensive patient's severe pressure and heart-rate response after intense badminton: training history, age, baseline control, exertion, and symptoms matter together. A gradual return or build-up is appropriate when capacity is improving, while pallor, numbness, nausea, dizziness, chest tightness, inability to lie flat, disproportionate breathlessness, persistent tachycardia, irregular palpitations, faintness, or syncope should pause progression and trigger assessment.

## Key Claims
- Heart rate is a useful and easy-to-monitor exercise signal, but it is not a complete cardiovascular safety assessment.
- Known organic heart disease changes the decision from generic exercise advice to individualized professional guidance.
- Baseline blood pressure matters: two people at the same exercise heart rate may have very different exercise blood-pressure load.
- The heart and lungs may not warn as clearly as sore limbs, so risk can accumulate through volume, intensity, pressure, and recovery mismatch.
- Sleep deprivation, late high-intensity training, and sympathetic arousal can make exercise less restorative and increase recovery debt.
- Frequent exercisers, endurance enthusiasts, competition-oriented participants, and near-professional trainees may benefit from cardiopulmonary exercise testing and exercise prescription.
- Body-shape or performance goals can encourage pushing through fatigue, while older age, uncontrolled hypertension, limited training history, acute infection, and incomplete recovery can narrow the safe training range.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide a universal heart-rate ceiling, blood-pressure threshold, return-to-sport protocol, testing schedule, or training-zone table. They are public cardiology discussions that push high-risk, high-ambition, older-hypertensive, or post-infectious exercise decisions toward qualified assessment. VOL.43's badminton case and daily blood-pressure-peak claims, and VOL.51's myocarditis timing, recovery ranges, and anecdotal rhabdomyolysis case, remain source-scoped. Emergency symptoms, known disease, abnormal recovery, or concerning exercise response remain outside generic podcast advice.

## What Changed
- Added post-infectious graded return and symptom-based pause criteria.
- Added a cardiovascular safety boundary for exercise advice: wearables and heart rate help, but baseline disease, blood pressure, sleep, and cardiopulmonary testing may decide the safe training range.
- Added older-adult hypertension, gradual progression, and symptom-based stopping to the exercise boundary.
