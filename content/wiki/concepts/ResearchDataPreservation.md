# Research Data Preservation

## Definition

Research data preservation is the durable retention of raw data, metadata, methods, codebooks, media, and interpretive records so later investigators can verify, reanalyze, or reinterpret a study.

## Current Synthesis

The episode shows preservation as part of scientific method rather than clerical aftercare. The [[SydneyDietHeartStudy]] survived because a former assistant kept a nine-track tape; the larger [[MinnesotaCoronaryExperiment]] survived because [[IvanFrantz]] retained records that [[RobertFrantz]] later recovered from a basement. In both cases, individual stewardship preserved an opportunity that ordinary institutional disposal might have erased.

Survival of a medium is not enough. Reanalysis also requires documentation, compatible hardware or migration, participant and variable definitions, intervention records, outcome coding, and enough provenance to distinguish the original design from later analytic choices. Preservation creates the option for correction; it does not guarantee that a later analysis is complete or correct.

## Key Claims

- Raw data and supporting documentation preserve opportunities for verification and new analysis.
- Obsolete or fragile media can make scientific evidence disappear even when a study was large or expensive.
- Individual custodians sometimes preserve records that institutions fail to retain.
- Theses, codebooks, protocols, and publication history are needed to interpret data, not merely the data carrier itself.
- Reanalysis can reveal neglected outcomes, but later analytic choices remain open to scrutiny.
- Ethical preservation must account for participant privacy, consent, access, and responsible reuse.

### Sydney recovery

- [[the-basement-tapes-the-basement-tapes]] reports that a former assistant preserved the 458-participant Sydney trial on nine-track tape.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not reconstruct chain of custody, data completeness, format migration, consent, privacy governance, metadata quality, or whether every original record survived. Preserved data can still be biased, incomplete, misread, or analyzed selectively. Modern stewardship should rely on documented institutional systems rather than assume that private basements will rescue important evidence.
