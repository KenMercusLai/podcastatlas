# Dual-Use Tech Infrastructure Targeting

Dual-use tech infrastructure targeting is the risk pattern where private cloud, AI, chip, data-center, and platform infrastructure becomes a wartime target because it is used by military, intelligence, or national-security customers. [[tech-20260403-0403-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260403-0403-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[PareshDave|Paresh Dave]]'s discussion of [[Iran]]-linked threats against U.S. technology companies with infrastructure in the Middle East.

The concept extends [[DigitalInfrastructureWarRisk]] by explaining why a nominally commercial facility can be treated as strategic infrastructure. If [[AmazonWebServices|AWS]], [[Microsoft]], [[Google]], [[Nvidia]], [[Palantir]], or [[Apple]] supply systems used in attacks, targeting claims can shift from military bases to vendors, data centers, chips, cloud regions, and business-service operations.

## Key Claims
- Dual-use exposure rises when private technology systems support military or intelligence workflows.
- The relevant infrastructure includes physical data centers and regional cloud operations as well as model-serving, chips, identity systems, and data pipelines.
- Commercial site selection optimized for latency, customer growth, and funding access can conflict with wartime resilience.
- Attribution and legality remain source-sensitive: a threat or reported incident should be recorded as a claim unless independently verified.
- The business risk includes service interruption, staff evacuation, insurance pressure, partner concern, customer trust loss, and expensive recovery after limited physical damage.
