# Meiji Capitalism–Imperial Entanglement

## Definition

Meiji capitalism–imperial entanglement is the episode's account of corporate modernization, national defense, ethical reform, and colonial expansion developing as connected rather than separable projects.

## Current Synthesis

Japan's forced reopening and fear of Western domination made institutional catch-up appear existential. [[ShibusawaEiichi]] represents joint-stock organization, infrastructure, and a Confucian effort to moderate capitalist conflict. Yet the same modernizing economy expanded through opportunities in Korea, while strategic and racial arguments helped normalize empire in China and Korea.

## Key Claims

- Western pressure made modernization a security project as well as an economic one.
- Joint-stock companies helped organize industrial catch-up.
- Confucian cooperation was offered as an ethical correction to perceived Western capitalist harms.
- National modernization and overseas empire reinforced one another.
- Ethical language cannot by itself establish fair labor or colonial practice.

## Evidence

- Strategic pressure: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] links Meiji reform to fear that Japan might share China's subordination.
- Corporate institution: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] presents Shibusawa as a major promoter of joint-stock enterprise.
- Ethical aspiration: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] describes a Confucian model of cooperation among investors, workers, and managers.
- Imperial benefit: [[277-japan-samurai-and-shoguns-glt3851919583]] connects Shibusawa's opportunities to Japanese control of Korea.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode compresses Meiji political economy and does not demonstrate how consistently Shibusawa's ethics shaped practice. Its chronology, firm list, company count, Korean profits, and strategic explanations of empire remain source-scoped; explanation does not excuse colonial coercion or racism.

## What Changed

- Created a synthesis that keeps institution-building, ethical aspiration, security pressure, and imperial benefit together.
