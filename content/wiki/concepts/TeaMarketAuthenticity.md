# Tea Market Authenticity / 茶叶市场真实性

## Definition

Tea market authenticity is the degree to which a sold tea's name, origin, cultivar, harvest, process, blend, age, storage history, available volume, and sensory presentation form a traceable and internally consistent claim.

## Current Synthesis

Authenticity is not guaranteed by price, packaging, fluency, ceremony, or a prestigious label. The episode's strongest test is material consistency: a tiny core site cannot supply a mass market at claimed purity; a tea cannot credibly date from a period when its production had stopped; and a name tied loosely to leaf shape or processing is easier to borrow than one tied to distinctive plant material and appearance.

Retail tasting complicates verification. Skilled brewing, favorable water, selective samples, and even substitution can produce a shop experience that buyers cannot reproduce. Honest authentication therefore needs provenance and volume checks alongside comparative tasting. Terms such as tribute tea, mother tree, core site, pure cultivar, ancient tree, and pre-Qingming can describe real distinctions, but only when their operational meaning and evidence are explicit.

## Key Claims

- Authenticity is a chain of compatible claims rather than a synonym for prestige or price.
- Claimed volume should be tested against land, yield, harvest, labor, and processing capacity.
- Documented production gaps constrain credible vintage and continuity claims.
- Seller-controlled water, brewing, sampling, and language can widen information asymmetry.
- Geographic and cultivar terms need clear boundaries because they can shift from descriptors into commodity names.
- Comparative samples and field access improve judgment but do not remove status and expectation bias.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Large volume does not by itself prove fraud because geographic indications, processing styles, blends, and product categories can legitimately broaden over time. Blending is not inherently inferior, and seller skill can create a genuinely better brew. The source does not provide audited production data, chemical authentication, or a universal legal definition for each tea name.

## What Changed

- Established a chain-of-claims model for tea authenticity.
- Added volume plausibility, production interruption, and seller-controlled tasting as practical tests.
- Separated legitimate blending and category evolution from false purity claims.
