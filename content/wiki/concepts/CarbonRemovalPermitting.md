# Carbon Removal Permitting

Carbon removal permitting is the regulatory approval work needed before a physical carbon-removal pathway can operate at meaningful scale. [[peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted-peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted]] adds the concept through [[CharmIndustrial|Charm Industrial]] and [[BioOilCarbonRemoval|bio-oil carbon removal]].

[[PeterReinhardt]] says Charm's largest barrier over five years was regulatory: federal and state agencies took about four years to agree on the permitting pathway for bio-oil injection, and the first Louisiana permit then took about 14 months. The episode frames this as a [[HardTechRegulatoryPathfinding]] problem because the company had to fit a novel removal method into existing underground-injection and environmental rules.

## Key Claims
- Novel carbon-removal methods can be delayed when regulators do not know which existing pathway should apply.
- Permitting delay is a commercialization constraint because equipment, feedstock, customers, and financing cannot fully scale while the storage pathway is unresolved.
- Faster permitting still has to preserve public trust: storage permanence, monitoring, environmental safety, and local impacts decide whether acceleration is legitimate.
- For climate tech, policy work can be core operating work rather than an afterthought after engineering.
