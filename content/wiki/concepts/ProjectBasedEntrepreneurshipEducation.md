# Project-Based Entrepreneurship Education

## Definition
Project-based entrepreneurship education uses a real problem, a bounded delivery cycle, user contact, iterative building, and public presentation to develop a learner's agency and judgment rather than treating venture success as the only valid outcome.

## Current Synthesis
The [[Technovation]] case treats entrepreneurship as developmental practice. Young people work in teams on problems they can observe, conduct user interviews, test and revise a solution, and face a deadline. Technology and AI matter as tools, but the educational object is the participant's experience of acting under uncertainty, incorporating feedback, and helping another person.

The model is stronger than artifact-first project work when it preserves conceptual learning, reflection, mentorship, user reality, and the possibility of failure. An app can be abandoned while the learner retains courage, self-knowledge, systems thinking, and evidence of having built something. Reported career and income effects are suggestive but do not establish causality without the program's longitudinal methods and comparison groups.

## Key Claims
- Real problems can connect technical learning to empathy, local knowledge, and consequential use.
- Deadlines, teams, user interviews, testing, and iteration make uncertainty part of the curriculum.
- The durable educational outcome may be identity and agency even when a project does not win or survive.
- Mentors can support process and courage without supplying every technical answer.
- Broadening access requires active safeguards so already underrepresented learners are not displaced.
- Outcome claims must separate participant testimony and program-reported associations from causal evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- One successful prototype or compelling finalist story does not establish representative learning quality or long-term impact.
- Project work can become shallow performance if the artifact, pitch, or AI-generated output replaces conceptual understanding, user validation, reflection, or learner ownership.
- The source's reported longitudinal outcomes may reflect selection, access, mentor support, survivorship, or other factors in addition to the program.
- Opening a girls-focused intervention to boys may expand reach while also changing group dynamics and resource allocation; promised safeguards need outcome evidence.

## What Changed
- Established learner identity and agency as the model's primary outcome.
- Added lived experience as a mechanism for discovering overlooked problems.
- Added explicit evidence and inclusion boundaries around program scaling.
