# Strategic Title Concession / 战略性名位让步

Strategic title concession / 战略性名位让步 is the late-Han status tactic [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1005-sunce-zhuding-zaoshi-shi-xingge-jueding-mingyun-lpqnere2cwcrngggyezd-bsqahx4|Hanji 1005]] extracts from [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s handling of [[YuanShao|袁绍]]. After [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s court reproaches Yuan Shao and then appoints him taiwei and Marquis of Ye, Yuan Shao resents that Cao Cao still holds the higher great-general title. Cao Cao fears an early confrontation and yields that visible rank, accepting sikong plus acting chariots-and-cavalry general instead.

The concession does not mean Cao Cao abandons power. The episode presents it as a way to trade symbolic precedence for time, legitimacy, and room to consolidate around [[Xudu|许都]]. Rank becomes negotiable because the strategic need is not to win the title contest immediately, but to keep Yuan Shao from turning status resentment into premature conflict.

This concept sits inside the wiki's broader title-politics map. [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] explains why titles and symbols matter, while [[TitleRealityRestraint|名实相符式名号节制]] stresses keeping name and substance aligned. Strategic title concession covers a different move: temporarily giving up the superior title so practical capacity can continue growing.

## Key Claims
- Visible rank can be conceded without surrendering the underlying strategic position.
- A title dispute can become militarily dangerous when a stronger rival reads lower rank as humiliation.
- A weaker or consolidating actor may benefit from letting an opponent enjoy symbolic precedence.
- Concession works only if the actor retains real offices, resources, or institutional leverage elsewhere.
- The tactic depends on reading status emotion as part of strategy, not as separate court etiquette.
