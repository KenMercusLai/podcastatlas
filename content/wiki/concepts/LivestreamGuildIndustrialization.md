# Livestream Guild Industrialization / 直播公会工业化

Livestream guild industrialization / 直播公会工业化 describes the shift from low-cost individual livestreaming toward organized recruitment, training, styling, staging, operation, compliance, and review. In [[baiwan-ge-jingguan-shehui-tingshuo-ni-ye-xiang-dang-zhubo-lgd0yapclwvsessg7xnhaarpyiwy]], [[TianFeng|田峰]] contrasts earlier livestream guilds that screened many anchors after they had started with newer guilds that train anchors before they become successful.

The concept is important because it qualifies creator-economy freedom. Guilds can lower some individual burdens by providing makeup, equipment, stage design, practice, traffic support, and operational judgment, but they also raise fixed costs, intensify discipline, and make the anchor's performance part of a larger production system.

## Key Claims
- Industrialized guilds provide training, value guidance, styling, dance practice, image design, staging, and team support.
- The guild takes organizational risk: salaries, staff, equipment, venue, costume, traffic, and uncertain returns.
- Professionalization raises the competitive floor, so new entrants need more skill and discipline than the old "start with a phone" image suggests.
- Review meetings and SOPs make livestreaming closer to repeated production than spontaneous self-expression.
- The source frames guilds as both support structures and control structures.
