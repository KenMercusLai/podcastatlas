## Definition

Capacity-backed diplomatic hierarchy is the pattern where ritual language, seals, titles, gifts, and status performances cannot create or restore a hierarchy unless the regime has enough military, fiscal, logistical, and political capacity to make counterparties treat the rank order as consequential.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-674-ruoguo-wuwaijiao-gengshi-huangdi-liuxuan-you-duo-biequ-lu59astedsljoefnl6yr9d4ilirh|Hanji 674]] grounds the pattern through [[LiuXuanGengshiEmperor|更始帝刘玄]]'s Xiongnu mission. Liu Xuan sends Liu Sa and Chen Zun to return old Han-style seals and detained kin, hoping to restore the earlier Han-Xiongnu order after [[WangMang|王莽]]'s disruptions. The Xiongnu chanyu refuses the frame and says Han should instead honor Xiongnu because Xiongnu helped overthrow Wang Mang.

The diplomatic object is therefore not missing. The problem is enforcement credibility. Liu Xuan can perform restoration through seals, relatives, and old formulas, but he cannot make the chanyu accept lower status while Gengshi authority is visibly fragmented by Chimei pressure, Gongsun Shu's Shu claim, Liu Xiu's independent consolidation, and eastern regional breakaways.

Hanji 554 supplies an earlier, narrower Western Han variant. Han Chengdi's court is not as weak as Liu Xuan's Gengshi court, but the specific land demand still lacks enough capacity and authorization clarity. [[XiahouFanWesternHan|夏侯藩]] tries to use imperial will to pressure [[WuzhuliuChanyu|乌珠留单于]], yet the chanyu refuses after testing whether the demand is personal or official. Here the failed hierarchy is not a full restoration of status order; it is a demand that assumes Han prestige can obtain land without sufficient coercive leverage or public commitment.

Hanji 357-2 adds a Han Wudi-era distance variant. Even after Han battlefield victories and conspicuous displays of wealth, Western Regions states reportedly treat nearby [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] envoys more generously because retaliation can arrive quickly. The case separates aggregate imperial strength from local enforceability: prestige, victory, and stored wealth do not guarantee deference where transport, route security, and response time make coercive support remote.

Hanji 341-1 adds the corresponding formation-stage variant. [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]] can require the [[Wusun|乌孙]] ruler to receive Han gifts under Han protocol, and the combination of expensive gifts with recent anti-Xiongnu success makes refusal costly. Yet Wusun does not immediately accept relocation or alliance. It sends observers to inspect Han strength, separating ceremonial compliance from strategic commitment. Material display matters, but the counterpart still verifies whether displayed resources and battlefield reputation amount to usable protection.

## Key Claims

- Diplomatic hierarchy depends on actual regime capacity as well as inherited ritual precedent.
- Restoring old seals or kinship arrangements can fail when counterparties read the center as militarily weak.
- A counterpart can reverse the moral debt narrative if it can credibly claim that the weakened center needed its help.
- Even a strong court can suffer a local capacity mismatch when it makes a claim without enough leverage, commitment, or boundary clarity.
- Aggregate power and battlefield victory do not automatically become local diplomatic leverage when distance and response time make enforcement uncertain.
- Gifts and ritual compliance can open a hierarchy claim without proving durable allegiance; counterparties may independently inspect whether capacity backs the display.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not be generalized into a universal rule that diplomacy is only force or that ritual and gifts never matter. Hanji 674 shows old forms failing under weak central capacity; Hanji 554 shows a narrower failure where Han prestige and implied imperial will cannot substitute for leverage and clear public commitment in a specific demand; Hanji 357-2 adds a distance-and-response-time mechanism but does not measure actual Western Regions decisions or prove that fear alone determines reception.

Hanji 341-1 is especially incomplete as an outcome test: later Han-Wusun marriage and military cooperation show that the opening did matter, but this episode alone does not isolate the causal weight of the ritual demand, gifts, observers, battlefield change, or later pressure. Other pages such as [[RecognitionBackedFrontierDiplomacy|Recognition-Backed Frontier Diplomacy]] and [[HanXiongnuHeqinPolicy|Han-Xiongnu Heqin Policy]] cover cases where recognition, gifts, kinship, or negotiated hierarchy can still matter when backed by credible incentives or constraints.

## What Changed

- Added Wusun's ceremonial compliance plus observer mission as a formation-stage test of whether gifts and rank claims have usable backing.
- Distinguished opening a hierarchy claim from securing durable alliance or obedience.
