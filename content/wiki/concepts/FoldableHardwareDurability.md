# Foldable Hardware Durability

## Definition
Foldable hardware durability is the ability of a folding device's hinge, display, and visible surface to tolerate repeated operation without mechanical failure or unacceptable degradation.

## Current Synthesis
The evidence separates mechanical endurance, visible surface quality, fleet reliability, and user confidence. [[JoannaStern]]'s purpose-built test opened and closed [[IPhoneDuo|iPhone Duo]] more than 32,000 times without a significant change in hinge behavior, but a groove remained visible. First-week owners then show a behavioral layer: failure reports and stress-test media led one host to avoid unfolding the phone, while another relied on after-sales service and used it normally. Neither response estimates actual failure probability, but perceived fragility can reduce the very opening behavior that gives a foldable value.

## Key Claims
- Fold-cycle testing can reveal hinge deterioration under repeated mechanical use.
- A stable hinge does not imply an invisible or unchanged folding surface.
- Visual acceptability depends on angle, background, lighting, and comparison with rival foldables.
- Controlled cycling is only one part of durability because real owners add impact, contamination, temperature, pressure, and handling variation.
- Durability evidence should be kept separate from whether the larger screen creates enough everyday value to justify price and compromise.
- Reliability communication and after-sales confidence affect use because anxiety can suppress opening before any device failure occurs.

## Evidence
- Mechanical-endurance evidence: [[tech-20261023-1023-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261023-1023-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports no significant hinge-operation change after more than 32,000 machine-driven folds.
- Visual-surface evidence: [[tech-20261023-1023-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261023-1023-mp-tech-pod-128]] says a groove remains visible under some viewing conditions, though Stern judged it less pronounced than competing foldable creases.
- Behavioral evidence: [[podcast176-v1-podcast176-v1]] reports that one owner minimized unfolding after seeing damage reports and durability tests, while another chose ordinary use backed by after-sales service.
- Failure-report boundary: [[podcast176-v1-podcast176-v1]] mentions damaged inner screens and hinge-area handling concerns without supplying a denominator, failure analysis, or verified fleet rate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One early test does not establish lifetime reliability, failure rates across production units, resistance to drops or debris, repair cost, or display behavior after months of mixed real-world use. Conversely, isolated damage reports and owner anxiety do not invalidate controlled endurance evidence or establish a defect rate. Stern's crease comparison is not a standardized cross-device measurement, and the first-week episode does not independently verify each reported failure.

## What Changed
- Added perceived fragility and after-sales confidence as behavioral parts of durability.
- Kept controlled fold endurance separate from unquantified early damage reports and fleet reliability.
