# Predetermined Meeting Process / 既定结果会议程序

Predetermined meeting process / 既定结果会议程序 is the procedural pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-900-zhichang-quanshu-dou-budong-gai-zenyang-shengcun-lmhjwosy4eswyhfppdghfsmfycqr|Hanji 900]] extracts from [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] convening the court over [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦皇太后]]'s burial. The host argues that a high-level meeting can be less a genuine search for the answer than a formal space where an already shaped decision is confirmed, displayed, or assigned to public responsibility.

The concept does not mean every meeting is fake. It names cases where authority, factional bargaining, and prior communication have already narrowed the outcome before participants are asked to speak. In the Dou Miao case, [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]]'s supervisory role and the eunuch-side stakes make the meeting part of [[ImperialBurialLegitimacy|帝后合葬名分]] and [[PalaceAccessMonopoly|宫廷接触垄断]], not a neutral ceremony.

This differs from [[ArbitraryAuthorityProcedure]] because the rules need not be absurd or openly unstable. The danger is subtler: procedure looks deliberative, but actors still need to read whether the real decision has moved elsewhere.

## Key Claims

- Formal deliberation can ratify a decision already built through private communication and power alignment.
- A meeting can distribute responsibility, create record, or pressure dissenters even when the likely outcome is known.
- Participants need to read who convened the meeting, who supervises it, and which prior interests are already locked in.
- The late-Han burial dispute shows that ritual procedure can become a political test before the debate even begins.
