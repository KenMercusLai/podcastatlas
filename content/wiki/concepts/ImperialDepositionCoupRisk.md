# Imperial Deposition Coup Risk / 废立政变风险

Imperial deposition coup risk / 废立政变风险 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-941-shenchu-guanchang-ta-jing-xiang-ganfan-huangdi-liu-fqiz9pi7u6cltrn-cjmjz-r|Hanji 941]] as the reason [[CaoCao|曹操]] and [[HuaXin|华歆]] reject [[WangFenLateHan|王芬]]'s plan. The episode treats killing eunuchs as already dangerous, but replacing [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] with [[HefeiMarquisLateHan|合肥侯]] multiplies the risk because force, secrecy, legitimacy, and successor acceptance all have to succeed together.

The concept is distinct from later [[CoercedImperialReplacement|胁迫式废立]]. In Hanji 941, Wang Fen does not yet control the court, ceremony, seal, or coercive process needed to make replacement legible. The plan remains an attempted ambush around imperial travel, so the failure appears before any formal title procedure can be staged.

It also differs from [[PalaceCoupViolenceCascade|宫廷政变暴力级联]]. That later concept tracks how one successful palace killing can trigger wider violence. Here the danger is pre-execution: an underprepared actor takes on a target so institutionally large that even capable historical precedents such as Yi Yin and Huo Guang are invoked as warnings, not easy models.

## Key Claims
- Removing or replacing an emperor is a different risk class from removing one court faction.
- A plot that depends on intercepting imperial movement must solve secrecy, command reliability, and successor legitimacy at the same time.
- Citing successful ancient precedents can mislead if the current actor lacks comparable authority, timing, or institutional control.
- Refusal can be strategic judgment rather than cowardice when the proposed action makes failure catastrophic.
- Omen interpretation can expose or halt a plot before action begins when it changes the ruler's route or summons the mobilizer back to court.
