# European Security Interest Split

## Definition

The European security interest split is the divergence between western European states whose industry, energy, and strategic traditions may favor eventual accommodation with Russia and central or eastern members whose histories make Russian power an immediate security threat.

## Current Synthesis

[[XiangLanxin|Xiang Lanxin]] describes an “old Europe / new Europe” divide. France and Germany are presented as more likely over time to restore some relationship with [[Russia]], while Poland, the Baltic states, and other former Warsaw Pact countries interpret Russia through partition, occupation, and Soviet domination.

The split helps explain why “Europe” cannot be treated as one security actor. It affects threat perception, defense spending, energy choices, diplomatic room, and the feasibility of [[EuropeanDefenseAutonomy]]. The labels remain coarse, however, and can hide disagreement within each country or changes produced by war.

## Key Claims

- Historical experience shapes present threat perception and acceptable compromise.
- Energy and industrial interests can pull western European states toward renewed engagement.
- Frontline and former Soviet-bloc states attach higher costs to accommodation with Russia.
- Divergent threat perception constrains unified European defense policy.
- “Old” and “new” Europe are heuristics, not fixed or homogeneous blocs.

## Evidence

- Historical divergence: [[508-xiang-lanxin-tan-guoji-guanxi-shiye-xia-de-zhongmeiou-jinxi-cong-sitanfo-xiaojituan-dao-junshi-kaiensizhuyi-lqrjnxctet30isfmgt-wyavdwffu]] connects Polish and Baltic threat perception to partition and Soviet control.
- Western incentive: [[508-xiang-lanxin-tan-guoji-guanxi-shiye-xia-de-zhongmeiou-jinxi-cong-sitanfo-xiaojituan-dao-junshi-kaiensizhuyi-lqrjnxctet30isfmgt-wyavdwffu]] links German industrial strain to the loss of Russian energy and predicts some eventual engagement.
- Intellectual comparison: [[508-xiang-lanxin-tan-guoji-guanxi-shiye-xia-de-zhongmeiou-jinxi-cong-sitanfo-xiaojituan-dao-junshi-kaiensizhuyi-lqrjnxctet30isfmgt-wyavdwffu]] contrasts Brzezinski's Polish historical lens with Kissinger's balance-of-power realism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode offers an interpretive typology, not comparative polling, policy, trade, or defense data. France, Germany, Poland, the Baltic states, and other members each contain internal disagreement, and wartime behavior may not fit the two-bloc shorthand.

- Created a focused concept for divergent European threat histories and material interests.
