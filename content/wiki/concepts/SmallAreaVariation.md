# Small-Area Variation

## Definition

Small-area variation is a persistent difference in practice, service use, or belief among nearby geographic or institutional communities that is not fully explained by differences in underlying need.

## Current Synthesis

In [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt|The Tipping Point Revisited: An Excerpt]], [[JohnWennberg]]'s Vermont maps show neighboring medical-service areas with sharply different procedure rates despite no corresponding difference in illness. The episode extends the pattern to later physician-visit differences and to vaccination rates clustered around Waldorf schools.

The pattern is evidence of local structure, not a complete explanation. Shared professional judgment, institutional selection, incentives, capacity, referral patterns, peer influence, and community identity can all produce clustering. [[BoundedSocialContagion]] is one interpretation of how such local norms may form and persist.

## Key Claims

- National or state averages can conceal large differences among neighboring service areas and institutions.
- Differences in underlying illness or patient demand do not necessarily account for treatment-rate variation.
- Stable local clusters make shared professional or community norms a plausible explanatory factor.
- A geographic pattern alone cannot distinguish influence from selection, resource distribution, policy, or measurement effects.

## Evidence

### Medical procedure geography

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] reports three- to fivefold differences in some procedures and childhood tonsillectomy rates ranging from roughly one-fifth to more than three-fifths across communities.

### End-of-life treatment intensity

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] contrasts reported physician-visit totals in Minneapolis and Los Angeles to illustrate durable treatment-culture differences.

### Institutional vaccination clusters

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-an-excerpt]] reports very low vaccination rates at several Waldorf schools amid much higher rates at many other California private schools.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied file does not include Wennberg's studies, methods, denominators, risk adjustment, uncertainty estimates, or the school datasets. Procedure use and vaccination are different outcomes with different causal structures. Local variation may identify overuse, underuse, preference-sensitive care, selection, access, capacity, regulation, or data artifacts; it does not automatically show inappropriate medicine, irrational belief, or direct peer transmission.

## What Changed

- Established a qualified cross-domain pattern linking treatment geography and institution-level vaccination clustering.
- Separated observation of local variation from causal claims about contagion.
