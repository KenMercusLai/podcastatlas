# Space Launch Capacity Bottleneck

Space launch capacity bottleneck is the constraint that appears when demand for orbital launches grows faster than usable pads, spaceports, licensing throughput, and launch-site upgrades. In [[tech-20260818-tech-pod-128-tech-20260818-tech-pod-128]], [[MarketplaceTech]] frames this as the hidden infrastructure problem behind a fast-growing commercial space sector.

The episode's key point is that [[ReusableRocketEconomics]] and cheaper satellites do not remove the need for physical launch capacity. [[CommercialSatelliteConstellations]], especially [[Starlink]], create repeated launch demand, while crowded pads around the [[FloridaSpaceCoast]], scarce [[SpaceportSitingConstraint|suitable sites]], and contested [[SpaceLaunchEnvironmentalReview]] can still slow expansion.

## Key Claims
- Launch capacity is not only rocket supply; it includes pads, range scheduling, airspace, ports, licensing, upgrades, and public tolerance.
- [[CommercialSatelliteConstellations]] turn launch from occasional bespoke missions into a repeat infrastructure need.
- Record launch counts can signal strength and scarcity at the same time when existing pads become crowded.
- More spaceports could reduce bottlenecks, but siting and environmental review make expansion politically and geographically constrained.
- If U.S. capacity remains tight, companies and countries may look to foreign sites, ocean platforms, or new coastal regions.
