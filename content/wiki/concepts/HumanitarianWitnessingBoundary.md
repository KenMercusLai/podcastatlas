# Humanitarian Witnessing Boundary / 人道见证边界

## Definition
Humanitarian witnessing boundary / 人道见证边界 is the ethical limit of listening, documenting, and carrying stories outward when the witness cannot directly solve the immediate suffering in front of them.

## Current Synthesis
The source grounds the concept in Wang Hongrui's field encounter with a mother who pawned her WFP aid card to feed her children. Wang can tell her he hopes to carry her voice outward and seek more resources, but she answers that this does not solve dinner tonight.

The concept does not dismiss witness work. The episode itself is built on the belief that public attention and donations can matter. The boundary is that storytelling is a slower institutional route, while hunger, safety, and family survival are immediate.

## Key Claims
- Humanitarian witnessing can create attention and donor support, but not instant relief.
- Personal charity can be ethically tempting and operationally risky in crowded aid settings.
- Institutional rules protect fairness and safety while also making individual encounters feel morally inadequate.
- The person being witnessed may reasonably judge the promise of future attention against present hunger.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide an aid-worker ethics manual or compare field protocols. This concept is grounded in one narrated encounter and should be used as an interpretive frame for testimony, not as a universal rule.
