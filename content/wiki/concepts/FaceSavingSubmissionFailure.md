## Definition

保面子式归降失败 / Face-saving submission failure is a political pattern where a defeated or exposed actor seeks forgiveness, rank preservation, or alliance entry without visibly accepting lower status. The actor uses apology, justification, delay, or repeated testing to keep dignity and bargaining space, but that ambiguity makes the proposed submission non-credible.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-703-donghan-kaiguo-hou-zui-xianhe-de-jiazu-pingsha-shi-ta-lgg-fra8ostp2xsuugph6o-ilzac|Hanji 703]] now supplies the warning-phase prelude. [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]] tells [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|Wei Xiao]] that continued resistance will waste earlier Han loyalty, renew civilian suffering, and leave him with poor alternatives such as [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]] or [[LuFangEasternHan|Lu Fang]]. Wei Xiao ignores the warning, while [[LiangTongEasternHan|Liang Tong]] publicly breaks away and [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] helps [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] pressure Wei Xiao's circle through insider persuasion and letters.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-704-weixiao-wo-da-ni-shi-bu-dui-dan-ni-ping-sha-hai-shou-lgi-cjyledgzldrie6kwpvejwsls|Hanji 704]] then grounds the apology-stage pattern. After defeat, Wei Xiao still has a survivable route: Ma Yuan reads his military position as structurally untenable, and Liu Xiu offers to preserve his title and stipend if he restrains himself and sends another hostage. Wei Xiao's letter, however, mixes臣节 language with self-exculpation and uses loyalist rhetoric to justify evasion.

The failure is therefore not simply that Wei Xiao lacks options. It is that he cannot make the authority transfer visible enough for Liu Xiu to trust. Liu Xiu's reply sets a hard boundary: accept restraint and hostage-backed submission or stop replying. Wei Xiao then turns to [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]], choosing a worse strategic patron because that move preserves more face in the short term.

## Key Claims

- Apology can fail when it protects the apologizer's self-image more than it transfers authority.
- The failure can begin before apology, when warnings from former peers and insiders are heard but not converted into a credible camp change.
- Viable political exit usually requires a costly credibility mechanism such as restraint, hostage, ritual, or direct submission.
- Advisers can see structural imbalance before the principal can emotionally accept loss of rank.
- A receiver can distinguish repentance from bargaining by requiring a clear costly signal and ending the reply cycle.
- Face-saving ambiguity can push an actor toward a strategically worse patron if that patron preserves dignity better than the victor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on two host-interpreted episodes, and the host's evaluation of Wei Xiao's psychology is strong. The sources do not fully reconstruct Wei Xiao's factional fears, local obligations, military geography, or why a hostage demand might have seemed unsafe. Hanji 703's Zhang Xue, Ren Yu, Wang Yuan, Qiang leaders, and Wei Xun details remain source-scoped. Hostage-backed terms can be a credible submission mechanism, but they can also reduce autonomy and expose family members, so the pattern should not be read as a universal claim that surrender terms are always fair.

- Preserved Liu Xiu's hostage-and-title counteroffer as the concrete credibility test.
