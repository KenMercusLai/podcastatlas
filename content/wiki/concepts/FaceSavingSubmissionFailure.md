## Definition

保面子式归降失败 / Face-saving submission failure is a political pattern where a defeated or exposed actor seeks forgiveness, rank preservation, or alliance entry without visibly accepting lower status. The actor uses apology, justification, delay, or repeated testing to keep dignity and bargaining space, but that ambiguity makes the proposed submission non-credible.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-704-weixiao-wo-da-ni-shi-bu-dui-dan-ni-ping-sha-hai-shou-lgi-cjyledgzldrie6kwpvejwsls|Hanji 704]] grounds the pattern through [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|Wei Xiao]]. After defeat, Wei Xiao still has a survivable route: [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] reads his military position as structurally untenable, and [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] offers to preserve his title and stipend if he restrains himself and sends another hostage. Wei Xiao's letter, however, mixes臣节 language with self-exculpation and uses loyalist rhetoric to justify evasion.

The failure is therefore not simply that Wei Xiao lacks options. It is that he cannot make the authority transfer visible enough for Liu Xiu to trust. Liu Xiu's reply sets a hard boundary: accept restraint and hostage-backed submission or stop replying. Wei Xiao then turns to [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]], choosing a worse strategic patron because that move preserves more face in the short term.

## Key Claims

- Apology can fail when it protects the apologizer's self-image more than it transfers authority.
- Viable political exit usually requires a costly credibility mechanism such as restraint, hostage, ritual, or direct submission.
- Advisers can see structural imbalance before the principal can emotionally accept loss of rank.
- A receiver can distinguish repentance from bargaining by requiring a clear costly signal and ending the reply cycle.
- Face-saving ambiguity can push an actor toward a strategically worse patron if that patron preserves dignity better than the victor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one host-interpreted episode, and the host's evaluation of Wei Xiao's psychology is strong. The source does not fully reconstruct Wei Xiao's factional fears, local obligations, military geography, or why a hostage demand might have seemed unsafe. Hostage-backed terms can be a credible submission mechanism, but they can also reduce autonomy and expose family members, so the pattern should not be read as a universal claim that surrender terms are always fair.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 704.
- Defined Wei Xiao's failure as non-credible, face-protective submission rather than simple lack of an available exit.
- Added Liu Xiu's hostage-and-title counteroffer as the concrete credibility test.
