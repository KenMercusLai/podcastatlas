## Definition

保面子式归降失败 / Face-saving submission failure is a political pattern where a defeated or exposed actor seeks forgiveness, rank preservation, hostage-backed breathing room, or alliance entry without visibly accepting lower status. The actor uses apology, justification, delay, partial concession, or repeated testing to keep dignity and bargaining space, but that ambiguity makes the proposed submission non-credible.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-698-wangchao-xingshuai-qishilu-weiao-de-yihan-yu-jinchao-zhi-jing-lqim2hbjtdzb0wwmsaxcyzj7u8ff|Hanji 698]] supplies the pre-apology layer for [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|Wei Xiao]]. [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]] tests Wei Xiao through the proposed attack on [[GongsunShuEasternHan|Gongsun Shu]], but Wei Xiao refuses, avoids Luoyang, and sends [[WeiXunEasternHan|Wei Xun]] as a hostage instead of personally transferring authority. The episode shows why a partial concession can postpone rupture while still failing as credible submission.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-703-donghan-kaiguo-hou-zui-xianhe-de-jiazu-pingsha-shi-ta-lgg-fra8ostp2xsuugph6o-ilzac|Hanji 703]] preserves the warning-phase prelude. [[DouRongEasternHan|Dou Rong]] tells Wei Xiao that continued resistance will waste earlier Han loyalty, renew civilian suffering, and leave him with poor alternatives such as Gongsun Shu or [[LuFangEasternHan|Lu Fang]]. Wei Xiao ignores the warning, while [[LiangTongEasternHan|Liang Tong]] publicly breaks away and [[MaYuanEasternHan|Ma Yuan]] helps Liu Xiu pressure Wei Xiao's circle through insider persuasion and letters.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-704-weixiao-wo-da-ni-shi-bu-dui-dan-ni-ping-sha-hai-shou-lgi-cjyledgzldrie6kwpvejwsls|Hanji 704]] then grounds the apology-stage pattern. After defeat, Wei Xiao still has a survivable route: Ma Yuan reads his military position as structurally untenable, and Liu Xiu offers to preserve his title and stipend if he restrains himself and sends another hostage. Wei Xiao's letter, however, mixes臣节 language with self-exculpation and uses loyalist rhetoric to justify evasion.

The failure is therefore not simply that Wei Xiao lacks options. It is that he cannot make the authority transfer visible enough for Liu Xiu to trust. Liu Xiu's reply sets a hard boundary: accept restraint and hostage-backed submission or stop replying. Wei Xiao then turns to Gongsun Shu, choosing a worse strategic patron because that move preserves more face in the short term.

## Key Claims

- Apology can fail when it protects the apologizer's self-image more than it transfers authority.
- The failure can begin before apology, when partial compliance and deferential language replace a clear alignment choice.
- Hostage or court-service concessions can postpone conflict without making submission credible if the principal preserves independent command.
- Viable political exit usually requires a costly credibility mechanism such as restraint, hostage, ritual, or direct submission.
- Advisers can see structural imbalance before the principal can emotionally accept loss of rank.
- A receiver can distinguish repentance from bargaining by requiring a clear costly signal and ending the reply cycle.
- Face-saving ambiguity can push an actor toward a strategically worse patron if that patron preserves dignity better than the victor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on host-interpreted Wei Xiao episodes, and the host's evaluation of Wei Xiao's psychology is strong. The sources do not fully reconstruct Wei Xiao's factional fears, local obligations, military geography, or why a hostage demand might have seemed unsafe. Hanji 698 leaves Liu Yong, Shanglinyuan logistics, exact Wei Xun title wording, and Wang Yuan's later service source-scoped. Hanji 703's Zhang Xue, Ren Yu, Qiang leaders, and later Wei Xun details remain source-scoped. Hostage-backed terms can be a credible submission mechanism, but they can also reduce autonomy and expose family members, so the pattern should not be read as a universal claim that surrender terms are always fair.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 698 as the pre-apology layer: refusal to attack Gongsun Shu, hostage-only compliance through Wei Xun, and ignored submission advice.
- Preserved Hanji 703 as the warning-phase prelude: Dou Rong's ignored counsel, Liang Tong's break, Ma Yuan's persuasion mission, and Wei Xun family-risk pressure.
- Preserved Hanji 704 as the apology-stage failure after earlier warnings and camp fracture.
- Preserved Liu Xiu's hostage-and-title counteroffer as the concrete credibility test.
