# Marriage Alliance War Exposure / 联姻同盟战场暴露

Marriage alliance war exposure / 联姻同盟战场暴露 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1016-sanguo-luanshi-li-chengui-jiazu-de-mingyun-jueze-ltuckdcxpyxk-xmzp8rt-2gjfzcs|Hanji 1016]], where [[YuanShu|袁术]] sends [[HanYinLateHan|韩胤]] to arrange a marriage between his son and [[LyuBu|吕布]]'s daughter. The marriage is not treated as private family business. It would bind Lü Bu to Yuan Shu's imperial claim and make [[Xuzhou|徐州]] part of Yuan Shu's northern strategic path.

[[ChenGui|陈珪]]'s advice defines the concept. He argues that if Lü Bu joins Yuan Shu through marriage, Lü Bu will carry the reputation cost of siding with an unjust claimant and Xuzhou will become exposed to the war Yuan Shu is drawing toward himself. The source therefore treats marriage diplomacy as an exposure mechanism: it can create alliance, but it can also move battlefield risk onto the weaker or geographically exposed participant.

This concept differs from [[WarringStatesMarriageDiplomacy|战国联姻外交]] by focusing less on ruling-house relation-building and more on local war exposure after a marriage path is accepted. It also sits near [[InterestStructuredAllianceFragility|利益结构式联盟脆弱性]] because the marriage attempt fails once Lü Bu's grievance, legitimacy concern, and local danger are reframed against Yuan Shu.

## Key Claims
- A marriage alliance can transfer not only prestige and kinship ties but also military exposure.
- Local actors may reject a marriage path because it turns their territory into another power's forward line.
- The moral status of the stronger partner matters: alliance with an illegitimate claimant can impose reputation cost on the recipient.
- Marriage diplomacy remains fragile when the recipient's immediate security and resentment point in the opposite direction.
