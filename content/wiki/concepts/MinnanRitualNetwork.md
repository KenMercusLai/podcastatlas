# Minnan Ritual Network / 闽南仪式网络

Minnan ritual network is the episode's core model for understanding [[YongningAncientCity|永宁古城]]. In [[ep249-minnan-gucheng-youshenji-chuantong-yishi-beihou-de-xiangtu-wangluo-lpk1vb9-zmgmuous1nyzefgapgww]], deity processions, ancestor worship, temples, lineage halls, deity committees, donations, routes, and local territorial categories are treated as one working social system rather than as separate "folk customs."

The network has two overlapping bases. Deity networks are territorial: gods protect areas, travel through routes, receive incense, and can arbitrate public conflict. Lineage networks are genealogical: ancestral halls, surname groups, donation rosters, and return-home ceremonies maintain descent memory and social obligation. The episode's synthesis is that these two bases reinforce each other but also compete over prestige, seats, money, routes, and who counts as an insider.

This is not only a belief system. The source repeatedly treats ritual as public organization: committees handle money, audits, scheduling, supervision, and disputes; overseas or Hong Kong-based descendants return for lineage work; and local rules such as "角落" or "姓许的女儿" decide belonging through shared memory and negotiation rather than through formal administrative categories alone.

## Key Claims
- Ritual can be social infrastructure when it organizes money, labor, return, trust, and public negotiation.
- The same temple can be sacred space, heritage object, committee resource, and lineage-politics arena.
- Deity networks and lineage networks are different ordering systems; their overlap can produce both cooperation and conflict.
- Local categories may be stable in practice before they are cleanly explainable as written rules.
- The concept extends [[MinnanMaritimeCommercialCulture]] inward: the older Minnan branch emphasized maritime risk, diaspora, and trade, while this source emphasizes hometown ritual, lineage, and territory.
