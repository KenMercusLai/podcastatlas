# Vibrio vulnificus Wound Escalation / 创伤弧菌伤口升级处置

## Definition

Vibrio vulnificus wound escalation is the time-sensitive boundary for recognizing when a seafood puncture or marine-exposed wound may be progressing from a small local injury toward severe soft-tissue infection, sepsis, or tissue loss.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses fish, crab, and shellfish punctures to show why wound size is a poor proxy for risk. The useful decision pattern joins exposure, host susceptibility, speed, pain, skin change, and systemic illness: rapid worsening over hours matters more than whether the original puncture looked trivial.

Initial washing and ordinary wound care do not replace escalation. Rapidly increasing redness, warmth, swelling, severe pain, blistering, spreading change, or systemic illness after seafood or seawater exposure should prompt emergency assessment, especially when liver disease, diabetes, immunosuppressive treatment, or reduced immunity raises vulnerability.

## Key Claims

- Seafood punctures and marine-exposed wounds can create a route for severe infection even when the skin injury initially appears small.
- Rapid progression, disproportionate pain, redness, heat, swelling, blisters, spreading tissue change, or systemic illness are escalation signals rather than watch-and-wait reassurance.
- Chronic liver disease, cirrhosis, diabetes, immunosuppression, and reduced immunity are presented as important vulnerability factors.
- Severe infection can compromise vessels, muscle, fascia, and systemic circulation, requiring urgent multidisciplinary treatment.
- Debridement, drainage, reconstruction, or amputation are consequences of advanced disease, not household treatment choices.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode is public medical education, not a diagnostic or antibiotic protocol. It does not establish that every seafood puncture contains *Vibrio vulnificus*, and redness or pain has many possible causes. Exposure type, water conditions, wound depth, vaccination history, comorbidities, examination, microbiology, local treatment standards, and current clinical guidance determine care; rapidly worsening symptoms or systemic illness require emergency evaluation.

## What Changed

- Created a specific exposure-speed-host-risk framework for severe seafood and marine wound infection.
- Separated emergency treatment and reconstruction from household wound care.
