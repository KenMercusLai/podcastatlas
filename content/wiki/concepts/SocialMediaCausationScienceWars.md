# Social Media Causation Science Wars

Social media causation science wars are disputes over whether social media causes youth harms or merely correlates with them. [[tech-20260716-0716-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260716-0716-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[GaiaBernstein]]'s comparison between current platform debates and earlier cigarette-harm disputes.

[[tech-20260812-0812-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260812-0812-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the internal-document version through [[TikTok]]. The episode does not resolve population-level causation, but it says TikTok internally reviewed thousands of videos [[ChaseNasca]] watched and had a safety feature intended to reduce harmful filter bubbles. That makes company knowledge and feature exposure part of the causation fight, not only external epidemiology.

The source says evidence is increasing around depression, anxiety, attention, cognitive development, sleep, and obesity, while technology companies fund research denying responsibility and internal memos allegedly show awareness of harms. The concept is therefore less about one definitive study and more about how evidence, funding, public-health institutions, and internal company knowledge interact under litigation pressure.

[[tech-20260205-0205-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260205-0205-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the trial-level version through [[EricGoldman]]. He says jurors would have to assess whether "social media addiction" exists and whether services such as [[Meta]] and [[YouTube]] are legally responsible for harms connected to it. That makes causation both a scientific dispute and a [[PlatformLegalCausation]] problem.

## Key Claims
- Causation disputes are predictable when a profitable product is alleged to create diffuse health harms.
- Industry-funded research can preserve uncertainty even as independent and institutional evidence accumulates.
- Internal documents matter because they can show what companies believed their products caused or risked.
- Internal safety reviews can add case-specific evidence about what a platform saw, tested, withheld, or changed.
- Public-health institutions can shift the legal environment by naming a problem before every causal pathway is fully settled.
- Jury trials translate causation disputes into practical questions about experts, injury categories, and legally assignable responsibility.
