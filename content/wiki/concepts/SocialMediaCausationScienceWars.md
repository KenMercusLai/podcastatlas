# Social Media Causation Science Wars

## Definition
Social media causation science wars are disputes over whether social media causes youth harms or merely correlates with them, and over how much evidence policy makers and courts should require before regulating platform design.

## Current Synthesis
The current wiki synthesis is that causation disputes are real but are also strategically useful to platform companies. The tobacco-litigation comparison in [[tech-20260716-0716-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260716-0716-mp-tech-pod-128]] shows harms remaining contested while public-health institutions, attorneys general, internal company knowledge, and cumulative litigation increase pressure. The Chinese-language synthesis in [[400-shejiao-meiti-de-dayancao-shike-shui-gai-wei-qingshaonian-de-chengyin-fuze-32ada8c4]] makes the same point in policy terms: "science is not settled" does not mean "science has proved no harm," especially when platform data is not fully open and when social/psychological causation is harder to prove than some physical-health risks.

The internal-document branch in [[tech-20260812-0812-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260812-0812-mp-tech-pod-128]] works through [[TikTok]]. The episode does not resolve population-level causation, but it says TikTok internally reviewed thousands of videos [[ChaseNasca]] watched and had a safety feature intended to reduce harmful filter bubbles. That makes company knowledge and feature exposure part of the causation fight, not only external epidemiology.

The concept is therefore less about one definitive study and more about how evidence, funding, public-health institutions, internal research, and legal proof interact under litigation pressure. The trial-level version in [[tech-20260205-0205-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260205-0205-mp-tech-pod-128]] works through [[EricGoldman]]: jurors must assess whether "social media addiction" exists and whether services such as [[Meta]] and [[YouTube]] are legally responsible for harms connected to it. That makes causation both a scientific dispute and a [[PlatformLegalCausation]] problem.

## Key Claims
- Causation disputes are predictable when a profitable product is alleged to create diffuse health harms.
- Industry-funded research can preserve uncertainty even as independent and institutional evidence accumulates.
- Internal documents matter because they can show what companies believed their products caused or risked.
- Internal safety reviews can add case-specific evidence about what a platform saw, tested, withheld, or changed.
- Public-health institutions can shift the legal environment by naming a problem before every causal pathway is fully settled.
- Jury trials translate causation disputes into practical questions about experts, injury categories, and legally assignable responsibility.
- Platform data asymmetry makes the evidence problem harder because outside researchers may not be able to inspect the same exposure, engagement, or safety data companies hold.

## Evidence
- Tobacco analogy and public-health pressure: [[tech-20260716-0716-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260716-0716-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes causation fights, industry-funded research, internal knowledge, and attorney-general litigation as recurring public-health litigation dynamics.
- Platform data and policy threshold: [[400-shejiao-meiti-de-dayancao-shike-shui-gai-wei-qingshaonian-de-chengyin-fuze-32ada8c4]] argues that incomplete scientific certainty should not be treated as proof of safety, especially when platforms control much of the relevant data.
- Case-specific internal evidence: [[tech-20260812-0812-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260812-0812-mp-tech-pod-128]] connects TikTok's internal video review and withheld safety feature to a more concrete causation chain.
- Trial-level proof problem: [[tech-20260205-0205-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260205-0205-mp-tech-pod-128]] frames causation as a jury question involving addiction, mental-health harm, and legally assignable responsibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current sources do not settle population-level causation. They preserve uncertainty around family context, school context, preexisting mental-health conditions, social support benefits, and differences among users and services. The 声东击西 source argues for a lower policy threshold than scientific certainty, but that is a policy judgment rather than a completed causal proof. Internal documents can show knowledge or concern without by themselves proving every user's injury was legally caused by the platform.
