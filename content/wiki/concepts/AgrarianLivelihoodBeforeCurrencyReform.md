## Definition

Agrarian livelihood before currency reform is the Hanji 843 policy-diagnosis pattern where poverty should be read first through food, clothing, farming damage, official seizure, tax/corvee burdens, and household recovery time, before treating coin form or coin weight as the binding problem.

## Current Synthesis

The concept is grounded in [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]]'s Taixue memorial in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]]. When some officials propose casting larger coins to address peasant poverty, Liu Tao rejects the diagnosis: commoners lack grain and cloth because disasters damage fields, officials seize production, and repeated extraction prevents recovery. The source therefore treats a technical monetary proposal as a misdiagnosis when the real constraint is subsistence and administrative predation.

This page does not deny that money and fiscal systems matter. Its narrower synthesis is that in this episode, currency reform is not the relevant first lever because household survival depends on production, access, and state restraint. The same source places Zhu Da's rebellion and Chen Gui's frontier-relief program nearby, strengthening the link between poverty, official behavior, and disorder.

## Key Claims

- Poverty diagnosis should begin with whether households have food and clothing, not with the metal or weight of money alone.
- Coin reform can become a false technical fix when the binding constraints are agricultural damage, seizure, and extraction.
- Disaster pressure and administrative predation compound each other: locusts and insects reduce production while officials and tax demands strip what remains.
- Real recovery requires reducing requisition, plunder, taxes, and corvee so households can rebuild capacity.
- Misreading livelihood distress as a currency problem risks missing rebellion triggers and frontier disorder.

## Evidence

- Food-and-clothing priority: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] says Liu Tao argues that people care more about eating and being clothed than about heavier coins.
- Misdiagnosed coin lever: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] says the large-coin proposal is not implemented after Liu Tao argues the root problem lies elsewhere.
- Disaster plus extraction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] links locusts, insects, fields, cloth, taxes, and corrupt officials in Liu Tao's explanation.
- Rest-for-recovery remedy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] says Liu Tao recommends stopping repeated requisition and plunder so people can recover.
- Disorder adjacency: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] places Zhu Da's corrupt-magistrate revolt and Chen Gui's Bingzhou/Liangzhou tax/corvee remission program in the same source layer.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is source-bounded to one late-Han episode and does not prove that currency policy is irrelevant in all agrarian crises. It only says that Hanji 843 treats the proposed large-coin reform as a wrong first diagnosis for this poverty field. The source also gives podcast-level synthesis rather than a full independent fiscal history, so coinage mechanics, prices, and tax rates remain outside the current evidence.
