## Definition

Epistemic infrastructure power is the capacity to shape collective decisions by controlling scarce expertise, research facilities, data, instruments, verification processes, and channels through which knowledge becomes publicly actionable.

## Current Synthesis

[[SalomonsHouse|Salomon's House]] does more than possess facts. It chooses research priorities, operates facilities unavailable to ordinary citizens, decides which discoveries to publish, authenticates unusual claims, predicts hazards, and advises the public. Its power therefore arises from the full chain connecting inquiry to interpretation and action.

The episode's modern analogy concerns asymmetric dependence. Governments may hold lawmaking and coercive powers yet still rely on technology firms for cloud systems, AI, cybersecurity, or defense expertise that public institutions cannot quickly reproduce. This does not erase state authority or prove corporate domination, but it can make regulation a negotiation among unequally informed and mutually dependent actors.

## Key Claims

- Control of facilities and expertise can create political leverage without elections, legislation, taxation, or armed force.
- Research agendas shape public possibility by deciding which capabilities receive resources and sustained attention.
- Disclosure power matters because an institution can affect what governments and publics know about its discoveries.
- Verification authority becomes especially consequential when outsiders cannot independently test claims or detect manufactured evidence.
- Dependence on privately controlled infrastructure can narrow a regulator's practical options even when formal legal authority remains intact.
- Epistemic power is strongest when knowledge production, interpretation, infrastructure, and public communication are concentrated together.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Expertise is not identical to unchecked rule. Governments retain legal, fiscal, procurement, investigative, and coercive tools; technical capacity is distributed across firms, universities, civil society, and states; and secrecy can sometimes protect safety or legitimate research. The source offers a literary-political analogy, not an empirical measurement of Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, or any government's dependence.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish infrastructure-backed knowledge power from formal political office.
- Added agenda setting, disclosure, verification, and regulator dependence as linked mechanisms.
