# Community Gunshot First Aid

## Definition

Community gunshot first aid is practical bystander training for recognizing and controlling life-threatening bleeding while alerting emergency services and waiting for professional responders.

## Current Synthesis

[[AbdullahPratt]] teaches an ABC sequence: alert 911 and communicate scene safety, locate the bleeding, and compress the wound. Students learn direct pressure, correct tourniquet use, exposure of the injury, improvisation with absorbent material, and the legal basis for helping.

The intervention treats time before professional arrival as a trainable community capacity. It can make care immediate and concrete, but it operates after violence has occurred and depends on scene safety, emergency dispatch, supplies, professional trauma care, and wider prevention systems.

## Key Claims

- Simple, rehearsed steps can improve a bystander's ability to act during the pre-arrival interval.
- Training should prioritize alerting emergency services, finding the bleeding source, and controlling blood loss.
- Confidence and practical rehearsal matter because knowledge that cannot be used under stress has limited value.
- Young people in highly affected communities can become capable helpers without being treated as substitute clinicians.
- Scene safety, legal clarity, supplies, dispatch, and professional follow-through are part of the intervention system.
- First aid may reduce downstream mortality but cannot prevent the shooting itself.

## Evidence

### Training model and reach

- [[guns-part-6-sin-is-the-failure-to-bother-to-care]] describes Pratt's ABC method and reports that his team trained more than 7,000 young people on Chicago's South Side.

### Use under pressure

- [[guns-part-6-sin-is-the-failure-to-bother-to-care]] recounts a teenage student calling 911, retrieving a medical kit, asserting her ability to help, and providing equipment after a shooting outside her home.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide a curriculum, outcome evaluation, comparison group, adverse-event record, or evidence that training reduces mortality at population scale. Bystanders should not enter unsafe scenes, and an episode summary cannot establish individualized medical or legal guidance. Post-injury response complements rather than replaces firearm policy, primary prevention, emergency medical services, or equitable trauma-center access.
