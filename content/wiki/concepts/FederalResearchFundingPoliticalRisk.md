# Federal Research Funding Political Risk

## Definition
Federal research funding political risk is the risk that research capacity, grant continuity, and early-career scientific work become unstable when federal funding is pulled into partisan legitimacy fights, administrative control, or contested definitions of what counts as real science.

## Current Synthesis
The current synthesis is not simply "more funding good, cuts bad." Earlier sources show that grant cuts, freezes, and legal uncertainty can damage labs, hiring, public-health projects, and strategic capacity before courts or Congress settle the formal question. The Kratsios source adds the administration's countercase: some funding is portrayed as politicized or low-productivity, so reallocation toward scientists, basic research, and tested funding mechanisms is presented as pro-science rather than anti-science.

## Key Claims
- Grant interruptions can harm research capacity before legality, appropriations, or later restoration are resolved.
- Research funding has strategic value because NIH, NSF, DARPA, universities, and national labs feed U.S. competition with China.
- Politically exposed topics can be targeted first, but administrative disruption can spill into less obviously ideological public-health and science projects.
- The Kratsios source adds a counterclaim that politicized grant criteria and stagnant output per dollar also create a pro-science case for funding reform.
- The unresolved test is whether reform improves scientific output while preserving labs, talent, and long-horizon research continuity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Kratsios's countercase does not erase the operational damage risk from sudden grant disruption. At the same time, the earlier risk pages should not assume every reallocation is anti-science; the funding-risk question is whether selection criteria, timing, evidence, and replacement mechanisms preserve or improve research capacity.
