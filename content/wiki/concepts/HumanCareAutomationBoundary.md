# Human-Care Automation Boundary / 人文照护自动化边界

## Definition
The human-care automation boundary distinguishes household tasks that technology may execute reliably from care relationships that still depend on touch, observation, emotional response, trust, context, and accountable human judgment.

## Current Synthesis
The episode divides domestic service into work directed at things and work directed at people. Cleaning appliances already change household routines and require workers to learn new tools, suggesting that automation often enters through task redesign and human-machine collaboration. Cleaning, cooking support, and other bounded chores may therefore automate earlier than infant care or companionship.

Person-focused care is not protected by mystique alone. Its current resistance comes from variable bodies, rapidly changing situations, attachment, tactile and emotional response, household values, and the need to decide when something is wrong. The best near-term frame is task substitution inside a continuing human role, with parents retaining primary responsibility rather than using either workers or machines to outsource the whole relationship.

## Key Claims
- Object-focused household tasks are more immediately automatable than relationship-intensive care.
- Automation often changes the worker's toolset before it removes the occupation.
- Infant care depends on touch, observation, emotional response, and context-sensitive escalation.
- Human care workers provide companionship and co-regulation that a task-completion metric may miss.
- Parents remain accountable for education and attachment even when workers or technology assist.
- Claims of near-term replacement or permanent human immunity both exceed the evidence in this source.

## Evidence
- Task distinction - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] separates person-focused maternity, childcare, and elder work from object-focused cleaning and appliance maintenance.
- Tool adoption - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] notes that robot vacuums, dishwashers, and other devices have already become tools domestic workers must learn.
- Care-resistance evidence - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] argues that newborns still need one-to-one tactile and emotional response and that person-focused service is harder to replace.
- Judgment evidence - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] includes a worker noticing an infant's abnormal condition and recommending medical evaluation.
- Responsibility boundary - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] says childcare workers are assistants and parents remain the first caregivers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is one practitioner's forecast, not a technical benchmark or labor-demand model. The episode does not test robotic manipulation, affective computing, remote supervision, liability, cost, or whether households accept machine-delivered care. It also should not romanticize poorly paid human emotional labor: difficulty automating a task does not guarantee dignity, fair wages, or good service.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish chore automation from relational-care substitution in domestic service.
