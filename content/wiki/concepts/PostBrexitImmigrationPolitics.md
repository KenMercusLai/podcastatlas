# Post-Brexit Immigration Politics

## Definition
Post-Brexit immigration politics is the conflict between the promise of sovereign migration control and the economic, administrative, demographic, and electoral consequences of how Britain actually uses that control.

## Current Synthesis
The earlier source shows that moving authority from Brussels to Westminster did not automatically reduce immigration: non-EU work, study, health, and labor-market routes helped drive a post-Brexit surge. The newest source adds a sharp reversal. Temporary arrivals are leaving, and restrictions imposed after political alarm may take net migration toward zero or below it.

The combined judgment is therefore cyclical rather than linear. Formal control permits both liberalization and restriction, but policy changes operate with lags and interact with temporary migration. Governments can first lose trust because numbers rise despite sovereignty promises, then create economic and service-pressure concerns by tightening more strongly than intended.

## Key Claims
- Brexit shifted the migration-policy lever to Westminster without guaranteeing lower migration.
- Work, study, health-care, university, and other domestic choices drove substantial non-EU inflows after free movement ended.
- Temporary inflows create later outflows, so annual net migration can reverse quickly even without a permanent demographic regime change.
- Restriction can restore a sense of control while weakening headline growth, fiscal forecasts, or labor supply.
- Public consent depends on pace, composition, visibility, and management, not only aggregate economic benefit.
- Small-boat crossings can remain politically dominant even when total net migration falls.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The newest source forecasts rather than confirms a durable period of net emigration. It does not supply the underlying statistical release or disaggregate students, dependants, workers, citizens, asylum routes, or sectoral effects. A lower total does not by itself establish higher living standards, stronger public consent, improved public services, or better fiscal outcomes; nor does a higher total establish the reverse.

## What Changed
- Replaced the one-way promise-versus-surge account with a full surge-and-reversal cycle.
- Added temporary migration and policy lags as explanations for rapid changes in net numbers.
- Added the risk that irregular migration becomes more salient as a share of a smaller total.
