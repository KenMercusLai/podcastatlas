# Greater Bay Area Airport Cluster / 大湾区机场群

## Definition
Greater Bay Area airport cluster is the regional aviation pattern in which Hong Kong, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, and nearby Pearl River Delta cities compete and cooperate through airport capacity, cargo, manufacturing, international rights, and passenger access.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats the Greater Bay Area as one of the world's densest airport fields. Hong Kong Chek Lap Kok, Guangzhou Baiyun, and Shenzhen Bao'an appear as large nearby airports with overlapping catchments but different institutional and logistics advantages.

Hong Kong's distinct role comes from international connectivity, free-port status, institutional environment, and high-value cargo routes serving Pearl River Delta manufacturing. Cathay's remote check-in and mainland-city routing further show that the airport cluster is experienced by passengers as a multi-city access system, not only as separate airports.

## Key Claims
- Dense regional airport systems create both competition and complementarity.
- Hong Kong's advantage in the cluster is tied to free-port status, international rights, cargo, and institutional environment.
- Pearl River Delta manufacturing strengthens air-cargo demand for high-end products and electronic components.
- Passenger access can stretch across city borders through remote check-in, ferry or ground links, and transfer pricing.
