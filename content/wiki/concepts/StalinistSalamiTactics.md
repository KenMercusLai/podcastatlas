# Stalinist Salami Tactics

## Definition

Stalinist salami tactics are the sequential elimination of political rivals and independent institutions, allowing a weak party to accumulate monopoly power slice by slice while each individual move appears narrower than an immediate total seizure.

## Current Synthesis

Postwar Hungary supplies the bounded case. [[MatyasRakosi|Mátyás Rákosi]]'s communists won only about 17 percent of the 1945 vote, so they entered coalition government and presented themselves as moderate while using Soviet backing to control the Interior Ministry and police. Prosecutions, accusations of fascism or conspiracy, institutional purges, forced party mergers, and the closure of independent associations removed opponents in sequence. By 1949, a new constitution and renamed ruling party formalized a monopoly that the electorate had not initially granted.

The mechanism joins institutional capture to calibrated escalation. Control of coercive and administrative nodes makes later removals easier; dividing opponents prevents a unified response; and legal or coalition forms can obscure the cumulative endpoint. The later purge of [[LaszloRajk|László Rajk]] and imprisonment of [[JanosKadar|János Kádár]] also show that the slicing logic can move inward after external rivals have been removed.

## Key Claims

- Sequential removal can convert minority support into monopoly without one openly declared abolition of pluralism.
- Early control of police, interior administration, courts, and personnel systems compounds later political advantage.
- Labeling isolated targets as fascists, conspirators, or traitors divides opposition and provides legal-political cover.
- Coalition participation and moderate presentation can coexist with a long-term monopoly strategy.
- Once external rivals are gone, purge logic can turn inward against party members and institution builders.
- The Hungarian case depended on Soviet occupation and backing and is not a context-free template for every authoritarian transition.

## Evidence

- Electoral minority and institutional capture: [[719-revolution-in-the-soviet-empire-the-hungarian-uprising-part-1-glt4398850202]] connects the 1945 vote, coalition strategy, Interior Ministry, police, prosecutions, and rival-party destruction.
- Formal monopoly: [[719-revolution-in-the-soviet-empire-the-hungarian-uprising-part-1-glt4398850202]] connects the cumulative removals to the 1949 socialist constitution and single legal party.
- Inward purge: [[719-revolution-in-the-soviet-empire-the-hungarian-uprising-part-1-glt4398850202]] connects Rajk's execution and Kádár's imprisonment to coercive institutions consuming communists after opposition parties were destroyed.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept describes a mechanism, not a claim that each step was centrally scripted in full detail or that institutions and opponents lacked agency. Soviet military presence and political pressure were decisive enabling conditions in Hungary, while postwar devastation and Hungary's Axis history constrained Western intervention. The episode does not provide a comparative sample sufficient to generalize the mechanism's frequency or necessary conditions.

## What Changed

- Established the concept from Rákosi's own reported label and the 1945-49 consolidation sequence.
- Extended the mechanism from inter-party elimination to later intra-party purge.
