## Definition

礼仪让步式核心诉求谈判 / protocol-concession core-demand bargaining is a diplomatic pattern in which an envoy accommodates the counterpart's symbolic or audience protocol to secure access and preserve face while retaining the sending side's substantive mandate.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-368-1-ewu-zhizhan-beihou-de-tanpan-boyi-lm1l-z62lcewag-x6kxwgyusukh|Hanji 368-1]] grounds the concept through [[WangWuWesternHan|王乌]]. After [[GuoJiWesternHan|郭吉]] is detained following a demand for either decisive battle or submission, Han Wudi chooses an envoy familiar with Xiongnu custom. Wang Wu leaves his tally outside, changes clothing, marks his face, and enters alone, allowing the chanyu to receive him within Xiongnu ritual expectations.

Those accommodations do not erase the political demand. Wang Wu still asks Xiongnu to submit and pairs that requirement with material generosity. The reported offer to send the chanyu's son to Chang'an is therefore an apparent bargaining breakthrough produced by separating symbolic form from substantive content.

The concept does not imply that every ritual concession is prudent or that every core demand survives softened presentation. Its usefulness lies in identifying three separate questions: what is merely protocol, what constitutes the mandate, and whether the counterpart's response is implemented rather than only promised.

## Key Claims

- Symbolic protocol and substantive bargaining terms can be separated, but only when the envoy knows which elements are negotiable.
- Cultural fluency can preserve access and reduce avoidable status conflict without requiring abandonment of the mandate.
- Face-saving accommodation can be paired with pressure or inducement rather than replacing them.
- A counterpart's promise is an intermediate result, not proof of a completed settlement.
- The same ritual act can signal tactical flexibility in one setting and political submission in another.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one short structured podcast summary. Guo Ji and Wang Wu do not provide a controlled comparison: their timing, instructions, audience conditions, and counterpart incentives may differ. The political meaning of the tally, clothing, and face marking is not independently established here, and the chanyu's promise may be delay, exploration, or tactical signaling rather than acceptance. The pattern therefore explains the episode's negotiating logic without proving a universal rule that ritual flexibility causes agreement.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 368-1's contrast between Guo Ji's ultimatum and Wang Wu's protocol-flexible mission.
- Added an implementation gate separating a promised concession from a completed settlement.
