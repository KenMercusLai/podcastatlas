## Definition

礼仪让步式核心诉求谈判 / protocol-concession core-demand bargaining is a diplomatic pattern in which an envoy accommodates the counterpart's symbolic or audience protocol to secure access and preserve face while retaining the sending side's substantive mandate. Access is only the first gate: commitments must then be clarified, staged, and verified before favorable language is treated as settlement.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 368-1 grounds the access mechanism through [[WangWuWesternHan|王乌]]. After [[GuoJiWesternHan|郭吉]] is detained following a demand for either battle or submission, Han Wudi chooses an envoy familiar with Xiongnu custom. Wang Wu leaves his tally outside, changes clothing, marks his face, and enters alone. He still presents Han's political demand and pairs it with material generosity, producing a reported offer to send the chanyu's son to Chang'an.

Hanji 368-2 supplies the missing implementation test. [[YangXinWesternHan|杨信]] refuses the same ritual accommodations but still receives an outdoor audience; the chanyu rejects the hostage condition and invokes older heqin terms. Ritual rigidity therefore affects the setting and tone, but the substantive disagreement is independent of tent access.

Wang Wu's renewed accommodation again produces favorable language, this time a proposed chanyu visit to Chang'an. Han acts on the report by preparing a residence, yet the promise is not implemented. The source presents both offers as strategic assurances intended to obtain more Han goods. Protocol concession thus remains useful for access and information, but it can also expose the sender to extraction when words are mistaken for costly commitment.

The combined pattern separates four gates: access, substantive clarity, reciprocal cost, and implementation. Cultural fluency helps chiefly at the first gate; it does not substitute for a mutually specified bargain, observable steps, or readiness for coercive fallback when talks fail.

## Key Claims

- Symbolic protocol and substantive bargaining terms can be separated, but access success does not prove substantive agreement.
- Cultural fluency can reduce avoidable status conflict while leaving deception, incentive divergence, and commitment failure intact.
- Ritual refusal may preserve office symbolism without eliminating dialogue, as Yang Xin's outdoor audience shows.
- Favorable words become credible commitments only when terms, reciprocal costs, and implementation steps are observable.
- Material inducement without verification can reward delay or strategic reassurance rather than purchase settlement.
- Military preparedness can become the fallback after diplomatic access and repair measures fail, but it does not retroactively validate earlier promises.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is grounded in two consecutive short podcast summaries of one Han-Xiongnu episode, not a controlled comparison. Guo Ji, Wang Wu, and Yang Xin act at different stages with different mandates, so their results cannot be attributed to ritual posture alone. Hanji 368-2's deception judgment comes from the transmitted narrative, but it does not establish Wang Wu's private knowledge or prove that every unfulfilled promise was false from inception. Nor does the later military response prove a universal rule that force is the only effective diplomatic language. The pattern is best used as a sequence of verification gates, not as a claim that accommodation is naïve or firmness automatically succeeds.

## What Changed

- Reclassified the hostage offer from an open apparent breakthrough to a failed implementation test.
- Added Yang Xin's outdoor audience to separate ritual access from substantive disagreement.
- Added reciprocal-cost and verification gates after cultural access.
- Added extraction risk when material inducement follows favorable words without staged commitment.
