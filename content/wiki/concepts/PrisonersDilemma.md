# Prisoner's Dilemma / 囚徒困境

## Definition

Prisoner's dilemma is a [[GameTheory|game-theory]] structure in which each participant has an individually protective reason to defect under uncertainty, even though mutual defection leaves both worse off than sustained cooperation.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded sources apply the dilemma at two levels. [[79-gewei-lingdao-danfan-zan-xuedian-boyilun-690004262]] uses it to explain workplace conflict and price wars: actors can rationally copy a damaging move because restraint is costly when competitors may not reciprocate. Repeated interaction, credible signals, enforceable consequences, and reciprocal response can change those incentives.

[[080-quhua-maboyong-taibai-jinxing-youdianfan-p2-zhe-cai-shi-xiyouji-heishenhua-lol40ork2v5byoc75flyicbl88ko]] supplies a fictional interrogation case. Li Changgeng and Guanyin are questioned separately and told or led to believe the other may already have disclosed their Yellow Wind settlement. Each could reduce immediate personal exposure by defecting, but consistent non-betrayal preserves both parties and converts a tactical partnership into demonstrated trust. The case emphasizes that cooperation is not merely prior goodwill: it becomes more credible after surviving an incentive-compatible test.

## Key Claims

- Collectively bad outcomes can emerge even when each actor responds rationally to uncertainty and downside risk.
- A threat that the other participant has already defected can manufacture urgency without supplying reliable evidence.
- One-shot moral appeals are weak when defection remains privately safer and difficult to punish.
- Repeated interaction, credible signals, enforceable consequences, and reciprocal response can make cooperation more stable.
- Mutual non-defection under real pressure creates stronger evidence of trustworthiness than an untested promise.
- Leaving or redesigning a low-value game can be better than repeatedly demanding virtue inside a damaging incentive structure.

## Evidence

### Price competition and repeated cooperation

- [[79-gewei-lingdao-danfan-zan-xuedian-boyilun-690004262]] uses workplace conflict and publishing price wars to show how individually defensive moves can create a worse shared equilibrium, while tit-for-tat and repeated feedback can support cooperation.

### Separated questioning and alliance formation

- [[080-quhua-maboyong-taibai-jinxing-youdianfan-p2-zhe-cai-shi-xiyouji-heishenhua-lol40ork2v5byoc75flyicbl88ko]] interprets the separate questioning of Li Changgeng and Guanyin as an attempt to induce disclosure by making each fear that the other has already defected.
- [[080-quhua-maboyong-taibai-jinxing-youdianfan-p2-zhe-cai-shi-xiyouji-heishenhua-lol40ork2v5byoc75flyicbl88ko]] treats their matching accounts as evidence that a transactionally useful relationship has become a more durable alliance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The exact prisoner's dilemma has a specific payoff structure; not every conflict, interview, price cut, or trust problem satisfies it. Repeated interaction can stabilize retaliation or collusion as well as beneficial cooperation, and apparent non-defection can reflect shared concealment rather than public value. The interrogation example is a podcast interpretation of fictional events, not evidence about real investigative practice or a formal reconstruction of every payoff.

## What Changed

- Added separated questioning as a mechanism that can create perceived defection risk.
- Added successful non-betrayal under pressure as evidence that can deepen an alliance.
- Qualified cooperation by noting that stable coordination is not automatically transparent or socially beneficial.
