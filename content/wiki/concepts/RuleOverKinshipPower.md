# Rule Over Kinship Power / 法度压过亲缘特权

Rule over kinship power / 法度压过亲缘特权 is the governance pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1006-guojia-buman-yuanshao-weihe-que-neng-guishun-caocao-lup-p-inz1sdlhpnaggqjp0eavar|Hanji 1006]] draws from [[ManChong|满宠]] and [[CaoHong|曹洪]]. Cao Hong's clients repeatedly break the law in Xu, Man Chong arrests them, Cao Hong seeks protection through kinship access, and Man Chong executes the offenders before that access can turn into exemption.

The episode treats [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s praise for Man Chong as the decisive signal. Cao Cao is not merely admiring severity; he is showing that his own clan network cannot openly neutralize local law. That supports official confidence because magistrates and legal officers can believe enforcement will not be reversed whenever a powerful relative complains.

This is not modern rule of law. The concept names a power technique inside a personal military-political camp: legal severity becomes useful because it disciplines kin, restrains retainers, balances clan generals against officials, and makes the ruler's order more predictable. Its nearest neighbors are [[LegalistRulerTechnique|法家君术]], [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|因功赏罚]], and [[EliteFiscalLawCompliance|贵族税法同遵]], but this source is specifically about kinship privilege inside Cao Cao's own organization.

## Key Claims
- Legal authority becomes credible only when the ruler's kin and their dependents are visibly subject to it.
- Backing a severe official against a relative can strengthen the ruler's control over both clan forces and the bureaucracy.
- The source reads law as a political tool for balance and trust, not as an apolitical standard independent of power.
- The case complements talent recruitment: lower-status or non-clan officials have reason to serve when rules are not always overridden by family access.
