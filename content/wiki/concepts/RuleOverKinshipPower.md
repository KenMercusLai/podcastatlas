## Definition

Rule over kinship power / 法度压过亲缘特权 is the governance pattern where law or public office becomes credible when a ruler, commander, or high official lets enforcement stand against people protected by royal kinship, clan, household, or marriage ties.

## Current Synthesis

This is not modern rule of law. The concept names a power-order technique inside personal courts and military-political camps: legal authority becomes useful when it disciplines private access, restrains protected dependents, and reassures officials that enforcement will not automatically be reversed by a powerful relative.

Hanji 392-1 adds the Western Han royal-core variant through [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], [[LongluePrincessWesternHan|隆虑公主]], and [[ZhaoPingjunWesternHan|赵平君]]. Zhao is not merely a dependent of a powerful family; he is Wudi's nephew and son-in-law, and his mother has already paid for future leniency. Wudi's decision to execute him after he kills an attendant makes the rule visible precisely because the pressure for exception is emotionally and politically strong.

Hanji 721 adds the early Eastern Han imperial-household enforcement variant through [[DongXuanEasternHan|董宣]], [[HuyangPrincessEasternHan|湖阳公主]], and [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]]. Dong Xuan executes a murderer shielded by the princess household, refuses forced apology, and survives because Liu Xiu finally accepts the case as a public-governance problem rather than a private complaint by his sister.

Hanji 1006 supplies the Cao-clan client case through [[ManChong|满宠]] and [[CaoHong|曹洪]]: Cao Hong's clients break the law, Man Chong executes them before kinship access can create exemption, and [[CaoCao|曹操]] praises the action. Hanji 1022 adds a wife-side kinship case through [[ZhaoYanLateHan|赵俨]] and [[LiTongLateHan|李通]]: Zhao Yan executes Li Tong's wife's uncle, and Li Tong refuses to let private pleading weaken public duty.

The durable synthesis is that legal credibility depends on the powerful side accepting limits. Enforcement against ordinary people proves little; enforcement against protected households works as a signal only when the higher patron declines to reverse, punish, or privately avenge the official. Hanji 392-1 is the strongest current emotional-pressure case because the same ruler who feels family grief is also the actor who refuses legal exemption.

## Key Claims

- Legal authority becomes credible only when the ruler's kin, close clients, or marital dependents are visibly subject to it.
- Backing a severe official against a protected household strengthens the center's control over both privileged families and bureaucratic enforcement.
- The protected actor can be royal-core, royal-household, clan-based, or marriage-side; all routes try to convert private access into public exemption.
- The pattern works through power as much as law: the rule holds because a higher patron chooses not to override the official.
- The concept complements talent recruitment because non-kin officials have reason to serve when rules are not always reversed by family access.
- Prior payment, deathbed trust, or family grief can intensify the exemption claim, but they do not make the law signal credible unless the ruler still lets punishment stand.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not be expanded into liberal equality before law. In these sources, enforcement remains selective and ruler-centered; its political force comes from visible discipline inside a court or camp, not from an independent legal order outside personal power. Hanji 392-1 is told admiringly and does not prove consistent royal-kin enforcement across Han Wudi's reign. Hanji 721 also shows the contingency clearly: Liu Xiu first threatens Dong Xuan and only later chooses to back him. In Hanji 1006 and Hanji 1022, the punished people are dependents or wife-side relatives, not the central powerful figures themselves.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 392-1 as the Western Han royal-core variant through Han Wudi, Longlü Princess, and Zhao Pingjun.
- Preserved Hanji 721 as the early Eastern Han imperial-household variant through Dong Xuan, Princess Huyang, and Liu Xiu.
- Broadened the definition into a cross-case pattern covering royal-core, royal-household, clan-client, and marriage-side privilege.
- Preserved Hanji 1006 and Hanji 1022 as the Cao-camp evidence branches.
