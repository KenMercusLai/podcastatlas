# Rule Over Kinship Power / 法度压过亲缘特权

## Definition
Rule over kinship power / 法度压过亲缘特权 is the Cao-camp governance pattern where legal enforcement becomes politically credible when officials can punish people protected by family, clan, or marriage ties.

## Current Synthesis
This is not modern rule of law. The concept names a ruler-centered power technique inside a personal military-political camp: law becomes useful when it disciplines private access, restrains kinship-backed clients, and reassures officials that enforcement will not automatically be reversed by a powerful relative.

Hanji 1006 supplies the clan-client case through [[ManChong|满宠]] and [[CaoHong|曹洪]]: Cao Hong's clients break the law, Man Chong executes them before kinship access can create exemption, and [[CaoCao|曹操]] praises the action. Hanji 1022 adds a wife-side kinship case through [[ZhaoYanLateHan|赵俨]] and [[LiTongLateHan|李通]]: Zhao Yan executes Li Tong's wife's uncle, and Li Tong refuses to let private pleading weaken public duty.

## Key Claims
- Legal authority becomes credible only when the ruler's kin and their dependents are visibly subject to it.
- Backing a severe official against a relative strengthens the ruler's control over both military-clan power and bureaucratic enforcement.
- Marriage-side kinship pressure matters alongside direct clan privilege because both try to convert private access into public exemption.
- The source reads law as a political tool for balance and trust, not as an apolitical standard independent of power.
- The pattern complements talent recruitment because non-clan officials have reason to serve when rules are not always overridden by family access.

## Evidence
- Clan-client enforcement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1006-guojia-buman-yuanshao-weihe-que-neng-guishun-caocao-lup-p-inz1sdlhpnaggqjp0eavar|Hanji 1006]] presents Man Chong arresting and executing Cao Hong's lawbreaking clients before Cao Hong can obtain exemption.
- Ruler signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1006-guojia-buman-yuanshao-weihe-que-neng-guishun-caocao-lup-p-inz1sdlhpnaggqjp0eavar|Hanji 1006]] treats Cao Cao's praise as a signal that clan access will not always outrank local law.
- Wife-side kinship pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1022-luanshi-sanguo-sanwei-mingshi-de-shengcun-jueze-lgmbekomxwiu7hjnpxkxggyhvwaf|Hanji 1022]] has Li Tong's wife plead for her uncle and Li Tong refuse to subordinate public duty to private affection.
- Enforcement credibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1022-luanshi-sanguo-sanwei-mingshi-de-shengcun-jueze-lgmbekomxwiu7hjnpxkxggyhvwaf|Hanji 1022]] makes Zhao Yan's execution credible because Li Tong accepts the result rather than retaliating through rank.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not be expanded into liberal equality before law. In these sources, enforcement remains selective and ruler-centered; its political force comes from visible discipline inside Cao Cao's camp, not from an independent legal order outside personal power.
