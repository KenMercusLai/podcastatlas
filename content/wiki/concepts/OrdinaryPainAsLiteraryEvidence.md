# Ordinary Pain as Literary Evidence / 日常微痛作为文学证据

## Definition
Ordinary pain as literary evidence is the practice of treating small, socially dismissible injuries as consequential evidence about dignity, power, memory, and how a person learns to interpret the world.

## Current Synthesis
The episode distinguishes scale from significance. A child falsely accused over five cents, threatened until confessing, and denied an apology after the money is found may appear trivial beside public catastrophe, yet the private crying that follows records a durable lesson about authority and justice. Accent laughter, a bystander's smile, family members crying in a courtyard, and an unacknowledged sacrifice can work similarly.

Literature preserves these events because ordinary social narration often buries them as oversensitivity or inconvenience. Their evidentiary value lies not in proving a single universal causal chain, but in showing how power is felt at close range and how an apparently small interaction can reorganize trust, shame, belonging, or self-worth.

## Key Claims
- Socially small events can have large interpretive consequences for the person experiencing them.
- Refusal of repair—such as withholding an apology—can deepen harm beyond the initial mistake.
- Family power often becomes visible through repeated minor humiliations rather than only exceptional violence.
- Unintended laughter can still injure because impact does not require malicious intent.
- Literature can preserve experiences that institutional or family memory has normalized and discarded.

## Evidence
- False accusation and absent repair: [[lu_1rrenfntcaoijjpbqjlbcao_-lu_1rrenfntcaoijjpbqjlbcao_]] recounts the five-cent ice-pop story and the child's unheard crying after no apology arrives.
- Domestic power: [[lu_1rrenfntcaoijjpbqjlbcao_-lu_1rrenfntcaoijjpbqjlbcao_]] describes [[DuTaibai|杜太白]]'s father exploiting family members' vulnerabilities and a household repeatedly marked by tears.
- Shame and spectatorship: [[lu_1rrenfntcaoijjpbqjlbcao_-lu_1rrenfntcaoijjpbqjlbcao_]] connects accent ridicule and a fellow soldier's smile to injuries that exceed the apparent event.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not establish that every slight causes lasting trauma, nor does it convert memory into independently verified fact. Effects vary by context, repetition, repair, support, and later interpretation. Literary attention can restore significance, but it can also overread causality if it ignores ambiguity or competing recollections.

## What Changed
- Established scale and significance as separate dimensions of harm.
- Added absent apology, spectatorship, and repetition as mechanisms that can intensify ordinary injury.
- Framed literature as preservation rather than automatic proof of a causal diagnosis.
