# Orexin Agonists

Orexin agonists are drugs that mimic orexin, a brain neuropeptide the source describes as involved in wakefulness and the boundary between sleep and waking. In [[wake-up-haul-an-ozempic-moment-for-the-brain-6a85798d1ad8716bb6078744]], [[ShaileshChitnis]] presents them as a possible next major drug class after [[GLP1Agonists]], but keeps the broad-use claim early and uncertain.

The first grounded use case is [[NarcolepsyWakeSleepBoundary]], with [[Ovoperextone]] named as [[TakedaPharmaceutical]]'s drug. The commercial excitement comes from possible future application to depression, ADHD, and addictions, plus market estimates such as [[MorganStanley]]'s cited 2035 scenario.

## Key Claims
- Orexin agonists are framed as wakefulness drugs before they are framed as broad "everything drugs."
- The [[Ozempic]] comparison is an analogy about category expansion, not evidence that orexin will match [[GLP1Agonists]] in scale or breadth.
- [[EmmanuelMignot]]'s narcolepsy research supplies the source's discovery bridge from sleep biology to drug development.
- Company interest from [[TakedaPharmaceutical]], [[Alkermes]], [[EliLilly]], and [[SentessaPharmaceuticals]] turns orexin into a market and M&A story.
- The source explicitly warns that plausible drug ideas often fail before reaching the market.
