# Comparative Judgment Assessment

## Definition

Comparative judgment assessment measures extended work by aggregating repeated pairwise decisions about which of two responses is better.

## Current Synthesis

In the episode, [[DaisyChristodoulou|Daisy Christodoulou]] presents comparative judgment as a way to preserve holistic professional judgment while improving consistency and speed. A teacher need not translate every feature of an essay into a detailed rubric score; many simpler comparisons can be combined into a scale.

The method addresses one side of [[ExamStandardizationValidityTradeoff]] by standardizing the aggregation process rather than fully standardizing the evaluator's reasoning. Its value still depends on sampling, judge quality, task design, aggregation, and validation against the intended construct.

## Key Claims

- Pairwise choices can be cognitively simpler than absolute rubric scoring.
- Many judgments can be aggregated into a ranked measurement scale.
- Holistic comparison may preserve qualities that detailed checklists fragment.
- Reliability and validity remain empirical properties, not automatic consequences of the method.

## Evidence

- Procedure: [[84-exams-glt6507641896]] describes teachers selecting the better of two pieces of writing across many comparisons.
- Claimed performance: [[84-exams-glt6507641896]] reports Christodoulou's view that the method is more reliable, valid, and quick than traditional rubric marking.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not provide study designs, effect sizes, failure cases, inter-rater composition, scale-construction details, or independent replication. Pairwise ranking can improve consistency without proving that the task measures the right knowledge or that all candidates receive equitable treatment.
