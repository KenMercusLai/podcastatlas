# Matcha Supply Transition

## Definition

Matcha supply transition is the slow conversion of tea land, plants, labor, equipment, and market positioning toward matcha after demand changes.

## Current Synthesis

Global matcha demand gives Japanese growers a revenue opportunity but cannot instantly reverse decades of declining domestic green-tea consumption and productive capacity. [[unrest-in-peace-misery-in-gaza-145c0cf22e24027ee45e02b7352ac088]] says Japanese tea acreage has contracted by about one-third in two decades, farmers are ageing without successors, brewed-tea and matcha production require different methods and equipment, and new plants need at least five years to mature. [[Kagoshima]] shows how suitable land and investment can accelerate adjustment, while [[China|China's]] lower-cost scale pushes [[Japan]] toward differentiated flavor, origin, and craft rather than volume leadership.

## Key Claims

- A demand boom cannot immediately recreate land, labor, plants, or processing capacity lost during a long decline.
- Switching tea categories is a capital and agronomic transition, not a simple relabeling of existing output.
- Long plant-maturation cycles make rapid supply response costly and uncertain.
- Mechanizable geography and prior investment create uneven regional outcomes.
- Chinese scale weakens Japan's mass-market position while increasing the appeal of premium regional differentiation.
- Terroir language creates value only if distinctive varieties, origin, flavor, and craft are traceable.

## Evidence

- Capacity contraction: [[unrest-in-peace-misery-in-gaza-145c0cf22e24027ee45e02b7352ac088]] reports a roughly one-third decline in Japanese tea-growing area over two decades and widespread succession problems.
- Conversion lag: [[unrest-in-peace-misery-in-gaza-145c0cf22e24027ee45e02b7352ac088]] says matcha needs different cultivation and processing equipment and that new plants require at least five years to mature.
- Regional variation: [[unrest-in-peace-misery-in-gaza-145c0cf22e24027ee45e02b7352ac088]] identifies [[Kagoshima]]'s flat land and investment as advantages.
- Competitive pressure: [[unrest-in-peace-misery-in-gaza-145c0cf22e24027ee45e02b7352ac088]] describes China as the largest exporter with roughly 70% of world supply and lower-cost scale.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The acreage, export-share, price, and global-supply figures are source-reported and not independently verified here. Premium positioning may improve margins but does not guarantee consumer education, authenticity, farm succession, ecological resilience, or enough demand to support all producers.

## What Changed

- Established matcha growth as a slow capacity-conversion problem rather than a demand-only story.
- Added regional mechanization and Chinese scale as constraints on Japan's response.
- Framed premium terroir as a conditional strategy rather than an automatic defense.
