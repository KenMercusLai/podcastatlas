# Ancient Chinese Book Materiality / 中国古籍物质性

## Definition
Ancient Chinese book materiality is the reading of guji as physical artifacts as well as carriers of text: paper, binding, format, edition, layout, marks, production method, and handling history shape what the book means.

## Current Synthesis
The current [[BowuzhiPodcast|博物志]] sources show why ancient books need material explanation. The Hangzhou episode emphasizes visitor legibility through labels for format, binding, layout, and production. The writing-traditions episode adds how calligraphic copying, carving style, character design, paper quality, physical scale, hand-painted page furniture, storage, and patterns of use affect both aesthetic authority and survival.

## Key Claims
- Ancient books should be read as both textual content and material form.
- Labels must explain edition, format, line layout, printing features, and rare-book status for non-specialists.
- Binding and folding videos can make invisible production logic visible.
- Ming and Qing printed books cannot be understood only through author and title; period style, paper, typeface, and printing economy matter.
- Material explanation protects ancient-book exhibitions from becoming rows of opened but unreadable cases.
- High craft investment can improve durability and collecting value, while mass circulation may expand readership without ensuring later survival.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is grounded in two museum-led podcast episodes and focuses on Chinese ancient books. It complements [[OldBookMateriality]] but is more specialized in guji form, edition, production, storage, and printing history. Survival cannot be inferred from cost alone: use, copying, war, dispersal, and institutional custody also matter.

## What Changed
- Created a specialized ancient-Chinese-book materiality concept distinct from the older general old-book materiality page.
- Added craft investment, mass-circulation survival, manuscript scale, storage, and use as preservation variables.
