# Ancient Chinese Book Materiality / 中国古籍物质性

## Definition
Ancient Chinese book materiality is the reading of guji as physical artifacts as well as carriers of text: paper, binding, format, edition, layout, marks, production method, and handling history shape what the book means.

## Current Synthesis
The [[BowuzhiPodcast|博物志]] episode uses [[YiweiHangzhiExhibition|“意味杭之”]] to show why ancient books need material explanation. Visitors can understand a book only after the exhibition explains categories such as block-printed books, manuscripts, movable-type editions, annotated editions, binding forms, page layout, fish-tail marks, sky margin, ground margin, and line structure.

## Key Claims
- Ancient books should be read as both textual content and material form.
- Labels must explain edition, format, line layout, printing features, and rare-book status for non-specialists.
- Binding and folding videos can make invisible production logic visible.
- Ming and Qing printed books cannot be understood only through author and title; period style, paper, typeface, and printing economy matter.
- Material explanation protects ancient-book exhibitions from becoming rows of opened but unreadable cases.
