## Definition

政军能力的时代适配 / Political-Military Era Fit is the principle that an official's established strengths must be evaluated against the ruler's present objectives, threat model, tempo, reward system, and acceptable risk rather than treated as permanently useful in the same form.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-275-dapo-xiongnu-bubai-shenhua-weiqing-ruhe-zuodao-de-2-lpn6t8p8d0iumsd7gabohtevxxqo|Hanji 275]] develops the concept through [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]]. The episode credits him with steadiness, calculation, flexibility, and interest balancing, then associates those strengths with the conservative and restorative political style of the Wen-Jing period. Under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], however, frontier initiative, sustained readiness, and visible military results receive greater weight, while [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] represents the rising merit profile.

The concept does not turn generational change into destiny. Han Anguo's decline includes a testable operational error: he reportedly trusts a captive's claim that the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] have withdrawn and disperses troops before the danger has passed. Era fit therefore cannot excuse poor verification, and one failure cannot prove that all caution has become obsolete. The useful synthesis is that capability is relational: a strength can remain real while its weight, application, or institutional value changes, and adaptation still requires sound execution.

## Key Claims

- Proven ability is not timelessly portable; its value depends on current objectives, threats, tempo, and decision rights.
- A change in ruler or strategy can reprice traits such as caution, balance, aggression, readiness, and visible military merit.
- Era mismatch should not become a total explanation when a concrete judgment or execution failure better explains the immediate loss.
- New political demand can favor a different talent profile without proving that the older profile was always incompetent or is now useless everywhere.
- Adaptation requires both recognizing changed incentives and maintaining basic safeguards such as intelligence verification and force readiness.

## Evidence

Changing value of strengths:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-275-dapo-xiongnu-bubai-shenhua-weiqing-ruhe-zuodao-de-2-lpn6t8p8d0iumsd7gabohtevxxqo|Hanji 275]] contrasts Han Anguo's balancing and steadiness with Wudi's more aggressive military climate and Wei Qing's rising merit.

Concrete failure inside the broader transition:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-275-dapo-xiongnu-bubai-shenhua-weiqing-ruhe-zuodao-de-2-lpn6t8p8d0iumsd7gabohtevxxqo|Hanji 275]] says Han Anguo trusts a captive's report, demobilizes, and is exposed when the Xiongnu return.

Institutional consequence:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-275-dapo-xiongnu-bubai-shenhua-weiqing-ruhe-zuodao-de-2-lpn6t8p8d0iumsd7gabohtevxxqo|Hanji 275]] treats the transfer to Youbeiping and loss of central prospects as the political conversion of battlefield failure into marginalization.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one episode's interpretation of one official's final decline. The source does not compare a representative set of Wen-Jing and Wudi officials, prove that caution was generally maladaptive, or separate every effect of age, faction, kinship, military experience, prior reputation, and imperial preference. Han Anguo's intelligence and demobilization failure is sufficient to explain immediate punishment without assuming a complete historical rupture. The framework should therefore be used to ask how conditions changed, not to declare that people have fixed generational essences or that newness automatically equals fitness.

## What Changed

- Established the concept through the contrast between Han Anguo's durable balancing ability and Wudi-era frontier demands.
- Bounded the era explanation with the more immediate intelligence-verification and readiness failure.
