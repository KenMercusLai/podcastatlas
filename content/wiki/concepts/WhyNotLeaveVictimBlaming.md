# Why-Not-Leave Victim Blaming / “为什么不离开”受害者责难

Why-not-leave victim blaming is the habit of treating an abuse victim's delayed exit as the main mystery or fault. [[84-binglu-kuanghua-hao-guniang-ni-zhishi-wangle-yonggan-699443676]] criticizes this frame through [[AmyButcher|Amy Butcher / 艾米]]: she has education, money for a ticket, professional status, feminist vocabulary, and a therapist, yet still struggles to leave Dave.

The source's point is not that leaving is unimportant. It is that exit has to be understood inside fear, love, shame, [[TraumaticAttachment|traumatic attachment]], [[IntermittentReinforcement|intermittent reinforcement]], and practical support. When outsiders only ask why she stays, they may intensify the victim's shame and miss the control pattern.

The frame also protects against class stereotypes. The episode stresses that abuse can appear in respectable, educated, middle-class settings and not only in families already coded as chaotic or poor.

## Key Claims
- "Why didn't she leave?" can become a disguised accusation when it ignores coercion, fear, attachment, and resource differences.
- Public competence does not prove private safety.
- The useful question is often what witnesses, resources, naming, and practical steps make leaving more possible.
- Victims may need repeated attempts, outside naming, and concrete action before a relationship becomes exit-able.
