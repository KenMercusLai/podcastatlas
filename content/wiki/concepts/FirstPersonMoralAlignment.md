# First-Person Moral Alignment / 第一人称道德贴近

First-person moral alignment / 第一人称道德贴近 is a narrative effect where a morally compromised narrator becomes easier to understand because the reader is placed inside the narrator's fear, love, and self-justification before judging the conduct. [[24-hanwei-yagebu-xiangxin-ni-shi-wo-weiyi-de-zhinian-576218441]] identifies this effect in [[DefendingJacob|《捍卫雅各布》 / Defending Jacob]] through [[AndyBarber|Andy Barber]]'s account of his son's murder case.

The concept is not the same as exoneration. In the episode, Andy's first-person voice makes his destruction of evidence and denial emotionally legible, but the hosts still name those acts as serious wrongdoing. The reading value is that narration slows moral certainty long enough for [[ParentalDefenseConflict]] to become visible.

## Key Claims
- First-person narration can create sympathy by controlling sequence, access, and emotional framing.
- Understanding a narrator from inside can reveal why bad choices felt necessary without making those choices right.
- The effect supports [[MoralSuspensionInArtReading]] because delayed judgment can be a method of attention.
- The danger is that the reader may confuse intimacy with reliability or love with moral authority.
