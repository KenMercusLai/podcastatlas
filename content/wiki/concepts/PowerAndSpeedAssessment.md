# Power and Speed Assessment / 爆发与速度测试

## Definition
Power and speed assessment is the episode's treatment of the two fastest adaptations: power, defined as speed multiplied by force, is measured with a standing broad jump, while pure speed is treated as unnecessary to test for most people outside high-performance sport.

## Current Synthesis
The broad jump is deliberately simple. It is performed from a standing position with no running approach, and the measurement runs from the start line to the furthest point back where the back heel lands. A basic benchmark is jumping your own height, a target that ratchets down about 15% for females, with elite reference points far higher, such as six-foot NFL players jumping nine to 11 feet.

Galpin's stated point is to spot red flags rather than to optimize this particular test, which is what makes it a screening item: the jump is free, quick, and non-fatiguing, so it sits early in the day during [[FitnessAssessmentTestingWeek|testing weeks]]. Pure speed matters mainly for high-performance athletes, where a 40-yard dash or a velocity transducer on a barbell can be used, so the assessment layer keeps speed optional and power universal.

## Key Claims
- Power is defined as speed multiplied by force and is measured with a standing broad jump rather than a sprint.
- The broad-jump benchmark is jumping your own height, with about a 15% reduction for females and much higher elite reference points.
- The purpose is red-flag screening rather than optimizing jump performance.
- Because the jump is free, quick, and non-fatiguing, it is well suited to the early part of a testing day.
- Pure speed testing is mainly for high-performance athletes, where a 40-yard dash or a barbell velocity transducer can be used.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Broad-jump distance depends on height, limb length, body mass, landing skill, and technique, so the jump-your-height benchmark is a rough screen rather than a norm, and the episode itself frames it as a red-flag check rather than a performance target. Landing from a maximal jump also loads ankles, knees, and hips, so it is inappropriate for people with unresolved joint pain, recent injury, or balance problems without appropriate progression.

## What Changed
- Added power and speed as an early, non-fatiguing assessment item and kept pure speed testing optional outside high-performance sport.
