# Rescuer Mindset In Intimacy / 亲密关系拯救者心态

## Definition
Rescuer mindset in intimacy is the pattern where one partner tries to save, fix, or over-provide for the other in ways that can create exhaustion, control, pressure, or loss of mutual space.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.202 treats rescue as attractive but risky. Care can be real, and support can matter, but a relationship becomes distorted when one person turns love into a duty to repair the other's wounds or when the rescued person becomes burdened by another's overfunctioning. The episode's healthier picture is not emotional abandonment but shared closeness with enough distance for each person to keep growing.

The source places this beside push-pull communication: a person who wants to be saved may test the other, while a person who wants to save may over-enter. Both patterns need clearer boundaries and self-translation.

## Key Claims
- Rescue can look loving while making one partner tired and the other pressured.
- Over-care may become control when it leaves no room for the other person's own growth.
- Healthy intimacy needs both shared space and separate space.
- A rescuer pattern can pair with push-pull communication, especially when one side tests whether the other will stay.
- Support remains valuable, but it should not turn one partner into the other's entire treatment system.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept should not be used to shame ordinary care, disability support, crisis support, or mutual aid inside relationships. The episode's boundary is about over-responsibility and control, not about refusing help when help is needed.
