# 诗性语言对抗人言喧嚣 / Poetic Language Against Public Noise

## Definition
Poetic language against public noise is EP284's contrast between speech that rapidly labels, displays cleverness, and claims mastery and language that slows perception enough to let things and inner experience become strange and audible again.

## Current Synthesis
The episode reads Rilke's “I Am Much Too Afraid of People's Words” as both poetic principle and media self-critique. Public reporting and social platforms can reward fast naming and an all-knowing posture; poetry interrupts that confidence through quieter rhythm, renewed words, and attention to what exceeds labels. The practice is not withdrawal from all public speech but the cultivation of a higher threshold for the language one consumes and produces.

## Key Claims
- Repeated labeling can make words and things feel exhausted, settled, and unavailable to fresh perception.
- Cleverness and fluency can conceal inattentiveness rather than demonstrate understanding.
- Poetic rhythm and arrangement can return strangeness or sacred weight to ordinary words.
- Choosing what language to read is part of maintaining perceptual and ethical sensitivity.
- Quiet attention is an alternative speech discipline, not proof that poetry is apolitical or universally superior.

### Attention as a reading standard
- [[ep284-yu-miao-shifu-mantan-li-er-ke-naxie-lingxing-juexing-de-shike-lkkydmjftqa-fjhg2uaym_8teinq|EP284]] recommends reducing exposure to low-quality prose and speech in order to protect a more exacting sensitivity to language.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's contrast can become too binary if all journalism, social speech, or ordinary language is classified as noise. Poetry also uses convention and can become empty prestige, while direct public language can be precise, necessary, and ethically serious. The claim is best read as an attentional warning rather than a genre hierarchy.

## What Changed
- Established “people's words” as a critique of labeling and performative mastery.
- Linked poetic renewal to media self-examination and attention hygiene.
- Qualified the contrast so quiet poetry is not automatically treated as superior speech.
