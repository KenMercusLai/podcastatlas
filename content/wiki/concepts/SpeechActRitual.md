# Speech Act Ritual / 言语行为仪式

## Definition

Speech act ritual / 言语行为仪式 is the episode's frame for words whose value lies in what they perform, bind, or train, rather than in whether they transparently report an inner mental state.

## Current Synthesis

The source links Japanese kotodama, [[JLAustin]]'s performative utterances, and ritual formulas to explain why words such as "I promise," wedding vows, thanks, and apology can matter as acts. A sentence may be awkward, formulaic, or not backed by intense feeling and still create responsibility when it is uttered in a recognizable practice.

This concept gives the episode a way around a purely sincerity-based objection. The proposed apology to birds after playback is not valuable because the bird understands it or because the speaker proves perfect inner remorse. It is valuable if it changes the speaker's relation to the act, brings the disturbance into language, and becomes a repeated condition that shapes later choices.

## Key Claims

- Some utterances perform social or ethical work rather than only describing facts.
- Ritual speech can remain meaningful even when inner sincerity is incomplete or unstable.
- "As if" seriousness can guide action without requiring literal metaphysical belief.
- Formulaic words can train speakers because repeated speech changes attention and responsibility.
- Speech act ritual should not be confused with permission, absolution, or empty display.

## Evidence

- Kotodama comparison: [[episode-226-shuochukou-de-hua-zhengzai-suzao-nide-shijie-8211256494-013318]] introduces language as having force through being spoken.
- Austin frame: [[episode-226-shuochukou-de-hua-zhengzai-suzao-nide-shijie-8211256494-013318]] uses Austin's performative utterances and felicity conditions to explain speech as action.
- Promise example: [[episode-226-shuochukou-de-hua-zhengzai-suzao-nide-shijie-8211256494-013318]] notes that an insincere promise can still bind the speaker.
- "As if" ritual: [[episode-226-shuochukou-de-hua-zhengzai-suzao-nide-shijie-8211256494-013318]] reads "祭如在" as acting seriously as if the absent were present.
- Playback application: [[episode-226-shuochukou-de-hua-zhengzai-suzao-nide-shijie-8211256494-013318]] applies the frame to thanking and apologizing to birds.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not argue that any utterance works merely because it is spoken. Austin's conditions, ritual context, repeated practice, and behavioral consequences matter. The concept also does not deny sincerity's value; it only refuses to make sincerity the sole test of speech.
