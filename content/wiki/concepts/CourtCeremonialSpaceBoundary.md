## Definition

宫廷礼制空间的公私边界 / court ceremonial space boundary is the use of venue, entry, seating, and event type to distinguish activity tolerated as a ruler's private recreation from activity granted the symbolic legitimacy of a state ceremonial setting.

## Current Synthesis

The current case comes from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-271-xihan-mishi-wudi-yuemu-yu-yangzi-de-bulunlian-2-loajctyv6no5zfho92kces8pbiel|Hanji 271-2]]. [[DongfangShuo|东方朔]] does not fully prevent [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] from entertaining [[DongYanWesternHan|董偃]]. Instead, he argues that Weiyang Palace's main hall is where former emperors handled state affairs and that a private favorite unrelated to government should not enter it.

Wudi's compromise is spatial: the planned banquet is stopped in the main hall and moved to the North Palace. This makes venue a constitutional signal. The relationship and entertainment remain tolerated, but they no longer borrow the same public meaning from the principal state space.

The seating variant makes the distinction between private intimacy and public consort rank equally visible. [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] does not seek to end [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s favor toward [[ShenFurenWesternHan|慎夫人]]; he moves her Shanglin banquet seat below Empress Dou's. Liu Heng's later explanation and Shen's acceptance again preserve affection while narrowing its ceremonial expression. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-1-ln6bbls0skoqrhutd3rm-kznfpjm|Hanji 212 part 1]]

A functional variant appears through [[JiaShanWesternHan|贾山]]. His objection is not only where advisers stand or sit, but what kind of event defines their role: ordinary companions may share banquets and hunting, while major ministers and recruited upright advisers should meet Liu Heng at court for political deliberation. Repeated hunting risks turning a public feedback office into personal companionship even if formal titles remain unchanged. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-211-wendi-liuheng-qingjian-jiashan-xian-zhiluan-ce-loc6cjf-qgkz5jgjckeur4h3wo7qp|Hanji 211]]

The boundary is therefore neither a complete private-public separation nor a formal legal rule established by these episodes. It is a graded distinction enforced through ritual geography, placement, and event type. The durable mechanisms are narrow: relocate an event, lower a seat, or restore an adviser's deliberative function rather than claim that private favor or recreation has been eliminated.

## Key Claims

- Court venue can change the public meaning of the same people and activity.
- Admission to a principal state hall can imply institutional recognition beyond private tolerance.
- Equal ceremonial seating can imply rank equivalence beyond private intimacy.
- Event type can redefine an official role: treating recruited advisers as hunting companions can weaken the public deliberative purpose of their appointment.
- Relocating an event can preserve ruler discretion while protecting a ceremonial boundary.
- Remonstrance can succeed partially by narrowing where an act occurs rather than abolishing the act.
- A spatial compromise does not prove that the underlying relationship, entertainment, or patronage has ended.

## Evidence

Main-hall exclusion:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-271-xihan-mishi-wudi-yuemu-yu-yangzi-de-bulunlian-2-loajctyv6no5zfho92kces8pbiel|Hanji 271-2]] says Dongfang Shuo defines Weiyang Palace's main hall as a place for state affairs and opposes Dong Yan's entry for private entertainment.

Spatial compromise:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-271-xihan-mishi-wudi-yuemu-yu-yangzi-de-bulunlian-2-loajctyv6no5zfho92kces8pbiel|Hanji 271-2]] says Wudi moves the banquet to the North Palace rather than ending all contact with Dong Yan.

Seating hierarchy:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-1-ln6bbls0skoqrhutd3rm-kznfpjm|Hanji 212 part 1]] says Yuan Ang moves Shen Furen below Empress Dou at a Shanglin banquet because consort favor cannot erase empress-concubine rank.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-1-ln6bbls0skoqrhutd3rm-kznfpjm|Hanji 212 part 1]] says Liu Heng and Shen ultimately accept the correction while the underlying favorite relationship continues.

Deliberative-role boundary:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-211-wendi-liuheng-qingjian-jiashan-xian-zhiluan-ce-loc6cjf-qgkz5jgjckeur4h3wo7qp|Hanji 211]] says Jia Shan distinguishes recreation with ordinary companions from court deliberation with major ministers and upright advisers, and that Liu Heng accepts the criticism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept rests on three compressed popular-history summaries. They do not establish the complete institutional functions of the palace areas, a codified access, seating, or adviser-role law, exact speeches, or whether the corrections produced later political outcomes. Dongfang Shuo's capital-crime language, Yuan Ang's invocation of Qi Ji, and Jia Shan's Qin-collapse comparison may be strategic escalation rather than neutral legal assessments. Hanji 211 does not quantify the hunting practice or show the exact operational change after Liu Heng's reported acceptance.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept from venue and seating to event type and official function.
- Added Jia Shan's distinction between recreational companionship and the court-deliberation role of recruited advisers.
- Preserved the gap between reported acceptance and evidence of durable behavioral change.
