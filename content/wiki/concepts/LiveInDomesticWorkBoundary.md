# Live-In Domestic Work Boundary / 住家家政关系边界

## Definition
The live-in domestic work boundary is the negotiated line between paid labor and quasi-family intimacy when a worker lives inside a household, enters private space, cares for children, and may provide companionship as well as tasks.

## Current Synthesis
Live-in care does not remain a clean transaction. A worker may become a mother's daily ally, a child's attachment figure, or a participant in household routines, while still being an employee whose workload, rest, pay, privacy, and right to leave require explicit protection. Employers also expose their home, child, conflict, and personal information to someone who initially arrives as a stranger.

The source's practical answer is neither forced distance nor fictional kinship. Scope, facilities, habits, authority, closeness, communication, and escalation routes should be discussed as early as possible, and consultants should mediate remediable skill or communication problems. Where conflict comes from entrenched habits or incompatible expectations, replacement may be healthier than demanding indefinite emotional adaptation.

## Key Claims
- Live-in domestic work combines employment, privacy exposure, care, companionship, and emotional attachment.
- Calling a worker "family" can express real affection but must not erase pay, rest, scope, authority, or the worker's independent life.
- A child's attachment to a caregiver can be beneficial while also triggering parental anxiety or competition.
- Workload and household rules should be disclosed before placement because many conflicts appear only after service begins.
- Consultants can mediate trainable skill and communication problems, but deep habit or fit conflicts may justify prompt replacement.
- Safety screening does not justify unlimited inquiry into a worker's marriage, children, or family arrangements.

## Evidence
- Dual-role evidence - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] compares joining a household to joining a company while describing companionship and quasi-family closeness that exceed an ordinary workplace.
- Attachment evidence - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] reports workers buying children gifts and parents sometimes resenting an overly close child-caregiver bond.
- Boundary-detail evidence - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] discusses bedding, personal supplies, work scope, household habits, and many details for which no shared norm exists.
- Screening tension - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] contrasts legitimate identity and criminal-record checks with employer questions about marriage, children, and family care used to infer stability.
- Resolution evidence - [[vol-276-zuo-yuesao-yuersao-de-jiejie-dou-you-gushi-1017890906]] distinguishes consultant-mediated skill or communication issues from difficult-to-change living habits that make replacement more appropriate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a legal analysis of working hours, accommodation, discrimination, privacy, dismissal, or social insurance, and its practical norms should not be mistaken for employment-law guidance. Household intimacy also varies across day work, short maternity contracts, long-term childcare, and companionship roles, so one boundary model will not fit every arrangement.

## What Changed
- Created the concept for the employment-and-intimacy ambiguity distinctive to live-in domestic care.
