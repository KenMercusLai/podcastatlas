# Zeus Law Trust Ethic / 宙斯法则信任伦理

## Definition

Zeus law trust ethic is the episode's name for expanding Homeric guest-host duty into a minimum civilizational trust rule: strangers, hosts, guests, gifts, and requests must remain legible enough that social life does not collapse into predation.

## Current Synthesis

The episode treats "宙斯法则" as more than a custom inside [[TheOdyssey]]. It becomes Nolan's way of asking what holds civilization together when people encounter strangers. The [[TrojanHorse]] matters because it turns a gift and act of acceptance into a weapon, damaging the trust grammar that [[HomericHospitalityTest]] normally tests through guest and host behavior.

## Key Claims

- Hospitality is a minimal trust infrastructure, not only etiquette.
- A gift can become a civilizational problem when its meaning is weaponized.
- The Trojan Horse breaks trust on both sides of the guest-host relation: giving, receiving, sheltering, and believing all become dangerous.
- The rule is ethically attractive but politically incomplete because the film depends on personal awakening more than institutional justice.
- The concept connects ancient xenia to modern trust collapse without making the two historically identical.

## Evidence

- Minimum trust rule: [[dang-xila-lishi-meiguo-zhengzhi-nuolan-meixue-zai-aodesai-li-da-qilai-gkwrijiov2xkbtdknqthj-ue]] says the film expands Zeus law into the most basic principle for human trust.
- Gift weaponization: [[dang-xila-lishi-meiguo-zhengzhi-nuolan-meixue-zai-aodesai-li-da-qilai-gkwrijiov2xkbtdknqthj-ue]] links the Trojan Horse to the destruction of trust between gift and acceptance.
- Institutional limit: [[dang-xila-lishi-meiguo-zhengzhi-nuolan-meixue-zai-aodesai-li-da-qilai-gkwrijiov2xkbtdknqthj-ue]] contrasts Nolan's personal moral awakening with the Oresteia's move from revenge to law court and civic adjudication.
- Cross-era qualification: [[dang-xila-lishi-meiguo-zhengzhi-nuolan-meixue-zai-aodesai-li-da-qilai-gkwrijiov2xkbtdknqthj-ue]] keeps the concept attached to a modern film interpretation rather than a complete account of Greek xenia.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode itself raises a weakness: a hospitality ethic can identify trust collapse, but it may not supply enforceable institutions for cycles of revenge, war trauma, or political breakdown.

## What Changed

- Created the concept for the episode's expansion of Homeric hospitality into a social-trust ethic.
