## Definition

Ritualized command appointment / 仪式化拜将留才 is the conversion of a ruler's private judgment into publicly legible command authority through ceremony, testimony, rank, or other shared evidence. Its practical test is whether the appointee and surrounding organization understand that the role is serious and why this person has been placed above others.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 146 supplies the strongest formal version. [[XiaoHe|萧何]] argues that if [[LiuBang|刘邦]] intends to contest the empire, [[HanXin|韩信]] must receive a role matching that ambition. A casual tent summons is inadequate: bathing, fasting, choosing a date, building a platform, and appointing Han Xin publicly make his authority legible to established generals and help retain someone ready to leave an under-sized post.

Hanji 147 supplies the downstream test. Because the appointment has made Han Xin chief commander rather than merely a clever subordinate, he can define the [[HanzhongDui|汉中对]], critique [[XiangYu|项羽]], and make [[ThreeQinContainment|三秦压制]] the first campaign problem. Ceremony is validated by usable strategic authorship.

Hanji 1157 adds a less formal but equally public Eastern Wu variant. [[SunQuan|孙权]] appoints [[ZhouTaiEasternWu|周泰]] over officers who initially resist the hierarchy. At a banquet, Sun Quan has Zhou Tai explain the scars received while protecting him and then states the debt behind the appointment. The public evidence does not create Zhou Tai's service, but it lets [[ZhuRanEasternWu|朱然]] and [[XuShengEasternWu|徐盛]] understand why private trust has become command authority.

## Key Claims

- Private praise or confidence does not by itself create usable command authority.
- Appointment signals must match the ruler's actual strategic ambition and the appointee's expected contribution.
- Ceremony is operational when it makes hierarchy credible to established subordinates as well as to the appointee.
- Shared evidence can legitimate an appointment when pedigree, reputation, or prior visibility would otherwise point toward another candidate.
- Underused talent may exit when an organization offers only small posts, casual attention, or vague future promise.
- Public authority matters only if it enables downstream judgment, strategy, and coordinated action.

## Evidence

- Strategic condition for appointment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-146-qiannian-jingdian-xiaohe-yuexia-zhui-hanxin-lq0oc3vqr-tsr0r-iwztifxeezl|Hanji 146]] links Han Xin's role to whether Liu Bang means to remain in Hanzhong or contend for the realm.
- Formal public ceremony: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-146-qiannian-jingdian-xiaohe-yuexia-zhui-hanxin-lq0oc3vqr-tsr0r-iwztifxeezl|Hanji 146]] has Xiao He reject a casual summons and demand a public platform appointment.
- Strategic output: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-147-pimei-zhugeliang-longzhongdui-de-hanzhongdui-lqucctzhipgtcvtjlixvj5qbdeai|Hanji 147]] shows Han Xin using the commander role to set an eastward program through the Three Qin.
- Evidence-backed hierarchy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1157-duanzan-de-suncao-lianmeng-yaobai-de-sunquan-lpngfqsoz-zjr2iytaaox89df1zcj|Hanji 1157]] says Sun Quan publicly connects Zhou Tai's scars and past rescue service to the Ruxu appointment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Ceremony cannot substitute for ability, resources, a workable reporting structure, or a ruler willing to honor the delegation. The Han Xin case is retrospectively validated by strategic output, while the Zhou Tai case is bounded to a podcast summary whose wound count, banquet dialogue, officers' objections, and immediate change of attitude require primary-text comparison. Public testimony can also become favoritism theater if the claimed evidence is false, irrelevant, or insulated from challenge.
