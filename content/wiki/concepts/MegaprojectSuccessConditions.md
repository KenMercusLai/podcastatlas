# Megaproject Success Conditions / 超级工程成功条件

## Definition

Megaproject success conditions are the source's three-part requirement for sustained political commitment, adequate funding, and clear staged objectives in a large, technically interdependent public program.

## Current Synthesis

[[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]] uses [[ApolloProgram|Apollo]] as the positive comparison and the shuttle as the stressed comparison. A clear goal and exceptional backing can align tradeoffs, while mixed civilian, military, industrial, electoral, and publicity demands under constrained funding encourage optimistic promises and designs that defer cost into integration, inspection, and maintenance. The three conditions are necessary framing devices, not a guarantee of safety or success.

## Key Claims

- Stable political commitment matters because long programs outlive administrations and budget cycles.
- Adequate funding matters because underfunding does not remove requirements; it often moves cost and risk into design compromises, labor, maintenance, or future operations.
- Clear staged objectives help teams decide which capabilities are essential and which demands should be excluded.
- Adding military, scientific, industrial, representational, and publicity goals can create unresolved requirements rather than productive multifunctionality.
- Credible planning must include lifecycle operations and refurbishment, not development cost alone.
- Political support and money do not replace independent safety authority, contingency planning, or technical learning.

## Evidence

### Apollo comparison
- [[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]] describes Apollo as benefiting from Cold War commitment, exceptional resources, and the clear goal of landing people on the Moon.

### Shuttle stress case
- [[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]] describes the shuttle as spanning multiple administrations, mixed NASA and military requirements, reduced funding, reuse promises, and unrealistic launch economics.

### Lifecycle consequence
- [[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]] uses thermal-tile, booster, inspection, and refurbishment examples to show how cheaper development choices can generate expensive and safety-critical operational complexity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Apollo's high funding and clear objective did not eliminate fatal accidents, technical uncertainty, political conflict, or opportunity cost.
- Programs can adapt productively across administrations and missions; changing objectives are not inherently a failure.
- The episode attributes the three-condition framework to project-management expert Scott Pace, but the supplied summary does not provide the original publication or full argument.
- The comparison does not establish a universal threshold for “adequate” funding or “clear” objectives.

## What Changed

- Created the three-condition framework and linked it to lifecycle cost and safety tradeoffs.
