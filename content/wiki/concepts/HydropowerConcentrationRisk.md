# Hydropower Concentration Risk

## Definition
Hydropower concentration risk is the vulnerability created when too much power generation and related infrastructure sit along the same exposed river systems, so one flood, landslide, or glacial surge can become both a disaster and an energy shock.

## Current Synthesis
The Nepal sources present hydropower concentration as a planning failure under mountain climate risk. Hydropower is not criticized simply because it is river-based or renewable. The problem is correlated exposure: facilities, tunnels, roads, and workers along vulnerable river systems can fail together, turning a sudden flood into rescue difficulty, generation loss, damaged export capacity, and a larger reconstruction burden.

## Key Claims
- Energy infrastructure can convert a local flood into a national power and economic shock.
- Concentration along one river system raises correlated-failure risk when hazards move downstream.
- Climate-resilient infrastructure requires distribution, monitoring, and siting discipline, not only stronger individual assets.
- Hydropower concentration interacts with rescue risk when workers or civilians are trapped in tunnels or facilities.
- Poor or politically unstable states have less room to absorb concentrated infrastructure losses.
- Export dependence can transmit physical plant damage into national trade and fiscal pressure.

## Evidence
- Concentrated siting: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] says Nepal placed too many hydropower plants along single rivers instead of spreading them out.
- Energy shock: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] reports that about 10% of Nepal's power generation was knocked out within hours.
- Rescue overlap: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] describes people trapped in hydropower tunnels filled with mud while engineers pumped in air.
- Recovery burden: [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]] says recovery costs could reach $5 billion for a poor and politically unstable country.
- Plant and trade exposure: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] says twelve hydropower plants were destroyed and describes electricity trade with India and Bangladesh as a national economic pillar.
- Long rescue horizon: [[niboer-teda-hongzai-xianchang-yichang-meiyou-yiti-de-zangli-cd38365e015f056efaa8b0506c123504]] says liquid mud in power-station tunnels could require months of clearing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not compare hydropower with alternative energy systems, reconcile their different loss measures, or quantify how much dispersion would have reduced damage. The concept remains a risk-concentration warning rather than a general argument against hydropower.

## What Changed
- Added plant-destruction, export-dependence, and months-long tunnel-clearance evidence.
- Kept the argument bounded to correlated exposure rather than opposition to hydropower.
