# Bicycle Product Evolution

Bicycle product evolution is the two-century design sequence in [[no-199-zixingche-200-nian-973489465]] from [[KarlDrais]]'s foot-propelled running machine to the safety bicycle. The source treats the bicycle as a repeated constraint-solving process: steering, propulsion, road shock, speed, balance, price, social acceptance, and repairability all had to be worked through before the product became mass-market.

The sequence is not a simple invention story. [[PierreMichaux]] and [[PierreLallement]] made front-wheel pedals important, but that solution encouraged high-wheel danger; [[JohnKempStarley]] and the [[RoverBicycle]] separated speed from wheel height through chain drive; [[JohnBoydDunlop]]'s pneumatic tire then improved comfort enough for wider use.

## Key Claims
- A transport product can fail despite technical novelty if roads, comfort, safety, and price do not fit ordinary users.
- Direct-drive front-wheel designs solved propulsion while worsening the speed-versus-safety tradeoff.
- The safety bicycle stabilized the product architecture by making gearing, posture, and wheel size work together.
- Pneumatic tires show that a seemingly peripheral component can change the whole product's adoption curve.
