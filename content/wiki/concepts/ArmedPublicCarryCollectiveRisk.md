# Armed Public-Carry Collective Risk

## Definition

Armed public-carry collective risk is the possibility that individually motivated firearm carrying becomes more dangerous at group scale because carriers act under fear, incomplete information, physical density, and uncertainty about one another's roles.

## Current Synthesis

[[BarbaraUnderwood]]'s subway response supplies the core mechanism. A single-carrier hypothetical asks whether one law-abiding person could stop a threat; a system-level analysis asks what happens when several people draw weapons inside an enclosed, crowded space. Missed shots, simultaneous reactions, interpersonal fights, and misidentification can expose bystanders and turn an initially bounded event into wider violence.

The problem continues when police arrive. Officers may see multiple armed people without knowing who initiated the encounter, making lawful intent difficult to infer in real time. The concept does not assume that licensed carriers are malicious or that every defensive use fails; it identifies interaction effects omitted when public-carry policy is evaluated only one defender at a time.

## Key Claims

- Individual self-defense benefit and population-level safety are different units of analysis.
- Density and enclosure can increase the consequences of inaccurate fire and simultaneous response.
- Lawful intent does not eliminate mistakes, panic, escalation, or role ambiguity.
- Multiple visible guns can make the initiating aggressor harder for police to identify.
- Carry restrictions can target interaction risk without resolving every form of gun violence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source offers an oral-argument mechanism and Gladwell's imagined scenario, not comparative incident data or a causal estimate. It does not measure defensive gun use, licensed-carrier behavior, police error, or the effect of particular permitting systems. The framework should therefore guide system-level questions and evidence collection rather than predetermine every public-carry policy conclusion.
