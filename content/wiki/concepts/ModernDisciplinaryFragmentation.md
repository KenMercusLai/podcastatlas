# Modern Disciplinary Fragmentation / 现代学科分割

## Definition

Modern disciplinary fragmentation is the division of older, overlapping bodies of learning into specialized university fields with separate methods, departments, curricula, evaluation systems, and professional identities.

## Current Synthesis

The sources treat fragmentation as a productive loss. Specialization enables deeper research in literature, history, philosophy, linguistics, philology, politics, science, and professional fields, but it can make materials that once belonged to one learned practice appear to have only one disciplinary home. The *Book of Songs*, rites, histories, and classical commentaries remain simultaneously textual, political, historical, philosophical, performative, and linguistic.

Modern research and applied institutions make this division organizationally efficient: departments, journals, laboratories, seminars, credentials, and employment paths reward bounded expertise. The educational answer is not to abolish expertise or reconstruct an idealized undivided past. It is to keep disciplinary competence while crossing boundaries when the object requires it, using intellectual integration rather than interdisciplinary branding as the standard.

## Key Claims

- Modern departments produce depth by narrowing objects, methods, training, and evaluation pathways.
- Classical texts and lived problems often exceed the borders assigned by those departments.
- Chinese and English departments contain theory, criticism, linguistics, and research rather than only creative writing or translation.
- Research and applied university models strengthen specialization by tying knowledge fields to laboratories, journals, professions, and credentials.
- Cross-disciplinary study is most useful when it restores dimensions required by the object, not when breadth substitutes for competence.
- Appeals to an undivided “national learning” can express a real desire for wholeness while remaining institutionally and conceptually ambiguous.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources provide public-history interpretation and retrospective educational experience, not a comparative survey of departments or proof that older education was more complete for all learners. Premodern learning also had exclusions, hierarchies, canonical constraints, and specialist traditions. Integration can become superficial if it avoids methods, evidence standards, or sustained practice, and the counterfactual claim that late-Qing academies might have produced a different evaluation system remains speculative.

## What Changed

- Added the research-university, applied-education, department, and evaluation-system layer.
- Strengthened the distinction between recovering integrated objects and romanticizing premodern education.
