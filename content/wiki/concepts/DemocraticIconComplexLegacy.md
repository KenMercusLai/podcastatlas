# Democratic Icon Complex Legacy

Democratic icon complex legacy is the problem of holding symbolic democratic achievement and later political failure together. [[the-case-of-the-missing-totem-aung-san-suu-kyi-6a560fc4f96471dde4821d2f]] adds it through [[AungSanSuuKyi]], who appears both as a long-detained democratic figure and as a former leader criticized for defending the army over atrocities against the [[Rohingya]].

The source refuses a simple reversal from hero to villain. Aung San Suu Kyi's detention and possible ill health can still demand attention, while her governing record remains part of the moral and political assessment. That dual reading is why the page sits near [[OverPersonalizedCrisisDiplomacy]] rather than pure biography.

## Key Claims
- A symbolic figure can retain real coercive importance to an authoritarian regime even after losing moral authority for many observers.
- Earlier democratic credentials do not erase later decisions involving minority rights, press freedom, or state violence.
- Criticizing the icon does not remove the humanitarian need to verify detention conditions.
- A mature crisis narrative has to keep individual status, political record, and population suffering in view at the same time.
