# 学在王官式知识垄断 / Official Learning Monopoly

学在王官式知识垄断 / official learning monopoly is the way [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-124-fenshu-kengru-wo-zai-bzhan-wei-qinshihuang-pingfan-ljnhwcouqu35rmvb-cnrzyzj2j6l]] explains the institutional logic behind [[LiSi|李斯]]'s book-burning proposal. The host says the policy is not only about burning texts; it also bans private teaching and redirects legitimate learning to officials.

The source links this to the older phrase "学在王官": in an earlier ritual order, learning was imagined as held by official institutions. After the collapse of that order, knowledge dispersed among private teachers and the诸子百家. Li Si's Qin proposal tries to pull learning back under state authority, but now inside a centralized empire rather than a Zhou-style feudal world.

This concept is narrower than [[KnowledgeMonopoly]]. It names a specific official-channel structure: law is learned from officials, classical-political argument is suppressed outside official control, and private interpretive communities become threats to political uniformity. The effect is to make scholarship answer to [[QinState|秦国]]'s command structure.

## Key Claims
- Book burning and private-teaching prohibition are connected in the source's reading.
- "学在王官" is used as an analogy for knowledge returning to official channels, not as a claim that Qin simply restored Zhou practice.
- Official legal instruction remains available, but independent classical argument is politically constrained.
- The concept explains why the policy can spare practical books while attacking historical and classical-political texts.
- The policy extends [[ImperialHomogenization|imperial homogenization]] into education and interpretation.
