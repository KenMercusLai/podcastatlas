# Visual Representation Literacy / 视觉表征素养

Visual representation literacy is the learned ability to treat photographs, drawings, film images, ID photos, and screen scale as representations rather than as direct objects. [[35-tianzhende-renleixuejia-zuo-tianye-hui-shangyin-de-603418624]] introduces it through [[NigelBarley|Nigel Barley]]'s account of the [[DowayoPeople|Dowayo people]] encountering animal photographs, identity-card photos, voting procedures, and a malaria film.

The concept parallels [[LinguisticRelativity]]: visual media do not only carry content, they also require conventions. If those conventions are not shared, a photo of a leopard, a cartoon cat, a giant mosquito on a screen, or a state ID portrait can be read through very different categories.

## Key Claims
- Seeing a picture as "the same thing as" a real animal or person is a learned convention, not a universal default.
- Modern state administration can assume visual literacy before people share the conventions behind ID photos or ballots.
- Public-health media can fail when image scale and representation rules are interpreted differently by the audience.
- Visual misunderstanding should be treated as cultural and technological translation friction, not as stupidity.
