## Definition

英雄记忆与政治信用 / Hero memory political credibility is the pattern where public treatment of heroic service becomes a test of whether a state, organization, or culture can credibly ask future people to sacrifice, take risks, and uphold shared values.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 570 defines the pattern through [[GengYuWesternHan|耿育]]'s defense of [[ChenTangWesternHan|陈汤]]. The argument is not only that Chen Tang personally deserves comfort in old age. It is that a court that celebrates a victory over [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]] while letting the victorious commander be slandered, imprisoned, or abandoned damages the promise behind future service.

The source then generalizes from Western Han court politics to civic hero memory. Heroes are not presented as flawless people or only battlefield figures. They are people whose visible responsibility, courage, small-place integrity, or crisis-bearing work gives others a standard to imitate. When a society treats such figures mainly through mockery or erasure, it weakens the cultural vocabulary by which ordinary people understand duty and trust.

## Key Claims

- Hero memory is political infrastructure because future action depends partly on whether past sacrifice was honored.
- Vindicating a hero does not require denying fault; it requires preserving proportional judgment between service, error, and punishment.
- The public use of heroic achievement creates an obligation not to abandon the person when factional hostility or later faults make him inconvenient.
- Degrading honored service can chill later strategic initiative because capable people learn that success may not protect them from envy or slander.
- The source broadens heroism from battlefield glory to ordinary ethical steadiness, making hero memory a civic rather than purely military category.

## Evidence

- Court credibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] says Geng Yu warns that people will hesitate to give plans or risk death if Chen Tang's great service ends in disgrace.
- Proportional judgment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] acknowledges Chen Tang's Changling-related fault while arguing it should not erase the Zhizhi campaign.
- Public memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] connects contempt for heroes to the loss of historical and cultural confidence.
- Civic extension: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-570-xinyang-buke-queshi-yingxiong-burong-dihui-lrrcbw5rrurytgjxasbp12buf82l|Hanji 570]] extends heroism to ordinary people carrying responsibility in difficult positions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not turn every admired person into an untouchable hero. Hanji 570 itself acknowledges Chen Tang's faults, so the pattern requires proportional memory rather than immunity from accountability. The source is a short interpretive episode and its modern civic extension is the host's value argument, not a comprehensive sociology of hero worship, propaganda, or historical education.
