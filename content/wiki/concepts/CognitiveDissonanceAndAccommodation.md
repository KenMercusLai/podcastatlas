# Cognitive Dissonance And Accommodation / 认知失调与顺应

## Definition

Cognitive dissonance is discomfort or pressure created when beliefs, expectations, evidence, values, or behavior conflict. Accommodation changes the underlying model or conduct; assimilation and rationalization reduce the conflict while preserving more of the existing commitment.

## Current Synthesis

[[132-zhenglun-yu-shuofu-women-weishenme-chaojia-zenme-cai-suan-ying-807583318]] treats dissonance as a normal learning problem rather than a disease or insult. Because perception is filtered through prior models, conflicting evidence can be assimilated at low cost or can force accommodation, which demands uncertainty tolerance, motivation, and sometimes social support. Identity, group belonging, distance from personal risk, and reward structures change the cost of revision.

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-1-the-blue-eyed-tornado-hitler-s-olympics-part-1-the-blue-eyed-tornado|Hitler's Olympics, Part 1]] extends the mechanism from belief revision to moral action. Olympic participants could profess fair competition and international understanding while entering a Nazi propaganda spectacle. They could reduce dissonance either by refusing participation, as [[DorothyThompson|Dorothy Thompson]] urged, or by revising the meaning and consequences of participation until it seemed compatible with their ideals.

Commitment under direct disconfirmation is visible in [[defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-3bb43a3a1bc3b7d73f72cf6318a8ff07-56990038-defaultmp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-3bb43a3a1bc3b7d73f72cf6318a8ff07-56990038|When You Need It To Be True]]. The Seekers could reinterpret failed spacecraft arrivals and an absent flood because jobs, relationships, reputation, and identity had already been sacrificed. In Liz's romance-scam account, emotional disclosure, money, conflict with friends, and a hoped-for shared future made warnings and missed meetings costly to accept; partial repayment and tragic explanations supplied selective routes back to belief.

The combined synthesis is that dissonance can be resolved by revising a model, changing conduct, narrowing a claim, or reinterpreting evidence and consequences. Prior sacrifice raises the stakes, but persistence is not automatic. Dissonance describes pressure and possible responses; it does not prove conscious bad faith, low intelligence, or a universal “backfire effect.”

## Key Claims

- Dissonance begins when expectations, evidence, values, identity, or behavior cannot all remain untouched.
- Assimilation preserves an existing model by reinterpreting the new fact, while accommodation changes the model or conduct.
- Moral dissonance can be reduced by changing behavior to fit values or by redescribing behavior and consequences.
- Identity, group belonging, institutional loyalty, hope, reward, and prior sacrifice alter the cost of revision.
- Strong disconfirmation can prompt temporary doubt without lasting accommodation when a plausible repair preserves the valued belief.
- Supportive correction must account for shame and exit cost as well as the factual error being challenged.
- Rationalization may preserve sincere ideals or attachment while enabling practical contradiction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources provide explanatory frameworks and narrative cases, not controlled evidence identifying every person's internal state or the prevalence of each response. Apparent inconsistency can reflect incomplete information, competing values, coercion, uncertainty, or a genuine counterargument rather than dissonance reduction. Accommodation is not automatically correct, and preserving a model is not automatically irrational. The Berlin application is not a diagnosis of every participant; the Seekers and romance-scam stories do not show that all failed predictions or warnings strengthen belief. “Backfire effect” language in the episode should remain bounded to its narrative and not be generalized into a universal law of persuasion.

## What Changed

- Added costly commitment and direct disconfirmation to the earlier learning and moral-action synthesis.
- Distinguished temporary doubt, selective reassurance, and lasting accommodation.
- Added shame and loss of hope as costs that corrective support may need to address.
