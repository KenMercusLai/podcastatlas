# AI Macro Capital-Labor Transmission / AI 的资本—劳动宏观传导

## Definition

AI macro capital-labor transmission is the path through which AI investment, financing, equity wealth, trade, productivity, labor substitution, prices, and income distribution become large enough to alter aggregate demand and policy.

## Current Synthesis

The source treats AI as macro because infrastructure spending can support investment demand and rates, equity gains can raise consumption through wealth effects, and AI-linked imports and exports can widen global imbalances. Capital markets matter not only as a pool for liquidity but as a credit and collateral system that finances the buildout.

The labor side may move differently. AI can improve productivity and reduce service costs while replacing or narrowing entry points in locally priced, non-traded cognitive services. If efficiency gains accrue mainly to capital owners, greater capability does not guarantee more jobs or household income. Fiscal spending, tax design, social protection, and distribution rules may therefore become part of AI policy rather than afterthoughts.

## Key Claims

- AI capex becomes macro when its scale materially affects investment, financing demand, rates, and trade.
- Equity wealth effects can connect AI valuations to consumption and external imbalance.
- Real technological productivity does not rule out asset-price bubbles or interim market reversals.
- Cognitive-service substitution may be broader than earlier automation concentrated in tradable or manual production.
- Lower service costs can create disinflationary pressure, but net demand and employment effects remain uncertain.
- Productivity gains do not automatically become worker income, job creation, or broad welfare.
- Tax, transfer, social-insurance, education, and transition buffers may need to change with the distribution of AI surplus.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode says aggregate employment had not yet deteriorated at the recording point. It provides no causal labor estimates, capex audit, or price decomposition. Cheaper services can expand demand and create complementary work, while investment can raise supply and productivity without permanently supporting high rates. The 3%-4%-of-GDP investment estimate and all forward labor, inflation, and policy claims remain source-scoped scenarios.
