# Nature-City Proximity / 城市自然邻近

Nature-city proximity is [[ep265-dang-qing3xiu13-chongshang-resou-women-ruhe-gei-rensheng-fang-yige-changjia-lsloczlnkmcrrfpn9coh8rrqo-wb]]'s travel-design pattern where city life and natural immersion are close enough that a person can move between them within one holiday rhythm. [[Vancouver]], Stanley Park, [[Whistler]], [[BanffNationalPark|Banff]], [[QuebecCity|Quebec City]], and other [[Canada]] examples make the pattern concrete.

The concept matters because it lowers the threshold for [[TravelSensoryRecovery|travel sensory recovery]]. A vacationer does not have to choose between dense urban culture and remote nature; the same route can include parks, sea, mountains, food, museums, cycling, history, wildlife, and ordinary neighborhoods.

## Key Claims
- Natural access near cities can make rest more practical for travelers with limited planning energy.
- City-natural adjacency lets a holiday vary intensity: a person can move from park to restaurant, ferry to neighborhood, museum to mountain route.
- The pattern can reduce the performance pressure of both urban check-in tourism and extreme outdoor travel.
- Proximity does not remove safety or environmental rules; wildlife, water, and mountains still require attention.
- Canada is the source case, but the concept can apply to any place where urban life and natural systems are physically and culturally close.
