# Weak Signal Detection / 微弱信号探测

## Definition

Weak signal detection is the evidence regime for measuring rare, faint, noisy, or environment-dependent phenomena whose interpretation requires specialized instruments, noise suppression, long observation, and rival-source testing.

## Current Synthesis

The large-science source establishes why scale and location matter: cosmic microwave background polarization, neutrinos, neutron-scattering patterns, and radio signals become legible only through shielding, cooling, geometry, statistical accumulation, and specialized detectors. The Keating interview adds the participant-level failure mode. BICEP measured a polarized microwave pattern, but a foreground source—aligned Milky Way dust—could produce the observation without primordial gravitational waves.

Weak-signal work therefore has two separable tasks: detecting a pattern and attributing its source. An extreme site can suppress some noise while leaving other foregrounds unresolved; a technically functioning instrument can support an incorrect interpretation. Strong claims require environmental control, calibration, multi-frequency or independent comparison, and correction when the source model changes.

## Key Claims
- Weak-signal science makes environment and geography parts of the instrument.
- Long accumulation, calibration, and statistical discipline can matter as much as physical size.
- Detecting a real pattern does not uniquely identify the process that produced it.
- Noise and foreground models are components of the scientific claim, not logistical footnotes.
- Engineering and inference must be revised together when a foreground mimics the target signal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources are public-science summaries rather than full technical analyses. They do not establish current detection thresholds, statistical significance, detector specifications, or the complete BICEP–Planck comparison. A corrected interpretation does not imply that all weak-signal claims are unreliable, and a favorable site does not remove every instrumental or astrophysical foreground.
