# 帝王故乡免租徭役 / Imperial Home-County Tax Exemption

帝王故乡免租徭役 / imperial home-county tax exemption enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-188-gudai-zhongguo-de-tianxia-wushui-xian-lnoib0bvz8w0ihvhxmtx19tr4jdz|Hanji 188]], where [[LiuBang|刘邦]] returns to [[PeiCounty|沛县]] after defeating [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] and declares that Pei's people will be permanently exempt from rent, taxes, and corvee labor.

The episode links the exemption to [[TangmuYi|汤沐邑]] language, but the policy direction is unusual inside the wiki's current evidence. A normal Tangmu Yi frame assigns local revenue to an honored person or rulerly household; here Liu Bang calls Pei his personal root while releasing the locals from the fiscal and labor burdens that would normally flow upward.

The later extension to [[FengCounty|丰邑 / 丰县]] makes the concept more than hometown nostalgia. Liu Bang first hesitates because Feng had followed [[YongChi|雍齿]] away from him in the anti-Qin period, but Pei elders and old followers such as [[CaoShen|曹参]], [[FanKuai|樊哙]], and [[XiahouYing|夏侯婴]] plead for the same treatment. The exemption therefore turns imperial memory into local material privilege while also converting an old betrayal into a settled hometown order.

## Key Claims
- Home-county exemption translates personal origin memory into concrete fiscal privilege.
- In Hanji 188, [[TangmuYi|汤沐邑]] language is used to mark personal attachment while suspending extraction rather than redirecting revenue.
- The extension from Pei to Feng shows that old grievance and local belonging can coexist until public pleading and prior reward settlement make reconciliation politically available.
- The concept belongs beside [[BattlefieldVictoryToFoundingOrder|战场胜利到建国秩序]] because it happens immediately after final suppression of a major rebel king and helps stage Liu Bang's late founding memory.
