# Jealousy and Mate Retention

## Definition
Jealousy is an emotional response to perceived threats to a valued relationship; mate retention comprises behaviors intended to reduce defection, infidelity, or partner loss.

## Current Synthesis
The source interprets jealousy as an evolved pair-bond defense rather than automatically as immaturity or pathology. Cues can include suspected sexual or emotional infidelity, distance, attention from rivals, unequal perceived mate value, or signs that a partner may leave.

That possible function does not make every response safe or justified. Retention behavior ranges from attention and reassurance to monitoring, phone intrusion, isolation, denigration, stalking, coercion, and violence. The concept therefore requires a hard boundary: explaining why an emotion or behavior may recur is not approval, and behaviors that remove autonomy or create fear belong to [[MatingConflictRisk]], not healthy relationship care. The Essentials edit reinforces this continuum but is not an independent source.

The bounded [[WenQingqing|温青青]] case shows that perceived threat can depend on context rather than objective infidelity alone. She reacts aggressively to minor or imagined rivals but withdraws when [[AjiuBixueJian|阿九]] appears to be a genuinely reciprocated attachment. Her insecure family position may help explain abandonment sensitivity, yet the case also shows why explanation cannot excuse conduct that endangers a rescue or pressures unrelated women to resolve her fear.

## Key Claims
- Jealousy can signal perceived threats to attachment, investment, or partner continuity.
- Mate-value discrepancy and perceived alternatives can intensify threat appraisal.
- Retention responses range from noncoercive reassurance to dangerous control.
- Surveillance, isolation, denigration, stalking, and violence are escalation signs rather than evidence of love.
- Verbal aggression and social isolation can precede or accompany physical abuse.
- Evolutionary function does not excuse conduct or establish that every jealous response is adaptive.
- Fiction can illustrate how insecurity and perceived rival credibility alter threat appraisal, but it cannot validate one empirical or clinical explanation by itself.

## Evidence
- Pair-bond account: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] and [[essentials-how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-the-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim3609265030]] present jealousy as protecting long-term bonds and investment.
- Trigger range: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] and [[essentials-how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-the-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim3609265030]] list infidelity cues, emotional distance, mate poachers, and mate-value discrepancies.
- Escalation continuum: both [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] and [[essentials-how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-the-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim3609265030]] move from vigilance and monitoring to stalking and violence.
- Coercive tactics: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] identifies appearance denigration and isolation from friends or family as attempts to reduce perceived alternatives.
- Violence warning: [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839]] reports verbal violence predicting physical violence in Buss's married-couple studies.
- Threat credibility and conduct: [[liiidmjxhrngcffnld51y-l7_oan]] contrasts Wen Qingqing's public anger toward lesser rivals with private withdrawal before A Jiu, while preserving the danger caused by her suspicion during a rescue.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The two Buss editorial versions do not establish that jealousy is always useful, accurate, or biologically fixed, and they do not fully compare attachment, learning, culture, power, trauma, and situational explanations. The Wen Qingqing case is literary interpretation rather than psychological evidence and should not be used to diagnose a fictional or real person. Reported violence prevalence and sex differences require underlying methods not supplied in the notes. People facing stalking, coercion, or violence need safety-oriented professional and local support rather than evolutionary interpretation alone.

## What Changed
- Added Wen Qingqing as a fictional contrast between generalized rival sensitivity and response to a credible reciprocal attachment.
- Clarified that developmental explanation and threat appraisal do not excuse dangerous or autonomy-reducing conduct.
