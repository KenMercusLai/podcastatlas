# Semen Sample Collection Quality / 精液标本采集质量

## Definition
Semen sample collection quality is the pre-analytic framework that collection method, preparation, completeness, container cleanliness, temperature, elapsed time, privacy, and laboratory handoff can affect whether a semen specimen is usable and how confidently its measurements can be interpreted.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.79 treats the collection room as laboratory infrastructure rather than a mysterious or comic setting. Handwashing, private self-collection, a designated cup, an adjacent transfer cabinet, controlled warming, and rapid handoff are organized around contamination control, liquefaction, sperm activity, and reduced delay.

The practical judgment is that specimen quality begins before measurement. A missed fraction, temperature excursion, long trip, or inability to produce a sample can change what reaches the analyser and, in assisted reproduction, can disrupt a time-sensitive treatment chain. Privacy and familiar material are therefore operational accommodations, while assisted ejaculation or surgical retrieval belongs to indication-based care.

## Key Claims
- Semen-analysis quality depends partly on pre-analytic collection and transport, not only on laboratory measurement.
- Complete capture matters because losing part of an ejaculate may make the submitted specimen less representative.
- Temperature extremes and delay can affect the sample, so prompt handoff and laboratory-specific transport instructions matter.
- Privacy, a clean dedicated space, and a direct transfer route can reduce avoidable collection and contamination problems.
- Home or off-site collection may be possible for selected tests when the receiving laboratory's container, transport, temperature, and time requirements can be met.
- Assisted or surgical retrieval is an indication-based alternative when ordinary collection is not possible, not the standard collection method.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode gives one institution's public explanation rather than a complete laboratory standard. Abstinence, container, transport, temperature, timing, repeat-testing, and acceptance rules can differ by test and laboratory. Holding a cup near body temperature is a source-described tactic, not a universal transport protocol. Collection instructions should come from the receiving laboratory, and one result should not be interpreted without clinical context.

## What Changed
- Created a pre-analytic quality frame connecting privacy and room design to specimen validity and treatment timing.
