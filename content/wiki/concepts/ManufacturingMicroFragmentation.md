# Manufacturing Micro-Fragmentation / 制造业小微化

Manufacturing micro-fragmentation is the source's claim that some large-factory production has been split into smaller process units. In [[ruci-dagong-30-nian-liushuixian-shang-de-miankong-yu-shijian-lodsuktwtcfxnhmzwum5iesbzbme]], [[ZhanTeacher|占老师]] says products with hundreds of process steps may now be divided among many small shops, with some couple-run factories handling worker, manager, driver, purchasing, quality-control, and loading roles at once.

The concept complements [[FlexibleManufacturing|柔性制造]] with a risk-distribution lens. Fragmentation can improve responsiveness and reduce fixed overhead for upstream customers, but it can also push capital, labor, logistics, and quality burdens into smaller firms and families.

## Key Claims
- Micro-fragmentation is not the same as smart-factory flexibility, although the two can coexist in one supply chain.
- Smaller shops survive by owning narrow process know-how, fast response, and customer trust.
- Fragmentation can blur employee, owner, manager, and household roles.
- The pattern turns [[ContinuousLearningAgainstDisplacement|continuous learning]] into a survival condition for workers and small operators.
