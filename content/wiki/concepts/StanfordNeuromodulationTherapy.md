# Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy

## Definition
Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy is the accelerated, high-dose TMS treatment protocol Williams describes as a targeted stimulation approach for high-acuity depression.

## Current Synthesis
In both sources, [[NolanWilliams]] says SAINT originally stood for Stanford Accelerated Intelligent Neuromodulation Therapy and is now called Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy. He distinguishes the TMS device from the treatment protocol: the therapy combines stimulation with a chosen region, individualized resting-state connectivity target, timing, spacing, and dose for a specific psychiatric condition. Its design goal is high-acuity depression, where slower or less intensive options may not fit the clinical urgency.

The key synthesis is individualized targeting plus compression and learning-like timing. Traditional TMS is described as once daily across six weeks, while SNT delivers ten hourly blocks per day across five days, with roughly 90 minutes of actual stimulation inside the larger schedule. Williams frames the signal as asking the prefrontal cortex to turn on and remain on. Reported remission rates of roughly 60% to 90% and durability ranging from relapse to multi-year remission are promising, but remain source-scoped and context-dependent.

## Key Claims
- SNT is a protocol, not merely the presence of a TMS machine.
- The protocol targets a specific brain region for a specific high-acuity psychiatric use case.
- It reorganizes stimulation around spaced learning theory and repeated hourly sessions.
- Resting-state functional connectivity is used to individualize the dorsolateral-prefrontal target relative to the subgenual cingulate.
- The source contrasts six-week daily TMS with a five-day, high-dose schedule.
- Williams reports rapid remission in substantial fractions of treated patients, depending on trial or open-label context.
- Durability varies, with some patients maintaining remission much longer than others.

- Outcome claims - [[essentials-psychedelics-neurostimulation-for-brain-rewiring-dr-nolan-williams-scim5296180263]] and [[psychedelics-neurostimulation-for-brain-rewiring-dr-nolan-williams-scim3664218578]] report 60% to 90% remission in different contexts and variable durability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide a patient-selection protocol, contraindication list, operational manual, or independent systematic review. Reported remission ranges combine different trial and open-label contexts, and FDA clearance does not settle access, comparative effectiveness, maintenance schedules, or long-term durability. SNT should remain a clinically delivered treatment concept, not a generic productivity or mood-reset technique.

## What Changed
- Added individualized functional-connectivity targeting, actual stimulation-time context, access limits, and the unresolved maintenance problem.
