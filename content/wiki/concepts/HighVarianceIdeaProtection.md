# High-Variance Idea Protection

## Definition
High-variance idea protection is the deliberate preservation of people and early concepts whose rare exceptional contributions may coexist with messy, inconsistent, or initially unconvincing output.

## Current Synthesis
[[DanielEk|Daniel Ek]] uses model “temperature” as an analogy in [[scim3646778859-scim3646778859]]: some people produce unreliable or ordinary material much of the time but occasionally generate an idea with outsized value. Large organizations are designed to improve known products predictably, so new ideas may need explicit protection before evidence and consensus are available. The useful principle is not tolerance without limit; it is to judge a creative contributor across a portfolio while giving fragile ideas a bounded route to development and evaluation.

## Key Claims
- Average-output evaluation can miss rare ideas whose value dominates a creator's portfolio.
- Mature operating systems tend to reward reliable optimization more readily than uncertain invention.
- Early ideas need protection because their first form rarely contains the proof demanded of established products.
- Leaders can evaluate best contributions without ignoring reliability, conduct, or execution costs.
- Bounded experiments can preserve variance while limiting organizational exposure.

## Evidence
- Temperature analogy - [[scim3646778859-scim3646778859]] compares high-variance creators to high-temperature model output that occasionally produces exceptional ideas.
- Portfolio judgment - [[scim3646778859-scim3646778859]] describes judging people by their best ideas rather than only by their worst output.
- Organizational protection - [[scim3646778859-scim3646778859]] says Ek spends substantial time protecting the first seed of new ideas inside an older company.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Rare brilliance does not excuse harmful behavior, chronic non-delivery, or unlimited resource use. “High variance” can become an unfalsifiable label applied after success, and organizations need explicit time, safety, budget, and review boundaries so creative protection does not shift hidden costs onto others.
