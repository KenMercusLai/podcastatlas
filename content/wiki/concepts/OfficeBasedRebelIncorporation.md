# 授官式招安 / Office-Based Rebel Incorporation

授官式招安 / office-based rebel incorporation enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-933-weiguan-zhidao-yousuo-wei-yousuo-buwei-lopulnufsagoqyx7nngvcqi2nhrj|Hanji 933]] as the court response to [[ZhangYanLateHan|张燕]] and the [[HeishanArmy|黑山军]]. After the mountain forces grow large enough to disturb commanderies and counties north of the Yellow River, [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s court no longer has enough suppression capacity. Zhang Yan's envoy to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] therefore receives office rather than annihilation.

The pattern is pragmatic but corrosive. Granting Zhang Yan a Zhonglangjiang role, delegated local order, annual xiaolian recommendation, and reporting channel gives the court a cheap way to convert an armed outsider into a semi-official manager. At the same time, it shows how weak coercive capacity can force the court to recognize the very armed network it failed to control.

This concept is adjacent to [[OccupationPacificationStrategy|占领区怀柔治理]], but the sequence is different. Occupation pacification follows victory or entry; office-based rebel incorporation follows non-victory, when title grant substitutes for suppression and makes an armed leader useful enough to tolerate.

## Key Claims

- A weak court can use title and office to incorporate a rebel leader when direct suppression is unavailable.
- Incorporation may reduce immediate conflict while preserving the rebel leader's local power base.
- Recommendation and reporting privileges turn a military outsider into a partial administrator without proving full loyalty.
- The policy keeps [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] alive as a tool, but also exposes how formal title can be used to cover loss of command capacity.
