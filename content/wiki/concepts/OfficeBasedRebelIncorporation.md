# 授官式招安 / Office-Based Rebel Incorporation

## Definition
授官式招安 / office-based rebel incorporation is the statecraft pattern in which a ruler or court grants office, title, or delegated authority to an armed rebel or semi-autonomous force because suppression is costly and incorporation can convert danger into usable order.

## Current Synthesis
The concept begins with [[ZhangYanLateHan|张燕]] and the [[HeishanArmy|黑山军]] in Hanji 933. After the mountain force grows large enough to disturb commanderies north of the Yellow River, [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s court cannot suppress it directly. Zhang Yan's envoy to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] therefore receives a Zhonglangjiang role, local-order responsibilities, and recommendation/reporting privileges.

Hanji 1062 adds the later warlord version under [[CaoCao|曹操]]. Zhang Yan again has something stronger powers need: a hard-to-clear mountain base, remaining troops, and influence in the Yuan-family rear. When [[YuanShao|袁绍]]'s heirs weaken each other, Zhang Yan offers help to Cao Cao and receives appointment as Pingbei general; the episode also frames later title and marquis treatment as a way to convert remaining military value into security.

The pattern is pragmatic but double-edged. Office grants can reduce immediate conflict and make surrender or cooperation legible, but they also reveal the limits of coercive control. The stronger side is not simply rewarding virtue; it is pricing the cost of suppression against the benefit of turning an armed outsider into a subordinate.

## Key Claims
- Title and office can substitute for battlefield suppression when an armed group is too costly to erase.
- Incorporation works best when the rebel leader still has local capacity worth converting.
- Formal rank gives surrender or cooperation a public vocabulary, making it easier for followers and other powers to recognize the new arrangement.
- The policy preserves [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]] as an instrument but exposes how weak or overstretched power uses names to cover incomplete control.
- Hanji 1062 adds that the same actor can pass through multiple incorporation phases: imperial-court delegation first, then Cao-side warlord settlement later.
- The pattern is adjacent to defector incorporation, but narrower: it focuses on armed rebel or semi-autonomous forces being brought under office rather than any former enemy being pardoned.

## Evidence
- Early court grant: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-933-weiguan-zhidao-yousuo-wei-yousuo-buwei-lopulnufsagoqyx7nngvcqi2nhrj|Hanji 933]] says Zhang Yan sends an envoy to Luoyang and receives office after the court cannot suppress the Black Mountain force directly.
- Delegated order: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-933-weiguan-zhidao-yousuo-wei-yousuo-buwei-lopulnufsagoqyx7nngvcqi2nhrj|Hanji 933]] says the appointment gives Zhang Yan responsibility in the northern Yellow River mountain zone, including recommendation and reporting functions.
- Cao-side appointment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1062-shanzei-de-nixi-daozei-chushen-de-ta-shi-ruhe-zhandui-de-lvkhmezd68key-p-kup55zypbjet|Hanji 1062]] says Zhang Yan approaches Cao Cao, asks to help attack the Yuan brothers, and receives the Pingbei-general appointment.
- Value-based settlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1062-shanzei-de-nixi-daozei-chushen-de-ta-shi-ruhe-zhandui-de-lvkhmezd68key-p-kup55zypbjet|Hanji 1062]] interprets Zhang Yan's settlement through the value of remaining difficult-to-destroy force and useful mountain position.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Office-based incorporation does not prove stable loyalty or clean state capacity. Hanji 933 is explicitly a non-suppression settlement by a weak late-Han court, and Hanji 1062 still says Cao Cao pairs military pressure with reward. Exact offices, territorial authority, and later title sequence remain source-scoped. This concept should also stay distinct from [[StrategicDefectorIncorporation|纳叛安抚式整合]], which covers broader former-enemy and defector settlement after battlefield or political collapse.
