# Primary Electorate Distortion

Primary electorate distortion is the risk that candidates mistake primary-voter preferences for the preferences of the wider electorate. [[socialist-mobility-a-telling-michigan-vote-6a74600ec3e1347e8b5bf216]] uses [[AbdulElSayed]]'s Michigan win as a live case: [[JohnPrideaux]] says Democratic primary voters may want change and disruption, but some of El-Sayed's positions are not popular nationally.

The concept is not anti-primary. It treats primaries as necessary selection mechanisms that can still push candidates toward positions, rhetoric, or self-understanding that may be costly in a general election.

## Key Claims
- Primary voters can be more ideological or more change-seeking than general-election voters.
- Winning a primary can make a candidate's platform feel more nationally popular than it is.
- The distortion is strongest when party activists reward disruption and the general election rewards coalition breadth.
- General-election opponents can exploit positions that helped distinguish a candidate in the primary.
