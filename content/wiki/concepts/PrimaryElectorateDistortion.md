# Primary Electorate Distortion

## Definition

Primary electorate distortion is the risk that candidates, parties, or leaders mistake the preferences and loyalty tests that select a nominee for the preferences of the wider electorate that decides the general election.

## Current Synthesis

The Michigan source introduces the problem from the Democratic left: [[AbdulElSayed]]'s primary victory can show demand for disruption without proving that his full platform is nationally popular. The 2026 midterm source supplies a Republican version: [[DonaldTrump]] is reported to retain near-total power over endorsed primary candidates while their general-election win rate falls sharply.

Together the sources show that distortion is not tied to one ideology. Primaries are necessary selection mechanisms, but their participants, incentives, issue salience, and loyalty signals can differ from those of a statewide or national electorate. The effect is conditional rather than automatic because candidate quality, the opponent, partisanship, and the national environment still shape the final result.

## Key Claims

- Primary voters can be more ideological, loyal, or change-seeking than general-election voters.
- Winning nomination can make a candidate's positions or personal alliances feel more broadly popular than they are.
- The mismatch is strongest when party selectors reward contrast or loyalty while the general election rewards coalition breadth.
- Distortion can operate in both progressive and conservative parties.
- General-election results test translation but do not isolate the primary's causal effect from the national environment.

## Evidence

- Democratic case: [[socialist-mobility-a-telling-michigan-vote-6a74600ec3e1347e8b5bf216]] treats El-Sayed's Michigan win as meaningful while noting that he won below 50% and that some positions may be unpopular nationally.
- Republican cycle comparison: [[375-zhongqi-xuanju-canbai-hou-gonghedang-hequ-hecong-lgz0q02naefuostkur2-w84ksjnr]] reports Trump-endorsed candidates winning 96% of 2026 primaries but 50% of general elections.
- Candidate examples: [[375-zhongqi-xuanju-canbai-hou-gonghedang-hequ-hecong-lgz0q02naefuostkur2-w84ksjnr]] uses Ken Paxton in Texas and an inexperienced South Carolina nominee to illustrate how loyalty-backed primary success may fail to expand outward.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The Michigan primary source predates the general-election result, while the 2026 episode's endorsement figures lack a supplied candidate list, competitiveness adjustment, or counterfactual. A candidate can lose because of a national wave, local conditions, opponent quality, or campaign performance rather than primary distortion alone.
