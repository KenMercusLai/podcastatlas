# Rejection Tolerance Practice

## Definition
Rejection tolerance practice is the source-scoped idea that a person builds relational, professional, and entrepreneurial capacity by repeatedly making respectful approaches where "no" is expected and survivable.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats rejection as a training signal rather than a verdict on identity. Galloway argues that dating, money, public work, and entrepreneurship all require exposure to refusal; Huberman frames this as an especially important issue for young men who fear modern surveillance, workplace punishment, online judgment, or being seen as intrusive. The practice is therefore not pressure tactics. It is learning respectful initiative, tolerating embarrassment, and updating without resentment.

## Key Claims
- "No" is part of the goal because successful social and professional life requires repeated attempts.
- Respectful romantic approaches are usually less catastrophic than many young men imagine, but they still require judgment and context.
- Rejection tolerance should reduce resentment, not license entitlement.
- The same skill transfers across dating, friendship, work, entrepreneurship, and public criticism.
- Mortality awareness can increase willingness to express affection, take risks, and endure criticism.
- Rejection practice works best when paired with offline routines that build competence and real social contact.

## Evidence
- Opening frame: [[male-roles-obligations-options-for-building-a-fulfilling-life-scott-galloway-scim7501986841]] opens with the idea that "no" is not failure but part of the goal.
- Approach practice: [[male-roles-obligations-options-for-building-a-fulfilling-life-scott-galloway-scim7501986841]] has Galloway teach young men to practice friendship or romantic approaches with the explicit aim of surviving rejection.
- Dating fear qualification: [[male-roles-obligations-options-for-building-a-fulfilling-life-scott-galloway-scim7501986841]] says respectful approaches are usually not the personal or professional risk many young men imagine.
- Mortality and risk: [[male-roles-obligations-options-for-building-a-fulfilling-life-scott-galloway-scim7501986841]] has Galloway say mortality acceptance helped him take risks, express affection, start businesses, and endure criticism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not erase real risks around harassment, workplace boundaries, safety, power imbalance, or unwanted attention. The practice depends on respect, situational judgment, and accepting refusal immediately. It should not be used to pressure people or dismiss women's accounts of bad approaches.
