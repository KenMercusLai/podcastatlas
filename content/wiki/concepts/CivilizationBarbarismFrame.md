# Civilization-Barbarism Frame / 文明-野蛮框架

Civilization-barbarism frame / 文明-野蛮框架 is the rhetoric [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-3-lj-cr4vg4e1qdfjs6yez85-3zuiv]] draws from [[LuZhonglian|鲁仲连]]'s argument against [[XinyuanYan|新垣衍]]. Lu Zhonglian does not treat [[SubmissionThroughStatusElevation|honoring Qin as emperor]] as a neutral title adjustment. He reframes it as accepting a violent order that has abandoned ritual and righteousness.

The frame turns a diplomatic choice into a civilizational boundary. If [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] were a sage-king figure, submission might be defensible as joining legitimate order. Lu Zhonglian's move is to deny that premise: [[QinState|秦国]] is cast as rule by force and head-taking, so recognizing Qin's imperial status becomes complicity in the destruction of civilized life.

The episode also keeps the frame under critique. The host says the binary is rhetorically powerful but historically unstable: the eastern states were not necessarily more humane, [[FanJu|范雎]]'s abuse in Wei shows elite private violence outside Qin, and [[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] gave Qin a kind of legal procedure that complicates the "barbaric Qin, civilized six states" split.

## Key Claims
- A civilization-barbarism frame raises the stakes of ordinary strategy by making accommodation look like moral and cultural surrender.
- The frame can support anti-submission politics by making survival under the stronger power appear worse than death or continued resistance.
- The frame is not automatically reliable as history; it may select examples that make one side look civilized and the other side monstrous.
- In this source, the frame works together with [[DignityBasedPersuasion|尊严激将式说服]] and [[InterestReframingPersuasion|利益重构式说服]] rather than replacing practical self-interest.
- The episode's critique makes the concept useful only when paired with source discipline: rhetorical force and evidentiary strength are separate questions.
