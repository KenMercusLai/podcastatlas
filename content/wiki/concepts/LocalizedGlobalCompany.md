# Localized Global Company / 中国籍全球公司本地化

## Definition
A localized global company is a Chinese-headquartered business that becomes part of a foreign market's manufacturing, supply, service, regulatory, talent, and R&D system rather than treating that market only as an export destination.

## Current Synthesis
The annual-report source defines three deep layers: manufacturing localization, supply-chain and industrial-cluster localization, and R&D localization. Factories alone do not solve labor cost, ramp efficiency, tariff sharing, compliance, repair, talent, or product-validity problems; coordination advantages must travel through local institutions.

The vehicle cases add a consumer-facing layer. [[BYD]]'s Korean growth is qualified by a much smaller service footprint than Hyundai and Kia, while [[XPeng]] and [[Chery]] are reported to be establishing local entities and considering Canadian production. Durable overseas share therefore requires the product, service network, legal presence, and trade-policy response to develop together.

## Key Claims
- Overseas manufacturing is necessary in some markets but insufficient by itself.
- Supply-chain localization includes warehousing, suppliers, customs, repair, and service capacity.
- R&D and user understanding matter where local regulation, evidence, climate, infrastructure, or preferences shape product validity.
- Local service density can constrain growth even when design and price are competitive.
- Localization converts globalization from a sales decision into a management, capital, knowledge, and institution-building task.

## Evidence
### Three-layer localization model
- [[165-nianbaoji-zhong-de-zhenshi-zhongguo-2026-lpredevu-gakn92dwutmulytmslo]] uses annual-report cases to separate manufacturing, supply-chain, and R&D localization under a security-first trade order.

### Vehicle service and production cases
- [[dafeixin-guonei-hangsi-tiaozheng-hangxian-buju-jianfeiyao-liuxing-huo-yingxiang-pijiuye-gkwriaiotragi7matv3jls]] contrasts BYD's reported Korean sales growth with its limited service points and cites XPeng and Chery legal-entity or production moves.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Localization can raise wages, duplicate assets, reduce near-term efficiency, and still fail to overcome tariffs or weak demand.
- The vehicle sales, service-center, tariff, and production-plan figures are episode-attributed and time-sensitive.
- A local legal entity or announced factory is not evidence of an integrated local operating system.

## What Changed
- Added after-sales service density as a first-order localization constraint.
- Added Korean and Canadian vehicle cases linking market entry to legal presence and production planning.
- Qualified announcements and sales growth as incomplete evidence of durable local integration.
