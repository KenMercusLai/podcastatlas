# Expected Goals as Process Metric

Expected goals as process metric is the source's warning that xG should be read as evidence about chance creation, attacking structure, and shot quality rather than as a simple blame score for strikers. In [[ep-13-soccer-analytics-through-the-lens-of-coaching]], [[BrunoSoccerCoach|Bruno]] says chance quality depends on the pass, body position, final-third entry, and how the attack develops before the shot.

The [[HoustonDynamo]] example makes the point concrete. Bruno argues that attacking struggles can come from weak offensive playmaking and poor final-third creation, not only from lacking a finisher. That keeps xG tied to [[CoachingIntegratedSoccerAnalytics]] because the coach has to decide whether the training response should target movement, service, positioning, set pieces, or personnel.

The concept also narrows [[SportsPredictiveModeling]]. xG can be predictive and comparative, but this source uses it mainly as a diagnostic language for action: what kind of chance did the team create, why did it look that way, and what practice change might improve the next one?

## Key Claims
- xG should be interpreted through game context, not only through the final shot.
- Poor finishing and poor chance creation are different problems that can look similar in the scoreline.
- Pass quality, body position, final-third entry, and tactical structure shape shot quality.
- Coaches need to turn the metric into a training or tactical adjustment.
- The metric is a beginner-friendly entry point, but not a complete model of soccer performance.
