# Emotional Trigger-Source Separation / 情绪触发源分离

## Definition
Emotional trigger-source separation is the practice of distinguishing the facts and responsibility in a current event from the older emotional pattern that makes the event feel disproportionately threatening, humiliating, unjust, or abandoning.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats intense reactions as meaningful without assuming that their full force comes from the present incident. A criticism can contain a factual question while also activating an older history of unjust blame; a missed appointment can be inconsiderate while also reactivating abandonment fear. Separating these layers preserves both reality and agency: first establish what happened, then identify what the reaction is protecting, and only then choose an action.

The practice is neither self-gaslighting nor a demand to excuse others. "Facts are facts, responsibility is responsibility" means present harm and accountability stay visible. The added question is whether improving one's content, winning the argument, or forcing reassurance could ever resolve a wound that predates the current scene. Support, reflection, dialogue, courses, and repeated practice may help, while severe depression, trauma, or safety risk can require professional care.

## Key Claims
- Current events and older emotional wounds can both contribute to the intensity of a reaction.
- Fact checking should precede interpretation when criticism or perceived rejection triggers anger or grievance.
- Naming the older fear can widen response choice without erasing present responsibility.
- Overcorrecting the current task may fail when the actual pain comes from unjust blame, abandonment, or another repeated pattern.
- Regulation improves through repeated observation and practice rather than a one-time insight.
- The method must not be used to excuse abuse, deny grief, or privatize every social conflict as personal pathology.

## Evidence
- Unjust-blame trigger - [[vol-56-duitan-mizi-rensheng-shi-renling-ziji-yu-queli-shiming-de-guocheng-lgrbpfubc8s6y59jb6tsd6glz-c]] has Mizi connect unusually strong reactions to being wronged with a childhood history of carrying a friend's responsibility.
- Abandonment trigger - [[vol-56-duitan-mizi-rensheng-shi-renling-ziji-yu-queli-shiming-de-guocheng-lgrbpfubc8s6y59jb6tsd6glz-c]] has Yang Tianzhen trace her intolerance of being stood up to an activated feeling of abandonment.
- Fact-first practice - [[vol-56-duitan-mizi-rensheng-shi-renling-ziji-yu-queli-shiming-de-guocheng-lgrbpfubc8s6y59jb6tsd6glz-c]] recommends confirming the event, observing the reaction, talking with the injured self, and not letting the stress state directly choose behavior.
- Learning process - [[vol-56-duitan-mizi-rensheng-shi-renling-ziji-yu-queli-shiming-de-guocheng-lgrbpfubc8s6y59jb6tsd6glz-c]] presents emotional management as improvement through courses, conversation, and practice over several years.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The examples are personal reflections, not diagnoses or a validated treatment protocol. A strong response can be proportionate to current discrimination, betrayal, danger, or repeated boundary violation. Childhood explanation is not required for every emotion, and self-dialogue is not a substitute for clinical care during severe depression, trauma symptoms, or acute risk.

## What Changed
- Created a fact-preserving framework for separating present incidents from older emotional amplification.
