# Animal Uplift Ethics

## Definition
Animal uplift ethics examines whether and under what conditions humans should genetically or technologically expand another species' cognition, communication, agency, or ability to manipulate its environment.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents uplift as a double-edged intervention. Expanded capacities might reduce some suffering or enable political voice, but they could also impose human values, labor demands, self-conscious anxiety, accelerated conflict, and irreversible change without meaningful consent. The ethical baseline is therefore not maximum resemblance to humans but species-sensitive flourishing and governance of imposed risk.

## Key Claims
- Technical feasibility does not establish moral permission.
- Greater cognition can create new burdens as well as capabilities.
- Human traits are not a neutral or universal standard of flourishing.
- Consent is especially difficult before an intervention creates the capacity to consent.
- Uplift can transform ecological and political relationships beyond the individual subject.

## Evidence

- [[02026-entangled-intelligences-podcast-02026-entangled-intelligences-podcast]] discusses larger cognitive capacities, manipulators, and humanized genetic changes as a moral dilemma.

### Unwanted consequences
- [[02026-entangled-intelligences-podcast-02026-entangled-intelligences-podcast]] uses the banana-slug and post-uplift political scenes to dramatize involuntary acceleration, anxiety, war, labor, and representation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The scenarios are speculative and do not establish that uplift is technically achievable at the depicted scale. Refusing intervention can also preserve preventable suffering, while conservation and welfare measures may change animals without being uplift. Species interests are internally diverse and cannot be inferred from one imagined representative.

## What Changed
- Established uplift as a consent, flourishing, burden, and system-governance problem.
