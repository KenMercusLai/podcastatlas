# Living Neuron Computing

## Definition
Living neuron computing is the attempt to use living neural cultures as dynamic information-processing systems by stimulating them, recording their electrical activity, and interpreting their responses.

## Current Synthesis
The source makes living neuron computing both tangible and unresolved. Researchers can place neurons on electrodes, deliver electrical stimulation, measure spikes, and convert signals between analog biological activity and digital analysis. The hard problem is control and meaning: neurons are plastic, continuously changing systems, and the episode says researchers do not yet fully understand the encoding scheme of neural activity in vitro.

## Key Claims
- Living neurons already process information in brains, but isolated cultures require new control methods.
- Neural cultures are dynamic and plastic, so they change over time rather than behaving like fixed logic circuits.
- Electrical spikes can be recorded and analyzed digitally after analog-to-digital conversion.
- Digital instructions can be converted back into analog stimulation through electrodes.
- Learning in vitro and neural encoding are the main scientific bottlenecks.

## Evidence
### Interface mechanics
- [[ep-37-neurons-future-of-ai-processing]] describes neurons on electrodes, analog spikes, digital analysis, and electrical stimulation sent back through the same interface.

### Plasticity and control
- [[ep-37-neurons-future-of-ai-processing]] says neurons are dynamic systems that process information continuously and change over time.

### Encoding uncertainty
- [[ep-37-neurons-future-of-ai-processing]] says researchers can see activity and signals being processed but do not yet know exactly what that activity means.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The source does not establish a stable programming model comparable to software for silicon processors.
- Observing spikes is not equivalent to understanding the neural code.
- In vitro learning may not map cleanly onto how brains learn inside living organisms.
