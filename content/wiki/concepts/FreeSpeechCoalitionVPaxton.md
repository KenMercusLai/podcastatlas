# Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton

## Definition
Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton is the Supreme Court age-verification case cited in [[metas-youth-safety-settlement-could-change-how-people-access-the-internet]] as allowing Texas to require identity verification for adult content that would be obscene for minors.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats the case as a boundary marker, not a blanket permission slip for identity checks across the web. [[WillCreeley]] says the decision concerned pornography or other adult material and should not be read as authorizing broad identification requirements for access to general-purpose platforms such as Facebook.

## Key Claims
- The case is relevant to online age verification because it concerns a Texas adult-content verification requirement.
- The source reads the holding as narrower than general social-media access.
- Its relevance depends on content category: adult or obscene-for-minors material is different from broad lawful speech on social platforms.
- The case can be misread if policymakers treat it as support for identity checks across ordinary online communication.
