# AI Empire Sovereignty Risk

## Definition
AI Empire Sovereignty Risk is the danger that societies become dependent on AI systems controlled by foreign states or corporations, giving outside actors durable influence over information, infrastructure, decisions, and political life without formal conquest.

## Current Synthesis
[[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] reframes AI diffusion as a sovereignty problem. Harari's warning is that AI systems built in the [[UnitedStates]] or [[China]] can enter other societies through phones, apps, assistants, infrastructure, or enterprise systems while remaining loyal to their builders' incentives and jurisdictions. The "empire" risk is less about armies and more about who controls the cognition, mediation, and decision systems on which a society depends.

## Key Claims
- AI dependence can create sovereignty loss without direct territorial control.
- Foreign-controlled AI systems may mediate information, economic choice, and public administration inside another country.
- Corporate loyalty and state jurisdiction matter because AI systems are not neutral once embedded in daily infrastructure.
- The risk applies especially to smaller or less technically sovereign countries.
- Democratic accountability weakens when critical AI systems answer to external builders, investors, or governments.

## Evidence
- Foreign-control frame: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] discusses AI systems from the United States or China entering other societies while remaining controlled elsewhere.
- Infrastructure dependence: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] ties AI to devices, digital services, finance, and decision systems rather than treating it as a single app category.
- Political loyalty problem: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] asks whom AI systems serve when their builders' interests diverge from users' societies.
- Democratic accountability: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] links sovereignty concerns to elections and public questioning of AI policy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not argue that all imported AI is domination or that domestic AI automatically protects sovereignty. Foreign models can provide benefits, and local systems can still be abusive or unaccountable. The risk concerns asymmetric dependence on external AI infrastructure without credible accountability or exit options.
