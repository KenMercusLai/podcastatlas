# 降人传令式安民 / Surrendered Local Messenger Pacification

## Definition
降人传令式安民 / surrendered local messenger pacification is the post-capture order tactic of sending a recently surrendered but locally trusted intermediary to carry the victor's commands, so frightened officials and commoners can interpret the new regime as governable authority rather than indiscriminate violence.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1068 creates the concept through [[LiFuLateHan|李孚]] after [[CaoCao|曹操]] takes [[NanpiLateHan|南皮]]. Li Fu diagnoses the city as unstable because officials and commoners cannot yet tell what the new power will do or whether they should keep fighting, flee, or kill each other.

His proposed solution is not simply proclamation from the conqueror. He asks Cao Cao to use someone who has just surrendered but remains trusted by people inside the city. Li Fu then enters, relays Cao Cao's order that people should keep their posts and stop internal killing, and the episode says order quickly stabilizes.

The pattern is narrower than general [[OccupationPacificationStrategy|occupation pacification]]. It focuses on the credibility of the first messenger after capture: the message works because it combines the victor's coercive authority with a familiar local voice.

## Key Claims
- Newly captured cities face an information problem as much as a coercion problem.
- A victor's direct order may not be trusted until it is carried through a locally credible channel.
- Recent surrender can become useful capacity when the surrendered person still has social trust inside the city.
- The first pacification message should reduce uncertainty about ordinary offices, daily life, and mutual violence.
- The tactic belongs to occupation order repair, but it is distinct from broader amnesty, elite recruitment, or long-term governance reform.

## Evidence
- Disorder diagnosis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1068-yuanshao-qushi-hou-caocao-shi-ruhe-xiaomie-yuantan-de-llebqjk-dz18ajvgcjkut7-qha1o|Hanji 1068]] says Li Fu describes Nanpi's officials and commoners as frightened and unable to distinguish the new balance after the city falls.
- Messenger selection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1068-yuanshao-qushi-hou-caocao-shi-ruhe-xiaomie-yuantan-de-llebqjk-dz18ajvgcjkut7-qha1o|Hanji 1068]] has Li Fu advise using a recently surrendered person trusted by the city to transmit Cao Cao's order.
- Order content: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1068-yuanshao-qushi-hou-caocao-shi-ruhe-xiaomie-yuantan-de-llebqjk-dz18ajvgcjkut7-qha1o|Hanji 1068]] says Li Fu tells officials and commoners to remain at their posts and stop killing one another.
- Result: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1068-yuanshao-qushi-hou-caocao-shi-ruhe-xiaomie-yuantan-de-llebqjk-dz18ajvgcjkut7-qha1o|Hanji 1068]] reports that Nanpi's people quickly settle after the message.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept currently rests on one short source. It should not be generalized into proof that surrendered intermediaries always pacify captured cities. The tactic depends on the messenger's real credibility, the victor's willingness to restrain disorder, and the city's remaining administrative structure. Hanji 1068 also keeps Li Fu's motive and the speed of stabilization source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Created from Hanji 1068 to capture Li Fu's post-Nanpi order-repair tactic.
