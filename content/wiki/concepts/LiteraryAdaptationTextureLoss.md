# Literary Adaptation Texture Loss / 文学改编质感流失

## Definition
Literary adaptation texture loss is the pattern where a screen adaptation preserves plot events but loses the roughness, duration, material conditions, interior ambiguity, or point-of-view discipline that made the source text meaningful.

## Current Synthesis
The Culture Limited episode names this problem through [[WhereTheCrawdadsSingFilm|《沼泽深处的女孩》]]. The hosts' complaint is not simply that the film differs from [[WhereTheCrawdadsSing|《蝲蛄吟唱的地方》]], but that prettified images, compressed childhood, simplified poetry, and unstable perspective make [[KyaClark|基亚]]'s poverty, loneliness, and moral ambiguity less credible.

## Key Claims
- Plot fidelity is not enough if the adaptation removes the material pressure that explains character behavior.
- Beautification can make marginality look aesthetic rather than lived and costly.
- Compression is most damaging when it reduces childhood survival into backstory instead of formation.
- Omitting or simplifying a literary identity can remove how the source text processes experience.
- Point-of-view shifts can weaken a mystery's final moral force when the reveal depends on information withheld by a different narrative logic.

- Beautified marginality: [[vol-350-lagu-yinchang-de-difang-nengbuneng-rangwo-ananjingjing-dangge-bianyuanren-gkwridooapxpaz2h0wtphrfr]] criticizes the film's polished visuals and Kya's overly beautiful presentation.
- Childhood compression: [[vol-350-lagu-yinchang-de-difang-nengbuneng-rangwo-ananjingjing-dangge-bianyuanren-gkwridooapxpaz2h0wtphrfr]] says the film shortens Kya's childhood survival material.
- Literary identity simplification: [[vol-350-lagu-yinchang-de-difang-nengbuneng-rangwo-ananjingjing-dangge-bianyuanren-gkwridooapxpaz2h0wtphrfr]] notes the simplification of the poet identity.
- Point-of-view instability: [[vol-350-lagu-yinchang-de-difang-nengbuneng-rangwo-ananjingjing-dangge-bianyuanren-gkwridooapxpaz2h0wtphrfr]] says the ending's suspense reveal jars against the earlier subjective frame.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is an adaptation-critique concept, not a rule that every film must reproduce every novelistic element. It applies where omitted texture changes the story's moral or emotional intelligibility.
