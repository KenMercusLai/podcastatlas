# Urban Everyday Fiction

Urban everyday fiction is the source's frame for novels that make ordinary market-town life, households, food, clothing, business, law, worship, gossip, transport, and entertainment narratively central. In [[fanwai-15-banlatte-jiang-jinpingmei-la-erqie-haishi-luse-de-991508433]], [[Banlatte|半拿铁]] uses [[JinPingMei|《金瓶梅》]] as the primary case, calling it a 市井小说 rather than reducing it to erotic scandal.

The concept matters because everyday detail can carry literary force and historical evidence at once. [[XimenQing|西门庆]]'s drug shop, [[WangPo|王婆]]'s tea shop, [[WuDalang|武大郎]]'s炊饼 route, [[PanJinlian|潘金莲]]'s window, sworn-brother gifts, funerals, religious ceremony, and banquet/food detail all show how social life works through spaces and transactions that elite histories may flatten.

## Key Claims
- Everyday urban scenes can be major literary architecture, not decorative background.
- Food, housing, clothing, shops, ritual, and gossip can reveal status, gender, money, and power.
- The method complements [[FictionAsHistoricalEvidence]] because fiction can preserve social possibility and atmosphere without serving as a direct record.
- It also complements [[EverydayFoodCultureHistory]] and [[MaterialHistoryNarrative]] when repeated objects and meals expose social organization.
