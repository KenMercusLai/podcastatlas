# Pistachio Supply Shock / 开心果供应冲击

## Definition
Pistachio supply shock is a shortage mechanism in which concentrated production, simultaneous climate damage, irrigation and groundwater constraints, shipping disruption, import dependence, and slow orchard maturation tighten available supply faster than growers can respond.

## Current Synthesis
The source describes a compound rather than single-cause shock. China's small domestic output makes it dependent on the United States and Iran. Iranian heat, drought, power-related irrigation interruptions, and transport risk coincide with California heat, pollination failure, empty shells, and groundwater limits. This leaves importers exposed to two leading origins at once.

The adjustment is slow because pistachios are a perennial crop: new acreage does not become prompt market supply. Warehouse-club limits, resale markups, manufacturer price pressure, and possible recipe substitution are downstream signals of the mismatch, but each reflects retailer and product choices as well as the underlying harvest shock.

## Key Claims
- Concentrated import origins make simultaneous regional losses more consequential than an isolated harvest problem.
- Heat affects output through biological timing such as pollination and shell filling, not only through a generic annual temperature average.
- Power, irrigation, groundwater, and shipping systems can amplify agricultural weather losses.
- New planting cannot quickly rebalance supply because first harvest and full yield take years.
- Retail limits can ration scarce low-priced inventory while also creating resale opportunities.
- Manufacturers can raise price, reduce availability, or substitute ingredients, but defining pistachio products have less flexibility.

## Evidence
- Import concentration: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] reports that the United States and Iran supplied about 68% and 31% of China's pistachio imports.
- Simultaneous production pressure: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] attributes Iranian losses to heat, drought, and irrigation interruption and California losses to heat-disrupted pollination and empty shells.
- Logistics and resource constraint: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] adds Hormuz-related shipping risk, alternative-route cost, and California groundwater restrictions.
- Biological lag and retail signal: [[kafeidou-shanmu-xiangou-huangniu-jiajia-kaixinguo-weishenme-biande-jinqiao-gkwriw4or9-vag3oegttwe_s]] reports roughly eight years to first harvest, twelve to maximum output, warehouse-club limits, and resale markups.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
All quantities and causal attributions are source-reported. A purchase limit does not by itself measure national scarcity, and resale activity can reflect a temporary price gap rather than durable shortage. Argentina and other regions may expand supply, while future weather, inventories, yields, demand substitution, and trade routes could change the duration. The episode does not separate every contribution to the observed price increase.

## What Changed
- Established a pistachio-specific compound-shock model linking two major origins, infrastructure, logistics, and biological lag.
- Distinguished a real supply constraint from the retailer-specific rationing and resale behavior that makes it visible.
