# 体检结果情境化解读 / Screening Result Interpretation

## Definition
Screening result interpretation is the process of reading a checkup finding through its magnitude, trend, reference system, symptoms, temporary influences, imaging, history, and clinical actionability rather than treating an arrow, plus sign, or isolated number as a diagnosis.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-139-dangdai-dagongren-tijian-zhinan-ruhe-xuan-xiangmu-ruhe-bikeng-baogao-zenme-kan-gongli-yiyuan-yisheng-lai-zhizhao-lucd43f_m0x0fdw09zm6iszatgtb|VOL.139]] distinguishes abnormal detection from diagnostic meaning. Laboratory reference ranges can vary by assay and institution, while sleep, exercise, food, alcohol, infection, pregnancy, medication, and recent health state can shift some measurements. The relevant question is not only whether a marker is outside a range, but how far, whether it persists or changes, what symptoms and prior results show, and whether imaging or other evidence points in the same direction.

The same boundary applies to tumor markers, hormones, antibodies, viral markers, cysts, plaques, and nodules. Screening can surface a signal that deserves observation or additional evaluation without proving cancer or another feared disease. Conversely, a normal report is only a time-bounded observation and does not cancel long-term health management.

## Key Claims
- Reference-range flags identify statistical or laboratory deviations, not self-interpreting diagnoses.
- Magnitude, persistence, direction of change, symptoms, and prior measurements affect clinical importance.
- Temporary state and assay differences can alter some laboratory results.
- Tumor markers and small imaging findings require multimodal context and should not be equated automatically with cancer.
- Normal results describe the examination moment and do not guarantee future health.
- Report recommendations and qualified interpretation help route observation, repeat testing, specialist review, or urgent care.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Contextual interpretation is not a reason to dismiss abnormal findings. Marked changes, symptoms, dangerous patterns, or report-directed urgency can require prompt care. Exact thresholds and repeat intervals vary by test, laboratory, condition, and patient, so this page is not a report-reading algorithm or medical advice.
