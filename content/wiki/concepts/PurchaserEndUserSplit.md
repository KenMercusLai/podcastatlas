# Purchaser-End-User Split

## Definition

The purchaser-end-user split occurs when the person paying for a product differs from the person who selects, carries, uses, or socially evaluates it, creating two related but non-identical customer motivations.

## Current Synthesis

[[Desden]] illustrates an intergenerational version: mothers and grandmothers often buy licensed stadium bags for daughters and granddaughters. The transaction must reassure or motivate the gift buyer, but the brand's product and cultural relevance should begin with the student who carries the bag. If the end user finds it visually distinctive, socially appropriate, and shareable, the purchaser has a stronger reason to choose it.

The split creates a data problem because checkout records identify the payer more easily than the user. Campus ambassadors, game-day encounters, visible collaborations, and QR-code offers can reconnect the company with the person experiencing the product. That evidence can inform design, messaging, events, graduation follow-up, and license selection without pretending that purchaser needs are irrelevant.

## Key Claims

- Payment data alone can misidentify the primary product audience.
- Product relevance should usually be tested with the end user even when acquisition messaging also addresses a purchaser.
- Purchaser and user motivations should be modeled separately rather than collapsed into one persona.
- Product use can create a direct research surface after a gift or third-party purchase.
- A strong end-user signal can increase purchaser confidence, but user enthusiasm does not by itself prove gift conversion or repeat buying.

## Evidence

- Intergenerational purchase pattern: [[advice-line-with-seth-berkowitz-of-insomnia-cookies-vmp8144262595]] reports that mothers and grandmothers often buy Desden products for daughters and granddaughters.
- End-user priority: [[advice-line-with-seth-berkowitz-of-insomnia-cookies-vmp8144262595]] advises keeping students primary in product and cultural-relevance decisions regardless of who pays.
- Direct learning: [[advice-line-with-seth-berkowitz-of-insomnia-cookies-vmp8144262595]] recommends ambassadors, campus moments, collaborations, and a QR-code contest or offer to identify and learn from the carrier.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode offers one company case and does not quantify how often purchaser and user differ. In some categories, the purchaser controls budget, safety, compliance, or access strongly enough to be the decisive customer. The correct emphasis depends on who chooses, who can veto, who experiences value, and who repeats the purchase.

## What Changed

- Initial synthesis established from Desden's intergenerational gifting pattern.
- Distinguished end-user cultural relevance from purchaser conversion and control.
