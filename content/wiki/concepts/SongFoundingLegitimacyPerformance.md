# 宋初建国合法性表演 / Song Founding Legitimacy Performance

Song founding legitimacy performance / 宋初建国合法性表演 names the episode's reading of how [[ZhaoKuangyin|赵匡胤]]'s takeover had to be staged as reluctant, orderly, and ritualized. In [[169-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-shang-957052909]], the [[ChenQiaoMutiny|陈桥兵变]] story depends on soldiers forcing a yellow robe on Zhao, ministers being told he was compelled by the army, and a formal abdication procedure making the transfer look lawful.

The concept does not claim that every detail is fake. Its point is that seizure of power needed a public form that could reduce moral and administrative resistance. "被迫", "禅让", troop discipline, and later honors to [[HanTong|韩通]] all work to turn a military coup into a dynasty-founding narrative.

The episode also connects this performance to later history writing. If [[ZhaoGuangyi|赵光义]]'s role was emphasized under later official histories, then founding legitimacy was not a one-day ritual but an ongoing memory project.

## Key Claims
- A coup can require ritual language to become governable after the fact.
- Forced-abdication stories make political violence appear compatible with order.
- "Reluctant ruler" rhetoric can protect a founder from the moral cost of ambition.
- Honoring loyalist victims can help absorb the violence that the founding story cannot fully hide.
- Later revision can continue the original legitimacy performance by reallocating credit and restraint.
