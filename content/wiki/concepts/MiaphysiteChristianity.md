# Miaphysite Christianity

## Definition

Miaphysite Christianity is the Christological tradition emphasizing the united nature of the incarnate Christ; the episode calls its adherents “Monophysites” and contrasts them with the Council of Chalcedon's formula maintaining Christ's full divinity and humanity in two natures.

## Current Synthesis

The source presents Christological controversy as institutional and geopolitical as well as doctrinal. Alexandria and parts of Syria were major centers of resistance to the Chalcedonian settlement, so arguments about Christ's nature also expressed tension among Constantinople and powerful provincial churches.

[[Theodora]] is said to have adopted this commitment after a religious experience in Alexandria under Patriarch Timothy's influence. Her faith therefore became part of the confessional complexity of the Justinianic court rather than a private biographical detail.

## Key Claims

- Christological language carried consequences for imperial unity, episcopal authority, and provincial allegiance.
- Chalcedon affirmed Christ as fully divine and fully human while maintaining two natures.
- Alexandrian and Syrian resistance made doctrinal disagreement geographically and politically consequential.
- Theodora's commitment connected personal conversion to later court patronage and conciliation.
- “Monophysite” can flatten or polemicize a tradition better represented here by the qualified label “Miaphysite.”

## Evidence

- Doctrinal setting - [[218-theodora-empress-of-byzantium-part-1-glt5957583440]] explains the dispute over whether Christ's human and divine natures remained distinct or formed one united nature.
- Institutional geography - [[218-theodora-empress-of-byzantium-part-1-glt5957583440]] identifies Alexandria and parts of Syria as major centers and links the controversy to provincial tension with Constantinople.
- Political biography - [[218-theodora-empress-of-byzantium-part-1-glt5957583440]] associates Theodora's Alexandrian conversion and Patriarch Timothy with her durable religious identity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode gives a compressed popular explanation of a technically complex controversy. “Monophysite” is its stated terminology, but not all communities described by outsiders with that term accepted the implication that Christ's humanity was absorbed or denied. Theodora's precise conversion experience, theological formation, and later policy influence are not independently demonstrated here.

## What Changed

- Created a qualified framework connecting Christological doctrine, provincial church politics, and Theodora's court role.
