# Human Knowledge Commons Sustainability

## Definition
Human knowledge commons sustainability is the ability of an openly useful knowledge ecosystem to keep attracting current human participation when platforms, search products, and AI systems can extract or summarize its value without reproducing its access, recognition, traffic, or contributor incentives.

## Current Synthesis
The combined [[Wikipedia]] and [[Reddit]] evidence shows that a knowledge commons has at least three renewal systems: contributors need a public purpose and workable governance, institutions need revenue and infrastructure, and readers need meaningful access to the source and its evidence. Wikipedia's early nonprofit, volunteer, and small-donor design aligned these systems before AI answer layers existed. The Reddit case shows how licensing and direct answers can later separate corpus value from source visits and contributor recognition. Compensation to a platform can help operations, but it does not by itself preserve open access, contributor motivation, community legitimacy, or the flow of new human knowledge.

## Key Claims
- Human knowledge production is a renewable social process, not a fixed corpus that can be extracted indefinitely.
- Answer layers can create public convenience while weakening visits, recognition, and participation at the source.
- Platform compensation does not automatically compensate contributors or preserve their reasons for participating.
- Access walls may protect platform value in the short run while reducing public learning and the future supply of contributions.
- AI companies share an ecosystem interest in sustaining current, diverse, human-generated knowledge rather than relying on recursively generated material.
- Funding architecture and editorial governance are part of commons renewal because operating revenue can either support or redirect the public mission.

## Evidence
- Contribution mission - [[tech-20261111-1111-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261111-1111-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses AskHistorians to show that experts may contribute for public education and that restricted access can weaken that purpose.
- Platform-AI dependency - [[tech-20261111-1111-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261111-1111-mp-tech-pod-128]] connects Reddit data licensing and AI summaries to reduced source visits while arguing that AI still needs daily human discussion.
- Recursive-data warning - [[tech-20261111-1111-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261111-1111-mp-tech-pod-128]] cites model degradation after repeated synthetic-data training as a reason to preserve fresh human production.
- Pre-AI institutional design - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] links Wikipedia's volunteer mission, nonprofit status, advertising refusal, small-donor funding, and reserves to free global access and continued operation.
- Contribution and verification loop - [[vmp5702291377-vmp5702291377]] says low-barrier edits expanded participation while citations, discussion pages, and community rules kept the encyclopedia oriented toward correction rather than unquestioned authority.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not measure how many contributors will stop posting, whether login users contribute more, whether licensing or donation revenue best subsidizes community health, or which compensation and access designs work best. Login requirements can reduce scraping or abuse, advertising can fund public access under some governance arrangements, and filtered synthetic data can remain useful. The Wikipedia account is also a founder retrospective rather than an independent institutional audit. The concept therefore identifies interacting sustainability constraints rather than proving that one funding or access model is universally superior.

## What Changed
- Added pre-AI evidence that volunteer purpose, nonprofit governance, distributed small donations, and open access can be designed as one renewal system.
- Added editorial and funding alignment to the commons-sustainability judgment rather than treating source traffic as the only operating input.
