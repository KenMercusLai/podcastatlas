# Illinois AI Liability Bill

The Illinois AI liability bill is the proposed state legislation discussed in [[tech-20260417-0417-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260417-0417-mp-tech-pod-128]]. The episode says it would limit liability for large AI model developers when critical harms are caused by their models, as long as the developer did not intentionally or recklessly cause harm and had published a safety protocol.

The bill matters because it reveals a split among frontier model companies. [[OpenAI]] testified in favor, while [[Wired]] reported that [[Anthropic]] wanted major changes or wanted the legislation killed. [[MariaCurie|Maria Currie]] frames the fight as a state-level governance issue because federal AI legislation appears unlikely to move.

The source does not treat the bill as final. [[BillCunningham]] says it is highly unlikely that final Illinois law would include sweeping liability relief for AI developers, so the wiki records the bill as a contested proposal rather than an enacted immunity regime.

## Key Claims
- The bill would condition liability limits on lack of intentional or reckless conduct and publication of a safety protocol.
- Frontier AI companies can support or oppose the same liability proposal depending on how they read accountability, safety incentives, and litigation exposure.
- State AI bills matter more when federal legislation is stalled.
- Liability shields become more controversial when AI harms include critical infrastructure cyberattacks or bioweapon creation, not only ordinary user misuse.
- The final law may change substantially before enactment.
