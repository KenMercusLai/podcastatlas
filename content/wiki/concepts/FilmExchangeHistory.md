## Definition
Film exchange history studies the mechanisms by which films enter another society, the people and institutions that receive them, and the effects that circulation has on interpretation and later creative work.

## Current Synthesis
The concept shifts film history from a list of masterworks toward a chain of production, selection, transport, translation, exhibition, audience access, criticism, and influence. Festivals, state cultural agreements, dubbed distribution, explanatory materials, archives, restoration funds, and retrospectives can all enable access while filtering what becomes visible. Claims of influence therefore require evidence connecting circulation to actual reception rather than assuming that availability equals impact.

## Key Claims
- Cross-border film reception depends on institutions and material access, not artistic quality alone.
- Different circulation channels produce different audiences, contexts, and canons.
- Restoration and retrospective programming can reopen access to overlooked work.
- Influence claims need a traceable path from availability through viewing to later practice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Circulation records do not by themselves prove audience size, comprehension, or creative influence. Festival, diplomatic, commercial, and educational channels overlap but should not be treated as equivalent. The episode's individual box-office and influence anecdotes remain unverified examples rather than foundation-level evidence.
