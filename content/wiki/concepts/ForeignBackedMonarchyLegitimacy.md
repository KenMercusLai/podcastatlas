# Foreign-Backed Monarchy Legitimacy

## Definition

Foreign-backed monarchy legitimacy is the difficulty of converting an externally sponsored dynastic claim into accepted domestic authority when the throne arrives through foreign military intervention.

## Current Synthesis

The [[SecondMexicanEmpire]] illustrates why pedigree, ceremony, reform, and invited local allies do not by themselves overcome an occupation origin. [[NapoleonIII]] chose [[FerdinandMaximilian]] partly because a Habsburg could appear more independent than a Bonaparte, but French arms remained the regime's decisive foundation and [[BenitoJuarez|Juarez]] preserved a constitutional republican rival.

Maximilian's liberalism did not solve the problem. It alienated conservative sponsors while republicans could still reject the throne as foreign-imposed, leaving the empire without a coalition broad enough to survive the removal of French coercion.

## Key Claims

- Dynastic prestige can disguise but not eliminate dependence on foreign force.
- An invitation from one domestic faction does not establish national consent.
- Reformist policy can broaden appeal only if it does not simultaneously dissolve the sponsoring coalition.
- A continuing constitutional rival prevents de facto control from becoming uncontested legitimacy.
- Durable legitimacy requires institutions and resources that can survive the patron's departure.

## Evidence

- Candidate selection: [[163-the-last-emperor-of-mexico-glt3817272823]] says Napoleon III preferred a Habsburg so Mexican independence would appear credible.
- Coercive origin: [[163-the-last-emperor-of-mexico-glt3817272823]] describes French invasion, counter-insurgency, and Juarez's continuing resistance.
- Policy dilemma: [[163-the-last-emperor-of-mexico-glt3817272823]] shows Maximilian retaining liberal reforms and losing conservative confidence without winning republicans.
- Survival test: [[163-the-last-emperor-of-mexico-glt3817272823]] records rapid collapse after French withdrawal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Foreign support does not make all local adherence unreal, and the episode acknowledges conservatives who actively wanted monarchy. The source does not measure opinion across Mexico or prove that no alternative imperial policy could have built wider support.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the Second Mexican Empire's gap between dynastic presentation, local invitation, and coercive dependence.
