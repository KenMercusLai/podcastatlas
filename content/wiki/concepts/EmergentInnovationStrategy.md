# Emergent Innovation Strategy / 涌现式创新战略

## Definition
Emergent innovation strategy is the view that original products, research directions, and organizational practices often arise from local exploration and interaction rather than from a fully specified top-down plan.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses Yang Bin's Mintzberg reference to challenge the idea that innovation spaces can be cleanly "cultivated" by planning for targets and efficiency. In the episode, Dartmouth-style discovery, AI coding, agents, Anthropic internal tools, and Tencent work-body examples all point toward emergence: people build something useful locally, others notice, and the practice grows before it is named as strategy.

The concept complements AI organization design by drawing a boundary: planning and execution matter, but they are weak substitutes for discovery when the problem is still not legible.

## Key Claims
- Innovation planning becomes fragile when it defines outputs before the field knows what matters.
- Local interest and internal usefulness can be early signals before formal strategy exists.
- Efficiency-first management can suppress exploration when the goal is still undefined.
- Emergence does not remove accountability; it changes when and how evaluation should enter.
- AI-era tools may accelerate emergent practice because internal prototypes can spread quickly once they work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not say planning is useless. It says planning is insufficient for original innovation before goals and metrics are clear; mature delivery still needs execution discipline.
