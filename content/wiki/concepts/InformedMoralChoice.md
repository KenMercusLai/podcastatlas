# Informed Moral Choice / 识恶后的主动择善

## Definition
Informed moral choice is the episode's claim that goodness becomes deliberate when a person can recognize harmful conduct, understand some of its motives and enabling conditions, set a moral baseline, and still choose not to reproduce the harm.

## Current Synthesis
Episode 477 develops this idea from a listener's experience of bullying, humiliation, and anonymous death threats. Emotional distance can keep a person functioning, but “not caring” may also skip the fact that dignity was violated. Understanding harm can therefore serve two linked purposes: protecting self and others, and making goodness a chosen position rather than an untested lack of exposure.

The episode also distinguishes individual wrongdoing from enabling environments. Competition, evaluation systems, and group atmosphere may make attacks on vulnerable people more likely, but explanation does not erase agency or responsibility. Moral understanding should sharpen boundaries, not romanticize perpetrators or require unsafe confrontation.

## Key Claims
- Innocence and goodness are not identical because inability to recognize harm leaves moral choice untested.
- Naming a dignity violation can support proportionate boundary-setting without requiring retaliation.
- Motive and environment can help explain harm without excusing it.
- A moral baseline can provide orientation in confusing or coercive settings.
- Knowledge of harmful patterns can improve protection for oneself and people one hopes to help.

- Chosen goodness - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-477-zixunshi-huixin-lijie-shenme-shi-e-caineng-zhudong-xuanze-weishan-lj-c37-abvr5oa0a7vbhvl93pvia]] argues that recognizing evil makes it possible to establish a baseline and actively refuse participation in harm.
- Environmental contribution - [[shi-di-fu-shuo-477-zixunshi-huixin-lijie-shenme-shi-e-caineng-zhudong-xuanze-weishan-lj-c37-abvr5oa0a7vbhvl93pvia]] separates individual conduct from competitive, evaluative, and group conditions that may help produce attacks on vulnerable people.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
“Evil” is a morally charged category that can flatten context, intensify polarization, or license dehumanization. Understanding harmful motives does not establish them as fact, require engagement, or make confrontation safe. The concept is strongest when it supports evidence-based recognition, dignity, proportionate protection, and refusal to harm.
