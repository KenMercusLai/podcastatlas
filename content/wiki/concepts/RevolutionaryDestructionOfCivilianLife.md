# Revolutionary Destruction Of Civilian Life / 革命对日常生活的摧毁

Revolutionary destruction of civilian life / 革命对日常生活的摧毁 is the source's warning that justified grievances can pass through violence into the destruction of ordinary homes, habits, relationships, and accumulated culture. In [[05-ta-laizi-maliwuboer-ruguo-ni-kanjian-guo-wo-ceng-jiandao-de-533375925]], the family's white house, class identity, poverty, school humiliation, arrests, famine, and exile make revolution and civil war visible at household scale.

The concept does not deny the reality of inequality or the reasons people seek change. The episode explicitly holds those origins in view. Its caution is about what happens when political rupture lets class labels, factional fear, and state coercion decide whether a person is protected, humiliated, exiled, or erased.

## Key Claims
- Political rupture destroys more than institutions; it can destroy daily trust, inherited skills, spaces, and family continuity.
- Class injustice can be real while class vengeance still becomes indiscriminate.
- Children absorb political identity through schools, peers, food access, language, and fear.
- Civilian life is often where the cost of grand historical transformation becomes legible.
