# Youth Online Speech Rights

## Definition
Youth online speech rights is the claim that minors, like adults, have constitutional and civil-liberties interests in speaking, reading, organizing, and accessing lawful speech online.

## Current Synthesis
The current source set treats youth online speech rights as the user-side counterpart to [[PlatformFirstAmendmentDefense]]. Platform defenses ask whether companies have protected editorial rights in ranking or presenting content; youth online speech rights ask whether users, including minors, can be broadly excluded from communication spaces where lawful speech happens.

[[tech-20260406-0406-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260406-0406-mp-tech-pod-128]] introduces the concept through [[AaronMackey]] of the [[ElectronicFrontierFoundation|Electronic Frontier Foundation]], who criticizes [[CaliforniaAB1709]] and similar laws that ban minors from social media or require parental permission. [[metas-youth-safety-settlement-could-change-how-people-access-the-internet]] extends the frame through [[WillCreeley]] of [[FoundationForIndividualRightsAndExpression|FIRE]], who argues that courts have consistently recognized minors' First Amendment interests and that adults also should not have to prove identity to access protected speech.

## Key Claims
- Minors' speech access matters even when the policy goal is child safety.
- Social media is not only an addictive or risky product; it is also a speech venue for religious practice, political organization, social commentary, friendships, family connections, and community finding.
- A state can burden youth speech rights if it blocks access to broad platforms instead of targeting specific unlawful conduct or harmful business practices.
- The concept makes child-safety policy harder because harm reduction must be balanced against constitutional access to lawful communication.
- Youth speech rights are tied to adult anonymous-speech rights when age checks require everyone to prove identity.
- Settlement-based remedies can implicate youth speech rights if they functionally exclude young users from broad communication spaces.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not deny that children can face real social-media harms or that platforms may need safer defaults. The source set instead says the remedy must be evaluated by how directly it burdens lawful communication, anonymous access, family choice, and user autonomy. The available evidence is primarily civil-liberties legal critique, not a complete child-safety policy record.
