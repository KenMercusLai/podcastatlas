# ADHD Attention-Control Model

## Definition
The ADHD attention-control model treats ADHD as impaired control over where attention goes, how long it stays, and when it shifts, rather than as a uniform absence of attention.

## Current Synthesis
[[JohnKruse]] separates three problems: directing attention toward the intended target, sustaining it, and disengaging when a different target becomes more important. This explains why distractibility and intense hyperfocus can coexist. Hyperfocus can resemble flow when interest, challenge, and engagement align, but it remains impairing when a person cannot reliably choose its target or exit it.

The model also connects attention to motivation and context. Kruse's “interest driven” shorthand does not mean importance is irrelevant; it means immediate engagement may exert unusually strong control over performance. Ranganath adds appraisal, values, curiosity, and personal coaching as ways to connect difficult tasks to purpose. External structure can therefore help align present attention with longer-term goals without proving that ADHD is only environmental or voluntary.

## Key Claims
- ADHD can impair directing, sustaining, and shifting attention.
- Hyperfocus does not disprove ADHD because control, not total attention quantity, is the central issue.
- Interest, challenge, novelty, and engagement can make sustained attention easier but less predictable.
- Working memory, impulse control, emotional regulation, and time management interact with attentional control.
- Performance can vary sharply by task and setting, so isolated moments of focus do not settle diagnosis or impairment.
- Appraisal and values can make a task more engaging, but they do not convert a clinical control problem into a simple choice.

## Evidence
- Three-part control model - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] distinguishes directing, sustaining, and shifting attention.
- Hyperfocus boundary - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] compares hyperfocus with flow while emphasizing unreliable entry and exit.
- Interest sensitivity - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] contrasts interest-driven engagement with importance-driven task selection.
- Executive-function context - [[improve-focus-with-behavioral-tools-medication-for-adhd-dr-john-kruse-scim4112569463]] links attention with working memory, impulse, emotion, time estimation, and structure.
- Curiosity and purpose - [[how-to-improve-memory-focus-using-science-protocols-dr-charan-ranganath-scim5612458885]] uses video-game focus, appraisal, mindfulness, values, and ADHD coaching to show how task meaning can alter engagement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a clinical explanatory model drawn from public interviews, not a diagnostic test or a claim that every person with ADHD hyperfocuses. “Interest driven” is useful shorthand but can overgeneralize individual differences, and the sources do not establish that attention-control difficulty is unique to ADHD. Values work, mindfulness, or coaching may support function but are not replacements for individualized diagnosis or treatment.
