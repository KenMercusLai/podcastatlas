# Criminal Justice Clemency Framework

Criminal justice clemency framework is [[DanLoeb|Dan Loeb]]'s source-specific way of deciding which individual cases deserve advocacy. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-dan-loeb-the-lost-art-of-short-selling-and-why-stock-picking-is-back-41552605]], he identifies three categories: falsely convicted people, people who show contrition and rehabilitation, and people whose sentences are disproportionate to their offense.

The framework is connected to philanthropy because Loeb says his work began through education reform and concern for opportunity. The source then moves from [[SuccessAcademies|Success Academies]] to individual cases such as [[RossUlbricht|Ross Ulbricht]], where ordinary legal relief was no longer realistic and pardon advocacy became the available tool.

## Key Claims
- Clemency advocacy can focus on innocence, rehabilitation, or proportionality, but those are different arguments.
- Source-scoped casework needs caution because advocacy accounts may omit prosecution, victim, public-safety, and procedural details.
- The episode criticizes versions of criminal-justice reform that become non-prosecution of serious bad actors, so its reform frame is individual and bounded rather than abolitionist.
- A pardon can correct or mitigate a sentence, but it does not by itself settle the full moral, legal, or factual history of a case.
