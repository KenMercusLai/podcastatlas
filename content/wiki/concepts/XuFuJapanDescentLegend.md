# Xu Fu Japan Descent Legend / 徐福日本后裔传说

Xu Fu Japan descent legend / 徐福日本后裔传说 is the reception-history question centered by [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh|Qinji 122-2]]: whether [[XuFu|徐福]]'s voyage can support claims that Japanese people, or people in some Japanese localities, descend from his party. The episode answers cautiously. It recognizes Japanese Xu Fu graves, shrines, local research, surname stories, and textual notices, but says these do not amount to proof that "Japanese people are Xu Fu's descendants."

The concept separates three layers that are easy to collapse: an early Chinese textual record around Xu Fu and sea-immortal searching, later overseas-settlement stories, and Japanese local memory that gives Xu Fu place-specific religious and cultural roles. The source values the legend as a cultural-exchange story while refusing to turn it into settled population history.

## Key Claims
- Shrine, grave, surname, and local-text traditions can preserve reception history without proving biological descent.
- The early [[Shiji|《史记》]] account is treated as a stronger evidentiary anchor than later stories of foreign kingship or broad ancestry.
- The episode uses Xu Fu as a boundary case for [[HistoricalDetectiveReasoning|historical detective reasoning]]: interesting stories still need source hierarchy.
- The safest synthesis is cultural contact and legend transmission, not a demonstrated national-origin claim.
