# Public Health Invisibility

Public health invisibility is the source's critique of systems that succeed by making nothing dramatic happen. In [[yi-fen-laizi-bingchuang-shang-de-xiangguan-shudan-598284093]], [[ShixinPublicHealth|《失信：公共卫生体系的崩溃》]] leads the hosts to emphasize clean water, sewage, food safety, school-canteen hygiene, and routine disease prevention as foundations of longer life.

The concept explains why public health is politically fragile. A spectacular surgery, new drug, or prestigious hospital is easier to see than contamination that never occurs, an outbreak that is contained early, or a water system that simply keeps working. Success can therefore make the system look wasteful until failure arrives.

## Key Claims
- Public health often produces absence: no epidemic, no food poisoning, no unsafe water, no uncontrolled outbreak.
- Prevention can lose budget and status because voters and politicians notice failures more easily than quiet maintenance.
- Clean water and sanitation can matter more to population health than a single dramatic intervention.
- Public-health memory decays after a crisis, making societies repeat preparedness failures.
