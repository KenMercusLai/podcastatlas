# Chevron Doctrine

## Definition
Chevron doctrine is the administrative-law deference doctrine referenced in the episode as an overturned framework for courts deferring to federal agency interpretations of ambiguous statutes.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses Chevron doctrine only as legal context for the [[Monsanto]]/[[Roundup]] Supreme Court argument. Friedberg says the opposing attorney invoked the doctrine's overturning to argue that courts should read federal law directly rather than defer to an agency position. In this source, Chevron matters because less agency deference may alter the preemption fight between EPA pesticide labeling and state failure-to-warn claims.

## Key Claims
- Chevron's overturning can change how courts treat federal agency interpretations in regulatory disputes.
- The doctrine's absence can strengthen arguments that courts, not agencies, decide the best statutory reading.
- Preemption cases may become harder to predict when agency expertise and state-law safety claims point in different directions.
