# Policy-Driven Talent Retention Gap / 政策引才与留才落差

## Definition

Policy-driven talent retention gap is the difference between inducing skilled people to relocate through registration, housing, education, or promotional incentives and giving them enough career depth, income, services, relationships, and personal fit to remain.

## Current Synthesis

EP167 uses Hainan's “million talents” ambition to separate recruitment from durable settlement. Entry benefits can reduce the immediate cost of moving, and a free-trade-port narrative can raise expectations, but people still compare jobs, wages, schools, healthcare, city amenities, family ties, and social environment. Regional variation matters too: competitive eastern posts and hard-to-fill central or western assignments exist inside the same policy regime.

The guest's own choice clarifies that retention is not purely economic. She values Hainan but prefers stranger interaction and a broader social world outside a familiar local network. A place can therefore offer comfort and belonging to one returnee while feeling professionally or personally narrow to another.

A mature-city failure mode appears in [[ep138-laile-jiushi-shenzhenren-cmcoosydpdsgbanyaqbwgizp|EP138]] through [[Shenzhen]]. The city is already attractive, economically deep, and symbolically open to newcomers; the gap appears later, when high housing costs, intense work, limited cultural life, school capacity, healthcare depth, and family needs make residents consider leaving or commuting from Dongguan and Huizhou. Talent retention therefore matters even where recruitment has succeeded without a single headline talent campaign.

## Key Claims

- Recruitment incentives solve relocation barriers more directly than long-run career and life satisfaction.
- Retention depends on job variety, wages, public services, urban amenities, family ties, and personal social fit.
- Province-wide talent targets can conceal large differences between attractive cities and hard-to-fill regions.
- Return migration is heterogeneous: comfort and family can attract some people while career diversity and anonymity pull others away.
- Policy credibility should be judged by durable settlement and contribution, not headline recruitment alone.
- Strong job attraction and low symbolic entry barriers do not remove later housing, service, work-life, and family-formation constraints.

## Evidence

- Incentive layer: [[ep167-duihua-hainanren-hainan-de-dashidai-zhende-laile-zhe-yici-keyi-tengfei-ma-ckwriw4esa88abaaaacdea4y|EP167]] describes household-registration, housing, and children's-education benefits attached to Hainan's talent campaign.
- Retention limit: [[ep167-duihua-hainanren-hainan-de-dashidai-zhende-laile-zhe-yici-keyi-tengfei-ma-ckwriw4esa88abaaaacdea4y|EP167]] questions whether actual arrivals and stays matched the slogan because jobs and city services still influence decisions.
- Regional difference: [[ep167-duihua-hainanren-hainan-de-dashidai-zhende-laile-zhe-yici-keyi-tengfei-ma-ckwriw4esa88abaaaacdea4y|EP167]] contrasts stronger competition in the east with some central and western public posts that attract fewer applicants.
- Personal-fit boundary: [[ep167-duihua-hainanren-hainan-de-dashidai-zhende-laile-zhe-yici-keyi-tengfei-ma-ckwriw4esa88abaaaacdea4y|EP167]] says the guest values Hainan yet prefers working outside its familiar social environment.
- Mature-city retention: [[ep138-laile-jiushi-shenzhenren-cmcoosydpdsgbanyaqbwgizp|EP138]] shows that Shenzhen can attract and include newcomers while still losing some residents when housing, schools, healthcare, work intensity, and family life become decisive.
- Metropolitan workaround: [[ep138-laile-jiushi-shenzhenren-cmcoosydpdsgbanyaqbwgizp|EP138]] describes Dongguan or Huizhou residence with cross-city commuting as partial housing relief that preserves substantial time and family costs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source provides a complete administrative count of applicants, arrivals, departures, occupations, tenure, or reasons for moving. EP167 predates later free-trade-port development, and EP138 reflects a September 2020 Shenzhen discussion; one guest's classmates or three speakers' trajectories are not representative migration samples. Incentives may still be decisive for some households, and retention is not always the correct goal when circular migration or temporary expertise transfer is valuable.

## What Changed

- Extended the framework from incentive-led Hainan recruitment to a mature Shenzhen case where attraction succeeds but later housing and service constraints threaten retention.
- Added family formation, work-life pressure, and metropolitan commuting to the durable-settlement test.
