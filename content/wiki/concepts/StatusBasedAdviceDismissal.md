## Definition

Status-based advice dismissal / 身份资历式建议否决 is the organizational failure in which a proposal is rejected primarily because its advocate is young, junior, disreputable, or outside the accepted inner circle rather than because the proposal's assumptions, evidence, costs, and execution requirements have been tested.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-235-liubi-zhisi-jielu-zhichang-shengcun-qian-guize-lp1wxe_buqe_zrilsfwsfzqoa2ef|Hanji 235]] supplies two related but distinct cases. An unnamed young general argues that Wu infantry should avoid the open terrain favorable to court cavalry, bypass defended cities, strike Luoyang, seize its arsenal and the Ao granary, and establish a defensible strategic base. [[LiuBiWuKing|刘濞]] asks senior commanders, who reject the proposal through the general's youth and alleged lack of deep judgment rather than an operational rebuttal.

[[ZhouQiuWesternHan|周丘]] supplies the personnel-side parallel. He is excluded because his criminal past, drinking, and demeanor mark him as untrustworthy, yet a very limited grant of symbolic authority reveals strong local execution and mobilization ability. The case does not prove that reputation is irrelevant: Zhou uses deception, coercion, and killing, and his force remains dependent on Liu Bi. It shows why status signals should guide risk controls and role design rather than substitute for testing relevant capability.

The concept is narrower than meritocracy in general. It concerns the reasoning step by which identity becomes a complete answer to a proposal. A sound organization may still reject the young general's plan or restrict Zhou Qiu, but it should state operational reasons, compare alternatives, and design bounded tests where feasible.

## Key Claims

- Age, rank, reputation, and insider status can carry relevant risk information but are not themselves a substantive evaluation of a proposal.
- Rejecting advice through the advocate's identity can hide disagreement, protect senior authority, and prevent comparison of alternatives.
- The cost rises in time-sensitive decisions because a missed option may become impossible to recover later.
- Underestimated people may reveal real capability when given a bounded instrument or task.
- Capability evidence does not erase ethical, trust, or durability risks; Zhou Qiu's coercion and dependence remain material qualifications.
- Better evaluation separates proposal quality, execution capacity, authorization risk, and moral cost instead of collapsing them into status.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current evidence is one episode and does not establish that the unnamed young general's plan would have succeeded. Bypassing cities, deep advance, supply, coordination, court cavalry response, and control of Luoyang all require independent evaluation. Senior commanders may have had reasons omitted by the supplied summary. Zhou Qiu is not uncomplicated evidence for empowering every excluded person: his method includes false authority, fabricated charges, killing, intimidation, and a force that dissolves as a viable project after Liu Bi's defeat.
