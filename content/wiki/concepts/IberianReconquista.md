# Iberian Reconquista / 伊比利亚收复失地运动

## Definition

Iberian Reconquista is a retrospective and historically actor-grounded frame for the long expansion of Christian polities against Islamic rule in the [[IberianPeninsula]], used here with explicit caution about continuity, inevitability, and the implication that centuries of Muslim society were merely an intrusion.

## Current Synthesis
The Ceuta episode uses the Reconquista less as a simple civilizational slogan than as a strategic transition. The Portugal episode adds the kingdom-formation layer: Henri of Burgundy's county, [[AfonsoHenriques|Afonso Henriques's]] royal claim, and the 1147 Lisbon campaign show Portuguese consolidation developing inside a shifting patchwork of Iberian kingdoms rather than fixed modern nations. Once Portugal had completed its mainland expansion by 1249, further Iberian conquest was constrained by Castilian claims around Granada. That made [[Ceuta]], North Africa, and Atlantic islands more plausible directions for Portuguese ambition.

The Castilian endpoint adds a second outward transition: Granada's surrender in January 1492 released attention while reinforcing Ferdinand and Isabella's crusading mission. Columbus linked Asian wealth to a future Jerusalem campaign, while postwar financial need and Portuguese rivalry helped make his small expedition attractive, as described in [[306-columbus-the-adventure-begins-part-1-glt6089901915]].

The Cordoba episode adds a contingency guardrail to this long sequence. The [[CordobaCaliphate]]'s civil war and Taifa fragmentation gave northern kingdoms greater opportunity, but the final displacement of Muslim rule was neither automatic nor predetermined. State weakness in the south and growing economic, religious, and military confidence in the north interacted over time.

[[12-days-reconquest-of-spain-and-the-old-queen-of-hawaii-glt9407596415]] sharpens the endpoint and the terminology dispute. Muhammad XII's surrender of Granada to [[FerdinandIIOfAragon]] and [[IsabellaIOfCastile]] on 2 January 1492 initially came with protections, but later forced conversions, confiscations, and expulsions turned victory into confessional exclusion. The source also resists reducing the conquerors' apocalyptic Christianity and conversion aims to camouflage for material interest: sincere belief, dynastic consolidation, extraction, and domination can operate together.

## Key Claims
- The term reflects a Christian recovery narrative but can falsely naturalize a single continuous project or treat long-established Muslim societies as temporary outsiders.
- The Reconquista creates the precondition for Portuguese outward expansion by ending Portugal's mainland frontier earlier than Castile's.
- Portuguese kingdom formation and the Lisbon conquest joined transnational crusading networks to local dynastic rivalry, while cross-religious alliances qualified rigid bloc narratives.
- Ceuta becomes intelligible as a continuation and displacement of Iberian frontier energy across the strait without proving a fully formed global plan.
- Granada's fall shaped Atlantic sponsorship through political timing, crusading imagination, and postwar financial need.
- Granada's initial surrender protections gave way to forced conversion and expulsion, making 1492 a confessional rupture as well as a territorial endpoint.
- Cordoba's fragmentation shifted the balance toward northern kingdoms without making the eventual Christian conquest inevitable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide a full Reconquista history. They use the concept to explain Portuguese kingdom formation and later direction, Castile's post-Granada sponsorship context, and the consequences of Cordoban fragmentation while explicitly complicating simple Christian-versus-Muslim boundary stories. The Lisbon episode's national comparison among crusaders depends on a partisan chronicler. “Reconquista” is retained because Christian actors understood conquest as recovery, not as proof of one continuous, inevitable project or a modern national identity. Boabdil's farewell and maternal rebuke belong to romantic memory rather than secure eyewitness history.

## What Changed
- Added the term's actor-grounded meaning while making its exclusionary and retrospective risks explicit.
- Extended the Granada endpoint through the failure of initial protections, forced conversion, and expulsion.
- Distinguished sincere religious causation from any claim that conquest lacked material or coercive aims.
