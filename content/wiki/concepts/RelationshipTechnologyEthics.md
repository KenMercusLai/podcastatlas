## Definition
Relationship technology ethics is the question of whether tools that optimize intimacy by guaranteeing fidelity, simulating ideal partners, sharing pain, deleting memory, or scoring compatibility preserve the agency, responsibility, privacy, and common history that make a relationship meaningful.

## Current Synthesis
The current source treats technology as a pressure test for love rather than as a product roadmap. Each imagined tool offers a cleaner outcome: fewer temptations, a perfect partner, half the pain, no memory of betrayal, or a more compatible mate. The panel's disagreement shows why cleaner experience does not settle the ethical question.

The strongest synthesis is that intimate technologies become ethically unstable when they convert a relationship into a managed state while hiding what is being displaced. Fidelity may lose the element of choice; AI companionship may give real experience without human authenticity; shared pain may become forced transfer; memory deletion may reduce suffering while weakening accountability; and compatibility scoring may turn an existing partner into an opportunity-cost problem.

## Key Claims
- Relationship tools should be judged by what they do to agency, not only by whether they reduce conflict or pain.
- Optimized experience can conflict with responsibility, common history, and the duty not to outsource another person's emotional burden.
- The same mechanism can look humane in one role and coercive in another, especially when one partner has more pain, guilt, leverage, or alternatives.
- Existing commitments change the ethics of choice because a real relationship carries shared time, economic entanglement, family duty, and "义气."
- Technological certainty can damage intimacy when uncertainty, privacy, friction, and repair are part of the relationship's meaning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a live comedy panel using hypothetical technology, so it does not establish real efficacy, safety, or policy for any actual drug, AI companion, memory tool, emotion-sharing device, or matching algorithm. Its value is ethical mapping: it shows which tradeoffs speakers notice when the imagined technology is stipulated to work.
