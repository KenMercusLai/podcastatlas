## Definition

[[FrugalImperialBurialRestraint|节俭帝陵约束]] is the pattern in which an emperor's tomb planning, death instructions, admired court exemplars, and postdeath sacrificial arrangements limit later ritual construction, treasure use, tomb-town expansion, and public burden. In the current wiki it is grounded in [[FanHongEasternHan|樊弘]]'s thin-burial model praised by [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]], [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]]'s Shouling construction order, his 75 CE death instructions, and [[LiuCangDongpingKing|刘苍]]'s successful remonstrance to [[EmperorZhangOfHan|汉章帝]].

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 728 adds an antecedent court-exemplar layer. Fan Hong is not an emperor, but his deathbed will asks for thin burial, no grave goods, and a specified joint-burial arrangement with his wife. Liu Xiu praises the will before officials and says his own death should be handled the same way. This makes burial restraint a norm that can move upward from admired ministerial conduct into imperial self-instruction.

The current Mingdi pattern has a predeath planning layer. Mingdi orders Shouling to be limited to drainage and water flow, forbids a raised mound, restricts postdeath offerings to simple water and dry food after the hundred-day sacrifice, limits staff to cleaning, and threatens unauthorized additions with ancestral-temple law. Burial restraint therefore begins before his final will: it is built into construction rules, sacrificial simplicity, and administrative enforcement.

The death-and-succession layer continues the same restraint. Mingdi's will avoids a new sacrificial temple, places his tablet in Yin Taihou's side hall, and calls for a simplified Xianjie Ling burial without walls, mound-like display, or treasures. When Zhangdi considers building a tomb town for Xianjie Ling, Liu Cang argues that the project would violate Mingdi's frugal intent, drain resources, and disturb the people. Zhangdi's acceptance turns the dead emperor's restraint into a living check on successor expenditure.

This concept is adjacent to [[ImperialBurialLegitimacy|帝陵合法性]] and [[RitualAffectiveOrder|礼制情感秩序]], but it is narrower. It asks how burial practice disciplines government behavior, while those concepts focus on status recognition and the emotional-political force of ritual form.

## Key Claims

- Frugal imperial burial restraint can be modeled by high-status non-imperial exemplars before it becomes imperial instruction.
- Public praise of a thin-burial will can turn private death arrangements into court-facing governance norms.
- Frugal imperial burial restraint can begin at tomb-planning time, not only in a ruler's deathbed will.
- Mingdi's Shouling order limits construction, offerings, staffing, and unauthorized expansion through explicit rules.
- Mingdi's later death instructions extend the same pattern by limiting temple, wall, treasure, mound, and display practices.
- Liu Cang's remonstrance shows that burial frugality can be invoked after succession as a governance argument against wasting resources and disturbing common people.
- The pattern is compatible with ritual order; it limits excess rather than rejecting ceremony.

- Shouling construction restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]] records Mingdi's instruction that the tomb work focus on drainage and avoid a raised mound.
- Sacrifice, staffing, and enforcement restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]] says later offerings should be simple, staff should remain few and cleaning-focused, and unauthorized enlargement should be punished under ancestral-temple law.
- Death-instruction restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] records Mingdi's instruction not to create a separate sacrificial temple and to place his tablet in Yin Taihou's side hall.
- Material burial restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] describes Xianjie Ling as deliberately plain, without walls, treasures, or mound-like display.
- Successor-policy restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] records Liu Cang persuading Zhangdi not to build a tomb town because it would violate Mingdi's intent and impose public burdens.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Hanji 728's Fan Hong case is a model praised by Liu Xiu, not an imperial tomb order in itself, and the source does not prove exactly how Liu Xiu's own burial was executed. The stronger imperial-instruction evidence still comes from Mingdi's Shouling order and death instructions. The source notes do not prove the full archaeological condition of Xianjie Ling or Shouling; they record the narrative-political meaning of the arrangements. Frugality is not anti-ritual. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-899-hao-qing-dou-tanguan-ljhic1plzmv8k6lu73j5terihanx|Hanji 899]] shows Mingdi also associated with filial ritual precedent, so this concept should be read as bounded ceremony rather than ritual negation. The concept should not absorb every tomb dispute; [[ImperialBurialLegitimacy|帝陵合法性]] remains the better page for burial-status recognition problems.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 728's Fan Hong thin-burial will as a ministerial exemplar that Liu Xiu publicly praises and says he wants to imitate.
- Reframed frugal imperial burial restraint as a norm that can move from court exemplar to imperial planning and successor policy.
- Preserved Mingdi's Shouling order, death instructions, and Liu Cang remonstrance as the strongest imperial-rule evidence.
