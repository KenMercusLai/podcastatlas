## Definition

[[FrugalImperialBurialRestraint|节俭帝陵约束]] is the pattern in which an emperor's death instructions and tomb arrangements limit later ritual construction, treasure use, tomb-town expansion, and public burden. In the current wiki it is grounded in [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]]'s 75 CE death instructions and [[LiuCangDongpingKing|刘苍]]'s successful remonstrance to [[EmperorZhangOfHan|汉章帝]].

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] presents burial frugality as a political constraint, not merely as personal modesty. Mingdi's will avoids a new sacrificial temple, places his tablet in Yin Taihou's side hall, and calls for a simplified Xianjie Ling burial without walls, mound-like display, or treasures. These arrangements turn death ritual into a bounded public form.

The same source shows the restraint becoming actionable after succession. When Zhangdi considers building a tomb town for Xianjie Ling, Liu Cang argues that the project would violate Mingdi's frugal intent, drain resources, and disturb the people. Zhangdi's acceptance makes the dead emperor's restraint a living check on the successor's ritual expenditure.

This concept is adjacent to [[ImperialBurialLegitimacy|帝陵合法性]] and [[RitualAffectiveOrder|礼制情感秩序]], but it is narrower. It asks how imperial burial practice disciplines government behavior, while those concepts focus on status recognition and the emotional-political force of ritual form.

## Key Claims

- Frugal imperial burial restraint converts a ruler's death instructions into limits on later construction and expenditure.
- Mingdi's simplified temple and Xianjie Ling arrangements define the current evidence base for the pattern.
- Liu Cang's remonstrance shows that burial frugality can be invoked as a governance argument against wasting state resources and disturbing common people.
- Zhangdi's acceptance makes restraint part of early succession management, not only a private mourning preference.
- The pattern is compatible with ritual order; it limits excess rather than rejecting ceremony.

## Evidence

- Death-instruction restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] records Mingdi's instruction not to create a separate sacrificial temple and to place his tablet in Yin Taihou's side hall.
- Material burial restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] describes Xianjie Ling as deliberately plain, without walls, treasures, or mound-like display.
- Successor-policy restraint: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-744-dahan-genggong-qiusheng-caipu-jingrang-beiye-wangchenmoji-lvsumpsnpb1tpk7t0xrx7vatd5mx|Hanji 744]] records Liu Cang persuading Zhangdi not to build a tomb town because it would violate Mingdi's intent and impose public burdens.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- This is currently a single-source concept; later evidence may broaden, narrow, or rename the pattern.
- The source does not prove the full archaeological condition of Xianjie Ling; it records the narrative-political meaning of the arrangements.
- Frugality is not anti-ritual. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-899-hao-qing-dou-tanguan-ljhic1plzmv8k6lu73j5terihanx|Hanji 899]] shows Mingdi also associated with filial ritual precedent, so this concept should be read as bounded ceremony rather than ritual negation.
- The concept should not absorb every tomb dispute; [[ImperialBurialLegitimacy|帝陵合法性]] remains the better page for burial-status recognition problems.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 744 to capture Mingdi's frugal death instructions and Liu Cang's successful anti-tomb-city remonstrance.
- Distinguished burial restraint from broader ritual legitimacy and ritual affect concepts.
