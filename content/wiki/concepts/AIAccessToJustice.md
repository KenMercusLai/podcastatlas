# AI Access To Justice

## Definition
AI access to justice is the claim that AI can reduce the cost, delay, scarcity, and practical friction of understanding legal rights, preparing legal work, and obtaining legal help.

## Current Synthesis
The access claim is promising but conditional. AI can lower the marginal cost of legal explanation, drafting, research, and procedural challenge, making more help available to people or businesses that could not afford manual support. At the same time, cheaper legal output is not automatically fair legal output: professional responsibility, same-rights treatment, verification, privacy, and auditability still matter. The British bureaucracy source adds a capacity warning: if every claimant can generate more appeals and objections, institutions may become overloaded. The Legora interview adds a private-market capacity branch, where law firms and enterprises can serve more matters by turning manual diligence and review into supervised agent workflows.

## Key Claims
- AI can lower the marginal cost of explaining law, drafting materials, researching questions, and preparing disputes.
- Access gains are real only if legal AI improves fairness, accountability, and checkability rather than merely increasing output volume.
- AI-enabled claims and appeals can overload courts, agencies, and tribunals if institutional processes remain complex.
- Legal AI can expand the addressable market for professional help by serving clients or matters that were previously too small or costly for manual delivery.
- The strongest access model pairs abundance with [[HumanInTheLoopLegalAI]], [[LegalAIVerificationAuditability]], and privacy-aware design.

## Evidence
- Abundance frame: [[tech-20260805-0805-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260805-0805-mp-tech-pod-128]] presents [[SuperJustice|Super Justice]] as a way to relieve legal scarcity by making legal reasoning and assistance more abundant.
- Fairness and accountability: [[tech-20260805-0805-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260805-0805-mp-tech-pod-128]] says cheap legal help is not the same as fair legal help and ties the access claim to verification, human-centered systems, and professional responsibility.
- Institutional overload: [[under-strain-breaking-the-british-state-6a799563523b3dfcd83c7271]] says AI-assisted citizens can generate more numerous and complex claims across fines, benefits, employment disputes, planning applications, parking tickets, and rent increases.
- Market expansion: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-trillion-dollar-industries-ai-is-disrupting-voice-law-the-end-of-the-billable-hour-42064555]] argues that legal AI can create products for customers previously too small or too costly to serve manually.
- Capacity through agents: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-the-trillion-dollar-industries-ai-is-disrupting-voice-law-the-end-of-the-billable-hour-42064555]] links in-house diligence, document review, and agent orchestration to expanded legal capacity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The access-to-justice claim can be captured by private productivity rather than public fairness if tools remain expensive, proprietary, or limited to large firms and enterprises. More output can also worsen backlogs when procedural systems are not simplified. Legal AI still risks hallucination, unequal personalization, surveillance-heavy data use, and shallow human review under time pressure.
