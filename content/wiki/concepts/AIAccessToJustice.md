# AI Access To Justice

AI access to justice is the claim that AI can reduce the cost, delay, and scarcity of legal help. In [[tech-20260805-0805-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260805-0805-mp-tech-pod-128]], [[BenjaminAlarie]] frames this through [[SuperJustice|Super Justice]]: legal reasoning and assistance could become abundant enough to help more people understand and navigate the law.

The source keeps the access claim conditional. Legal AI can widen help only when users can verify outputs, when responsible professionals remain accountable, and when personalization does not become unequal treatment or data-heavy surveillance. This connects the optimistic access-to-justice branch to the wiki's existing warnings about [[VibeLawyering]], [[LegalAIHallucination]], and [[HumanInTheLoopLegalAI]].

[[under-strain-breaking-the-british-state-6a799563523b3dfcd83c7271]] adds the public-system capacity warning through [[AIBureaucracyArmsRace]]. If AI makes it much easier to contest fines, benefits decisions, employment disputes, planning applications, parking tickets, and rent increases, access improves for individual claimants but can create backlogs and higher complexity for agencies and tribunals.

## Key Claims
- AI can lower the marginal cost of explaining law, drafting materials, and supporting legal research.
- Access gains are real only if the system improves fairness, not merely speed or volume.
- Legal AI has to be designed around who it serves and who answers when it is wrong.
- The strongest version of the access claim pairs abundance with [[LegalAIVerificationAuditability]] and [[HumanJudgmentUnderAI]].
- Access tools can become an institutional load problem when every claimant can generate more filings, appeals, objections, or requests.
