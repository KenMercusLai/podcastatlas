## Definition

局部胜利与战役态势不确定性 / Local Victory and Campaign Uncertainty is the gap between defeating the enemy formation actually encountered and knowing where the decisive enemy force is, whether detached units are safe, and whether the wider campaign objective has been achieved.

## Current Synthesis

The two Dingxiang episodes show the same warning at different stages. In [[FirstDingxiangCampaignWesternHan|the first sortie]], [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] rapidly defeats a [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] rear guard but treats the missing chanyu main force as a reason to withdraw, rest, and scout. The local result supplies information—this is not the decisive formation—without supplying campaign closure.

In [[SecondDingxiangCampaignWesternHan|the second sortie]], Wei Qing divides the renewed search into three routes. His central army wins another local engagement, yet neither wing arrives and the chanyu is again absent. Here tactical success coexists not only with incomplete enemy information but with uncertainty about friendly detachments, one of which is later shown to have suffered disaster.

The synthesis is not that commanders should always retreat after victory. It is that the evidentiary value of victory depends on target identity, friendly-force accountability, route knowledge, reconnaissance, and the remaining enemy capacity. A defeated rear guard or isolated formation can be a genuine success while still revealing that the decisive problem remains unsolved.

## Key Claims

- Winning contact with one formation does not prove that the enemy main body, commander, or campaign objective has been reached.
- An unexpectedly easy victory can be new evidence of displacement, screening, delay, or an unresolved counterstroke rather than proof of safety.
- Friendly-force location matters alongside enemy location; absent wings can make a victorious center operationally exposed or strategically uninformed.
- Rest, reconnaissance, and renewed search can be active exploitation of information rather than timidity after success.
- Tactical superiority in sustained contact can coexist with an opponent's campaign-level mobility and freedom to avoid decisive engagement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both sources are short popular-history summaries and do not independently reconstruct the campaign sequence, enemy plan, route geometry, scouting reports, force totals, casualty totals, or Wei Qing's private reasoning. The first episode's claim that danger could be “smelled” after victory is narrative characterization; the operationally supportable point is narrower: the identified battlefield result did not locate the main force.

The concept does not make caution universally superior. Delay can surrender initiative, reconnaissance can fail, and rapid pursuit can be necessary when reliable evidence shows that the decisive force is breaking. The current sources support an information-boundary principle, not a rule that every local success should be followed by withdrawal.

## What Changed

- Created a cross-episode distinction between battlefield result and campaign knowledge.
- Added friendly-detachment accountability to the earlier warning about an absent enemy main force.
