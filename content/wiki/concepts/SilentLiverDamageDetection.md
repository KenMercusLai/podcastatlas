# Silent Liver Damage Detection

## Definition
Silent liver damage detection is the source's boundary against using pain or everyday sensation as the main test of liver health. Because early injury may produce few noticeable symptoms, exposure history, laboratory findings, imaging, and planned follow-up carry more weight than waiting to feel ill.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents the liver as a high-capacity metabolic and synthetic organ with limited pain signaling in its parenchyma. That combination can hide early injury: abnormal liver-function results or fatty-liver imaging may be the first visible signal, while capsular pain, ascites, or bleeding can occur later. The practical judgment is not that every abnormal result predicts severe disease, but that symptom absence should not close the case.

## Key Claims
- Early liver injury may be clinically silent and should not be screened by pain alone.
- Liver-function tests and imaging can create an earlier opportunity for cause review and follow-up.
- A fatty-liver or transaminase finding is a signal to investigate, not by itself a final prognosis.
- Later symptoms can reflect more advanced disease, making planned review more useful than waiting.
- Public education cannot determine an individual's cause, stage, or treatment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish a screening schedule, diagnostic threshold, or individual prognosis. Liver tests and imaging require clinical interpretation, and pain can have many causes. The mechanism and examples remain source-scoped public education rather than hepatology guidance.

## What Changed
- Initial synthesis records the episode's pain-versus-testing distinction.
- Symptom absence is treated as diagnostic uncertainty rather than reassurance.
