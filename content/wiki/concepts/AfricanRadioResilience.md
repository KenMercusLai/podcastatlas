# African Radio Resilience

African radio resilience is the source's explanation for why radio remains a major medium across Africa despite podcasts, music platforms, social media, and streaming video. In [[in-arms-way-gaza-deal-sticking-points-6a71b49647235c354d3c9b74]], radio is cheap, available on many phones, usable in traffic, historically embedded, and independent of expensive mobile data.

The concept is not only nostalgia. The episode says more than half of Africans aged 18 to 35 regularly get news from radio, and [[Afrobarometer]] data has around 60% of urban and rural listeners using radio multiple times a week. The internet can strengthen radio too, because presenters can become social-media celebrities and earn from endorsements.

## Key Claims
- Radio can remain resilient when it fits cost, device, commute, and connectivity constraints better than higher-data media.
- Colonial, liberation, and post-colonial broadcast histories gave radio a deep political and cultural role.
- Young listeners can keep using radio even when social media is also important.
- Internet platforms may amplify radio personalities rather than simply replacing broadcast listening.
- Crowded FM bands can create demand for [[DABRadioCapacityExpansion]] instead of an internet-only transition.
