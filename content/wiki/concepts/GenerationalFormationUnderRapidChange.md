## Definition
Generational formation under rapid change is the process by which people develop a shared cohort identity through the institutions, technologies, opportunities, and crises they encounter at formative life stages, rather than through birth decade alone.

## Current Synthesis
[[zanmen-80hou-yige-daiji-yu-zhongguo-zhuigan-de-40nian-lkwrwuj1jcfxwte6dbvt1elvgl6e]] uses China's 80后 as a case of unusually dense change: scarcity and abundance, rural life and urban migration, education expansion and credential inflation, housing appreciation and balance-sheet repair, traditional family duty and individual autonomy, and the internet and AI all arrive within one lived arc. A cohort can therefore share a historical baseline without becoming a uniform social type. Life-stage timing explains common pressures; class, region, occupation, gender, wealth, and personal choices explain divergence inside the cohort.

## Key Claims
- Generational boundaries become more meaningful when institutional and technological conditions change quickly enough that adjacent cohorts face different worlds.
- Entry timing into education, work, housing, parenthood, and retirement shapes beliefs about effort, security, risk, and the future.
- Shared historical exposure creates family resemblance, not uniform values or outcomes.
- Older generations' advice becomes less reusable when the economic and technological environment changes faster than one life course.
- Generational labels are useful as a comparative lens but become misleading when they replace analysis of class, region, occupation, gender, or distribution.

## Evidence
- **Compressed-change case:** [[zanmen-80hou-yige-daiji-yu-zhongguo-zhuigan-de-40nian-lkwrwuj1jcfxwte6dbvt1elvgl6e]] connects 80后 life stages to urbanization, university expansion, housing commercialization, population pressure, the internet, and AI.
- **Timing over decade labels:** [[zanmen-80hou-yige-daiji-yu-zhongguo-zhuigan-de-40nian-lkwrwuj1jcfxwte6dbvt1elvgl6e]] compares Chinese decade labels with cohort definitions based on labor-market entry and shared events, and includes a 1991-born host who identifies culturally with older peers.
- **Internal diversity:** [[zanmen-80hou-yige-daiji-yu-zhongguo-zhuigan-de-40nian-lkwrwuj1jcfxwte6dbvt1elvgl6e]] explicitly describes one generation as containing different regional, occupational, class, and ideological units.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an interpretive conversation grounded mainly in urban, educated, content-industry experience. A coherent cohort story can overstate commonality, flatten people who did not benefit from growth, or turn structural distribution problems into age conflict. Birth-year labels remain useful for statistical comparison, but they do not demonstrate a shared identity by themselves.

## What Changed
- Created a cohort model based on social-change velocity and life-stage exposure rather than fixed decade boundaries.
- Made internal class, region, occupation, and value differences part of the concept rather than exceptions to it.
