# Non-Alcoholic Beer Growth / 无醇啤酒增长

## Definition
Non-alcoholic beer growth is the attempt to preserve beer taste, ritual, and social occasions while removing or sharply reducing alcohol.

## Current Synthesis
[[kafeidou-chuantong-meishi-guangchang-jielian-bidian-dashidaimen-yudao-naxie-fazhan-zuai-1007530222]] presents alcohol-free beer and soft drinks as a hedge for [[Carlsberg]] when conventional beer weakens. [[drone-truth-russia-has-the-upper-hand-6aad03f98a1508074de8337e]] places that company case inside a broader [[AlcoholDemandReset]] and argues that beer has a technical taste advantage over alcohol-free wine because producers can brew conventionally and remove the alcohol afterward.

## Key Claims
- Beer makers can treat no- and low-alcohol products as growth categories rather than defensive variants.
- The category preserves social-drinking occasions for consumers reducing alcohol intake.
- Dealcoholizing conventionally brewed beer can retain taste more successfully than current alcohol-free wine processes.
- Health, household-budget, and generational shifts can move demand away from conventional beer.
- Product taste still determines whether stated interest becomes repeat demand.

## Evidence
### Company hedge
- [[kafeidou-chuantong-meishi-guangchang-jielian-bidian-dashidaimen-yudao-naxie-fazhan-zuai-1007530222]] says soft drinks and alcohol-free beer helped [[Carlsberg]] grow revenue despite weaker traditional beer demand.

### Category and production advantage
- [[drone-truth-russia-has-the-upper-hand-6aad03f98a1508074de8337e]] compares beer favorably with alcohol-free wine on production method and taste while situating both inside declining alcohol consumption.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The sources do not establish uniform global adoption, category profitability, or long-term substitution rates.
- Better relative performance than alcohol-free wine does not mean consumers accept every no-alcohol beer; taste remains a binding qualification.

## What Changed
- Added the production-method explanation for beer's relative advantage over alcohol-free wine.
- Reframed the category inside a broader cross-alcohol demand reset.
