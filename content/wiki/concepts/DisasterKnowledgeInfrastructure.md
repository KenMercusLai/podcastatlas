# Disaster Knowledge Infrastructure

## Definition

Disaster knowledge infrastructure is the network of observers, records, reporting channels, questions, archives, and comparison methods through which a catastrophe becomes legible during response and reconstructable afterward.

## Current Synthesis

The comparison separates abundant evidence from timely operational knowledge. Huaxian generated gazetteers, inscriptions, testimony, poems, and memorial texts, yet reports reaching the Ming center were slow and imprecise enough to hinder targeting. After Lisbon, Pombal's standardized parish questionnaire used priests as distributed information nodes and preserved comparable observations. Records can therefore serve mourning and historical memory without automatically producing fast relief; useful infrastructure requires collection, transmission, standardization, preservation, and administrative use.

## Key Claims

- Local documentary abundance does not guarantee rapid central situational awareness.
- Standard questions make geographically dispersed observations more comparable.
- Existing trusted local institutions can act as emergency information networks.
- Archives extend disaster response into later scientific and historical reconstruction.
- Testimony, material damage, demographic estimates, and belief narratives answer different questions and should not be flattened into one evidence type.

## Evidence

- Documentary plurality: [[43-dongxifang-de-dizhen-1556nian-jiajing-dadizhen-vs-1755nian-lisiben-dadizhen-lqz5aznr86rd4a8xawybzotkkaps]] describes Huaxian gazetteers, inscriptions, personal reports, poetry, and memorial writing.
- Operational gap: [[43-dongxifang-de-dizhen-1556nian-jiajing-dadizhen-vs-1755nian-lisiben-dadizhen-lqz5aznr86rd4a8xawybzotkkaps]] reports a roughly forty-five-day delay and initially mismatched relief geography.
- Structured network: [[43-dongxifang-de-dizhen-1556nian-jiajing-dadizhen-vs-1755nian-lisiben-dadizhen-lqz5aznr86rd4a8xawybzotkkaps]] describes Pombal's questions on time, duration, direction, collapse, deaths, water, and fire sent through parishes and later archived.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source's claim that Lisbon was the first scientifically and systematically studied earthquake is retained as source-scoped. Standardization can improve comparability without guaranteeing accuracy, inclusion, political neutrality, or effective action, and local clerical reports are not equivalent to modern instrumentation.

## What Changed

- Established a distinction between historical record abundance and operational information speed.
- Added standardized parish inquiry as a distributed disaster-observation model.
- Connected archives to both governance and later reconstruction.
