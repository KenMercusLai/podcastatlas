## Definition

身体力行式家礼教化 / Embodied Household Ritual Discipline is the transmission of family norms through a senior member's repeated visible conduct, environmental consistency, and indirect correction rather than primarily through explicit doctrine or verbal instruction.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 251 part 1 presents [[ShiFenWesternHan|石奋]] as the model. Formal dress, deference at the palace, bowing before an imperial gift, and formal reception of descendants repeat the same rule across court and home. Because the behavior does not switch off in private, descendants and servants can read it as a standing household expectation rather than situational performance.

Correction works through social pressure. Shi Fen reportedly responds to wrongdoing by sitting silently and withholding participation in the meal until another senior relative explains the fault and the offender acknowledges it. The method may reduce angry confrontation and distribute teaching across the family, but it still uses patriarchal approval, food, and collective discomfort as leverage. “Leading by example” and coercion are therefore not mutually exclusive.

## Key Claims

- Repeated conduct can teach a household norm more continuously than occasional verbal instruction.
- Consistency across public and private settings strengthens the credibility of the modeled rule.
- Ritualized appearance and response make abstract hierarchy visible in ordinary interactions.
- Indirect correction can recruit peers and senior relatives into norm enforcement.
- Silence and withheld approval may avoid direct rage while still exerting coercive pressure.
- Family culture can travel into public office when descendants reuse its habits of caution, audience control, and verification.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one laudatory historical narrative, not observation of the household across generations. It cannot establish how descendants or servants experienced these norms, whether compliance was voluntary, or whether the practices generalized beyond elite court service. The episode's contrast between embodied practice and “empty” Confucian talk is rhetorically useful but does not show that doctrine and example are opposites; ritual example itself depends on learned meanings.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the complete Shi household sequence.
- Added the coercive-pressure qualification to the episode's positive “lead by example” frame.
