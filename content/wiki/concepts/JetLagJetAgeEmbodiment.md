# Jet Lag Jet Age Embodiment / 喷气时代的时差身体感

## Definition
Jet lag jet age embodiment is the way jet aircraft made technological speed physically perceptible by moving passengers across time zones faster than older ship or train travel allowed.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses jet lag as a small but revealing sign of aviation modernity. Ma Xiao notes that the term emerged after the jet-airliner era, because slower ships and trains gave bodies more time to adjust while jet aircraft made time-zone displacement immediately felt.

This concept is useful because it translates infrastructure into bodily experience. Aviation does not only compress distance on maps; it changes sleep, fatigue, arrival rhythm, and the felt relation between local time and body time.

## Key Claims
- Jet travel makes speed legible through bodily disruption as well as shorter travel time.
- Time-zone mismatch is an infrastructure effect experienced at the level of sleep and perception.
- Jet lag links passenger experience to aircraft technology and global route networks.
- Comfort improvements in newer aircraft matter because bodily recovery is part of long-haul service quality.
