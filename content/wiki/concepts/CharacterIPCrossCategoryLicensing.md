# Character IP Cross-Category Licensing / 角色IP跨品类授权

## Definition
Character IP cross-category licensing is the use of outside partners to place a recognizable character in product, retail, fashion, beauty, food, travel, or entertainment contexts beyond its original category.

## Current Synthesis
The Tamagotchi case treats licensing as distribution and audience acquisition, not only royalty income. A consumer who will not begin with an electronic toy may encounter the character through a phone case, cosmetic, suitcase, restaurant collaboration, or music fandom. The strategy can reactivate nostalgia and make the IP culturally current, while the underlying device gains awareness without Bandai manufacturing every category itself.

## Key Claims
- Cross-category partners supply channels, use occasions, and audiences the original product owner may not reach.
- A portable character identity can lower the consumer's entry barrier before purchase of the core product.
- Licensing can convert nostalgia into current fashion and lifestyle relevance.
- The licensor gains reach while surrendering some control over execution, quality, context, and customer data.
- Collaboration attention creates value only if it strengthens durable character or core-product demand.

## Evidence
- **Category breadth:** [[gaojiesi-doubao-zengjia-shuidian-jiaofei-gongneng-ruixing-yinru-naicha-pinlei-gkwrirwopt2saflztgtozfrl]] lists Tamagotchi collaborations across phone cases, cosmetics, luggage, conveyor-belt sushi, and K-pop.
- **Audience objective:** [[gaojiesi-doubao-zengjia-shuidian-jiaofei-gongneng-ruixing-yinru-naicha-pinlei-gkwrirwopt2saflztgtozfrl]] says [[Bandai]] uses partners to reach people its traditional toy business struggles to cover and to support device sales.
- **Regional objective:** [[gaojiesi-doubao-zengjia-shuidian-jiaofei-gongneng-ruixing-yinru-naicha-pinlei-gkwrirwopt2saflztgtozfrl]] places this strategy inside expansion in China, South Korea, and other Asian markets.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode provides no partnership-level sales, royalty economics, brand-lift study, or evidence that licensed attention converts into device purchases.
- Too many weak or inconsistent collaborations can dilute the character, create quality risk, or exhaust novelty.
- Reported regional shipment shares do not reveal current demand, active fandom, or the economics of individual licenses.
