# Precedent-Locked Creative Financing

## Definition

Precedent-locked creative financing is a selection pattern in which decision-makers evaluate unfamiliar work mainly through prior commercial categories, making demonstrated execution easier to reward than original authority and systematically disadvantaging creators or stories poorly represented in the precedent set.

## Current Synthesis

[[PattyJenkins]] describes a split between being trusted to direct projects conceived by others and being trusted to originate an unfamiliar one. After *Monster*, readers praised [[IAmSupermanFilm|I Am Superman]], but its adult dog story, morally ambiguous prison setting, unconventional protagonist, and unusual production method did not fit an established category that financiers could use to predict demand. She says even a budget of roughly $5 million could not overcome that uncertainty.

Precedent is not neutral when the historical sample reflects a narrow group of creators and interests. Jenkins argues that sexism helped decision-makers treat her success as exceptional rather than transferable, then interpret [[WonderWoman|Wonder Woman]] as evidence only for women directing women-centered stories. The mechanism can therefore reproduce unequal authority without an explicit ban: past exclusion limits precedents, and limited precedents are then used to justify future caution.

Independent financing and retained control are one possible response, but they transfer capital assembly and execution risk back to the creator. They protect an unconventional work from fearful notes only if the creator can secure resources, collaborators, distribution, and sufficient room to carry out the intended process.

## Key Claims

- Commercial precedent can become a backward-looking filter rather than a neutral forecast.
- Success at executing an assigned project does not necessarily produce trust in a creator's original ideas.
- A narrow historical creator pool can make precedent-based selection reproduce earlier exclusion.
- Industry learning may reduce a success to its most visible category instead of recognizing transferable judgment and craft.
- Independent financing can protect creative authority while shifting financial, operational, and distribution burdens to the creator.

## Evidence

### Original-authority gap

- [[i-am-superman-with-patty-jenkins-development-hell-i-am-superman-with-patty-jenkins-development-hell]] reports Jenkins's claim that the industry welcomed her as director of other people's projects while repeatedly distrusting her original ideas.

### Category-based interpretation

- [[i-am-superman-with-patty-jenkins-development-hell-i-am-superman-with-patty-jenkins-development-hell]] says executives responded to *Monster* by asking for more female-serial-killer projects and interpreted *Wonder Woman* mainly as evidence for women directing women's stories.

### Financing and control

- [[i-am-superman-with-patty-jenkins-development-hell-i-am-superman-with-patty-jenkins-development-hell]] says Jenkins could not secure approximately $5 million for *I Am Superman* and would now prefer to assemble financing herself to protect the film from excessive notes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is grounded in Jenkins's retrospective account and lacks studio records, competing pitches, budget analysis, market research, counterfactual financing decisions, or testimony from decision-makers. Sexism and precedent dependence may coexist with concerns about rating, animal production, prison access, genre, distribution, or screenplay execution; the source does not identify their relative causal weight. *Monster* and *Wonder Woman* are selected successes and do not establish a general outcome for all original projects or women filmmakers.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish demonstrated directing competence from institutional trust in original creative authority.
