# Strategic Depth Defense / 战略纵深防御

Strategic depth defense is the survival pattern in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-82-daqin-de-hewuqi-baiqi-laile-lo0w8mifkijhzmx7fdwcimmv7ade]] where a damaged state can use space to buy time, complicate enemy logistics, and make further pursuit politically risky. The episode applies it to [[ChuState|楚国]] after [[BaiQi|白起]] breaks the old capital region: [[MiHeng|楚顷襄王]] moves toward Chen, away from the newly conquered area and closer to [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]].

The source's point is not that Chu becomes strong again immediately. It argues that distance changes Qin's problem. If [[QinState|秦国]] pursues Chen directly, it needs passage through Han and Wei, risking a severed rear route; if it avoids those states, it must cross difficult mountain and river terrain with poor supply. That makes Qin's later attack on Wei part of the Chu campaign logic rather than a random target switch.

The concept connects geography to sequencing. Strategic depth can turn defeat into delay, and delay can force the attacker to solve transit, alliance, and supply problems before finishing the original campaign.
