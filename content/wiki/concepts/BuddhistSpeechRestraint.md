## Definition

佛门止语修行 / Buddhist speech restraint is the host's practice frame for reducing false, divisive, harsh, frivolous, judgmental, or grievance-driven speech so attention returns to self-cultivation, calm observation, and kinder expression.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 892 gives the wiki's earlier "修止语" precursor: restraint is a daily self-command practice that reduces complaint, quarrel, and restless verbal impulse before the episode turns toward late-Han political exposure. Hanji 389-3 makes the Buddhist branch explicit. It links "止语" and "少语" to口戒, retreat silence, reduced gossip, and the idea that judging close relations wastes blessing and disturbs practice.

The useful practical synthesis is that speech restraint is a trained pause between perception and expression. It is not merely refusing to communicate; it is a discipline of not converting every event into blame, gossip, or self-justifying talk. The stronger merit language, including "福报" and "功德语," remains source-scoped spiritual interpretation.

## Key Claims

- Speech restraint is treated as a cultivated practice rather than a personality trait.
- The Buddhist branch centers restraint from false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, frivolous speech, and interpersonal judgment.
- Not discussing others' right/wrong or good/bad is framed as a way to turn events back into self-cultivation.
- Retreat silence provides an intensive training environment for reducing verbal impulse.
- Long practice is said to reshape the quality of speech toward praise, kindness, and "功德语."
- The practice overlaps with [[AdversitySilenceDiscipline|逆境守口]] but is gentler: its first concern is inner clarity and relational kindness, not only risk control.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not recommend universal muteness or avoidance of necessary communication. Hanji 389-3 is a short spiritual-practice talk, not a full Buddhist doctrinal treatment or a communication manual for conflict, abuse, law, medicine, or workplace safety. Its "福报" and "功德语" claims are preserved as the host's worldview; the wiki does not treat them as empirically demonstrated causal mechanisms.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 389-3 as the explicit Buddhist "止语/少语" source, separating the religious-practice branch from the older political-survival branch.
- Folded Hanji 892's "修止语" precursor into a narrower concept focused on speech restraint rather than all adversity silence.
