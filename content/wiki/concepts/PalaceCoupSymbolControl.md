## Definition

Palace Coup Symbol Control is the pattern where a court coup succeeds by first forming a trusted private access chain and then capturing the practical signs and channels of authority: palace entry, halls, shangshu communication, gates, armed guards, imperial tallies, seals, certificates, and the target's office insignia.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 804 adds an earlier Shun-restoration layer. [[EmperorShunOfHan|Liu Bao]]'s supporters make accession operational by seizing the imperial seal in the North Palace, moving him to Jiade Hall, opening and controlling gates, and forcing commitment at Zhangtai Gate through the killing of Yan-side generals. The palace symbol sequence does not merely announce the restoration; it produces the command environment in which [[YanXianLateHan|Yan Xian]] can be destroyed.

Hanji 847 gives the concept a later pre-coup layer. [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]] cannot move directly against [[LiangJiLateHan|Liang Ji]] while Liang dominates the outer field, so he first uses [[TangHengLateHan|Tang Heng]] to identify close attendants with grievances against the Liang family and binds them through a secret oath. The later seizure of symbols depends on this earlier selection of people who can act inside the palace.

Hanji 848 gives the execution layer. Huan does not defeat Liang by open battlefield superiority; he wins by acting first inside the palace, neutralizing Liang's monitor, convening the shangshu channel, guarding the palace offices, controlling tallies and seals, and sending troops before Liang can turn his apparent network into command.

The concept's main claim is that symbols are not merely decorative. In this coup, whoever holds access to the palace, the documents, the seals, the tallies, and the guards can make authority operational. Liang's status as general-in-chief collapses when the palace-centered command chain makes that title unusable and then physically removes his seal.

## Key Claims

- A palace coup is decided by trusted insider selection, timing, and channel control before formal titles can be mobilized.
- Seizing the imperial seal and installing the preferred ruler in a named palace hall turns a succession claim into executable authority.
- Gates and guards decide whether a title can become action and whether the losing side can still communicate or assemble force.
- Point-of-no-return violence at a gate can bind conspirators and distinguish committed participants from hesitant followers.
- Secret recruitment, oath-binding, and leak-risk control can be the precondition for later symbol control.
- Controlling shangshu offices, palace gates, seals, tallies, and office insignia turns imperial will into executable command.
- Public settlement after the coup uses confiscation, tax remission, title changes, certificates, and rewards to make the victory legible.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept does not claim that all coups are purely symbolic or that military force is secondary in every context. Hanji 804 shows restoration symbol control and gate violence but does not reconstruct every armed unit in the palace. Hanji 847 shows recruitment and oath-binding as a necessary precondition in the Huan case, not as a sufficient cause by itself. Hanji 848 shows a specific palace-centered pattern: armed pressure works because the emperor's side controls the channels that make force legitimate and coordinated. The sources do not fully reconstruct Liang Ji's possible counterforce; they record how quickly his network fails once the palace sequence begins.

## What Changed

- Hanji 804 adds an earlier Shun-restoration case: North Palace seal seizure, Jiade Hall installation, gate control, and Zhangtai Gate commitment.
- Hanji 847 adds the pre-coup private recruitment, oath, and leak-risk layer before symbol seizure.
- Hanji 848 remains the palace monitor arrest, shangshu mobilization, tally-and-seal control, armed encirclement, and title-removal sequence.
