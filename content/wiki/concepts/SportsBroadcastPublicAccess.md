# Sports Broadcast Public Access

Sports broadcast public access is the rule-and-norm layer in [[e237-yangshi-he-fifa-tanpan-fenzheng-beihou-tiyu-saishi-zhuanboquan-de-boyi-yu-shengyi-c3607c08-6eb6-48bd-8250-b41b5b8272e6]] that keeps some major sports events visible to broad audiences even when rights prices are high. The episode says [[FIFA]] requires important [[FIFAWorldCup]] matches such as opening, semifinal, and final stages to remain publicly available in some form, while Chinese viewers can watch the full tournament free through [[ChinaCentralTelevision]]'s arrangement.

The concept matters because free access is not anti-commercial. Public reach can increase advertising value, sponsor exposure, social conversation, and platform status. But it also limits pure paywall logic and helps explain why a public broadcaster may remain central to national-event rights even as streaming platforms, telecom operators, and traffic platforms want parts of the package.

## Key Claims
- Some elite sports rights carry public-event obligations or norms that constrain exclusive paywalls.
- Free viewing can support commercial returns through mass advertising and sponsor reach.
- A public broadcaster's strategic value rises when a rights package has national cultural status, not only subscriber value.
- Public access can coexist with [[SportsRightsSublicensing]] when the upstream broadcaster keeps universal availability and sells platform-specific extensions.
