## Definition
天下中国 / Tianxia China is the sources' term for a historical Chinese order that is larger than direct territorial rule: it includes cultural recognition, suzerainty, protectorates, jimi offices, tribute hierarchy, and shared civilizational order.

## Current Synthesis
In the Longshan episode, [[YanHaijun|严海军 / 阎海军]] ties Tianxia China to the east-west movement organized around [[LongshanGansuShaanxi|陇山 / 龙山]]. The source says Qin-Han and Sui-Tang are the clearest periods when a fuller Tianxia China becomes visible, because military movement, institutions, cultural transmission, and frontier governance expand beyond an inner agrarian core.

The concept's current value is boundary-setting. It does not mean every relationship becomes direct county-and-commandery administration. It includes looser but still ordered forms such as cultural identity, tributary or suzerain relations, jimi prefectures, and protectorates. This makes it relevant to the [[WesternRegionsProtectorate|Western Regions Protectorate]] and to the source's argument that east-west connection helped make historical China larger than a single interior region.

Hanji 422-2 adds a cost-side qualification. Tribute order can make hierarchy visible, but it may require reward payments,回赐, hospitality, and favorable trade terms. When "天朝上国" status is treated as nonnegotiable under changed treaty conditions, Tianxia imagination can harden into [[TributaryStatusDiplomaticRigidity|朝贡身份秩序外交僵化]] rather than flexible frontier order.

## Key Claims
- Tianxia China in these sources is broader than direct territorial annexation.
- Qin-Han and Sui-Tang are presented as the periods when the source's fuller historical China becomes most visible.
- East-west corridor movement through Longshan helps make the larger order plausible by connecting Guanzhong, Longyou, Hexi, and the Western Regions.
- Governance forms such as protectorates, jimi offices, suzerainty, and cultural recognition belong to the concept's source definition.
- The source contrasts this order with pure elimination or simple incorporation of outsiders.
- Tribute hierarchy can carry real fiscal incentives and diplomatic face costs rather than only cultural recognition.
- Under treaty-era pressure, Tianxia status imagination can become rigid if equal resident diplomacy is read as ritual demotion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page records two source formulations and should not be treated as a complete intellectual history of tianxia thought. The sources' positive descriptions of shared order do not erase coercion, war, fiscal extraction, frontier violence, economic inducement, or conflicts with non-Han and foreign polities. Hanji 422-2's late-Qing branch is especially source-scoped because it compresses the Second Opium War into a tribute-status lesson.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 422-2's economic and diplomatic qualification: tribute hierarchy can be materially subsidized and can harden into status rigidity under treaty pressure.
