# Bushido as Invented Tradition

## Definition
Bushido as invented tradition is the source's claim that "the way of the warrior" became a formalized modern code largely after the Meiji period, even though it drew on older samurai ideals of honor, shame, service, and reputation.

## Current Synthesis
[[658-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shadow-of-the-sword-part-1-glt7378881498]] separates medieval samurai practice from the later idea of a fixed ancient code. The episode does not deny that medieval warriors had demanding expectations around shame, honor, loyalty, reputation, and self-mythologizing. It argues instead that formal Bushido was assembled after the samurai order had been abolished, mixing Japanese materials with modern national, military, European chivalric, and gentlemanly influences.

The concept therefore sits between dismissal and romantic acceptance. Bushido is not treated as timeless law handed down unchanged from the first samurai, but neither is samurai honor treated as pure fantasy.

## Key Claims
- Formal Bushido is presented as largely post-Meiji codification rather than an unchanged medieval constitution.
- Medieval samurai still had real ideals of honor, shame, service, and reputation.
- The Meiji abolition of samurai status helped make samurai identity available for later nostalgic and political reconstruction.
- Early 20th-century militarism could appropriate Bushido for imperial violence.
- Postwar film, television, manga, games, and global media reworked samurai tradition again.

## Evidence
- Modern codification: [[658-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shadow-of-the-sword-part-1-glt7378881498]] says Bushido was largely a modern post-Meiji invention influenced by Japanese traditions, European chivalry, and the English gentleman.
- Medieval residue: [[658-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shadow-of-the-sword-part-1-glt7378881498]] says mythologizing does not erase real medieval ideals around shame and honor.
- Political appropriation: [[658-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shadow-of-the-sword-part-1-glt7378881498]] links early 20th-century Bushido to Japanese military justification in Korea, China, and the Second World War.
- Popular afterlife: [[658-dawn-of-the-samurai-the-shadow-of-the-sword-part-1-glt7378881498]] connects samurai tradition to Kurosawa films, [[StarWars]], manga, television, and games such as [[GhostOfTsushima]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's "invented tradition" claim is about formalized Bushido, not a claim that premodern warriors lacked values, loyalty, ritual, or reputational ethics. It also remains source-scoped to this episode's framing rather than a full historiography of Bushido scholarship.

## What Changed
- Created this concept to preserve the episode's distinction between medieval warrior ideals and modern Bushido codification.
