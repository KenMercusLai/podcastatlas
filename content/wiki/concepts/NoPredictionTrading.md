# No-Prediction Trading

## Definition

No-prediction trading is a repeated-game approach in which observable conditions trigger bounded trades without asserting that the next position will win; viability depends on the distribution of many outcomes after sizing, costs, exits, and execution.

## Current Synthesis

The two 面基 sources separate signal from prophecy. A market can be stronger, broader, or “hotter” without making any one trade certain. The trader therefore evaluates a system through comparable repetitions: hit rate, payoff ratio, opportunity density, costs, drawdown, and adherence. Small probes make failure informative, while stop losses prevent a failed observation from becoming an identity defense.

The newer episode adds an operational requirement. No-prediction trading is not permission to act randomly; it needs a written universe, entry and exit states, portfolio limits, exception rules, and a workflow that prevents commentary or recent profit from changing the game midstream.

## Key Claims

- A market-state signal is an entry condition, not a forecast about the next return.
- Low hit rates can remain compatible with positive expectation when winners are materially larger than losers.
- Many small, diversified repetitions are more informative than a few oversized conviction bets.
- Failed trades are feedback only when their size is survivable and the rules remain comparable.
- Written execution rules protect the statistical game from emotional parameter changes after wins or losses.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not provide a complete audited dataset demonstrating stable positive expectation. A historical win rate or payoff ratio may be estimated badly, change with regime, or disappear after costs. “No prediction” does not remove assumptions: universe choice, signal construction, execution, liquidity, and the belief that observed asymmetry will persist are all model decisions.
