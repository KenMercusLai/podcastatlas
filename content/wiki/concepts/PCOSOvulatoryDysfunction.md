# PCOS Ovulatory Dysfunction / 多囊卵巢综合征排卵障碍

## Definition
PCOS ovulatory dysfunction is the reproductive pattern in which multiple follicles begin developing without regular selection, maturation, and release of a dominant follicle, contributing to irregular or absent ovulation and menstrual disruption.

## Current Synthesis
The “polycystic” label can be misleading if interpreted as many ordinary surgical cysts. In the episode's simplified model, the visible small follicles reflect disrupted follicular maturation and ovulation. This connects ovarian appearance with cycle irregularity while leaving diagnosis, causes, and treatment to a fuller clinical assessment.

The reproductive explanation is only one layer. Existing wiki evidence treats PCOS as potentially relevant to long-term metabolic and cardiovascular risk, so an ovulation-focused account should not reduce the syndrome to fertility or ultrasound appearance alone.

VOL.27 reinforces the diagnostic boundary. Irregular menstruation, weight change, acne, increased body hair, ultrasound findings, and androgen-related laboratory results may contribute to an assessment, but no single feature establishes the syndrome. Management can combine lifestyle and medication according to phenotype and goals, with recurrence or persistent manifestations remaining possible.

Aliabadi adds heterogeneity and mechanism detail. Her interview uses a two-of-three criteria frame across androgen excess, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovarian morphology or elevated AMH, while warning that adolescent cycles and follicle counts require age-specific interpretation. Regular bleeding may not prove ovulation, and normal serum testosterone does not by itself exclude a clinically relevant androgen pattern.

## Key Claims
- The polycystic appearance is not equivalent to a collection of ordinary large ovarian cysts.
- Disrupted follicular maturation and ovulation can contribute to irregular menstruation.
- Ovarian appearance alone is not a complete diagnosis or severity measure.
- Assessment combines cycle history, clinical features, imaging, laboratory findings, and exclusion of alternative explanations.
- Reproductive symptoms should be integrated with endocrine and metabolic context.
- PCOS criteria and phenotypes are heterogeneous; age, symptoms, ovulation, androgen evidence, and ovarian morphology must be interpreted together.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources offer accessible mechanism and assessment sketches rather than a current diagnostic guideline or management protocol. PCOS is heterogeneous; ultrasound, AMH, cycle history, androgen-related features, metabolic status, medication use, age, and exclusion of other causes require qualified interpretation. Aliabadi's prevalence, phenotype, ovulation, and adolescent claims remain source-scoped, while VOL.27's statements about cure, control, fertility, and recurrence remain source-scoped.
