# PCOS Ovulatory Dysfunction / 多囊卵巢综合征排卵障碍

## Definition
PCOS ovulatory dysfunction is the reproductive pattern in which multiple follicles begin developing without regular selection, maturation, and release of a dominant follicle, contributing to irregular or absent ovulation and menstrual disruption.

## Current Synthesis
The “polycystic” label can be misleading if interpreted as many ordinary surgical cysts. In the episode's simplified model, the visible small follicles reflect disrupted follicular maturation and ovulation. This connects ovarian appearance with cycle irregularity while leaving diagnosis, causes, and treatment to a fuller clinical assessment.

The reproductive explanation is only one layer. Existing wiki evidence treats PCOS as potentially relevant to long-term metabolic and cardiovascular risk, so an ovulation-focused account should not reduce the syndrome to fertility or ultrasound appearance alone.

## Key Claims
- The polycystic appearance is not equivalent to a collection of ordinary large ovarian cysts.
- Disrupted follicular maturation and ovulation can contribute to irregular menstruation.
- Ovarian appearance alone is not a complete diagnosis or severity measure.
- Reproductive symptoms should be integrated with endocrine and metabolic context.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers an accessible mechanism sketch rather than diagnostic criteria, phenotyping, prevalence, or a management protocol. PCOS is heterogeneous; ultrasound, cycle history, androgen-related features, metabolic status, medication use, age, and exclusion of other causes require qualified interpretation. The episode does not develop the cardiometabolic branch.
