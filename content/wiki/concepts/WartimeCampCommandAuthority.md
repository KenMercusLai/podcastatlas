## Definition

Wartime camp command authority / 战备营垒指挥权 is the bounded delegation that lets a commander enforce entry, movement, readiness, and military ritual inside an active-defense camp, including against a sovereign's unprocessed arrival.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-228-zhouyafu-jiang-wendi-ju-yu-yingwai-liuheng-de-biaoxian-lingren-yiwai-lg4dt0ojthk1rvvma4prssxyx6w9|Hanji 228 Xiliu]] contrasts three camps during a reported [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] emergency. Bashang and Jimen admit the emperor's convoy freely, whereas [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]]'s Xiliu camp stays armed, blocks both the advance party and [[LiuHeng|刘恒]], opens only after formal notice, and continues to prohibit galloping and kneeling court ritual inside the defenses.

The case does not place a general above the ruler. Formal imperial authorization still opens the gate, and Liu Heng remains able to inspect, judge, appoint, and demobilize. Its narrower claim is that delegated command loses operational credibility if personal status can bypass the rules designed to distinguish an authorized arrival from surprise, confusion, or deception. Civil authority reinforces military discipline here by accepting the procedure that temporarily constrains it.

## Key Claims

- Entry control is part of readiness because a prestigious convoy can still create surprise, congestion, or a security breach.
- A field-command rule becomes credible when it binds high-status visitors as well as ordinary personnel.
- Formal authorization can reconcile sovereign supremacy with the commander's immediate control of the camp.
- Movement and ritual rules inside the camp communicate that operational conditions temporarily supersede normal court display.
- Civil authority can strengthen delegated command by rewarding disciplined compliance rather than demanding personal exemption.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one popular-history episode's retelling of a canonical anecdote, not on a comparative study of camp security or civil-military law. The contrast with Bashang and Jimen infers weak readiness from easy admission but supplies no enemy test or later combat outcome. The quoted order, ritual, timing, and appointment sequence require textual comparison. The case supports bounded operational delegation, not autonomous military power, routine refusal of lawful civilian orders, or the claim that every procedural obstacle indicates competence.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the Xiliu contrast between formal sovereign authority and credible internal camp control.
