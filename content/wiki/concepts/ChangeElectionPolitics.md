# Change Election Politics

## Definition
Change election politics is the campaign environment where voters reward candidates who credibly represent disruption, renewal, or anti-status-quo agency over continuity and managerial competence.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses the change-election frame to connect midterm backlash, Democratic factionalism, and 2028 candidate risk. A party can benefit from the public wanting change when it is out of power or opposing an unpopular president, but its own continuity candidates can still look stale in a primary or presidential cycle.

## Key Claims
- Recent U.S. elections are framed as change elections where status quo candidates are vulnerable.
- Trump's low approval, economic anxiety, Iran, gas prices, and political fatigue create a difficult 2026 environment for Republicans.
- A Democratic candidate can still weaken a favorable environment if they embody unpopular continuity or blue-state governance problems.
- Newsom's redistricting move is presented as tactically smart but not enough to solve the broader continuity problem.
- AOC's outsider scenario becomes more plausible if Democratic primary voters want anti-establishment agency rather than restoration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept does not imply that disruption is always electorally optimal or good governance. It records an electoral demand pattern; the source also values functional government, basic quality of life, optimism, and credible governing competence.
