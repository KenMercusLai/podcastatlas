# Orbital Debris Governance

Orbital debris governance is the practical problem of managing human-made objects, discarded hardware, satellites, rockets, and collision risk as the space economy grows. In [[tech-20260807-0807-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260807-0807-mp-tech-pod-128]], [[JoannaStern]] connects a discarded rocket's collision with the [[Moon]] to broader scientific concern about space junk from satellites and other devices.

The concept extends [[SpaceEconomyInfrastructure]] and [[SpaceBasedAIInfrastructure]]. If companies and states want manufacturing on the Moon, satellite networks, lunar staging, or orbital compute, then debris tracking, collision avoidance, end-of-life disposal, and accountability for abandoned hardware become infrastructure requirements rather than cleanup afterthoughts.

## Key Claims
- Space infrastructure creates externalities when launch stages, satellites, or abandoned devices remain unmanaged.
- Lunar and orbital industrial plans have to account for where old hardware goes and who is responsible for collision consequences.
- Debris governance is linked to [[Starlink]]-style constellation scale, [[SpaceX]] launch cadence, and future lunar manufacturing ambitions.
- The issue is not only environmental; it affects reliability, insurance, safety, diplomacy, and whether space can function as a durable platform.
