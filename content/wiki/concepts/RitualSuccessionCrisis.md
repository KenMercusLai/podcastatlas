# Ritual Succession Crisis / 仪式传承危机

Ritual succession crisis is the future-risk concept added by [[ep249-minnan-gucheng-youshenji-chuantong-yishi-beihou-de-xiangtu-wangluo-lpk1vb9-zmgmuous1nyzefgapgww]]. The source argues that the main threat to Minnan ritual practice is not whether tourists keep watching, but whether local younger generations will keep carrying the physical, organizational, and belief-laden work.

In the episode, many young people leave [[YongningAncientCity|永宁古城]] or nearby villages for education and livelihoods in Jinjiang, Shishi, [[Quanzhou|泉州]], Xiamen, or larger cities. Some returners run hostels, make self-media, shoot documentaries, produce short videos, or organize events, but that does not necessarily mean they enter lineage committees, temple work, ancestor worship, or deity processions as responsible insiders.

The source gives a concrete symptom: some processional sedan chairs are being adapted with carts or small trucks because elders can no longer carry them and younger people are unwilling or unavailable. Lineage associations can try youth fellowships, but the episode treats succession as unresolved because the work asks for time, body, belief tolerance, and local obligation, not just cultural appreciation.

## Key Claims
- A tradition can become more visible online while becoming weaker as a local practice.
- Return migration and cultural entrepreneurship do not automatically solve ritual succession.
- Physical labor matters: carrying, waiting, preparing, funding, and organizing are part of the tradition, not logistics outside it.
- Mechanizing a ritual object can preserve a procession while signaling that the social body behind it has changed.
- Youth succession depends on whether local belonging remains valuable enough to compete with education, city work, and modern identity.
