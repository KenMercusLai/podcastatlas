# Human Value Beyond Efficiency

Human value beyond efficiency is the source's closing answer to AI replacement anxiety: if machines become better at speed, memory, coding, and required work, human value may remain in slow, unnecessary, emotional, and chosen acts. In [[suanli-kuangxiangqu-wo-zai-ai-gongchang-de-qiyu-lorijulltfhttspka22jnn4qjf-i]], the narrator names examples such as traveling far to see someone, crying at a film known to be fictional, and spending time on things that look irrational from an efficiency view.

The concept extends [[HumanAgencyUnderAI]] and [[AIUsePacing]]. It is not an argument against AI tools; it is a claim that better automation makes the user's answer to "what is worth doing" more important rather than less.

## Key Claims
- Human value should not be measured only by speed, recall, execution, or output volume once AI can outperform people on those axes.
- Inefficient choices can still carry relational, aesthetic, bodily, or moral meaning.
- [[AIWorkOptionality]] creates a meaning problem: if necessary work shrinks, people need non-output reasons for action.
- [[IntelligenceDevaluation]] may make generic cognitive production cheaper, increasing the importance of taste, care, responsibility, and lived context.
- [[AutomatedLifeDelegation]] is the negative boundary: delegating too much can remove the very friction that made an experience matter.
