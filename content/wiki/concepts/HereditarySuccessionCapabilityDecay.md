## Definition

世袭继承能力衰减 / hereditary succession capability decay is Hanji 1074's `Changyan`-based claim that inherited position reliably transfers legal status but does not reliably transfer the founder's governing ability, judgment, restraint, or contact with ordinary hardship.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] attributes the diagnosis to [[ZhongchangTongLateHan|仲长统]]. The episode connects household “wealth does not pass three generations” language to a larger political-cycle argument: a founder may rise through broad capability and exposure to constraint, but hereditary selection cannot ensure an equally capable successor.

The claimed mechanism is not biology or a mechanical three-generation clock. It is institutional insulation. Successors raised inside durable privilege may become less responsive to popular hardship, while their desires remain backed by forced labor and taxation. Capability mismatch and insulation then turn private consumption into public extraction, helping explain decline without proving that every hereditary successor is worse than the founder.

## Key Claims

- Hereditary rules transfer position more reliably than governing competence.
- Founders selected by struggle and successors selected by birth face different capability filters.
- Long exposure to privilege can reduce attention to ordinary hardship and corrective feedback.
- Elite consumption becomes politically dangerous when supported by forced labor and heavy taxation.
- The pattern offers one mechanism for political cycles, not a deterministic law of generational decline.

- Succession mismatch: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] says hereditary designation cannot ensure that later rulers match the first generation's comprehensive ability.
- Elite insulation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] argues that successors raised in luxury and indulgence become numb to popular suffering.
- Extraction mechanism: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] connects labor demands and heavy taxation used to satisfy elite desires with regime collapse.
- Political-cycle frame: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1074-quan-qing-chaoye-de-simayi-weihe-meici-jian-ta-dou-guibai-lpiw4ebdo2s5cucfcpmpkrhfnubi|Hanji 1074]] presents the argument as a bridge from “富不过三代” to recurring order and disorder.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in one podcast's compressed interpretation of `Changyan`, not a direct inventory of Zhongchang Tong's text. The source does not test capable later-generation rulers, incompetent founders, institutions that train or constrain heirs, or nonhereditary systems with similar elite-insulation problems. “Second” and “third” generations should therefore be read as rhetorical shorthand, not a universal timetable.
