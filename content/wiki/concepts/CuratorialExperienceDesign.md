# Curatorial Experience Design / 策展体验设计

## Definition
Curatorial experience design is the organization of works, sequence, space, light, interpretation, participation, and sensory conditions into a visitor journey whose effect exceeds a list of displayed objects.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf|EP163]] treats curation as an arrangement of attention and state. A retrospective can turn isolated famous images into a life and practice unfolding across time; scale and light can quiet a room or weaken self-consciousness; interpretation can deepen a response without dictating one; and food, installation, interaction, or other media can form part of a coherent story. Visitor freedom remains essential: the journey works through selective attention, pauses, memory, return, and changes in mood rather than compulsory completion.

## Key Claims
- Sequence can convert disconnected works into a developmental, historical, or emotional narrative.
- Space, scale, lighting, sound, crowd behavior, and photography rules materially affect attention.
- Interpretation should offer context without requiring a prescribed emotional response.
- Participation and multisensory elements add value when they serve a coherent question or story.
- A visitor's before-and-after state is one useful, incomplete test of curatorial effect.

## Evidence
- Sequence and biography - [[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf]] compares an Andy Warhol retrospective with seeing a complete film rather than scattered trailers.
- Space and mood - [[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf]] uses a Rothko exhibition's scale and natural, subdued light to show environment shaping behavior and perception.
- Visitor agency - [[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf]] advises skipping, pausing, remembering, and returning rather than treating a museum as a completion task.
- Multisensory storytelling - [[ep163-bei-hulianwang-chongji-de-yishu-ckwriaieiyu2abaaaacscgxf]] describes a Chinese American food-history exhibition that joins installation with tasting.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode supplies visitor and practitioner examples rather than comparative exhibition research. A strong mood change does not by itself prove historical accuracy, accessibility, ethical interpretation, conservation quality, or intellectual depth; similarly, interaction and photogenic spectacle can support or distract from a curatorial argument depending on execution.

## What Changed
- Created a general exhibition-experience framework distinct from object-label legibility and place-based curation.
