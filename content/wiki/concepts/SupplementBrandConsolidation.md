# Supplement Brand Consolidation

Supplement brand consolidation is the pattern where large consumer-health or CPG companies acquire supplement brands to enter recurring wellness categories. [[fuzhuang-pinpai-a-f-xunzhao-zhongguo-hezuo-huoban-fufei-tiqian-kan-telangpu-tiewen-fuwu-shangxian-1004810677]] adds the concept through [[ProcterGamble|Procter & Gamble]]'s reported $3.8 billion acquisition of [[Thorne]].

The concept connects business appetite to the wiki's supplement-risk branch. Supplements can be attractive because they support repeat purchase, premium positioning, and wellness storytelling, but [[DietarySupplementRegulation]], [[SupplementStructureFunctionClaims]], [[SupplementLabelAccuracy]], and [[ThirdPartySupplementTesting]] determine how much trust the category deserves.

## Key Claims
- Large CPG buyers can use acquisitions to move faster into vitamins, minerals, probiotics, fiber, gut health, and other wellness categories.
- Premium supplement brands can command high valuations when revenue growth and repeat-purchase behavior look durable.
- Consolidation does not eliminate evidence or label-risk questions; it may instead make consumer-trust infrastructure more important.
- The business logic of recurring wellness demand should be read alongside regulatory limits and product-verification concerns.
