# Emotional Granularity

## Definition
Emotional granularity is the ability to differentiate one's own emotional states at a fine enough level that regulation can target the actual state rather than a vague global feeling.

## Current Synthesis
In the Adolphs episode, granularity sits between emotion science and practical regulation. People do not need a perfect word for every feeling, but they do need usable concepts that let them notice whether a state is anger, sadness, anxiety, awe, gratitude, panic, or some mixture. Granularity matters because emotion is necessary for decision and motivation; the goal is not to mute emotion but to understand the state early enough to choose a better response.

## Key Claims
- Granularity turns emotion from a diffuse takeover into a more inspectable state.
- Language can help regulation, but a usable concept matters more than a specific label.
- Fine-grained differentiation supports early intervention before the emotion fully controls behavior.
- Emotional granularity depends on monitoring emotion, not denying that it is present.
- The concept fits regulation because many emotional problems come from regulation failures rather than from emotion itself.
