# Processing-Speed Training Dementia Signal

## Definition
Processing-speed training dementia signal is the source's claim that training rapid interpretation and recall, especially with booster sessions, has some long-term dementia-risk signal while remaining narrower than broad real-world skill learning.

## Current Synthesis
Wood distinguishes simple reaction time from processing speed. Processing speed requires interpreting and remembering information after brief exposure, so it trains more than pressing a button quickly. The episode uses BrainHQ, Maintain Your Brain, POINTER, and ACTIVE-trial follow-up to show why targeted cognitive training remains part of dementia-prevention research.

The signal is qualified by transfer limits. Crosswords, Sudoku, or computer-based drills may challenge attention, but Wood argues that broader activities can recruit more systems at once. The best synthesis is therefore not "brain games solve dementia"; it is that some cognitive training may matter when it is challenging, repeated, and embedded in a wider prevention ecology.

## Key Claims
- Processing speed is different from simple reaction time because it requires interpreting and remembering information.
- Processing-speed decline may be later than reaction-time decline, making it a candidate capacity to train in older adulthood.
- ACTIVE-trial secondary analysis is presented as a long-term dementia-risk signal when training included booster sessions.
- BrainHQ is the named platform example in the episode's processing-speed discussion.
- Multidomain programs such as Maintain Your Brain and POINTER place cognitive training beside diet, exercise, behavioral therapy, and cardiovascular-risk management.
- Targeted training remains narrower than embodied, social, multimodal learning.

## Evidence
- Definition boundary - [[accelerate-learning-increase-cognitive-capacity-dr-tommy-wood-scim4201272551]] distinguishes processing speed from reaction time by requiring interpretation and recall after brief exposure.
- Age pattern - [[accelerate-learning-increase-cognitive-capacity-dr-tommy-wood-scim4201272551]] says reaction time declines slowly from adulthood while processing speed is more stable until around age 60 and then tends to drop.
- ACTIVE-trial signal - [[accelerate-learning-increase-cognitive-capacity-dr-tommy-wood-scim4201272551]] says processing-speed training with one-year and three-year boosters was associated with lower dementia risk 20 years later in a secondary analysis.
- Program context - [[accelerate-learning-increase-cognitive-capacity-dr-tommy-wood-scim4201272551]] places BrainHQ, Maintain Your Brain, and POINTER in a broader risk-factor intervention context.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source keeps the signal cautious. Secondary analysis, platform-specific claims, and transfer beyond trained tasks should not be inflated into a claim that generic brain games prevent dementia.
