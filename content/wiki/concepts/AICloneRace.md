## Definition

An AI clone race is the pre-release competition created when public demonstrations of a creative work give technically capable imitators enough information to reproduce its visible mechanics, style, or premise before the original creator can ship.

## Current Synthesis

For independent games, promotion and protection now pull in opposite directions. Developers need demos, playtests, videos, wish lists, and crowdfunding to validate demand and finance completion, but those same signals can expose a compact design to fast AI-assisted imitation. The threatened return is not only revenue: an earlier clone can capture attention, search position, credit, and the encouragement that motivates creators to continue, encouraging secrecy that weakens collaborative culture.

## Key Claims

- Publicity can supply both demand generation and an imitation specification.
- AI compresses the interval between observing a concept and producing a playable approximation.
- Pre-release clones can capture attention before an original builds sales or reputation.
- Credit and recognition may matter as much as direct income for small creators.
- Defensive secrecy can reduce playtesting, feedback, fundraising, and open exchange.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode gives illustrative cases but no prevalence, sales-impact, legal-outcome, or platform-enforcement data. A playable prototype is not necessarily a commercially durable substitute, and similarity can range from lawful genre borrowing to misleading appropriation or infringement. The “dark forest” outcome is a risk mechanism, not proof that all creators will stop sharing.

## What Changed

- Added pre-release timing as a distinct form of AI-related creator risk.
- Expanded harm beyond revenue to attention, credit, and openness.
