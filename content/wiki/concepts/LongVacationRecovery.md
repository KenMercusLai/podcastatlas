# Long Vacation Recovery / 长假恢复

Long vacation recovery is the claim in [[ep265-dang-qing3xiu13-chongshang-resou-women-ruhe-gei-rensheng-fang-yige-changjia-lsloczlnkmcrrfpn9coh8rrqo-wb]] that rest often needs more than a short break. [[YanFeiSociologist|颜飞]] argues that two or three days may leave the body still tense, while a longer period such as the "请3休13" arrangement can allow people to begin loosening around the fourth or fifth day.

The concept is not a universal number of days. It names a recovery curve: the first stage may be decompression, message-checking, and still performing the role of vacationer; later the body may adapt to local rhythm, lower phone reliance, sleep differently, and regain direct perception.

## Key Claims
- Short breaks can be too compressed for people who enter them already tense.
- A longer holiday can let work rhythms decay gradually rather than requiring instant relaxation.
- Environment change helps because local pace, time zones, natural settings, and unfamiliar routines interrupt habit loops.
- Recovery begins before departure: overdrawn work in the week before vacation can reduce the value of the holiday.
- A long holiday is most effective when paired with [[WorkRestBoundary]] and [[TravelSensoryRecovery]].
