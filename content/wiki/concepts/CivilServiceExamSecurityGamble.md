# Civil-Service Exam Security Gamble / 考编安全豪赌

## Definition
Civil-service exam security gamble / 考编安全豪赌 is the episode's reframing of civil-service exam preparation as a high-cost bet on future job security rather than a simple preference for comfort.

## Current Synthesis
[[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] argues that "young people seek stability, so they take civil-service exams" is too flat. Full-time preparation can consume income, time, resume continuity, and alternative career options, while most candidates fail. What makes the gamble attractive is the promise of a form of safety that is perceived as hard to take back.

The concept belongs inside [[YouthPrecautionaryCulture]] and [[IndividualizedRiskResponsibility]]. It shows how people may accept high certain cost today to reduce future uncertainty when ordinary labor-market failure feels too punishing.

## Key Claims
- Civil-service exam preparation can be risk-taking, not merely risk avoidance.
- The gamble trades present certainty of cost for a hoped-for future certainty of security.
- Failed preparation can create resume gaps and new labor-market penalties.
- The target is often non-retractable safety more than high income.
- The pattern exposes how insecurity can push people into crowded and costly safety races.

## Evidence
- Risky preparation: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] says full-time exam preparation has large opportunity costs and many candidates fail.
- Resume penalty: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] notes that failure can leave a resume blank that is then punished in job search.
- Security target: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] argues that candidates seek security perceived as hard to withdraw, not necessarily high wages.
- Precautionary logic: [[episode-227-ershiyishiji-qingchun-wuyu-8211230332-278487]] summarizes the pattern as paying a high definite cost now to eliminate future uncertainty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is source-scoped and does not classify every exam taker. Some candidates may seek public service, family approval, location stability, status, or professional fit; the episode isolates one risk-security logic.
