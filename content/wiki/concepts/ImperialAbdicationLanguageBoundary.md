## Definition

禅让话语边界 / imperial abdication language boundary is the political limit around language that suggests a ruler may transfer the realm outside ordinary dynastic succession, especially when Yao-Shun abdication precedent is invoked around a favorite, office-holder, ominous sign, or unnamed worthy successor.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-401-suihong-zhiming-hanzhaodi-tuiwei-rangxian-huoguang-zaile-ll6pyzpjl0mlcfn0g1zjot2wf8s8|Hanji 401]] now gives the concept an early Han Zhao case. [[SuiHongWesternHan|眭弘]] reads standing stones, revived trees, white crows, and "公孙病已立" marks as signs that the throne should pass beyond the present ruler. He then invokes Yao's yielding to Shun and advises [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝]] to seek a worthy person, abdicate, and withdraw to a small fief. [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] treats the memorial as dangerous false omen speech and has Sui Hong and the forwarding friend executed.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-601-1-chule-yuhui-tade-wenhua-shuiping-ye-neng-gaiming-lnt4p2mo2nwfm3xqhg0dmfwiip1n|Hanji 601-1]] gives the concept a later Han Aidi case in two stages. First, [[XiaoXianWesternHan|萧贤]] treats "云止其中" in Dong-family appointment language as an abnormal Yao-Shun signal. Second, [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] says at a banquet that he intends to imitate Yao yielding to Shun with [[DongXian|董贤]], making explicit what Xiao Xian had inferred from wording.

[[WangHongWesternHan|王洪]] defines the boundary by objecting immediately. His claim is not only that Dong Xian lacks merit; it is that the realm belongs to Gaozu's dynastic line and cannot be spoken of as the emperor's private possession. The boundary therefore links succession, title language, public office, and ruler speech: even a drunken or playful statement can become politically dangerous when it names an alternative transfer of the realm.

## Key Claims

- Yao-Shun abdication precedent is politically explosive when applied to a ruler's favorite rather than to abstract moral antiquity.
- Omen-based abdication argument can be even more dangerous when it claims Heaven points beyond the current ruler without identifying a controllable successor.
- Succession language differs from ordinary praise because it suggests who may inherit or receive the realm.
- "The emperor cannot joke" is a governance claim: ruler speech can create precedent, expectation, fear, or opportunistic response.
- The boundary is dynastic as well as personal because Wang Hong grounds his objection in Gaozu's line rather than in Aidi's mood.
- Office and succession language can interact when a favorite already holds high office, military authority, and family reward.
- Classical literacy can either create the dangerous argument, as in Sui Hong's memorial, or detect and block it, as in Xiao Xian and Wang Hong's response.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 401 and Hanji 601-1. Hanji 401 preserves omen reports as political language rather than verified supernatural causality; Sui Hong's exact reasoning, the identity of "公孙," and the legal procedure remain source-scoped. Hanji 601-1 presents Aidi's remark as drunken and politically dangerous, but it does not prove a completed transfer plan. Dong Xian's own intended response is unknowable there because Wang Hong interrupts before he can answer.
