# Actor Likeness Authorization / 演员脸部授权

## Definition
Actor likeness authorization / 演员脸部授权 is the commercial permission to use an actor's face, body, or identity signal in AI-generated performance without necessarily transferring the actor's own artistic authorship to the generated output.

## Current Synthesis
The episode separates two questions that are often collapsed. One question is whether an actor can license a face or persona for commercial use, such as platform-generated clips or additional livestreams. The other is whether a third party controlling that face with prompts, motion, or scripts is still producing the actor's own performance.

The current judgment is conditional: authorization can be commercially reasonable when it creates clear value and compensation, but it does not automatically solve authorship, consent scope, credit, labor replacement, or audience expectation. The actor's face can travel farther than the actor's intentional expression.

## Key Claims
- A licensed face is not the same as a self-authored performance.
- Commercial authorization may still be rational when it creates value for the actor, platform, and audience.
- Consent scope matters because a third party may control script, movement, tone, and usage context.
- Likeness authorization sits between IP rights, labor protection, and creative authorship.
- Audience trust depends on whether viewers know who controlled the performance and how it was generated.

- Commercial qualification - [[no-232-wuhankun-ai-shidai-yanyuan-diyige-diufanwan-gkwriw4oa148aw4mzwtp-xj]] notes Wu's concession that actors need income and that practical arrangements depend on the case.
- Livestream example - [[no-232-wuhankun-ai-shidai-yanyuan-diyige-diufanwan-gkwriw4oa148aw4mzwtp-xj]] uses authorized streamer faces to open more livestreams as a potentially value-creating scenario.
- Industry boundary - [[no-232-wuhankun-ai-shidai-yanyuan-diyige-diufanwan-gkwriw4oa148aw4mzwtp-xj]] distinguishes technical feasibility from whether the industry should allow substitution of workers in particular roles.
- Audience expectation - [[no-232-wuhankun-ai-shidai-yanyuan-diyige-diufanwan-gkwriw4oa148aw4mzwtp-xj]] connects AI performers to the problem of whether viewers believe a human subject is behind what they see.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a legal framework, contract template, union position, or platform policy. It defines a creator-side ethical and authorship boundary rather than a settled rights regime.
