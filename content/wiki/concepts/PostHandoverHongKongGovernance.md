# Post-Handover Hong Kong Governance / 回歸後香港管治

## Definition
Post-handover Hong Kong governance is the source's frame for how [[HongKong|Hong Kong]] was governed after 1997 through executive leadership, Beijing selection, business-elite participation, limited electoral legitimacy, and policy crisis management.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses [[TungCheeHwa|董建華]]'s tenure to argue that post-handover governance cannot be reduced to one weak leader. Tung's inexperience mattered, but the more durable issue was an institutional design that expected executive-led administration and business-class consultation to substitute for broad democratic authorization.

This frame links economic crisis, land and housing policy, public-resource privatization, mainland integration, legal interpretation, and national-security legislation into one early post-1997 governance pattern.

## Key Claims
- Tung-era crisis exposed both personal administrative weakness and structural limits in the chief-executive system.
- Executive-led governance lacked universal-suffrage legitimacy when major policy shocks arrived.
- Business-elite governance made consultation and elite brokerage central but could not absorb mass public distrust.
- Legal, economic, and mainland-integration choices from the early handover years shaped later Hong Kong tensions.

## Evidence
- Structural diagnosis: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] explicitly asks whether Tung-era crisis was personal failure or institutional defect, then emphasizes both.
- Legitimacy stress: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] links executive-led design, lack of universal suffrage, and business-class consultation to weak recognition.
- Policy legacy: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] groups right-of-abode interpretation, CEPA, individual visits, land policy, MTR listing, and Link REIT privatization as lasting Tung-era legacies.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short news explainer, not a complete constitutional or policy history. It presents Tung's era as an origin point for later tensions, but it does not fully compare later administrations or alternative explanations such as global economic cycles, colonial institutional inheritance, or local party-system constraints.
