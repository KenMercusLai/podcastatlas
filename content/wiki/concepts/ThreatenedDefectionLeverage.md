# Threatened Defection Leverage / 投敌威胁式求援

Threatened defection leverage / 投敌威胁式求援 is the weak-state diplomatic move [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-83-zhuhou-bailan-jing-chengle-jingdian-ji-zhong-ji-ljp7nbqnrctr5mw6i9u982abmx-1|Zhouji 83]] extracts from [[HanState|韩国]]'s appeal to [[QinState|秦国]] during [[HuayangBattle|华阳之战]]. Han is under attack from [[WeiState|魏国]] and [[ZhaoState|赵国]], but its envoy tells [[WeiRan|魏冉]] that the crisis is not yet desperate; if it were, Han would surrender to the attackers instead of asking Qin for help.

The point is not sincerity but incentive design. By making non-rescue mean that Qin's rivals may absorb or control Han, the envoy turns Han's survival into Qin's own strategic problem. The episode calls this an open-faced tactic: the threat is visible, but the target cannot easily ignore it without accepting a worse balance of power.

This concept belongs beside [[WarringStatesAllianceSwitching|战国同盟转向]] and [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] because it shows the small-state version of alliance politics. Instead of building a durable camp, Han uses the possibility of switching sides to force a larger state to act now.

## Key Claims
- A weak state can create leverage by making abandonment costly to the would-be rescuer.
- The threat works best when surrender would strengthen the rescuer's rival, not merely harm the weak state.
- The tactic is an "阳谋" in the episode's sense: the logic is overt, but refusal becomes hard once the strategic cost is recognized.
- It does not create loyalty; it buys intervention by aligning survival with the larger power's self-interest.
