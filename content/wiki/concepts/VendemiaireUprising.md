# Vendémiaire Uprising

## Definition

The Vendémiaire uprising was the October 1795 royalist challenge in Paris that Napoleon suppressed with troops and grapeshot, securing the Directory's immediate survival and his own political ascent.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents Vendémiaire as a transfer point between revolutionary crowd politics and concentrated state force. After General Menou hesitated, [[PaulBarras|Barras]] selected [[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon]], who positioned artillery around government sites and fired grapeshot against a larger insurgent force. The result defended the constitutional regime while demonstrating that restoring order could require lethal coercion and could rapidly elevate the commander who delivered it.

## Key Claims

- Constitutional uncertainty and fear of royalist revival produced an armed Parisian challenge in October 1795.
- Menou's hesitation exposed the regime's need for a commander willing to use decisive force in dense urban space.
- Napoleon prepared artillery around strategic government sites before the decisive confrontation.
- Grapeshot dispersed the insurgents at severe but uncertain human cost.
- The victory ended the immediate uprising and strengthened the state's control of organized violence.
- Appointment as commander of the Army of the Interior let Napoleon turn coercive success into office, celebrity, family patronage, and political power.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode's contrast between a “mob” and restored state order reflects its interpretive framing and should not erase the insurgents' political composition or the regime's contested legitimacy. Casualty estimates range widely, reported dialogue differs between Napoleon and Barras, and the exact sequence of preparations remains source-scoped.
