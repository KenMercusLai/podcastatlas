# Round Table Fellowship

## Definition

Round Table fellowship is the Arthurian image of knightly equality in which a circular table removes head and foot positions, symbolically making warriors peers without abolishing monarchy or real aristocratic hierarchy.

## Current Synthesis

[[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] attributes the Round Table's earliest appearance to the mid-twelfth-century poet Wace. Its geometry supplies a compact political fiction: every seated knight occupies an equivalent place, so fellowship can coexist with service to [[KingArthur|Arthur]].

The image answers a social tension described by [[MountedKnightSocialFormation]]. Heavy cavalry benefited from cohesion, and upwardly mobile knights wanted recognition as peers, but medieval courts remained intensely ranked. The Round Table does not document actual equality; it imagines enough equality to organize brotherhood. [[Lancelot]]'s affair, the deaths of Gareth and Gaheris, and Gawain's vengeance then show how personal loyalties can destroy that ideal from within.

## Key Claims

- Circular seating symbolizes the removal of privileged head and foot positions.
- The fellowship makes different knights imaginatively equal while keeping Arthur as king.
- Its appeal grows from tension between military solidarity and aristocratic precedence.
- The image offers status to lesser knights without describing a democratic institution.
- Dubbing, loyalty, romance, and shared adventure give the fellowship personal bonds as well as geometry.
- Camelot's tragedy depends on the gap between the fellowship's ideal and divided loyalties in practice.

## Evidence

### Literary symbol

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] places the Round Table in Wace's mid-twelfth-century adaptation and explains its circle as a sign of equivalent positions.

### Social function

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] connects knightly brotherhood to rulers' need for cohesive heavy cavalry and lesser warriors' desire for aristocratic standing.

### Internal collapse

- [[714-king-arthur-lancelot-and-the-round-table-part-2-glt6459272632]] follows Lancelot's divided loyalty, Gawain's vengeance, Mordred's seizure, and Arthur's final defeat as the destruction of fellowship through its own relationships.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Wace's authorship of the motif and the hosts' functional explanation remain source-scoped. Symbolic equality at a fictional table should not be generalized into legal, economic, gender, or political equality in medieval society. The concept identifies an ideal and its narrative work, not a verified institutional blueprint.

## What Changed

- Established the Round Table as a fictional mediation between knightly solidarity and persistent hierarchy.
