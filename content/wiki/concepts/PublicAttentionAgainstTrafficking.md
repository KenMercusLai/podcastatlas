# 反拐公共关注 / Public Attention Against Trafficking

## Definition
Public attention against trafficking is the civic posture of refusing to treat trafficking as invisible, distant, or already solved, especially when victims are hidden inside families, villages, labor arrangements, or coercive marriages.

## Current Synthesis
The episode ends by translating outrage into attention: thinking and speaking are presented as the beginning of being "not blind." This does not replace law or rescue work; it names a public precondition. Trafficking persists partly through social blindness, local normalization, and the distance between urban audiences and hidden coercive worlds, so sustained attention becomes part of the anti-trafficking response.

## Key Claims
- Trafficking remains modern, not only ancient or cinematic.
- Public speech matters because silence makes hidden coercion easier to normalize.
- The "blind mountain" metaphor names a failure to imagine victims' confinement from outside.
- Attention should include both victim sympathy and buyer-side accountability.
- The concept is civic and interpretive, not a substitute for legal enforcement or survivor services.
