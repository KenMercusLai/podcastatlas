# Synthetic Spud Cells

Synthetic spud cells are the lab-built cell-like systems described by [[OliverMorton]] in [[its-not-easy-being-green-zack-polanski-6a82d1a0ff328abd84724cf0]]. The source says [[KateAdamalla]]'s [[UniversityOfMinnesota]] lab created them from chemicals, without biological ancestors, and that the line can reproduce for a limited number of generations.

The episode treats them as an engineering milestone rather than a fully life-like organism. They use membrane bits containing internal material, seven small DNA molecules acting as chromosomes, and feeder blobs that help grow membranes until gene activity causes division. But they reproduce imperfectly and have almost no metabolism, so they sit inside the [[LifeDefinitionSpectrum]] rather than at the ordinary-cell end.

## Key Claims
- Bottom-up synthetic cells can show which cell behaviours can be engineered from known parts.
- Limited self-reproduction is a major step even when the system lacks robust metabolism and natural-cell complexity.
- The value is mostly scientific and engineering insight for now, not immediate commercial or medical use.
- Knowing each component's role can make synthetic cells useful research tools for understanding life from the builder's side.
