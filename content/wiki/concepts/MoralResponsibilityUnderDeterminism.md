# Moral Responsibility Under Determinism / 决定论下的道德责任

## Definition
Moral responsibility under determinism asks how accountability, prevention, praise, blame, punishment, and social protection should work when behavior arises from causes the actor did not independently choose.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] uses [[RobertSapolsky|Robert Sapolsky]] to frame crime, addiction, temperament, and opportunity through genes, family, education, poverty, nutrition, inequality, and other background conditions. It does not conclude that responsibility disappears; it shifts emphasis from isolated bad will toward prevention, repair, and context-aware accountability.

In [[scim5791514102-scim5791514102]], Sapolsky states the stronger claim directly and asks how society could live without notions of deserved blame, punishment, praise, or reward. His historical analogies suggest that removing agency-based blame from misunderstood phenomena can make institutions more humane. The combined synthesis remains narrower than abolishing every consequence: causal explanation can reduce retribution while preserving protection, behavior shaping, repair, and distinctions among intentions and capacities.

## Key Claims
- Explaining causes is not the same as excusing harm.
- A justice system built only on bad-will assumptions misses background conditions that can be changed.
- Social supports such as education, nutrition, and poverty reduction can be responsibility infrastructure, not only welfare.
- Political disagreements over responsibility often hide different assumptions about how free a person is under unequal starting conditions.
- Reducing deserved-blame language can support more humane prevention and management without requiring indifference to victims or future risk.
- Praise, punishment, and reward can be evaluated as causal tools rather than metaphysical proof that a person authored themselves.

## Evidence
- Social causation - [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]] applies constrained agency to crime, poverty, family background, education, addiction, and political disagreement.
- Humane implication - [[scim5791514102-scim5791514102]] argues that abandoning blame for previously moralized phenomena has historically enabled more humane responses.
- Change remains possible - [[scim5791514102-scim5791514102]] treats information, learning, and circumstance as mechanisms that alter organisms rather than exceptions to determinism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source supplies a complete legal or institutional design. Rejecting ultimate desert does not determine sentencing, risk management, restitution, rehabilitation, or how to weight intention, negligence, coercion, and incapacity. Historical analogies can motivate reduced blame without proving that all praise, punishment, or reward is ineffective or unjust.

## What Changed
- Added Sapolsky's direct distinction between rejecting deserved blame and accepting fatalism.
- Clarified that consequences can remain as protective or behavior-shaping tools even when retributive desert is weakened.
