# Moral Responsibility Under Determinism / 决定论下的道德责任

Moral responsibility under determinism is the EP256 problem of what justice, blame, and prevention mean if behavior is strongly shaped by causes outside the individual. In [[ep256-ai-shidai-ziyou-yizhi-hai-cunzai-ma-lk9aci8oqnwerk26jy683nbdddcx]], [[TuMotuo|土摩托]] uses [[RobertSapolsky|Robert Sapolsky]] to argue that crime and harmful behavior should be read through birth, genes, family, education, poverty, social inequality, nutrition, and other conditions.

The source does not conclude that responsibility disappears. It shifts the emphasis from pure punishment toward prevention, repair, and context-aware accountability. The important move is that [[CausalDeterminism]] can make punishment less vindictive without making society indifferent to harm.

## Key Claims
- Explaining causes is not the same as excusing harm.
- A justice system built only on bad-will assumptions misses background conditions that can be changed.
- Social supports such as education, nutrition, and poverty reduction can be responsibility infrastructure, not only welfare.
- Political disagreements over responsibility often hide different assumptions about how free a person is under unequal starting conditions.
