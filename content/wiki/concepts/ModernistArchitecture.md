# Modernist Architecture / 现代主义建筑

## Definition
Modernist architecture is the episode's broad frame for architecture that moves away from heavy historical ornament toward simpler forms, new materials, new building technologies, and new living arrangements.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses modernism in two linked ways: as a European theoretical movement associated with [[LeCorbusier|Le Corbusier]] and collective housing, and as a Shanghai practice adapted by [[LaiAnArchitect|Lai An]], Chinese architects, and later Bauhaus-influenced builders. In Shanghai, modernism appears through apartments, villas, cinemas, interiors, factories, and public buildings, but its simplicity can also make it vulnerable to underrecognition.

## Key Claims
- Modernism is linked to industrial materials, construction technology, elevators, and simplified form.
- Lai An's Shanghai path differs from Le Corbusier's collective-housing model but remains modernist in style and spirit.
- Shanghai's modernism is hybrid, mixing Art Deco, New Art traces, neoclassical constraints, and local residential requirements.
- Some Chinese architects returned to Shanghai specifically to pursue modernist design ambitions.
- Modernist buildings may be easier to demolish politically or culturally when the public reads them as plain rather than historic.

## Evidence
- European evidence: [[498-xunzhao-laian-wei-jindai-shanghai-wutongqu-chuangzao-fengmao-de-faguo-jianzhushi-lskf8bdkhs71lbwjomt3gu1ko8be]] discusses Le Corbusier's apartment model and Lai An's participation in an international modernist architecture group.
- Shanghai evidence: [[498-xunzhao-laian-wei-jindai-shanghai-wutongqu-chuangzao-fengmao-de-faguo-jianzhushi-lskf8bdkhs71lbwjomt3gu1ko8be]] says Lai An brought modernism and Art Deco into Shanghai through apartments, villas, and public buildings.
- Chinese-architect evidence: [[498-xunzhao-laian-wei-jindai-shanghai-wutongqu-chuangzao-fengmao-de-faguo-jianzhushi-lskf8bdkhs71lbwjomt3gu1ko8be]] describes returned architects such as Zhuang Jun, Dong Dayou, Li Jingpei, Fan Wenzhao, Lu Qianshou, and Xi Fuquan working in public, commercial, and factory contexts.
- Preservation evidence: [[498-xunzhao-laian-wei-jindai-shanghai-wutongqu-chuangzao-fengmao-de-faguo-jianzhushi-lskf8bdkhs71lbwjomt3gu1ko8be]] links modernism's simple appearance to the risk that later observers fail to recognize its heritage value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode's modernism category is broad and sometimes overlaps with Art Deco, Bauhaus, New Art, and neoclassical transitional forms. Individual buildings need more precise stylistic classification than the interview format can provide.
