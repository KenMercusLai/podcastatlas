# 朝廷名义高于主君私属 / Court Legitimacy Over Patron Loyalty

## Definition
Court legitimacy over patron loyalty is the late-Han loyalty conflict where a recognized imperial court, even when controlled by a stronger warlord, can claim higher duty than a regional patron's personal authority.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1037 makes the problem concrete through [[HanSongLateHan|韩嵩]]. Liu Biao sends him to [[Xudu|许都]], but the trip places Han Song inside the court's appointment system. Once Han Song accepts court office and returns urging Liu Biao to send a son to court, Liu Biao reads formal loyalty as personal betrayal. The concept captures that collision: a regional ruler may want the court's prestige without accepting that court service can reorder the envoy's obligations.

## Key Claims
- A weak but recognized court can still define official duty when local rulers remain formally inside the old order.
- Envoy missions are risky because contact with the court can convert a patron's representative into a court officer.
- "守节" in this source means preserving role-duty, not simply choosing the strongest side.
- A son-at-court proposal makes symbolic submission practical by turning loyalty language into hostage-like dependency.
- Patron suspicion intensifies when formal court loyalty threatens local autonomy and elite opinion.

## Evidence
- Envoy-to-court-official conversion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]] says Han Song receives appointments after reaching Xudu and returns praising the court and Cao Cao.
- Role-duty claim: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]] uses "圣达节，次守节" to explain why Han Song presents himself as keeping duty rather than betraying Liu Biao.
- Submission signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]] treats sending Liu Biao's son to court as a visible act of allegiance and constraint.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The court in this branch is not a neutral institution free of Cao Cao's power. The concept therefore does not say Han Song's position is politically innocent; it says that the continuing legitimacy of [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] gives his choice a public-duty vocabulary beyond personal defection.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Hanji 1037's Han Song-Liu Biao conflict.
