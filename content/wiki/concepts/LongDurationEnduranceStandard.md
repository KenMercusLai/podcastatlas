# Long-Duration Endurance Standard / 长时间耐力标准

## Definition
The long-duration endurance standard is the episode's test of sustaining consistent submaximal work output for more than 20 minutes with no breaks, no intervals, and no downtime, ideally at a non-walking pace and with nasal breathing only.

## Current Synthesis
This is deliberately the least standardized of the nine tests, so that it stays accessible rather than prescriptive. Galpin describes 20 to 60 minutes as typical and 30 straight minutes as a good practical marker, and he suggests picking something that fits the person's lifestyle, such as a loop around their house, then simply testing whether they can keep working without stopping.

Walking does not really count unless the person is very unfit, in which case walking 30 minutes without a break being a challenge is itself the finding worth acting on. Because the test depends on local conditions rather than equipment or a formula, its value comes from being repeated in the same form during [[FitnessAssessmentTestingWeek|testing weeks]].

The endurance-training episode explains why continuous work can be worth training beyond the test itself: repeated efforts are described as improving movement and fuel-use efficiency, muscle capillary beds, and mitochondrial abundance. It also uses a broader duration boundary—about 12 minutes through several hours—so that figure is treated as a training category rather than a replacement for the assessment source's 20-to-60-minute test.

## Key Claims
- The test is continuous submaximal work with no breaks, no intervals, and no downtime for more than 20 minutes.
- Twenty to 60 minutes is the typical range, and 30 straight minutes is offered as a good practical marker.
- Ideally the work is at a non-walking pace and with nasal breathing only.
- Walking does not count unless the person is very unfit, in which case a 30-minute unbroken walk is itself meaningful information.
- The test is intentionally unstructured so that it stays available to anyone, and it is defined by repeatability rather than by a standard protocol.
- Continuous long-duration training is proposed to improve movement efficiency, fuel use, muscle capillary supply, and mitochondrial capacity.

- Form guidance: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] says the work should ideally be at a non-walking pace and with nasal breathing only.
- Walking boundary: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] says walking does not really count unless the person is very unfit, in which case a 30-minute unbroken walk being a challenge is itself the finding.
- Open choice: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] suggests picking something that fits the person's lifestyle, such as a loop around their house, and simply testing whether they can keep working without stopping.
- Least standardized: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] describes this as the least standardized of the nine tests, deliberately, so that the assessment stays accessible.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Because the standard is unstructured, absolute results are not comparable between people or between different activities, and neither source defines an intensity, heart-rate zone, or pace that would isolate a specific physiological system. The 30-minute marker is a practical screen rather than a universal benchmark; the training episode's broader duration range is likewise a category, not a prescription. Both should be adapted for people with joint, cardiovascular, metabolic, or fatigue-limiting conditions.

## What Changed
- Added the no-break steady-state standard as the episode's long-duration endurance test and its most open-ended assessment item.
- Added the continuous-training rationale and separated its broader duration category from the assessment standard.
