## Definition

Credit-based bandit surrender is a local-pacification pattern in which an official makes surrender safer than continued banditry by keeping promises of leniency, releasing surrendered or captured actors, and letting their survival become public proof that the state can be trusted.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 709 defines the pattern through Guo Ji's Yingchuan handling. When bandits such as Zhao Hong and Zhao Wu are brought in, Guo Ji does not simply maximize punishment. He speaks with them, releases them to return to farming, and then self-impeaches for going beyond ordinary punitive expectations. Liu Xiu does not punish him, which keeps Guo Ji's promise from becoming a trap.

The key mechanism is credit. Released men carry news that surrender is not automatically death, and later bandits from multiple regions reportedly come in because they believe Guo Ji. This is different from pure amnesty, because the policy depends on one official's demonstrated reliability; it is also different from mutual-reporting pacification, because the engine is trust rather than peer surveillance.

## Key Claims

- Surrender can become rational for bandits when the state proves that it will keep leniency promises.
- Released returnees are political evidence; their survival advertises that submission is safer than flight.
- Official self-impeachment strengthens credibility because it shows Guo Ji understands that his leniency exceeds normal punitive expectations.
- The ruler's non-punishment matters because local discretion collapses if the center later reverses the promise.
- Trust-based surrender differs from fear-based pacification by changing the expected outcome after submission.
- The pattern still depends on public authority and reputation, not on private kindness alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently a single-source local-governance pattern. The source does not prove that leniency works in every bandit context, and it does not erase the coercive background of state authority. The transcript geography and personal names are kept source-scoped where uncertain. The core claim is narrower: credible official promise-keeping can convert some anti-bandit work from pursuit and punishment into surrender and reintegration.

## What Changed

- Created this concept from Hanji 709 to capture Guo Ji's trust-based bandit-pacification mechanism.
- Distinguished the pattern from amnesty timing, mutual reporting, and root-cause pacification.
