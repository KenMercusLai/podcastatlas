# Digital Game Distribution

Digital game distribution is the sale and delivery of games through platform-controlled stores instead of physical retail channels. In [[jiu-shidai-diantai-28-shiti-youxi-de-shidai-zhongjie-zhiji-buru-chongxin-dingyi-yongyou-998430652]], it is the economic alternative to discs, cartridges, warehouses, retailers, and used-game circulation.

The episode treats digital distribution as structurally attractive for platform holders and publishers: marginal delivery is cheaper, inventory risk is lower, leaks are easier to control, and revenue flows through the store. The tradeoff is that players often receive a license and platform account entry rather than a transferable object.

[[ep242-duli-youxi-shi-yitiao-gaoqian-hao-saidao-ma-lrqbzrceumbuggzuero5qb4jv3zu]] adds the creator-side version through [[Steam]]. Digital distribution lowers listing and international-sale friction for [[IndieGameCommercialization|independent games]], but the source shows that platform access still leaves creators exposed to storefront competition, revenue splits, taxes, currency costs, publisher terms, and the need to fund the next game.

## Key Claims
- Digital stores reduce physical production, logistics, retail, and forecasting complexity.
- Platform holders gain more pricing, storefront, account, and access control.
- Digital distribution can be user-friendly when service quality is high, as the episode argues through [[Steam]].
- Digital distribution can be creator-friendly at the access layer while still harsh at the income layer.
- Digital distribution can also intensify [[DigitalGameOwnershipAnxiety]] when purchases depend on licenses, stores, servers, and account policies.
- DRM-free distribution, represented by [[GOG]] in the episode, is a more ownership-friendly digital variant but not the expected console mainstream.
