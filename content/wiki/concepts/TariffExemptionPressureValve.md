# Tariff Exemption Pressure Valve

Tariff exemption pressure valve is [[vol-128-guanshui-zhan-xiayibu-zouxiang-hefang-meiguo-renmin-ruhe-kandai-telangpu-bairi-xinzheng-kuangxi-bokejie-duihua-zhongshu-talich-ltivoqhue1t-q2ejjlzlenormkhb]]'s implicit mechanism for reconciling high headline tariffs with the need to avoid visible consumer pain. [[Talich]] argues that if high tariffs persist without exemptions, categories such as toys, strollers, car seats, and holiday goods can run into practical shortages or price spikes; he expects many exemptions if political pressure rises.

The concept matters because it separates announced tariff pressure from realized tariff burden. A government can keep a tough bargaining posture while carving out products, firms, or routes that reduce the shock. That makes [[EffectiveTariffRateShock]] a moving target and increases [[TariffPolicyPlanningRisk]] for businesses trying to decide whether to order, reroute, or wait.

## Key Claims
- Exemptions can preserve a high-pressure political story while lowering the actual burden on sensitive goods.
- The more exemptions are expected, the harder it is for firms to plan inventory, pricing, and supplier strategy.
- Exemptions can coexist with gray routing chains, so the policy result may be messier trade intermediation rather than clean reshoring.
- A pressure valve does not eliminate price pass-through; it decides where and when the pass-through becomes visible.
