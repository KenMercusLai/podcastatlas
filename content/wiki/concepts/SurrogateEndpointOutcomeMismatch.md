# Surrogate Endpoint–Outcome Mismatch

## Definition

Surrogate endpoint–outcome mismatch occurs when an intervention changes a biomarker or intermediate measure in the expected direction without producing the predicted improvement in survival, disease, function, or quality of life.

## Current Synthesis

The [[MinnesotaCoronaryExperiment]] supplies the core case: replacing saturated animal fat with vegetable oil lowered serum cholesterol, yet the trial did not demonstrate lower coronary or all-cause mortality. The episode therefore separates evidence that an intervention reaches an intermediate target from evidence that the target change improves the outcome people ultimately care about.

Mismatch does not prove that the surrogate is useless or that changing it is generally harmful. It can reflect offsetting mechanisms, intervention-specific harms, population differences, inadequate exposure, insufficient power, adherence, missing data, subgroup selection, or a surrogate that captures only one part of the causal pathway. Interpretation must remain tied to the exact intervention, comparator, population, duration, and clinical outcome.

## Key Claims

- A biomarker response demonstrates target engagement, not automatically clinical benefit.
- Clinical outcomes can diverge when an intervention has effects not represented by the surrogate.
- The intervention used to change a marker matters; different routes to the same marker can have different net effects.
- Mortality, disease events, function, and quality of life should not be replaced by a favorable intermediate result without validation.
- Null or adverse clinical outcomes require attention even when the biological mechanism and surrogate response appear favorable.
- Subgroup findings can generate concern without automatically overriding the full randomized comparison.

### Parallel recovered trial

- [[the-basement-tapes-the-basement-tapes]] reports a similar conclusion from the reanalyzed [[SydneyDietHeartStudy]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied source is a narrative summary, not the primary trials or complete later literature. Its subgroup, historical intervention, institutional population, adherence, attrition, and mechanistic limitations prevent a general conclusion that lower cholesterol is harmful or that every unsaturated-fat replacement worsens outcomes. A validated surrogate can still be useful, especially when clinical endpoints are rare or slow, but its validity is claim-, intervention-, and population-specific.

## What Changed

- Created a clinical-trial concept separating target engagement from patient-relevant benefit.
