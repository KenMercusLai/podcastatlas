# Adaptation Ending Ethics / 改编结局伦理

## Definition

Adaptation ending ethics asks how a changed ending redistributes judgment, suffering, agency, recognition, and hope—and whether the adaptation has rebuilt enough character and causality to earn that new outcome.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded cases reject a simple hierarchy in which fidelity, tragedy, survival, or happiness is automatically superior. In [[FarewellMyConcubine|《霸王别姬》]], the film lets [[ChengDieyi|程蝶衣]] die at a performance high point after [[DuanXiaolou|段小楼]] finally calls him “小豆子.” The source episode reads this as romantic tragic completion, while the novel's later Hong Kong encounter leaves both men alive and makes belated recognition psychologically crueler.

[[QingShenShenYuMengMeng|《情深深雨濛濛》]] moves in the opposite direction from [[YanYuMengMeng|《烟雨濛濛》]]. The television adaptation gives Rǔping a more active, educated, wartime-tested character and a path beyond romantic collapse. Its brighter outcome is judged plausible because the person reaching it is no longer constructed like the novel's withdrawn Rǔping. Changed endings therefore need to be assessed with changed characterization, not against tone alone.

## Key Claims

- An ending can shift a story's moral and emotional center even when much of the plot remains recognizable.
- Death can create romantic completion while survival can preserve harsher recognition, compromise, or humiliation.
- A happier ending is earned only when the adaptation constructs capacities and choices that can plausibly reach it.
- Fidelity debates should ask what ethical and emotional work the new ending performs, not only whether events match.
- Tragedy and comedy have no automatic rank outside the causal and character structure that supports them.

## Evidence

### Tragic completion versus cruel survival

- [[64-bawangbieji-fengmo-yu-chenghuo-656094350]] contrasts Dieyi's self-killing performance and final recognition in the film with the novel's later-life encounter, where survival prolongs psychological injury.

### Rebuilt agency and survivable outcome

- [[042-jinian-qiongyao-liangdairen-de-jiyi-du-qingshenshen-yumengmeng-yuanzhu-yanyumengmeng-liufoc8w7fhdkv8gfsj7uuggjy3t]] contrasts the novel Rǔping's passivity and death with the television Rǔping's education, initiative, wartime action, and continued life.

### Character causality as the test

- [[042-jinian-qiongyao-liangdairen-de-jiyi-du-qingshenshen-yumengmeng-yuanzhu-yanyumengmeng-liufoc8w7fhdkv8gfsj7uuggjy3t]] argues that the adaptation's brighter ending works because it follows a revised growth trajectory rather than being attached to an unchanged character.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Both cases come from podcast interpretation rather than production archives or systematic comparison of every textual and screen version. A beautiful death can romanticize self-destruction, while survival can be sentimentalized or stripped of consequence. Conversely, changing characterization does not by itself justify every altered ending. The framework evaluates narrative and moral effects; it does not prescribe real-world choices about life, death, trauma, or recovery.
