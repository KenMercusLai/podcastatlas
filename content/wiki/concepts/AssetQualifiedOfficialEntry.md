## Definition

Asset-qualified official entry / 赀选式财产资格入仕 is an appointment route in which a minimum level of household property establishes eligibility and helps absorb the private costs of service, while identity, conduct, and ability still determine who can enter or be used.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-245-hanjingdi-shiqi-dangguan-weishenme-yao-daotieqian-lpyc0otmtfn9520s3cgx9pt9ng1j|Hanji 245]] presents a late-[[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] order under the transcript form “资算四得官.” The episode interprets this as allowing households assessed at forty thousand cash to participate in selection because officeholding required substantial expenditure that an ordinary family could not sustain.

The route is wealth-gated but not identical to [[OfficeSaleLegitimacyDamage|office sale]]. Property qualifies the candidate and finances service; it does not directly purchase a specified office in the episode's account. Registered merchants, dependent sons-in-law, and officials associated with corruption remain excluded, while [[SimaXiangru|司马相如]] and 张释之 are used to argue that ability still matters after the asset gate is crossed.

The institutional tradeoff is therefore two-sided. Requiring a private financial buffer may reduce the immediate risk that service costs force an officeholder into extraction, but it also narrows access toward wealthy and already privileged households. The source identifies that tension without showing actual appointment rates, administrative performance, or whether the rule reduced corruption.

## Key Claims

- A property threshold can function as eligibility and financial-capacity screening rather than as the sale price of an office.
- Private officeholding costs make household wealth part of who can realistically enter public service.
- Wealth alone does not erase separate screens for status, prior conduct, and ability.
- A financial-buffer rationale can coexist with severe exclusion of ordinary households from political opportunity.
- The route belongs within a wider selection system in which family position, nobility, reputation, recommendation, and talent also shape access.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one popular-history episode rather than a reconstructed history of Han selection law. The transcript's “资算四得官” may not preserve the historical wording or graph accurately, and the source does not specify the property assessment procedure, offices covered, appointment success rate, private expense categories, enforcement of exclusions, or relationship to recommendation and later 察举. The proposed anti-corruption benefit is an institutional inference, not an outcome demonstrated by the episode. The Sima Xiangru and 张释之 examples show the host's interpretation of talent screening, not a representative sample of entrants.

## What Changed

- Initial concept created to distinguish wealth-qualified eligibility from direct office purchase while preserving the route's class exclusion and additional screening rules.
