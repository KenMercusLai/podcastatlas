# Receivables Risk

Receivables risk is the possibility that booked sales will not convert into cash. [[ep86-mianzi-dizi-rizi-caibao-zhi-jiang-zhe-san-jian-shi-lukmnlkkuxvrvv12frosd9teg7bj]] uses the [[SichuanChanghong]] and [[ApexDigital]] case to show how a company can report revenue growth while accounts receivable grow even faster, eventually forcing bad-debt provisions and losses.

[[no-199-zixingche-200-nian-973489465]] adds a platform-order version through [[Ofo|OFO]] and [[PhoenixBicycle]]. The source says shared-bike financing stress and under-fulfilled purchasing plans pushed risk upstream, turning apparently large manufacturing demand into collection and lawsuit exposure.

## Key Claims
- Accounts receivable growth above revenue growth can signal loose credit, channel stuffing, customer stress, or weak collection.
- Strong reported income is less convincing when operating cash flow deteriorates at the same time.
- A receivable is an asset only if the customer actually pays; otherwise it can become a future expense through provisions or write-offs.
- Receivables risk turns the income statement, balance sheet, and cash-flow statement into one connected question.
- Platform customers can create receivables risk when supplier orders depend on continuing venture financing rather than operating cash generation.
