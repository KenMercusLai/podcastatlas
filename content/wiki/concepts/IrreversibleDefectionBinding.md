## Definition

Irreversible defection binding / 断路式策反绑定 is the persuasion-and-commitment pattern in which a wavering actor is pushed from private inclination or ambiguous affiliation into a public act that makes return to the old camp politically or militarily impossible.

## Current Synthesis

The strongest current form is the externally engineered defection. [[SuiHe|随何]] first uses [[ChuaimoTechnique|揣摩]] and [[InterestReframingPersuasion|利益重构式说服]] to get [[YingBu|英布]] to admit that apparent loyalty to [[XiangYu|项羽]] is hollow and that siding with [[LiuBang|刘邦]] is safer over time. A private promise is not enough because Ying Bu still fears Chu.

The decisive move is public exposure. When Xiang Yu's envoy arrives to demand troops, Sui He sits above the envoy and announces that Jiujiang has already submitted to Han. He then urges Ying Bu to kill the envoy, crossing the [[EnvoyKillingTaboo|不斩来使]] boundary so Xiang Yu will treat Ying Bu as an enemy rather than a wavering subordinate. The tactic succeeds because it forces the target to act consistently with the new camp after the persuader leaves.

A lower-intensity Eastern Han form appears when the defector binds himself. [[LiangTongEasternHan|梁统]] fears that hesitation will leave his alignment unclear while [[WeiXiaoEasternHan|隗嚣]]'s camp is being split. He kills Wei Xiao's envoy Zhang Xue, severs the relationship, and discards Wei Xiao's general seal and ribbon. Unlike the Sui He case, this source does not show a persuader forcing the action in the room; the binding effect comes from Liang Tong's own public no-return signal.

Hanji 1066 adds a non-killing frontier variant. [[QianZhaoLateHan|牵招]] does not force [[Wuhuan|乌桓]] to murder [[HanZhongLiaodongLateHan|韩中]], but he makes the Wuhuan meeting a public no-hedging scene. By overpowering the Liaodong envoy, denying [[GongsunKangLateHan|公孙康]]'s chanyu-seal authority, and then explaining the costs of wrong alignment, Qian Zhao turns Wuhuan's private uncertainty into an open dismissal of Liaodong and cancellation of the cavalry prepared for [[YuanTan|袁谭]].

## Key Claims

- A private defection promise remains fragile when the target still fears the old patron's retaliation.
- Public exposure can turn ambiguous intent or wavering affiliation into a political fact that other actors must answer.
- The commitment device may be morally or diplomatically costly because killing an envoy is useful precisely when it destroys the route back.
- The tactic depends on timing: it works best when the old camp still thinks recovery is possible but the actor has already moved psychologically or strategically away from it.
- Irreversibility can create strategic value beyond the local court by forcing the enemy to divide troops, attention, and trust.
- The Eastern Han variant shows that binding can be self-executed by a defector, not only imposed by an outside persuader.
- Hanji 1066 shows that the exit-closing act need not be envoy killing; public dismissal of a rival envoy and demobilization of aid can also end useful ambiguity.

## Evidence

- Private promise fragility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-158-koucai-hao-de-tianhuaban-yiren-ding-baiwan-dajun-lnup-vmw-ff-nqmuh6-x8si0k8zu|Hanji 158]] says Ying Bu privately agrees with Sui He but wants the matter kept secret because he still fears Chu.
- Public exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-158-koucai-hao-de-tianhuaban-yiren-ding-baiwan-dajun-lnup-vmw-ff-nqmuh6-x8si0k8zu|Hanji 158]] says Sui He publicly tells Xiang Yu's envoy that Jiujiang has submitted to Han, converting Ying Bu's hidden option into open evidence.
- Envoy killing as exit closure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-158-koucai-hao-de-tianhuaban-yiren-ding-baiwan-dajun-lnup-vmw-ff-nqmuh6-x8si0k8zu|Hanji 158]] says Sui He urges Ying Bu to kill the Chu envoy so Xiang Yu will treat him as an enemy.
- Eastern Han self-binding signal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-703-donghan-kaiguo-hou-zui-xianhe-de-jiazu-pingsha-shi-ta-lgg-fra8ostp2xsuugph6o-ilzac|Hanji 703]] says Liang Tong kills Wei Xiao's envoy, severs the relationship, and discards Wei Xiao's general seal and ribbon.
- Strategic field effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-703-donghan-kaiguo-hou-zui-xianhe-de-jiazu-pingsha-shi-ta-lgg-fra8ostp2xsuugph6o-ilzac|Hanji 703]] places Liang Tong's break beside Dou Rong's warning and Ma Yuan's persuasion mission, so the signal contributes to the wider splitting of Wei Xiao's camp.
- Non-killing public closure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1066-sanguo-zui-manheng-de-waijiaoguan-pingshenme-shi-qianzhao-ll17spch6x4zyo61vno-oi0lk7ft|Hanji 1066]] says Qian Zhao forces Wuhuan leaders to stop hedging among Cao Cao, Yuan-family remnants, and Liaodong by dismissing Han Zhong and disbanding the cavalry prepared for Yuan Tan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not treat every defection as virtuous or every envoy killing as strategically justified. Hanji 158 is the harsher, externally engineered case; Hanji 703 is a self-binding analogy where Liang Tong appears to bind himself rather than being trapped by a persuader; Hanji 1066 is a non-killing public-closure variant where the envoy is spared but the hedging route still collapses. Zhang Xue's broader identity, Liang Tong's exact prior office, the legal or moral judgment on killing Wei Xiao's envoy, and the unnamed Wuhuan leader in Hanji 1066 remain source-scoped. The concept tracks the mechanism of exit closure, not an endorsement of the act.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 1066 as a non-killing frontier variant: Qian Zhao publicly closes Wuhuan's hedging room by forcing dismissal of Liaodong's envoy and cancellation of Yuan Tan aid.
- Reframed the concept around exit closure across violent envoy-killing, self-binding break, and public demobilization signals.
