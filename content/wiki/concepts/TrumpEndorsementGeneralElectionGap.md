# Trump Endorsement General-Election Gap

## Definition

Trump endorsement general-election gap is the difference between [[DonaldTrump]]'s ability to help loyal candidates win [[RepublicanParty]] primaries and those candidates' ability to win the broader electorate.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded source reports that Trump-endorsed candidates won 96% of Republican primaries in both 2024 and 2026, while their general-election success fell from 89% to 50%. The gap suggests that intraparty authority and mass-electoral transferability are separate forms of power: an endorsement can decide who carries the party label without making that nominee acceptable to independents, moderates, or cross-pressured conservatives.

The mechanism is not endorsement alone. A national backlash, economic dissatisfaction, war, candidate experience, district or state composition, and opponent quality can all affect the same races. The concept therefore identifies a selection-and-translation problem rather than proving that Trump personally caused every loss.

## Key Claims

- Primary dominance and general-election reach are distinct political capacities.
- Loyalty signals that help a candidate in a Republican primary can narrow room for later coalition expansion.
- A large gap can leave a party leader powerful inside the organization while weakening the party's office-winning capacity.
- Candidate experience, rhetoric, opponent quality, national conditions, and local electorate composition mediate the gap.
- Repeated election-level comparison is needed before treating one cycle as a stable endorsement effect.

## Evidence

- Reported cycle comparison: [[375-zhongqi-xuanju-canbai-hou-gonghedang-hequ-hecong-lgz0q02naefuostkur2-w84ksjnr]] gives primary/general figures of 96%/89% for 2024 and 96%/50% for 2026.
- Texas case: [[375-zhongqi-xuanju-canbai-hou-gonghedang-hequ-hecong-lgz0q02naefuostkur2-w84ksjnr]] presents Trump-backed Ken Paxton's loss to [[JamesTalarico]] as an example of primary strength failing to translate into statewide victory.
- Candidate-selection mechanism: [[375-zhongqi-xuanju-canbai-hou-gonghedang-hequ-hecong-lgz0q02naefuostkur2-w84ksjnr]] also describes an inexperienced Trump-backed South Carolina nominee losing after winning the party contest.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not supply the candidate universe, race competitiveness, an endorsement counterfactual, official results, or a model controlling for the national environment. Win-rate comparisons can be distorted by which races receive endorsements and by the scale of the wider electoral wave. The figures and causal interpretation remain source-scoped.
