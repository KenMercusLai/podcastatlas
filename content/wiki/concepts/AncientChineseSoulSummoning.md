## Definition

Ancient Chinese soul summoning / 招魂 names ritual practices that interpret illness, fright, fainting, dreams, bereavement, or death through a soul's departure, capture, recall, or temporary appearance. The category describes a cultural and social practice, not proof that spirits literally returned.

## Current Synthesis

The broader fangshu survey roots soul summoning in Chu ritual, 《楚辞》, and soul-body ideas that make difficult bodily states and death narratable. Calling a name or performing recall can give families a language for waiting, care, grief, and action even when the explanation is not medically reliable.

Hanji 331-1 adds a court-performance risk. [[ShaowengWesternHan|少翁]] reportedly offers grieving [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] a nighttime view of Wang夫人 behind a screen while forbidding close inspection. The arrangement joins bereavement relief to controlled visibility: emotional need supplies demand, while darkness, distance, and ritual prohibition limit verification. The host calls the appearance a likely shadow-play trick, but that mechanism is not established by the supplied evidence.

## Key Claims

- Soul-summoning traditions give illness, fright, bodily uncertainty, and death anxiety a narratable structure.
- Soul-and-body distinctions allow ritual practice to classify departure, loss, capture, recall, and post-death handling.
- Ritual recall can provide comfort and coordinated family action without validating its supernatural explanation.
- The same vocabulary can support specialist authority when the claimant controls access to an unseen or partially seen result.
- Bereavement heightens vulnerability because emotional relief and evidentiary judgment operate in the same encounter.
- Darkness, distance, barriers, and no-inspection rules can turn ritual conditions into protection against falsification.

Court performance and restricted verification:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-1-200sui-de-ta-ruhe-ba-huangdi-pian-de-tuantuanzhuan-lvn0xka_dxl88qo5iroseks852ha|Hanji 331-1]] says Shaoweng shows Wudi an apparition of the deceased Wang夫人 behind a screen at night and prohibits approach, after which the emperor rewards him.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources are podcast syntheses rather than ritual manuals, medical evidence, or a primary-source dossier. They do not establish that a soul leaves the body, that a dead person appeared, or that all soul-summoning rites were fraudulent. Hanji 331-1 does not identify the performer, apparatus, lighting, or observation conditions needed to demonstrate shadow play, so its mechanism claim remains source-scoped. Comfort, cultural meaning, deliberate staging, mistaken perception, and literal belief should not be collapsed into one explanation.

## What Changed

- Added a court case in which bereavement demand and controlled visibility make a spirit appearance politically valuable.
- Distinguished the social function of ritual recall from a specialist's possible use of ritual conditions to avoid inspection.
- Kept the episode's shadow-play explanation explicitly unverified.
