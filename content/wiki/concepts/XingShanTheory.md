# Xingshan Theory / 性善论

Xingshan theory / 性善论 enters this wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-101-2-ruhe-kandai-xingbenshan-yu-xingbene-lmawsqkttjmitwkczhyfs7f7-mqt]], where the host introduces it as [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]]'s famous position before contrasting it with [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s [[XingETheory|性恶论]]. The source uses the familiar "人之初，性本善" formula to orient listeners, while the doctrinal content is Mencius's claim that people have moral beginnings such as compassion, shame, respect, and a sense of right and wrong.

In this episode, 性善论 is not only a psychology claim. It supports Mencius's politics: because people have moral capacity, a ruler should practice [[MencianBenevolentGovernment|仁政]], value the people, and create conditions in which moral sprouts can be preserved and extended. The episode also keeps the historical limit visible: Mencius's program was treated as too remote from practical affairs and was not basically implemented by the rulers he visited.

The concept is paired with [[HumanNaturePlasticity|人性可变性]]. Even when the source presents Mencius as optimistic about original nature, it also says those good tendencies can be gained, lost, strengthened, or buried through later circumstance.

## Key Claims
- The source presents Mencius as the main representative of 性善论.
- Moral sprouts are described as compassion, shame, respect, right and wrong, and the roots of ren, yi, li, and zhi.
- 性善论 supports benevolent rule rather than only private moral cultivation.
- The episode does not make Mencian optimism politically automatic; it notes the gap between doctrine and ruler uptake.
- The theory sits inside a wider human-plasticity frame, not a simple fixed-good label.
