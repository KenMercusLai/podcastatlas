## Definition

谋而不断式时机丧失 / planning-without-decision timing loss is the failure pattern in which repeated planning seeks greater certainty while delay erodes the capacity, secrecy, initiative, or political window needed to act at all.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-2-saiweng-shima-de-zuozhe-shi-zenme-si-de-lp9u6yu1la2w6sfqwqfikcvgq9pt|Hanji 303-2]] builds the pattern from its diagnosis of [[LiuAn|刘安]]: “王犹豫，计未决.” The episode says he repeatedly considered mobilization and repeatedly stopped, so by the time [[WuBeiWesternHan|伍被]] disclosed the plan and imperial arrest approached, the earlier option to initiate action had disappeared.

The useful concept is narrower than the host's counterfactual. Planning and caution are not defects by themselves, and faster rebellion is not shown to have been wise or winnable. The failure occurs when the actor has already incurred the risks of a consequential project but postpones a decision without preserving the resources, secrecy, exit routes, or trigger conditions that would keep real options open.

## Key Claims

- Planning can become a liability when it accumulates exposure without producing a decision rule.
- Delay is not neutral: evidence leaks, opponents mobilize, resources decay, and externally imposed deadlines can close the window.
- Seeking complete certainty is especially hazardous in projects whose outcome is inherently contingent.
- Timely action should mean acting against defined triggers and objectives, not treating speed or aggression as virtues in themselves.
- A failed hesitant actor does not prove that earlier action would have succeeded; feasibility and moral legitimacy remain separate questions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not establish that Liu An had a viable winning plan, that immediate rebellion was lawful or desirable, or that hesitation was the sole cause of defeat. The quoted military maxims are presented through a course-promotion segment, and the supplied summary does not verify their wording or textual context. This concept therefore diagnoses option decay after commitment and exposure; it is not a blanket rule to act quickly, reject deliberation, or prefer irreversible action.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded distinction between useful planning and delay that consumes the very option being planned.
- Preserved the gap between diagnosing a lost window and claiming that earlier action would have produced success.
