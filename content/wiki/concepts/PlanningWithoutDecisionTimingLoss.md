## Definition

谋而不断式时机丧失 / planning-without-decision timing loss is the failure pattern in which an actor keeps generating or revising plans after a consequential project has begun creating exposure, while failing to define the evidence, trigger, objective, authority, or exit condition that would turn deliberation into action or withdrawal.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-1-huainanwang-ruhe-bei-jiujiexin-bisi-de-ljtqn-nrxyf8yqx-yowsainkzmjc|Hanji 303-1]] gives the pattern a concrete prehistory. [[LiuAn|刘安]] moves among forged orders and seals, assassination, palace fire, a false border alarm, and an official roundup, but the alternatives do not reflect improving evidence or a stable decision rule. Each additional scheme adds people, documents, and discoverable intent while the underlying conditions remain poor: [[WuBeiWesternHan|伍被]] says there is no princely or popular support and gives his own manufactured-crisis plan only a one-in-ten chance.

The failure becomes visible when the external deadline arrives. Court officers come for [[LiuQianHuainanCrownPrinceWesternHan|刘迁]], almost no senior kingdom officials answer the summons, and Liu An releases the only chancellor who appears. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-2-saiweng-shima-de-zuozhe-shi-zenme-si-de-lp9u6yu1la2w6sfqwqfikcvgq9pt|Hanji 303-2]] then compresses the result as “王犹豫，计未决”: Wu Bei confesses, arrests follow, and the remaining option to control the timing disappears.

The joined evidence sharpens an important boundary. The problem is not merely slowness, because the project may never have been feasible or legitimate. Nor is having several options inherently bad. The failure lies in accumulating irreversible exposure while neither selecting a viable course nor stopping, and in mistaking new plan generation for preserved optionality when organizational support and secrecy are already decaying.

## Key Claims

- Plan proliferation is not progress when alternatives are not evaluated against stable objectives, feasibility thresholds, and triggers.
- Delay becomes costly once deliberation itself creates evidence, participants, commitments, or an approaching external deadline.
- A decision process must include a withdrawal rule; otherwise rejecting each current plan can still leave the underlying project active and exposed.
- Organizational response is part of feasibility: a strategy that cannot mobilize its own officials has lost more than timing.
- Timely action means acting or exiting against defined conditions, not treating speed, aggression, or irreversibility as virtues.
- Diagnosing option decay does not prove that an earlier version of the project would have succeeded or should have been attempted.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-2-saiweng-shima-de-zuozhe-shi-zenme-si-de-lp9u6yu1la2w6sfqwqfikcvgq9pt|Hanji 303-2]] invokes 《六韬》 maxims about using the moment at hand while presenting “多谋无断” as the host's explanation of defeat.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source establishes that Liu An possessed a viable winning plan, that immediate rebellion was lawful or desirable, or that hesitation was the sole cause of defeat. Hanji 303-1 instead supplies counterevidence to a simple “act sooner” lesson: weak support, extremely low estimated odds, implausible devices, and failed official mobilization. Hanji 303-2's military maxims appear in a course-promotion segment, and their wording and context are not independently verified here. The concept therefore diagnoses unmanaged exposure and decaying options; it does not recommend rash commitment to a bad project.

## What Changed

- The concept now distinguishes repeated plan generation from evidence-driven refinement.
- It adds explicit withdrawal criteria and organizational support to the decision-quality test.
- The joined sources shift the judgment away from “too slow to win” toward “unable to choose, exit, or preserve a viable option.”
