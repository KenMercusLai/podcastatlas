## Definition

Technological disaster as institutional failure is the frame for disasters produced by technical design, organizational routines, material constraints, incentives, information control, and accountability systems together rather than by a single faulty device or individual mistake.

## Current Synthesis

The core case remains [[ChernobylNuclearPowerPlant|Chernobyl]] as reconstructed by [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]]. That source links reactor design, graphite-tipped control-rod effects, operator procedures, material shortages, flammable roofing, production pressure, official confidence, and post-accident blame into one causal field.

Hanji 394-3 broadens the frame from causality to public comparison. It does not reconstruct Fukushima or Chernobyl in the same technical depth, but it shows how nuclear disasters are remembered as institutional responsibility problems when accident aftermath, public fear, and risk disposal become political disputes.

The concept therefore has two layers: a technical-institutional layer that explains how disaster occurs, and a public-responsibility layer that asks how institutions narrate, contain, or externalize harm after disaster.

## Key Claims

- Complex technologies fail through coupled technical, organizational, material, and political conditions.
- Hidden design flaws are more dangerous when operators are expected to supply the missing safety margin.
- Visible construction and production targets can coexist with invisible safety debt.
- Public certainty about safety can delay recognition after failure begins.
- Accountability after disaster needs to distinguish operator error, design defect, managerial responsibility, political secrecy, and later risk transfer.

## Evidence

- Coupled causality: [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]] treats Chernobyl as the result of design, construction, operations, incentives, secrecy, and responsibility systems together.
- Operator burden: [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]] links complex procedures and hidden design conditions to [[HumanDependentSafetySystem]].
- Safety debt and certainty: [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]] links material compromises and "absolute safety" claims to [[NuclearSafetyPropaganda]].
- Responsibility after disaster: [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]] uses Bryukhanov, Dyatlov, and Legasov to separate managerial, operational, design, and truth-telling responsibility.
- Public aftermath comparison: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-3-shenwa-lishi-heweiji-weihe-dou-fasheng-zai-riben-lspztkfaaq0hcq1no3yum1xdox8a]] places Fukushima and Chernobyl in one nuclear-risk memory sequence, showing how institutional failure remains politically active after the initiating accident.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The frame should not become a vague way to blame "the system" without preserving agency. The Chernobyl evidence still keeps individuals and roles visible. Hanji 394-3 adds a strong moral and political comparison, but it is not a detailed engineering source for Fukushima or Chernobyl, so its technical claims remain source-scoped.
