# Rule-Bound Overthinking / 规则化过度思考

Rule-bound overthinking is the episode's frame for systems that reduce some individual uncertainty by externalizing decision rules, while creating new anxiety around whether the person is following the correct process, script, hierarchy, or context. In [[132-dang-guodu-sikao-de-dagongren-yu-shang-di-yuwang-de-shidai-luaqswjqezbakxdrcshifxi0ermo]], [[YoumamaMaomao|尤妈妈 / 猫猫]] describes Japanese workplaces and services as highly rule-aware: overtime, customer-service speech, paperwork, disclaimers, and institutional responsibility are often more explicit than in Chinese internet-work settings.

The source does not romanticize rules as mental peace. Rules can protect workers from unlimited demands and reduce arbitrary decisions, but they can also turn edge cases into procedural loops. The postal-forwarding example makes this concrete: a bank credential cannot be forwarded, the old forwarding service cannot be canceled cleanly, and the proposed fix is another process workaround rather than direct discretionary judgment.

The concept also captures a cross-cultural contrast. Chinese workplace speech may overthink whether something is politically or socially sayable, while Japanese workplace speech may overthink whether the script permits a reply. Both patterns manage risk, but they distribute the cognitive burden differently.

## Key Claims
- Rules can reduce decision load without eliminating anxiety.
- Boundary rules, such as overtime approval, can protect off-work time and strengthen [[WorkplacePacing]].
- Process rules can become brittle when the situation falls outside the expected path.
- Scripted communication can reduce liability while weakening direct problem-solving.
- Overthinking can be institutional, not only personal: the person thinks because the system requires constant context-reading.
