# Qing Mid-Dynasty Accumulated Maladies / 清中叶积弊

## Definition
清中叶积弊 is the source's name for problems accumulated during prosperity that have hardened into routines and interests without yet producing unmistakable general disorder.

## Current Synthesis
The concept joins structure and perception. Population and livelihood pressure, expensive recurring state projects, underfunded informal staffing, layered rules, unofficial charges, and constrained information channels reinforce one another. Because order still appears recoverable, actors can both diagnose real decline and underestimate the pressure needed for reform.

## Key Claims
- Accumulated maladies grow inside successful systems rather than only after visible collapse.
- Informal fees and personnel can become functional supports as well as abuses, making simple abolition destabilizing.
- Salt, grain tribute, and river works reveal how recurrent logistics and large projects concentrate leakage and dependence.
- Procedural accretion can preserve control while reducing problem-solving capacity.
- Apparent peace can sustain reform hope while weakening urgency.

## Evidence
- Structural evidence: [[501-cong-hongliangji-gongzizhen-dao-weiyuan-qingzhongye-de-ren-ruhe-kandai-ziji-guojia-weilai-lgu0h9kaxmfwzoe1byxeyq7png9o]] links population, social differentiation, staffing, informal fees, and the salt-grain-river systems.
- Interest evidence: [[501-cong-hongliangji-gongzizhen-dao-weiyuan-qingzhongye-de-ren-ruhe-kandai-ziji-guojia-weilai-lgu0h9kaxmfwzoe1byxeyq7png9o]] uses “食弊者” and “有不能不然者” for actors whose livelihood depends on institutional gaps.
- Perception evidence: [[501-cong-hongliangji-gongzizhen-dao-weiyuan-qingzhongye-de-ren-ruhe-kandai-ziji-guojia-weilai-lgu0h9kaxmfwzoe1byxeyq7png9o]] uses “在太平忘太平” to capture the gap between accumulated weakness and continued expectations of stability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is an interpretive framework from one scholar interview, not a settled explanation of all Qing decline. The source itself resists monocausal accounts: foreign weapon disparity, population, fiscal design, institutions, political culture, and timing interact. Quantities and direct quotations require primary-source checking.
