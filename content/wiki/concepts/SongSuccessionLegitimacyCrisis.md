# 宋初继承合法性危机 / Song Succession Legitimacy Crisis

Song succession legitimacy crisis / 宋初继承合法性危机 is the episode's frame for why [[ZhaoKuangyin|赵匡胤 / 宋太祖]]'s death and [[ZhaoGuangyi|赵光义 / 宋太宗]]'s accession remained suspicious. In [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115]], the issue is not a single clue but a chain of succession facts and political incentives.

The crisis begins with Taizu's sudden death and [[CandleShadowAxeSound|烛影斧声]], then moves through [[SongEmpress|宋皇后]]'s expected summons for [[ZhaoDefang|赵德芳]], [[WangJien|王继恩]]'s turn toward Taizong, and the need for [[GoldenCabinetPledge|金匮之盟]] to justify bypassing Taizu's sons. It deepens when [[ZhaoDezhao|赵德昭]], Zhao Defang, and [[ZhaoTingmei|赵廷美]] each disappear from the viable succession field.

The concept links dynasty-level inheritance to personality and institutions. The source argues that Taizong's later military adventurism, cultural patronage, source management, and political cleaning can all be read as consequences of rule gained under legitimacy pressure.

## Key Claims
- Succession crises are often visible in procedure before they are visible in confession.
- A legitimacy argument can solve one problem while creating new claimants.
- Missing texts, terse records, and later revised histories do not prove guilt, but they raise the value of contextual source criticism.
- Alternative heirs can become dangerous even if they do not actively rebel.
- Personal insecurity can become institutional action when attached to the throne.
