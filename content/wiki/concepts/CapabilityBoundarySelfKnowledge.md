## Definition

Capability-boundary self-knowledge / 能力边界自知 is the ability to distinguish a real strength from the wider roles, authority, and decisions that strength can safely support.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1140-ziwo-renzhi-queshi-ruhe-huidiao-yidai-xiaoxiong-machao-lkpfzorf-o-zilyczvepedryc7ouk|Hanji 1140]] develops the concept through [[MaChao|马超]]. The episode does not deny his courage, combat skill, or military reputation. It argues that he mistakes those assets for evidence that he can judge political threat, bear family consequences, hold a coalition together, preserve credibility, and rebuild an independent power base.

Self-knowledge in this sense is operational rather than merely introspective. It requires identifying which capability is strong, what complementary capacities are missing, which costs a choice transfers to other people, and when dependence or withdrawal is more realistic than renewed escalation. Ma Chao's later movement from [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]] to [[LiuBei|刘备]] shows that a person can retain visible talent after losing the trust, forces, alliances, and autonomy needed to use it independently.

## Key Claims

- A genuine strength does not automatically validate ambition in a wider role.
- Self-knowledge includes missing complementary capacities, not only confidence in one's best skill.
- Major choices should be tested against foreseeable costs to family, allies, reputation, and future options.
- Repeated escalation can become self-vindication when it aims to prove continuing value rather than improve the strategic position.
- Damaged trust can leave capability usable only under another actor's supervision.
- Clear self-assessment can require accepting dependence, retreat, or a narrower role without denying real talent.

## Evidence

- Strength-role distinction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1140-ziwo-renzhi-queshi-ruhe-huidiao-yidai-xiaoxiong-machao-lkpfzorf-o-zilyczvepedryc7ouk|Hanji 1140]] contrasts Ma Chao's bravery and military reputation with the political and strategic capacities required for independent rule.
- Consequence awareness: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1140-ziwo-renzhi-queshi-ruhe-huidiao-yidai-xiaoxiong-machao-lkpfzorf-o-zilyczvepedryc7ouk|Hanji 1140]] says Ma Chao proceeds despite warnings that rebellion exposes his relatives, making cost recognition part of the judgment problem.
- Escalation after loss: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1140-ziwo-renzhi-queshi-ruhe-huidiao-yidai-xiaoxiong-machao-lkpfzorf-o-zilyczvepedryc7ouk|Hanji 1140]] interprets the Qishan attempt as both survival and an effort to prove that Ma Chao still has value.
- Capability without autonomy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1140-ziwo-renzhi-queshi-ruhe-huidiao-yidai-xiaoxiong-machao-lkpfzorf-o-zilyczvepedryc7ouk|Hanji 1140]] presents Zhang Lu and Liu Bei as willing to use Ma Chao's reputation while limiting trust and independent command.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not turn defeat into proof that the original decision was irrational, or reduce action under coercive threat to personality. Earlier Weinan evidence shows that Cao Cao's movement could reasonably appear dangerous to Ma Chao and other Guanzhong commanders. Hanji 1140 is a popular-history interpretation, not a clinical assessment or controlled decision study; its claims about arrogance, self-deception, and gambling psychology remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created an operational self-knowledge concept that separates a strong skill from the larger role it can support.
- Added foreseeable third-party cost, credibility, and future-option preservation to the boundary test.
- Used Ma Chao's post-defeat dependence to show that talent can survive after autonomous capacity has collapsed.
