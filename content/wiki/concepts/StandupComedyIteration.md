# Stand-up Comedy Iteration

## Definition
Stand-up comedy iteration is the live process of turning a captured premise into working material through performance, audience response, trimming, reframing, sequencing, and eventual retirement.

## Current Synthesis
[[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] presents stand-up development as an embodied feedback loop. [[TomSegura]] captures kernels, tests them in clubs, revises setup and wording, organizes long sets through cues, and eventually retires successful material. [[yangli-luoyonghao-buxiang-chengwei-baxin-ye-wuyi-chengwei-dengta-lprytonoy3jq2z8dnxgygcqk_zuo]] adds the mature-tour phase through [[YangLiStandup|杨笠]]: repetition can first drain novelty, then free attention for pacing, pauses, control, recovery, and enjoyment of performance itself. A teleprompter can operate as psychological backup even when unused, while cold rooms reveal how silence changes delivery speed and perceived rejection.

## Key Claims
- Capturing a small premise preserves possibility without pretending the finished bit is already known.
- Live testing supplies information that private writing and reasoning cannot fully reproduce.
- Revision can change setup, detail, wording, framing, sequence, or the performer's relationship to the premise.
- A compact cue list can support structure without turning a long-form set into recitation.
- Mastery through repetition can move attention from remembering text toward timing, room control, and recovery.
- Willingness to bomb is a development cost, especially when replacing proven material.
- Retiring material restores creative pressure and prevents a past success from becoming a fixed identity.

## Evidence
- Kernel capture - [[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] records Segura using voice notes and preserving the seed rather than drafting a full bit.
- Club testing - [[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] describes laughter as immediate evidence and lists excess detail, insufficient setup, and weak lines as revision targets.
- Set structure - [[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] has Segura describe cue words and an hour divided into larger chunks.
- Tour mastery and support - [[yangli-luoyonghao-buxiang-chengwei-baxin-ye-wuyi-chengwei-dengta-lprytonoy3jq2z8dnxgygcqk_zuo]] has Yang describe repetition fatigue, later performance fluency, an unused backup teleprompter, and the shift toward pacing and room control.
- Cold-room adaptation - [[yangli-luoyonghao-buxiang-chengwei-baxin-ye-wuyi-chengwei-dengta-lprytonoy3jq2z8dnxgygcqk_zuo]] records delivery accelerating when the audience does not respond and emphasizes that identical material can land differently across rooms.
- Renewal - [[the-science-art-of-comedy-creativity-tom-segura-scim7538555033]] says stale delivery becomes perceptible and praises elite comics who risk failing while developing new work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources describe two comedians' practices, not a universal formula. Concise joke writers may work differently, audience response can vary by room and culture, and laughter or silence shows that something happened without always revealing why. Neither source compares live-testing or tour-support methods experimentally.

## What Changed
- Added the mature-tour phase in which repetition shifts attention from textual recall toward timing, control, recovery, and renewed performance enjoyment.
- Added cue support and cold-room acceleration as practical qualifications to apparent stage ease.
