## Definition
Journalistic independence against faction / 拒绝站队的新闻独立 is the practice of keeping factual and moral judgment answerable to evidence and conscience rather than granting automatic loyalty to a political camp, government, movement, or audience identity.

## Current Synthesis
The Fallaci source defines the ethic through a reporter who could admire courage and condemn atrocity within the same political camp, criticize the shah without becoming loyal to Khomeini, and challenge institutions that claimed protective purpose when they acted coercively. Independence is therefore more demanding than superficial balance: it can require criticism of one's presumed allies and recognition of an opponent's humanity.

Episode 504 adds a Hong Kong editorial version through [[LinXingzhi|林行止]]'s “不左不右，只求其是.” The source presents Lin as willing to criticize the colonial administration, Beijing, London, and Hong Kong elites while using local residents' interests as his reference point. His open disagreement with [[JinYong|金庸]] over constitutional design shows that public conflict need not erase recognition of another person's cultural contribution.

## Key Claims
- Independence means refusing to let camp membership settle truth in advance.
- It may require condemning one's presumed side and acknowledging value in an opponent.
- A local or public-interest reference point can guide judgment without becoming loyalty to a power center.
- Refusal of faction does not eliminate error and therefore must coexist with factual correction.
- Audiences often experience cross-camp criticism as betrayal, making misunderstanding part of the cost.

## Evidence
- Cross-camp judgment: [[77-ta-shi-ziyou-de-yegou-ta-shi-falaqi-685710187]] presents [[OrianaFallaci|Oriana Fallaci]] criticizing both existing rulers and revolutionary replacements.
- Institutional refusal: [[77-ta-shi-ziyou-de-yegou-ta-shi-falaqi-685710187]] includes Fallaci's challenge to AFTRA as a case where protective rhetoric did not exempt coercion from criticism.
- Hong Kong orientation: [[504-lin-xingzhi-xinbao-yu-xianggang-jingji-huangjin-niandai-lsqh0b2fi11uc5udp330ibwuyih]] describes Lin criticizing British, Chinese, colonial, and local power from a claimed Hong Kong-interest position.
- Public disagreement: [[504-lin-xingzhi-xinbao-yu-xianggang-jingji-huangjin-niandai-lsqh0b2fi11uc5udp330ibwuyih]] recounts Lin and Jin Yong's political dispute alongside continued personal civility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Refusing faction is not proof of correctness, neutrality, or equal criticism of all sides. The Fallaci source explicitly leaves her late controversies open to criticism, while the Lin source gives limited space to opponents of his market liberalism. Claims of independence must be evaluated against actual evidence, correction practice, ownership, and revenue pressure.
