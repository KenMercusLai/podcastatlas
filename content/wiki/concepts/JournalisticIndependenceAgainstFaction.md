# Journalistic Independence Against Faction / 拒绝站队的新闻独立

Journalistic independence against faction / 拒绝站队的新闻独立 is the reporting ethic [[77-ta-shi-ziyou-de-yegou-ta-shi-falaqi-685710187]] attributes to [[OrianaFallaci|Oriana Fallaci]]. The episode says Fallaci was repeatedly misunderstood because people expected her to join left or right, revolution or counterrevolution, religion or anti-religion, feminism or anti-feminism. Her pattern was to report from what she thought she saw, even when that broke an audience's preferred camp story.

The source illustrates this through Fallaci's ability to admire courage and condemn atrocity in the same political camp, to criticize [[MohammadRezaPahlavi|the shah]] without becoming loyal to [[RuhollahKhomeini|Khomeini]], and to challenge protective institutions such as [[AFTRA]] when they acted coercively. The point is not that Fallaci was always correct; the source explicitly leaves room for criticism of her late controversies. The point is that factional belonging is not allowed to decide truth in advance.

## Key Claims
- Independence is more demanding than balance because it may require condemning one's supposed side and acknowledging courage in an opponent.
- A journalist can oppose oppression without granting immunity to the forces that replace the old oppressor.
- Refusing faction does not eliminate error; it only keeps judgment answerable to evidence and conscience rather than loyalty.
- Public misunderstanding is part of the cost because audiences often read criticism as camp betrayal.
