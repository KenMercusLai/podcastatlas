# Disruptive Innovation

Disruptive innovation is the adoption pattern foregrounded in [[zhenzheng-gaibian-shijie-de-jishu-weishenme-yikaishi-dou-bu-bei-kanhao-s10e16-8c95b3dc-d75a-4bdd-84d4-2c06fd2d85b1]]. The episode's strongest technical case is [[MOSFET|MOS]]: compared with [[BipolarJunctionTransistor|BJT]] devices, early MOS looked slower, less stable, and less suited to [[ATT|AT&T]]'s immediate switching market, but it had a scalable advantage in cost, structure, and integration.

The source uses this to explain why world-changing technologies are often misread. They do not begin as universally better; they begin with enough of a different advantage that long iteration, manufacturing learning, and system redesign can eventually make the old evaluation metric less important.

The episode extends the same idea into organizations. [[Google]] could possess transformer-era technology while hesitating to attack search, [[Intel]] could embody the earlier chip revolution while missing the GPU-era shift, and [[Nvidia]] could fight inertia by replacing its own products before the market forces it to.

## Key Claims
- A new route can be correctly judged weak by incumbent metrics and still be strategically important.
- The smallest scalable advantage can matter more than current all-around performance when manufacturing learning and ecosystem changes compound.
- Incumbents are vulnerable when the new route threatens existing revenue, evaluation systems, or organizational identity.
- Protected teams, alternate metrics, and deliberate self-replacement are possible countermeasures, but the source does not treat them as automatic solutions.
