# Dull Dirty Dangerous Robotics

## Definition
Dull dirty dangerous robotics is the deployment principle that robots should first take work that is repetitive, unpleasant, hazardous, remote, or physically harmful before trying to replace broad human judgment or social labor.

## Current Synthesis
The All-In robotics special uses this frame across industrial inspection, oil-and-gas leak risk, warehouse tote movement, remote facilities, and future job-quality arguments. The concept is both commercial and moral: dangerous or low-quality work gives robots a clearer business case and a stronger social justification.

## Key Claims
- Robots are commercially strongest when the avoided risk, downtime, or injury cost is larger than the robot's operating cost.
- The frame explains why inspection and warehouse work look nearer than general home autonomy.
- Remote and hazardous infrastructure can justify robots even when human labor is available because access itself is risky or expensive.
- Repetitive material movement can be valuable if it frees people from low-quality physical work and shifts them into supervision, maintenance, or knowledge work.
- The social case for robotics remains conditional: safety, worker transition, privacy, and real deployment evidence still matter.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept can become too broad if every automation project claims to remove bad work. The episode itself contains unresolved worker-displacement questions, and the social value of replacing a task depends on wages, transition paths, safety, privacy, and whether the robot actually performs reliably.
