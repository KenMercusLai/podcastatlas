## Definition

Retaliatory political terror is the pattern where a powerholder converts omen accusation, refusal, warning, omission, lawful enforcement, property resistance, partial compliance, elite reputation, or association with critics into lethal punishment. Its tools include fabricated accusation, prison torture, exemplary killing, public display, mourning threats, confiscation, and family liability.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 830 now adds a court-ministerial branch to [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]]'s terror record. [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] and [[DuQiaoLateHan|杜乔]] are accused of hidden conspiracy, and public pressure can only briefly interrupt the case. Liang Ji reasserts the accusation, both men die in prison, and their bodies are displayed with mourning forbidden; terror therefore targets reputation, followers, and funeral memory as well as the officials themselves.

Hanji 833 supplies a wealth-extortion branch. [[SunFenFufengLateHan|孙奋]] is targeted because he is rich; Liang turns a gift and loan request into coercive payment, treats partial compliance as offense, then uses a false claim against Sun Fen's mother, prison torture, and property seizure to destroy the household. The same source's rabbit-park deaths show Liang making private pleasure legally lethal for outsiders.

Hanji 843 adds an omen-accusation branch. [[ChenShouTaishiLingLateHan|Chen Shou the Taishi ling]] blames a solar eclipse on Liang through [[XuHuangLateHan|徐璜]], after which Liang has the Luoyang magistrate arrest and torture him until he dies; [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Emperor Huan]] knows but does not visibly act.

Hanji 846 grounds the concept in a larger victim chain. [[WuShuWanMagistrateLateHan|Wu Shu]] is poisoned after enforcing against Liang's guests, [[HaoMengDongjunLateHan|Hao Meng]] is executed after failing to visit Liang first, [[YuanZhuRemonstrantLateHan|Yuan Zhu]] and [[CuiQiLateHan|Cui Qi]] are killed after warnings, [[HuWuTaiyuanLateHan|Hu Wu]]'s household is exterminated, [[HaoJieTaiyuanLateHan|Hao Jie]] kills himself to save kin, and [[GengChengLinruHouLateHan|Geng Cheng]] is killed with family after refusing a grave-goods demand.

## Key Claims

- Terror begins when private grievance can be converted into official accusation, execution, poisoning, prison death, or household punishment.
- The victim does not need to rebel; reputation, warning, lawful enforcement, omitted deference, partial compliance, or mourning can be enough.
- Public display and mourning bans extend retaliation from the condemned person to followers and memory.
- Omen or disaster-responsibility claims become dangerous when they name an overmighty actor rather than staying abstract.
- Protected retainers make local office dangerous because public law collides with the powerholder's private network.
- Family liability extends the terror beyond the named actor and forces kin-preservation calculations.
- Corrective speech fails when warning a powerholder is treated as exposing or threatening him.

- Terror beyond death: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-830-donghan-ligu-wo-sui-si-yourong-lt-j7n9gotiw-negqxojoacf6ji|Hanji 830]] says Liang displays Li Gu and Du Qiao's bodies and forbids mourning, making followers and funeral memory part of the intimidation target.
- Coerced payment punished: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-833-tanguan-neng-you-duo-lipu-kanwan-dadieyanjing-lqudlijjyqmcro-wsmrvwkodgt72|Hanji 833]] says Liang Ji demands a large loan from Sun Fen, receives only part of it, and then retaliates.
- False accusation, prison violence, and confiscation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-833-tanguan-neng-you-duo-lipu-kanwan-dadieyanjing-lqudlijjyqmcro-wsmrvwkodgt72|Hanji 833]] says Liang falsely accuses Sun Fen's mother, Sun Fen and his brother die under prison torture, and Liang seizes the household wealth.
- Private rule made lethal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-833-tanguan-neng-you-duo-lipu-kanwan-dadieyanjing-lqudlijjyqmcro-wsmrvwkodgt72|Hanji 833]] says a mistaken killing in Liang's rabbit park leads to more than ten deaths.
- Omen accusation punished: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]] says Chen Shou the Taishi ling blames a solar eclipse on Liang Ji, then is arrested, tortured, and killed after Liang hears of the report.
- Retainer enforcement punished: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]] says Wu Shu kills harmful Liang guests after refusing protection and is later poisoned by Liang.
- Omitted deference punished: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]] says Hao Meng is executed before taking office after he has not yet visited Liang.
- Corrective speech punished: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]] says Yuan Zhu is beaten to death after warning Liang to reduce power, and Cui Qi is hunted down after admonitory essays and direct criticism.
- Family liability and kin-preservation pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-846-bahu-jiangjun-bingshan-yijiao-de-fanzui-jilu-lv7fbvoqr1nureqgvwiwixjyk4pq|Hanji 846]] says Hu Wu's household is exterminated, Geng Cheng is killed with more than ten family members, and Hao Jie drinks poison at Liang's gate to exchange his life for family safety.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently grounded in four late-Han source notes and should not be treated as a universal model of all political violence. Hanji 830 adds a reputation-and-funeral branch, but it also shows partial limits: petitions force a temporary release, Wu You can speak in defense, and Liang Na can later pardon mourners and permit burial. Detailed counts, legal forms, property figures, final-letter wording, and exact chronology of each case remain source-scoped. The concept differs from lawful punishment: its diagnostic feature is retaliation for offense to an overmighty actor, not proof-based public adjudication.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 830 as a ministerial-persecution and funeral-intimidation branch through Li Gu and Du Qiao.
- Refined the concept to include reputation, followers, public body display, and mourning bans as targets of retaliatory terror.
