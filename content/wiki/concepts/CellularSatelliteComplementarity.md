# Cellular-Satellite Complementarity

## Definition
Cellular-satellite complementarity is the view that terrestrial cellular and satellite networks occupy different coverage and cost regimes: cellular systems dominate dense populated areas, while satellites extend service to oceans, remote terrain, and terrestrial-network failures.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats the two systems as complements rather than simple substitutes. Cellular networks reuse spectrum efficiently where enough users justify local infrastructure. Satellite constellations serve places outside that infrastructure's economic coverage and can provide resilience when earthquakes or other disasters damage terrestrial equipment. Satellite scale still requires capital, launch capacity, replacement cycles, and a service model that can absorb those costs.

## Key Claims
- Dense cities favor cellular infrastructure because local reuse and mature deployment make it economical.
- Remote land, oceans, and emergency conditions create a comparative advantage for satellite coverage.
- Satellite connectivity can provide backup resilience without replacing terrestrial capacity in ordinary high-density use.
- Constellation economics depend on repeated replenishment, not only initial deployment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not compare capacity, latency, spectrum coordination, device power, indoor availability, price, launch externalities, or regulatory constraints in depth. “Complementary” describes the current source judgment, not a guarantee that providers will avoid competition at the margin.
