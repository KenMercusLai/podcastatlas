# Entrepreneur Public Communication / 企业家公共传播

## Definition
Entrepreneur public communication is the use of a founder's visible voice and persona to explain products, build or defend trust, reach stakeholders, and direct attention toward company or public goals.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats founder visibility as a change in distribution rather than an entirely new institution. Television, speeches, letters, and press interviews previously carried executive communication; short video now reaches consumers, enterprise buyers, officials, employees, and critics directly. The strategic test is whether visibility clarifies products and relationships or becomes attention detached from operating value.

The same channel creates risk. A founder can humanize technical work, respond to misunderstanding, and lend traffic to places or industries, but can also consume management time, intensify controversy, distort company identity, or turn spontaneous events into unstable reputational bets. Public communication is therefore an operating capability with boundaries, not a substitute for product quality.

## Key Claims
- Founder visibility can serve marketing, public relations, product education, and trust defense at once.
- Stakeholders in business and government markets also consume mass social media, so public reach is not limited to consumer sales.
- Attention creates value when it can be directed toward a product, organization, industry, place, or public purpose.
- Founder-led distribution carries backlash, misunderstanding, time-allocation, and key-person risks.
- Rapid response can shape an accidental media event, but cannot make the outcome fully controllable.

- Product and public-purpose use: [[lhg2boyb8begpx0hyq-n6qur47ks-lhg2boyb8begpx0hyq-n6qur47ks]] connects founder visibility to AI education, domestic products, regional digital industry, and collaboration opportunities.
- Risk and improvisation: [[lhg2boyb8begpx0hyq-n6qur47ks-lhg2boyb8begpx0hyq-n6qur47ks]] uses criticism, backlash, time cost, and the Maybach auction as cases where attention required adaptation rather than complete advance planning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is Zhou's retrospective account of his own media strategy. It does not measure sales lift, institutional trust, opportunity cost, or reputational harm, and it cannot establish that founder visibility is necessary or suitable for every company. A strong public persona may also crowd out teams, create succession risk, or make company trust depend excessively on one person.

## What Changed
- Established founder visibility as a cross-stakeholder communication capability with explicit operating and reputational limits.
