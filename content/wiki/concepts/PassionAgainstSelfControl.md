# Passion Against Self-Control / 激情与自我控制

Passion against self-control is the conflict in [[21-hong-yu-hei-ta-si-yu-zhencheng-569042001]] between strategic restraint and emotional force. The source uses [[WilliamSomersetMaugham|Maugham]]'s criticism of [[RedAndBlackNovel|《红与黑》]] as a way to sharpen the issue: if a reader prizes control and plausible self-interest, [[JulienSorel|Julien]]'s final break can look like a failure of construction; if a reader sees passion as central to the character, the break makes him intelligible.

The concept also organizes the novel's relationships. With [[MadameDeRenal|Madame de Renal]], conquest and pride give way to unguarded happiness. With [[MathildeDeLaMole|Mathilde]], passion is mixed with theatricality, jealousy, strategy, and aristocratic fantasy.

In the episode's title symbolism, red belongs to blood, passion, fantasy, and ambition, while black belongs to restraint, institution, control, and death. The novel's tragedy is that Julien needs both, and each destroys the other at decisive moments.

## Key Claims
- Self-control can be socially rewarded while making the person less able to receive sincere love.
- Passion can expose truth without becoming ethically clean.
- A critic's discomfort with a character's loss of control can reveal a deeper disagreement about what kind of literature is being read.
- Symbolic contrasts such as red/black work best when tied to character pressure rather than treated as a fixed code.
