# Occam's Razor

## Definition

Occam's Razor is a comparative reasoning rule: when multiple explanations fit the relevant evidence, prefer the one that does not multiply unsupported agents, mechanisms, powers, or exceptions.

## Current Synthesis

The wiki treats simplicity as a pressure on explanation, not as proof. In the UFO and pseudoscience branch, the razor works with observation, independent confirmation, falsifiability, and willingness to revise. A mundane account can outrank aliens or a cover-up when it explains the evidence with fewer unsupported assumptions, but it cannot erase contrary observations merely because it is simpler.

The Kennedy-assassination branch applies the same rule to a historical crime. The episode argues that [[LeeHarveyOswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]]'s prior violence, access, rifle, flight, killing of Tippit, and lies already explain the event without adding a coordinated institutional conspiracy. That is a comparative judgment about the evidence presented, not a shortcut around disputed facts or a guarantee that every official finding is correct.

## Key Claims

- Explanatory complexity needs evidence; additional hidden actors do not become likely merely because they make a story more dramatic.
- Simplicity matters only after relevant observations, alternatives, and counterevidence have been checked.
- A compact cumulative explanation can be stronger than a conspiracy that requires many unsupported links.
- The razor ranks explanations rather than proving one from simplicity alone.
- [[RationalHumility]] requires reopening the comparison if new evidence stops the simpler account from fitting.

### Historical-crime comparison

- [[398-jfk-the-mystery-is-solved-part-7-glt6409828878]] uses the cumulative Oswald case to argue that a lone-gunman explanation carries more evidentiary weight than a CIA-style conspiracy with no comparably grounded motive or chain.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Simple explanations can be false, and real conspiracies exist. The relevant question is not which story has fewer words, but which account explains the observations while making the fewest unsupported commitments. The Kennedy episode is a selective retrospective synthesis, so invoking the razor does not independently settle its ballistic, motive, or investigative claims.
