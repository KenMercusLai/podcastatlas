# Fiscal-Monetary Transmission Difference / 财政与货币传导差异

## Definition
Fiscal-monetary transmission difference is the distinction between government spending or transfers that directly alter private-sector income and central-bank actions that first change liquidity, financing conditions, rates, and asset markets.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses pandemic policy to separate channels that are often collapsed into “stimulus.” Cash payments and unemployment benefits can move quickly into household spending, while rate cuts, asset purchases, and liquidity operations first protect market functioning and borrowing conditions. Both can support demand, but their timing, recipients, distributional effects, and inflation or asset-price signatures differ.

## Key Claims
- Direct transfers can raise disposable income without waiting for new private borrowing.
- Unemployment and child benefits target household cash flow more directly than asset purchases do.
- Central-bank liquidity can prevent funding-market failure even when its household effect is indirect.
- Asset purchases and low rates may transmit through valuations and balance sheets before consumer prices.
- Fiscal and monetary policy interact, especially when large deficits are financed in a low-rate environment.

## Evidence
- Household channel: [[ep171-yiqing-xia-de-zhongmei-jingji-zhengce-faqian-ciji-xiaofei-yisheng-neng-yudao-jihui-ckwriueebny5abaaaacoc8to]] notes short-run consumption increases after direct payments and describes unemployment support as a substitute when ordinary coverage was inadequate.
- Financial channel: [[ep171-yiqing-xia-de-zhongmei-jingji-zhengce-faqian-ciji-xiaofei-yisheng-neng-yudao-jihui-ckwriueebny5abaaaacoc8to]] describes Federal Reserve liquidity, low rates, and government-bond purchases as safeguards against financial-system disruption.
- Joint effects: [[ep171-yiqing-xia-de-zhongmei-jingji-zhengce-faqian-ciji-xiaofei-yisheng-neng-yudao-jihui-ckwriueebny5abaaaacoc8to]] distinguishes consumer-price pressure, supply constraints, and asset inflation while warning that the channels can overlap.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The distinction is analytical, not absolute. Monetary policy can affect employment and consumption, fiscal spending can lift asset prices, and the source does not estimate channel size or causal lags.

## What Changed
- Added a transmission-based distinction between household-income support and financial-liquidity support.
