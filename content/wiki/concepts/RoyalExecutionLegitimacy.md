# Royal Execution Legitimacy

## Definition

Royal execution legitimacy is the problem of making a sovereign's killing legally, theologically, and politically tolerable. In the Mary Stuart sequence, it is the reason [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] can believe [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] is dangerous while still resisting her execution.

## Current Synthesis

The legitimacy problem appears early, not only at Fotheringhay. After Mary's arrival in England and the first conspiracy scares, [[WilliamCecil|William Cecil / Lord Burleigh]] wants Elizabeth to authorize execution, but Elizabeth resists because Mary is an anointed monarch. The later Babington episode supplies stronger evidence and legal machinery, yet the same problem remains: executing Mary removes a claimant but advertises that a sovereign can be killed through procedure.

The concept therefore sits between security necessity and monarchical self-protection. Law can make execution possible, but it can also make the precedent clearer and give enemies symbolic material.

## Key Claims

- A royal execution requires more than proof of danger because sovereign status creates a legitimacy barrier.
- Elizabeth's reluctance is a defense of monarchy as an institution, not only personal mercy toward Mary.
- Ministers try to convert execution from private vengeance into public law, but legal process also formalizes the precedent.
- Removing a claimant can intensify confessional war by producing martyr symbolism or foreign justification.

- Early execution dilemma: [[691-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-the-queens-spymaster-part-1-glt8312620561]] shows Cecil pressing for Mary's execution while Elizabeth resists because Mary is an anointed monarch.
- Later legal mechanism: [[696-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-killing-the-queen-of-scots-part-6-glt2334035496]] grounds the Act for the Queen's Safety, Fotheringhay trial, signed warrant, and council action around Mary.
- Precedent risk: [[691-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-the-queens-spymaster-part-1-glt8312620561]] and [[696-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-killing-the-queen-of-scots-part-6-glt2334035496]] both show Elizabeth fearing the precedent of killing an anointed queen.
- Escalation aftereffect: [[696-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-killing-the-queen-of-scots-part-6-glt2334035496]] links Mary's execution to martyr staging and the wider Armada path.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not imply that Elizabeth would never execute a royal rival. She eventually signs Mary's warrant after the Babington evidence path. The point is that the execution requires legal, symbolic, and political management because it threatens the sacred monarchy norm that Elizabeth herself needs.

## What Changed

- Adds the 1569-1571 execution dilemma as an early version of the later Fotheringhay problem.
- Clarifies that Cecil's security logic and Elizabeth's monarchical-legitimacy logic are both stable across the sequence.
