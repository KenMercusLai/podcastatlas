# Royal Execution Legitimacy

Royal execution legitimacy is the problem of making a sovereign's killing legally and politically tolerable. [[696-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-killing-the-queen-of-scots-part-6-glt2334035496]] uses [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]]'s hesitation over [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]] to show why the issue is not only guilt or innocence.

Executing Mary could remove a Catholic claimant, but it could also inflame Europe, justify [[Spain]]'s hostility, and establish the precedent that an anointed queen can be killed through legal and parliamentary procedure. The [[ActForTheQueensSafety|Act for the Queen's Safety]] therefore both solves and intensifies the problem: it supplies legal process while making the precedent explicit.
