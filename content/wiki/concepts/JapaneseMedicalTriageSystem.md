# Japanese Medical Triage System / 日本分诊制

The Japanese medical triage system is the clinic-first and referral-based service structure in [[vol-120-riben-yiliao-tixi-de-bengkui-yu-zhongsheng-yiyuan-yaopin-yibao-sanjiao-kunju-de-yizhong-jiefa-lp-p5p1cybbldphe0qajteggsitt]]. Patients commonly start at neighborhood clinics; large hospitals become more accessible when a doctor writes an introduction letter, while direct large-hospital visits without referral can incur extra charges.

The episode treats triage as a practical answer to [[JapaneseMedicalCollapse|medical collapse]], but not as a pure benefit. It reduces pressure on large hospitals and improves ordinary-care efficiency, while making emergency and holiday access feel worse to patients used to direct hospital access.

[[147-zaitan-riben-yiliao-yu-zhaohu-hangye-zhi-wo-ceng-zai-beihaidao-de-yiyuan-dang-hushi-lgokb-l-nvnqsstfzlqwhtnkqzhp]] adds a hospital-function comparison. [[QiongQiong|琼琼]] says Japanese hospitals are often differentiated by acute, recovery, and chronic functions rather than ranked mainly by an upward hierarchy. That functional split reduces some pressure for every institution and patient to chase the highest-status hospital, though it depends on trust that the floor is stable across regions and providers.

## Key Claims
- Triage protects scarce specialist and hospital capacity by routing common disease to clinics.
- The model works only when clinics, pharmacies, and referral pathways are dense enough to feel usable.
- Patient convenience and system efficiency are in tension, making [[HealthcareImpossibleTriangle]] visible.
- Function-based hospital roles can reduce top-hospital concentration when patients believe routine and chronic care have a reliable system floor.
