# Hidden Hearing Loss

## Definition
Hidden hearing loss is the episode's frame for auditory damage or functional difficulty that is not captured by standard quiet pure-tone thresholds, especially impaired speech understanding in noise, tinnitus, or excess listening effort after synaptic or neural injury.

## Current Synthesis
[[KonstantinaStankovic]] explains that apparent recovery after loud noise does not guarantee full biological recovery. Hair-cell-to-neuron synapses or auditory neurons can be permanently reduced while threshold audiometry remains within a conventional normal range. The deficit becomes more visible when the system must separate speech from competing sound rather than merely detect a tone in quiet.

The concept is a measurement boundary, not a self-diagnosis. Speech-in-noise testing may complement an audiogram, but tinnitus, difficulty in noise, asymmetry, or listening fatigue can have multiple causes and still require qualified evaluation. The source does not establish one validated population-wide test, biomarker, or treatment for every hidden deficit.

## Key Claims
- Normal pure-tone thresholds do not prove that cochlear synapses, auditory neurons, or complex listening function are intact.
- Loud-noise exposure can produce lasting synaptic injury even when a temporary threshold shift appears to resolve.
- Speech-in-noise difficulty can reveal functional loss that quiet tone detection misses.
- Tinnitus or unusual listening effort may coexist with normal threshold results but is not diagnostic by itself.
- Better classification may eventually combine functional testing, genetics, imaging, inner-ear biomarkers, and computational interpretation.

## Evidence
- Noise-recovery boundary: [[scim4605688764-scim4605688764]] describes permanent synaptic damage after apparent recovery from concert-related ringing or fullness.
- Test limitation: [[scim4605688764-scim4605688764]] contrasts standard audiometry with speech-in-noise testing and notes that many auditory neurons may be lost before thresholds change.
- Diagnostic future: [[scim4605688764-scim4605688764]] discusses liquid biopsy, genetics, improved imaging, and AI-assisted interpretation as possible subtype tools.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not provide a consensus diagnostic definition, validated screening cutoff, prevalence estimate, or treatment trial for hidden hearing loss. Speech-in-noise problems can reflect auditory, cognitive, attentional, linguistic, or environmental factors, and tinnitus is heterogeneous. The concept should not be used to infer damage from symptoms alone.

## What Changed
- Created a concept separating threshold detection from synaptic, neural, and speech-in-noise function.
