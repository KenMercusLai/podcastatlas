# Status-Conditioned Risk Taking / 地位条件化行动尺度

Status-conditioned risk taking / 地位条件化行动尺度 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-925-tuoxie-bushi-rensong-xuehui-tuoxie-lu-geng-changliang-limafqskgblrwetestyh39w-hzm4|Hanji 925]] uses to explain why the same action is not equally available to every actor. Starting point, family backing, office, and audience all change what a person can safely do and how the action will be read.

The episode applies the point to [[CaoCao|曹操]] as a young Luoyang official. Punishing [[JianShuoLateHan|蹇硕]]'s uncle after a curfew violation is dangerous because it touches a powerful eunuch family, but it is also survivable and useful because Cao Cao has background resources and needs to signal distance from the eunuch-family label. The result connects risk-taking to [[HighStatusLawEnforcementSignal|高位执法信号]] and reputation construction rather than to courage alone.

## Key Claims

- An action's risk cannot be judged apart from the actor's office, backing, and public need.
- High-status targets can make enforcement visible, but they also raise retaliation risk.
- A lower-status actor may need compromise or delay where a better-backed actor can take a sharper public action.
- The concept helps distinguish bravery, recklessness, and calibrated political signaling.
