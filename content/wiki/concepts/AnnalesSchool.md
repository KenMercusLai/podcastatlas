## Definition
The Annales School is a French historiographical tradition associated with problem-led research, varied evidence, ordinary and collective life, interdisciplinary explanation, and social processes extending beyond elite political events.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents Annales less as a fixed doctrine than as a continuing research orientation anchored in a journal, institutions, mentorship, and the refusal to let official archives or event chronology define history's whole field. [[MarcBloch|Marc Bloch]] and Lucien Febvre's early practice widened both subjects and sources: royal healing, rumor, prices, account books, medical texts, literature, wills, games, geography, psychology, and ordinary wartime experience could become evidence when a historical problem required them. Later organizational succession under Fernand Braudel supports speaking of generations, but the tradition's changing methods and internal disagreement make its boundary a historiographical construction rather than an uncontested lineage.

## Key Claims
- Historical questions should organize research rather than inherit their limits from one archive type or academic department.
- Political events and elite actors remain relevant but cannot explain society without economic, geographic, cultural, psychological, and material processes.
- Ordinary people, collective mentalities, and long-lived structures can be causal subjects rather than background detail.
- Diverse evidence becomes rigorous when its selection and interpretation answer a defined problem.
- Total history seeks relationships among domains, not an unbounded accumulation of facts.
- Annales has institutional continuity through its journal, positions, and mentorship, but no single timeless doctrine or universally accepted generational scheme.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Annales did not replace all empirical political history, and source expansion does not make every cross-disciplinary analogy sound. The episode offers an intellectual overview rather than a full historiography of the journal, internal disputes, national variants, or critiques of total and long-duration history. “School,” “movement,” and “tradition” emphasize different kinds of continuity and should not be treated as interchangeable proof of doctrinal unity.

## What Changed
- Established a bounded account of Annales as both a methodological orientation and an institutional lineage with contested borders.
