# Hong Kong-Mainland Integration Legacy / 中港融合遺產

## Definition
Hong Kong-mainland integration legacy is the source's frame for policies that brought Hong Kong and mainland China closer after the handover while also changing legal, economic, and social tensions.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents integration as ambivalent. CEPA and the individual-visit scheme helped stimulate Hong Kong after SARS, but the episode argues that they also helped lay groundwork for later cross-border tensions. The right-of-abode interpretation adds a legal version of the same dynamic: integration and support arrived with institutional dependence on mainland authority.

## Key Claims
- Tung-era integration policies delivered short-term economic support after crisis.
- The same policies could intensify later Hong Kong-mainland social and political tension.
- Legal interpretation and economic integration belong to the same broader reorder of post-handover relations.

## Evidence
- Economic support: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] says CEPA and individual visits stimulated Hong Kong after SARS.
- Later tension: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] argues these policies also buried seeds for later sharper Hong Kong-mainland conflict.
- Legal layer: [[dong-jianhua-shishi-aiguozhe-yuanlao-de-xianggang-rensheng-7d743a8515cf65334add1a55cd20148e]] treats the right-of-abode interpretation as an early institutional reorder.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not quantify CEPA or tourism benefits, separate mainland-visitor effects by sector, or compare alternative post-SARS recovery tools.
