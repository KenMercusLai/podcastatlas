## Definition
70mm film projection is large-format analog movie exhibition using physical film stock wider than 35mm; in this source it includes both standard 70mm projection and the distinct IMAX 70mm format.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats 70mm projection as a premium experience produced by format geometry, equipment, venue design, and skilled labor together. IMAX 70mm is described as horizontal 15-perforation film, while standard 70mm runs vertically with five perforations and is positioned as much larger than 35mm.

The practical constraint is scarcity. Demand for [[TheOdyssey]] in its intended large-format presentation meets a limited supply of equipped theaters, aging projectors, hard-to-source parts, and projectionists who know how to keep the system running. That makes [[SeventyMillimeterFilmProjection|70mm projection]] both a technical format and a venue-capability problem.

## Key Claims
- 70mm film projection depends on physical film handling, projector condition, booth setup, screen fit, and trained operators, not only on image resolution.
- IMAX 70mm and standard 70mm are different film systems, with different film orientation, perforation count, image shape, and theater requirements.
- Scarcity of equipped venues can turn a format into a release-window event when a major film is marketed around its intended presentation.
- Legacy maintenance relies on parts, tacit knowledge, and apprenticeship networks because old projectors require many mechanical components to work together.
- Standard 70mm offers a premium image over 35mm in the source, but the source also stresses that format value includes aspect ratio, venue, and communal experience.

## Evidence
- Format mechanics - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] distinguishes IMAX 70mm's horizontal 15-perforation film from standard 70mm's vertical five-perforation film and from digital IMAX.
- Image-quality basis - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] says standard 70mm is about four times the size of 35mm and offers more clarity, color, and overall image quality.
- Venue scarcity - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] frames The Odyssey ticket demand around scarce IMAX 70mm and non-IMAX 70mm theaters.
- Retrofit requirements - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] uses the Alex Theatre's projection, acoustics, screen, booth, budget, and schedule work as a concrete example.
- Maintenance labor - [[tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260827-0827-mp-tech-pod-128]] says old projectors depend on scarce parts and a Los Angeles network of older projectionists and apprentices.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a full technical standard for every 70mm or IMAX variant, and its market evidence is centered on one release and one theater. The claim that younger workers can replenish projection expertise is promising but anecdotal.
