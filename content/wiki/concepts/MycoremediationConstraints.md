# Mycoremediation Constraints

## Definition
Mycoremediation constraints are the contaminant, nutrient, organism, and field-condition limits that determine whether fungi can degrade, capture, transform, or only immobilize environmental pollution.

## Current Synthesis
The source describes fungi breaking down petroleum hydrocarbons and helping filter bacteria or particles from runoff. These are plausible, testable applications of extracellular digestion and mycelial structure. Their usefulness depends on whether the target compound is biologically accessible, whether fungi prefer easier available nutrients, whether temperature, moisture, oxygen, pH, and competing organisms permit activity, and whether breakdown products are actually safer.

The concept therefore distinguishes degradation from filtration and immobilization. Passing a selected environmental test in one demonstration does not establish removal of every contaminant or reliable field-scale performance. Persistent chemicals, dioxins, and radioactive material may resist degradation; for some hazards containment or immobilization is the more realistic goal.

## Key Claims
- Remediation performance is contaminant-specific rather than a general property of “mushrooms.”
- Laboratory or controlled-pile degradation may not survive changing field conditions and competing food sources.
- Filtration, biodegradation, transformation, and immobilization are different outcomes and should be measured separately.
- Disappearance of a parent compound is insufficient unless metabolites, toxicity, and mass balance are assessed.
- Persistent organic pollutants and radioactive contamination expose hard limits where containment may outperform attempted degradation.

## Evidence
- Hydrocarbon demonstration - [[scim1074623919-scim1074623919]] describes petroleum-contaminated material treated with mushroom mycelium and later passing an environmental test.
- Field limitation - [[scim1074623919-scim1074623919]] notes that fungi consume easier nutrients before harder contaminants.
- Filtration branch - [[scim1074623919-scim1074623919]] proposes mycelial systems for bacteria and particulate capture in agricultural runoff.
- Recalcitrant hazards - [[scim1074623919-scim1074623919]] acknowledges weak performance against some persistent chemicals, dioxins, and radioactive contamination.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied summary does not provide contaminant concentrations, controls, replication, metabolites, toxicity testing, timescale, cost, or field durability. Fungal biomass can concentrate hazards and itself require disposal. The episode motivates contaminant-specific engineering trials; it does not establish a universal cleanup method.
