## Definition

势利作为价值排序 / Snobbery as value ranking is the episode's reframing of "势利" from simple worship of money, power, and rank into the broader habit of sorting people by scarce valued traits: wealth, authority, intelligence, education, taste, appearance, fashion, moral standing, and values.

## Current Synthesis

The source argues that snobbery is uncomfortable because it is both morally suspect and socially functional. [[LuoYonghao|罗永浩]] begins from the claim that almost everyone has some form of snobbery, then expands the object from visible hierarchy to qualities people often treat as more legitimate, such as intelligence, rationality, taste, and morality.

The episode uses [[DunbarNumber|邓巴数]] to give the claim a resource-allocation base: because social attention is limited, people cannot maintain equal intimacy, time, patience, or opportunity for everyone. Snobbery becomes morally dangerous when this selectivity turns into contempt, humiliation, or rigid human worth ranking, but the episode resists pretending that all real-world decisions can be made without priority.

The concept is therefore not a defense of looking down on others. Its durable contribution is diagnostic: when people say they are not snobbish, the episode asks what they actually rank highly, whether that ranking is fair, and how much hidden contempt is attached to it.

## Key Claims

- Snobbery is not limited to money, power, and formal status; intelligence, education, taste, appearance, morality, and values can also become hierarchy markers.
- Limited attention and relationship capacity make selective treatment structurally unavoidable, even if contempt is not.
- Intelligence-based contempt is morally risky because ability differences may be partly unchosen.
- Moral superiority can become a snobbish hierarchy when it turns difference into worth-ranking rather than disagreement.
- The practical question is not whether humans ever rank others, but which ranking criteria they use, how rigidly, and with what degree of humility.

- Multi-dimensional definition: [[luoyonghao-de-x-zilukou-women-neng-huodao-jintian-duokui-le-zuchuan-de-shiliyan-lhkqsz8pkcblnmaebsz1m50tysos]] lists money-power-status, intelligence-learning-rationality, culture-taste, appearance-fashion, and moral values as snobbery dimensions.
- Resource boundary: [[luoyonghao-de-x-zilukou-women-neng-huodao-jintian-duokui-le-zuchuan-de-shiliyan-lhkqsz8pkcblnmaebsz1m50tysos]] invokes [[DunbarNumber|邓巴数]] to argue that people cannot allocate social energy equally.
- Self-critique: [[luoyonghao-de-x-zilukou-women-neng-huodao-jintian-duokui-le-zuchuan-de-shiliyan-lhkqsz8pkcblnmaebsz1m50tysos]] has Luo describe shame around "厌蠢" and broaden the critique to moral and values-based superiority.
- Scenario testing: [[luoyonghao-de-x-zilukou-women-neng-huodao-jintian-duokui-le-zuchuan-de-shiliyan-lhkqsz8pkcblnmaebsz1m50tysos]] uses survival, school-admission, career, workplace, and consumption dilemmas to reveal each participant's ranking criteria.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode is a comedy-panel discussion, not a systematic theory of class, status, cognition, or moral psychology. Its value is in exposing hidden ranking criteria through examples; it does not prove how common each type of snobbery is across society.
