## Definition
AI industry self-regulation is the use of industry-led standards, testing bodies, disclosure practices, ratings, or technical-review processes to govern AI systems before direct state rulemaking becomes the main control layer.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded sources distinguish standards-and-ratings, pre-release permissioning, plural independent auditors, and company-created oversight. Self-regulation looks strongest when it creates shared technical language, public evidence, incident learning, and multiple contestable evaluators. It looks weakest when firms grade themselves, one body becomes a gatekeeper, review lacks outside enforcement, or costly procedures exclude startups.

Webb's Facebook Oversight Board example sharpens the durability test: creating an ostensibly independent body does not establish adequate scale, authority, or permanence. The current judgment is conditional rather than simply pro- or anti-industry governance. Useful self-regulation needs broad representation, narrow scope, independent access, concrete engineering controls, durable accountability, and incentives that make compliance viable across differently resourced participants.

## Key Claims
- Shared standards and ratings have a different risk profile from pre-release permissioning and should not be treated as the same model.
- Broad startup and open-source representation is necessary because closed frontier labs can absorb heavier compliance costs.
- Plural independent evaluators reduce dependence on both company self-certification and a single captured gatekeeper.
- Review should be tied to concrete incidents, root-cause analysis, sandboxes, monitoring, regression testing, and release discipline.
- Company-created oversight lacks credibility when it has insufficient capacity, outside authority, evidence access, or institutional durability.
- Industry norms work only when incentives discourage defection and when evidence is public enough for meaningful outside critique.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Governments may lack the technical staff and operating cadence needed to evaluate frontier systems, so industry expertise is not optional. Aviation, pharmaceuticals, financial audit, and biotechnology are analogies rather than directly transferable templates. The Facebook example and claims about the board's capacity or closure remain source-attributed in this bounded input, and the sources do not specify a complete funding or enforcement model.

## What Changed
- Added institutional capacity, durability, and outside enforcement as explicit design tests.
- Added startup affordability to the existing representation requirement.
- Integrated company-created oversight as a distinct and weaker model of self-regulation.
