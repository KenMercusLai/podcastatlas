# Stuttering Speech Coordination

## Definition
Stuttering speech coordination is the episode's frame for stuttering as a disruption in the fluent motor coordination of speech rather than a deficit in language, intelligence, or ideas.

## Current Synthesis
[[EddieChang]] describes stuttering as a speech problem: meaning, grammar, and ideas can remain intact while words fail to come out smoothly. The source places the problem in the highly precise coordination of vocal-tract structures such as the larynx, lips, and jaw, with some cases involving difficulty initiating speech.

The episode also sets a causality boundary. Anxiety can trigger or worsen stuttering, but Chang does not treat anxiety as the root cause itself. Auditory feedback matters because changing what someone hears from their own speech can change fluency for better or worse, and speech therapy can create conditions or strategies that help words emerge.

## Key Claims
- Stuttering is framed as a speech coordination problem, not a language or intelligence problem.
- Ideas, meaning, and grammar can remain intact even when output is dysfluent.
- Fluent speech requires precise timing across the larynx, lips, jaw, and other vocal-tract structures.
- A predisposition to stutter does not mean a person stutters all the time.
- Anxiety can worsen stuttering without being the cause per se.
- Auditory feedback can alter stuttering, showing that perception and motor output interact.
- Speech therapy can help create conditions or strategies for more fluent output, while mechanisms remain unsettled.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source says the cause of stuttering is still not clear. It is public neuroscience education, not individualized diagnosis, speech therapy, psychological treatment, or a comprehensive review of stuttering subtypes and interventions.
