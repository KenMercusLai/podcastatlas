# Broken-Windows Policing

## Definition

Broken-windows policing is the strategy of treating visible disorder and minor offenses as signals and possible causes of more serious crime, often by increasing enforcement against low-level conduct.

## Current Synthesis

The episode separates two propositions that public debate often merges. Cleaner, safer, well-maintained streets can be valuable in themselves. That does not establish that arrests or stops for minor disorder caused New York's homicide decline.

[[MalcolmGladwell]] retracts his earlier endorsement of the stronger causal proposition after Stop and Frisk fell sharply without the predicted rise in homicide. The source therefore supports rejection of indiscriminate enforcement as a necessary crime-control mechanism while leaving the city's multicausal long-run decline unresolved.

The human and constitutional costs matter independently of aggregate effectiveness. [[DavidFloyd]]'s encounter illustrates humiliation, fear, and liberty risk imposed on people treated as suspicious, while low weapon-recovery rates challenge claims that millions of frisks were precise.

## Key Claims

- Public order as an intrinsic civic good is distinct from the claim that low-level arrests prevent serious violence.
- A policy's asserted necessity is weakened when its sharp contraction is not followed by the predicted harm.
- Mass stops distribute dignity, liberty, and constitutional costs across many people who are not found to possess weapons.
- Neighborhood-wide suspicion is a poor substitute for current, individualized evidence.
- Rejecting broad disorder enforcement does not identify every cause of crime decline or rule out lawful, proportionate responses to specific conduct.
- Literal repair of neglected places belongs to [[PlaceBasedViolencePrevention]], not automatically to coercive policing.

## Evidence

### Failed prediction

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-broken-windows-the-tipping-point-revisited-broken-windows]] reports that stops fell roughly 90–95 percent while New York homicide continued to fall.

### Human and enforcement costs

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-broken-windows-the-tipping-point-revisited-broken-windows]] connects [[DavidFloyd]]'s stop to 2.3 million frisks and a reported 1.5 percent weapon-recovery rate.

### Alternative interpretations

- [[the-tipping-point-revisited-broken-windows-the-tipping-point-revisited-broken-windows]] contrasts mass stops with [[ViolenceConcentration]] and vacant-lot repair.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode is a retrospective argument rather than a balanced review of broken-windows scholarship. Continued crime decline after policy contraction challenges a simple necessity claim but does not isolate the effects of every low-level enforcement practice, identify all concurrent changes, or establish that visible disorder never affects safety or public use. Comparative crime figures, stop totals, and trend estimates require the underlying definitions and datasets.

## What Changed

- Established the distinction between public-space order and the stronger causal claim for aggressive low-level enforcement.
- Made failed prediction, constitutional burden, and intervention precision part of the current judgment.
- Separated physical repair from arrest-centered use of the broken-windows metaphor.
