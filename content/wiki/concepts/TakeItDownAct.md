# Take It Down Act

The Take It Down Act is the U.S. law discussed in [[tech-20260109-0109-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260109-0109-mp-tech-pod-128]] as a response to non-consensual intimate image publication, including AI-generated material. [[MariaCurie|Maria Curi]] says the law comes into effect in May 2026 and prohibits publication of these kinds of images.

In the source, the law is important but not complete. It gives authorities a publication-focused enforcement route, while the episode says the [[DefianceAct|Defiance Act]] may fit some AI-generation cases better because it reaches creation and solicitation.

[[tech-20260727-0727-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260727-0727-mp-tech-pod-128]] treats the Take It Down Act as active federal context for nudify apps after its May 2026 effective date. The episode adds that law has to work alongside platform policies, app-store rules, state laws, and pressure on payment channels because [[NudifyAppEcosystem|nudify services]] can be promoted, installed, and paid for before a specific image-removal dispute reaches authorities.

## Key Claims
- The law is framed as a criminal-enforcement tool rather than primarily a private lawsuit tool.
- Publication is the central trigger discussed in the episode, which can leave earlier creation or solicitation behavior to other remedies.
- The law matters for [[AINonConsensualIntimateImageAbuse]] because AI systems can produce harmful intimate images even when no real photo was leaked.
- The episode connects enforcement interest to the [[USDepartmentOfJustice|U.S. Department of Justice]] and to lawmakers pressing for action.
- The July 27 source adds an ecosystem caveat: emerging AI companions, AI girlfriends, wearable devices, and smart devices may create adjacent abuse surfaces faster than statutory coverage can adapt.
