# Temperature Minimum Clock Shifting

## Definition
Temperature minimum clock shifting is the source's practical rule for moving sleep-wake timing earlier or later by placing light, caffeine, movement, food, and social cues before or after the body's estimated low-temperature point.

## Current Synthesis
The episode defines temperature minimum as roughly two hours before a person's typical wake-up time. It then uses that estimate to explain phase shifting: cues such as bright light, exercise, or caffeine in the two to four hours before the temperature minimum tend to delay the clock, while light, exercise, food, socializing, or movement after the minimum tend to advance it.

This concept matters because the same tool can have opposite effects depending on timing. Morning light after the minimum can help people wake and sleep earlier; similar stimulation before the minimum can push the rhythm later. The source applies this to jet lag, shift work, children, and unusually early wake-ups, while keeping red-light and middle-of-night wakefulness claims source-scoped.

The daily-tools episode uses the same estimate for performance rather than clock shifting alone. Huberman records wake time, treats the minimum as about two hours earlier, and proposes four to six hours after that point as a possible high-focus window. The stronger bounded conclusion is to use the estimate as a planning hypothesis and retain an earlier work time when direct experience shows it works better.

## Key Claims
- Temperature minimum is defined in the source as roughly two hours before typical wake-up time.
- Bright light, exercise, or caffeine in the two to four hours before temperature minimum tends to delay the clock.
- Bright light, exercise, food, socializing, or movement after temperature minimum tends to advance the clock.
- The rule can be used before or during travel to shift the clock earlier or later.
- The same timing logic is presented as relevant for shift work, children, and unusually early wake-ups.
- Red light is suggested for necessary middle-of-sleep-cycle wakefulness because the source says it offers visibility while appearing less disruptive to cortisol rhythm.
- Four to six hours after the estimated minimum is presented as a possible focus window, with observed personal performance taking priority.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not provide a validated individual circadian assessment, occupational safety plan, sleep-disorder treatment, pediatric sleep protocol, productivity trial, or travel medicine guide. The red-light claim, exact timing windows, focus window, and clock-shifting advice remain source-scoped and may not fit people with insomnia, bipolar disorder, medication effects, pregnancy, caregiving constraints, or safety-critical shift work.
