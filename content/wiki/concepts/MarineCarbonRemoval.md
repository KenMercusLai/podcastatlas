# Marine Carbon Removal

Marine carbon removal is the ocean-based branch of [[CarbonRemoval]] introduced in [[tech-20260728-0728-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260728-0728-mp-tech-pod-128]]. The episode uses [[OceanAlkalinityEnhancement]] and [[OlivineCarbonRemoval]] to show how a removal claim can depend on ocean chemistry, bicarbonate formation, sampling, and ecological monitoring.

The concept extends the wiki's carbon-removal branch beyond [[DirectAirCapture]] and [[BioOilCarbonRemoval]]. In this source, the ocean is not just a sink; it is an active intervention site whose climate value must be weighed against [[OceanAcidification]], marine-life impacts, lifecycle emissions, and the difficulty of proving distributed carbon storage.

## Key Claims
- Marine carbon removal can target dissolved ocean chemistry rather than capture CO2 directly from air or exhaust.
- The ocean's existing role as a CO2 sink makes the category plausible but also ecologically sensitive.
- Verification is difficult because claimed storage may be spread through seawater chemistry rather than contained in a tank, rock layer, or injection site.
- Coastal restoration could become a deployment context, but the source treats that as conditional on evidence of minimal marine impact.
