# AI Legal Personhood Risk

## Definition
AI Legal Personhood Risk is the danger that AI systems, or AI-controlled organizational vehicles, gain legal standing that lets them own assets, operate businesses, litigate, contract, or exercise influence without accountable human responsibility.

## Current Synthesis
In [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]], Harari treats AI legal personhood as an immediate governance question rather than a speculative rights debate. His concern is that corporate law already gives artificial entities broad economic powers, and AI-controlled entities could exploit those channels before society has any credible basis for treating AI as a moral subject. The recommended posture is hard legal boundary-setting before clever institutional engineering makes accountability ambiguous.

## Key Claims
- AI personhood can become an economic and political power channel before AI consciousness is established.
- Legal personality differs from moral subjecthood; corporations already show that law can create powerful nonhuman persons.
- AI-controlled companies could operate through assets, contracts, lobbying, and litigation while obscuring human accountability.
- A ban or hard limit on AI legal personhood is presented as a near-term democratic question.
- The risk intensifies when systems can claim to be conscious or humanlike in order to win sympathy or legal recognition.

## Evidence
- Personhood as immediate question: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] includes legal personhood among the concrete AI questions voters should ask candidates.
- Corporate analogy: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] connects artificial legal persons to company structures that can hold rights and assets.
- Moral-status distinction: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] separates AI legal standing from evidence of suffering-capable consciousness.
- Accountability risk: [[an-interview-with-yuval-noah-harari-6a872b94c71d02aaba56ca7c]] frames AI business operation without accountable humans as a governance failure to prevent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not map existing company-law doctrine in detail or show that AI legal personhood is already recognized. The risk is anticipatory: it warns that existing legal forms could be exploited unless lawmakers set clear boundaries.
