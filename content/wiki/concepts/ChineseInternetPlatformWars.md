# Chinese Internet Platform Wars / 中文互联网平台战争

## Definition
Chinese internet platform wars are high-intensity platform conflicts in which companies use client software, default settings, public accusation, lawsuits, alliances, media pressure, and sometimes user lock-in to protect or seize distribution.

## Current Synthesis
This concept enters the wiki through [[ZhouHongyi|周鸿祎]]'s career. The episode shows platform war as more than ordinary competition: the disputed assets are user desktops, browser defaults, search traffic, security labels, mobile channels, and public trust. Because those assets sit close to users, conflict can become personal, legal, reputational, and regulatory at the same time.

## Key Claims
- Entry-point control raises conflict intensity because a lost browser, search, security, or app-store position can cut off attention and monetization.
- Public moral language can become a competitive weapon, as seen when anti-rogue-software claims are used against rival plugins and toolbars.
- Product-level blocking or deletion can turn company conflict into a user-facing "choose one" crisis.
- Lawsuits and adverse judgments do not necessarily settle the broader platform conflict; they can become one layer in a longer distribution war.
- Founder persona can amplify platform war when public feuds, social media, and live appearances make business conflict part of personal brand.
- Platform wars may force strategic adaptation, such as openness, investment, platformization, or category migration after the immediate fight ends.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is grounded in a Zhou/360-centered source. It should not be generalized to every Chinese internet rivalry without additional sources, and some episode claims reflect public narratives, founder memory, or media shorthand rather than complete legal or technical records.

## What Changed
- Added a platform-war concept for conflicts where distribution, trust, legal strategy, and founder persona converge.
