# Extraction Promise Ethics / 撤离承诺伦理

Extraction promise ethics / 撤离承诺伦理 is the moral problem created when an intelligence service asks a source to keep taking mortal risk. [[81-jiandie-yu-pantu-shuwozhiyan-007-de-gushi-ruobaole-xia-693711209]] frames [[OperationPimlico]] as more than a clever escape plan: it is the visible promise that [[MI6]] would not abandon [[OlegGordievsky|Oleg Gordievsky]] if the [[KGB]] closed in.

The concept becomes politically sharp when the rescue threatens diplomacy. The episode says British officials worried that extraction through [[Finland]] could harm improving relations with the [[SovietUnion]], but [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] approved the operation because keeping faith with the source mattered. The same frame also preserves the human cost: the promise to the source did not erase harm to his wife, children, colleagues, or future life under protection.

## Key Claims
- Source handling creates obligations that cannot be reduced to later diplomatic convenience.
- A rescue promise needs practical infrastructure long before the emergency.
- Honoring the source can conflict with family safety, diplomatic timing, and institutional self-protection.
- An ethically justified extraction can still leave damaged relationships and lifelong risk.
