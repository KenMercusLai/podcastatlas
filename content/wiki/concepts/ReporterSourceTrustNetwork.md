# Reporter-Source Trust Network / 记者与信任网络

Reporter-source trust network / 记者与信任网络 is the episode's account of how reporting gets made through temporary help rather than only through a journalist's individual skill. In [[xianchang-xianchang-2025-nianzhong-teji-sanlian-nvjizhe-jiemi-xinwen-beihou-de-ku-yu-guang-loz-sggelhx166n2me8v6d8k9a3c]], [[WuLiwei|吴立伟]] summarizes this dependence as reporters relying on strangers' generosity, while [[QinSi|秦思]], [[ChengJing|程静]], and [[WeiZhaoyang|魏昭阳]] give field examples.

The network can include a student who becomes a Russian translator, Palestinian contacts who introduce [[AishaGaza|Aisha]], local environmental volunteers who drive into remote mountains, family members who keep telling a victim's story, and podcast listeners who suggest leads in comments. The concept matters because it makes [[JournalisticPublicness|journalistic publicness]] relational: public facts often emerge from fragile, negotiated, and emotionally costly cooperation.

## Key Claims
- Reporting access often depends on improvised trust between reporters, sources, translators, local volunteers, and audiences.
- A helper can contribute not only language or logistics, but also empathy that keeps an interview possible.
- Trust networks can deepen stories, but they also increase the reporter's responsibility not to betray or flatten sources.
- The concept links [[FieldworkAsKnowledgeMethod|fieldwork]] to [[DeepReportingEmotionalLabor|deep reporting emotional labor]].
