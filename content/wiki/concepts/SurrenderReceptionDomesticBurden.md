## Definition

归降接纳的内外成本冲突 / surrender reception domestic burden is the governance tension that arises when a state must make surrender credible to outsiders while the money, transport, labor, legal exceptions, or punishments used for reception damage legitimacy among its own population.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-314-1-xiongnu-wang-guijiang-wudi-weihe-nusha-500-hanren-ljb-ocshiauhvyra88aykkqlxkak|Hanji 314-1]] places both sides of the conflict in the reception of [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]]. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] has a strategic reason to display sincerity: inadequate transport or an ungenerous welcome could make surrender appear unsafe and damage state prestige. But the required vehicles, borrowed horses, rewards, and civilian service arrive after costly war and a strained treasury, so households experience the same display as another extraction.

The merchant case shows that the conflict is legal as well as fiscal. A frontier rule meant to restrict strategic exports is applied to local trade with surrendered Xiongnu already in Chang'an. [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] argues that subjects could not reasonably recognize this as prohibited export and that killing them protects the rule's “leaf” by injuring the state's own “branch.” Credible external incorporation therefore cannot be judged only by how the surrendering group is treated; it also depends on whether domestic burdens remain intelligible and proportionate.

## Key Claims

- Generous reception can make surrender credible and signal that submission will be honored.
- Reception costs become politically dangerous when they are shifted onto a war-exhausted population without consent or visible compensation.
- Diplomatic prestige and domestic legitimacy can diverge even when both are framed as state interests.
- Rules designed for hostile frontier exchange can become unjust when applied without attention to the changed status and location of surrendered people.
- A successful incorporation policy must account for transport, fiscal, labor, and legal costs together rather than treating ceremony as free.

## Evidence

Credibility and display:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-314-1-xiongnu-wang-guijiang-wudi-weihe-nusha-500-hanren-ljb-ocshiauhvyra88aykkqlxkak|Hanji 314-1]] presents Wudi's demand for vehicles and horses as an effort to show sincerity and preserve imperial standing before the surrendering population.

Domestic extraction:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-314-1-xiongnu-wang-guijiang-wudi-weihe-nusha-500-hanren-ljb-ocshiauhvyra88aykkqlxkak|Hanji 314-1]] says the treasury is strained, households hide horses, and civilians are compelled to serve the surrendered group.

Context-sensitive legality:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-314-1-xiongnu-wang-guijiang-wudi-weihe-nusha-500-hanren-ljb-ocshiauhvyra88aykkqlxkak|Hanji 314-1]] attributes to Ji An the argument that Chang'an merchants could not reasonably equate local trade with illicit export beyond the frontier.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current concept rests on one source and does not establish the optimal scale of reception, the actual strategic effect of generosity, the fiscal capacity available to Wudi, or whether less costly alternatives existed. It also does not show that every frontier restriction was unreasonable: weapons, money, and ordinary goods may create different security risks. The supplied source most clearly establishes death sentences, not that all sentences were carried out, and its account of legal meaning requires comparison with primary statutes and transmitted histories.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to join surrender credibility, reception logistics, civilian burden, and contextual law in one evaluative frame.
