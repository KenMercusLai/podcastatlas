# Potential as Growth Rate

## Definition
Potential as growth rate is the view that future capability is better inferred from how a person learns through obstacles than from their starting level of skill.

## Current Synthesis
The concept does not deny ability differences. It rejects the shortcut of treating early performance as a ceiling before motivation, opportunity, coaching, feedback, practice, and environmental fit have had time to operate. [[AdamGrant]] uses his late start in diving as an illustration of substantial improvement despite an unpromising initial profile.

The practical implication is to evaluate trajectory and learning conditions together. [[GrowthMindset]] supplies the belief that change is possible, while scaffolding supplies temporary support and [[AdviceSeekingForImprovement]] supplies information. A fair assessment asks both how the person responds and whether the environment offers real chances to improve.

## Key Claims
- Starting ability is an incomplete predictor of eventual capability.
- Progress through obstacles reveals learning capacity that a one-time performance can hide.
- Motivation, opportunity, coaching, feedback, and practice jointly shape observed growth.
- Temporary scaffolding can reveal capability before independent performance is possible.
- Evaluation should examine trajectory and conditions rather than romanticize effort alone.

## Evidence
- Starting-point critique - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] says people underestimate potential when they judge it mainly from initial ability.
- Growth illustration - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] uses Grant's diving experience to show substantial improvement after a late and difficult start.
- Context and support - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] pairs motivation with opportunity, coaching, feedback, scaffolding, and malleable work design.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Growth rate can vary by domain and life stage, and short-term improvement need not continue indefinitely. Retrospective success stories are selected and cannot prove a general rule. Structural barriers, health, time, resources, biological constraints, and poor instruction can limit observed progress, so the concept must not turn unequal opportunity into a character judgment.

## What Changed
- Established a trajectory-based definition of potential with explicit opportunity and constraint boundaries.
