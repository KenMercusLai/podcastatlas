# Potential as Growth Rate

## Definition
Potential as growth rate is the view that future capability is better inferred from how a person learns through obstacles than from their starting level of skill.

## Current Synthesis
The concept does not deny ability differences. It rejects the shortcut of treating early performance as a ceiling before motivation, opportunity, coaching, feedback, practice, and environmental fit have had time to operate. [[AdamGrant]] uses his late start in diving as an illustration of substantial improvement despite an unpromising initial profile.

The practical implication is to evaluate trajectory and learning conditions together. [[GrowthMindset]] supplies the belief that change is possible, while scaffolding supplies temporary support and [[AdviceSeekingForImprovement]] supplies information. A fair assessment asks both how the person responds and whether the environment offers real chances to improve. It also asks whether apparent natural talent is actually the visible endpoint of long practice and whether easy early success has left later obstacle-management skills untested.

## Key Claims
- Starting ability is an incomplete predictor of eventual capability.
- Progress through obstacles reveals learning capacity that a one-time performance can hide.
- Motivation, opportunity, coaching, feedback, and practice jointly shape observed growth.
- Temporary scaffolding can reveal capability before independent performance is possible.
- Evaluation should examine trajectory and conditions rather than romanticize effort alone.
- Peak performance without practice history can make developed skill look innate.
- Early success is not always an advantage if it prevents practice with mistakes and obstacles.

- Growth illustration - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] uses Grant's diving experience to show substantial improvement after a late and difficult start.
- Context and support - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] pairs motivation with opportunity, coaching, feedback, scaffolding, and malleable work design.
- Early judgment - [[unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant-unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant]] uses Grant's remedial-writing placement and weak diving start to show how initial performance can be mistaken for a ceiling.
- Hidden practice - [[unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant-unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant]] uses Martin Luther King Jr.'s accumulated speaking experience to qualify the appearance of effortless talent.
- Obstacle practice - [[unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant-unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant]] argues that easy early success can leave learners less prepared for later discomfort and mistakes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Growth rate can vary by domain and life stage, and short-term improvement need not continue indefinitely. Retrospective success stories are selected and cannot prove a general rule. Early struggle may build skills, reveal poor fit, or simply cause harm; early success can coexist with later resilience. Structural barriers, health, time, resources, biological constraints, and poor instruction can limit observed progress, so the concept must not turn unequal opportunity into a character judgment.

- Preserved the boundary that struggle is not inherently developmental.
