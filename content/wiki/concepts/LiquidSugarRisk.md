# Liquid Sugar Risk / 液体糖风险

## Definition
Liquid sugar risk is the episode's warning that sugars in drinks, juices, sweetened dairy, and flavored beverages can be consumed quickly, repeatedly, and with weaker chewing or satiety signals than structurally intact foods.

## Current Synthesis
The source makes liquid sugar the first practical reduction target. Fruit juice, milk tea, sweet beverages, sweetened coffee, lactic-acid drinks, sour drinks, kombucha, and sweetened yogurt may feel lighter or healthier than desserts, but the liquid format can bypass chewing and deliver sugar with less awareness. For juice, the issue is not that fruit is bad; it is that blending or squeezing changes food structure, fiber function, oxidation, and intake pace.

This concept also clarifies the fruit-versus-juice distinction. Whole fruit includes cell walls, peel, fiber, volume, and chewing time. Juice and concentrated juice can deliver glucose and fructose quickly, and the episode treats excess fructose as a liver-metabolism and fatty-liver risk boundary when consumed heavily.

## Key Claims
- Reducing liquid sugar is the episode's highest-priority sugar-control action.
- Juice is not nutritionally identical to whole fruit because juicing changes chewing, fiber, cell structure, and absorption pace.
- Health-coded beverages can still rely on sugar for palatability, especially sour or fermented-tasting drinks.
- Fructose from juice, honey, syrup, and fructose-glucose syrup should not be ignored just because it may look natural or have a lower immediate glucose response.
- Frequency matters: repeated small drinks can create meaningful sugar exposure even when any one serving feels minor.

- Health-halo beverages: [[vol-215-di-gi-fengmi-ling-zhetang-kangtangwan-zhejie-kongtangren-daodi-caile-duoshao-keng-ft-dashihua-ltb5lyaycaxoijiktbo4pxne2a5z]] names kombucha, yogurt, lactic-acid drinks, lemon juice, mixed fruit juice, and other sour or health-coded drinks as possible sugar carriers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not argue that fruit, all fermented drinks, or all dairy products are harmful. It also does not quantify dose thresholds. The risk claim is strongest for frequent, sweetened, quickly consumed beverages and for juice replacing whole fruit as a default habit.

## What Changed

- Separated whole fruit from juice and other liquid sugar carriers.
