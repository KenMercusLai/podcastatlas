# Psychedelic Identity Disruption

Psychedelic identity disruption is the episode's risk frame for profound psychological change after intense psychedelic experience. In [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-bryan-johnson-i-just-took-the-most-powerful-dose-of-dmt-in-the-world-heres-what-it-was-like-40626165]], the [[AllIn|All-In]] hosts ask [[BryanJohnson|Bryan Johnson]] whether [[5MeODMT|5-MeO-DMT]] can change a person's values, duties, relationships, religion, family commitments, or founder responsibilities so much that continuity becomes ethically unclear.

The source treats this as more than a bad-trip problem. Johnson says his own experience made him more committed to his work, but the hosts cite cases where people left companies, families, or obligations after intense psychedelics. That makes the concept adjacent to [[FounderRiskTaking]] and [[FounderRiskCalibration]]: a founder's private self-modification can become a governance and stakeholder risk if it changes priorities abruptly.

## Key Claims
- Psychedelic transformation can be experienced as healing, but it may also alter values and obligations.
- The source separates acute psychiatric danger from slower life-disruption risk.
- Investor or founder restrictions on psychedelics are mentioned as a possible governance response, not as an endorsed solution.
- Family, partners, employees, and investors can become affected parties when self-experimentation changes a person's commitments.
