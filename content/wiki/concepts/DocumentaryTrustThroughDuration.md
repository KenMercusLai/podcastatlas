# Documentary Trust Through Duration

## Definition
Documentary trust through duration is the method of reducing camera-reactive behavior by spending enough time with participants to build familiarity and relational confidence, rather than equating truth with covert capture or an absent filmmaker.

## Current Synthesis
The camera always enters a social situation, but its effect is not fixed. Repeated presence, demonstrated reliability, and a relationship that survives beyond a single extraction can make equipment less salient and let ordinary rhythms reappear. Duration is therefore part of the evidence method, not merely a production cost.

Trust does not make footage unmediated. Participants can still perform, withhold, misunderstand, or change their behavior, and filmmakers still select, frame, and edit. The stronger claim is narrower: sustained contact can reduce one important distortion and give the creator more contextual knowledge for interpreting what is filmed.

The method also has an industrial form. Research can identify behavior, seasons, locations, and likely events before a bounded shoot, while directors coordinate plans, budgets, and distributed crews. Planned production and patient observation are not opposites when research directs attention without predetermining what subjects must mean.

## Key Claims
- Camera presence affects behavior, but the size and form of that effect can change through familiarity.
- Time and relational trust can support less self-conscious observation without guaranteeing unperformed truth.
- Covert capture is not the defining criterion of documentary authenticity.
- Context learned off camera is necessary for responsible interpretation of observed behavior.
- Research and production planning can make observation more sustainable without replacing openness to the scene.
- Editorial construction, consent, power, and post-release harm remain after trust is established.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies a practitioner's retrospective rather than comparative research on participant behavior, consent, or finished-film accuracy. Long access may create dependence, familiarity bias, or unequal obligation as well as candor. A participant ignoring equipment does not establish that the creator understands the scene, that release is harmless, or that the edit is fair. Planned shoots can also pressure reality toward a proposal if schedule and budget become stronger than contradictory evidence.

## What Changed
- Established the initial synthesis separating reduced camera reactivity from claims of unmediated truth.
- Joined patient relational observation to research-led industrial production while preserving their ethical limits.
