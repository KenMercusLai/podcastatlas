## Definition

外圆内方式处世 / outer-round inner-square conduct is the source's name for principled adaptability: retaining a firm internal judgment while presenting flexible, non-frontal behavior in dangerous political settings.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 767 uses the phrase to clarify what "acting" means in political life. Smart performance is not presented as pure deception or shapeless smoothness; it is the ability to preserve judgment and principle while choosing a form of action that does not trigger unnecessary exposure.

The source's clearest case is [[LiHeEasternHan|Li He]]. He agrees to carry the gift to [[DouXianEasternHan|Dou Xian]], satisfying the outward form his superior fears to abandon, but delays the route until the political situation resolves. [[LiuQingQingheKing|Liu Qing]] supplies a quieter personal version: favor after the Dou purge does not make him careless because earlier family trauma taught him to remain cautious. [[DengXunEasternHan|Deng Xun]] and [[NieShangLateHan|Nie Shang]] form the boundary condition: flexible gestures work only when backed by real trust and judgment.

Hanji 329 moves the concept from tactical survival into speech. Its opening argues that honesty and inner edges do not require harsh wording; [[DiShanWesternHan|狄山]] then supplies a negative case when a policy dispute with [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] escalates into a personal attack on [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]]. The case supports tact as risk control, but Di's lethal frontier assignment also shows why communication style cannot absorb responsibility for coercive punishment or substitute for evaluating the underlying policy.

## Key Claims

- The concept separates principled adaptability from both rigid confrontation and unprincipled smoothness.
- Outward compliance can preserve inner judgment when the actor changes timing, exposure, or implementation.
- Delay is legitimate in this pattern only when it remains operationally defensible and attached to a real political reading.
- Caution after success or favor is treated as intelligence, not cowardice, when the surrounding field remains dangerous.
- The frontier contrast shows that flexible conduct without grounded trust assessment can become wishful exposure.
- Tact can preserve a principled disagreement, but poor delivery does not make coercive retaliation justified or the disputed policy false.

- Boundary case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-767-congming-ren-dou-shanchang-yanxi-lq3ol7y-jdh7dnnfkmtfdg4ibryq|Hanji 767]] contrasts Deng Xun's real frontier legitimacy with Nie Shang's failed, one-sided conciliation of Mi Tang.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is source-bounded and partly interpretive. Hanji 767 uses moralized language about "acting," but the concrete evidence is a cluster of episodes rather than a formal doctrine. Hanji 329's opening lesson is a modern framing of Di Shan's fate, not proof that abrasive speech caused or deserved his death. The pattern can also be confused with opportunism unless the "inner square" judgment is grounded in real risk, duty, or trust rather than convenience alone.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 767's opening conduct frame.
- Connected Li He, Liu Qing, Deng Xun, and Nie Shang as positive and negative cases of principled adaptability.
- Added speech delivery as a second domain while separating tact advice from victim-blaming under coercive authority.
