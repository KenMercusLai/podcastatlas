## Definition

边吏侵害引发反叛螺旋 / Frontier Official Abuse Rebellion Spiral is the pattern where abuse by a local frontier official turns a personal injury into fear of state punishment, then into collective violence and rebellion as surrounding groups decide that remaining passive is unsafe.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence is source-bounded to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-786-guren-weishenme-paichi-fanji-shuiguo-lmcwqwdxwe0ehuusfeoaqk82msm7|Hanji 786]]. In the episode's explanation of the first large Han-Qiang war after Ban Chao's death, a Han official in Anyi County seizes a Qiang woman as his wife. Her husband kills the official, and the county magistrate continues pursuing the killer.

The key mechanism is panic, not only revenge. The pursuit makes nearby Qiang people fear that the case will expand against them, so they kill the county magistrate and several tribes enter rebellion. The host uses the case to reject a single-cause account of Han-Qiang war: ethnic oppression matters, but local official abuse, fear of punishment, tribal security calculation, and later climate stress also belong in the explanation.

This page should stay narrower than [[RootCausePacification|Root-Cause Pacification]]. It does not prescribe the repair path. It names the escalation mechanism by which a frontier state creates rebellion pressure when local power violates households and then answers the fallout through pursuit rather than trusted adjudication.

## Key Claims

- Frontier disorder can begin with a concrete abuse by a local official rather than with a preexisting ethnic war plan.
- Household violation can become politically explosive when the abusive actor represents state authority.
- Official pursuit after the first killing can widen fear from one household to a whole local group.
- Panic can convert a defensive calculation into collective rebellion before the center fully understands the cause chain.
- The mechanism complicates simple ethnic-oppression explanations without denying that unequal frontier power is part of the background.

## Evidence

- Abuse trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-786-guren-weishenme-paichi-fanji-shuiguo-lmcwqwdxwe0ehuusfeoaqk82msm7|Hanji 786]] says a Han official in Anyi County seizes a Qiang woman as wife.
- First retaliation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-786-guren-weishenme-paichi-fanji-shuiguo-lmcwqwdxwe0ehuusfeoaqk82msm7|Hanji 786]] says the woman's husband kills the official.
- Panic widening: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-786-guren-weishenme-paichi-fanji-shuiguo-lmcwqwdxwe0ehuusfeoaqk82msm7|Hanji 786]] says continued pursuit by the county magistrate causes Qiang fear.
- Rebellion escalation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-786-guren-weishenme-paichi-fanji-shuiguo-lmcwqwdxwe0ehuusfeoaqk82msm7|Hanji 786]] says Qiang forces then kill the magistrate and several tribes rebel.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode presents this as one trigger in a larger Han-Qiang conflict, not as the whole cause of the wars. Climate cooling, official oppression, tribal migration, fear, and state military response remain broader factors, and the source explicitly reserves fuller climate-war analysis for the following episode.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 786's explanation of how local official abuse and pursuit can turn a Qiang household injury into broader rebellion.
