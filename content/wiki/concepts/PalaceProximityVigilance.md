## Definition

Palace proximity vigilance / 近侍警觉 is a court-security pattern in which trusted access is first earned through useful work and controlled conduct, then converted into continuous observation, boundary sensitivity, and rapid action when concealed danger becomes visible.

## Current Synthesis

The two bounded sources make proximity a formed capacity rather than a formal title alone. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-315-2-shasi-qin-erzi-de-ta-jing-chengle-huangdi-xinfu-lgxsxh75vhnle6_zplatmincwgrg|Hanji 315-2]] says [[JinMidiWesternHan|金日磾]] first attracts [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] through composure at a palace horse display and the quality of the animals under his care. Promotion gives him access, but the episode treats long-term competence, correct ritual behavior, and sustained self-command as what keeps that access credible.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-390-1-xihan-diyi-da-an-jinridi-ruhe-jiu-le-hanwudi-xingming-ls4snv189gvasy6yke7aw0kwhz8m|Hanji 390-1]] shows the security payoff. Jin detects abnormal behavior without turning suspicion immediately into accusation, stays near Wudi, and occupies the decisive threshold when Ma Heluo approaches the sleeping quarters with a hidden blade. Palace vigilance therefore combines earned trust, weak-signal reading, restraint, physical availability, and boundary enforcement; it is not merely bodyguard force or supernatural intuition.

## Key Claims

- Reliable close access is built through repeated useful performance and self-command before it becomes a security asset.
- Proximity creates informational advantage because attendants can notice small deviations before formal institutions react.
- Suspicion becomes useful when paired with restraint: quiet observation can preserve the ability to act without making an unsupported public charge.
- Palace intimacy is a risk surface because favor and informality can weaken boundaries around a vulnerable ruler.
- Household behavior can affect palace security when relatives also receive access through imperial favor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept rests on two narrative podcast sources about one figure. It does not prove that outsiders are generally more loyal, that silent surveillance is preferable to formal reporting, that informal attendants can replace guard institutions, or that violent household discipline is justified. Hanji 315-2 infers character from posture, horse care, and later conduct; Hanji 390-1 dramatizes Jin's detection of danger without settling every primary-source variant or the precise evidence behind his suspicion. The concept therefore describes the sources' trust-and-security logic, not a universal prescription.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept backward from assassination response to the competence and controlled conduct through which close access is first earned.
- Distinguished initial favor from durable trust: a striking first impression opens access, while repeated performance makes vigilance actionable.
