## Definition

Palace proximity vigilance / 近侍警觉 is the security and statecraft pattern in which a trusted close servant protects a ruler not mainly through formal command, but through continuous proximity, quiet observation, boundary sensitivity, and instant action when hidden danger becomes visible.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-390-1-xihan-diyi-da-an-jinridi-ruhe-jiu-le-hanwudi-xingming-ls4snv189gvasy6yke7aw0kwhz8m|Hanji 390-1]] defines the current concept through [[JinMidiWesternHan|金日磾]]'s handling of [[MaHeluoWesternHan|马何罗]]. Jin notices abnormal behavior but does not immediately turn suspicion into a public charge. Instead, he watches quietly, stays close to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], and places himself at the critical threshold of the sleeping quarters when the plot reaches its dangerous moment.

The concept is therefore not ordinary bodyguard force alone. It combines social reading, restraint, loyalty, physical access, and boundary enforcement. The same episode extends that boundary logic into Jin's household: his mother's discipline and his own harsh response to a son behaving improperly near palace women are presented as part of the same fear of blurred lines around imperial intimacy.

## Key Claims

- Close service can become a security advantage because the attendant observes small deviations before formal institutions react.
- Suspicion is most useful when paired with restraint: premature accusation can misfire, while silent proximity preserves the ability to act at the decisive point.
- Palace intimacy is dangerous because favor, access, and informality can lower boundaries around a vulnerable ruler.
- A trusted outsider may be especially useful when lacking a separate local power base makes betrayal less attractive.
- Household discipline can become political risk control when family members also move near imperial favor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept currently rests on one source and should remain narrow. It does not prove that outsider status always increases loyalty, that violent household discipline is normatively justified, or that informal close servants can replace formal guard systems. Hanji 390-1 dramatizes Jin's intuition and vigilance, but the precise evidentiary basis for his suspicion remains source-scoped.
