# Civilian Infrastructure Air Campaign

## Definition
Civilian infrastructure air campaign is the sustained use of drones and missiles against energy, transport, logistics, retail, water, heat, and population centers to weaken a country's economic capacity and social endurance behind a relatively stable front.

## Current Synthesis
[[drone-truth-russia-has-the-upper-hand-6aad03f98a1508074de8337e]] applies the pattern to [[Russia]]'s attacks across [[Ukraine]]. The strategic effect is cumulative: damaged services and supply chains can increase frustration and emigration, burden soldiers' families, distract troops, and interact with domestic political distrust even without a decisive territorial breakthrough.

## Key Claims
- Long-range strike advantage can matter even when the front line barely moves.
- Civilian, commercial, and utility targets connect military pressure to wartime economic endurance.
- Family hardship behind the front can affect morale and attention within the armed forces.
- Repeated infrastructure damage can combine with corruption concerns and political rivalry.
- The effect is conditional, not automatic; battlefield balance and social resilience can persist despite severe attacks.

## Evidence
### Infrastructure and supply-chain pressure
- [[drone-truth-russia-has-the-upper-hand-6aad03f98a1508074de8337e]] cites attacks on railways, warehouses, commuter trains, petrol stations, retail systems, heat, power, and water.

### Social and political transmission
- [[drone-truth-russia-has-the-upper-hand-6aad03f98a1508074de8337e]] connects worsening family conditions, emigration, corruption protests, and political opposition to the possibility of a reinforcing downward spiral.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The same source says Ukraine is still holding Russian forces back and calls the frontline innovation contest broadly balanced.
- Damage estimates and predictions of desertion or political spirals are uncertain, source-scoped assessments rather than established outcomes.

## What Changed
- Added a concept separating rear-area economic and social pressure from frontline territorial movement.
