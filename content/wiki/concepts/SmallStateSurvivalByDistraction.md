# Small-State Survival by Great-Power Distraction / 小国因大国无暇而存续

Small-state survival by great-power distraction / 小国因大国无暇而存续 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-2-kongzi-zai-luguo-de-shenshi-zhimi-lphy12exg4u7f-30qkir96gv634r]] uses to explain [[LuState|鲁国]]'s late survival. The episode stresses that Lu did not remain independent because it was militarily strong. Its fate sat in the priorities of larger states.

In the source's explanation, [[QinState|秦国]] and the Three Jin states, [[HanState|韩国]], [[ZhaoState|赵国]], and [[WeiState|魏国]], were consumed by the western war field. [[QiState|齐国]] was closest, but the damage from [[LeYi|乐毅]]'s campaign and [[HouSheng|后胜]]'s Qin-funded isolationism kept it from acting. [[YanState|燕国]] was too far away. That left [[ChuState|楚国]], under [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] and [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]], as the state with both capacity and room to destroy Lu.

This differs from [[SmallStateBufferDiplomacy|小国夹缝外交]]. A buffer state may switch patrons to survive. Lu's survival in this episode is more passive: the great powers have better or more urgent things to do until one of them no longer does.

## Key Claims
- Weak-state survival can be an artifact of other states' priorities, not a sign of internal strength.
- Geographic proximity does not guarantee conquest if the nearby power is weakened, bribed, isolated, or strategically distracted.
- A small state's final destroyer may be the state with available attention and expansion appetite, not the nearest or strongest state in the abstract.
- "Weak states have no diplomacy" in this source means their survival and death are externally timed.
