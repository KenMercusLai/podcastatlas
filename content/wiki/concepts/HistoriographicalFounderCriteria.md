# Historiographical Founder Criteria / 史学奠基者标准

## Definition

史学奠基者标准 is a framework for treating “father” or “founder” labels as judgments about what historical writing is supposed to accomplish, rather than as neutral titles awarded only by chronological priority.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded comparison identifies several non-equivalent bases for founding status. [[Herodotus]] represents inquiry, travel, attributed testimony, cultural range, and narrative preservation; [[Thucydides]] represents concentrated analysis of war, politics, speeches, and power; [[Confucius|孔子]] represents historical narrative as moral and canonical ordering; [[SimaQian|司马迁]] represents general history as investigation, biography, philosophical authorship, and personal judgment; and [[BanGuEasternHan|班固]] represents a more court-bound but teachable and repeatable dynastic form.

These candidates cannot be placed on one ladder without first choosing a criterion. Priority favors one answer, investigative method another, moral-cultural authority another, literary-philosophical originality another, and institutional reproducibility another. Cross-civilizational comparison is therefore most useful when it reveals the standards hidden inside the title and least useful when it forces distinct genres, offices, and political conditions into one genealogy.

## Key Claims

- A historiographical “founder” label is retrospective canon formation, not a self-evident fact supplied by chronology alone.
- Investigation, source attribution, political analysis, moral instruction, literary synthesis, and institutional reproducibility are distinct founding achievements.
- A highly original work can be less reproducible than a more standardized successor, so individual greatness and tradition-building may point to different figures.
- Claims of objectivity do not eliminate composition: selection, speech reconstruction, moral judgment, and silence remain authorial acts.
- Cross-cultural pairing can clarify shared questions while leaving genre, office, evidence base, and political setting non-equivalent.
- Later criticism of predecessors can help create a new historiographical identity, but “anxiety of influence” remains an interpretive analogy unless independently evidenced.

## Evidence

- Competing founder criteria: [[1-dongxifang-lishi-de-zushiye-cong-xiluoduode-dao-simaqian-lrpay8q4h2epyehgwgfvl_uj867]] compares five figures through inquiry, politics, moral purpose, authorial ambition, and repeatable form.
- Western comparison: [[1-dongxifang-lishi-de-zushiye-cong-xiluoduode-dao-simaqian-lrpay8q4h2epyehgwgfvl_uj867]] contrasts Herodotus's wide, attributed, ethnographic narrative with Thucydides's political-military concentration and reconstructed speeches.
- Chinese comparison: [[1-dongxifang-lishi-de-zushiye-cong-xiluoduode-dao-simaqian-lrpay8q4h2epyehgwgfvl_uj867]] distinguishes Confucian moral history, Sima Qian's general and personal synthesis, and Ban Gu's dynastic institutional model.
- Reproducibility criterion: [[1-dongxifang-lishi-de-zushiye-cong-xiluoduode-dao-simaqian-lrpay8q4h2epyehgwgfvl_uj867]] argues that Ban Gu's techniques may be easier to inherit even if Sima Qian's work is judged more singular.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The framework is derived from one comparative podcast episode rather than a complete Greek or Chinese historiographical survey. “Father of history” is itself a later reception label, and the Chinese search for an equivalent may import modern disciplinary assumptions into traditions that classified knowledge differently. The pairings are heuristic: Herodotus is not Confucius, Thucydides is not Ban Gu, and formal similarity does not establish direct influence or institutional equivalence. Biographical anecdotes, authorial motives, reconstructed speeches, and claims about what successors could imitate remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established founder labels as criterion-dependent judgments rather than a single chronological ranking.
- Separated individual originality from the ability to establish a reproducible historical-writing tradition.
