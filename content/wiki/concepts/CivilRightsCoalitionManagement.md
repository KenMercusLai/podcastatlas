# Civil-Rights Coalition Management

## Definition

Civil-rights coalition management is the work of turning organizations with different constituencies, tactics, vulnerabilities, and political relationships into coordinated collective action.

## Current Synthesis

The [[MarchOnWashington1963|1963 March on Washington]] shows that coalition success depends on more than agreement with a moral goal. [[APhilipRandolph|A. Philip Randolph]] supplied historical authority and a labor-movement strategy, while [[BayardRustin|Bayard Rustin]] coordinated organizations, transport, sanitation, food, scheduling, public discipline, and negotiation with officials who feared disorder.

The coalition also managed exposure. Communist associations, Rustin's sexuality, Kennedy administration caution, segregationist attack, white-moderate delay, and pressure from more radical Black activists all shaped who could be visible and what could be said. The peaceful march demonstrates organizational capacity, while the exclusion of women from speaking demonstrates that coordination can reproduce hierarchy even inside an equality movement.

## Key Claims

- Shared moral purpose does not eliminate differences in organization, constituency, ideology, or risk.
- Logistics are part of political strategy because transport, food, sanitation, timing, and crowd discipline determine whether mass action can express its intended meaning.
- Government cooperation can reduce operational risk without giving government actors control over a mobilization already likely to occur.
- Coalitions may protect vulnerable organizers by limiting visibility while still relying on their labor.
- Public nonviolence is an organizational achievement rather than only a declared principle.
- Coalition unity can coexist with internal exclusions that contradict the movement's public ideals.

## Evidence

### Authority, organization, and negotiation

- [[359-martin-luther-kings-dream-glt8593313865]] links Randolph's earlier march strategy, Rustin's operational planning, multiple civil-rights organizations, Kennedy administration negotiation, and official security preparation.

### Visibility and internal hierarchy

- [[359-martin-luther-kings-dream-glt8593313865]] describes attacks on Rustin's politics and sexuality, his reduced public role, and the denial of speaking roles to women including Rosa Parks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One successful march does not establish a universal coalition model or allow precise attribution among hundreds of organizers and participants. Cooperation with officials may constrain a movement as well as protect it, and strategic limits on visibility can reproduce stigma. The source is speech-centered and does not fully represent every participating organization or women organizers.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the march's combination of senior authority, detailed logistics, government negotiation, protected vulnerability, and internal exclusion.
