# Neurodegenerative Risk Levers

## Definition
Neurodegenerative risk levers are the source's cautious prevention inputs for Alzheimer disease, dementia, Parkinson's disease, Lewy body dementia, and related brain-health decline under genetic and mechanism uncertainty.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats Alzheimer's disease as the most common dementia and neurodegenerative disease, but it does not reduce brain aging to amyloid alone. [[PeterAttia]] discusses APOE isoforms, deterministic early-onset genes, vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, Parkinson's disease, and the possibility that amyloid beta has been overemphasized.

The practical levers are conservative: sleep, lower LDL/ApoB, insulin sensitivity, exercise, and avoiding repeated head injury. These overlap with cardiovascular and metabolic prevention rather than forming a separate brain-hack protocol. The concept should therefore preserve uncertainty around biomarkers and anti-amyloid therapies while making ordinary vascular, metabolic, sleep, exercise, and injury-prevention levers visible.

## Key Claims
- Alzheimer disease is treated as the most common dementia and neurodegenerative disease in the source.
- APOE4 raises risk but is not deterministic; rare genes such as PSEN1, PSEN2, and APP can be deterministic for early-onset disease.
- Vascular dementia and Alzheimer-like symptoms keep cardiovascular prevention relevant to cognitive health.
- The source says amyloid beta may have been overemphasized and many anti-amyloid therapies have underperformed expectations.
- Sleep, LDL/ApoB lowering, insulin sensitivity, and exercise are presented as clearer levers than speculative supplements.
- Repeated head injury avoidance is framed as highly plausible brain-health protection, especially for susceptible people.

## Evidence
- Disease map: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] identifies Alzheimer's disease, Lewy body dementia, Parkinson's disease, and vascular dementia as major neurodegenerative or dementia concerns.
- Genetics: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] explains E3/E3, E3/E4, E4 risk, and rare deterministic genes such as PSEN1, PSEN2, and APP.
- Amyloid boundary: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] says the field may have overemphasized amyloid beta and that many anti-amyloid therapies have not shown expected efficacy.
- Prevention levers: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] names sleep, lower LDL/ApoB, insulin sensitivity, and exercise as clearer brain-health levers.
- Head-injury branch: [[improve-vitality-emotional-physical-health-lifespan-dr-peter-attia-scim5907126956]] treats repeated head hits from sports, crashes, biking, construction, and military exposure as important to avoid.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove that any one lifestyle or biomarker intervention prevents dementia for an individual. APOE, amyloid biomarkers, vascular risk, sleep, exercise, traumatic brain injury, supplements, hyperbaric oxygen, and medication decisions require individualized clinical context.
