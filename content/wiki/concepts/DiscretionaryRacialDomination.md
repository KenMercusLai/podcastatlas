# Discretionary Racial Domination

## Definition

Discretionary racial domination is a system in which variable enforcement, exceptions, informal judgment, and selective leniency preserve a racial hierarchy without requiring every case to receive the same outcome.

## Current Synthesis

[[hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville-hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville|Hitler’s Olympics, Part 8]] uses [[LisaLindquistDorr]]'s Virginia case research to show that Jim Crow did not operate as one automatic penalty. Accused Black men faced outcomes ranging from lynching, execution, and long imprisonment to short sentences, acquittal, pardon, or parole. White testimony, gender hierarchy, class, labor dependence, local interests, and prosecutorial or judicial choice could alter a case.

Variation did not dissolve domination. The capacity to make exceptions helped white authorities manage cross-racial labor, service, consumption, sex, and community relations while retaining the power to punish. Flexibility can therefore be a source of resilience: it absorbs contradictions and local needs without conceding equal status.

The concept also explains part of [[HeinrichKrieger]]'s divided reaction. American discretion offered ways around impossible biological precision, but its extralegal violence and inconsistent administration offended his preference for centralized, orderly racial policy.

## Key Claims

- Unequal systems do not require uniform enforcement to remain systemic.
- Exceptions can stabilize hierarchy by accommodating labor, gender, class, and local interests.
- Formal prosecution, extralegal violence, acquittal, and leniency can coexist within one governing order.
- Discretion allocates power to officials and dominant-group witnesses even when it produces relief in particular cases.
- Administrative critics may oppose disorder while preserving the underlying oppressive objective.

## Evidence

- Outcome variation: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville-hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville]] reports Dorr's distribution across killings, execution, prison terms, acquittal, pardon, and parole.
- Case-level flexibility: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville-hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville]] describes white testimony overturning William Harper's initial death sentence.
- Political-economic durability: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville-hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville]] links exceptions to white dependence on Black labor, consumption, and domestic service.
- Comparative reaction: [[hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville-hitler-s-olympics-part-8-vater-it-is-to-be-fayetteville]] contrasts usable classification discretion with Krieger's objection to uncontrolled lynching.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Variable case outcomes do not show that Jim Crow was benign, fair, or weak; acquittal in an individual case could coexist with disfranchisement, segregation, terror, and unequal authority. The supplied statistics cover a particular Virginia accusation category and period, not every Southern jurisdiction or form of racial control. The concept should not turn the agency of defendants, witnesses, lawyers, or communities into mere system maintenance, nor assume that every exception was consciously designed to stabilize hierarchy.

## What Changed

- Established enforcement flexibility as a possible mechanism of hierarchical durability rather than evidence against systemic domination.
- Integrated racial, gender, class, labor, and local authority into the explanation of variable outcomes.
