## Definition

主动让位式风险转移 / strategic position yielding is the deliberate surrender of formal precedence to recognize another actor's merit while reducing one's own exposure and moving the more visible political burden onto the higher-ranked position.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-1-lmw61vriwsknnyex3cdrbdlusafd|Hanji 208 part 1]] applies the pattern to [[ChenPing|陈平]] before [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s accession ceremony. Chen says [[ZhouBo|周勃]] deserves the senior chancellorship because Zhou's anti-Lü merit exceeds his own, and Liu Heng accepts the reordered precedence.

The concession has two simultaneous meanings. It publicly aligns title with recent merit, reducing an immediate ranking grievance, while the host argues that it also lowers Chen's visibility in the unstable relationship between a new emperor and the veterans who enabled his accession. This second meaning is plausible because the source places Chen's move after Zhou's awkward presentation of the imperial seal, but the episode does not directly prove Chen's private intent.

The concept is therefore narrower than generic humility or retirement. Chen does not leave government; he accepts a secondary office and remains useful. The move changes who stands in the most conspicuous position while preserving Chen's access, adaptability, and capacity for later office.

## Key Claims

- A concession can recognize another actor's merit and alter political exposure at the same time.
- Yielding rank is distinct from leaving office when the actor preserves access and operating capacity.
- The highest visible position may carry extra danger during an unsettled succession or coalition transition.
- Strategic interpretations require evidence separate from the observable act of yielding precedence.
- Successful yielding depends on the concession remaining publicly legible as legitimate rather than evasive.

## Evidence

Formal concession and merit rationale:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-1-lmw61vriwsknnyex3cdrbdlusafd|Hanji 208 part 1]] says Chen Ping asks Liu Heng to place Zhou Bo above him because Zhou's anti-Lü merit is greater.

Preserved office and altered exposure:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-1-lmw61vriwsknnyex3cdrbdlusafd|Hanji 208 part 1]] reports Zhou as right chancellor and Chen as left chancellor, so the change is a reordering inside government rather than retirement.

Risk-transfer interpretation:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-208-yi-tui-wei-jin-chenping-de-houheixue-1-lmw61vriwsknnyex3cdrbdlusafd|Hanji 208 part 1]] links the concession to the new ruler's tension with accession-making veterans and to Zhou's earlier role in presenting the seal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode directly establishes the request, stated merit rationale, and office ordering, but not Chen Ping's private strategy. Respect for Zhou's achievement, avoidance of rivalry, institutional convention, self-protection, and calculated risk transfer can coexist. The source also does not show that the concession itself caused Zhou's later exposure or Chen's survival. The concept should not turn every act of deference into concealed manipulation.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the Chen Ping–Zhou Bo chancellorial-order case.
- Separated the observable merit concession from the host's inferred risk-transfer motive.
