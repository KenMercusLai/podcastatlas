## Definition

Nuclear risk externalization is the pattern named from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-3-shenwa-lishi-heweiji-weihe-dou-fasheng-zai-riben-lspztkfaaq0hcq1no3yum1xdox8a]] in which a nuclear accident's aftermath is framed as being shifted from the responsible institution or country onto wider publics, ecosystems, or future generations.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 394-3 develops the concept through the [[FukushimaDaiichiNuclearPowerPlant|Fukushima Daiichi]] discharge dispute. The host's core claim is not only that nuclear contamination is dangerous, but that disposing of contaminated water into the sea converts a local accident-management problem into a global and intergenerational burden.

The concept differs from generic externality because the source stresses invisibility, duration, and fear. Nuclear risk is hard for ordinary people to inspect directly, can outlast political decision cycles, and can produce public reactions far beyond the accident site. The episode's China salt-buying example is used as a fear-transmission case, while the foreign-ministry quotation supplies the explicit language of risk transfer.

## Key Claims

- Nuclear-risk externalization converts accident aftermath into burdens borne by outsiders, including neighboring publics, ocean ecosystems, and future generations.
- The Fukushima discharge controversy is the source's main example of risk transfer, but the argument depends on a larger memory chain of nuclear harm.
- Invisible or long-lived hazards make externalization politically tempting because the damage is hard for ordinary publics to verify in real time.
- Public fear can travel faster and wider than the physical hazard, making risk communication part of the externalized burden.

## Evidence

- Fukushima discharge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-3-shenwa-lishi-heweiji-weihe-dou-fasheng-zai-riben-lspztkfaaq0hcq1no3yum1xdox8a]] says Japan's discharge sends nuclear-contaminated water into the sea and frames that as transferring risk to the world.
- Regional fear: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-3-shenwa-lishi-heweiji-weihe-dou-fasheng-zai-riben-lspztkfaaq0hcq1no3yum1xdox8a]] recalls panic buying in Japan and a salt-buying episode in China after Fukushima to show cross-border anxiety.
- Intergenerational language: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-394-3-shenwa-lishi-heweiji-weihe-dou-fasheng-zai-riben-lspztkfaaq0hcq1no3yum1xdox8a]] quotes diplomatic criticism that the discharge extends pain to humanity's descendants.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is currently a source-scoped concept from one critical podcast episode. It does not incorporate the Japanese government's technical defense, international monitoring claims, radiological dose modeling, ocean-dilution arguments, fisheries evidence, or international-law analysis. The concept is therefore best used as a wiki frame for the episode's moral and political argument, not as a settled scientific assessment of Fukushima discharge risk.
