## Definition
Naturalized player policy in Chinese football is the attempt to improve national-team performance by recruiting or converting foreign-born players into Chinese national-team-eligible players, often through club-level spending and registration work.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat Chinese football naturalization as a short-term national-team tool with a difficult institutional legacy. In the Evergrande branch, recruitment is tied to political demand, club-carried costs, and incentives that do not fit normal club ROI. Vol.227 nevertheless argues that naturalization remains pragmatically relevant when the existing domestic-player pool cannot quickly raise the national team's ceiling. The combined judgment is conditional use with transparent responsibility, not abandonment of league and youth development.

## Key Claims
- Naturalized-player recruitment can be a national-team shortcut when domestic player development is too slow for administrative timelines.
- Club-level spending can hide or carry public national-team objectives.
- Costs and incentives become difficult to evaluate when the buyer is formally a club but the benefit is expected at national-team level.
- Naturalization policy does not remove the need for youth-development and professional-league health.
- The policy's legacy should be evaluated separately from ordinary foreign-player recruitment.
- Earlier disappointing execution can justify administrative caution without proving that every future naturalization route is unsound.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not supply a full policy history, eligibility analysis, independent cost audit, or counterfactual performance study. They do not establish that any named candidate can or will qualify, nor that naturalization would produce a specific result. Short-term recruitment can also distract from development reform if treated as a substitute rather than a complement.
