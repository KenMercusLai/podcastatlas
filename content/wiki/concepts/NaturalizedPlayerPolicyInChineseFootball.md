## Definition
Naturalized player policy in Chinese football is the attempt to improve national-team performance by recruiting or converting foreign-born players into Chinese national-team-eligible players, often through club-level spending and registration work.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats Chinese football naturalization as a boundary case rather than a standard transfer-market strategy. In the Evergrande branch, naturalized-player recruitment is tied to national-team urgency, political demand, and unusually high costs that do not fit normal club ROI. It therefore belongs beside [[ClubNationalTeamBoundaryBlur]] rather than only beside player-market openness.

## Key Claims
- Naturalized-player recruitment can be a national-team shortcut when domestic player development is too slow for administrative timelines.
- Club-level spending can hide or carry public national-team objectives.
- Costs and incentives become difficult to evaluate when the buyer is formally a club but the benefit is expected at national-team level.
- Naturalization policy does not remove the need for youth-development and professional-league health.
- The policy's legacy should be evaluated separately from ordinary foreign-player recruitment.

## Evidence
- Player list: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] discusses Alan, Luo Guofu, Fernandinho, Elkeson, Xiao Taotao, and other naturalization-related players.
- Cost frame: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] says financial reports showed more than 800 million yuan in costs around several Brazilian-player naturalizations.
- Political-demand interpretation: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] has Pan Weili describe the spending as political demand rather than ordinary professional-football logic.
- Separation principle: [[vol-225-hengda-de-zuqiu-yichan-ft-panweili-gkwrijeozrpkafcpbatn-ivb]] frames club and national-team football as categories that should remain distinct.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not supply a full policy history or independent cost audit. It also does not establish whether any individual naturalized player helped or hurt team performance; the claim is about institutional incentives and spending logic.
