# Fair Comparison Frames

Fair comparison frames are the episode's rule for making comparison less distorting: compare against a relevant peer group, time horizon, metric, and context. In [[ep-3-demystifying-the-imposter-syndrome]], [[SamDataScienceWithSam|Sam]] uses computer vision posts on LinkedIn and his YouTube subscriber counts as examples of how public achievement signals can create [[ImpostorSyndrome|imposter feelings]] when the comparison set is unfair.

[[StephenMathis|Stephen Mathis]] adds a workplace version. Passing actuarial exams faster than a colleague once made him feel superior, but he later recognized that exam speed measured one task, not total professional ability. The same comparison error can create inferiority or inflated ego depending on which side of the metric someone is on.

The concept does not eliminate comparison. It narrows comparison until it becomes diagnostic: people who started at a similar time, do similar work, or face similar constraints may provide useful information; celebrities, veterans, viral outliers, and coworkers measured on one dimension often do not.

## Key Claims
- Comparison becomes misleading when the benchmark has a different time horizon, resource base, or field context.
- One metric rarely represents the whole person or the whole job.
- Social media increases comparison pressure by making outlier achievement highly visible.
- The fairest endpoint of comparison is often [[ProgressTrackingSelfAssessment|self-comparison over time]].
