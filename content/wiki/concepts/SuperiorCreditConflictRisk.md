## Definition
Superior credit conflict risk is the danger that a subordinate's real contribution becomes politically damaging when it is asserted in direct competition with a superior, patron, or superior-linked powerholder who controls recognition, protection, reporting channels, or punishment.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now has three connected variants. In [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]]'s case, he and [[MaXianLateHan|Ma Xian]] had real battlefield merit against Langmo, but [[DengZunLateHan|Deng Zun]] received decisive court credit after arranging Langmo's assassination. Once Ren Shang competed for credit against a Deng-family actor, unrelated or adjacent vulnerabilities such as kill-count inflation, embezzlement, and gift-giving became instruments for punishment.

The sharper public-speech variant comes through [[XuYouLateHan|Xu You]]. Xu You's [[GuanduCampaign|Guandu]] contribution was real, but after [[CaoCao|Cao Cao]] takes [[JizhouLateHan|Jizhou]], Xu You publicly claims Cao could not have obtained the province without him and calls Cao by the familiar "曹阿瞒" name. The concept therefore covers both disputed allocation of credit and the status signal created when credit is asserted in front of an audience. It does not mean that subordinates should never seek recognition; it describes the exposure created when recognition claims collide with superior-linked political power or publicly diminish the authority that controls reward and punishment.

Hanji 1065 adds the time-decay and role-boundary variant. It argues that old contribution loses protective value as the superior's current need changes, while public familiarity, entitlement, or threat posture can remain visible. In that frame, superior credit conflict is one failure inside [[HighMeritThreatManagement|high merit threat management]]: the subordinate not only claims contribution, but also forces an audience to compare the superior's authority with the subordinate's past indispensability.

## Key Claims
- Credit risk depends on hierarchy as much as on factual contribution.
- Incomplete victories increase ambiguity, allowing another actor to claim decisive completion.
- Superior-linked credit competition can convert administrative irregularities into existential threats.
- Gifts, informal access, and household ties can widen a command dispute into a family-political scandal.
- Public boasting can turn real contribution into a visible authority challenge when the subordinate implies the superior's success depends on him.
- Old merit decays as protection when the superior's current need changes, but public entitlement can keep the perceived threat alive.
- Modern workplace advice in the episode is a source-scoped analogy, not a timeless rule to surrender all credit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not prove that Ren Shang's accusations were fabricated, nor do they establish that all superior-linked credit claims are illegitimate. Xu You's case also comes through a moralized host reading that emphasizes character and workplace boundary lessons; Cao Cao's own coercive power remains part of the structure. Hanji 1065's safety formula is a source-scoped heuristic rather than a measurable rule. The concept should be used narrowly: it concerns recognition claims under asymmetric power, especially when a superior or patronage bloc can frame the subordinate's ordinary vulnerabilities, public words, or informal access as punishable offenses.
