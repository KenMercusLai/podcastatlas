## Definition
Colonial category afterlife is the persistence of territorial partitions, ethnic classifications, institutional rules, and economic positions after formal colonial rule ends.

## Current Synthesis
[[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] argues that decolonization does not automatically remove the categories through which colonial power governed. Successor states may inherit borders and administrative identities because they need a workable territory and legal order, while communities continue to live with forced separation and unequal positions in the global economy.

The episode uses India-Pakistan partition, Israel-Palestine, and Rwanda as interpretive examples. Its deeper claim is that apparent ethnic or religious conflict should not end analysis: researchers should ask how governance hardened categories, who controls the political-economic structure, and why postcolonial actors may reproduce inherited arrangements.

## Key Claims
- Formal independence can leave colonial borders, classifications, and institutions intact.
- Administrative categories can harden previously overlapping religious, ethnic, or local identities.
- Successor states may rely on colonial territorial forms to secure their own legitimacy.
- Cultural reconciliation is limited when underlying political-economic hierarchy remains unchanged.
- Explaining inequality as group character obscures colonial and economic structure.

## Evidence
- Partition case: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] presents India-Pakistan partition as a colonial proposal implemented through mass sorting and migration by successor governments.
- Rwanda case: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] treats Hutu-Tutsi hardening and later reconciliation as evidence that classifications are politically made and difficult to undo.
- Palestine case: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] argues that a partition plan cannot be judged only from later outcomes without asking who designed it and why those affected would accept it.
- Economic afterlife: [[405-cong-dili-bianjie-dao-siwei-bianjie-women-ruhe-bei-huajie-suzao-16a80abc]] says many postcolonial states entered world production in low-value positions with limited room to build autonomous industry.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode offers a broad comparative interpretation, not detailed histories of each case. Colonial inheritance is not a total explanation: local political choices, class interests, institutions, violence, and later international interventions also matter. The source's positive account of Rwanda and mixed account of South Africa should remain source-scoped.
