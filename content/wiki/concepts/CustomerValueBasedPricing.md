# Customer Value-Based Pricing / 消费者价值定价

## Definition
Customer value-based pricing is the 4P pricing logic that sets and explains price according to the value created for the customer, rather than only by cost-plus markup or competitor comparison.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki evidence places price inside producer surplus, consumer surplus, unrealized surplus, delivery quality, and relationship durability. The marketing job is not simply extracting more money; it is making real value legible while preserving trust and a retention path. [[AdButler]] adds an enterprise case: pricing can remain many orders of magnitude above small-business plans while deliberately stopping short of maximum extraction so the vendor can fund strong delivery and become the customer's default partner. Value-based pricing therefore requires judgment about both willingness to pay and the long-run value split.

## Key Claims
- Price should be judged against the customer's perceived and real value, not only against production cost.
- A small technical improvement can justify a much larger premium if it changes a high-value customer workflow.
- Marketing can surface unrealized surplus by making hidden value legible to consumers.
- Price promotions need a retention answer before they are launched.
- Value-based pricing becomes ethically fragile if the seller manufactures fear or exploits information asymmetry instead of clarifying real benefits.
- Enterprise value pricing can favor durable account economics over maximizing the initial contract when service quality, expansion, and voluntary retention matter.

## Evidence
- Surplus frame: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] explains producer surplus, consumer surplus, and unrealized surplus as the context for pricing.
- Keyboard example: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] argues that a 10% typing-speed improvement can be worth far more than a 10% price premium to heavy writers.
- Promotion-retention link: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] uses music-membership discounts to ask how low-price-acquired users will be retained after the first year.
- Ethical boundary: [[ep85-yingxiao-guanli-gkwrimaoomq-bvejtas8o50a]] connects pricing to broader [[MarketingEthics]] concerns such as price discrimination, manipulation, and respect for consumers.
- Enterprise relationship pricing: [[b765334b]] says [[AdButler]] charges enough to support healthy margins and service but avoids pursuing every possible dollar, using customer value and continued choice rather than contract lock-in as the desired retention mechanism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source gives a quantitative pricing model or enough data to compare realized value with price. The AdButler source does not disclose enterprise contract values, margins, expansion revenue, cohort definitions, or independently verified retention. Value-based language can still rationalize extraction unless sellers distinguish genuine customer benefit from information asymmetry, switching costs, and dependence.

## What Changed
- Extended the framework from consumer surplus and promotions into enterprise relationship pricing.
- Added a distinction between pricing for sustainable delivery and extracting the maximum available contract value.
- Made voluntary retention and long-run account value part of the pricing judgment.
