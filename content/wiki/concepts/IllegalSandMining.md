# Illegal Sand Mining

[[IllegalSandMining]] is the extraction of sand outside lawful permission, often from beaches, rivers, or other hard-to-police environments. [[sand-heists-and-property-rights-in-the-caribbean-summer-school]] grounds the concept in [[Jamaica]], where locals describe trucks moving sand at night and [[MichaelHilton]] investigates a stolen beach.

The source shows why the crime is hard to solve. Sand is bulky but fungible enough to be spread across beaches or construction sites, witnesses may face threats, and small governments may not have the money or capacity to monitor open-access spaces continuously.

## Key Claims
- Illegal sand extraction can be profitable because sand is a high-demand input for tourism, construction, and land creation.
- Enforcement fails when the extraction site is vast, evidence is physically mixed, and buyers can absorb stolen material into ordinary development.
- The social cost is environmental and institutional: communities lose beaches while law enforcement struggles to prove origin and responsibility.
