# Succession Non-Designation Risk / 未定继承人风险

## Definition
Succession non-designation risk / 未定继承人风险 is the exposure created when a ruler or founder leaves no public, legible, and enforceable successor. The danger grows when rival claimants, armed bases, palace access, private preference, or document custody let factions manufacture the transition after the incumbent can no longer control it.

## Current Synthesis
Across the source set, non-designation exports an incumbent's unresolved choice into factional conflict. Wei Wuhou's silence produces claimant war and foreign intervention; Qin Wuwang's sonless death requires force-backed settlement; Chu Kaolie Wang's heir problem opens space for hidden pregnancy substitution and later household destruction; Emperor Ling's private preference and deathbed trust fail to prevent immediate palace violence; and Yuan Shao's private preference plus provincial deployments gives ambiguity armed form that Cao Cao can exploit.

Qin Shi Huang now supplies a particularly sharp mechanism. The episode attributes prolonged non-designation partly to his refusal to hear death-related discussion, making ministers unable to raise succession safely. When he finally orders a letter summoning Fusu, hope of recovery delays dispatch and leaves the credential with Zhao Gao. The case shows that a last-minute intention is not an effective designation unless it is delivered, authenticated, accepted, and enforceable. The Zhao Zheng Shu variant prevents the wiki from treating the received forged-edict narrative as uncontested, but both traditions reveal the cost of entering the deathbed without a settled public handoff.

## Key Claims
- Non-designation can preserve the incumbent's discretion while transferring conflict to the transition.
- Private preference, oral trust, or an undelivered letter does not substitute for public and enforceable designation.
- Physical proximity, document custody, seal access, and control of the dying ruler's information circle can decide which succession story becomes actionable.
- Distributed military or provincial bases give rival claims coercive capacity and invite outside intervention.
- Advisers' fear of retaliation can cause them to manufacture, harden, or enforce a succession story for self-protection.
- Even an explicit designation remains vulnerable when elite acceptance and immediate enforcement are absent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Not every succession conflict begins in non-designation, and naming an heir does not guarantee compliance. The Zhao Xiangzi case shows the separate enforcement problem. Qin Shi Huang's death-avoidance explanation is the episode's psychological interpretation, while the Zhao Zheng Shu account disputes the received sequence of post-death forgery. The Yuan and Wei cases also depend on provincial bases, adviser interests, outside armies, and prior family arrangements; silence is an enabling condition, not a complete cause.

## What Changed
- Added death avoidance as a court-silencing mechanism that can postpone succession discussion.
- Added the undelivered-letter variant: expressed intention remains politically weak until dispatch, authentication, acceptance, and enforcement.
- Added document custody and seal access to the transition-control mechanisms.
- Reframed the Qin source conflict around a shared finding: no stable public handoff existed before the deathbed emergency.
