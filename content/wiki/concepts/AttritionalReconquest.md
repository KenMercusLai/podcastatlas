# Attritional Reconquest

## Definition

Attritional reconquest is the recovery of occupied or ceded territory by rebuilding state capacity, sustaining pressure, exploiting local allegiance and legal openings, and avoiding the decisive battles in which the opponent is strongest.

## Current Synthesis

The episode associates the pattern with [[CharlesVOfFrance|Charles V]] and [[BertrandDuGuesclin|Bertrand du Guesclin]]. Charles restores revenue, order, and local loyalty, while du Guesclin declines open battle and makes English possession progressively harder to sustain.

This approach reverses the logic of Crécy and Poitiers. France does not need one symmetrical triumph if English courts, garrisons, debts, raids, taxation, illness, and long communications steadily weaken control. Aquitanian appeals provide a legal-political opening, while English chevauchées fail to restore territorial authority.

## Key Claims

- State rebuilding can be a military strategy when revenue and loyalty determine territorial endurance.
- Refusing decisive battle denies a tactically strong opponent its preferred path to victory.
- Local appeals and allegiance can matter as much as field-army destruction.
- Time, debt, illness, garrison costs, and damaged territory can erode an occupier's gains.
- Raids may display reach without reversing a loss of administrative control.

## Evidence

- State capacity: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] credits Charles V with restoring order, revenue, and loyalty.
- Battle avoidance: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] describes du Guesclin's preference for attrition over open battle.
- Legal opening: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] links Aquitanian tax appeals to renewed French intervention.
- English decline: [[321-hundred-years-war-a-storm-of-swords-part-4-glt1570526345]] records collapsing positions and a ruinous 1373 chevauchée.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source compresses multiple campaigns and should not make attrition a complete explanation. English fiscal choices, illness, succession, local politics, diplomacy, and individual operations may carry independent causal weight. Avoiding battle is useful here because it is combined with positive state recovery, not because passivity alone wins territory.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to model Charles V and du Guesclin's state-backed recovery strategy.
