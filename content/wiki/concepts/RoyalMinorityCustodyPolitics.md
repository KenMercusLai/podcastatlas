# Royal Minority Custody Politics

## Definition

Royal minority custody politics is the struggle to convert physical access to a child monarch into control of appointments, information, ceremony, force, and government while the heir holds legal title but cannot rule independently.

## Current Synthesis

The Edward V crisis shows that custody politics begins before accession. Edward IV's decision to establish the heir at Ludlow under Anthony Woodville joined education and regional preparation to future affinity power. A clear hereditary succession therefore did not settle practical authority: guardianship, Richard of Gloucester's protector claim, the queen's sanctuary, council deliberation, retinue limits, coronation timing, and Tower lodging were contests over who could speak and act through the young king.

## Key Claims

- A child monarch's legal title can coexist with severe dependence on whoever controls physical access and trusted advisers.
- Pre-accession household design can prepare an heir while also positioning a guardian family to exercise future influence.
- Custody becomes political infrastructure because it shapes information, patronage, public appearance, council consent, and armed protection.
- Coronation timing and location can either convert inherited title into public authority or preserve an opening for displacement.
- Sanctuary and rival household networks can protect alternative access, but their force depends on broader institutional and military support.
- Claims of protection can become mechanisms of exclusion when the protector controls the heir while criminalizing rival guardians.

## Evidence

- Household formation: [[138-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-1-glt6274021473]] describes Edward V's Ludlow offices, moral and educational regime, and guardianship by Anthony Woodville.
- Dispersed authority: both [[138-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-1-glt6274021473]] and [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] place Edward V with Anthony Woodville, Elizabeth Woodville in London, and Richard in the North when Edward IV dies.
- Seizure and isolation: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] follows the arrests at Stony Stratford, postponed coronation, Tower lodging, and dismissal of the princes' servants.
- Sanctuary and access: [[139-the-princes-in-the-tower-part-2-glt4685691538]] treats Elizabeth's protection and eventual transfer of Richard of Shrewsbury as part of the same custody struggle.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Custody is not sufficient by itself: Richard also needed council compliance, armed retainers, executions, preaching, illegitimacy claims, and coronation. Nor should every guardianship be reduced to a cynical power grab; Edward V's household was also an educational and administrative institution. The framework should not imply that every royal minority ends in usurpation or that legal title has no independent force.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept backward from the 1483 seizure to pre-accession household formation and regional preparation.
