# Frankenstein Complex in AI Cinema / AI电影中的弗兰肯斯坦情结

## Definition
Frankenstein complex in AI cinema is the recurring story pattern where humans create artificial life or intelligence, then fear that the created being will gain autonomy, rebel, replace its maker, expose human moral failure, or demand responsibility from the creator.

## Current Synthesis
[[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] uses [[Frankenstein|《弗兰肯斯坦》]] as the root of AI film anxiety, but its point is not only "machines are dangerous." The source argues that later film history often shifts Mary Shelley's parent-responsibility problem into fears of automation, slave revolt, rationalized rule, android replacement, simulation, and posthuman judgment.

The current synthesis is that the Frankenstein complex is a mirror. It reveals less about what current AI systems are and more about how humans imagine creation, labor, power, parenthood, and responsibility when their tools begin to look back.

## Key Claims
- AI cinema inherits a creation-responsibility problem from [[Frankenstein|《弗兰肯斯坦》]], even when the artificial being is a robot, android, computer, or software companion.
- The source distinguishes the original creator-responsibility emphasis from later adaptations that often blame the creature or machine.
- Robot-rebellion stories become morally charged because humans first build robots as servants or slaves.
- Android and replica stories sharpen the anxiety by making artificial beings visually or emotionally close to humans.
- The complex remains culturally useful even when current LLMs lack the subjectivity and self-preserving goals common in movie AI.

## Evidence
- Source pattern: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] explicitly names the Frankenstein thread as an old source for AI-film imagination.
- Responsibility shift: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] contrasts Mary Shelley's parent-responsibility frame with later readings that emphasize forbidden god-playing or machine danger.
- Robot labor relation: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] treats Metropolis and R.U.R.-style robot stories as power relations in which humans invent artificial servants.
- Android replacement: [[dianying-li-de-ai-qingren-juying-he-ducai-zhe-gkwriuen9djgazhkswsh-d-b]] uses Alien, Blade Runner, The Stepford Wives, [[AIArtificialIntelligenceFilm|《人工智能》]], and [[ExMachinaFilm|《机械姬》]] to show the "like human or human" problem.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a film-philosophy interpretation, not a technical prediction. It explicitly warns that science fiction imagines "what if" scenarios and that present large language models are not the same as film AIs with agency, goals, and self-preservation.
