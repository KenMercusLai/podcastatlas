# Embodied Taste

Embodied taste is the source's account of taste as a human relationship to the world rather than only a preference ranking. In [[tech-20260813-0813-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260813-0813-mp-tech-pod-128]], [[SophieHagney]] says taste is shaped by upbringing, sociology, social media, bodily experience, consciousness, attention, and accumulated encounters with physical objects and cultural environments.

The concept matters because it sets a boundary around [[AITasteSimulation]]. A model can learn from the traces of human judgment and produce outputs that look more tasteful, but Hagney argues that it does not have the body, history, or independent response to the world that make human taste more than a statistical average.

## Key Claims
- Taste is formed through lived exposure, sensory experience, social setting, and cultural timing.
- Human taste can recognize value before it becomes obvious or fashionable.
- Scarcity and discovery are part of how taste becomes socially meaningful.
- Embodied taste can change as styles become saturated, stale, or associated with low effort.
- AI can reproduce signs of taste without sharing the lived conditions that produced them.
