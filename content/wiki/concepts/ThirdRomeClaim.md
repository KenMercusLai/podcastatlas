# Third Rome Claim

## Definition

The Third Rome claim is the doctrine that Roman Christian imperial authority moved from Rome to Constantinople and then to Moscow, with no legitimate fourth successor.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents Moscow's succession claim as spiritual, dynastic, and Orthodox rather than primarily architectural or classical. Philotheus's early-sixteenth-century formula, Byzantine-linked ancestry around Vasili's court, the title tsar, and translatio imperii made Muscovy a stronger self-conscious claimant than powers merely using Roman republican imagery.

Crimea adds sacred geography to the doctrine through Chersonesus, Greek and Roman antiquity, Byzantine Christianity, and the baptism tradition of Vladimir of Kiev. The source then traces possible echoes through Catherine the Great, a probably mythical Stalin icon story, Putin-era cultural production, and Lavrov's succession rhetoric. These modern applications are evidence of political memory and self-presentation, not proof that one doctrine determines Russian policy.

## Key Claims

- Moscow's claim depends on the transfer of Christian imperial authority from first Rome through Constantinople.
- Orthodoxy, dynasty, and the title tsar matter more to this succession than classical architecture.
- Crimea can function as sacred-historical connective tissue among antiquity, Byzantine Christianity, and Rus.
- Modern Russian actors may reuse Third Rome language without sharing one coherent doctrine.
- Symbolic inheritance can shape rhetoric without providing a sufficient cause of geopolitical action.

## Evidence

- Succession formula - [[157-byzantium-and-the-ghosts-of-rome-glt1014708592]] attributes the “two Romes” formula to Philotheus in 1510 and connects it to Vasili's Byzantine lineage.
- Imperial translation - [[157-byzantium-and-the-ghosts-of-rome-glt1014708592]] links tsar and translatio imperii to movement of authority from Rome to Constantinople to Moscow.
- Crimean memory - [[157-byzantium-and-the-ghosts-of-rome-glt1014708592]] connects Chersonesus, Vladimir's baptism, Catherine's annexation rhetoric, and classical-Byzantine geography.
- Modern reuse - [[157-byzantium-and-the-ghosts-of-rome-glt1014708592]] records film, clerical, and diplomatic examples while treating their reach and influence cautiously.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not show that all Russian rulers, institutions, or citizens accepted one continuous Third Rome program. The Stalin icon-flight story is described as probably mythical, influence around Putin is inferred rather than demonstrated, and Crimea's symbolism does not independently explain annexation or war. The United States and France also adopted Roman forms, but the episode distinguishes aesthetic republican reference from an organic Orthodox succession claim.

## What Changed

- Established a bounded doctrine separating Orthodox-dynastic succession from generic Roman imagery.
