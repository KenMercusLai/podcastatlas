# Spy Network Entrapment

[[692-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-massacre-in-paris-part-2-glt4665799025]] adds a precursor rather than a full controlled-channel trap. [[FrancisWalsingham]] builds a wider intelligence system after the [[StBartholomewsDayMassacre]], and [[CharlesSled]]'s disclosure of Catholic names, pensions, and descriptions shows how informant dossiers could prepare the ground before later embassy penetration and controlled correspondence channels.

[[694-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-a-treacherous-conspiracy-part-4-glt1627515087]] adds the embassy-penetration prelude to the later controlled-channel case. [[FrancisWalsingham]] has moles inside [[MichelDeCastelnau]]'s French embassy, obtains copies of [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]' cipher correspondence, and tracks [[FrancisThrockmorton]] before arresting him during the [[ThrockmortonPlot]].

Spy network entrapment is the intelligence pattern surfaced by [[696-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-killing-the-queen-of-scots-part-6-glt2334035496]]: a target believes a communication channel is secure while the channel is actually controlled, read, and shaped by hostile intelligence officers. In the episode, [[FrancisWalsingham]]'s network uses [[GilbertGifford]], the beer-barrel route at [[ChartleyManor]], and [[ThomasPhillips]]'s cryptanalysis to expose the [[BabingtonPlot]].

The concept matters because it turns latent suspicion into admissible political evidence. [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]' reply becomes dangerous not only because of what it says, but because the intelligence operation can preserve, decrypt, copy, and deliver it into the [[ActForTheQueensSafety|Act for the Queen's Safety]] process.
