# Good Life As Happy Together / 好的生活是 happy together

## Definition
Good life as happy together / 好的生活是 happy together is Xie Jin's episode-level answer that a good life is being able to live happily with many people, without building one's happiness on consumption, exploitation, hierarchy, or other people's diminished lives.

## Current Synthesis
Near the end of [[shi-di-fu-shuo-464-xiejin-women-zhichi-pingdeng-dan-moren-butongderen-peide-butongdeshenghuo-lsYpOsseeWaQIMMSv6Q2lyGsOCSw]], [[XieJinPhilosopher|谢金 / Xie Jin]] says her current answer to the good-life question is simple because it came after subtraction. She describes moving away from high standards inherited from British education, elitism, and meritocracy, and toward a relational standard: whether one can be happy together with many people.

The concept is not a retreat into private niceness. It directly criticizes happiness built through consumption, money-making, and buying as if those practices were morally neutral. The episode links personal good life to resource use and harm: a life cannot be fully good if it depends on making others less happy, less safe, or less able to participate in public life.

## Key Claims
- A good life is relational rather than only individual achievement or self-optimization.
- Meritocratic, elite, and consumerist standards can make life feel unnecessarily high-stakes and extractive.
- Happiness gained through consumption, waste, or exploitation is morally unstable.
- A good life should not require other people to be unhappy or excluded.
- The idea depends on vulnerability and recognition because people must be seen as humans before they can be happy together.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is a philosophical and ethical orientation, not a complete welfare metric. It does not claim that money, consumption, achievement, or individual ambition are always bad. Its boundary is that they cannot be the sole definition of good life when they rely on exclusion, waste, or harm.
