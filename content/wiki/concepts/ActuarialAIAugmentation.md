# Actuarial AI Augmentation

## Definition
Actuarial AI augmentation is the use of AI and automation to accelerate research, coding, data preparation, reporting, assumption checking, and customer operations while actuaries retain responsibility for validation, interpretation, regulated decisions, and professional sign-off.

## Current Synthesis
Across the sources, augmentation is not merely faster answer generation. The durable model joins AI literacy and governed infrastructure to [[ActuarialWorkflowInversion]]: machines take more routine preparation and retrieval work, while actuaries spend more time on risk meaning, alternatives, business consequences, and accountable judgment. This shift remains constrained by traceability, hallucination, bias, privacy, law, data quality, and the risk that automation creates more output than professionals can safely review.

## Key Claims
- AI can compress terminology lookup, coding assistance, reporting, routine data work, and internal knowledge retrieval.
- The strongest work-design gain is reallocating actuarial time from preparation toward interpretation, exploration, and decision support.
- Faster output does not remove the need to verify sources, assumptions, calculations, traceability, and factual accuracy.
- Insurance AI must pass legal, privacy, bias, actionability, and professional-accountability gates before affecting production decisions.
- AI literacy can improve career leverage, but domain expertise and creativity remain necessary for identifying useful questions and judging consequences.
- Governed business logic, APIs, and human review determine whether isolated productivity gains become reliable organizational capability.

## Evidence
### Career and knowledge-work assistance
- [[ep-10-a-thought-provoking-chat-with-an-actuary-and-tedx-speaker]] presents AI as a career amplifier for terminology search, coding, onboarding, and future retrieval from proprietary knowledge, while warning about hallucination and missing traceability.

### Infrastructure and production governance
- [[ep-11-growing-technology-footprints-in-insurance-sector]] adds chatbot, report, formula, and BI support within privacy, security, legal, statistical, bias, and operational controls; it also connects reusable spreadsheet logic to governed APIs.

### Time allocation and professional judgment
- [[ep-18-insurance-transformed-an-actuarys-take-on-ai]] adds the inverted actuarial time pyramid: automate data preparation and routine processing so experts can interpret results, explore options, and evaluate implications.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The sources are practitioner interviews and do not provide controlled productivity measurements, error rates, workforce outcomes, or audited implementation results.
- Automating data preparation can conceal rather than eliminate data-quality problems; domain review is still required.
- Generative systems can produce average or plausible outputs that are poorly grounded, so throughput is not equivalent to insight.
- Career gains and labor-market effects remain forecasts rather than demonstrated outcomes.
