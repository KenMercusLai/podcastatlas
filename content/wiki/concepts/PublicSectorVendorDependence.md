# Public-Sector Vendor Dependence

## Definition

Public-sector vendor dependence is the risk that a government agency becomes strategically or operationally reliant on a supplier whose systems, expertise, contracts, or data access are hard to replace.

## Current Synthesis

The source grounds the concept through [[Palantir]] in British public services. Dependence is not simply that a vendor is foreign or controversial. It appears when public agencies need the tool to keep services productive, lack equivalent domestic alternatives, and would face switching, procurement, security, or service-delivery costs if the relationship ended.

The episode's strongest point is that dependence can coexist with genuine usefulness. The [[NHS]] and [[MetropolitanPolice|Metropolitan Police]] examples show that a system can solve real workflow problems while still creating lock-in, evidence-quality, lobbying, and [[DigitalSovereignty]] concerns.

## Key Claims

- Vendor dependence grows when the supplier controls workflows, integrations, data models, and operational know-how.
- Public agencies face a higher legitimacy burden because citizens cannot always choose a different provider of health, policing, or military protection.
- Lack of domestic alternatives can make sovereignty arguments difficult to act on even when they are analytically real.
- Break clauses, open competition, and procurement scrutiny are partial controls, not automatic solutions.
- Dependence can be underestimated when immediate productivity gains dominate political attention.

## Evidence

- Lock-in concern: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] names vendor lock-in as a major objection to Palantir in Britain.
- Alternative gap: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] says critics want homegrown alternatives, but Georgia Banjo says Britain does not currently have equivalent domestic capability.
- Break-clause pressure: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] says a select committee urged the government to use a break clause in the NHS contract from next February.
- Productivity pressure: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] reports Sir Mark Rowley's warning that blocking a Palantir contract would cost productivity and frontline staff capacity.
- Wider dependency context: [[right-in-front-afd-could-win-german-state-6a97f0a5a4a22c581728b85f]] says the NHS already depends heavily on American firms such as [[Oracle]] and [[Microsoft]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Dependence is not proof that a vendor should be rejected. A foreign or controversial supplier may still outperform available alternatives, and dependence risk can be managed through contracts, interoperability, open competition, audit rights, and exit planning. The source does not prove which controls Britain currently has in place.
