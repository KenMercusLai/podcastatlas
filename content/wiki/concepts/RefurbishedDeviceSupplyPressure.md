# Refurbished Device Supply Pressure

## Definition
Refurbished device supply pressure occurs when demand, parts recovery, trade-in flows, or new-device prices reduce the quantity or affordability of usable secondhand electronics.

## Current Synthesis
The episode argues that the refurbished market does not automatically insulate consumers from new-device inflation. Higher new-phone prices can lift secondhand prices, while brands that recover memory from existing devices for new production may divert inventory or components away from refurbishment. Reuse can therefore support resource efficiency in one production channel while weakening affordable-device supply in another.

## Key Claims
- New-device price increases can transmit into refurbished-device prices.
- Component harvesting can reduce the devices or parts available to independent refurbishment channels.
- The effect is especially important for buyers who use secondhand phones as their main affordability option.
- Component reuse has channel-dependent benefits and costs rather than a single circular-economy outcome.

## Evidence
- Price transmission: [[tech-20261029-1029-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261029-1029-mp-tech-pod-128]] says rising new-phone prices also affect refurbished-phone prices.
- Inventory competition: [[tech-20261029-1029-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261029-1029-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that some brands are considering recovering memory from existing devices and repackaging it into new products, leaving less inventory for refurbishment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source names no company, program, recovery volume, yield, device category, or measured effect on refurbished supply. Component harvesting could also extend material life or lower new-production demand, so the net environmental and affordability effects cannot be inferred from this episode alone.

## What Changed
- Created a concept for competition between manufacturer component recovery and consumer-facing refurbishment.
