# 伪史论 / Pseudo-History Conspiracy

## Definition

伪史论 / pseudo-history conspiracy is historical explanation that behaves like a closed conspiracy system: it begins with a desired reversal, then rejects the documents, archaeology, inscriptions, professional communities, and ordinary standards of proof that would test that reversal.

## Current Synthesis

The wiki now has two complementary pseudohistory cases. The Chinese pseudohistory source shows totalizing denial: Greek, Roman, or Tang history is declared fabricated, and every inconvenient record must then be dismissed or reclassified. The Atlantis source shows a less purely denialist but still evidence-weak form: [[Atlantis]] becomes an origin machine for civilizations, racial stories, esoteric technology, and advanced lost-civilization claims.

The shared mechanism is protection from falsification. In the Tang-history case, the theory discards an entire evidence ecology. In the Atlantis case, interpreters keep moving location, moral meaning, and mechanism until an ancient story can explain the Americas, Sweden, Aryan origins, flood myths, crystal energy, Santorini, or [[GrahamHancock]]'s advanced seafaring civilization. Both patterns borrow the emotional force of skepticism while abandoning [[ScientificSkepticism]]'s burden of evidence.

## Key Claims

- Pseudohistory becomes stronger to believers by becoming less falsifiable to everyone else.
- The mechanism escalates because every rejected fact or unmatched detail creates new evidence that must also be rejected, relocated, or reinterpreted.
- It often borrows the surface of skepticism while abandoning [[ScientificSkepticism]]'s burden of evidence.
- Its emotional payoff can be status, revenge, national superiority, hidden wisdom, personal comfort, or relief from uncertainty.
- It can be denialist, as in fabricated-dynasty claims, or origin-making, as in Atlantis diffusion and lost-civilization claims.
- When pseudohistory needs a culprit, it can move from entertainment into scapegoating and hatred.
- Public rebuttal often works best as [[PublicArgumentForBystanders]] rather than direct conversion of committed believers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept does not label every historical revision, myth comparison, diffusion argument, or archaeological hypothesis as pseudohistory. [[EvidenceBoundHistoricalRevision]] remains legitimate when it works from source timing, material evidence, rival explanations, and graded confidence. [[AtlantisReception]] is also broader than pseudohistory because literary utopias, political parables, and reception history can reuse Atlantis without pretending to prove a hidden ancient civilization.
