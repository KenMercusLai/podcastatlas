# Road Rage and Driving Emotion / 路怒与驾驶情绪

## Definition
Road rage and driving emotion is the source's safety framework for anger, urgency, excitement, distraction, and retaliatory impulse behind the wheel, with the boundary drawn at persistence, functional impairment, degraded judgment, or unsafe action rather than at any experience of frustration.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.189 treats road rage as a layered interaction rather than a fixed diagnosis or proof of a universally aggressive personality. Congestion, uncertain arrival time, cutting in, phone use, slow obstruction, horns, glare, and perceived rule-breaking can trigger anger, while work pressure, family demands, online hostility, temperament, and weak recovery routines determine how much arousal the driver brings into the car.

The central distinction is between emotion and action. Anger can signal threat, unfairness, or a violated boundary, but hard acceleration, retaliation, repeated overtaking, aggressive lane changes, and distracted driving convert an ordinary state into immediate public risk. Strong excitement, conversation, and calls can also capture attention, so calmness alone is not an adequate measure of safe driving.

Prevention therefore has three layers. The driver can slow breathing, lower stimulation, leave more time, choose routes, avoid using the road as an emotional outlet, and pull over safely when control is failing. Other road users and institutions can reduce conflict through predictable rule-following, enforcement, clearer design, and glare control. Everyday sleep, exercise, outdoor activity, relationships, and non-driving outlets shape the emotional baseline before the trip starts.

## Key Claims
- Road rage is not presented as a standalone DSM-5 or ICD-10/11 diagnosis; persistence, impairment, danger, and context matter.
- Traffic stress becomes recursive when one driver's horn, maneuver, delay, or hostility raises arousal in others.
- Perceived unfairness and rule violation can trigger anger more strongly than delay alone.
- The safety threshold is crossed when arousal narrows attention, impairs judgment, or turns retaliatory fantasy into driving action.
- Enclosed, vehicle-mediated interaction can reduce face-to-face accountability without proving a stable aggressive personality.
- Individual regulation and traffic-system design are complementary prevention layers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides expert discussion and everyday examples but no named studies, effect sizes, or diagnostic assessment. Its amygdala, “reptilian brain,” breathing, scent, cold-air, personality, upbringing, and environmental-color explanations remain source-scoped and should not be treated as complete neuroscience or universal interventions. Self-regulation does not excuse dangerous driving or replace safely stopping, emergency help, professional assessment, traffic law, or accountability. Opening windows, changing ventilation, or performing a breathing exercise must never compete with vehicle control.
