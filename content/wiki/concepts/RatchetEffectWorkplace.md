# Ratchet Effect In The Workplace / 职场棘轮效应

## Definition

The workplace ratchet effect is the tendency for exceptional performance or newly demonstrated capacity to become the minimum baseline for future workload, targets, or responsibility even when resources, authority, reward, and recovery do not rise with it.

## Current Synthesis

The complete source set links three versions of the same asymmetric update. Contract theory explains why workers may conceal peak sustainable effort when managers cannot distinguish normal capacity from exceptional output. AI can intensify the mechanism by making speed visible while leaving prompting, checking, accountability, and cognitive recovery hidden. The fictional project case in [[TaibaiJinxingYouDianFan|《太白金星有点烦》]] adds responsibility scope: after Li Changgeng completes one staged trial well, Guanyin assigns him the remaining eighty, converting proof of competence into ownership of nearly the entire project.

Across the cases, the problem is not higher expectations by themselves. It is an upward revision that becomes hard to reverse while the conditions that produced the peak remain unexamined. This can make strong performance self-punishing and can rationally encourage pacing, concealment, or refusal unless organizations also redesign scope, compensation, staffing, review burden, and recovery.

## Key Claims

- Performance baselines are signals, and managers may not know whether an output level is normal, lucky, or at the worker's limit.
- Ratcheting can make high effort self-punishing when future targets rise without matching resources or rewards.
- Ratcheting can enlarge responsibility as well as numeric targets: successful execution can cause an employee to inherit an entire problem domain.
- AI can intensify the effect because prompt work, review work, accountability, and recovery may remain hidden while faster output is visible.
- Managers can reduce the effect by distinguishing peak from sustainable output and aligning added scope with authority, staffing, reward, and recovery.
- Protective mechanisms have tradeoffs: expectation management or less precise evaluation can reduce short-run output even while improving truthful signaling and sustainability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

These sources do not show that every higher target is exploitative or that workers should withhold useful capacity. Raising expectations can be reasonable when tools, learning, staffing, authority, compensation, or process redesign genuinely increase sustainable output. The contract-theory and AI discussions are podcast applications rather than controlled evidence about every workplace, and the pilgrimage case is fictional. The concept is most useful when an upward baseline becomes sticky while the conditions and hidden costs behind it are ignored.
