# Algorithmic Cave Allegory / 算法洞穴隐喻

Algorithmic cave allegory is [[167-bailatu-lusuo-hayeke-alunte-sida-zhexuejia-hui-ruhe-jieshi-suanfa-shidai-chuantai-dushu-buchenglin-lmbsdbfeqqfz5vm3g67b-wjgdjfja]]'s use of [[Plato|Plato / 柏拉图]]'s cave to explain algorithmic media. The episode maps cave shadows onto search results, recommendations, rankings, hot lists, and platform interfaces: users see a projected world and often treat that projection as reality.

The source's important qualification is that the algorithmic cave does not need to lie. A feed can shape judgment before judgment begins by limiting what appears, how it is ordered, which metrics count, and which behavior feeds the next projection. In that sense, the cave is an extension of [[PublicRelevanceAlgorithms]] and [[AlgorithmicRelevanceAssessment]], not only a metaphor for deception.

The modern cave is also interactive. Unlike the original prisoners, users click, watch, comment, forward, search, and pause. Those actions enter [[AlgorithmicPredictionLoop]] and [[PlatformFeedbackLoop]], making the visible shadows partly co-produced by the people watching them.

## Key Claims
- Algorithmic visibility is already an interpretation of the world, not a neutral window onto it.
- The central risk is structural limitation before conscious choice, not only false content after choice.
- Convenience strengthens the cave because users often need filtering and therefore accept its defaults.
- "Turning around" means learning the boundary and mechanism of the cave, not imagining total escape from algorithms.
- User feedback makes the cave adaptive: users are subjects of projection and inputs into projection at the same time.
