# Hong Kong Film Gang Pressure / 港片黑社会压力

## Definition
Hong Kong film gang pressure / 港片黑社会压力 is the coercive pressure around locations, protection fees, contracts, intimidation, and entertainment-industry disputes described in 王晶's account of Hong Kong and Taiwan film work.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates gang pressure from the stylized image of gangster films. Wang describes ordinary production frictions such as location fees, cleaning fees, road fees, and protection payments, plus more serious pressure around crews, actors, contracts, and Taiwan buyers. Some pressure could be handled as business bargaining; other pressure required police attention or industry protest.

The concept is not a claim that all Hong Kong film was gang-controlled. Its value is to keep coercion visible as an operating condition while preserving evidence boundaries: many details are Wang's recollection of incidents, rumors, and private relationships, not independently adjudicated fact.

## Key Claims
- Gang pressure affected production logistics through location payments and crew intimidation.
- Contract and actor-pressure incidents could involve threats without always matching sensational rumor versions.
- Industry figures sometimes managed pressure through business settlement, limited availability, or police escalation.
- Ordinary Hong Kong life and screen gangster imagery should not be collapsed into one unsafe picture.
- Police scrutiny and celebrity visibility limited how far physical violence usually went.
- The concept needs source discipline because private disputes and gang affiliations are easy to overstate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is built from one practitioner's recollection. It should avoid naming unverified culpability beyond the source and should distinguish confirmed structural pressure from anecdote, rumor, and reputation.
