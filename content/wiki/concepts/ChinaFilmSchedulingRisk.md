# China Film Scheduling Risk / 中国电影档期风险

China film scheduling risk is the uncertainty that a film's box-office result depends heavily on release timing, screen allocation, competing titles, and distributor confidence. [[zhizhuxia-xinpian-naxia-jinban-guonei-piaofang-ai-moxing-baofa-jiagezhan-1004403588]] adds the concept through two linked claims: a new Spider-Man film captured nearly half of the prior week's domestic box office, while eight summer-slot films had already withdrawn.

The source frames the withdrawal cluster as unusual and tied to a weaker-than-expected film market. When exhibitors favor the title already proving demand, smaller or less certain films may fear weak screen allocation and choose to move, which can further concentrate attention around the biggest imported or franchise titles.

## Key Claims
- Release timing is strategic because theaters reallocate screens toward titles with stronger observed demand.
- A large franchise hit can validate remaining audience demand while making the same window more dangerous for weaker films.
- Summer-slot withdrawals show that producers and distributors may prefer delay over being crowded out in a high-profile period.
- The risk sits between consumer demand and channel behavior: audiences decide demand, but theaters translate demand into screen time.
- Franchise IP can reduce but not remove scheduling risk because market mood, competing titles, and release windows still matter.
