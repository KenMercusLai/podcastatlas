# Age-Gap Marriage Decline

[[taking-the-shine-off-albedo-and-global-temperatures-6a730fdaca067b295bab9760]] adds a second [[DougDawson]] marriage-data segment, this time on [[FemaleBreadwinnerNorms]]. Together, the two sources make Dawson's wiki role a broader test of whether visible celebrity relationship stereotypes match aggregate American couple data.

Age-gap marriage decline is the shift described in [[hard-cedar-lebanon-seeks-trumps-help-6a5f41e97bddc0bea5542de5]] where wealthy American men are no longer especially more likely than other men to marry much younger women. [[DougDawson]] says census data show that in 1980 about 8% of top earners married someone at least a decade younger, compared with about 5% among typical earners, but by 2024 the gap had closed.

The source treats the pattern as a cultural stereotype tested against data. It does not deny large age gaps among some very wealthy men, especially later remarriages, but it says the broad income-gradient pattern has weakened.

## Key Claims
- A visible elite stereotype can persist after the aggregate pattern becomes much weaker.
- Education may reduce age gaps by putting high earners into longer age-peer social environments.
- The source finds little evidence that high-earning women broadly mirror the older "trophy wife" pattern by marrying much younger men.
- The later female-breadwinner segment keeps the same caution: celebrity examples are useful hooks, but relationship norms have to be tested against distributions and reported behavior.
