# Exercise Load Management / 运动负荷管理

## Definition
Exercise load management / 运动负荷管理 is the practice of matching training frequency, volume, intensity, progression, recovery, and technique to a person's current capacity so exercise builds health without creating preventable injury.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] develops the concept for sedentary workers and weekend athletes. The source's core correction is that exercise risk is not simply "movement wears joints down"; risk rises when long sitting, weak muscles, sudden long-distance running, fatigue, poor technique, or external supports exceed the body's prepared capacity.

The episode treats gradual progression as a general rule across gym training, running, cycling, tennis, and [[HYROX]]. It recommends ordinary, repeatable training structures such as two or three gym sessions a week, lower weekend intensity when weekdays are sedentary, and weekday strength or core work that prepares the body for the weekend sport.

## Key Claims
- Safe exercise depends on matching load to current capacity, not on choosing a universally safe or unsafe sport.
- Gradual progression matters across endurance and strength activities: jumping from no base to long runs, marathons, or intense functional-fitness sequences raises risk.
- Weekend athletes should reduce single-session intensity and use weekday strength or function work to close the gap between daily inactivity and weekend demand.
- Fatigue is a risk multiplier because it makes movement form deteriorate, especially in later stages of HYROX-style training or other mixed endurance-strength sessions.
- Professional athletes and social-media high-intensity routines are poor default templates for ordinary workers without the same preparation, recovery, and supervision.
- Equipment and supports may reduce risk in specific contexts, but they do not replace progression, strength, technique, and recovery.

- Capacity matching: [[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] repeatedly distinguishes beneficial exercise from overloading the body through sudden distance, intensity, or uncontrolled competition.
- Weekend pattern: [[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] advises people who can exercise only on weekends to lower weekend intensity and add weekday lower-limb or core strengthening around the chosen sport.
- Fatigue and progression: [[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] explains HYROX risk through late-session fatigue and recommends gradually increasing total volume, speed, intensity, and sets.
- Sport-specific load: [[vol-212-pingshi-jiuzuo-zhoumo-wanming-yundong-yundong-yixueke-yisheng-gei-dagongrende-fang-lian-fei-zhinan-lqtwr4m-9npye9giib1-om-tylmy]] treats running terrain, bike fit, tennis calf loading, and gym equipment choice as load-management details rather than isolated tips.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not argue against intense training, competition, or endurance goals in general. It argues that intensity should be earned by preparation, technique, and recovery. The episode is public education, not an individualized training prescription, and pain, swelling, locking, neurological symptoms, or suspected acute injury still require qualified assessment.

## What Changed
- VOL.212 adds exercise load management as the reusable safety frame behind the episode's running, gym, cycling, tennis, HYROX, weekend-exercise, and support-device advice.
