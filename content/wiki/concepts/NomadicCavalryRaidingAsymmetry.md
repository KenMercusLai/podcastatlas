## Definition

Nomadic cavalry raiding asymmetry / 游牧骑兵劫掠不对称 is the strategic imbalance in which mobile raiders can choose contact, timing, and withdrawal while an agrarian border state must protect fixed people, fields, livestock, and stores.

## Current Synthesis

The concept begins with [[LiMu|李牧]] and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-105-zhanzheng-huayang-zhaoguo-limu-zui-hui-shua-ljtqdg46a8o13zzsjv-p-fpol5oh|Qinji 105]]. Li Mu does not simply chase raiders into the steppe because the raiding side can move fast, fight under favorable conditions, and withdraw after the defender has paid mobilization costs. His answer is to shelter people, livestock, and supplies, deny profitable contact, and lure the Xiongnu main force near prepared Zhao strength.

The early Han evidence shows that numbers do not erase the asymmetry. In [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-177-beikun-baidengshan-liubang-mingxuan-yixian-ljdmxf6bej8ju0gmvm9cp48gy0on|Hanji 177]], [[LiuBang|刘邦]] commands a large, infantry-heavy force in severe winter, while [[MaoDunChanyu|冒顿单于]] can avoid fixed battle, stage weakness, and choose the encirclement point at [[BaidengEncirclement|白登]]. Hanji 178-179 then turn the lesson into policy: after direct pursuit proves costly, the court seeks heqin as a lower-cost restraint, while still admitting that marriage, gifts, and envoys do not eliminate raids.

The upstream ecological incentive is now part of the concept through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-741-weishenme-gudai-xiongnu-zongyao-ruqin-zhongyuan-lllpgozgi6yd5pscw8z5ofidk5rp|Hanji 741]]. [[SteppePastoralResourcePressure|Steppe pastoral resource pressure]] means raids are not only opportunistic tactics; they are also tied to low rainfall, seasonal water and pasture movement, overgrazing risk, winter provisioning, and the attraction of agrarian stores. This explains why the same raiding asymmetry keeps recurring even after punitive campaigns or temporary settlements.

The production calendar behind the asymmetry is visible in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-841-juebu-shouruan-jiemi-zhanzheng-de-canku-zhenxiang-losza81gissyijywwe0vxadw5ia0|Hanji 841]]. Autumn raids make sense because the Han agricultural side has harvested grain, the Xiongnu side needs winter stores, and horses are especially strong after summer pasture. The same seasonal logic helps explain why [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s later spring attacks are framed as unusually destructive: they aim at pregnancy and livestock breeding as well as fighters.

## Key Claims

- Mobile raiders can impose repeated cost without needing to hold territory.
- Border defenders face a protection problem, not only a battle problem: farms, herds, civilians, and stores are fixed assets.
- Pastoral resource pressure makes agrarian stores attractive targets and helps explain why raids recur.
- Chasing mobile cavalry is expensive and may be strategically impossible for a weakened state.
- Walls, fortresses, withdrawal discipline, heqin, and baited counterattack are different answers to the same asymmetry.
- Seasonal production cycles make autumn raids and spring counterattacks strategically intelligible.
- Later imperial offensive campaigns against Xiongnu require a resource base that late Warring States Zhao and early Han do not yet have in these source accounts.

## Evidence

- Defensive answer to mobile raids: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-105-zhanzheng-huayang-zhaoguo-limu-zui-hui-shua-ljtqdg46a8o13zzsjv-p-fpol5oh|Qinji 105]] says Li Mu shelters people and herds, refuses bad contact, and later lures Xiongnu into a prepared encirclement.
- Early Han exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-177-beikun-baidengshan-liubang-mingxuan-yixian-ljdmxf6bej8ju0gmvm9cp48gy0on|Hanji 177]] shows Liu Bang's large army becoming vulnerable when winter, infantry speed, and Xiongnu false weakness let Modu choose the battlefield.
- Policy pivot after Baideng: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-178-maodun-weihe-yao-fangguo-liubang-zhe-cai-shi-zhenxiang-1-lumszkbr-klnlodxmxdf-n8p5wym|Hanji 178]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-179-lishishang-heqin-zenme-laide-lslks-ovbomneornk4j-q6-ns0x1|Hanji 179]] connect failed coercion to [[HanXiongnuHeqinPolicy|汉匈和亲政策]] while preserving the policy's limits.
- Ecological incentive: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-741-weishenme-gudai-xiongnu-zongyao-ruqin-zhongyuan-lllpgozgi6yd5pscw8z5ofidk5rp|Hanji 741]] explains recurring raiding through rainfall, water, pasture, overgrazing, winter need, and agrarian stored goods.
- Seasonal resource cycle: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-841-juebu-shouruan-jiemi-zhanzheng-de-canku-zhenxiang-losza81gissyijywwe0vxadw5ia0|Hanji 841]] explains autumn raids through harvest grain, winter need, and horse condition, then contrasts Han Wudi's spring attacks as a different form of seasonal pressure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not imply that nomadic groups always win or that agrarian states can only endure raids. Li Mu's prepared counterattack, Qin's wall-and-commandery approach, Han Wudi's later campaigns, and Mingdi's 73 CE offensive in Hanji 741 all show that state capacity can shift the balance. The asymmetry describes the default protection burden and contact-choice problem, not a permanent outcome.

The ecological layer also requires caution. Hanji 741 explains recurring raiding through pastoral resource pressure, but leaders still make choices about timing, diplomacy, alliance, and violence. The concept should not become environmental determinism.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 741's ecological pressure layer to the earlier tactical and seasonal account.
- Linked raiding asymmetry to the new [[SteppePastoralResourcePressure]] concept.
- Preserved the existing judgment that policy responses range from denial and defense to heqin and later offensive campaigns.
