# Racial Microaggression Cumulative Harm / 种族微歧视的累积伤害

## Definition
Racial microaggression cumulative harm is the psychological and participatory cost created when ambiguous suspicion, coldness, impatience, misrecognition, exclusion, or fetishization repeatedly treats a person as a racial category rather than an individual.

## Current Synthesis
The evidentiary difficulty is part of the harm. A single cold interaction, mistaken name, plagiarism suspicion, or awkward comment may admit several explanations, but repetition within a recognizable stereotype can make people doubt their own perception, anticipate exclusion, and withdraw from spaces where their contribution feels unseen. Cumulative interpretation should preserve uncertainty about any one actor's intent without treating the affected person's pattern recognition as meaningless.

## Key Claims
- Discrimination can operate through differential treatment without explicit slurs.
- Ambiguity makes incidents difficult to prove and can cause repeated self-doubt.
- Group-based suspicion assigns one person's alleged conduct to unrelated people who share appearance or origin.
- Name confusion and interchangeable treatment deny individuality even when followed by apology.
- Sexualized compliments can apply racial scripts while presenting themselves as admiration.
- Repeated small incidents can reduce participation, trust, and willingness to remain in a social or professional space.

## Evidence
- Group suspicion: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] recounts an Asian student being suspected after another Asian student's alleged citation misconduct.
- Differential warmth: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] describes a clerk changing from warm conversation with a white customer to cold formality with an Asian customer.
- Misrecognition and withdrawal: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] reports two distinct Asian women being confused in a familiar academic setting and one becoming less willing to participate afterward.
- Fetishization: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] includes a colleague opening an acquaintance with a generalized preference for Chinese women.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Ambiguous behavior does not prove racial motive in every case, and mistaken names, brusqueness, or suspicion can arise for other reasons. The concept concerns patterned context, asymmetry, stereotype fit, repetition, and effect; it should not convert uncertainty into certainty. The episode provides lived examples rather than prevalence estimates, controlled comparison, or diagnosis of cumulative trauma.

## What Changed
- Made evidentiary ambiguity and self-doubt part of the mechanism rather than a reason to erase reported harm.
- Connected repeated minor treatment to withdrawal from academic and social participation.
