# Perfectionism As Avoidance

## Definition
Perfectionism as avoidance is the use of impossible conditions or standards to protect against embarrassment, criticism, failure, or visible inadequacy while appearing to pursue excellence.

## Current Synthesis
[[129-ruhe-chengwei-zhenzheng-de-buwanmei-zhuyizhe-hunshen-shi-jiner-799220151]] separates healthy excellence from standards that block action. Situational perfectionism waits for ideal conditions, quality perfectionism waits for flawless output, and quantity perfectionism converts arbitrary metrics into entry barriers. The common mechanism is identity protection: no completed attempt means no visible failure.

The resource-allocation account from [[AdamGrant]] makes imperfectionism selective rather than careless. It calibrates standards to consequence, reserving exceptional effort for work where quality matters most and accepting good-enough completion elsewhere. His diving example adds a learning-allocation mechanism: polishing easy dives, restarting imperfect approaches, and ruminating over small errors reduced exposure to harder attempts and narrowed development. [[StevenPressfield]] adds a completion test: revision becomes avoidance when continued “noodling” protects the creator from shipping, readership, criticism, or rejection. Multiple drafts preserve craft, but release remains part of the work.

## Key Claims
- Perfectionism can look like ambition while functioning as procrastination, retreat, or risk avoidance.
- Situational perfectionism waits for ideal conditions; quality perfectionism waits for flawless output; quantity perfectionism turns arbitrary metrics into entry barriers.
- Failure becomes harder to tolerate when the person treats it as proof of identity rather than feedback from one attempt.
- School, family, social media, and gendered appearance standards can train people to keep evaluating themselves before acting.
- The alternative is not sloppiness; it combines [[ImperfectActionPractice]] with standards calibrated to task importance.
- Revision supports quality until it becomes a way to postpone exposure, feedback, or completion indefinitely.
- Perfect execution can reduce learning when it shifts practice toward mastered tasks and away from difficult attempts.

## Evidence
- Avoidance mechanisms - [[129-ruhe-chengwei-zhenzheng-de-buwanmei-zhuyizhe-hunshen-shi-jiner-799220151]] distinguishes ideal-condition, flawless-output, and arbitrary-metric barriers to action.
- Practical countermeasures - [[129-ruhe-chengwei-zhenzheng-de-buwanmei-zhuyizhe-hunshen-shi-jiner-799220151]] supports low thresholds, binary action, failure exposure, and concrete self-praise.
- Standard calibration - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] recommends high standards for consequential tasks and good-enough completion for lower-stakes work.
- Health and performance boundary - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] links perfectionism with burnout, depression, and anxiety while presenting imperfectionism as a character skill.
- Shipping boundary - [[how-to-overcome-inner-resistance-steven-pressfield-scim8607622458]] has Pressfield identify endless revision and refusal to send completed work as forms of Resistance.
- Drafting without premature judgment - [[how-to-overcome-inner-resistance-steven-pressfield-scim8607622458]] describes many drafts and delayed same-day evaluation as a way to preserve standards without blocking production.
- Practice narrowing - [[unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant-unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant]] uses Grant's diving experience to connect excessive refinement, restarts, rumination, and shame with reduced difficult practice.
- Social pressure - [[unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant-unlocking-hidden-potential-with-adam-grant]] reports rising parental expectations and criticism as possible contributors to perfectionism while leaving causation source-scoped.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish that every high standard, technical repetition, or long revision cycle is defensive or harmful. Mastering basics can be prerequisite to harder work, and some tasks have legal, clinical, engineering, safety, or ethical thresholds where "good enough" must remain demanding. Complex art may legitimately need extensive iteration. Associations with burnout, depression, anxiety, parenting, or competition do not by themselves prove a single causal direction. Standard calibration requires realistic consequence assessment, not using imperfectionism or “shipping” to excuse avoidable harm.

## What Changed
- Added practice narrowing: perfectionism can overinvest in mastered tasks and reduce exposure to harder attempts.
- Added parental expectation and competition as source-scoped contributors rather than established causes.
