# Perfectionism As Avoidance

## Definition
Perfectionism as avoidance is the use of impossible conditions or standards to protect against embarrassment, criticism, failure, or visible inadequacy while appearing to pursue excellence.

## Current Synthesis
[[129-ruhe-chengwei-zhenzheng-de-buwanmei-zhuyizhe-hunshen-shi-jiner-799220151]] separates healthy excellence from standards that block action. Situational perfectionism waits for ideal conditions, quality perfectionism waits for flawless output, and quantity perfectionism converts arbitrary metrics into entry barriers. The common mechanism is identity protection: no completed attempt means no visible failure.

The resource-allocation account from [[AdamGrant]] makes imperfectionism selective rather than careless. It calibrates standards to consequence, reserving exceptional effort for work where quality matters most and accepting good-enough completion elsewhere. This reduces the burnout, anxiety, and procrastination risk attributed to indiscriminate perfectionism while preserving deliberate craft.

## Key Claims
- Perfectionism can look like ambition while functioning as procrastination, retreat, or risk avoidance.
- Situational perfectionism waits for ideal conditions; quality perfectionism waits for flawless output; quantity perfectionism turns arbitrary metrics into entry barriers.
- Failure becomes harder to tolerate when the person treats it as proof of identity rather than feedback from one attempt.
- School, family, social media, and gendered appearance standards can train people to keep evaluating themselves before acting.
- The alternative is not sloppiness; it combines [[ImperfectActionPractice]] with standards calibrated to task importance.

## Evidence
- Avoidance mechanisms - [[129-ruhe-chengwei-zhenzheng-de-buwanmei-zhuyizhe-hunshen-shi-jiner-799220151]] distinguishes ideal-condition, flawless-output, and arbitrary-metric barriers to action.
- Practical countermeasures - [[129-ruhe-chengwei-zhenzheng-de-buwanmei-zhuyizhe-hunshen-shi-jiner-799220151]] supports low thresholds, binary action, failure exposure, and concrete self-praise.
- Standard calibration - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] recommends high standards for consequential tasks and good-enough completion for lower-stakes work.
- Health and performance boundary - [[how-to-unlock-your-potential-motivation-unique-abilities-dr-adam-grant-scim2670765142]] links perfectionism with burnout, depression, and anxiety while presenting imperfectionism as a character skill.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not establish that every high standard is defensive or harmful. Some tasks have legal, clinical, engineering, safety, or ethical thresholds where "good enough" must remain demanding. Associations with burnout, depression, and anxiety do not by themselves prove a single causal direction. Standard calibration requires realistic consequence assessment, not using imperfectionism to excuse avoidable harm.
