# Local Psychedelic Decriminalization

Local psychedelic decriminalization is the city-level policy posture described in [[a-trip-to-the-magic-mushroom-megachurch]], where places such as [[OaklandCalifornia]] make mushroom offenses a lower law-enforcement priority. The episode treats this as useful but incomplete protection for [[PsychedelicChurches]].

The key distinction is between enforcement priority and legality. [[ZydorChurch]] operates in a local environment that may reduce some police pressure, but [[PsilocybinMushrooms]] and [[DMT]] remain illegal under the [[ControlledSubstancesAct]] unless a religious exemption or other legal protection applies.

## Key Claims
- Local decriminalization can reduce practical risk without changing the federal baseline.
- It may be substance-specific; mushrooms can receive lower priority while other substances remain more exposed.
- The policy does not settle religious sincerity, donation models, clinical safety, or civil liability.
- Churches can still face raids, property seizure, federal action, or legal cases after participant injury.
