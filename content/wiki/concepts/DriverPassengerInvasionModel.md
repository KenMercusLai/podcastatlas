# Driver-Passenger Invasion Model / 驅動者與搭便車模型

## Definition
Driver-passenger invasion model is the causal distinction between alien species acting as direct drivers of native-species decline and alien species expanding as passengers of broader habitat or environmental change.

## Current Synthesis
The Duanwen episode uses the model to make Taiwan's introduced-myna debate more cautious. If introduced mynas are direct drivers, removal may address the main cause of native-bird decline. If they are passengers of urbanization, forest reduction, building expansion, or other landscape change, removal alone may not restore native populations.

The model matters because management follows diagnosis. A direct-driver case supports targeted removal more strongly; a passenger case points toward habitat repair, monitoring, and site-specific control rather than blanket killing.

## Key Claims
- Driver explanations attribute native decline directly to predation, competition, or other impacts from the introduced species.
- Passenger explanations attribute both alien expansion and native decline to external environmental change.
- Misdiagnosing passengers as drivers can waste resources and produce killing without recovery.
- Taiwan's introduced-myna evidence remains too limited for a confident direct-driver conclusion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not conclude that Taiwan mynas are harmless passengers. It says the stronger direct-causality claim needs more research and should not be assumed from alien status alone.
