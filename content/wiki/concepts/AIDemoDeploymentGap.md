# AI Demo Deployment Gap

## Definition

AI demo deployment gap is the distance between a visible or technically successful AI demonstration and a system that can be bought, governed, integrated, paid for, and kept reliable in a real workflow.

## Current Synthesis

The gap has commercial, technical, and governance layers. The [[WAIC]] source shows that booths, screens, compute clusters, robot performances, and application demos do not establish buyer demand, workflow change, autonomy, stability, latency, or viable unit economics. The [[MarketplaceTech]] source adds “demo limbo”: an experiment can work as shown yet remain undeployable because its data access, privacy, security, or regulatory posture is unacceptable.

The concept is broader than [[TechnicalDemoRetentionGap]]. A startup demo can fail after users try it; an AI deployment gap can appear before launch or scale, when the buyer, workflow, authority, economics, evidence, and operational controls do not close together.

## Key Claims
- Exhibition visibility is not evidence of customer pull or production readiness.
- [[EmbodiedAI]] demos should separate teleoperation, single-script behavior, remote takeover, and autonomous task planning.
- AI application teams need domain know-how, user data, workflow integration, and sales clarity, because generic AI can help competitors reproduce surface features.
- Deployment requires cost, latency, stability, routing, monitoring, and acceptance criteria, not only an impressive model answer.
- AI is more credible as efficiency improvement when the operator already understands the goal, process, and customer than as a magic search for business direction.
- A successful technical experiment can remain blocked when regulation, privacy, security, permissions, or recovery requirements are unresolved.
- Production readiness includes the authority to act and the ability to detect, stop, and repair unintended consequences.

### Engineering and economics evidence
- [[ai-bu-zhi-bi-zhishang-waic-he-kimi-k3-toulule-shenme-xin-jingzheng-1]] connects deployability to cost, latency, stability, model routing, monitoring, data, domain knowledge, and acceptance criteria.

### Governance evidence
- [[tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that some enterprise AI experiments remain in demo limbo because privacy, security, or regulatory requirements prevent deployment.
- [[tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261014-1014-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds deletion and unintended communication as operational consequences that a production system must control.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

A demo can be an appropriate discovery tool even when the buyer, workflow, or controls are not settled; the gap is not evidence that the underlying capability is false. Governance requirements differ by domain, and a low-risk internal assistant should not inherit every control required for regulated or externally acting systems. The sources are exhibition commentary and a short qualitative interview rather than measured deployment studies.

## What Changed

- Added privacy, security, regulation, permission, and recovery as distinct deployment gates.
- Clarified that demo limbo can follow genuine technical success.
