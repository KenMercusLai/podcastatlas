# Oral Myth As Magical Realism Source

Oral myth as magical realism source is the episode's frame for why [[NativeAmericanOralLiterature|美洲原住民口头文学]] can make [[GabrielGarciaMarquez|Gabriel Garcia Marquez / 加西亚·马尔克斯]] and Latin American magical realism feel less mysterious. In [[32-yindianren-jiang-le-yi-bai-wan-ci-de-gushi-shi-maerkesi-de-laoshi-596386714]], the host hears a shared narrative atmosphere: abrupt transformations, concrete matter-of-fact miracles, living landscapes, porous human-animal boundaries, and colonial or Christian fragments added to Indigenous story worlds.

The concept is an analogy with guardrails. The source does not prove a clean influence chain from one tale to one novel. Its useful claim is that magical realism can be read as culturally plausible when oral storytelling, family narration, Indigenous cosmology, colonial violence, Catholic and local religious mixture, and everyday rural memory are treated as overlapping layers.

## Key Claims
- Magical-realist atmosphere can arise from ordinary storytelling traditions that do not separate marvel from daily life.
- [[HumanAnimalContinuityInMyth]] and etiological myth make abrupt causal leaps feel internally normal rather than merely surreal.
- Colonial story accretion helps explain why European figures, missionaries, local gods, animals, and modern intrusions can coexist in one tale.
- The claim needs [[InterpretationAndOverinterpretation]] discipline: resemblance is meaningful for reading, but not automatically proof of direct literary borrowing.
