# Film Rating Calibration / 电影评分校准

## Definition

Film rating calibration is the practice of making the comparison set and meaning of score levels explicit before assigning a number, including whether personal preference is treated separately from perceived craft quality.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded source supplies one transparent scoring model rather than a universal system. A host compares [[DoubleVision2002|《双瞳》]] with films across genre and national origin, defines 9 as top-tier achievement, and reserves 10 for similarly high quality intensified by personal attachment. This makes the film's 8/10 legible as strong approval while exposing the subjective increment built into the top score.

## Key Claims

- A numerical score is interpretable only relative to its stated comparison set; a horror-only scale can mean something different from an all-film scale.
- Separating perceived achievement from personal attachment clarifies why two films of comparable quality may receive different top-end scores.
- Reference films make verbal score definitions concrete by showing what a reviewer actually places at 9 or 10.
- Explicit calibration reduces ambiguity but does not turn taste into objective measurement or establish group consensus.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This model comes from one participant in a short conversational excerpt. Scores remain compressed personal judgments, the boundaries among adjacent numbers are not operationalized, and the source supplies no evidence that the other hosts share the same scale. Cross-genre comparison can improve consistency while still obscuring different artistic aims.

## What Changed

- Established an explicit distinction between comparative quality and the personal-preference increment at the top of a rating scale.
- Added reference-film anchoring as a way to make numerical judgments more interpretable.
- Limited the framework to one speaker's method rather than presenting it as objective measurement.
