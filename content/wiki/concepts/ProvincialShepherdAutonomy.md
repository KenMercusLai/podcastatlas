## Definition

州牧集权与地方割据 / Provincial Shepherd Autonomy is the institutional progression in which a center creates or strengthens regional supervision to solve monitoring and security problems, but the same territorial office can later accumulate administrative, military, fiscal, personnel, and route-control power sufficient for semi-independent rule.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-369-1-weiqing-zhisi-jing-rang-hanwudi-ruci-fengkuang-lljxwjp9qxvpog9q2dh2kiyyccer|Hanji 369-1]] supplies the early Western Han prehistory. It presents [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s regional刺史 as centrally appointed inspectors intended to monitor commanderies and kingdoms after territorial expansion, weakening of princely domains, and growing administrative complexity strained earlier oversight. Their initial logic is centralization: the center sends agents downward because it cannot reliably see local government from the capital alone.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-939-donghan-luanju-de-fengkuang-sanguo-xuqu-lthmvw8ihk96fuez9vamlryzcngy|Hanji 939]] shows a later crisis-driven strengthening. After renewed rebellion, [[ZhangYiLateHan|张懿]]'s killing, and visible inspector weakness, [[LiuYanLateHan|刘焉]] proposes stronger州牧; the court appoints Liu Yan, [[HuangWanLateHan|黄琬]], and [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]]. The reform remains intelligible as an attempt to restore order, even though it places more usable capacity outside the center.

The local entry condition appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-940-de-minxin-zhe-de-tianxia-ni-xin-bu-xin-loipf7b0p5a-yh0rj2njnsml8a1j|Hanji 940]]. [[MaXiangLateHan|马相]] and [[ZhaoZhiLateHan|赵祗]] exploit extraction and absent authority in [[YizhouLateHan|益州]], [[JiaLongLateHan|贾龙]] restores temporary order, and Liu Yan enters with lenient pacification. Provincial consolidation can therefore begin as a credible answer to revolt and a public demand for government before becoming autonomous power.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-959-ruguo-ni-chuanyue-dao-sanguo-gai-ruhe-cunhuo-lroaihmpnrelmfcugym9zx2h2dhq|Hanji 959]] supplies the autonomy mechanism. Liu Yan combines office, local coercion, religious-military proxies, control of the Hanzhong route, retention of [[LiuZhang|刘璋]], and imperial-style symbols. Geography, troops, communication control, household succession, and title now reinforce one another, allowing a provincial office created under Han legitimacy to function increasingly like a regional regime.

The current judgment is therefore path-dependent rather than deterministic. Early inspection does not automatically create warlordism, and stronger provincial office can be necessary under disorder. The danger rises when supervision becomes territorial command and a weakened center can no longer rotate, audit, communicate with, or materially coerce the regional holder.

## Key Claims

- Regional inspection begins as a centralizing response to the center's limited visibility over commanderies and kingdoms.
- Administrative expansion can increase the demand for an intermediate supervisory layer even when the formal local hierarchy remains contested or incomplete.
- Rebellion and inspector failure can make stronger provincial office a rational short-term repair.
- Provincial consolidation can gain legitimacy through restored order, leniency, and local support before it hardens into coercive autonomy.
- Autonomy becomes durable when office, troops, personnel, routes, household succession, and political symbols reinforce one another.
- The same institution can therefore strengthen the center in one capacity environment and accelerate fragmentation in another.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources do not establish a straight institutional line in which Han Wudi's刺史 inevitably became late-Han warlords. Hanji 369-1 compresses监察区, ordinary administration,刺史,州牧, and later regional military office; its claim that the system immediately changed a two-tier structure into a settled州—郡—县 hierarchy requires specialist confirmation. Hanji 939-940 show genuine security and governance reasons for stronger regional authority, while Hanji 959 shows only one Yizhou conversion path. Differences in title, rank, legal jurisdiction, geography, court capacity, and chronology remain material.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 369-1's Western Han inspection prehistory before the late-Han州牧 reform.
- Reframed the concept as a capacity-dependent progression from central monitoring to possible regional autonomy, not an inevitable outcome.
- Separated initial inspection, crisis-driven strengthening, locally legitimate consolidation, and route-backed separatist capacity.
- Migrated the page to the synthesis-v1 evidence contract without removing or reordering prior sources.
