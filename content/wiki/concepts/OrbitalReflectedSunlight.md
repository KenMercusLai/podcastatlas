# Orbital Reflected Sunlight

## Definition
Orbital reflected sunlight is the deliberate redirection of sunlight from mirror-equipped satellites toward selected ground areas after local sunset.

## Current Synthesis
The source places [[ReflectOrbital]]'s proposal between a technical demonstration and an energy-system claim. One small satellite could produce a broad patch comparable to moonlight, but useful solar generation would require many coordinated mirrors combining beams around dusk. The most plausible commercial framing in the episode is hybrid: batteries handle routine shifting, while reflected sunlight seeks to reduce expensive reserve capacity during longer low-generation periods.

The same scale that could make the system economically useful creates externalities. Deliberately reflective spacecraft may interfere with astronomy more than incidental reflections from constellations such as [[Starlink]], while artificial night lighting can affect insect navigation and animal circadian rhythms. Telescope exclusion zones are a partial mitigation, not a complete governance model for ecosystems, communities, aviation, liability, and shared night skies.

## Key Claims
- A moon-bright demonstration is materially different from illumination sufficient to operate solar panels.
- Commercial usefulness depends on coordinated constellation scale, targeting reliability, launch and operating cost, and comparison with storage.
- The proposal is best assessed as a battery complement rather than a universal substitute.
- Astronomical interference and ecological disruption rise with intentional brightness and deployment scale.
- Site-specific exclusion zones do not settle wider questions about consent, liability, ecology, or ownership of the night sky.

## Evidence
- Demonstration boundary: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] distinguishes the planned Earendil-1 moonlight-level test from a multi-satellite beam capable of activating panels.
- Storage comparison: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] says batteries are falling in cost and frames orbital light as a possible substitute only for costlier reserve capacity.
- Astronomy and ecology: [[valley-of-the-shadow-of-debt-bond-market-jitters-69104aa52182b702669d450d025a7dac]] cites existing constellation light pollution, proposed telescope exclusion zones, insect lunar navigation, and animal circadian effects.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The project is not shown at commercial scale in the source. The discussion does not quantify lifecycle emissions, debris risk, full storage alternatives, grid value, weather dependence, ground-level safety, ecological dose-response, or regulatory authority. Historical mirror experiments demonstrate visibility, not economic viability. This is also distinct from [[SolarGeoengineering]], which aims to alter climate forcing rather than deliver localized illumination.

## What Changed
- Created a framework separating demonstration brightness, energy usefulness, and system-scale externalities.
- Bounded the battery comparison and telescope-exclusion mitigation.
