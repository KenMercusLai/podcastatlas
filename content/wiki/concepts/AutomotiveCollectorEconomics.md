# Automotive Collector Economics

Automotive collector economics is the demand system where cars function as collectible, serviced, authenticated, and traded cultural assets rather than only depreciating transportation. In [[ferrari-1]], [[Ferrari]] is the source case.

The episode argues that many Ferrari buyers already own multiple cars, many cars spend much of their time in garages, and a very high share of all Ferraris ever made remains drivable. Programs such as [[FerrariClassiche]], limited models such as [[FerrariIcona]], and historically loaded cars such as the [[Ferrari250]] and [[FerrariF40]] help convert scarcity and provenance into recurring value.

## Key Claims
- Collector economics are stronger when the brand can certify provenance and support long-term service.
- Repeat buyers may absorb new production without making the cars feel common on ordinary roads.
- Secondary-market confidence can reinforce primary-market willingness to pay, but it also depends on disciplined allocation and product continuity.
