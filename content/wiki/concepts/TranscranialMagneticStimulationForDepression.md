# Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation for Depression

## Definition
Transcranial magnetic stimulation for depression is the use of externally delivered magnetic stimulation to alter activity in mood-related brain circuits, especially prefrontal control networks.

## Current Synthesis
The sources present TMS as a non-drug route into [[CircuitBasedPsychiatry]]. [[NolanWilliams]] describes the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex as a control-related region whose stimulation can influence mood-regulatory networks and transiently decelerate heart rate through connected brain-body pathways. The heart response is best treated here as evidence of circuit engagement, not proof that lowering heart rate itself treats depression. In the depression frame, TMS is compared to externally activating cognitive control systems that psychotherapy may recruit more gradually or in milder cases.

The strongest synthesis is functional rather than device-centered. TMS matters because target, laterality, timing, dose, and network connectivity determine what a magnetic device does. Left-prefrontal excitation and right-prefrontal inhibition are described as antidepressant routes, while individualized functional connectivity helps distinguish [[StanfordNeuromodulationTherapy]] from generic delivery. TMS may restore top-down regulation when deeper regions overpower the prefrontal cortex, making some patients better able to use psychotherapy.

## Key Claims
- TMS is framed as a device method, while the therapeutic effect depends on target, timing, dose, and condition.
- Dorsolateral prefrontal stimulation is linked to mood regulation and brain-heart effects in the source.
- Laterality and functional connectivity matter: the full interview distinguishes left excitation, right inhibition, and individualized prefrontal-cingulate targeting.
- Depression can involve weakened prefrontal control over deeper mood and salience circuits.
- TMS may function like exogenous cognitive control rather than a serotonin-adding intervention.
- Dense stimulation can produce rapid improvement for some patients, but anecdotes and protocol-specific results must remain bounded.
- TMS may support psychotherapy use in some cases by restoring access to cognitive control.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page does not claim that TMS works for all depression, replaces psychotherapy or medication, lowers resting heart rate, or can be self-administered. The sources distinguish ordinary TMS delivery, Stanford's accelerated protocol, and treatment claims that depend on laterality, individualized target, clinical condition, dose, and timing.
