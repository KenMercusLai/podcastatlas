# Product Liability Federal Preemption

## Definition
Product liability federal preemption is the legal question of when federal regulatory approval or labeling authority blocks state-law product-liability claims such as failure to warn.

## Current Synthesis
The episode introduces the concept through [[Monsanto]], [[Roundup]], [[Bayer]], and the [[EnvironmentalProtectionAgency]]. Friedberg explains that EPA labels pesticides and, in the source's account, determined Roundup does not require a cancer warning for labeling purposes. The Supreme Court argument then asks whether that federal label decision preempts state failure-to-warn litigation.

The source also links the issue to broader administrative law. If courts give less deference to agencies after the fall of [[ChevronDoctrine|Chevron]], state-law plaintiffs may argue that courts should read federal law directly and preserve state authority to protect citizens. The page therefore treats preemption as a conflict among federal expertise, statutory interpretation, state police power, and manufacturer reliance on approved labels.

## Key Claims
- Federal labeling approval can become a shield argument for manufacturers, but the scope of that shield depends on statutory interpretation.
- State failure-to-warn claims can function as a parallel safety mechanism when plaintiffs argue federal agencies under-protect consumers.
- The post-Chevron environment may make courts more willing to interpret federal statutes directly rather than defer to agency positions.
- Preemption disputes convert scientific and regulatory disagreement into institutional-design questions about who gets final authority.
- The business impact can be large when mass tort exposure survives despite federal regulatory compliance.
