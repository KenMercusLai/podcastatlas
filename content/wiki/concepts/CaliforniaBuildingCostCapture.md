# California Building Cost Capture

## Definition
California building cost capture is the source's claim that California housing and construction costs are inflated by a linked system of litigation, fees, labor rules, building mandates, and union influence.

## Current Synthesis
Hilton's housing argument overlaps with the wiki's broader supply mechanics but adds a capture diagnosis. The issue is not only that California builds too little housing; it is that multiple policy layers can make each unit more expensive and can make reform politically costly for legislators who depend on organized labor and allied interest groups.

The concept should remain source-scoped because the episode does not independently quantify each cost driver. Its value is as a campaign theory of why housing reform stalls even when many policymakers say they support affordability.

## Key Claims
- Housing scarcity and high construction costs are presented as linked, not separate.
- Building codes, EV-charging rules, energy mandates, impact fees, litigation, project labor agreements, and prevailing wage rules are named as cost layers.
- Union influence is treated as a veto point that can block otherwise popular housing reforms.
- The episode frames recent CEQA exemptions as incomplete if they preserve union-linked labor conditions.
- Ballot initiatives are presented as one possible route around legislative capture, though Hilton says his effort did not qualify in time.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not compare official California construction-cost data, union wage benefits, environmental review benefits, or affordability outcomes in jurisdictions with different rules. It should therefore be read as Hilton's capture thesis rather than a complete housing-cost model.
