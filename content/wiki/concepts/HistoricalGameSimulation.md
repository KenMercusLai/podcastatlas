# Historical Game Simulation

## Definition

Historical game simulation is the representation of past systems through rules, variables, roles, and player decisions that make constraints experiential while inevitably simplifying causation and possible outcomes.

## Current Synthesis

[[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] presents historical games as both teaching devices and designed funnels. Risk, Diplomacy, wargames, and grand strategy can expose players to alliance, betrayal, logistics, production, institutional change, and the pressure of incomplete choices. Yet a simulation must specify what counts, what can be chosen, and what constitutes success. Conquest, territorial coloring, industrial growth, colonization, and military victory can then feel historically natural because they are legible and rewarding mechanics.

## Key Claims

- Rules make selected historical pressures graspable by turning them into repeated decisions and consequences.
- Simulation can teach strategic interaction without reproducing the full social, moral, and causal field of history.
- Detail increases texture but can also create administrative burden or false confidence in authenticity.
- Counterfactual agency operates only inside possibilities, incentives, and victory conditions chosen by designers.
- Conquest is mechanically clear and compelling, which helps explain its prevalence while also narrowing historical imagination.

## Evidence

### Strategic pressure

- [[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] uses Diplomacy and reported state use of wargaming to show how games model simultaneous action, betrayal, variables, and uncertain success.

### Detail and playability

- [[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] contrasts the extreme logistics of *The Campaign for North Africa* with the burden that hyper-detailed systems impose on players.

### Designed development paths

- [[40-history-as-entertainment-glt9519276470]] uses [[CivilizationGameSeries|Civilization]] and Europa Universalis to show how technological, institutional, colonial, and state-development assumptions become rules.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not evaluate game editions, player strategies, classroom outcomes, military exercises, or designer intent systematically. A conquest mechanic need not make a player endorse conquest, and players can use systems critically or pursue alternative goals. Conversely, complexity and factual detail do not by themselves make a model historically sound.

## What Changed

- Established the tradeoff between experiential strategic learning and designer-imposed historical funnels.
