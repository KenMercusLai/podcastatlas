## Definition
The Guanlong Group is Chen Yinke's model of a military-political coalition formed by Western Wei's integration of heterogeneous elites around Guanzhong and Longyou and claimed to persist into Sui-Tang rule.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats coalition formation as more secure than long duration. [[YuwenTai|Yuwen Tai]] had reasons to bind weaker, diverse forces through origin reassignment, bestowed Xianbei surnames, military hierarchy, and a common regional orientation. Those mechanisms support identifying a Guanlong political community in Western Wei and Northern Zhou. Whether the same group remained a coherent ruling bloc until Wu Zetian is disputed: unification expanded the recruitment field and could dilute regional exclusivity even if families, institutions, and political habits retained influence.

## Key Claims
- The model explains political integration under conditions of eastern demographic and material superiority.
- Shared identity was constructed through administrative, genealogical, symbolic, and military measures rather than inherited unchanged.
- Western Wei and Northern Zhou provide the strongest setting for treating the coalition as an organized political community.
- Elite families and political habits may persist without proving an unchanged, monopolistic group across generations.
- The Sui empire's need to recruit nationally weakens strong claims of uninterrupted regional exclusivity.
- Wu Zetian's role as the group's final destroyer remains a thesis, not an uncontested endpoint.

## Evidence
- Formation mechanisms: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] links origin, surname, and military reorganization to Guanzhong cohesion.
- Institutional carrier: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] connects the coalition with the Eight Pillars and fubing hierarchy.
- Long-duration thesis: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] attributes persistence into Tang and destruction under Wu Zetian to Chen Yinke's interpretation.
- Dilution objection: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] reports Huang Yongnian's argument that Sui unification required broader recruitment.
- Political-culture test: [[ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj-ltxaost4pgqhjdirwas15vizersj]] presents Li Mi's behavior as a possible, but inferential, survival of Guanlong legitimation practice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Continuity of families, offices, or abdication practices is not by itself proof of a bounded group with stable membership and shared action. The source distinguishes the relatively well-supported integration policy from the far more disputed duration thesis. Li Mi's supposed plan to imitate Li Yuan remains an interpretation of behavior.

## What Changed
- Established the formation-versus-duration distinction as the central judgment on the Guanlong Group thesis.
