# Couple-Level Decision-Making / 伴侣共同决策

## Definition
Couple-level decision-making is the practice of evaluating consequential choices through their effects on both partners and the shared household rather than treating one person's career, relocation, fertility, or financial choice as private until its costs arrive.

## Current Synthesis
The episode's Shanghai-job example distinguishes disagreement with an outcome from exclusion from the decision process. A move can be reasonable, and one partner need not always come first, but distance, duplicate housing, income, care, career loss, and the effect on common life belong inside the original calculation. This makes the couple a real decision unit without dissolving either person into it.

Shared decision-making also changes the meaning of sacrifice. A concession that is discussed, understood, and chosen differs from a sacrifice one person silently makes and later expects the other to value automatically. Sustainable partnership therefore needs visible tradeoffs, alternating accommodation where possible, and protection against a permanent pattern in which only one person's plans define the household.

## Key Claims
- Major individual choices can create household-level costs and therefore require shared consideration.
- Joint decision-making does not require one partner's interests to win every time.
- Career, location, housing, income, care, and risk should be evaluated together rather than sequentially.
- Sacrifice should be explicit enough for both people to understand its value and alternatives.
- Accommodation can be uneven in one decision without becoming permanently one-sided.
- Treating the couple as a unit should preserve each person's agency, work, and independent worth.

## Evidence
- Career-and-location case: [[ep132-lingzheng-santianqian-wo-konghunle-cmcoosadbrdubrkxrgbjepiy]] describes conflict when a proposed Shanghai job is considered without first including distance, rent, and shared-life effects.
- Sacrifice visibility: [[ep132-lingzheng-santianqian-wo-konghunle-cmcoosadbrdubrkxrgbjepiy]] argues that giving up work or relocating should be openly evaluated rather than assumed to be mutually understood.
- Alternating accommodation: [[ep132-lingzheng-santianqian-wo-konghunle-cmcoosadbrdubrkxrgbjepiy]] permits flexible compromise on non-principled issues while rejecting chronic unilateral burden.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers participant experience rather than a tested decision protocol. Equal voice does not imply equal resources, bargaining power, urgency, legal rights, or exposure to harm. Emergencies may require fast unilateral action, and shared-unit language can be abused to suppress a partner's work, bodily autonomy, finances, outside relationships, or safe exit. Joint process is not a substitute for consent or independent support.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish household-aware choice from either individual unilateralism or loss of self.
