# 建国功臣排序 / Founding Merit Hierarchy

建国功臣排序 / founding merit hierarchy enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-174-liubang-weihe-dafeng-zijide-chouren-1-lnzzvpieqtgatmvgogz3msyhs60u|Hanji 174]] as [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s answer to generals who object that [[XiaoHe|萧何]] receives more households despite not fighting on the front line. Liu Bang uses the hunting metaphor of "功狗" and "功人": those who chase prey are valuable, but the person who directs the hunt and organizes the system is ranked higher.

The concept is narrower than generic [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|因功赏罚]]. It concerns how a founding ruler defines different kinds of contribution after victory: frontline combat, logistics, recruitment, supply, strategic counsel, and recommendation channels must be ranked against one another before rewards can become legitimate.

Hanji 174 uses Xiao He as the central case, but it also sets up [[ZhangLiang|张良]] and [[ChenPing|陈平]] as nearby variants. Zhang Liang lowers his own reward exposure, while Chen Ping receives credit and then redirects credit to [[WeiWuzhi|魏无知]]. The hierarchy therefore includes both contribution type and political handling of reward.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-174-liubang-weihe-dafeng-zijide-chouren-2-luet7gqkako8idt9o3ccqq7cgpbt|Hanji 174 part 2]] makes the hierarchy explicit after the symbolic [[YongChi|雍齿]] reward stabilizes the room. The first-class ranking places Xiao He first, [[CaoShen|曹参]] second, and [[ZhangAo|张敖]] third; [[EQianqiu|鄂千秋]] then explains why Cao Shen's battlefield wounds and city captures are still "一时之功" beside Xiao He's continuous supply of troops and grain from [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]].

## Key Claims
- Founding reward order must compare unlike contributions, not only count battlefield kills.
- Rear logistics and organizational command can be ranked above frontline danger when the ruler defines victory as system output.
- A public explanation of reward hierarchy prevents veterans from treating unequal grants as arbitrary favoritism.
- Reward acceptance itself can become political behavior: restraint, credit-sharing, and refusal may lower future suspicion.
- Formal ranking needs interpreters as well as rulers: E Qianqiu's explanation makes Liu Bang's allocation defensible to combat-focused observers.
