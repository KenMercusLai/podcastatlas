# Systemically Important Bank Capital Coverage / 系统重要性银行资本覆盖

## Definition
Systemically important bank capital coverage is a prudential requirement that a banking parent fund specified subsidiary exposures with common equity capable of absorbing losses, reducing the risk that a thinly capitalized parent must sell assets or seek state support when a foreign unit fails.

## Current Synthesis
The Swiss proposal described by [[ShangyeJiushiZheyang|商业就是这样]] targets the mismatch between a globally large banking group and a comparatively small home-state backstop. Requiring [[UBS|UBS / 瑞银]] to cover 90% of the carrying value of foreign-subsidiary holdings with common-equity Tier 1 capital would strengthen loss absorption, but it would also retain funds that shareholders might otherwise receive and could encourage asset sales or a smaller balance sheet. The rule is therefore both a resilience mechanism and a distributional choice.

## Key Claims
- Parent-level equity coverage protects against losses passing upward from foreign subsidiaries into a weakly capitalized home entity.
- Common equity remains usable in banking operations but cannot simultaneously serve as distributable dividends or buybacks.
- A rule becomes more economically consequential when one bank is exceptionally large relative to its home country's economy.
- Higher capital can be met through new equity, retained earnings, lower distributions, asset sales, or balance-sheet contraction, each assigning cost differently.
- Cross-country similarity in prudential aims does not imply identical coverage ratios or calculations.

## Evidence
- Mechanism and scale: [[shangye-xiaoyang-51-shenme-ruiyin-yaoba-zongbu-banli-ruishi-gkwrijiotaj1agndggtvuvds]] explains the proposed 90% coverage rule against UBS's reported $1.7tn asset base and foreign-subsidiary structure.
- Cost channels: [[shangye-xiaoyang-51-shenme-ruiyin-yaoba-zongbu-banli-ruishi-gkwrijiotaj1agndggtvuvds]] gives about $16bn as the revised additional-capital estimate and identifies issuance, reduced distributions, and asset sales as responses.
- Policy origin: [[shangye-xiaoyang-51-shenme-ruiyin-yaoba-zongbu-banli-ruishi-gkwrijiotaj1agndggtvuvds]] connects the proposal to the 2023 Credit Suisse rescue and the larger combined UBS.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The proposal is unfinished, and the episode does not supply the full draft law, transition period, deductions, group accounting, stress assumptions, or official cross-country comparison. More capital can reduce shareholder returns without proving that a rule is excessive; conversely, a high ratio does not by itself guarantee resolvability or prevent a run. The 90%, $16bn, $17.7bn, asset, and GDP figures remain source-scoped and time-sensitive.

## What Changed
- Added the parent-versus-foreign-subsidiary capital mismatch as a distinct systemic-risk mechanism.
- Connected prudential resilience directly to dividends, buybacks, asset sales, and bank scale.
- Added the home-state capacity problem created when a rescued bank becomes larger after consolidation.
