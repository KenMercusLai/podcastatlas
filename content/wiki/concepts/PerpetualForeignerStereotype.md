# Perpetual Foreigner Stereotype / 永远的外国人刻板印象

## Definition
The perpetual-foreigner stereotype treats Asian-looking people as externally rooted, culturally alien, or conditionally loyal regardless of citizenship, birthplace, language, or generation.

## Current Synthesis
Foreignness can be assigned through appearance, name, accent expectation, media casting, or international rivalry rather than actual legal or biographical connection to another state. The stereotype makes belonging endlessly provable: later-generation Asian Americans may still be asked where they “really” come from, while political tension can turn ordinary people into proxies for foreign governments.

## Key Claims
- Citizenship and birthplace do not automatically secure social recognition as American.
- Questions about “real” origin can convert curiosity into a repeated denial of stated belonging.
- Media demands for accents or interchangeable Asian casting reproduce foreignness even when performers are native English speakers.
- Geopolitical tension can turn assumed foreignness into suspicion of loyalty or espionage.
- The stereotype collapses distinct Asian nationalities and migration histories into racial appearance.
- Attempts to prove Americanness can encourage distance between later generations and newer immigrants.

## Evidence
- Everyday belonging: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] contrasts recent immigrants' language and integration concerns with later generations' sensitivity to questions implying they are not truly American.
- Media reproduction: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] reports that Asian actors may be asked to use foreign accents or substitute across national identities.
- Political suspicion: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] connects state rivalry and espionage discourse to distrust of Asian people in the United States.
- Historical example: [[ep169-meiguo-shehui-ruhe-zhizao-mofan-shaoshu-he-beihou-de-yinxing-qishi-ckwriaiewlfyabaaaacimxkl]] uses [[VincentChin]] to show American birth and Chinese identity overridden by anti-Japanese racial misidentification.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Not every origin question or discussion of transnational ties is exclusionary. Meaning depends on context, relationship, repetition, power, and whether a person's stated identity is respected. Some immigrants actively maintain economic, political, and cultural ties abroad, but those ties do not justify inferring uniform loyalty from appearance.

## What Changed
- Established social foreignness as distinct from legal citizenship or actual transnational connection.
- Connected everyday origin questions, media casting, and geopolitical suspicion within one belonging mechanism.
