# Atlantic Slave Trade-Privateering Link

Atlantic slave trade-privateering link names the episode's pattern where early English maritime expansion joins slaving, illegal trade, Protestant rivalry, revenge, and raiding. [[693-elizabeth-i-vs-the-catholics-englands-greatest-hero-part-3-glt5069845329]] develops it through [[JohnHawkins]] and [[FrancisDrake]] before Drake becomes a celebrated privateering figure.

The source says Hawkins's slaving voyages combine Protestantism, patriotism, and greed. Drake's presence on the 1567 expedition and the disaster at [[SanJuanDeUlua]] connect the brutality of Atlantic slavery with the later anti-Spanish grievance that pushes Drake toward raids on [[NombreDeDios]] and the Pacific treasure system.

The same source complicates any simple moral label because Drake later relies on [[DiegoDrakeAlly|Diego]] and the [[Cimarrones]], and is said to offer enslaved people aboard the [[NuestraSenoraDeLaConcepcion]] freedom and sailors' wages. That does not erase his slaving role; it shows how anti-Spanish alliance and profit could coexist with inconsistent moral behavior.

## Key Claims
- English maritime expansion in the source is not cleanly separated into exploration, trade, slavery, and war.
- A participant in slaving can later ally with escaped enslaved fighters for strategic and anti-Spanish reasons.
- Moral contradiction is part of the historical explanation, not a detail to resolve away.
