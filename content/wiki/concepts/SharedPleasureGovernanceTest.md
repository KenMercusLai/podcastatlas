# Shared Pleasure Governance Test / 与民同乐治理检验

The shared pleasure governance test is the Mencian criterion used in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-919-xiang-huode-hao-xian-tikai-shanliang-de-ziji-lrqxfzir4bqxt0v3ccvfef8faade|Hanji 919]] to judge whether a ruler's park, garden, or pleasure project is legitimate. The source contrasts [[ZhouWenwang|King Wen of Zhou]]'s large park, which common people could still use for woodcutting and hunting, with [[QiXuanWang|King Xuan of Qi]]'s smaller park, where exclusion and punishment made it feel like a trap.

The concept matters because it moves the judgment away from size or precedent and toward access, livelihood, and public benefit. When [[EmperorLingOfHan|Emperor Ling of Han]] builds gardens by taking fields and displacing residents, the "shared pleasure" language fails the test even if it borrows the vocabulary of [[MenciusText|Mencius]].

## Key Claims
- Public enjoyment is legitimate only when common people can share in or at least not be harmed by the project.
- Classical precedent cannot be applied without its institutional conditions.
- A garden that destroys fields and excludes residents is private extraction, not shared pleasure.
