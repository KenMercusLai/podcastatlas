# Opponent Resolve Miscalculation

## Definition

Opponent resolve miscalculation is the strategic error of treating an adversary's earlier restraint, internal debate, procedural delay, or preference for negotiation as proof that it will not act when commitments and thresholds change.

## Current Synthesis

The episode grounds the concept in [[AdolfHitler]]'s forecast of British and French behavior before the [[InvasionOfPoland1939|invasion of Poland]]. Munich, democratic deliberation, fear of war, and weak Anglo-French diplomacy toward Moscow encouraged him to read caution as permanent weakness. The [[MolotovRibbentropPact]] then appeared to remove the coalition condition he thought Britain and France required.

Contrary evidence accumulated without correcting the forecast. Britain reaffirmed its guarantee, Neville Henderson delivered explicit warnings, Chamberlain refused another compelled concession, mobilization followed the invasion, and an ultimatum set a deadline. Hitler continued to interpret these signals through the expectation that British politics would fracture. The error was therefore not absence of warning but a model that discounted warnings inconsistent with prior success.

## Key Claims

- Past accommodation can reflect temporary constraints or threshold choices rather than durable unwillingness to fight.
- Democratic debate and procedural delay are poor proxies for ultimate resolve.
- A favorable alliance shift may alter risk without neutralizing an opponent's independent commitments.
- Repeated success with coercive gambles can harden the decision-maker's model and reduce responsiveness to disconfirming signals.
- Explicit warnings do not deter when they are reinterpreted as bluff through an overlearned model of the opponent.

## Evidence

- Learning from prior restraint: [[531-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-pact-with-stalin-part-2-glt5437884494]] connects Hitler's confidence to Munich, beliefs about democratic weakness, and assumptions about British cabinet instability.
- Coalition inference: [[531-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-pact-with-stalin-part-2-glt5437884494]] shows Hitler treating the Soviet pact as sufficient to make Britain and France abandon Poland.
- Disconfirming signals: [[531-hitlers-war-on-poland-the-pact-with-stalin-part-2-glt5437884494]] records the reaffirmed guarantee, Henderson's warnings, mobilization, ultimatum, and declaration of war.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not imply that British and French policy before September 1939 was consistently credible, prompt, or militarily effective. Their pressure against Polish mobilization and weak mission to Moscow could reasonably create doubt, and their later declaration did not deliver immediate relief to Poland. The source does not isolate one psychological cause from ideology, risk preference, intelligence, institutional filtering, wishful thinking, or strategic incentive. Counterfactual claims about what different signaling would have deterred remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established the distinction between earlier restraint and resolve after a commitment threshold changes.
- Added alliance-change overconfidence and discounting of explicit warnings as linked failure mechanisms.
