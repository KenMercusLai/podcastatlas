# Lived Faith Discernment / 信仰作为生活中的信任与辨认

## Definition

Lived faith discernment is [[BaoZhiguang|包智光]]'s account of religious belief as trust received through testimony and community, tested over time in a life, and assessed by its practical fruit rather than by scientific proof, emotional intensity, or total certainty.

## Current Synthesis

[[ai-ziyou-yu-shangdi-de-chenmo-cong-wuli-boshi-dao-chuanjiaoshi-faguo-shenfu-baozhiguang-de-xinyang-zhi-lu-f48bcfd2-4449-4441-a892-26971c783974]] separates faith from an experiment that captures God and from knowledge that logically compels assent. Bao describes trust beginning through encounters with believers, deepening through the Gospels and communal testimony, and remaining open to questions because doubt can participate in a search for truth.

The proposed check is longitudinal and ethical: does this trust help a person move toward justice, peace, service, and [[LoveAsFreeSelfGift|love]]? Felt absence does not by itself falsify faith or prove moral failure, while confidence does not guarantee truth; discernment therefore includes humility, revisable conscience, and the risk of mistaking a self-serving image of God for God.

## Key Claims

- Scientific evidence is not offered as proof of the Christian God.
- Testimony and community can mediate trust without making belief logically compulsory.
- Faith can persist without unusual bodily, emotional, or intellectual sensations.
- Justice, peace, service, and love are practical fruits by which a faith-shaped life can be assessed.
- Honest doubt and questions can serve truth rather than merely oppose belief.
- Conscience should be followed sincerely while remaining open to correction.
- Certainty about one's own faith or divine approval is limited, making humility part of discernment.

## Evidence

- Origin and mediation: [[ai-ziyou-yu-shangdi-de-chenmo-cong-wuli-boshi-dao-chuanjiaoshi-faguo-shenfu-baozhiguang-de-xinyang-zhi-lu-f48bcfd2-4449-4441-a892-26971c783974]] presents Bao's believing friends and later Gospel reading as the path into trust.
- Practical fruit: [[ai-ziyou-yu-shangdi-de-chenmo-cong-wuli-boshi-dao-chuanjiaoshi-faguo-shenfu-baozhiguang-de-xinyang-zhi-lu-f48bcfd2-4449-4441-a892-26971c783974]] names justice, peace, loving others, and service as observable consequences more useful than demanding a special feeling.
- Doubt and uncertainty: [[ai-ziyou-yu-shangdi-de-chenmo-cong-wuli-boshi-dao-chuanjiaoshi-faguo-shenfu-baozhiguang-de-xinyang-zhi-lu-f48bcfd2-4449-4441-a892-26971c783974]] defends sincere questioning, warns against self-made idols, and accepts that believers cannot obtain total assurance about their own interpretation.
- Conscience: [[ai-ziyou-yu-shangdi-de-chenmo-cong-wuli-boshi-dao-chuanjiaoshi-faguo-shenfu-baozhiguang-de-xinyang-zhi-lu-f48bcfd2-4449-4441-a892-26971c783974]] presents conscience as binding in the present yet still requiring examination and possible revision.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is one Catholic priest's theological and autobiographical framework, not empirical proof that the beliefs are true or that moral fruit uniquely identifies Christian faith. Other religious and nonreligious traditions can produce justice, peace, service, and love, and harmful communities can also interpret loyalty as evidence of belief. The episode does not provide a general method for resolving conflicts among conscience, doctrine, community authority, psychological need, and external evidence.
