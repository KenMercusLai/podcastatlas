## Definition

Qin Collapse Overextension / 秦亡多因素解释 is the source's argument that Qin's fall should be read as a state-capacity and coalition failure, not as a sudden disappearance of Qin battlefield ability.

## Current Synthesis

The episode argues that [[QinState|秦]] still had real military strength during the rebellion, especially through [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]]'s campaigns. The collapse becomes legible only when military power is placed inside a wider system: conquest expanded faster than durable rule, eastern regions remained difficult to control through a rapidly imposed commandery-county order, court politics under [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] damaged central execution, frontier and southern campaigns strained available capacity, and the eastern restored states created a more effective anti-Qin alignment than they had achieved in the late Warring States.

## Key Claims

- Qin military capacity persisted into the rebellion; the empire did not simply forget how to fight.
- Expansion after unification exceeded the center's ability to integrate distant former states quickly.
- Full commandery-county implementation made control more brittle than looser delegated rule might have been.
- Court conflict weakened Qin's ability to convert field military pressure into stable recovery.
- The eastern states' unusually effective anti-Qin coalition mattered because their combined strength could exceed Qin when coordination held.

## Evidence

- Persistent military capacity: [[494-zhang-shi-ping-tan-zhan-zheng-shi-shi-jiao-xia-de-qin-mo-han-chu-hun-zhan-lrc9hn-yrw9lkwujl65ummq-qykj]] says Zhang Han's long confrontation with Xiang Yu shows Qin's main forces retained fighting ability.
- Overextension evidence: [[494-zhang-shi-ping-tan-zhan-zheng-shi-shi-jiao-xia-de-qin-mo-han-chu-hun-zhan-lrc9hn-yrw9lkwujl65ummq-qykj]] stresses that Qin annexed territory larger than its original base within a very short period and faced acknowledged distance-control problems in Yan, Qi, Zhao, and Chu regions.
- Political failure evidence: [[494-zhang-shi-ping-tan-zhan-zheng-shi-shi-jiao-xia-de-qin-mo-han-chu-hun-zhan-lrc9hn-yrw9lkwujl65ummq-qykj]] presents Zhao Gao-era court conflict as a major reason Zhang Han's field situation became politically untenable.
- Coalition evidence: [[494-zhang-shi-ping-tan-zhan-zheng-shi-shi-jiao-xia-de-qin-mo-han-chu-hun-zhan-lrc9hn-yrw9lkwujl65ummq-qykj]] says the eastern states finally achieved something like a real hezong after earlier failures to sustain coordination against Qin.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not deny Qin coercion, harsh law, revolt pressure, or battlefield defeats. It narrows the claim to causation: military defeat alone is insufficient to explain collapse without overextension, court politics, coalition pressure, and legitimacy problems. Troop numbers in the northern Xiongnu and southern Yue campaigns remain source-scoped estimates rather than fixed figures.

## What Changed

- Created a Qin-collapse concept that centers state capacity, overextension, court conflict, and coalition pressure.
