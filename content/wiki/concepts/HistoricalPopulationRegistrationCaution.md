## Definition

户口数字解读谨慎 / historical population registration caution is the methodological rule that official household and population records should be read as state-registration evidence, not as direct counts of everyone alive or everyone dead.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 388-3 creates this concept while discussing ancient Chinese dynastic population drops. The episode accepts that war and regime change caused enormous demographic loss, but it warns that the low points in official histories cannot simply be subtracted from earlier peaks to get actual deaths. Missing registration, hidden households,屯田 populations outside county rolls, uncounted minority groups, migration, and administrative breakdown all intervene between record and reality.

The concept is therefore a guardrail, not a minimization. It prevents over-literal arithmetic while still leaving room for severe human loss after correction.

## Key Claims

- Official household and population figures measure administrative reach as well as demographic reality.
- Registry collapse can reflect death, displacement, concealment, category exclusion, and state-capacity loss at the same time.
- Corrected estimates can still show catastrophic population loss even when raw records overstate the drop.
- The method is especially important when comparing dynastic-transition peaks and troughs across long periods.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode gives a compact popular-history presentation rather than a full demographic model. This concept should not be used to dismiss war mortality; it only says registry arithmetic is not enough by itself. Every period still needs separate evidence for the shares caused by death, migration, hidden registration, famine, epidemic, and administrative collapse.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 388-3 to preserve the episode's caution against literal population-record subtraction.
