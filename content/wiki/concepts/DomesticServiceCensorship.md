# Domestic Service Censorship

Domestic service censorship is censorship embedded inside state-approved local apps, search engines, and communication services rather than only at the boundary with foreign platforms. [[tech-20260402-0402-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260402-0402-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the [[Iran]] case through [[AmirRashidi]]'s claim that the [[NationalInformationNetwork|National Information Network]] includes domestic equivalents for many global services, but the national search engine returns no results for "war."

The concept distinguishes access from openness. A user may still have domestic connectivity, payment, search, delivery, or messaging, yet sensitive topics can be suppressed inside those services before outside platforms even enter the picture.

## Key Claims
- Blocking foreign platforms is only one layer of control; domestic services can also filter politically sensitive queries or functions.
- Domestic search censorship can make wartime conditions harder to discuss even when some networked life continues.
- The more useful the domestic service stack becomes, the more censorship can operate through ordinary convenience rather than obvious disconnection.
