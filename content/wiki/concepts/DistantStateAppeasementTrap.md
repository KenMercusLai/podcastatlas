# Distant-State Appeasement Trap / 远国安抚误读

## Definition
Distant-state appeasement trap / 远国安抚误读 is the error in which a distant state treats an expansionist power's temporary friendship as acceptance of its long-term independence, when the stronger power is actually sequencing nearer targets first.

## Current Synthesis
Late [[QiState|齐国]] shows why the trap can be attractive and real rather than merely cowardly. [[JunWangHou|君王后]]'s neutrality and caution preserve more than forty years of peace, while Qin has its own reason to reassure distant Qi under [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]]. The misreading hardens when peace becomes evidence against preparation: [[HouSheng|后胜]] and Qin-rewarded guests normalize friendship, Qi stays outside collective defense, and [[QiWangJian|齐王建 / 田建]] nearly travels personally to Qin. When Qin finally reaches Qi from the north, the policy has preserved lives and time but not a viable independent endgame.

## Key Claims
- Temporary safety may result from conquest sequencing rather than stable mutual restraint.
- Real peace benefits can make the weaker state's threat model progressively harder to revise.
- Adviser capture and selectively favorable cross-border contacts can turn diplomatic reassurance into an information-system failure.
- Repeated non-intervention removes buffers while allowing military readiness to decay.
- The endpoint may preserve population through surrender even while failing to preserve the state.

## Evidence
- **Peace and sequencing:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]] links Qi's long peace to both Jun Wang Hou's policy and Qin's far-friend/near-attack incentives.
- **Information capture and preparation failure:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-4-shishang-neng-cheng-juying-de-junwang-qiwangjian-livcg5uz60ne-6oje-qzjfpghhph]] shows Hou Sheng, rewarded guests, the proposed royal visit, absence from anti-Qin action, and declining readiness reinforcing one another.
- **Terminal outcome and humanitarian complication:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]] records Qin's northern entry, Qi Wang Jian's surrender, the broken land promise, and the casualties avoided by not fighting a final large war.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The policy did buy decades of peace, so its value cannot be judged only from the terminal outcome. Qi's final military inferiority also means that late resistance proposals may already have been unrealistic. The sources do not establish that an earlier intervention or coalition would certainly have defeated Qin, and the humanitarian value of avoiding a last war remains distinct from the failure to preserve sovereignty.

## What Changed
- The trap now includes a concrete captured-advice mechanism through Hou Sheng and rewarded guests.
- Military-readiness erosion is added as a consequence of prolonged strategic reassurance.
- Yongmen Sima's successful intervention shows that correcting one action does not automatically repair the underlying strategy.
