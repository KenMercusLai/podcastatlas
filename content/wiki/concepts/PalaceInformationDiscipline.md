## Definition

宫廷信息纪律 / palace information discipline is the habit of treating even small details about palace life, ruler behavior, recommendations, and internal deliberation as politically sensitive information that must not leak into ordinary conversation.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-540-taihou-de-zangli-lnd6gmr_bk7dv8cbb1zzoagtbwsd|Hanji 540]] grounds the concept through [[KongGuangWesternHan|孔光]]. Kong Guang's caution is not limited to formal state secrets. The episode says he tears up prepared drafts after speaking, avoids publicizing advice that would expose the ruler's faults, recommends capable people without letting them know, and refuses even harmless-seeming household questions about palace rooms or trees.

The political point is that palace information can create obligations, factions, embarrassment, or evidence. Kong Guang's silence is therefore both bureaucratic craft and self-protection: it preserves access by proving he will not convert proximity into status display or private advantage.

## Key Claims

- Palace information discipline treats small details as politically sensitive when they prove access to the ruler.
- Destroying drafts and avoiding post-conversation evidence can be part of official self-protection.
- Recommendation secrecy reduces private gratitude networks as well as exposure if the recommendation fails.
- Consistent silence in family and leisure settings matters because political leakage often begins outside formal office.
- The pattern can preserve office access, but it may also weaken forceful correction when caution becomes default non-confrontation.

## Evidence

Draft and advice control:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-540-taihou-de-zangli-lnd6gmr_bk7dv8cbb1zzoagtbwsd|Hanji 540]] says Kong Guang destroys drafts after speaking and avoids using the ruler's faults to display ministerial loyalty.

Recommendation secrecy:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-540-taihou-de-zangli-lnd6gmr_bk7dv8cbb1zzoagtbwsd|Hanji 540]] says Kong Guang recommends capable people cautiously and tries not to let them know he recommended them.

Household silence:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-540-taihou-de-zangli-lnd6gmr_bk7dv8cbb1zzoagtbwsd|Hanji 540]] says Kong Guang refuses family questions about palace circumstances and redirects a question about trees in the palace warm hall.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source praises Kong Guang's discretion, but the current evidence does not prove whether his caution always served public duty. Hanji 540 also says he did not press when his advice was not accepted, so this concept should remain paired with advice and accountability concepts rather than treated as an unqualified virtue.
