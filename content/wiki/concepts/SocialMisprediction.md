# Social Misprediction

## Definition
Social misprediction is the recurring error in forecasting other people's interest, warmth, willingness to help, trustworthiness, openness, or response to conversation and disagreement, often in a more pessimistic direction than later interaction supports.

## Current Synthesis
[[NickEpley]] frames social life as mind reading under uncertainty. People use self-projection, stereotypes, visible behavior, gaze, voice, and prior belief, but ambiguous cues such as silence, phones, or earbuds can make two interested people each infer that the other prefers distance. Avoidance then protects the forecast from correction.

[[JamilZaki]] extends the pattern beyond stranger conversation. People may expect gratitude, helping, vulnerability, confiding, and political disagreement to be more awkward or harmful than they prove to be; they may also underestimate general friendliness, compassion, openness, and trustworthy behavior. Both sources therefore treat beliefs about others as testable predictions, with safe real interaction providing evidence that detached rumination cannot.

[[LaurieSantos]] independently reinforces this under-sociality account and extends it to compliments, gifts, reaching out, and asking for help. The episode also qualifies trait labels: self-described introverts may prefer depth or solitude yet still enjoy a bounded interaction more than they predicted. The error concerns forecast calibration, not a requirement to maximize social exposure.

At larger scale, the same mechanism can exaggerate stereotypes and political rivalry. More information does not automatically improve judgment when platforms foreground fringe actors or when strong priors determine how evidence is interpreted.

A different error direction appears in [[face-value-face-value|Face Value]]: observers can overinterpret behavior that really occurred. [[MissyKurzweil]] accurately saw [[JJGoode]] look toward her and leave, but lacked the hidden fact that he could not identify her away from their usual table. Social inference can therefore fail even when observation is accurate, because the mapping from behavior to intention is underdetermined.

A conversation-specific extension is [[EricaBoothby]]'s [[LikingGap]]: conversation partners may like one another while each mistakes private self-criticism for evidence of the other's judgment. [[SocialAttentionAsymmetry]] explains why the error is not reducible to optimism. People can overestimate how visible a flaw is during interaction, underestimate hidden observation outside it, and underestimate how long their words remain in another person's thoughts. The recurring problem is unequal access to private experience, as presented in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125|Mind Reading 2.0]].

## Key Claims
- People often underestimate strangers' interest in conversation and others' willingness to help.
- People may forecast gratitude, vulnerability, confiding, and disagreement more negatively than the resulting interaction warrants.
- Stereotypes and vivid examples can exaggerate group differences and make fringe behavior seem typical.
- Visible behavior is useful evidence but remains ambiguous without context, voice, reciprocal response, or knowledge of an invisible cognitive difference.
- Avoidance prevents belief updating and can make pessimistic expectations persist without a fair test.
- Small, bounded social experiments can improve calibration in either direction.
- Introversion does not eliminate social reward, while mutual liking can remain hidden when both people use indirect signals or treat private insecurity as another person's verdict.

## Evidence
- Stranger interest and requests: [[how-to-overcome-social-anxiety-dr-nick-epley-scim7834121132]] reports underestimation of conversational interest and willingness to comply with requests.
- Inference limits: [[how-to-overcome-social-anxiety-dr-nick-epley-scim7834121132]] identifies self-projection, stereotypes, behavior, gaze, voice, silence, phones, and earbuds as imperfect mind-reading evidence.
- Broader social forecasts: [[how-to-cultivate-a-positive-growth-oriented-mindset-dr-jamil-zaki-scim9746981563]] extends pessimistic prediction to gratitude, help, vulnerability, confiding, trust, and disagreement.
- Learning interventions: [[how-to-cultivate-a-positive-growth-oriented-mindset-dr-jamil-zaki-scim9746981563]] describes Stanford peer-data interventions and encounter counting as ways to correct mistaken expectations.
- Political scale: both [[how-to-overcome-social-anxiety-dr-nick-epley-scim7834121132]] and [[how-to-cultivate-a-positive-growth-oriented-mindset-dr-jamil-zaki-scim9746981563]] connect prior beliefs and group categories to distorted judgment of political opponents.
- Independent under-sociality account: [[how-to-achieve-true-happiness-using-science-based-protocols-dr-laurie-santos-scim5061134579]] reports that people underestimate stranger conversation, compliments, gratitude, gifts, outreach, and help-seeking, including among introverts.
- Hidden perceptual cause: [[face-value-face-value]] shows Kurzweil revising an understandable rejection inference after Goode's prosopagnosia and a second recognition failure become visible.
- Reciprocal liking: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125]] reports a liking gap in initial conversations and among new roommates, along with developmental and shyness-related findings.
- Unequal access to attention: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-1dfa7e5aca191bd78c9a8028baf43cb1-50295125]] connects perceived transparency, embarrassing-feature salience, hidden observation, and underestimated thought persistence to information imbalance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not say optimistic forecasts are always correct or that all social approach is safe. Friendliness can be misread as intimacy, risk is unevenly distributed, cultural norms differ, and power or abuse can make vulnerability costly. Prosopagnosia should not become a default explanation for disregard, and one awkward encounter cannot diagnose it. Trait labels, preference for solitude, and the need for recovery time should not be treated as defects. Boothby's proposed lifespan and social-anxiety patterns remain unresolved, and apparently opposite attention errors should not be collapsed into a rule that people always notice less. Study findings and clinical claims are summarized in public interviews without complete methods, and the concept should not substitute for assessment of severe social anxiety, depression, trauma, loneliness, or recognition difficulty.

## What Changed
- Added reciprocal liking as a case where mutual positive evaluation is hidden by self-criticism and indirect signaling.
- Added attention asymmetry to explain why people can overestimate scrutiny in one setting and underestimate observation or thought persistence in another.
- Extended corrective evidence from behavioral testing to outside perspective, respectful questions, curiosity, and expressed appreciation.
