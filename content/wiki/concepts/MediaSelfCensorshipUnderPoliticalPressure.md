## Definition
Media self-censorship under political pressure is the anticipatory restriction of advertising, coverage, production, or distribution by media and technology companies seeking to avoid lawsuits, regulatory retaliation, access loss, or other government conflict without receiving a direct censorship order.

## Current Synthesis
The source describes a chilling-effect environment in which presidential lawsuits, FCC pressure, concentrated platform control, and corporate interest in government relations can shape decisions before officials make an explicit demand. The mechanism is anticipatory risk management: firms may narrow content or distribution because they expect conflict, not because a documented order exists.

This mechanism is difficult to prove from individual decisions. A platform error or commercial distribution choice can be genuine, yet the surrounding pressure makes neutral explanations less trusted. The concept therefore concerns both actual self-restraint and the credibility damage that opaque, concentrated decision systems create.

## Key Claims
- Chilling effects can operate through anticipated retaliation rather than explicit censorship orders.
- Platforms and studios may weigh regulatory exposure, litigation, access, and commercial relationships when handling politically sensitive media.
- Distribution concentration increases the consequence of cautious decisions by a small number of firms.
- Genuine moderation mistakes can become politically explosive when institutions lack transparency and trust.
- Motive should remain qualified unless internal records or direct evidence connect pressure to a specific decision.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove that government pressure caused any platform or studio decision. Mistakes, policy ambiguity, commercial judgment, legal review, or ordinary risk management may explain the outcomes. The concept should not be used to convert suspicion into a claim about intent without documentary evidence.

## What Changed
- Established anticipatory media restraint as distinct from direct censorship.
- Added concentrated distribution and government-relations incentives to the mechanism.
- Made institutional mistrust after genuine error part of the chilling-effect analysis.
