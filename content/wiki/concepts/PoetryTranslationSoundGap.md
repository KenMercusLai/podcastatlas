# 诗歌翻译的声音缺口 / Poetry Translation Sound Gap

## Definition
The poetry translation sound gap is the irreducible loss or transformation of lineation, syntax, rhythm, pronunciation, and bodily force when a poem moves between languages.

## Current Synthesis
EP284 argues that translated poetry cannot be evaluated as semantic transfer alone. Chinese habits of five- and seven-character rhythm can pull foreign verse toward familiar cadences, while enjambment, word order, participial motion, and sonic texture resist full relocation. Recitation and cautious return to the original do not erase the gap, but they help readers perceive what a printed translation may conceal.

## Key Claims
- A translation can carry propositional meaning while obscuring how grammar and sound generate the poem's movement.
- A reader's native prosodic habits shape what sounds natural and can hide the foreign poem's structure.
- Enjambment and word order are interpretive features, not ornamental leftovers.
- Recitation restores a bodily and auditory dimension that silent semantic reading can miss.
- Original-language study should deepen respect for translation's difficulty rather than turn translation into failure.

## Evidence
### Native rhythm and formal loss
- [[ep284-yu-miao-shifu-mantan-li-er-ke-naxie-lingxing-juexing-de-shike-lkkydmjftqa-fjhg2uaym_8teinq|EP284]] says Chinese readers' internalized five- and seven-character cadence can obstruct reception of translated foreign verse and that cross-line syntax and sound structures are hard to reproduce.

### Recitation and grammatical movement
- [[ep284-yu-miao-shifu-mantan-li-er-ke-naxie-lingxing-juexing-de-shike-lkkydmjftqa-fjhg2uaym_8teinq|EP284]] uses Dylan Thomas's self-recitation and the present participles in Rilke's “Roman Fountain” to show how audible and grammatical force can exceed translated meaning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not claim that only fluent readers can encounter foreign poetry, nor does it rank one translation against another. Original-language access is itself partial for learners, and sound can move a listener without guaranteeing semantic or historical understanding.

## What Changed
- Established sound and syntax as a single translation boundary rather than optional decoration.
- Added native prosodic habit as a factor in translated-poetry reception.
- Positioned recitation and original-language study as supplements, not cures.
