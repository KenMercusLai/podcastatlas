# Grief Physiology Support

## Definition
Grief physiology support is the source's frame for using sleep, circadian rhythm, autonomic regulation, bodily awareness, NSDR, and qualified support to help the nervous system move through grief remapping.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats grief as emotional and bodily. It links intense yearning to attachment and motivation circuits, including oxytocin-related variation, while cautioning that stronger yearning does not prove greater capacity for love. This keeps biology explanatory without turning grief intensity into a moral ranking.

The support layer is practical and bounded. Sleep, non-sleep deep rest, morning light, healthy cortisol rhythm, vagal-tone-related bodily awareness, slower exhales, heart-rate slowing, and professional or bereavement-group support are presented as ways to support the plasticity required for grief. These tools do not replace grieving itself; they stabilize the physiology in which remapping happens.

## Key Claims
- Grief intensity may vary partly through oxytocin-linked attachment and motivation circuits.
- Biological variation does not rank people by love, loyalty, or moral seriousness.
- Written emotional disclosure may help some bereaved people, with vagal tone presented as a possible moderator.
- Sleep and NSDR are framed as support for neuroplasticity during grief.
- Healthy cortisol rhythm and morning light are presented as baseline physiology supports.
- Exhale-based autonomic regulation may help build capacity to contact bodily feelings of attachment.
- Professional, psychiatric, psychological, or bereavement-group support remains important when grief exceeds self-directed tools.

## Evidence
- Oxytocin and yearning: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] connects prairie vole and human evidence to oxytocin receptors in motivation, craving, and pursuit circuits.
- Moral boundary: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] cautions that intense yearning does not necessarily mean greater attachment capacity than faster-moving grief.
- Emotional disclosure: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] discusses a bereavement writing study where benefit appeared more clearly among participants with higher vagal tone.
- Cortisol rhythm: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] notes that complicated grief was associated with higher late-day cortisol in one comparison.
- Morning light: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] recommends sunlight near waking, or bright indoor light when sun is unavailable, to support cortisol timing.
- Neuroplastic support: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] links sleep and NSDR to the rewiring needed after loss.
- Care support: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] names psychologists, psychiatrists, and bereavement groups as support options.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The physiology claims are source-scoped and do not establish individualized treatment rules. Vagal-tone writing evidence is preliminary and subgroup-dependent in the source's account. Oxytocin, cortisol, sleep, light, breathing, and NSDR claims should not be used to replace clinical assessment for complicated grief, depression, trauma, medication effects, sleep disorders, endocrine disorders, or suicidal ideation.
