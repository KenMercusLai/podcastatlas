# Grief Physiology Support

## Definition
Grief physiology support is the source's frame for using sleep, circadian rhythm, autonomic regulation, bodily awareness, NSDR, and qualified support to help the nervous system move through grief remapping.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat grief as emotional, social, and bodily. Yearning is linked to attachment and motivation circuitry, while acute bereavement is associated with transient cardiovascular and blood-pressure risk. Biology explains why grief can affect the whole organism without ranking the depth of anyone's love or making a single response trajectory mandatory.

Support therefore operates at several levels: sleep and circadian stability, movement, breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, social care, medical attention, and qualified grief treatment. These tools do not eliminate grief or replace [[GriefAttachmentRemapping]]; they help protect the body and widen the capacity to move between loss and restoration. Medication claims require a particularly strict boundary: the aspirin studies described by [[MaryFrancesOConnor]] were proof-of-concept, not randomized treatment evidence or advice for self-medication.

## Key Claims
- Grief intensity may vary partly through attachment, motivation, stress, and autonomic systems.
- Biological variation does not rank people by love, loyalty, or moral seriousness.
- Acute bereavement can increase cardiovascular strain and blood-pressure reactivity.
- Sleep, circadian stability, movement, breathing, and progressive muscle relaxation can support regulation.
- Written disclosure and mindfulness may help some people, but effects can vary by person and intervention.
- Social support and ordinary medical attention belong inside grief literacy, not outside it.
- Persistently worsening or disabling grief may require evidence-based psychological or psychiatric care.

## Evidence
- Oxytocin and yearning: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] connects prairie vole and human evidence to oxytocin receptors in motivation, craving, and pursuit circuits.
- Yearning circuitry: [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] reports greater nucleus-accumbens activity to deceased-person photographs among participants reporting more yearning.
- Moral boundary: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] cautions that intense yearning does not necessarily mean greater attachment capacity than faster-moving grief.
- Emotional disclosure: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] discusses a bereavement writing study where benefit appeared more clearly among participants with higher vagal tone.
- Acute bodily risk: [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] reports elevated heart-attack risk near a loved one's death and grief-wave-related blood-pressure increases.
- Regulation methods: [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] describes running, yoga, breathing, mindfulness, and a study in which progressive muscle relaxation outperformed mindfulness on grief outcomes.
- Circadian and plasticity support: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] links morning light, cortisol timing, sleep, and NSDR to the physiological conditions for adaptation.
- Care support: [[essentials-the-science-process-of-healing-from-grief-scim9466548315]] and [[healing-from-grief-loss-dr-mary-frances-oconnor-scim5061041393]] name social, bereavement-group, psychological, psychiatric, or medical support when self-directed tools are insufficient.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The physiology claims are source-scoped and do not establish individualized treatment rules. Cardiovascular multipliers, blood-pressure findings, grief prevalence, vagal-tone moderation, neuroimaging, oxytocin, cortisol, and comparative relaxation results require their original studies and clinical context. The aspirin work was not a randomized clinical trial and is not advice to start aspirin, which can cause harm. Sleep, light, breathing, movement, or relaxation should not replace clinical assessment for cardiovascular symptoms, prolonged or disabling grief, depression, trauma, substance misuse, sleep or endocrine disorders, or suicidal ideation.

## What Changed
- Added acute cardiovascular and blood-pressure risk to the bodily account of bereavement.
- Added social support, movement, breathing, and progressive muscle relaxation to the support layer.
- Added an explicit boundary against treating preliminary aspirin work as medical advice.
