# Split Globalization / 分裂的全球化

Split globalization is [[176-niuyue-yinian-yige-beiguanzhuyizhe-de-huofa-725-shalong-shilu-lte-k8lw8e1txxw3nfxvo3wal8ah]]'s distinction between two globalization layers moving in different directions. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] accepts that production-network globalization has weakened under geopolitics, supply-chain relocation, and decoupling pressure, but argues that cultural circulation may be accelerating through social media, algorithms, AI, Korean culture, Japanese anime, toys, and creator distribution.

The concept helps separate trade and supply-chain pessimism from cultural-flow observation. A country can become less tightly integrated as a manufacturing or capital partner while its media, design, consumer symbols, and lifestyle formats travel faster than before.

## Key Claims
- Globalization should not be treated as one undifferentiated trend.
- Supply-chain and production integration can retreat while cultural and attention flows intensify.
- Social media, recommendation algorithms, and AI lower translation and discovery costs for cultural products.
- China-U.S. and China-Japan relationships can diverge because the density of people, capital, goods, and business interdependence is different.
- The concept qualifies deglobalization narratives without denying real geopolitical and production-chain fragmentation.
