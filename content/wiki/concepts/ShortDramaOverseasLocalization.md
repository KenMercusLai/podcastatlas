# Short Drama Overseas Localization / 短剧海外本土化

Short drama overseas localization / 短剧海外本土化 is the adaptation work required when Chinese short-drama formulas move abroad. In [[ep240-ba-zong-tian-chong-zai-haiwai-duanju-chuhai-de-chanye-mima-ltvxvfj5ko6djhsjatiphdy-dzek]], localization means more than translation or dubbing: teams change settings, genre shells, casting, dialogue, consent cues, status symbols, and emotional assumptions while preserving the compact hook-and-reversal structure.

[[DuanNongyu|段农裕]]'s actor reporting is the concrete case. Overseas actors correct unnatural lines, question implausible or culturally awkward motivations, and can push scenes toward locally acceptable wording or consent framing. That makes local performers part of the adaptation loop inside [[LiveActionShortDrama]], not just downstream executors.

This concept sits above [[AIShortDramaOverseasLocalization]]. The AI-specific page adds model, asset, and generated-spectacle questions; EP240 shows that the same localization problem also exists in live-action crews, translated dramas, and web-fiction-derived scripts.

## Key Claims
- Localization can preserve a Chinese爽文 structure while changing the surface genre into werewolf, vampire, billionaire, or other locally legible fantasies.
- Native speakers and local actors can detect problems that headquarters script teams miss.
- Value cues such as consent, family obligation, status display, and romantic plausibility can require rewriting, not only better subtitles.
- Self-produced overseas short dramas trade higher cost for better cultural fit and performance credibility.
- Translation/dubbing routes can move faster, but they risk weaker local resonance when story assumptions remain too domestic.
