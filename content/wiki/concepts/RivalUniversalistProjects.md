# Rival Universalist Projects

## Definition

Rival universalist projects are opposing ideological programs that each claim relevance for humanity beyond one nation or locality, even when their values, institutions, and methods are radically different.

## Current Synthesis

[[95-9-11-glt8208243510]] uses the comparison to challenge a simple clash between modern globalisation and local religious reaction. [[OsamaBinLaden|Osama bin Laden]] sought a transnational Muslim struggle against American power, while [[GeorgeWBush|George W. Bush]] presented freedom, rights, and liberal democracy as universally valid. Both operated globally, spoke beyond a single polity, and inherited missionary religious vocabularies, but the comparison concerns scale and structure rather than moral equivalence.

The episode also places [[SayyidQutb|Sayyid Qutb]] and [[FrancisFukuyama|Francis Fukuyama]] in this field. Qutb's Islamic renewal offered a remedy for humanity, while Fukuyama's end-of-history argument was read as a liberal-democratic horizon. The framework therefore treats post-Cold War conflict as competition among global futures, not only resistance to globalisation.

## Key Claims

- Anti-Western militancy can itself be internationalist and globally networked.
- Liberal democracy can function as a culturally specific project while being presented as universal human aspiration.
- Missionary religious inheritances can persist inside both explicitly religious and partly secular political visions.
- Structural comparison does not erase differences in legitimacy, violence, institutions, or moral responsibility.
- Rival universalism helps explain why globalisation can intensify ideological conflict rather than dissolve it.

## Evidence

### Bin Laden and Bush

- [[95-9-11-glt8208243510]] explicitly frames bin Laden's global jihad and Bush's universal rights-and-freedom language as competing worldwide missions.

### Intellectual precursors

- [[95-9-11-glt8208243510]] contrasts Qutb's universal Islamic remedy with Fukuyama's liberal-democratic endpoint and Benjamin Barber's more localist “Jihad versus McWorld” frame.

### Local versus global militancy

- [[95-9-11-glt8208243510]] uses Taliban suspicion of al-Qaeda's rootless internationalism to show that jihadist actors cannot all be placed on one global-local axis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The comparison is analytical, not an equation between a terrorist organization and a constitutional state. It can obscure differences in coercive capacity, accountability, law, constituency, and deliberate targeting of civilians. The source also compresses Islam, Christianity, liberalism, nationalism, and globalisation into a conversational thesis that requires wider historical testing.

## What Changed

- Established a comparative frame for post-Cold War conflict among global missions rather than a binary of global modernity and local tradition.
- Made the non-equivalence qualification explicit.
