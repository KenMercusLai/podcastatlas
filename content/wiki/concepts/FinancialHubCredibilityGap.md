# Financial Hub Credibility Gap

Financial hub credibility gap is the distance between a city's aspiration to become a financial center and the trust needed for large, durable capital migration. [[the-case-of-the-missing-totem-aung-san-suu-kyi-6a560fc4f96471dde4821d2f]] adds it through [[Turkey]]'s effort to present [[Istanbul]] as a new Dubai or Riyadh.

The episode says tax breaks for wealthy foreigners and the [[IstanbulFinancialCenter]] show ambition, while [[MehmetSimsek]]'s more orthodox policy has improved Turkey's macro picture. But investors still worry about inflation, lira support, political risk under [[RecepTayyipErdogan]], weaker infrastructure, and whether announced interest is mostly small consultancies rather than major financial institutions.

## Key Claims
- A financial hub needs credibility, legal trust, currency confidence, infrastructure, talent, and political predictability, not only buildings and tax incentives.
- War-driven inflows can test a city but do not by themselves prove durable institutional migration.
- Macroeconomic improvement can remain fragile if investors think it depends on one minister or one leader's tolerance.
- Competing hubs with decades of investment can remain more attractive even when they face temporary disruption.
