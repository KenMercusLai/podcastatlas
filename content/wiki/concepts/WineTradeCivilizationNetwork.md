# Wine Trade Civilization Network

## Definition

Wine trade civilization network is the episode's frame for how [[Wine|wine]] moved from domesticated grape and local fermentation into a Mediterranean and Eurasian cultural system carried by pottery, trade routes, Greek sociability, Roman markets, religious ritual, legal restriction, and poetry.

## Current Synthesis

The source presents wine history as a sequence of material and institutional thresholds. Early Georgian pottery evidence and Armenia's Areni winery make wine archaeologically deep, while Canaanite and Phoenician export networks turn wine into a transportable commodity. Greek settlement and symposia add drinking rules and cultural prestige, and Rome then scales wine through an integrated imperial market that treats wine as a sign of civilization.

The post-Roman and religious layers keep the concept from being only a trade story. Wine survives western imperial fragmentation through river routes, abbey vineyards, and the Eucharist, while Islamic contexts produce a different pattern: legal prohibition and punishment coexist with wine poetry, taverns, technical distinctions, evasion, and mystical metaphor. The network is therefore not simply diffusion of a drink, but repeated redefinition of what wine means inside law, ritual, commerce, and identity.

## Key Claims

- Wine becomes historically visible through archaeology before it becomes a mass commercial good.
- Amphorae and maritime trade turn wine from a local product into a Mediterranean export system.
- Greek wine culture spreads norms of civilized drinking as well as alcohol.
- Roman integration makes wine an imperial market and a civilization marker.
- Christian Eucharistic need helps preserve wine production after western Roman collapse.
- Islamic wine history combines prohibition, punishment, loopholes, tavern culture, poetry, and mystical metaphor.
- Wine's meaning changes with the institutions that carry it: trade, empire, church, law, and literature.

## Evidence

- Archaeological depth: [[666-wine-and-the-birth-of-civilisation-glt1414718476]] places early chemical wine evidence near Tbilisi around 6000 BC and identifies Areni in Armenia as the oldest known winery in the episode's account.
- Export technology and trade: [[666-wine-and-the-birth-of-civilisation-glt1414718476]] credits Canaanites with mass export and amphorae and follows Phoenician merchants across the Mediterranean.
- Greek cultural export: [[666-wine-and-the-birth-of-civilisation-glt1414718476]] uses the Odyssey, dilution, Polyphemus, and symposia to contrast rule-bound drinking with barbarian excess.
- Roman scaling: [[666-wine-and-the-birth-of-civilisation-glt1414718476]] presents Rome as the first properly international wine culture and treats wine trade into Gaul as demand creation inside a conquest world.
- Christian survival route: [[666-wine-and-the-birth-of-civilisation-glt1414718476]] says post-Roman wine survives through remaining trade routes, especially the Rhine, and through abbey vineyards tied to Eucharistic practice.
- Islamic ambivalence: [[666-wine-and-the-birth-of-civilisation-glt1414718476]] describes mainstream prohibition and legal sanctions while also discussing Hanafi distinctions, Baghdad taverns, Abu Nuwas, Ibn Taymiyyah, and Rumi's wine metaphors.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept follows one narrative-history episode and does not settle archaeological priority, the full history of viticulture outside the episode's route, or every Islamic legal position. The source presents Roman wine as culturally integrative but also notes that wine was used commercially to create demand among Gauls before conquest, so the network can be civilizing language and power projection at the same time.
