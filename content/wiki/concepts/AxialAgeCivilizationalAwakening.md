# Axial Age Civilizational Awakening / 轴心时代文明觉醒

## Definition
Axial age civilizational awakening / 轴心时代文明觉醒 is [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-2-zhe-liangwei-shengren-de-lundao-cangzhe-renlei-de-zhongji-mimi-lsjl6awahc0gyhtwh3tagh1utqx4|Hanji 456-2]]'s comparative frame, borrowed from [[KarlJaspers|Karl Jaspers / 卡尔·亚斯贝尔斯]], for the clustering of major philosophical and religious figures across Eurasia between roughly 800 and 200 BCE.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses the axial-age frame as a staging device rather than as a full comparative-history argument. By placing [[Confucius|孔子]] and [[Laozi|老子]] beside [[ShakyamuniBuddha|释迦牟尼佛]], [[Socrates|苏格拉底]], [[Plato|柏拉图]], [[Aristotle|亚里士多德]], [[Mozi|墨子]], and the [[HundredSchoolsSynthesis|诸子百家]] field, it makes the first Kongzi-Laozi meeting feel like one local scene within a wider human search for self, suffering, order, and ultimate meaning.

## Key Claims
- The source treats 800-200 BCE as a period when several regions generated durable philosophical and religious breakthroughs.
- The concept is used to enlarge the meaning of Confucius visiting Laozi from private study into civilizational self-reflection.
- The episode links Buddhist no-self, Daoist naturalness, and Confucian public concern as neighboring attempts to answer human suffering and order.
- The frame is comparative and interpretive, not a claim that all named traditions teach the same doctrine.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not independently defend Jaspers's theory or compare all axial traditions in detail. Its use is rhetorical and interpretive: it helps explain why the Kongzi-Laozi meeting matters, while leaving chronology, transmission, and doctrinal comparison source-scoped.
