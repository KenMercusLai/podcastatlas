# Interdependent Future Governance

## Definition
Interdependent future governance is the practice of designing policy around coupled technological, ecological, economic, security, and social systems rather than treating each future challenge as an isolated portfolio.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded source argues that AI changes information power, employment, education, government, resource demand, military capability, and wealth distribution at the same time. Longevity changes work, retirement, care, relationships, and intergenerational authority; energy choices affect climate, water, data centers, industry, and geopolitics. The resulting governance test is not to find one master policy, but to make dependencies, time horizons, distributional effects, and institutional responsibility explicit before optimizing any single outcome.

## Key Claims
- Near-term decisions can create institutional paths that remain difficult to reverse for decades.
- Technology policy is also resource, labor, security, fiscal, and legitimacy policy.
- Efficiency in one domain can externalize costs into workers, communities, infrastructure, or future generations.
- Longer lives require joint redesign of retirement, care, employment, relationships, and public finance.
- Automation does not remove politics because people still choose objectives, thresholds, accountability, and distribution.
- Resilience is a system property built through adaptability, diversity, feedback, and recovery capacity rather than maximum short-run efficiency.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source maps dependencies more fully than it ranks them or supplies causal estimates. Systems framing can clarify spillovers while also becoming too broad to assign authority, budgets, sequencing, or measurable outcomes. The episode's proposed links are often conversational and do not show that one institution should govern every domain or that resilience should always dominate efficiency.

## What Changed
- Established a cross-domain governance frame joining AI, resources, institutions, work, security, and intergenerational stewardship.
