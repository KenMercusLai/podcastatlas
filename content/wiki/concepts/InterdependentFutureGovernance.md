# Interdependent Future Governance

## Definition
Interdependent future governance designs policy around coupled technological, ecological, economic, security, and social systems, then evaluates coordination at multiple levels rather than optimizing one portfolio or participant in isolation.

## Current Synthesis
The first source maps practical coupling: AI changes information power, education, employment, government, resource demand, warfare, and wealth distribution; longevity changes work, retirement, care, and intergenerational authority. The Long Now conversation adds a scale test. Cooperation within one group can benefit or damage a larger network, while acceleration increases rather than cancels obligations to cushion change. Together, the sources reject both a master policy and a single objective. Governance must make dependencies, time horizons, distributional effects, system boundaries, affected participants, and institutional responsibility explicit, then define health and unacceptable excess through accountable collective choice.

## Key Claims
- Near-term decisions can create institutional and technological paths that remain difficult to reverse for decades.
- Technology policy is simultaneously resource, labor, security, fiscal, relational, and legitimacy policy.
- Efficiency or cooperation at one level can externalize costs into workers, communities, ecosystems, hosts, or future generations.
- Automation does not remove politics because people still choose objectives, thresholds, representation, accountability, and distribution.
- Resilience depends on diversity, feedback, recovery capacity, and differentiated participation rather than maximum short-run efficiency.
- Faster change increases the need for care, transition support, and collective deliberation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Systems framing can reveal spillovers while becoming too broad to assign authority, budgets, sequencing, or measurable outcomes. The sources map dependencies and normative tests more fully than they rank tradeoffs or supply causal estimates. Choosing a larger system boundary can also suppress minority rights in the name of the whole; preserving differentiated selves, representation, and appeal therefore remains necessary. Neither source shows that one institution should govern every domain or that resilience should always dominate efficiency.

## What Changed
- Added a multilevel test for whether cooperation within one group supports or damages its wider host system.
- Added differentiated participant agency and collectively chosen well-being criteria to the governance frame.
- Made care and transition support explicit obligations under acceleration.
