# Comedy Public Reception Boundary / 喜剧公共接受边界

## Definition
Comedy public reception boundary / 喜剧公共接受边界 separates a comedian's intent, the live function of a joke, heterogeneous audience effects, later public amplification, and the creator's ethical responsibility without treating any one layer as the complete meaning of the work or person.

## Current Synthesis
[[yangli-luoyonghao-buxiang-chengwei-baxin-ye-wuyi-chengwei-dengta-lprytonoy3jq2z8dnxgygcqk_zuo]] resists two reductions. Public controversy is not always a mere misunderstanding, because different groups can genuinely experience the same routine differently. But supporters and opponents can also project missions, hostility, or identity onto [[YangLiStandup|杨笠]] that exceed what a joke or stage persona establishes.

Stand-up intensifies the problem because audience response belongs inside the medium. Laughter, silence, pacing, and room composition show what a set does, while viral circulation, headlines, and organized hostility move fragments into a different context. The practical boundary is therefore neither complete audience sovereignty nor complete creator immunity: protect the comic standard, learn from effects, revise avoidable harm, and retain limits around harassment, compelled explanation, and total-person judgment.

## Key Claims
- Comic intent matters but does not erase the effects experienced by different audiences.
- Audience response supplies medium-internal evidence without becoming a complete moral or biographical verdict.
- Supporters can project a representative or symbolic mission onto a performer just as opponents can project hostility.
- Live context and later platform amplification are different reception environments and should not be collapsed.
- Ethical revision can follow controversy without conceding that every hostile interpretation is accurate.
- Interview refusal, attention to one's actual audience, and privacy protection can be boundaries rather than proof that a creator has no answer.

## Evidence
- Intent and heterogeneous effect: [[yangli-luoyonghao-buxiang-chengwei-baxin-ye-wuyi-chengwei-dengta-lprytonoy3jq2z8dnxgygcqk_zuo]] has Yang say controversy is not completely misunderstanding because public jokes can produce different feelings across groups.
- Projection: [[yangli-luoyonghao-buxiang-chengwei-baxin-ye-wuyi-chengwei-dengta-lprytonoy3jq2z8dnxgygcqk_zuo]] records Luo's claim that both supporters and opponents may place their own ideas onto Yang, alongside her refusal to control either side completely.
- Stage and amplification: [[yangli-luoyonghao-buxiang-chengwei-baxin-ye-wuyi-chengwei-dengta-lprytonoy3jq2z8dnxgygcqk_zuo]] contrasts live room response with media headlines, public controversy, and Yang's decision not to release the full special online.
- Ethical adaptation: [[yangli-luoyonghao-buxiang-chengwei-baxin-ye-wuyi-chengwei-dengta-lprytonoy3jq2z8dnxgygcqk_zuo]] has Yang reconsider one past joke and describe greater care around harm, younger viewers, and public consequences.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept comes from one performer's account and one interviewer’s framing, not comparative audience research. It does not determine whether any specific controversial joke was justified, harmful, misunderstood, or well crafted. “Comic standard” cannot provide blanket immunity from criticism, while public impact cannot by itself establish intent or a complete private character.

## What Changed
- Created a layered reception model that preserves both genuine audience difference and the limits of public projection.
