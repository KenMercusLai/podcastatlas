# Companion Robots

Companion robots are robots designed primarily for emotional coexistence, social presence, and household relationship-building rather than task execution. [[wo-yudao-le-di-yige-zhenzheng-xiang-mai-de-peiban-jiqiren-duihua-shibo-yueban-dongli-chuangshiren-gonglu-boke-lrydelizm0-hbk68u5cqe3ti-epb]] uses [[Xiaoban]] as a concrete case: [[Shibo]] argues that the product should feel like an independent family member, not a domestic worker, pet replacement, AI speaker, or toy.

[[jushen-zhineng-de-taotian-da-paomo-zhong-ta-yijing-ba-jiqiren-songjin-300-ge-jiating-duihua-zhang-yi-weilaibuyuan-chuangshiren-ceo-lic8b7dkxts3qjrs6af1rgbf4xrf]] clarifies the boundary by adding [[F2HomeRobot]] as a more service-oriented household robot. F2 still needs child interaction and household presence, but [[ZhangYi]] frames its value through [[HomeServiceRobots]], child care, light chores, rental retention, and real-home data rather than emotional companionship alone.

[[ep253-baohuo-de-ai-haowu-daodi-shi-zhenxiang-haishi-zhishangshui-lgt0cdkotgnzjl0mu2tx41p9fw-4]] adds a toy-level branch through [[Ropet]] and [[FuzaiAIToy|福仔]]. The episode shows that companionship can be attempted without a full robot body: memory, nonverbal expression, speech restraint, child safety, and anthropomorphic attachment all matter even in small plush or desktop objects.

[[yushu-shangshi-baozhang-dan-renxing-jiqiren-de-qian-daodi-cong-nali-zhuan-s10e26-4a50d4a3-a6ff-4c89-b754-367b73ce924b]] adds a Disney toy-and-character branch. The source says small Disney robot toys can create companionship through restrained movement, lights, sound, and IP recognition rather than full conversation, making [[DisneyRobotExperienceCommercialization]] an adjacent commercial path for emotional robots.

## Design Claims
- Companionship can depend more on timing, posture, sound, gaze, and restraint than on a long feature list.
- Toy-like companions can share the same attachment problems as larger robots when children treat them as friends or family members.
- Non-human language can reduce user expectations of factual Q&A while preserving the feeling that the robot has understandable intent.
- Soft materials, bipedal movement, expressive eyes, and charging rituals can support [[RobotLiveliness]].
- [[EmotionalInteractionModels]] matter because companionship is not the same as pleasing; the robot may need to react, refuse, withdraw, or remember poor treatment.
- Long-term adoption depends on retention, reliability, safety, privacy, and whether users keep treating the robot as part of a lifestyle.
- Service-oriented home robots overlap with companionship when they interact with children, but they are judged more directly by task utility, service value, and household data loops.
- IP-linked companion robots may have a clearer willingness-to-pay path when the buyer is paying for character presence and fandom rather than general-purpose assistance.
