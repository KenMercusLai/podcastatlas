# Haircare Skincare-ization / 护发护肤化

Haircare skincare-ization / 护发护肤化 is the episode's frame for hair care borrowing the language, routines, and value logic of facial skincare. In [[meizhuang-jutou-jiti-dingshang-toufa-xihu-shengyi-weihe-you-re-qilai-1005860109]], the shift includes ingredient claims such as niacinamide, probiotics, and amino acids; scalp microbiome language; products for UV damage and hard-water deposits; and multi-step routines with serums, sprays, and oils.

The concept matters because it changes the category's economics. A shampoo purchase can be cheap, infrequent, and substitution-prone, but a skincare-like hair routine can add steps, make the scalp a treatment surface, justify higher prices, and create visible before-and-after expectations. The source keeps this as a market and behavior claim, not proof that every ingredient or routine works as marketed.

## Key Claims
- Hair care becomes more valuable when consumers treat scalp and hair condition as ongoing management rather than occasional cleaning.
- Ingredient labels help consumers feel rational and evidence-oriented, but the episode warns that buyers may still be paying mainly for fast visible improvement.
- Multi-step routines make hair care resemble skincare by adding pre-wash, pre-styling, repair, oil, scent, and finishing products.
- Skincare-ization can strengthen [[ConsumerBrandMoat]] when trust transfers from dermatology, salon authority, or proven product feel.
- The trend can also intensify [[BeautyProblemNaming]] when ordinary hair states become newly named defects.
