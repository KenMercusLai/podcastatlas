# Fixed-Camp Siege Engineering / 营垒攻防工程

## Definition
Fixed-camp siege engineering / 营垒攻防工程 is a battlefield pattern in which opposing field camps harden into a material contest of towers, earthworks, missile platforms, counter-artillery, tunnels, and trenches rather than a quick open-field decision.

## Current Synthesis
Hanji 1047 defines the pattern through the Guandu camp confrontation after [[YuanShao|袁绍]] advances from [[YangwuGuanduLateHan|阳武]] to [[ShaqiuGuanduLateHan|沙丘]] and [[CaoCao|曹操]] builds opposing camps. Cao initially fights and fails, then withdraws into defense. Yuan tries to dominate the camp line with high towers and earth hills for missile pressure, while Cao's troops carry shields and Cao answers with thunderbolt carts that destroy the towers.

The engineering contest then moves below ground. Yuan's side digs tunnels; Cao's side counters with long deep trenches. The pattern matters because it shows Guandu becoming a pressure system under supply limits before the later Wuchao decision, not merely a sequence of named battles.

## Key Claims
- Field camps can become siege-like systems when neither side can immediately break the other in open battle.
- Engineering measures translate manpower and material into pressure, protection, and denial.
- Counter-engineering is recursive: towers call forth catapults, tunnels call forth trenches.
- Fixed-camp pressure can intensify logistics stress because time, ammunition, labor, and food become campaign resources.
- The pattern bridges attrition strategy and decisive strike by creating the conditions under which a supply depot becomes the critical target.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Hanji 1047 stops before the Wuchao resolution, so this concept explains the pressure environment rather than the final campaign decision. The exact mechanics and terminology of "thunderbolt carts" are source-scoped here and should not be expanded into a full history of artillery without additional sources.
