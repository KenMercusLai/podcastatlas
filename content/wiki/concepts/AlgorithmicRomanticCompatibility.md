## Definition
Algorithmic romantic compatibility is the use or imagined use of a quantified matching system to compare an existing partner with a supposedly better romantic match.

## Current Synthesis
The current source treats compatibility scoring as a relationship-disrupting knowledge problem. The hypothetical system is stipulated to be perfectly accurate, so the panel does not debate prediction quality. Instead, it asks what happens once a person is told that an existing partner is only a 70% match while a 100% match is available nearby.

The strongest synthesis is that even perfect compatibility information cannot decide the relationship by itself. Marriage contract, shared years, family and economic entanglement, remaining lifespan, uncertainty about what perfection feels like, and refusal to let a number redefine one's partner all remain live constraints. The score creates opportunity-cost pressure, but it does not erase obligation or narrative attachment.

## Key Claims
- Compatibility scores can turn a current partner into a comparison object even when the relationship is still wanted.
- Existing commitment changes the meaning of a better match because leaving is not the same as selecting before attachment.
- People may rationally refuse compatibility knowledge if the score would damage a livable relationship narrative.
- Perfect match claims do not prove perfect life outcomes; romance satisfaction may not solve career, economic, creative, or existential pain.
- Remaining lifespan and current match quality change the opportunity cost of switching.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode uses a stipulated perfect technology, so the concept should not be read as evidence that real matching algorithms can calculate compatibility percentages accurately. Existing research-oriented wiki material on attraction and similarity already cautions that measured traits do not straightforwardly predict whether two people will click.
