## Definition
Algorithmic romantic compatibility is the use or imagined use of a quantified matching system to compare an existing partner with a supposedly better romantic match.

## Current Synthesis
The first source treats compatibility scoring as a relationship-disrupting knowledge problem. Its hypothetical system is stipulated to be perfectly accurate, so the panel does not debate prediction quality. Instead, it asks what happens once a person is told that an existing partner is only a 70% match while a 100% match is available nearby.

The second source challenges the real-world premise that a profile can generate such knowledge. It reports that extensive questionnaires did not predict speed-date connection and argues that durable fit is partly a distinctive dynamic built across situations. Together, the sources separate two questions: whether compatibility can be predicted and what even valid compatibility information should be allowed to decide. A score creates opportunity-cost pressure, but it neither proves a future life outcome nor erases obligation, narrative attachment, consent, or the path-dependent relationship already built.

## Key Claims
- Compatibility scores can turn a current partner into a comparison object even when the relationship is still wanted.
- Existing commitment changes the meaning of a better match because leaving is not the same as selecting before attachment.
- People may rationally refuse compatibility knowledge if the score would damage a livable relationship narrative.
- Perfect match claims do not prove perfect life outcomes; romance satisfaction may not solve career, economic, creative, or existential pain.
- Remaining lifespan and current match quality change the opportunity cost of switching.
- Real matching systems should not inherit the thought experiment's stipulation of perfect predictive accuracy.
- Compatibility may emerge through varied interaction rather than exist as a stable profile score waiting to be discovered.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The thought experiment's stipulated perfect technology should not be read as evidence that real matching algorithms can calculate compatibility percentages accurately. The live conversation's research claims are also presented without study identification, sample, measures, or effect sizes, so they qualify commercial matching promises without proving that profiles contain no predictive information at all. Interaction-developed compatibility does not guarantee safety, reciprocity, or long-term stability.

## What Changed
- Separated the ethics of acting on a score from the prior empirical problem of whether compatibility can be predicted.
- Added compatibility as an interaction-developed dynamic rather than a fixed profile property.
