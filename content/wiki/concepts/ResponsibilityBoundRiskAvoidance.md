## Definition

责任绑定风险回避 / responsibility-bound risk avoidance is the principle that people whose safety carries consequences for many others should avoid risks that might be acceptable for private individuals with fewer dependent obligations.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-475-rexue-yushi-xueguangde-gongran-weixie-han-yuandi-lj9b1raz_rjhijw17vxgsp6racuj|Hanji 475]] creates the concept through [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]'s proposed river crossing by boat. [[XueGuangdeWesternHan|薛广德]] and [[ZhangMengWesternHan|张猛 / 张萌]] both oppose the trip, not because travel is inherently wrong, but because the emperor's physical safety is tied to court, ritual, and state continuity.

The host extends the classical maxim about "千金之子" and the wise ruler not taking dangerous chances into modern management. The core lesson is not fearfulness. It is role-sensitive risk calibration: a leader, family-business head, or important operator may need stricter travel and safety rules because their accident would impose costs on institutions, dependents, or successors beyond themselves.

The earlier Wen-era evidence makes dependent obligation, not personal fear, the shared core across the slope and river cases. [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] reportedly restrains [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] from riding quickly down a steep slope near Baling and argues that even if the emperor discounts his own safety, he still owes protection to the ancestral and dowager order. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-1-ln6bbls0skoqrhutd3rm-kznfpjm|Hanji 212 part 1]]

## Key Claims

- The same physical risk can carry different meaning depending on the actor's public responsibility and replaceability.
- Avoiding unnecessary danger can be a duty rather than cowardice when many people depend on the actor's continuity.
- Practical risk reasons are stronger when separated from moral theatrics: Zhang Meng's wind, water, and wave argument makes the safer bridge route actionable.
- Modern leadership analogies in the source treat travel separation and risk diversification as responsibility practices, not status privilege alone.
- A concise warning can work when it names the people and institutions that bear the downside of a leader's private gamble.
- The concept should remain tied to avoidable, asymmetric risks rather than becoming a general excuse for inaction.

## Evidence

- Imperial safety warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-475-rexue-yushi-xueguangde-gongran-weixie-han-yuandi-lj9b1raz_rjhijw17vxgsp6racuj|Hanji 475]] says Xue Guangde blocks Yuan's carriage to oppose crossing the river by boat.
- Practical risk framing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-475-rexue-yushi-xueguangde-gongran-weixie-han-yuandi-lj9b1raz_rjhijw17vxgsp6racuj|Hanji 475]] says Zhang Meng cites autumn wind, broad river conditions, and dangerous waves as reasons to take the bridge.
- Classical principle: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-475-rexue-yushi-xueguangde-gongran-weixie-han-yuandi-lj9b1raz_rjhijw17vxgsp6racuj|Hanji 475]] uses "千金之子" and "圣主不乘危而徼幸" language to link status, responsibility, and risk.
- Modern extension: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-475-rexue-yushi-xueguangde-gongran-weixie-han-yuandi-lj9b1raz_rjhijw17vxgsp6racuj|Hanji 475]] maps the lesson to family businesses and leadership travel rules.
- Wen-era slope warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-1-ln6bbls0skoqrhutd3rm-kznfpjm|Hanji 212 part 1]] says Yuan Ang physically restrains Liu Heng's horse and frames the emperor's safety as an obligation to dynasty and dowager as well as self.
- Modern corporate analogy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-212-jiayi-lun-jizhu-shu-yu-zhongnong-zhuzhang-1-ln6bbls0skoqrhutd3rm-kznfpjm|Hanji 212 part 1]] extends the warning to a listed-company leader whose dangerous climbing may expose shareholders and the firm.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Neither source independently proves the objective danger level of the proposed river crossing or slope descent. Both modern leadership examples are analogies rather than historical evidence, and Hanji 212's listed-company mountaineer is unnamed and anecdotal. The concept should be applied where role, continuity, and dependent costs make an otherwise ordinary risk disproportionate.

## What Changed

- Added the earlier Yuan Ang–Liu Heng slope incident, making obligation to affected institutions the common mechanism across two dynastic cases.
- Kept both modern business extensions source-scoped and separated them from the historical claims.
