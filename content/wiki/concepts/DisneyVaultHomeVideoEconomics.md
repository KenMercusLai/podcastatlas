# Disney Vault Home Video Economics

Disney Vault home video economics is the source's account of turning scarce catalog access into repeat revenue in [[disney-the-renaissance-and-the-empire-1]]. Under [[MichaelEisner]], Disney releases classic films such as Pinocchio and Cinderella on VHS in limited windows rather than making the whole catalog permanently available.

The concept extends [[StrategicRerelease]]. The old theatrical rerelease logic becomes a home-video profit machine: scarcity creates urgency, families buy physical copies, and the same underlying IP feeds merchandise, parks, music, and later stage adaptations.

## Key Claims
- Scarcity can protect premium IP when access is limited, timed, and tied to family demand.
- Home video let Disney monetize catalog affection at much higher scale than theatrical rereleases alone.
- The strategy depended on owned IP and trusted family brands, not just distribution technology.
- Streaming weakens the same scarcity rhythm because subscribers expect broad, always-on access.
