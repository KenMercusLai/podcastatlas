# Autonomous Naval Vessels

Autonomous naval vessels are uncrewed maritime platforms designed to perform naval missions with onboard autonomy, sensors, compute, communications, and remote or mission-intent control. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-saronic-founders-autonomous-warships-chinas-230x-advantage-swarms-of-robot-ships-42334230]] grounds the concept through [[Saronic]]'s [[CorsairSaronic|Corsair]] and [[MarauderSaronic|Marauder]] products.

The source's key point is that autonomy changes the whole vessel, not only the navigation layer. [[VibhAltakar]] argues that removing humans removes support infrastructure and lets the builder optimize naval architecture, range, payload, and control authority differently; [[DinoMavroukis]] adds that autonomous platforms can shift some dangerous missions away from service members while remaining part of a hybrid crewed-uncrewed Navy.

## Key Claims
- Autonomy can reduce vessel complexity by removing human-support systems such as stairs, bathrooms, and habitability infrastructure.
- The military value depends on mission fit; the source does not claim every naval mission should become unmanned.
- The concept links risk reduction to mass: vessels can enter dangerous areas or persist at scale without exposing additional crews.
- The source treats autonomy as a system of vessels, sensors, communications, software, and commander intent rather than isolated self-driving behavior.
