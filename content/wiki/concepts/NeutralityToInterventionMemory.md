# Neutrality-to-Intervention Memory

## Definition

Neutrality-to-intervention memory is the process by which an event that does not immediately bring a neutral country into war later becomes part of the moral and political case for intervention.

## Current Synthesis

[[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] grounds the concept in the U.S. response to [[RMSLusitania|Lusitania]]. The sinking killed 128 Americans and generated intense pressure, but [[WoodrowWilson|Woodrow Wilson]] still kept the [[UnitedStates|United States]] out of the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]] in 1915. The event mattered because it changed the atmosphere: German submarine warfare now had a remembered civilian-casualty example attached to it.

When Germany resumed unrestricted submarine warfare in 1917, Lusitania could be recalled as proof of a pattern. The concept therefore separates immediate causation from stored political meaning.

## Key Claims

- A neutral country can absorb a shock without entering war immediately.
- The shock can still alter public vocabulary, elite expectations, and later threat interpretation.
- Civilian deaths involving neutral citizens are especially likely to become reusable intervention memories.
- Diplomatic restraint may coexist with escalating warning language and internal government division.
- Later enemy repetition or escalation can activate earlier memory as evidence of pattern, not accident.

## Evidence

- Immediate restraint: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] says Wilson emphasized peace after Lusitania and the United States did not enter the war in 1915.
- Political pressure: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] describes Theodore Roosevelt's stronger response and Wilson being mocked for restraint.
- Diplomatic hardening: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] links Wilson's protest notes and William Jennings Bryan's resignation to a more dangerous U.S.-German diplomatic setting.
- Later activation: [[673-the-first-world-war-the-submarine-strikes-part-3-glt3632974949]] says Lusitania became part of the American recruitment and propaganda case when unrestricted submarine warfare returned in 1917.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not claim that Lusitania alone caused American entry into the war. The source explicitly says it did not. The claim is narrower: memory of the sinking became politically available when later German submarine policy made intervention easier to argue.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish immediate nonintervention from later intervention memory.
