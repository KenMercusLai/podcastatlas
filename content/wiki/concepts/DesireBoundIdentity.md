# Desire-Bound Identity / 欲望绑定的身份

## Definition

Desire-bound identity is a condition in which status, possession, recognition, or fear of decline makes a valued name or role inseparable from a person's sense of self, so that preserving the identity reduces rather than expands freedom.

## Current Synthesis

[[TianyaMingyueDaoGuLong|《天涯·明月·刀》]], as discussed in [[118-gulong-tianya-mingyue-dao-yiba-dao-yilun-yue-yigerende-jianghu-ltcxpyzkcejdizjsnqigs8ytqjxj]], distributes this condition across three characters. 公子羽 tries to detach his prestigious name from aging flesh by transferring it to younger proxies. 燕南飞 accepts the proxy role and its privileges, but the arrangement makes him replaceable and implicates him in its violence. 傅红雪's movement in the opposite direction is not a confirmed ranking victory; it is a refusal of the offered identity and a turn toward ordinary attachment.

The concept therefore distinguishes possessing power from being free. A role can deliver wealth, intimacy, and public authority while requiring secrecy, fear of replacement, and abandonment of an independent name. Release is likewise not simple passivity: it can involve refusing the contest by which the role reproduces itself.

A second literary case centers on [[MurongFu|慕容复]], for whom desire-bound identity begins as inherited mission rather than an openly chosen office. Restoring Great Yan is embedded in his name and education, but the willingness to abandon his surname and seek the Dali throne shows that the role of emperor eventually outruns the stated dynasty. Because he has no ordinary self or valued life outside that role, strategic impossibility does not produce revision; it produces escalating sacrifice of relationships, ethics, organization, and finally reality. Murong Bo and [[DuanYanqing|段延庆]] supply contrasting releases when renunciation or paternity makes another identity possible.

## Key Claims

- A prestigious identity can become a constraint when maintaining it requires continuous victory or denial of aging.
- Transferable public identity can preserve a brand while making each human bearer disposable.
- Benefits obtained through a coercive role do not make its participant only a victim; privilege can coexist with dependence and complicity.
- Rankings can manufacture desire and conflict when a credible authority makes recognition scarce and visible.
- Refusing succession or supremacy can be a form of agency rather than evidence of defeat.
- Ordinary attachment can provide an alternative identity not organized by elite competition.
- An inherited mission can become personally desired and identity-defining even when its original political object changes.

## Evidence

### Identity as transferable power

- [[118-gulong-tianya-mingyue-dao-yiba-dao-yilun-yue-yigerende-jianghu-ltcxpyzkcejdizjsnqigs8ytqjxj]] says 公子羽 seeks younger representatives so his name can remain undefeated despite his own aging.

### Privilege and complicity

- [[118-gulong-tianya-mingyue-dao-yiba-dao-yilun-yue-yigerende-jianghu-ltcxpyzkcejdizjsnqigs8ytqjxj]] treats 燕南飞 as both controlled proxy and knowing participant who enjoys the arrangement's status, wealth, and relationships.

### Refusal and release

- [[118-gulong-tianya-mingyue-dao-yiba-dao-yilun-yue-yigerende-jianghu-ltcxpyzkcejdizjsnqigs8ytqjxj]] interprets 傅红雪's refusal to become the next 公子羽 and his return to 周婷 as thematic release from the prestige system.
- [[077-tianlongbabu-zhi-murongfu-pian-renjian-shige-de-fuguozhe-lhlkjiq4j4fqcldeggxxovwuolob]] contrasts Murong Fu's inability to release sovereign identity with Murong Bo's renunciation and Duan Yanqing's recovery of paternal identity.

### Inherited mission and object substitution

- [[077-tianlongbabu-zhi-murongfu-pian-renjian-shige-de-fuguozhe-lhlkjiq4j4fqcldeggxxovwuolob]] follows a restoration duty from naming and upbringing into personal imperial desire, then uses the Dali succession plan to show the desired role surviving a change in dynasty and surname.
- [[077-tianlongbabu-zhi-murongfu-pian-renjian-shige-de-fuguozhe-lhlkjiq4j4fqcldeggxxovwuolob]] links the absence of a self outside restoration to instrumental relationships, killing, organizational collapse, and delusional kingship.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is derived from two literary discussions and should not be generalized into an empirical account of identity or motivation. 公子羽's withdrawal may reflect insight, exhaustion, love, or narrative resolution; 燕南飞's knowledge and voluntariness are inferred from participation; and 傅红雪's release does not settle martial superiority. Murong Fu's motives likewise remain contested between imposed family mission and personal power desire. The concept explains narrowing freedom but does not turn ambition, inherited obligation, or mental collapse into moral acquittal.

## What Changed

- Extended the concept from a transferable prestige role to an inherited restoration mission that survives substitution of dynasty, surname, and political route.
- Added the availability of a non-sovereign identity as a possible condition of release.
