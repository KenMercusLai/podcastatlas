# Italian Irredentism

## Definition

Italian irredentism is the nationalist claim that Italian national unification remained incomplete while Italian-speaking or historically desired territories lay outside [[Italy]]'s borders, especially under [[AustriaHungary|Austria-Hungary]].

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses Italia irredenta, or "unredeemed Italy," to explain why an alliance with Austria-Hungary could be strategically convenient but emotionally unstable. Irredentist claims made Trentino, South Tyrol, Trieste, Gorizia, Istria, Dalmatia, and Adriatic influence feel like tests of national completion. In the May 1915 crisis, those claims turned secret Allied promises and elite bargaining into a language of glory, betrayal, and destiny.

## Key Claims

- Irredentism converted border and language questions into a moralized national-completion project.
- The concept made Austria-Hungary an awkward ally because it ruled territories Italian nationalists wanted.
- Irredentist aims helped Italy frame an offensive war as redemption rather than simple acquisition.
- The episode treats the ideology as elite-amplified rather than universally popular among ordinary Italians.
- Postwar disappointment made irredentist expectation part of the resentment later available to fascist politics.

## Evidence

- National-completion claim: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] explains Italia irredenta as "unredeemed Italy" and the source of the term irredentism.
- Austro-Hungarian target: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] links the claim to northern and eastern frontiers and Italian speakers under Austria-Hungary.
- War bargaining: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] lists South Tyrol, Trentino, Trieste, Gorizia, Istria, Dalmatia, parts of Albania, the Dodecanese, and money among Italian demands.
- Public qualification: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] contrasts nationalist intellectual enthusiasm with broad anti-war sentiment.
- Postwar effect: [[672-the-first-world-war-italys-doomed-campaign-part-2-glt3383347829]] connects unmet or partial territorial expectations to the "cheated winner" mood after the war.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not claim every Italian supported irredentism or war. It repeatedly contrasts interventionist rhetoric with reports of broad opposition, especially in poorer southern regions. The territorial list also mixes language, strategic, imperial, and bargaining claims rather than one pure ethnic principle.

## What Changed

- Created Italian irredentism as the territorial-nationalist frame behind Italy's 1915 intervention.
