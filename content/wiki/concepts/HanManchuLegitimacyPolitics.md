# 满汉合法性政治 / Han-Manchu Legitimacy Politics

满汉合法性政治 / Han-Manchu legitimacy politics is the source's frame for how the [[ZengJing|曾静]] case tied ethnic, dynastic, and moral legitimacy together. In [[11-yongzheng-wangchao-zhi-dayi-juemi-shijingqian-bi-xia-de-qiangu-qi-an-547265513]], [[ZhangXi|张熙]]'s letter tried to persuade [[YueZhongqi|岳钟琪]] through Han loyalist symbolism, while [[LuLiuliang|吕留良]]'s writings supplied Hua-Yi arguments against Qing rule.

The episode presents [[YongzhengEmperor|雍正]] as recognizing that the danger was not a strong military conspiracy but a legitimacy language. If Qing rule could be framed as alien, immoral, or illegitimate, a confused rural scholar could become politically meaningful despite having little organization. That is why the case moved toward [[QingLiteraryInquisition]] and [[DaiYiJueMiLu|《大义觉迷录》]].

## Key Claims
- Ethnic and dynastic legitimacy can turn weak conspiracies into serious political events.
- Han loyalist symbols made Yue Zhongqi's position especially dangerous because identity, office, and rebellion were placed in one frame.
- Hua-Yi arguments mattered less as abstract philosophy than as language that could make Qing rule morally challengeable.
- Yongzheng's rebuttal tried to convert legitimacy from ethnic origin back into imperial virtue and practical rule.
