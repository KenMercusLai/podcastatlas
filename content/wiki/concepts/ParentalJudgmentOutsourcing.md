# Parental Judgment Outsourcing

Parental judgment outsourcing is the pattern where parents let an app, monitor, score, or chatbot make routine care decisions that parents might otherwise learn through observation and experience. [[tech-20260811-0811-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260811-0811-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[SapnaMaheshwari]]'s discussion of sleep recommendations from [[Nanit]] and parents using [[ChatGPT]] or [[Claude]] to create baby sleep plans.

The source does not claim parents should reject all tools. Its concern is about placement in the decision loop: a monitor can alert parents to risks or provide useful context, but it can also become an extra decision-maker inside the family when parents stop trusting visible tiredness, their own routines, or local judgment.

## Key Claims
- Parenting tools can be useful when they surface information parents would otherwise miss.
- Outsourcing becomes risky when the tool's recommendation replaces the parent's first observation of the child.
- Sleep advice is a strong case because exhaustion can make parents want a rule even when the child-specific situation is messy.
- The risk resembles [[CognitiveSurrender]] because the outside answer can settle the parent's view before independent judgment forms.
