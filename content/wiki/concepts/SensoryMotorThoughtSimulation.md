# Sensory-Motor Thought Simulation

## Definition
Sensory-motor thought simulation is the hypothesis that thinking partly reuses perceptual and action systems to model absent objects, events, words, and possible movements.

## Current Synthesis
In [[scim2057835571-scim2057835571]], [[JenniferGroh]] proposes that thinking about a cat can recruit linked visual, auditory, olfactory, verbal, and motor associations rather than a single abstract symbol. Thought trains then follow learned and contextual pathways: a supposedly unrelated word often remains connected to the preceding discussion, and sustained engagement can deepen one path while competing cues activate others.

The hypothesis also supplies an interference prediction. Conversation and difficult visual-motor driving can compete because internal speech and external action may draw on overlapping systems or limited control resources. This supports deliberate context design, but it does not establish that every thought is imagery, that all distraction has one cause, or that environmental tactics substitute for clinical assessment.

## Key Claims
- Thinking can reactivate distributed sensory and motor features associated with an object or event.
- Associations constrain thought sequences, making apparently random ideas more context-dependent than they seem.
- Internal simulation and external action can compete when they require overlapping representations or control resources.
- Stable attention can be understood as a context-supported attractor without reducing all focus difficulty to one mechanism.
- Changing sensory context can interrupt an unproductive thought path and make a different path easier to enter.

## Evidence
- Distributed association example: [[scim2057835571-scim2057835571]] uses thinking about a cat to connect visual form, sound, smell, and action-related features.
- Context dependence: [[scim2057835571-scim2057835571]] describes classroom attempts to generate unrelated words that often remain linked to the ongoing discussion.
- Resource competition: [[scim2057835571-scim2057835571]] uses impaired difficult driving during conversation as an example of possible overlap.
- Attractor metaphor: [[scim2057835571-scim2057835571]] compares sustained engagement with a ball settling into a deeper trench.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents this as a theory and explanatory metaphor, not a complete or exclusive model of thought. It does not distinguish imagery from semantic, linguistic, emotional, or executive processes with experimental measures, and it gives no causal study details for the driving example. Fragmented thought or distractibility cannot be used here to infer or dismiss ADHD or another clinical condition.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded account of thought as possible reuse of perceptual and motor systems.
