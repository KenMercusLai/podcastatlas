# Dental Prosthesis Maintenance / 义齿与种植牙维护

## Definition
Dental prosthesis maintenance is the ongoing, design-specific cleaning, use, inspection and professional review required to keep removable dentures, fixed bridges, crowns and implant-supported teeth functional without treating restoration as a permanent end to oral care.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.75 rejects the idea that a replaced tooth is self-cleaning or maintenance-free. Removable dentures can be taken out for cleaning and stored according to their material and clinician instructions; fixed work and implants require cleaning around margins and between teeth with tools suited to the space. The common rule is continued plaque and food-debris control without damaging the prosthesis or surrounding tissue.

Maintenance also includes noticing change. Food trapping, bleeding, looseness, discomfort, altered bite or difficulty chewing can indicate a problem with the restoration, supporting tooth, implant or surrounding tissue. These signs belong in review rather than indefinite adaptation, and hard-object biting can damage both natural and restored teeth.

## Key Claims
- Dental restoration changes the cleaning task but does not end it.
- Removable, fixed and implant-supported work require different access, tools and material-aware instructions.
- Food trapping, bleeding, looseness, pain, bite change or reduced chewing function should prompt assessment.
- Implants require daily hygiene and periodic professional review like the rest of the mouth.
- Very hot water or improvised disinfection can damage some removable-denture materials.
- Restoration longevity depends partly on fit, tissue health, use, hygiene and follow-up rather than the device alone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a universal cleaning protocol for every denture resin, metal framework, crown, bridge or implant system. Removal at night, soaking solution, brush type, flossing aid, irrigator settings, review interval, repair and replacement depend on the device, oral tissues, dexterity and clinician instructions. Symptoms can have multiple causes and cannot be diagnosed from this maintenance framework alone.

## What Changed
- Created a maintenance model spanning removable, fixed and implant-supported dental restorations.
- Made symptoms and functional change part of maintenance rather than treating cleaning as the whole task.
