# Official Sports Ticket Resale

Official sports ticket resale is a sanctioned secondary market where the event organizer lets ticket holders resell tickets inside an approved platform while charging service fees or setting pricing rules. [[vol-267-ruhe-xianchang-kan-shijiebei-bing-da-kui-yi-bi-feat-yingyan-shijian-1002986145]] makes the concept concrete through the 2026 [[FIFAWorldCup|FIFA World Cup]] resale system.

The source contrasts stricter official resale models with the 2026 World Cup approach: sellers can price freely, and both buyer and seller pay a 15% fee. That lets [[FIFA]] internalize part of secondary-market upside, but it also makes fans bear price volatility, team-result risk, and platform-fee drag. [[TeteFei|特特飞]]'s $900-plus loss on a Mexico match ticket is the source's practical example.

## Key Claims
- Official resale does not remove speculation; it can legitimize and monetize it.
- Fee design matters because charging both sides lets the platform benefit even when an ordinary seller loses money.
- Match prices can move with team advancement scenarios, opponent appeal, schedule timing, and fans deciding whether to save money for knockout rounds.
- Price caps, no-premium resale, and free pricing produce very different access and revenue outcomes.
- [[SportsEventTicketing]] becomes a market-design problem when the rights holder controls both primary sales and official secondary liquidity.
