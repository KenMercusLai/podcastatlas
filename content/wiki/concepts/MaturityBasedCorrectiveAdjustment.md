# Maturity-Based Corrective Adjustment

## Definition

Maturity-Based Corrective Adjustment is a provisional talent-identification procedure that estimates biological maturity and adjusts observed youth-sport performance to reduce the ranking advantage of earlier development.

## Current Synthesis

In the episode, [[StephenCopley]] and [[MichaelRomann]] estimate maturity from height, weight, chronological age, and sitting height, then apply the estimate to adolescent swimming times. The purpose is diagnostic: show coaches which late-developing swimmers may have more potential than raw finishing order suggests before discouragement or deselection removes them from the pipeline.

Gladwell proposes extending the principle to gifted placement and academic scores. That transfer is not validated by the swimming case. Academic performance reflects development alongside schooling, family resources, language, disability, motivation, test design, and other influences that a defensible adjustment would need to define and test.

## Key Claims

- Raw youth performance can confound current ability with biological maturity.
- Anthropometric and chronological measures can be used to estimate developmental timing.
- Adjusted rankings may reveal late maturers hidden by uncorrected race results.
- The procedure is an aid to selection judgment, not a claim that adjusted order predicts adult success perfectly.
- Educational correction is a research and governance proposal, not an established application in this source.

## Evidence

### Adolescent swimming

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] reports that no late maturers appeared in the initially top-performing quarter of a 700-boy sample and that adjustment changed every examined race order.

### Proposed educational transfer

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] records Gladwell's proposal to adjust prior academic scores and Copley's question about how many additional influences would also need correction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source omits the model, uncertainty, validation sample, prospective outcomes, and potential classification errors. Adjusted results could create new gaming incentives or conceal normative choices inside an algorithm. Sport and academic assessment have different outcomes, causal inputs, and stakes, so evidence in swimming cannot establish educational validity.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded synthesis of the swimming procedure and its purpose.
- Marked the education analogy as unvalidated and governance-sensitive.
- Distinguished selection support from deterministic reranking.
