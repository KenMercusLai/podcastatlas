## Definition

父母官式地方官伦理 / Paternal Local Office Ethic is the expectation that a local official should relate to the people under his jurisdiction as protective household elders would: caring for their welfare, restraining his own power, and treating their safety as prior to career gain or punitive display.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-619-fumuguan-zhege-ci-shi-zenme-laide-lmluscbtl3faczssz5didybawa1r|Hanji 619]] makes the phrase "父母官" a compact political-ethical vocabulary for local office. The concept is not presented as modern bureaucratic accountability; it is an older family-ethics analogy in which people hope that officials will protect, care, and govern for their benefit.

The episode's key case is [[YanXuWesternHan|严徐]]. His filial recommendation, respect for subordinates, self-reflection, soft speech, and grief for [[YingchuanCommandery|颍川郡]]'s people show a local official whose authority is measured by self-restraint and concern for people's exposure. [[HeBingWesternHan|何病]] then supplies the negative control case: a replacement governor's immediate killings make the cost of bad appointment fit visible.

This concept overlaps with [[HumaneLocalGovernance|仁厚地方治理]] and [[LocalGovernanceAsCare|地方治理作为照护]], but it is narrower. Its distinctive feature is the family metaphor attached to local office: the official is not only expected to deliver services or reduce burdens, but to treat subjects' vulnerability as personally binding.

## Key Claims

- "父母官" frames local office through household care and obligation, not only administrative rank.
- The ideal depends on self-restraint by the official because local authority has immediate coercive power over ordinary people.
- Personnel changes matter because a commandery can move quickly from protection to fear when a restrained official is replaced by a severe one.
- The concept carries both warmth and hierarchy: it dignifies care while preserving a paternal analogy rather than equal citizenship.
- The term helps connect moral character, appointment fit, and everyday security in premodern local governance.

## Evidence

- Vocabulary origin and expectation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-619-fumuguan-zhege-ci-shi-zenme-laide-lmluscbtl3faczssz5didybawa1r|Hanji 619]] explains "父母官" through family ethics and the popular hope that officials care for local people like parents.
- Yan Xu exemplar: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-619-fumuguan-zhege-ci-shi-zenme-laide-lmluscbtl3faczssz5didybawa1r|Hanji 619]] presents Yan Xu's filial background, respectful subordinate relations, self-reflection, restraint in speech, and grief for Yingchuan's people.
- Harsh-successor contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-619-fumuguan-zhege-ci-shi-zenme-laide-lmluscbtl3faczssz5didybawa1r|Hanji 619]] says He Bing's immediate arrests and executions created fear across Yingchuan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The paternal metaphor can conceal hierarchy and dependency. It expresses care, but it does not equal modern rights-based accountability or institutional checks on power. Hanji 619 also gives a short moral episode, so the source does not prove the full legal merits of Yan Xu's or He Bing's actions, nor does it settle the broader historical origin of the term beyond the episode's explanation.
