# Civil Aviation Development Fund / 民航发展基金

The civil aviation development fund is the Chinese government fund explained in [[shangye-xiaoyang-46-mai-jipiao-shi-weishenme-zongyao-duojiao-liang-bi-qian-1005487014]] as the current official form of what travelers used to call the airport construction fee. The source says the fund is collected from both passengers and airlines, then turned over to [[MinistryOfFinanceChina|China's Ministry of Finance]] rather than kept by a specific airport or airline.

The fund's logic is pooled aviation infrastructure finance. Passenger charges are described as usually 50 yuan on domestic routes and 90 yuan on international routes, while airline payments vary by aircraft weight and route category. The episode says public disclosures point to airport construction, air-traffic-control construction, route subsidies, and airport subsidies as main uses, especially where new airports or less-developed routes still need support.

## Key Claims
- The fund is not the same as an [[AviationFuelSurcharge|aviation fuel surcharge]] because it is a government fund rather than airline revenue.
- Uniform collection and centralized redistribution can make mature passenger markets help fund airport and route expansion in less-developed markets.
- The fund makes some airfare cost visible at checkout even though the spending target is system-level civil aviation capacity.
