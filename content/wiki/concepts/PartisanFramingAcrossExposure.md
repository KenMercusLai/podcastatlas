# Partisan Framing Across Exposure

## Definition

Partisan framing across exposure is the presentation of opposing people, claims, or events through a hostile interpretive frame that preserves visibility while discouraging understanding.

## Current Synthesis

The concept separates informational contact from fair or curious representation. A partisan outlet may repeatedly show the other side, but select its most provocative moments, attach moral condemnation, and interpret ambiguous material through an existing group story. The audience is exposed without leaving an identity-protective account.

This mechanism qualifies a simple echo-chamber diagnosis. [[InformationCocoon|Cocoons]] can be leaky, yet [[AffectivePolarization]] can still rise because the form, selection, and commentary surrounding cross-party content teach viewers whom to fear or despise. It also complicates one-way media-effects claims because pre-existing identity helps determine both outlet choice and reception.

## Key Claims

- Visibility of opponents does not establish representative or constructive exposure.
- Selection and commentary can turn shared events into incompatible moral narratives.
- Hostile cross-viewpoint contact can increase affective polarization without narrowing raw exposure.
- Pre-existing identity influences both media choice and the interpretation of framing.
- Echo-chamber measurement should distinguish absence of contact from antagonistic contact.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied summary does not identify the underlying studies, content sample, causal design, or the full confirmation coverage. Not all partisan interpretation is false, disagreement about moral significance can be legitimate, and exposure can have different effects depending on format, prior belief, repetition, context, and audience choice.
