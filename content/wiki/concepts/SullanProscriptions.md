# Sullan Proscriptions / 苏拉公敌名单

## Definition

Sullan proscriptions / 苏拉公敌名单 are the posted death lists by which [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]] turns civil-war victory into named killing, confiscation, family punishment, and wealth redistribution.

## Current Synthesis

The Caesar source already treated Sulla's death lists as a crucial pre-Caesar memory of republican terror. Episode 704 supplies the mechanism. After victory at the Colline Gate and the massacre of Samnite prisoners, Sulla sends death squads after Marian supporters. When supporters ask for clarity, he posts lists in the Forum.

The proscriptions matter because they institutionalize terror while keeping a public administrative form. Named men can be killed, their property is confiscated, their descendants are barred from office, and helpers can also be punished. The lists expand beyond revenge against Marian enemies into a property market where Sulla's allies acquire estates cheaply. [[MarcusLiciniusCrassus]] becomes the clearest profiteering case, and Sulla's rebuke of him does not erase the system's larger dependence on confiscation.

## Key Claims

- Proscription turns enemy status into a visible list-backed killing regime.
- The system combines revenge, political cleansing, intimidation, and confiscation.
- Family penalties make civil-war defeat hereditary by excluding sons and grandsons from office.
- Property redistribution rewards supporters and finances a new Sullan order.
- Profiteering is not an abuse outside the system; it is a predictable effect of list-based confiscation.
- The lists become a pre-Caesar precedent for Roman political terror.

## Evidence

- Pre-Caesar memory: [[124-kaisa-zhisi-lishi-de-yunjiao-shifou-yi-shixiao-781894361]] treats Sulla's death lists as evidence that Roman politics had entered civil terror before Caesar.
- Death squads and lists: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says Sulla begins with death squads and then posts proscription lists in the Forum.
- Legal-social penalties: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says listed men lose property, descendants are barred from office, and helpers may also be killed.
- Wealth redistribution: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says confiscated properties are bought cheaply and redistributed among Sulla's circle.
- Crassus case: [[704-roman-civil-war-master-of-the-republic-part-4-glt9669112656]] says Crassus adds rich men for personal gain until Sulla publicly rebukes him.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current evidence is source-scoped to Sulla's regime and later memory. It does not provide a full prosopography of every victim or a complete legal history of Roman proscription outside this episode.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to distinguish Sulla's public list-based terror from the broader emergency-violence pattern.
