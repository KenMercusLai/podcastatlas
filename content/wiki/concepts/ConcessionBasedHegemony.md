# Concession-Based Hegemony / 让利式霸权

Concession-based hegemony / 让利式霸权 is the diplomatic pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-2-qiongshe-jichi-de-zushiye-guanzhong-lo8uei2u1qwj9tszftel1b4lswoa]] attributes to [[GuanZhong|管仲]] and [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]]. The episode says Qi's international relationships cannot be solved by court entertainment alone; Guan Zhong instead wins support by returning previously occupied cities, giving gifts, and trading benefits for recognition.

The concept names a specific hegemonic style: first build domestic strength, then make enough concessions that nearby states prefer Qi-led order to continued conflict. In the source's account, that strategy culminates in the 651 BCE Kuiqiu meeting and alliance, where rulers from Lu, Song, Cao, and a [[ZhouRoyalHouse|Zhou]] representative help recognize Qi Huan Gong's hegemonic status.

## Key Claims
- Hegemony can be purchased partly through credible benefits, not only imposed by force.
- Returning land can strengthen leadership when it turns fear into willingness to follow.
- Gifts and hospitality support diplomacy, but the source says they cannot substitute for material concessions and strategic order.
- The policy depends on prior domestic capacity: [[HouseholdOccupationControl|population control]], [[MilitaryCivilianIntegration|military organization]], and [[StateResourceMonopoly|economic control]] make concession affordable.
- The concept sits below [[WangdaoVsBadao|王道]] in the existing Confucian frame: it is effective hegemony, not necessarily ritual-righteous ideal rule.
