## Definition

把总授权式治理 / direction-setting delegated governance is a leadership pattern in which the responsible official selects capable subordinates, gives them usable authority, holds overall priorities and accountability, and avoids unnecessary intervention in minor execution details.

## Current Synthesis

The current case comes from [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]]'s Donghai administration. The episode links his Huang-Lao preference for quiet government to a concrete management design: choose capable officials, authorize them to handle administration and litigation, and retain control of the overall direction without examining every small matter. Ji is frequently ill and rarely appears in the hall, yet the account credits the commandery with becoming orderly after a little more than a year.

This is not leader absence as a virtue by itself. The claimed success depends on prior personnel judgment, real delegation, and continued responsibility for direction. The pattern differs from both micromanagement and abdication: execution is decentralized, while priorities and final accountability remain concentrated.

## Key Claims

- Delegation begins with selecting capable people rather than merely reducing the leader's workload.
- Subordinates need usable authority if responsibility is to move with execution.
- A leader can add value by setting direction and boundaries without repeatedly reopening minor decisions.
- Low-intervention administration is not the same as no administration; personnel design and accountability remain active functions.
- The model can reduce dependence on the leader's constant physical presence, but its transferability depends on subordinate quality and information flow.

## Evidence

Personnel selection and authority:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-1-lp-pohns6ymvfpytkih9xkhwcoaw|Hanji 257 part 1]] says Ji An selects capable officials in Donghai and grants them authority over administrative and judicial work.

Direction rather than micromanagement:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-257-handai-dagangjing-jiujing-you-duogang-1-lp-pohns6ymvfpytkih9xkhwcoaw|Hanji 257 part 1]] says he focuses on the main direction rather than minor details and is nevertheless credited with restoring order despite frequent illness.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current evidence is a single compressed episode and does not identify the delegated officials, decision rights, reporting routines, measures of order, or causal contribution of Ji An's method relative to local conditions. Illness and infrequent audiences are constraints, not proof that absence improves governance. Delegation can also hide error or local abuse when selection, reporting, or review is weak. The concept therefore preserves direction, information, and accountability as necessary parts of the pattern.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the episode's Donghai account of selection, authorization, and direction-setting.
- Distinguished delegated light-touch governance from simple nonintervention or leader absence.
