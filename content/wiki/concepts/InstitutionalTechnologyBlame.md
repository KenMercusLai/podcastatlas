# Institutional Technology Blame

## Definition

Institutional technology blame is the tendency of public institutions to prefer a mechanical or software cause when ordinary human fallibility is psychologically uncomfortable, politically costly, or easily mistaken for blaming a victim.

## Current Synthesis

[[blame-game-blame-game|Blame Game]] describes an institutional feedback loop. A horrifying 911 recording made sudden acceleration vivid; more drivers reported similar events; journalists, officials, lawyers, and advocates amplified defect explanations; and staged or incomplete demonstrations made an electronic cause appear concrete. Human error remained comparatively unattractive because it sounded like blaming victims.

The useful synthesis is narrower than saying technology was innocent. Institutions should investigate design, software, corporate disclosure, and mechanical failure. The error arises when a preferred technological account outruns evidence and excludes [[HumanErrorWithoutNegligence|non-negligent human error]] from causal analysis or safety advice. Blame should remain distributed and evidence-sensitive rather than moving categorically from person to machine or back again.

## Key Claims

- Vivid tragedy can increase the availability and public credibility of a technological-risk narrative.
- Mechanical and software explanations can appear more humane than discussing ordinary human error.
- Political, legal, media, and consumer institutions can reinforce one another before causation is settled.
- A demonstration can manufacture apparent mechanism if the test setup is not disclosed or representative.
- Excluding human error from safety guidance can make a recoverable event harder to correct.
- Correcting over-attribution to technology does not prove that every system, company, or institution acted properly.

## Evidence

### Salience and escalation

- [[blame-game-blame-game]] links repeated coverage of the fatal 911 call to fear, additional reports, recalls, hearings, lawsuits, fines, and settlements.

### Demonstration and framing

- [[blame-game-blame-game]] reports that an ABC demonstration used deliberately altered throttle wiring while dramatic revving footage showed a car in park.

### Omitted human mechanism

- [[blame-game-blame-game]] says consumer guidance described mechanical causes and firm braking without mentioning the possibility that the driver was pressing the wrong pedal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a forceful retrospective argument and gives less weight to software reliability, Toyota's conduct, litigation evidence, and limits of its own track tests. Recalls, penalties, and settlements cannot be interpreted from this summary alone. Institutional attention to product defects is often necessary, especially where companies possess asymmetric information. The concept identifies premature closure around one causal category, not a presumption that users are at fault.

## What Changed

- Created a focused account of how vivid evidence, institutional incentives, and victim-blame concerns can privilege a technological explanation before causation is settled.
