## Definition

Stage-contingent integrative leadership is the capacity to repair a divided organization, retain capable operators, reconcile former enemies, and build legitimacy, coupled with the recognition that these strengths may not suffice for succession warfare, coercive consolidation, or long-term regime control.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses [[ZhangWujiJinYong|张无忌]] to separate leadership quality from universal fitness for every stage. Mingjiao already possesses personnel, military routines, and operational capacity; its immediate problem is internal fracture, retaliation, and external illegitimacy. Zhang's neutral position among rival claimants, martial prestige, moral credibility, willingness to consult incumbents, and renunciation of revenge fit that problem unusually well.

The same traits produce exposure after integration. Zhang does not construct a personal army, purge rivals, or desire rule strongly enough to compete through every zero-sum transition. The concept therefore avoids both conclusions that compassion proves weakness and that moral authority guarantees political survival. A leader can make an organization more coherent and legitimate while remaining poorly matched to its next stage.

## Key Claims

- Leadership quality is conditional on the organization's dominant problem and historical stage.
- Integration can require moral credibility, neutral convening authority, restraint, and reconciliation more than coercive ambition.
- Retaining capable incumbents can be wiser than replacing them merely to display control.
- Symbolic authority and external legitimacy differ from a personal faction, coercive apparatus, or succession plan.
- Traits that lower conflict during repair can increase vulnerability during zero-sum competition.
- A leader's political defeat does not erase earlier organizational value, and earlier success does not prove fitness for regime consolidation.

## Evidence

### Fit for repair and reconciliation

- [[lns0yzi-qrvazjy3lx2fr_kjisii-lns0yzi-qrvazjy3lx2fr_kjisii]] credits Zhang Wuji with reunifying Mingjiao, halting internal killing and revenge, retaining experienced operators, rescuing former enemies, and raising the organization's legitimacy.

### Boundary between prestige and control

- [[lns0yzi-qrvazjy3lx2fr_kjisii-lns0yzi-qrvazjy3lx2fr_kjisii]] presents Zhang as an outsider acceptable to rival camps and a strong diplomatic symbol, but not as the builder of Mingjiao's system or owner of an independent force.

### Stage reversal

- [[lns0yzi-qrvazjy3lx2fr_kjisii-lns0yzi-qrvazjy3lx2fr_kjisii]] argues that compassion and non-retaliation help during coalition repair while weakening Zhang in later competition with more ruthless political actors.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The framework derives from a fictional case and a conversational organizational analogy. It does not establish that compassion is inherently incompatible with state-building, that coercive leaders necessarily win, or that Zhang's character alone explains later outcomes. Existing institutions, rival resources, succession rules, external threats, and narrative design also matter. “Stage fit” should describe a changing problem set, not excuse avoidable indecision or treat brutality as a required competence.

## What Changed

- Established a distinction between integrative success and power-consolidating success.
- Identified inherited capacity, neutral convening authority, and external legitimacy as separate from personal control.
- Preserved moral achievement even when a leader is vulnerable in the next organizational stage.
