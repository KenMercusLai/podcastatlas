# Partisan Turnout-Motivation Asymmetry

## Definition
Partisan turnout-motivation asymmetry is an electoral mechanism in which one coalition treats an election as urgent and sustains unusually high participation while dissatisfied members of the opposing coalition abstain without necessarily changing party identity.

## Current Synthesis
The source interprets the reported 2026 Democratic wave as the product of two simultaneous movements: Democratic voters in places such as Fulton and Wayne counties maintained participation close to presidential-election scale, while Republican votes fell sharply across Latino, agricultural, and evangelical strongholds. This distinction matters because a large margin can emerge from differential motivation without equivalent long-term partisan conversion.

The proposed mechanism is policy-specific but not yet causally verified. The episode links Democratic urgency to constraining the Trump administration and Republican demobilization to immigration enforcement, living costs, tariffs, agricultural markets, and the Iran war. County returns can reveal where votes disappeared, but exit polls, validated voter files, surveys, and later elections are needed to separate abstention, switching, population change, candidate effects, and durable realignment.

## Key Claims
- Electoral margins depend on who participates, not only on underlying ideological preference.
- Opposition voters can sustain presidential-year urgency in a midterm when they view the election as a check on executive power.
- Government supporters may express policy dissatisfaction through abstention before adopting the opposing party.
- Latino, agricultural, and evangelical constituencies can demobilize for different policy reasons within the same national wave.
- Candidate-specific appeal and policy distancing can moderate local outcomes without eliminating the national turnout environment.
- A wave driven by asymmetric motivation does not by itself establish durable partisan realignment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode infers motivation from aggregated returns and selected local examples; those data cannot identify individual abstention or switching. Population change, ballot completion, candidate quality, uncontested races, campaign spending, election administration, and differential reporting may also affect comparisons. The claims about policy causes, ethnic and religious groups, and future persistence require voter-level evidence and later elections.

## What Changed
- Created a mechanism separating differential participation from durable partisan conversion.
