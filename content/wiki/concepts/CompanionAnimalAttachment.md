# Companion-Animal Attachment

## Definition
Companion-animal attachment is the slower, multisensory bond through which a young or dependent animal learns whether a caregiver is a reliable source of safety, need response, reunion, and recovery after stress.

## Current Synthesis
The episode distinguishes attachment from imprinting. Imprinting can occur rapidly in a sensitive period and shape following or mate recognition, whereas attachment develops through repeated caregiver interaction. A secure pattern is associated with reliable response and help returning to regulation after stress; separation and reunion behavior can reveal whether the caregiver functions as a secure base.

This framework shifts socialization away from mere exposure counts. Novel experiences matter, but an animal's ability to process them may depend on felt safety, developmental timing, and the quality of caregiver support. The source proposes this as a useful direction while acknowledging that claims about later separation or reduced checklist needs are not yet fully established.

## Key Claims
- Attachment and imprinting are distinct developmental processes.
- Reliable caregiver response helps an animal recover from stress and build security.
- Reunion behavior can provide evidence about secure or insecure attachment patterns.
- Early maternal and litter contact may matter beyond minimal survival or legal transfer age.
- Exposure checklists cannot substitute for safety, regulation, and individual readiness.

## Evidence
- Developmental distinction - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] contrasts filial and sexual imprinting with slower dog-human attachment.
- Secure-base mechanism - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] links secure attachment to caregiver responsiveness and stress recovery.
- Separation and reunion - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] treats reunion behavior as informative and questions puppy separation at eight weeks from an ethological perspective.
- Socialization qualification - [[what-pets-actually-want-need-dr-karolina-westlund-scim2878719314]] suggests security may change how animals handle novelty while explicitly noting incomplete evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source says the literature beyond very early puppy separation is limited and does not establish that secure attachment removes the need for deliberate socialization. Attachment labels should not become amateur diagnoses, and developmental recommendations vary by species, individual, breeder, household, health, and local welfare rules.

## What Changed
- Created a companion-animal attachment framework distinct from imprinting and exposure checklists.
- Preserved uncertainty around separation timing and attachment-socialization causality.
