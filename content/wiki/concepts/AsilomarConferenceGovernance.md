## Definition
Asilomar conference governance is the episode's model of expert-designed safety rules made durable through a temporary research pause, a bounded agenda, risk-tiered containment, personal liability, and enforcement by a public funder.

## Current Synthesis
The Asilomar case is neither pure self-regulation nor ordinary command-and-control regulation. Scientists surfaced the risk, paused selected work, and designed experiment-specific guardrails; legal warnings made collective danger personally salient; and the [[NationalInstitutesOfHealth]] used funding eligibility to extend compliance beyond conference participants. The model therefore depends on a hybrid chain from domain expertise to an external lever.

Its relevance to AI is conditional. Narrow negotiation, concrete risk tiers, liability, and a hard-to-replace enforcement point may travel across domains, but NIH dependence does not map cleanly onto privately financed, internationally competitive AI companies. Representation is also part of institutional quality: [[JanetMertz]]'s exclusion and concentrated career loss show that a technically successful agreement can still distribute voice and cost unfairly.

## Key Claims
- Temporary restraint can create negotiating space without committing a field to permanent prohibition.
- A narrow safety agenda is more tractable than trying to settle every social consequence of a technology at once.
- Risk-tiered physical and biological containment lets controls scale with the proposed experiment.
- Personal liability can change incentives by translating diffuse public harm into consequences for decision-makers.
- Expert-authored rules become more durable when an external institution controls a resource participants need.
- Representation and burden allocation are governance criteria, not side issues.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not establish that Asilomar alone prevented harm, and the feared escape remained hypothetical. The conference also spread technical knowledge and nearly accelerated the work it sought to govern. Its attendees were not broadly representative, the final vote was not unanimous, and ordinary liability had defenders. Most importantly, AI firms do not share the same dependence on one public funder, so any modern analogy requires a different scarce enforcement point and international coverage.

## What Changed
- Established a hybrid governance model joining expert rule design to public-funder enforcement.
- Added representation and unequal burden as tests of safety-conference legitimacy.
