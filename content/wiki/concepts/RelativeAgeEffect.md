# Relative Age Effect

## Definition

The relative age effect is a systematic advantage or disadvantage created when a fixed eligibility cutoff groups children who can differ by almost a year in age and development, then treats their current performance as directly comparable.

## Current Synthesis

[[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited|Outliers, Revisited]] grounds the effect in Canadian youth hockey: a January 1 cutoff makes early-year children relatively older, and at ages nine or ten they may be larger, stronger, or better coordinated. Coaches select them more often, after which extra practice and competition make the initially arbitrary difference increasingly real.

The same logic can operate in education through grades, gifted placement, behavior judgments, and learning-difficulty referrals. The effect is not a claim that birth month fixes ability; it is a claim that developmentally uneven comparison can change which children receive investment.

## Key Claims

- A common cutoff can create nearly twelve months of developmental spread inside one nominal age group.
- Early-year overrepresentation in elite Canadian hockey is consistent with selection favoring relatively older children.
- Selection converts an initial maturity difference into unequal coaching, practice, competition, and confidence.
- Educational consequences can include different grades, gifted-program access, and learning or behavior labels.
- The direction and size of the effect depend on the cutoff, age, domain, participation system, and institutional response.

## Evidence

### Hockey birth distribution

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] attributes a roughly 40–30–20–10 quarterly pattern and its persistence on a later Canadian junior roster to the research tradition associated with [[RogerBarnsley]].

### Cross-domain selection

- [[outliers-revisited-outliers-revisited]] reports a large English basketball selection disparity through [[AdamKelly]] and describes parallel educational patterns in grades, gifted placement, and problem identification.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied summary does not provide study designs, effect sizes across ages, counterexamples, sex differences, attrition, or the later literature. Birth month does not determine individual ability, and relative age should not replace direct observation, developmental context, or domain-specific evidence. Delayed school entry may shift an individual child's relative position without repairing the selection system.

## What Changed

- Created a cross-domain synthesis centered on cutoff-based developmental comparison.
- Separated the initial age difference from the institutional process that compounds it.
- Preserved variation by domain, age, cutoff, and individual development.
