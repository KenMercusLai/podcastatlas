# Choice Closure and Satisfaction

## Definition
Choice closure and satisfaction is the idea that freedom among options can be valuable before a decision, while repeatedly reopening comparison after choosing can reduce satisfaction with the selected option.

## Current Synthesis
[[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] uses Dan Gilbert's choice experiments to separate option value from post-choice evaluation. Participants were often more satisfied when an initially selected picture or photograph could not later be exchanged. The proposed explanation is that a settled choice gives people room to synthesize value, whereas reversible choice keeps alternatives active and invites repeated comparison.

The practical implication is not to eliminate flexibility or remain in harmful commitments. Closure is useful when a choice is safe, sufficiently informed, and costly to keep reevaluating. Reversibility remains important under uncertainty, changing evidence, coercion, or risk.

## Key Claims
- More options before deciding do not guarantee more satisfaction after deciding.
- Keeping alternatives mentally active can dilute the value attached to a chosen option.
- Commitment can create space for appreciation and synthetic happiness.
- Repeated reevaluation consumes attention and may increase comparison or regret.
- Closure is conditional on safety, information quality, reversibility needs, and changing evidence.

## Evidence
- Irreversible-choice experiments: [[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] summarizes picture and photograph studies in which participants were often happier with choices they could not later exchange.
- Reward-dilution account: [[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] proposes that entertaining alternatives can weaken the reward attached to the selected option.
- Cognitive-load account: [[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] links ongoing evaluation to effortful prefrontal processing and discusses ego depletion as a possible qualification.
- Happiness model: [[science-based-tools-for-increasing-happiness-scim9472441223]] includes making the best of chosen options among the tools for synthetic happiness.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not show that irreversible choice is generally superior, that people should ignore new information, or that commitment should override safety and consent. Its ego-depletion and neural-resource explanations are host-level interpretations of a broader behavioral finding and remain source-scoped. High-stakes choices may benefit from deliberate review even when review temporarily lowers satisfaction.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish valuable freedom before choice from costly option maintenance afterward.
- Added safety, consent, uncertainty, and new-information boundaries to the commitment claim.
