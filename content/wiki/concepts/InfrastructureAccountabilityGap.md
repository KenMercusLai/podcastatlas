# Infrastructure Accountability Gap

Infrastructure accountability gap is the distance between formal safety rules and real prevention. [[taken-littorally-spains-sudden-crisis-in-ceuta-6a70712c034f16a52ebfaed7]] develops the concept through [[India]]'s falling-object deaths, where [[LeoMirani]] says regulations exist but enforcement, maintenance, corruption control, and incentives often fail.

The source emphasizes after-the-fact response. Major incidents can produce public outcry, suspensions, firings, arrests, and compensation, but those actions do not necessarily create durable inspection, repair, data collection, or accountability systems.

## Key Claims
- Rules do not become safety unless fines, inspections, procurement, and maintenance change day-to-day behavior.
- Small occasional fines can be cheaper than serious compliance.
- Corruption and negligence can make infrastructure look built or approved while leaving hidden risk in place.
- Scapegoat arrests and ex gratia compensation can satisfy the visible need for response without solving the prevention problem.
- Counting incidents is part of accountability; if the public system cannot measure the problem, it cannot reliably govern it.
