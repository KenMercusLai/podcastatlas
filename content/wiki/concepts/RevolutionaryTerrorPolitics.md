# Revolutionary Terror Politics / 革命恐怖政治

## Definition

Revolutionary terror politics is the interaction of emergency threat, material deprivation, factional purity, denunciation, surveillance, institutional coercion, and morally authorized violence in the claimed defense of a revolution and its people.

## Current Synthesis

The combined sources move terror from rhetoric into a staged political system. War, scarcity, the [[VendeeWar|Vendée]], military betrayal, and federalist revolt made emergency claims plausible; Marat and factional conflict recoded opponents as hidden enemies; the [[RevolutionaryTribunal]], surveillance committees, deputies on mission, and [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety]] supplied coercive capacity; and Marat's assassination added martyrdom and fear of ubiquitous assassins to a system already shedding procedural and representative restraint.

## Key Claims

- Terror politics grows when genuine danger and overgeneralized suspicion reinforce each other.
- Denunciation becomes a governing technology when opponents are defined as traitors rather than rivals.
- Material hardship makes sabotage explanations persuasive when revolution fails to improve daily life.
- Popular pressure can displace representative choice while still speaking in the name of "the people."
- Separate emergency institutions can accumulate into a system without a single initial master plan.
- Martyrdom can convert a leader's death into moral permission for further coercion.

## Evidence

### Moralized enemies and popular violence

- [[125-mala-zhisi-beibi-de-ansha-huo-zhengyi-de-chujue-783972516]] connects Marat, the September Massacres, virtue language, factional purity, and the refusal to recognize partial interests.

### Multi-front emergency and conspiracy

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] joins economic collapse, European war, Vendée revolt, Dumouriez's defection, and federalist resistance into the 1793 threat environment.

### Institutional machinery and factional purge

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] describes rapid political justice, surveillance, central emergency direction, armed encirclement of the Convention, and the removal of twenty-nine Girondins.

### Assassination feedback

- [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]] shows Marat's death increasing conspiracy fear, imprisonment of Girondins, Danton's removal, and Robespierre's ascent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The crisis was not wholly manufactured: France faced real war, rebellion, military betrayal, scarcity, and political violence.
- Institutional improvisation does not erase responsibility for later choices or imply that terror was inevitable.
- The sources center Marat and Corday and do not exhaust regional, colonial, social, or later chronological dimensions of the Terror.
- Episode-level death estimates and psychological comparisons remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- The concept now integrates material crisis, real betrayal, institutional formation, factional purge, and martyrdom feedback.
- The Terror is judged an accumulated system rather than only violent rhetoric or a pre-written program.
- Corday's assassination adds evidence that exemplary counter-violence can radicalize the regime it targets.
