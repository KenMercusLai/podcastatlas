# Designed Technological Desire / 技术欲望设计

## Definition

Designed technological desire is the deliberate shaping of an artifact's attraction, language, interface, or social meaning so that people want to accept or use it, including when acceptance carries costs they do not fully share or control.

## Current Synthesis

[[Pandora]] is manufactured not merely to exist but to be welcomed. Beauty, skill, charm, and persuasive language are part of the mechanism by which [[Zeus]]'s harmful gift passes through [[Epimetheus]]'s choice. The episode generalizes this structure to technology: tools can change the user's attention, wants, and decision environment rather than neutrally answering a pre-existing desire.

The ethical question is therefore wider than accuracy or efficiency. Evaluation must ask which behavior attraction is meant to produce, who selected that objective, what information or friction is withheld, and who bears the resulting costs.

## Key Claims

- Attraction and persuasion can be functional design features rather than incidental decoration.
- A user's acceptance does not by itself prove informed or welfare-enhancing choice.
- Technology can create, intensify, redirect, or normalize desire while expanding material capability.
- Ethical evaluation should examine the choice environment as well as the artifact's output.
- Goal setters remain responsible when persuasive design predictably transfers costs to users or outsiders.

- Designed attraction: [[369-panduola-shi-renlei-zuizao-de-keji-emeng-ma-hesie-de-gongzuo-yu-shiri-lkr0ibzoden-ip-es5xpnhnbvz7]] describes Pandora's beauty, language, and capacities as intentionally assembled to secure acceptance.
- Choice environment: [[369-panduola-shi-renlei-zuizao-de-keji-emeng-ma-hesie-de-gongzuo-yu-shiri-lkr0ibzoden-ip-es5xpnhnbvz7]] argues that technical products alter attention and desire, not only material conditions.
- Ethical boundary: [[369-panduola-shi-renlei-zuizao-de-keji-emeng-ma-hesie-de-gongzuo-yu-shiri-lkr0ibzoden-ip-es5xpnhnbvz7]] separates successful operation from the legitimacy of the objective being served.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source provides a literary analogy, not empirical proof about a particular product or a complete theory of persuasive design. Desire is not always manufactured, attraction is not inherently manipulative, and users retain varying degrees of agency. Harm depends on disclosure, alternatives, reversibility, power, distribution, and foreseeable effects.

## What Changed

- Created a design-ethics frame linking artifact attraction to user choice and externalized consequences.
