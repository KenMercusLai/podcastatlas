# Sleep Duration U-Shape

Sleep duration U-shape is the population-level pattern in which both short and long sleep durations are associated with worse health outcomes. [[the-case-of-the-missing-totem-aung-san-suu-kyi-6a560fc4f96471dde4821d2f]] adds it through a sleep segment that compares 2015 recommendations with a newer Nature study using [[UKBiobank]] data.

The source says a newer [[OrganSpecificAgingClock]] analysis found U-shaped patterns across nine body parts and estimated optimal ranges around the lower seven-hour mark. It immediately limits that claim through [[PopulationLevelHealthAdvice]]: association does not settle causality, and individual needs vary.

## Key Claims
- Too little sleep is linked in the source to next-day cognitive, stress, and mood costs, and chronic deprivation is linked to cognitive decline, psychiatric disorders, and early death.
- Too much sleep is also associated with risk, but the episode treats that association as harder to interpret because illness can cause longer sleep.
- Population ranges are useful guardrails but weak personal prescriptions.
- Duration is only one dimension of sleep health beside quality, regularity, and continuity.
