## Definition

Reckless courage decision risk / 有勇无谋决策风险 is the failure mode where willingness to face danger is mistaken for good judgment, so an actor accepts avoidable exposure before the action has a realistic path to success.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-474-guji-kangkai-fusi-wo-weihe-renwei-ta-yuchun-lp-07zvaxr21dt2mtva2ndp6b9by|Hanji 474]] builds the concept around [[GuJiWesternHan|谷吉]]. Gu Ji's courage is not denied: he knowingly escorts [[ZhizhiChanyu|郅支单于]]'s hostage son beyond the safer frontier limit and accepts possible death. The host's criticism is that the risk is foreseeable and poorly converted into outcome. If the envoy dies before changing the strategic field, death becomes loss, not achievement.

The episode strengthens the point through Confucius's answer to Zilu. Confucius rejects the person who fights a tiger barehanded, crosses a river on foot, and dies without regret; he wants someone cautious, planning-aware, and able to complete the task. Courage therefore needs timing, role awareness, and completion logic. Retreat or concealment can preserve future agency, while rash persistence can spend the actor too early.

## Key Claims

- Courage is not sufficient when the actor lacks a plan that can plausibly finish the task.
- Self-sacrifice can become a vanity frame if the death is foreseeable and does not solve the strategic problem.
- Advisers can correctly limit a mission when deeper exposure gives the hostile counterpart control over the envoy's life.
- Useful courage combines willingness to act with caution, planning, and outcome discipline.
- Flexible retreat or self-preservation can be wiser than visible death when the environment is unsuitable.
- The same principle applies to personal conduct and statecraft: survival preserves future options, while premature death closes them.

- Adviser caution: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-474-guji-kangkai-fusi-wo-weihe-renwei-ta-yuchun-lp-07zvaxr21dt2mtva2ndp6b9by|Hanji 474]] says Gong Yu and Kuang Heng judge Zhizhi Chanyu's submission unreliable.
- Failed sacrifice logic: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-474-guji-kangkai-fusi-wo-weihe-renwei-ta-yuchun-lp-07zvaxr21dt2mtva2ndp6b9by|Hanji 474]] says Gu Ji treats possible death as a useful exchange, but the episode reads the result as avoidable loss.
- Confucian courage test: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-474-guji-kangkai-fusi-wo-weihe-renwei-ta-yuchun-lp-07zvaxr21dt2mtva2ndp6b9by|Hanji 474]] uses Confucius's rejection of barehanded tiger-fighting and foot-crossing rivers to define courage as cautious completion rather than death-seeking.
- Zilu's death: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-474-guji-kangkai-fusi-wo-weihe-renwei-ta-yuchun-lp-07zvaxr21dt2mtva2ndp6b9by|Hanji 474]] uses Zilu's death while fixing his cap as a warning that rigid courage can erase future possibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not reject physical courage, martyrdom, or risk-taking as such. It is bounded to cases where the actor can foresee danger, has been warned by competent advisers, and lacks a convincing path from exposure to achievement. Hanji 474 is a podcast interpretation rather than a full diplomatic record, so Gu Ji's exact private motive and the original wording of the advice remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 474's comparison between Gu Ji's mission choice, Confucius's standard for a capable companion, and Zilu's rigid death.
