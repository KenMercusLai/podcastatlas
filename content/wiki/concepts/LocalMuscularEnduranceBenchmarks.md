# Local Muscular Endurance Benchmarks / 局部肌耐力基准

## Definition
Local muscular endurance benchmarks are the episode's repetition-capacity tests for one or a few muscle groups: a 60-second front plank, a 45-second side plank, push-up counts by sex, and an as-many-reps-as-possible set at 75% of an already established strength test.

## Current Synthesis
Muscular endurance is treated as a local limitation rather than a whole-body one, which is why the tests are small and specific. Galpin's standards are a 60-second front plank, a 45-second side plank, and 25 or more consecutive push-ups for men and 15 or more for women, with five to 15 push-ups described as marginal and below five as generally a problem.

The efficient alternative is to reuse the strength test from [[StrengthBenchmarkTesting]]: load it to 75% and do as many reps as possible, with more than eight reps as the target and below eight as a muscular-endurance problem. Because progress tracking matters more than a single score, push-up form has to stay consistent between tests, with full elbow lockout at the top and a full or near-full chest touch at the bottom.

## Key Claims
- Muscular endurance is usually local to a muscle group or movement rather than a global physiological limit.
- A 60-second front plank, a 45-second side plank, and 25-plus push-ups for men or 15-plus for women are the episode's headline standards.
- Push-up counts below five are treated as generally a problem, and five to 15 as a marginal range.
- Running an as-many-reps-as-possible set at 75% of an established strength test gets a second measurement from the same setup, with more than eight reps as the target.
- Consistent form between tests is what allows repetition progress to be compared over time.

- Plank and push-up standards: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] gives a 60-second front plank, a 45-second side plank, and 25-plus push-ups for men and 15-plus for women, with five to 15 described as marginal and below five as generally a problem.
- Repetition range: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] places muscular endurance in a typical five-to-fifty repetition range and calls it almost always local to one or a few muscle groups.
- Load-reuse method: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] suggests taking the strength test already performed, loading it to 75%, and doing as many reps as possible, with more than eight reps as the target and below eight indicating a problem.
- Form consistency: [[essentials-how-to-assess-improve-all-aspects-of-your-fitness-dr-andy-galpin-scim5641095924]] requires full elbow lockout at the top and a full or near-full chest touch at the bottom so progress can be tracked between tests.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Plank and push-up scores depend on body weight, limb length, technique, and pain status, so they are practical screens rather than norms, and a single repetition test is sensitive to motivation and pacing. The 75% load-reuse method requires an already-safe strength test and an estimated one-rep max, which makes it less suitable for beginners who have not cleared the movement and strength screens.
