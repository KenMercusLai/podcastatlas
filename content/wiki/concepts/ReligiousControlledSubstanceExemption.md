# Religious Controlled-Substance Exemption

Religious controlled-substance exemption is the legal possibility that a religious group may receive protection for using an otherwise prohibited substance as sacrament. In [[a-trip-to-the-magic-mushroom-megachurch]], the idea connects the [[NativeAmericanChurch]] peyote precedent, a 2006 [[SupremeCourt]] ayahuasca decision, and newer [[PsychedelicChurches]] such as [[ZydorChurch]].

The source keeps the distinction between assertion and protection clear. A church can claim religious freedom, but [[JohnRapp]] says there is no full legal protection unless the government has granted an exemption, so the [[ControlledSubstancesAct]] and [[DrugEnforcementAdministration]] remain central risks.

## Key Claims
- Religious status can matter under U.S. law, but it has to be made legible to courts and agencies.
- The legal question is not only sincerity; authorities also ask about harm, diversion risk, security, and institutional practice.
- Precedents can help newer churches argue, but they do not automatically cover every substance, dose, church, or distribution model.
- Applying for an exemption can itself create risk if the application documents ongoing or prior illegal conduct.
