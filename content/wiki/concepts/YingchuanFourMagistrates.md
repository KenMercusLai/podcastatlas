## Definition

颍川四长 / Yingchuan Four Magistrates is the source label for four late-Han Yingchuan-associated county heads, [[HanShaoLateHan|韩韶]], [[ZhongHaoYingchuanLateHan|钟皓]], [[XunShuLateHan|荀淑]], and [[ChenShiLateHan|陈寔]], remembered for virtuous local governance, public benefit, and a wider reputation culture where moral authority also shapes responsibility and self-protection.

## Current Synthesis

The concept still rests most directly on Hanji 842 and should be read as a reputation pattern rather than a complete collective biography. The episode first gives Han Shao's concrete case: during rebellion and refugee hunger, his reputation deters bandits and his granary decision saves displaced households. It then names Zhong Hao, Xun Shu, and Chen Shi with Han Shao as the Four Magistrates, moving the judgment from one official's emergency choice to a county-level ideal.

Hanji 832 adds texture around three of the four names. Chen Shi receives a concrete case of appointment-pressure responsibility and Taiqiu governance, while Zhong Hao and Xun Shu are placed in a famous-worthy comparison. Zhong Hao's counsel to Zhong Jin also shows that the same reputation field includes prudence and survival judgment, not only benevolent administration.

The important synthesis is that late-Han local office can become a moral and practical prestige site. County heads are not treated merely as low-level administrators. In crisis, the right magistrate can lower violence, shelter displaced people, make public stores serve survival before paperwork, and carry enough moral authority that later stories about blame, petitions, and self-protection attach to the same names. The sources do not prove that all four acted the same way; they show that they were remembered together under a shared local-governance and reputation standard.

## Key Claims

- The Four Magistrates label turns county-level service into a public moral reputation field.
- Han Shao's granary relief supplies the cohort's clearest current example of practical people-benefiting governance.
- Chen Shi's Hanji 832 branch gives the cohort a concrete responsibility and Taiqiu-governance case outside Han Shao's relief story.
- Zhong Hao and Xun Shu's Hanji 832 comparison broadens the label into a famous-worthy reputation field.
- Yingchuan functions as a shared origin or reputation marker linking otherwise separate local officials.
- The concept joins routine 德政 and emergency responsibility rather than treating good governance as only ordinary kindness.
- The current evidence supports cohort membership but not a detailed comparison among all four officials.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source notes provide a strong label but uneven detail. Han Shao receives the fullest crisis anecdote in Hanji 842, while Hanji 832 adds Chen Shi and Zhong Hao material without giving a comparable administrative scene for Xun Shu. The concept should therefore not rank the four or infer identical governing methods.

## What Changed

- Hanji 832 adds Chen Shi's responsibility and Taiqiu-governance evidence to the Four Magistrates field.
- Hanji 832 also adds Zhong Hao and Xun Shu reputation comparison, making the concept less dependent on Han Shao alone.
