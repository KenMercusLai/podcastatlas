## Definition

经典典故政治改写 / classical allusion political recoding is the court-politics pattern where a speaker's classical or moral reference is reinterpreted as a direct threat to the ruler, turning advice into evidence of disloyalty, resentment, faction, or treason.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-441-gaikuanrao-bei-hanxuandi-bisi-beihou-de-zhichang-zhenxiang-tai-fengci-lm7iqjl6m638ulcpvir1jv7gxho0|Hanji 441]] creates the concept through [[GaiKuanraoWesternHan|盖宽饶]]. Gai uses classical language to criticize the decline of sage learning and the substitution of law and trusted palace influence for older moral-political ideals. His reference to the distinction between "天下为公" and "天下为私" is then recoded as if he were asking [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] to abdicate to him.

The concept's key point is not that all classical allusions are safe if the speaker means well. In a tense court, meaning is politically produced after the text leaves the speaker. Hostile officials can select the most threatening implication, while an already dissatisfied ruler can treat that interpretation as confirmation of disloyalty. [[ZhengChangRemonstratorWesternHan|郑昌]]'s failed defense shows that restoring intended meaning may not defeat a usable hostile reading.

## Key Claims

- Classical language can create authority for advice while also creating interpretive openings for accusation.
- Political danger rises when an allusion touches rulership, succession, abdication, legitimacy, or the ruler's personal dignity.
- The hostile reading need not match the speaker's intention to become institutionally effective.
- Recoding works best when the ruler already dislikes the speaker or finds the criticism humiliating.
- Protective defense may fail once the hostile interpretation satisfies the ruler's need to punish or remove the speaker.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is currently grounded in a single source case. It should not be generalized into a rule that classical allusion always fails or that direct modern language is always safer. The current source also gives the host's interpretation of Gai's intended meaning, not an independent philological reconstruction of the full memorial. The concept tracks a mechanism of accusation, not a complete theory of Han memorial rhetoric.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 441's recoding of Gai Kuanrao's classical allusion into an abdication accusation.
