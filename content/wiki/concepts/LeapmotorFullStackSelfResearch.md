# Leapmotor Full-Stack Self-Research / 零跑全域自研

## Definition
Leapmotor full-stack self-research is the strategy of controlling core vehicle technology, software, electrical systems, manufacturing, platforms, and selected component production so that an automaker can reduce supplier dependence, compress external margins, and coordinate product cost, quality, and iteration.

## Current Synthesis
The current source presents full-stack self-research as Leapmotor's answer to low-margin car economics. Zhu Jiangming argues that cars require platformization, scale, self-manufacturing, and fine management; otherwise a startup cannot both price aggressively and survive. The self-research claim is not total vertical integration: Leapmotor still chooses where to extend, starting from electric drive and moving into related high-power electronics while leaving cells to a joint-venture path with a battery maker.

## Key Claims
- Full-stack self-research is used as a cost and quality strategy, not only as a technology-status claim.
- The strategy depends on early hiring of core technical leads across vehicle layout, body, chassis, battery, electric drive, and electric control.
- The economic claim is supplier-margin compression: self-made core parts can capture profit that would otherwise sit in the supplier layer.
- The strategy is bounded by scale and specialization, so not every component should be internalized immediately.
- Prior embedded hardware and chip-organization experience can make a car founder more willing to own core technical systems.

## Evidence
- Strategic origin: [[lingpao-qiche-zhujiangming-luoyonghao-lingpao-shinian-buhui-jiang-gushi-de-ren-ruhe-maicheng-diyi-lhei72y0mlqrh2xxbetnqdp-0stb]] says Zhu personally chose full-stack self-research while studying Toyota-like industrial-chain and parts-system logic.
- Early organization: [[lingpao-qiche-zhujiangming-luoyonghao-lingpao-shinian-buhui-jiang-gushi-de-ren-ruhe-maicheng-diyi-lhei72y0mlqrh2xxbetnqdp-0stb]] says Leapmotor recruited core people for layout, interior/exterior, body, chassis, battery, electric drive, and electric control from the beginning.
- Economic logic: [[lingpao-qiche-zhujiangming-luoyonghao-lingpao-shinian-buhui-jiang-gushi-de-ren-ruhe-maicheng-diyi-lhei72y0mlqrh2xxbetnqdp-0stb]] says self-research can capture roughly the supplier profit space that would otherwise sit outside the automaker.
- Scope boundary: [[lingpao-qiche-zhujiangming-luoyonghao-lingpao-shinian-buhui-jiang-gushi-de-ren-ruhe-maicheng-diyi-lhei72y0mlqrh2xxbetnqdp-0stb]] says Leapmotor extends from electric drive into compressors, power supplies, and other high-power electronics, while handling cells through a joint venture rather than full internal production.
- Capability inheritance: [[lingpao-qiche-zhujiangming-luoyonghao-lingpao-shinian-buhui-jiang-gushi-de-ren-ruhe-maicheng-diyi-lhei72y0mlqrh2xxbetnqdp-0stb]] connects Leapmotor's self-research confidence to Dahua's earlier chip and embedded-product experience.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is currently grounded in one founder interview. It does not independently prove that self-research lowers total cost after R&D, capex, warranty, inventory, management complexity, or opportunity cost. It also does not show which parts should be internalized across other automakers.
