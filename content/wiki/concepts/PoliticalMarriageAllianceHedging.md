## Definition

Political marriage alliance hedging is the use of a marriage tie to reinforce cooperation with a necessary but mistrusted partner while leaving the partners' underlying territorial and security interests unresolved.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1113 applies the pattern to [[SunQuan|孙权]], [[LiuBei|刘备]], and [[LadySunLiuBei|孙夫人]] after [[RedCliffsBattle|赤壁之战]]. Sun Quan needs Liu Bei to share resistance against [[CaoCao|曹操]], but Liu Bei's foothold and expansion in [[Jingzhou|荆州]] also create an autonomy risk. Marriage adds a family bond where military necessity alone does not generate trust.

The tie is therefore a hedge, not a resolution. For Sun Quan it helps retain a useful partner; for Liu Bei it is difficult to reject while his territorial position remains dependent. The episode's household fear scene makes the asymmetry personal, while its link to later Jingzhou conflict shows that kinship cannot erase divergent control claims.

## Key Claims

- Political marriage can add monitoring, obligation, prestige, and family symbolism to an alliance that remains strategically necessary.
- The stronger partner may use marriage to keep an increasingly autonomous ally closer without immediately coercing or destroying him.
- The weaker partner may accept the tie because refusal risks coalition support, territory, or security even when private comfort is low.
- Marriage can stabilize cooperation in the short run without changing the payoff structure that produces later rivalry.
- The person transferred through the marriage bears costs that state-centered alliance analysis can hide.

## Evidence

- Dual-threat setting: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1113-liubei-qu-sunquan-meimei-guocheng-zhong-you-duo-beiwei-lqvpwr7tkvzlg2o3-m46srk98cnm|Hanji 1113]] presents Cao Cao as Sun Quan's urgent enemy and Liu Bei as a needed but worrying partner.
- Marriage decision: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1113-liubei-qu-sunquan-meimei-guocheng-zhong-you-duo-beiwei-lqvpwr7tkvzlg2o3-m46srk98cnm|Hanji 1113]] says Sun Quan marries his sister to Liu Bei to reinforce the coalition.
- Unequal household effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1113-liubei-qu-sunquan-meimei-guocheng-zhong-you-duo-beiwei-lqvpwr7tkvzlg2o3-m46srk98cnm|Hanji 1113]] uses armed attendants and Liu Bei's fear to express a relationship that is intimate in form but political in structure.
- Durability limit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1113-liubei-qu-sunquan-meimei-guocheng-zhong-you-duo-beiwei-lqvpwr7tkvzlg2o3-m46srk98cnm|Hanji 1113]] connects the marriage to unresolved Jingzhou conflict while stopping short of proving direct causation for later war.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current evidence is one podcast account and does not reconstruct Lady Sun's own decision, the complete marriage negotiations, or the full territorial settlement. Marriage may have had household, ceremonial, diplomatic, and surveillance functions not developed here. The episode's claim that the tie planted the cause of later Jingzhou loss is narrowed to a structural warning: marriage did not remove the prior conflict, but this source alone does not show that it caused Guan Yu's death or the alliance rupture.

## What Changed

- Created the concept to separate short-run alliance reinforcement from long-run interest convergence.
- Made the transferred woman's political cost visible within the strategic mechanism.
- Bounded the later Jingzhou connection to unresolved structure rather than direct causation.
