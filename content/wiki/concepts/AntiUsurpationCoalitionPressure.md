# Anti-Usurpation Coalition Pressure / 讨逆同盟压力

Anti-usurpation coalition pressure / 讨逆同盟压力 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1015-sanguo-luanshi-weishenme-yuanshu-gan-chengdi-lim0jemkwyjcesj2nvsr3jndhxfk|Hanji 1015]] draws from [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s 197 CE self-coronation at [[Shouchun|寿春]]. When a non-consensus actor claims the imperial title, the problem is not only that rivals dislike him. His claim changes their public obligations: ignoring it can make them look as if they accept the usurpation.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]] supplies the pre-public internal pressure stage. Yuan Shu's generals give no open support, [[YanXiangLateHan|阎象]] warns through the [[ZhouWenwang|周文王]] precedent, and [[ZhangChengLateHan|张承]] says rule rests in virtue rather than strength. Pressure therefore exists before rival armies mobilize: internal actors already avoid becoming guarantors of the claim.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1002-yuanshu-chengdi-hou-sunce-chengwei-zui-da-ying-llya3c2-pefujwxdxefvqxwxjizjo|Hanji 1002]] supplies the warning-and-ally-loss stage before the formal self-coronation account. [[SunCe|孙策]] tells Yuan Shu that the current [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] has not forfeited the throne like a tyrant, and that prophecy-flatterers will not bear the consequences. The pressure begins before armies move: a close dependent can already see that supporting the claim would make him look complicit and would give rivals a clean attack pretext.

The source says this matters differently for different actors. [[CaoCao|曹操]] depends on the [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] court and the image of punishing disobedient subjects; [[LiuBei|刘备]] depends on a仁义 reputation; [[SunCe|孙策]], [[YuanShao|袁绍]], and [[LyuBu|吕布]] also gain opportunities to attack, detach themselves from Yuan Shu's illegitimate order, or avoid being seen as his accomplices. "讨逆" therefore becomes a temporary alliance language that joins strategic interest to reputation maintenance.

The concept overlaps with [[MoralizedWarPretext|道德化战争借口]] and [[StatusOrderBacklash|名分秩序反弹]], but is narrower. Moralized war pretext names the public reason that makes a war legible. Status order backlash names anger after rank hierarchy is disturbed. Anti-usurpation coalition pressure names the forced-response situation created when a claimant attacks the still-recognized sovereign title itself.

## Key Claims
- Hanji 1001 adds that anti-usurpation pressure can first appear as adviser silence and internal refusal before public coalition formation.
- Open usurpation can force rivals to respond because non-response looks like recognition.
- A weak sovereign can still supply a powerful legitimacy vocabulary if actors need his name to justify action.
- The anti-usurpation frame can make temporary cooperation possible among actors who otherwise remain competitors.
- The pressure is reputational as well as strategic: each actor's own claimed identity constrains what it can ignore.
- The mechanism does not prove the coalition is sincere or durable; it explains why the first move against the claimant becomes unusually easy to justify.
- Hanji 1002 adds that the pressure can start as pre-emptive ally exit before the claimant has completed the formal ritual program.
