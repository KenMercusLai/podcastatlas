## Definition

西汉帝王寿命比较 / Western Han imperial longevity comparison is the episode's frame for treating ruler lifespan as a political time scale. It compares [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] with other Western Han emperors and uses [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] chanyu turnover to make a long reign visible in geopolitical terms.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-381-1-hanwudi-ruhe-chengwei-xihan-zuichangshou-de-huangdi-lrrerer9sccohlnhy9kidsqjjnek|Hanji 381-1]] makes Han Wudi's longevity more than a curiosity. The episode says Wudi became the longest-lived ruler among fifteen Western Han emperors from [[LiuBang|刘邦]] to [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]], while the average life of Western Han emperors was under thirty-eight. It then converts that longevity into a frontier measure: Wudi outlasted six Xiongnu chanyu and saw the seventh, Hulugan Chanyu, come to power.

The concept is useful because it links biography, succession, and strategy. A ruler who lives and reigns unusually long can experience several leadership cycles in a rival polity, preserve policy continuity across decades, and accumulate both achievement and late-life burdens. Hanji 381-1 does not claim longevity alone explains Wudi's success; it uses lifespan as a lens for seeing how unusually long imperial time changes the scale of comparison.

## Key Claims

- Han Wudi's lifespan is presented as exceptional inside Western Han imperial history.
- Longevity is made politically concrete by counting the Xiongnu rulers Wudi outlasted.
- The chanyu sequence turns a biographical fact into a frontier-strategy time horizon.
- The comparison with Han Pingdi and the Western Han average highlights how fragile royal lifespan could be.
- The concept remains descriptive: it does not prove that longevity itself caused policy quality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source is a short podcast note, not a demographic study of Western Han emperors. Its lifespan average, Han Pingdi naming, and chanyu-counting sequence should remain source-scoped until corroborated by fuller chronological notes. Longevity is a visibility frame for Wudi's reign length, not a claim that he ruled better because he lived longer.
