# 检籍与特权户籍清理 / Household Registry Privilege Audit

## Definition

Household registry privilege audit is the [[SouthernQi|Southern Qi]] effort under [[XiaoZeSouthernQi|Xiao Ze]] to inspect household status, remove people falsely or corruptly entered into tax-privileged categories, and restore population and revenue to ordinary state obligations.

## Current Synthesis

The episode distinguishes this reform from the earlier problem of integrating northern migrants and southern residents through territorial registration. By Yongming, the central issue was the expansion of exemptions: officials and aristocratic households sheltered more people, while some non-privileged households used bribery or falsification to enter protected categories. Inspection sought to rebuild fiscal capacity by making formal status match the state's view of legitimate privilege.

Implementation converted classification into conflict. Fixed recovery targets encouraged officials and clerks to extract bribes or misclassify people, while households that lost exemptions experienced the reform as material dispossession. Tang Yuzhi's rebellion shows how a technically legible audit can destabilize rule when status, taxation, local discretion, and livelihood are fused.

## Key Claims

- Household registration can allocate tax and labor burdens as well as record population.
- Privileged status expands through elite sheltering, bribery, and document manipulation when exemptions have high value.
- Central audits can increase revenue while transferring costs onto households that lose recognized privilege.
- Quotas create incentives for local overreach and rent extraction when classification is discretionary.
- Reform legitimacy depends on procedure and distribution, not only the formal correction of false records.

## Evidence

- Changed problem definition - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] distinguishes earlier migrant integration from Yongming-era exemption expansion and false privilege.
- Audit mechanism - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] describes inspection and fixed official targets for removing improperly privileged registrations.
- Implementation failure - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] reports bribery and manipulation by clerks responsible for eligibility decisions.
- Political consequence - [[lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs-lvyohbnakaa_iop9nirokkjmhqs|Episode 513]] links loss of established advantages to Tang Yuzhi's rebellion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The supplied summary does not provide edicts, audit records, regional variation, household testimony, or a quantified distribution of who gained and lost. “False” registration is a state category that may conceal ambiguous customary claims. The causal path from inspection to rebellion and the relative roles of wealthy households, ordinary taxpayers, officials, and clerks require corroboration.

## What Changed

- Established registry inspection as a privilege-allocation and implementation problem, not merely a census technique.
- Separated central fiscal intent from quota-driven local incentives.
- Preserved Tang Yuzhi's rebellion as episode-attributed evidence of political backlash.
