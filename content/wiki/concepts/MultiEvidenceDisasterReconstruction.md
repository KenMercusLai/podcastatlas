# Multi-Evidence Disaster Reconstruction

## Definition

Multi-evidence disaster reconstruction combines literary testimony, archaeology, human and animal remains, objects, environmental traces, and physical science to infer how a catastrophe unfolded while retaining the limits of each evidence type.

## Current Synthesis

The Vesuvius episode derives its vividness from convergence without treating every detail as equally secure. Pliny's letters supply cloud shape, sensory experience, family action, darkness, and broad timing; deposits and ruins supply hazard sequence and structural effects; bodies and possessions make movement and social difference visible; seasonal food, clothing, wine activity, and an inscription challenge the transmitted date; volcanology explains column collapse, surge, and flow. The method is strongest when these sources constrain one another rather than being blended into an unmarked dramatic narrative.

## Key Claims

- Literary testimony can preserve sensory sequence and human response but may be retrospective, interested, transmitted imperfectly, and spatially limited.
- Archaeological context can recover behavior and timing only when objects, bodies, buildings, and deposits are interpreted together.
- Scientific hazard models can explain physical mechanisms without independently proving every narrated scene or motive.
- Conflicts among evidence types, such as the August date versus autumnal finds, are productive signals for qualification and revision.
- Representative individual traces can humanize catastrophe without licensing certainty about all identities, intentions, or movements.

## Evidence

- Literary contribution: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] uses Pliny the Younger's letters for the cloud, Misenum evacuation, darkness, and his uncle's rescue.
- Material contribution: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] cites buildings, victims, clothing, food, tools, sacred objects, shackles, and animal remains.
- Scientific contribution: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] organizes the event through pumice fall, surge, and column collapse.
- Evidentiary conflict: [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] sets the transmitted 24 August date against pomegranates, wine, heavy clothing, and a 17 October inscription.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Convergence can still produce an overconfident story if dating, provenance, terminology, representativeness, or literary shaping is ignored. The episode itself is a secondary narrative synthesis, so its victim total, exact chronology, particular escape stories, and causal interpretations require direct-source checking before being treated as settled scholarship.
