## Definition

Qiang official-abuse rebellion trigger is the frontier pattern where misconduct by a local official, followed by punitive pursuit and collective fear, turns a localized grievance into armed revolt. The trigger is not the same as the whole rebellion; it is the escalation point where official behavior destroys trust and expands the coalition.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 749 gives a sharp trigger sequence. A Han official abducts or takes a Qiang woman, the woman's husband kills the official, and the county head Zong Yan pursues the fleeing Qiang beyond the frontier. Other Qiang groups fear punishment, join together, and kill Zong Yan. Mi Wu and Buqiao then connect that panic to a broader attack on Longxi and Hanyang.

The source's governing lesson is that frontier rebellion can begin with a preventable administrative injury. Once state agents are seen as abusive and punitive, even groups not directly involved can decide that safety lies in collective resistance. Large imperial campaigns then become necessary to repair what local misconduct helped start.

This concept is narrower than general Qiang policy. It identifies the ignition mechanism before later military suppression, surrender, and pacification debates.

## Key Claims

- Local official abuse can become a strategic frontier problem when it converts personal injury into group fear.
- Punitive pursuit can escalate rather than restore order when communities expect collective punishment.
- Coalition leaders such as Mi Wu and Buqiao become more dangerous once state misconduct supplies a shared grievance.
- Military victory can end a revolt cycle without proving that the initial governance failure was repaired.
- The pattern should be kept distinct from later Qiang pacification methods that start after rebellion is already underway.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page does not claim that all Qiang unrest came from this one incident or that Mi Wu and Buqiao had no independent political motives. Hanji 749 provides one trigger and escalation path within a broader Qiang-frontier history. The woman, the initial official, Zong Yan, and Dian Wu remain source-scoped until further notes warrant standalone pages or disambiguation.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 749's official-abuse-to-rebellion sequence.
- Kept the trigger analytically separate from later campaign victory and pacification strategy.
