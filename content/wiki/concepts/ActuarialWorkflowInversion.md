# Actuarial Workflow Inversion

## Definition
Actuarial workflow inversion is a work-design goal in which automation reduces time spent acquiring, cleaning, moving, and routinely modeling data so actuaries can devote more time to interpretation, exploration, judgment, and business implications.

## Current Synthesis
The source's time pyramid is diagnostic rather than a claim that all preparation disappears. AI creates value when it removes bottlenecks while preserving the expertise needed to test inputs, evaluate outputs, generate alternatives, and accept professional responsibility.

## Key Claims
- Data preparation can consume most actuarial time and leave too little capacity for critical analysis.
- Automation should shift the balance toward interpretation and problem solving rather than merely increase report volume.
- Actuarial creativity includes exploring alternatives and judging consequences for customers, firms, and the industry.
- Generative averaging is not a substitute for novel reasoning or professionally accountable decisions.
- Productivity gains depend on trustworthy inputs, review, and workflow integration, not tool availability alone.

## Evidence
### Time allocation and bottlenecks
- [[ep-18-insurance-transformed-an-actuarys-take-on-ai]] reports Maddis's inverted-pyramid goal and Sam's estimate that data cleaning and processing can consume 70-80% of work time in his experience.

### Human contribution
- [[ep-18-insurance-transformed-an-actuarys-take-on-ai]] places creativity, iteration, interpretation, and implication analysis on the human side of the redesigned workflow.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode supplies no time study, deployment comparison, error rate, or measured productivity outcome; the 70-80% figure is experiential.
- Data preparation can itself require domain judgment, so automating it does not make validation optional.
- Faster throughput can create more review burden if systems generate low-quality or weakly grounded output.
