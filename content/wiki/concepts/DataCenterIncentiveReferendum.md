# Data Center Incentive Referendum

A data center incentive referendum is a local-vote mechanism requiring voter approval before certain large developer incentives can proceed. [[tech-20260410-0410-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260410-0410-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept through [[PortWashingtonWisconsin|Port Washington, Wisconsin]], where residents voted for a measure of this kind.

The concept narrows [[DataCenterBacklash]] from broad opposition to a governance demand. Voters may not be trying only to block construction; they may also be trying to control [[DataCenterTaxIncentives]], local benefit sharing, fiscal exposure, and accountability before large AI or cloud infrastructure receives public support.

## Key Claims
- Incentive referendums make data-center subsidy decisions visible to residents instead of leaving them only to developers and local officials.
- The mechanism can affect future projects without undoing projects that have already been approved or started, as described in the Port Washington segment.
- Referendums shift the negotiating frame from "should a data center be built?" to "who approves the public contribution and under what terms?"
- They can make [[DataCenterCostShifting]] and [[AIMetabolicInfrastructure]] politically legible before power, water, tax, or community impacts are fully locked in.
