# Food Value Context Assessment / 食品价值语境判断

## Definition
Food value context assessment is the practice of judging a food or supplement through the consumer, dose, complete formulation, realistic nutritional contribution, alternatives, price, and use context rather than labeling it universally healthy, harmful, or therapeutic.

## Current Synthesis
The source replaces binary food judgments with a sequence of practical questions. What nutrients does the product actually provide? Is the amount meaningful at a realistic serving? Does this person tolerate or need it? Are ordinary foods able to supply the same function? Does the price reflect useful value, convenience, taste, or mainly a premium story? Is a food being asked to do the work of diagnosis or treatment?

This makes nutritional value and consumer value related but distinct. Buffalo milk, high-protein milk, superfoods, bird's nest, or fish oil can contain real nutrients while still being unnecessary or poor value for a particular buyer. Pleasure, gifting, and convenience can remain legitimate reasons to buy, but they should not be misrepresented as clinical benefit. Balanced dietary variety remains the base because no single food repairs an otherwise inadequate pattern.

## Key Claims
- A useful nutrient does not make one product necessary, universally superior, or therapeutic.
- Dose and realistic serving size matter when translating an ingredient or experimental effect into an expected benefit.
- Population, tolerance, disease context, dietary gaps, and available alternatives change the same product's value.
- Price can include taste, convenience, scarcity, branding, or emotional value; those are distinct from nutritional superiority.
- Dietary balance and variety are more robust foundations than organizing a diet around one premium or “super” food.
- Existing disease or persistent symptoms should move the decision toward qualified care rather than a more ambitious food claim.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This framework does not imply that every product is nutritionally equivalent or that price never reflects quality, safety, production cost, or convenience. It also does not supply individualized dietary requirements. The source is conversational public education without a cited evidence review, so its numerical milk, calcium, vitamin, fish-oil, and supplement claims remain source-scoped and should be checked against current guidance and personal clinical context.

## What Changed
- Established a unified person-dose-formulation-alternative-price framework for food-value judgments.
- Separated nutritional benefit from convenience, pleasure, gifting, branding, and treatment claims.
