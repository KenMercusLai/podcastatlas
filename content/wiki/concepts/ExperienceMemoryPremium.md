## Definition

Experience-memory premium is the willingness to pay beyond direct material utility because participation, convenience, a scarce moment, or self-recording may produce a personally durable memory.

## Current Synthesis

[[fanwai-16-qingxu-jiazhi-jingjixue-huaqian-mai-kaixin-daodi-shi-bu-shi-yi-men-hao-shengyi-gkwriw4oo_7zase74qtnvvv8]] develops the commercial mechanism through a fireworks cruise, concerts, travel, live podcasting, and other time-bounded purchases. Buyers may be paying for a vantage point, reduced coordination, participation, and a story that cannot be reproduced exactly; that private value does not prove resale value or scalable business economics.

The family and self-documentation cases add further boundaries. A trip can be valued as a future family memory even when it may also produce fatigue or conflict, and a panoramic camera can let a person who usually records others appear in their own life archive. In both cases, a smaller trial or repeated use matters because imagined memory value is not guaranteed by payment.

## Key Claims

- Scarcity can be temporal: a familiar service becomes more valuable when joined to a hard-to-repeat occasion.
- Convenience and reduced coordination burden can be part of experience value rather than merely functional add-ons.
- Participation and self-recording can produce a more personal memory than possession alone.
- Memory value may include a person's ability to see themselves as present and worth documenting.
- The relevant return is subjective recollection, not necessarily financial appreciation or resale value.
- A compelling anticipated memory does not guarantee a harmonious event, durable satisfaction, repeat demand, or investable economics.

### Participation and personal memory

- [[e258-zhege-dongxi-wo-zhende-hao-xiang-yao-xiaojiuguan-gushihui-lmb4vjrqekll_dhzaff4gjtbewgn]] uses a nearby trip to test whether a family can travel together before committing to a longer, more expensive journey.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept rests on participant anecdotes, not willingness-to-pay or longitudinal memory research. An anticipated memory can become conflict, fatigue, disappointment, unused equipment, or marketing-induced urgency. The framework explains why an experience may matter; it does not establish affordability, fair pricing, operator margins, or that recording an event improves the event itself.

## What Changed

- Extended the premium from scarce events and convenience to family memory and the value of appearing in one's own life record.
- Added trials, conflict risk, and equipment non-use as boundaries on anticipated memory value.
