# Experience-Memory Premium / 体验记忆溢价

## Definition

Experience-memory premium is the willingness to pay above the direct material cost of an event or service because it provides convenience, participation, a scarce moment, and a personally owned memory.

## Current Synthesis

The source develops the mechanism through a Yokohama fireworks cruise, concerts, travel, a live podcast, and other “一期一会” purchases. The buyer is not merely paying for transport, food, a seat, or information. They are paying to avoid friction, occupy a better vantage point, participate in a bounded occasion, and retain a story that feels difficult to reproduce. The premium is real to the participant without proving resale value, universal appeal, or a scalable business.

## Key Claims

- Scarcity can be temporal: a familiar place or service becomes more valuable when joined to a one-time event.
- Convenience and reduced coordination burden can be part of emotional value rather than merely functional add-ons.
- Participation produces a more personal memory than possession alone, even when the experience leaves no tradable asset.
- The relevant return is subjective and durable recollection, not necessarily financial appreciation.
- A compelling event does not by itself prove repeat demand, standardized delivery, or investable economics.

## Evidence

- Time, place, and convenience - [[fanwai-16-qingxu-jiazhi-jingjixue-huaqian-mai-kaixin-daodi-shi-bu-shi-yi-men-hao-shengyi-gkwriw4oo_7zase74qtnvvv8]] describes a fireworks cruise whose value came from the deck view, food, drink, and avoiding the work of securing a crowded viewing place even though the boat did not sail offshore.
- Participation and memory - [[fanwai-16-qingxu-jiazhi-jingjixue-huaqian-mai-kaixin-daodi-shi-bu-shi-yi-men-hao-shengyi-gkwriw4oo_7zase74qtnvvv8]] groups concerts, live performances, travel, and attendance at the Chengdu recording as purchases of a personally meaningful occasion rather than of physical inventory.
- Investment boundary - [[fanwai-16-qingxu-jiazhi-jingjixue-huaqian-mai-kaixin-daodi-shi-bu-shi-yi-men-hao-shengyi-gkwriw4oo_7zase74qtnvvv8]] contrasts subjective satisfaction with the additional requirements of broad demand, replication, market size, and sustained operating performance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is derived from participant anecdotes, not willingness-to-pay research or longitudinal memory studies. Scarcity and convenience can also be manufactured marketing cues, and a remembered event can disappoint. The concept describes why a buyer may rationally value an experience; it does not show that every premium price is fair or that event operators have attractive margins.

## What Changed

- Created a bounded mechanism separating the value of a remembered occasion from product cost and investment return.
