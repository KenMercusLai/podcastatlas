# Direct-Teacher Edtech Pilot

A direct-teacher edtech pilot is a lower-friction path for individual teachers or departments to try a school product before a full district sale. In [[advice-line-strategy-sessions-4962a0d6-36bc-4797-8002-3bd8b748a89a]], [[SandyDininger]] wants [[BrainBuffs]] to offer affordable teacher access while preserving higher-value district contracts that can take six months to two years.

The advice is to use teacher access as evidence creation, not as a permanent cheaper duplicate of the district product. [[GuyRaz]] suggests freemium or free access for a few influential math teachers in exchange for outcome data, while [[ShaziVisram]] argues that teacher and district offerings should be differentiated so districts do not see the teacher price as the true price for the same thing.

## Key Claims
- Teacher-led access can shorten learning cycles in markets where district procurement is slow.
- Low price is less damaging when the low-tier product is clearly scoped differently from the district package.
- A small group of credible teachers can create feedback, case studies, and before-after outcome data.
- Teacher pilots should connect to a district sales story through evidence, referrals, and differentiated administrative value.
