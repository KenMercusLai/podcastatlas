# Bullying Prevention Through Emotional Skills

## Definition
Bullying prevention through emotional skills is the source's claim that rules and punishment are insufficient unless a school also teaches people to recognize repeated harm, power imbalance, emotional cues, perspective, empathy, intervention choices, and regulation.

## Current Synthesis
The episode defines bullying through repeated behavior, intent to harm, and a power imbalance. Brackett's childhood account adds the victim-side emotional sequence of fear, shame, and despair, especially when adults do not intervene. Prevention therefore requires more than telling a targeted child to toughen up or treating visible behavior as the whole emotional story.

The proposed school response combines clear rules and adult responsibility with practice: role play, perspective-taking, emotion perception, empathy, and regulation. These skills may help students and adults detect and interrupt harm, but they do not relocate responsibility from aggressors and institutions onto targets.

## Key Claims
- Repetition, intent to harm, and power imbalance distinguish bullying from every isolated conflict.
- Adult nonintervention can deepen fear, shame, despair, and institutional mistrust.
- Visible anger, withdrawal, or misbehavior may conceal victimization and should prompt inquiry before discipline.
- Rules need trained perception, empathy, perspective-taking, role play, and regulation to become usable prevention.
- Emotional skills supplement rather than replace safeguarding, evidence, reporting, accountability, and protection.

## Evidence
- Definition - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] defines bullying as repeated intentional harm within a power imbalance.
- Lived impact - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] connects Brackett's bullying experience to fear, shame, despair, and failed adult intervention.
- Behavior-reading problem - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] warns that stomping or anger-like behavior can reflect sadness, shame, fear, or bullying.
- Prevention practices - [[how-to-increase-your-emotional-intelligence-dr-marc-brackett-scim6059113217]] proposes empathy, perspective-taking, role play, emotional perception, and regulation alongside rules.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not present comparative program evaluations or establish that emotional-skills instruction alone reduces bullying. Definitions, reporting duties, and remedies vary by setting and jurisdiction. An emotional-intelligence frame must not psychologize away coercion, demand empathy from targets, or substitute classroom exercises for immediate safety and institutional accountability.
