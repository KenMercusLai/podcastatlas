# Social-Emotional Learning / 社会情感学习

## Definition
Social-emotional learning / 社会情感学习 (SEL) is the source's educational field for learning to understand and manage oneself, relate to others and resolve conflict, and adapt to the wider world through critical thinking and responsible decisions.

## Current Synthesis
The source resists reducing SEL to emotional calm or compliant behavior. It joins emotional recognition and regulation with self-knowledge, empathy, communication, conflict handling, critical interpretation, decision-making, and a positive orientation toward action. The desired outcome is summarized as “内心丰盈，积极行动.”

SEL is learned differently from a fact. Stories can make values intelligible, but skills and attitudes require repeated rehearsal across scenes, feedback, and real application. This makes curriculum design and parent-facing evidence part of the educational problem: families need to see what is being learned even when results do not arrive as a test score.

## Key Claims
- SEL covers three connected fields: the self, relationships with others, and responsible adaptation to the world.
- Emotional stability is one possible result, not a complete definition of social-emotional capability.
- Feelings should be accepted and interpreted before regulation techniques are expected to work.
- Observation, interpretation, alternative explanations, evidence, and belief updating make critical thinking a social-emotional practice as well as a cognitive one.
- Skill and attitude formation require repeated scenario practice rather than one-time explanation.
- Parent understanding and opportunity cost are adoption constraints because traditional subjects offer more familiar feedback.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode is a founder interview rather than an independent curriculum evaluation, controlled outcome study, or comprehensive review of SEL research.
- Lower tantrum frequency, shorter duration, and better recognition are reported as parent feedback, not population-level efficacy estimates.
- Accepting an emotion does not mean permitting harmful behavior; the synthesis keeps feeling, interpretation, and action distinct.
- SEL should not be treated as a substitute for safeguarding, clinical mental-health care, learning support, or subject knowledge when those are needed.

## What Changed
- Created a synthesis that defines SEL beyond emotional stability.
- Established repeated practice and parent-legible evidence as part of the implementation problem.
- Connected social-emotional learning to AI-era capability allocation without treating it as a replacement for academic foundations.
