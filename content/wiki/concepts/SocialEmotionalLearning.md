# Social-Emotional Learning / 社会情感学习

## Definition
Social-emotional learning / 社会情感学习 (SEL) is the source's educational field for learning to understand and manage oneself, relate to others and resolve conflict, and adapt to the wider world through critical thinking and responsible decisions.

## Current Synthesis
The source resists reducing SEL to emotional calm or compliant behavior. It joins emotional recognition and regulation with self-knowledge, empathy, communication, conflict handling, critical interpretation, decision-making, and a positive orientation toward action. The desired outcome is summarized as “内心丰盈，积极行动.”

SEL is learned differently from a fact. Stories can make values intelligible, but skills and attitudes require repeated rehearsal across scenes, feedback, and real application. The rural-school source shows that access is also an organizational problem: donated AI psychology devices may sit idle when students lack time, specialist teachers are scarce, and examinable subjects displace the course. Training ordinary subject teachers, giving them incentives, and supporting their own strained working lives can make delivery more durable while improving both student expression and teacher connection.

## Key Claims
- SEL covers three connected fields: the self, relationships with others, and responsible adaptation to the world.
- Emotional stability is one possible result, not a complete definition of social-emotional capability.
- Feelings should be accepted and interpreted before regulation techniques are expected to work.
- Observation, interpretation, alternative explanations, evidence, and belief updating make critical thinking a social-emotional practice as well as a cognitive one.
- Skill and attitude formation require repeated scenario practice rather than one-time explanation.
- Parent understanding and opportunity cost are adoption constraints because traditional subjects offer more familiar feedback.
- Devices do not create SEL capacity by themselves; teacher training, timetable priority, incentives, and relational continuity determine use.

- Critical-thinking method: [[ai-shidai-women-daodi-gai-xue-shenme-duitan-yuhong-sanzhong-buhui-guoshi-de-nengli-lkcmj80qyw-uduhijfxudufqboaf]] separates what happened from what a person inferred when a friend did not greet them or removed them online.
- Practice design: [[ai-shidai-women-daodi-gai-xue-shenme-duitan-yuhong-sanzhong-buhui-guoshi-de-nengli-lkcmj80qyw-uduhijfxudufqboaf]] describes [[TuDongdong|兔咚咚]] using stories, scenarios, tasks, and repeated breathing practice.

- Rural implementation: [[e253-ai-hui-gaibian-shijie-ke-ta-zhende-you-rang-shijie-bianhao-ma-lot2elu49vlmweanppqh5uz0xa1h]] contrasts underused donated AI psychology equipment with a model that trains and supports ordinary teachers to run social-emotional classes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The episode is a founder interview rather than an independent curriculum evaluation, controlled outcome study, or comprehensive review of SEL research.
- Lower tantrum frequency, shorter duration, and better recognition are reported as parent feedback, not population-level efficacy estimates.
- Accepting an emotion does not mean permitting harmful behavior; the synthesis keeps feeling, interpretation, and action distinct.
- SEL should not be treated as a substitute for safeguarding, clinical mental-health care, learning support, or subject knowledge when those are needed.
- The rural-school episode reports practitioner observations rather than a controlled evaluation and does not establish comparative efficacy between teacher-led lessons and AI devices.

## What Changed
- Added timetable priority, teacher scarcity, incentives, and staff wellbeing as implementation constraints.
- Distinguished donated equipment from durable school capacity.
- Added teacher-student relational feedback as a possible benefit that requires independent evaluation.
