# Indirect Sexual-Infection Exposure Risk / 间接性传播感染风险

## Definition
Indirect sexual-infection exposure risk is a framework for judging whether a shared object or public environment could plausibly transmit an infection by examining the source, route, organism viability, timing, infectious dose, contact site, and host susceptibility rather than equating contact with infection.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.82 separates the main route of sexually transmitted infection from uncommon indirect routes. A theoretical route through an instrument, paper product, towel, bed, toilet, bath, or other surface becomes clinically meaningful only when a pathogen reaches a suitable site while still viable and in sufficient quantity. Drying, elapsed time, cleaning, intact skin, and lack of direct mucosal contact can reduce risk, while poor sterilization, fresh contamination, broken skin, or substantial exposure can change it.

The framework also prevents tunnel vision. A hotel environment may be more plausibly associated with irritant dermatitis, bites, fungi, or ordinary skin inflammation than with an STI. Proportionate measures such as choosing reputable providers, expecting instrument sterilization, avoiding visibly contaminated items, using personal hygiene supplies when practical, and seeking care for actual symptoms address risk without treating every public surface as evidence of sexual infection.

## Key Claims
- Exposure possibility and transmission probability are different judgments.
- Indirect transmission requires a compatible source, viable route, sufficient dose and timing, and a susceptible contact site.
- Organisms differ in environmental survival, so one rule cannot be generalized across all STIs or surfaces.
- Proper instrument processing and environmental cleaning materially alter risk.
- Common dermatologic, irritant, allergic, fungal, or bite-related explanations should remain visible when symptoms follow travel or public-facility use.
- Symptoms or a credible exposure history should guide testing and care more than retrospective speculation about any shared object.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode's organism-specific survival times, infectious-dose descriptions, and transmission percentages are unsourced within the supplied summary and should not be treated as universal laboratory or clinical thresholds. Risk varies by pathogen, surface, moisture, temperature, cleaning, exposure site, and host factors. A low-probability route is not an impossible route, and an STI diagnosis cannot be assigned or excluded from an environmental story alone.

## What Changed
- Created a transmission-chain framework that separates theoretical possibility from contextual probability.
- Made ordinary dermatologic and environmental risks explicit as competing explanations.
