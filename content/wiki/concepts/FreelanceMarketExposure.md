# Freelance Market Exposure / 自由职业市场暴露

Freelance market exposure is the risk shift in [[ep257-bu-shangban-yi-chang-taoli-yu-xunzhao-de-fuza-maoxian-li-wflyd48dmxvgersphxfp-gnng]] where leaving an organization gives a worker more control but also exposes the individual directly to market pricing, customer acquisition, delivery, collections, after-sales work, and schedule design. The episode uses [[MiaFreelancer|Mia]] and other freelancers to show that "没有老板" does not mean "没有压力."

The concept qualifies [[SelfDirectedWork]]. A freelancer may recognize the project as their own and gain more voice over professional ethics or execution, but the whole chain that a company used to absorb becomes personal work: finding clients, demonstrating capability, judging whether the client can pay, fronting money, collecting receivables, maintaining reputation, and building external clocks to keep the day from dissolving.

## Key Claims
- Freelance autonomy increases control over some work choices while increasing exposure to customer, cash-flow, and self-management risk.
- A company can be oppressive and still provide infrastructure: leads, credibility, payment process, accounting, legal routines, teammates, and a clock.
- Personal branding and friendship networks can be early client channels, but they also make reputation, self-display, and selling part of the work.
- Freelancers may reproduce employment pressure internally by using former salary, always-on availability, or customer praise as self-worth measures.
- The source treats successful freelancers as a survivorship-biased subset, not as a universal replacement for employment.
