## Definition

谶纬证据批判 / Chenwei evidentiary critique is the practice of challenging prophetic or apocryphal texts by asking whether they deserve court authority. The current evidence has two forms: [[HuanTanEasternHan|桓谭]] rejects chenwei as non-classical and morally dangerous under [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]], while [[ZhangHengLateHan|张衡]] later tests tuchen materials through chronology and catalog evidence.

## Current Synthesis

The concept is narrower than [[ChenweiPolitics|谶纬政治]]. Chenwei politics asks how prophecy and apocrypha operate as political technology; chenwei evidentiary critique asks how an insider can challenge that technology using standards of classical authority, transmission, and practical deception risk. Hanji 731 shows the earliest current form through Huan Tan. His argument does not present a modern secular rejection of all sign discourse; it says forged prophetic books lure people away from the Five Classics and righteous governance, then becomes dangerous because Liu Xiu treats chenwei as part of his accession legitimacy.

Hanji 816 gives the more technical source-critical form through Zhang Heng. Zhang does not merely say prophecy feels superstitious. He points to anachronistic combinations of Warring States people and Han place names, then invokes the absence of the texts from earlier bibliographic work. The same source keeps the boundary subtle because Zhang still uses earthquake interpretation as political warning. The critique is therefore internal to omen-aware court culture, not a blanket rejection of every Heaven-sign argument.

## Key Claims

- Prophetic texts can be challenged by classical authority and moral-political risk before they are challenged by technical source criticism.
- Once chenwei is tied to imperial legitimacy, evidence critique can become a personal danger for the critic.
- Prophetic texts can be challenged by internal chronology, not only by disbelief.
- Catalog absence matters because a claimed ancient text needs a transmission history.
- Critique can arise inside omen-aware court culture rather than outside it.
- The target is uncontrolled textual authority that can deceive, extract money, or pollute classical learning.
- The concept should be used only where a source gives a test of textual authority, not for every general anti-superstition remark.

## Evidence

- Classical-authority challenge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-731-jinhu-wanmei-de-liuxiu-zui-da-quedian-shi-ta-lrhwupjmjm-hch3usx26jfxkhoma|Hanji 731]] says Huan Tan urges Liu Xiu to abandon forged prophetic books and follow the Five Classics and proper governance.
- Court-risk boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-731-jinhu-wanmei-de-liuxiu-zui-da-quedian-shi-ta-lrhwupjmjm-hch3usx26jfxkhoma|Hanji 731]] says Huan Tan nearly dies after saying chenwei books are not classics, showing that critique can be heard as an attack on the ruler's legitimacy frame.
- Anachronism test: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-816-xiangyao-naxia-nide-lingdao-qishi-hen-jiandan-lva3y4hgu7kn1z4jmhy6jn4-oyvc|Hanji 816]] says Zhang Heng criticizes tuchen content that mixes Warring States figures with Han-era place names.
- Catalog test: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-816-xiangyao-naxia-nide-lingdao-qishi-hen-jiandan-lva3y4hgu7kn1z4jmhy6jn4-oyvc|Hanji 816]] says he notes that Liu Xiang and Liu Xin did not find such books when organizing texts.
- Court remedy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-816-xiangyao-naxia-nide-lingdao-qishi-hen-jiandan-lva3y4hgu7kn1z4jmhy6jn4-oyvc|Hanji 816]] says Zhang Heng recommends collecting and banning the false tuchen materials.
- Boundary with omen warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-816-xiangyao-naxia-nide-lingdao-qishi-hen-jiandan-lva3y4hgu7kn1z4jmhy6jn4-oyvc|Hanji 816]] also shows Zhang Heng using earthquake symbolism for political warning, so the critique targets specific textual authority rather than every sign-based claim.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 816 and Hanji 731. It does not settle the full history of chenwei texts or their dating. Huan Tan's case gives a canonical and moral critique more than a detailed philological test; Zhang Heng's case gives chronology and catalog tests but still uses disaster interpretation. The concept should therefore not be flattened into modern rationalism or generalized anti-religion.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 731 as the earlier Guangwu-era Huan Tan layer.
- Distinguished Huan Tan's classical-authority critique from Zhang Heng's later chronology and catalog tests.
- Added court-risk qualification where chenwei critique threatens a ruler's legitimacy framework.
