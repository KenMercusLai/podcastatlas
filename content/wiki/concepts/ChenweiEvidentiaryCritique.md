## Definition

谶纬证据批判 / Chenwei evidentiary critique is the practice of challenging prophetic or apocryphal texts by testing their internal coherence, chronology, and documentary pedigree. In Hanji 816, [[ZhangHengLateHan|张衡]] argues that tuchen materials mix incompatible references and lack early catalog support, so they should not carry court authority.

## Current Synthesis

The concept is narrower than [[ChenweiPolitics|谶纬政治]]. Chenwei politics asks how prophecy and apocrypha operate as political technology; chenwei evidentiary critique asks how an insider can challenge that technology using evidence standards. Zhang Heng's argument does not merely say prophecy feels superstitious. He points to anachronistic combinations of Warring States people and Han place names, then invokes the absence of the texts from earlier bibliographic work.

The source also keeps the boundary subtle. Zhang Heng still uses earthquake interpretation as political warning, so the critique is not a modern secular rejection of all sign discourse. It is an internal distinction between disaster/omen reasoning that a court official can deploy and prophetic books that fail evidentiary checks.

## Key Claims

- Prophetic texts can be challenged by internal chronology, not only by disbelief.
- Catalog absence matters because a claimed ancient text needs a transmission history.
- Critique can arise inside omen-aware court culture rather than outside it.
- The target is uncontrolled textual authority that can deceive, extract money, or pollute classical learning.
- The concept should be used only where a source gives evidence tests, not for every general anti-superstition remark.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 816. It does not settle the full history of chenwei texts or their dating; it records Zhang Heng's source-scoped argument as represented in the episode. Because the same source also preserves disaster interpretation, the concept should not be flattened into modern rationalism or generalized anti-religion.
