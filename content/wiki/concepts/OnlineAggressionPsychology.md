# Online Aggression Psychology / 网络攻击心理机制

## Definition
Online aggression psychology is a bounded account of how platform distance, reduced accountability, emotional displacement, self-worth threats, recognition needs, rigid certainty, and limited perspective-taking may contribute to hostile online expression.

## Current Synthesis
The current source treats online aggression as multiply determined rather than as evidence of one personality type. Anonymity and pseudonyms can reduce inhibition; an account can replace a concrete person; and offline frustration may be redirected into a setting where immediate consequences feel weak. Insecurity, loneliness, unstable self-worth, and a wish for attention or superiority can then make attack feel temporarily compensatory.

The mechanism also has a justificatory layer. A commenter may construe attack as correction, justice, expertise, or defense of standards, allowing harmful conduct to feel principled. The durable distinction is therefore behavioral: disagreement and good-faith correction remain legitimate, while humiliation, personal attack, threat, rumor, discrimination, and coercive pile-ons create harm regardless of the attacker's private motive.

## Key Claims
- Online aggression can arise from several interacting situational, emotional, and interpersonal mechanisms rather than one stable trait.
- Anonymity and abstraction can lower restraint by making responsibility and the target's personhood less salient.
- Insecurity, low self-worth, loneliness, displaced frustration, or recognition seeking may make derogation feel compensatory.
- Rigid certainty and rationalization can recast harm as justice, expertise, or necessary correction.
- Understanding a possible mechanism neither excuses the conduct nor establishes a clinical diagnosis.
- Persistent hostile engagement can harm targets, narrow public expression, and reinforce the aggressor's own dysregulation.

## Evidence
- Situational inhibition and abstraction - [[vol-174-ni-ye-bei-jianpanxia-qi-daoguo-ma-jingshen-xinlike-yisheng-jiao-ni-ruhe-yingdui-lpt3ebh5z2bn-fkztfie3koxwjbi]] links pseudonymity, distance, and reduced perception of the target as a concrete person to weaker restraint.
- Compensatory and relational mechanisms - [[vol-174-ni-ye-bei-jianpanxia-qi-daoguo-ma-jingshen-xinlike-yisheng-jiao-ni-ruhe-yingdui-lpt3ebh5z2bn-fkztfie3koxwjbi]] discusses displaced aggression, insecurity, self-worth, recognition, loneliness, attention, and superiority needs as possible contributors.
- Justification and empathy - [[vol-174-ni-ye-bei-jianpanxia-qi-daoguo-ma-jingshen-xinlike-yisheng-jiao-ni-ruhe-yingdui-lpt3ebh5z2bn-fkztfie3koxwjbi]] connects limited perspective-taking and rationalization with attacks framed as moral or professional correction.
- Harm and feedback - [[vol-174-ni-ye-bei-jianpanxia-qi-daoguo-ma-jingshen-xinlike-yisheng-jiao-ni-ruhe-yingdui-lpt3ebh5z2bn-fkztfie3koxwjbi]] describes injury to targets, deterrence of public education, and continued emotional dysregulation among habitual aggressors.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short public-education conversation, not a systematic review or assessment of identified commenters. Its references to paranoid, histrionic, and antisocial characteristics describe possible features, not diagnoses; the source does not establish prevalence, causal direction, or whether any one mechanism explains a particular attack. The informal “网络孤独症” phrase should not be treated as a clinical category, and psychological explanation does not reduce legal or ethical accountability.

## What Changed
- Established a multi-mechanism model that separates situational disinhibition, compensatory needs, and justificatory framing.
- Made the behavior-versus-diagnosis boundary explicit.
- Preserved accountability while allowing motive-sensitive interpretation.
