## Definition

后宫灾异归咎转移 / consort omen blame transfer is the pattern where disaster or celestial-omen pressure that could implicate outer relatives, ministers, or the ruler is redirected toward harem favoritism, jealousy, childlessness, fertility access, or succession anxiety.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 505 creates the concept through the eclipse and earthquake sequence after the Chang'an flood-rumor case. The source says court opinion tends to see Wang-family outer-relative power as the real political problem, but [[DuQinWesternHan|杜钦]] and [[GuYongWesternHan|谷永]] answer [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s omen inquiry by making harem disorder and favored women the core explanation. This redirects disaster pressure away from [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]]'s side and toward [[EmpressXuWesternHan|许皇后]] and Chengdi's no-heir problem.

Hanji 506 adds a more direct White Tiger Hall variant. When many frank speakers blame anomalies on Wang Feng because Chengdi has entrusted affairs to him, Gu Yong argues instead that the real issue is harem fertility: Chengdi should broaden favor, not object to women by beauty, age, body, or prior marriage, and prioritize the production of an heir. The source then explains the hidden political use of that answer: Wang Feng had presented to Chengdi a previously married woman, so Gu Yong's fertility-first memorial converts a propriety controversy into a dynastic-survival argument.

The concept is related to [[DisasterOmenCrisisRedirection|灾异压力转移]], but more gendered and palace-specific. It accepts the omen frame, then changes the responsible object: instead of "outer-relative power is too strong," the answer becomes "women's favor, jealousy, monopoly over the ruler, or insufficiently broad fertility access endangers succession." The sources read Gu Yong's move as strategic attachment to Wang Feng after Xu-family decline, not as neutral omen interpretation.

## Key Claims

- Disaster interpretation can shift political pressure from a powerful court bloc to a more vulnerable harem target.
- Childlessness and heir anxiety make consort blame especially available because private favor can be linked to public succession failure.
- Fertility urgency can be used to override propriety objections, including objections to a woman's previous marriage.
- The same omen can carry different political meanings depending on who benefits from the explanation.
- Consort-focused blame can coexist with real palace problems while still functioning as pressure transfer.
- The maneuver gains force when the target family is already losing influence and a stronger patron bloc is rising.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on Hanji 505 and Hanji 506 and should remain narrower than the full [[GenderedConsortScapegoating|红颜祸水式替罪叙事]] pattern. It records a specific omen-interpretation maneuver, not every case where women are blamed for political failure. The sources also do not prove that Du Qin or Gu Yong's stated interpretation was wholly insincere; they record the political effect and the host's reading of alignment.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 506's White Tiger Hall branch, where Gu Yong uses harem fertility to redirect explicit Wang Feng blame and defend a married-woman palace controversy.
- Preserved Hanji 505's eclipse-earthquake branch as the concept's original empress/heir-risk form.
