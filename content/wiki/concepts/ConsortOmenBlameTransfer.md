## Definition

后宫灾异归咎转移 / consort omen blame transfer is the pattern where disaster or celestial-omen pressure that could implicate outer relatives, ministers, or the ruler is redirected toward harem favoritism, jealousy, childlessness, or succession anxiety.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 505 creates the concept through the eclipse and earthquake sequence after the Chang'an flood-rumor case. The source says court opinion tends to see Wang-family outer-relative power as the real political problem, but [[DuQinWesternHan|杜钦]] and [[GuYongWesternHan|谷永]] answer [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s omen inquiry by making harem disorder and favored women the core explanation. This redirects disaster pressure away from [[WangFengWesternHan|王凤]]'s side and toward [[EmpressXuWesternHan|许皇后]] and Chengdi's no-heir problem.

The concept is related to [[DisasterOmenCrisisRedirection|灾异压力转移]], but more gendered and palace-specific. It accepts the omen frame, then changes the responsible object: instead of "outer-relative power is too strong," the answer becomes "women's favor, jealousy, or monopoly over the ruler endangers succession." The source reads Gu Yong's move as strategic attachment to Wang Feng after Xu-family decline, not as neutral omen interpretation.

## Key Claims

- Disaster interpretation can shift political pressure from a powerful court bloc to a more vulnerable harem target.
- Childlessness and heir anxiety make consort blame especially available because private favor can be linked to public succession failure.
- The same omen can carry different political meanings depending on who benefits from the explanation.
- Consort-focused blame can coexist with real palace problems while still functioning as pressure transfer.
- The maneuver gains force when the target family is already losing influence and a stronger patron bloc is rising.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on Hanji 505 and should remain narrower than the full [[GenderedConsortScapegoating|红颜祸水式替罪叙事]] pattern. It records a specific omen-interpretation maneuver, not every case where women are blamed for political failure. The source also does not prove that Du Qin or Gu Yong's stated interpretation was wholly insincere; it records the political effect and the host's reading of alignment.
