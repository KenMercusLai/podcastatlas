## Definition
Persuasion as mutual transformation is a conversational model in which participants listen across different standpoints, revise at least some of their own understanding, discover shared ground, and identify continuing disagreement without requiring a single intellectual victor.

## Current Synthesis
The concept rejects a one-way picture in which the enlightened speaker moves a mistaken listener toward a preselected correct position. Political persuasion instead broadens judgment by bringing several perspectives into view. Success can mean more accurate understanding, partial adjustment, a clearer map of disagreement, and preservation of the relationship or shared world even when neither participant converts.

## Key Claims
- Persuasion begins with the possibility that each participant may need to change.
- Listening is epistemic work because another standpoint can disclose aspects of a common world one person cannot see alone.
- Shared ground and continuing disagreement can both be valid outcomes.
- Impartial judgment is approached by considering more standpoints, not by pretending to have no standpoint.
- Relationship preservation can be part of political success without requiring politeness, passivity, or agreement.
- One-way correction risks reproducing the hierarchy between authoritative truth and disqualified opinion.

## Evidence
### Reciprocal revision
- [[zuozhe-duitan-pengyou-shifou-bi-zhenli-gengjia-zhongyao-roger-berkowitz-ln7i59hzhpigtixkj-dvwmkqrrsf]] describes persuasion, through Berkowitz's use of Linda Zerilli, as conversation in which each participant changes somewhat.

### Enlarged judgment
- [[zuozhe-duitan-pengyou-shifou-bi-zhenli-gengjia-zhongyao-roger-berkowitz-ln7i59hzhpigtixkj-dvwmkqrrsf]] links impartiality to considering as many standpoints as possible rather than choosing between pure subjectivism and objectivism.

### Relationship-preserving outcome
- [[zuozhe-duitan-pengyou-shifou-bi-zhenli-gengjia-zhongyao-roger-berkowitz-ln7i59hzhpigtixkj-dvwmkqrrsf]] treats a maintained common world, clearer agreement, and clearer disagreement as political achievements even without conversion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Mutuality is not appropriate when one party cannot speak freely, is exposed to abuse, or faces an immediate safety threat.
- Reciprocal openness does not require symmetrical evidence standards or compromise over established facts.
- The episode offers a philosophical model and examples, not a measured protocol with demonstrated effect sizes.
- The concept complements rather than replaces strategic persuasion methods designed for elections, negotiation, or public argument.
