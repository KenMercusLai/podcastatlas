# Psychological Abuse Recognition / 精神虐待识别

Psychological abuse recognition / 精神虐待识别 is the source's frame for naming intimidation, verbal terror, shame, and reality distortion as abuse even when the victim cannot point to a clear physical assault. In [[84-binglu-kuanghua-hao-guniang-ni-zhishi-wangle-yonggan-699443676]], [[AmyButcher|Amy Butcher / 艾米]] fears that Dave may kill her, but still struggles to accept that what she is living through counts as abuse.

[[ep273-npc-haishi-npd-dou-bie-xiang-nazou-women-rensheng-de-yaokongqi-lqqnd-e29xvhx2mtf6uuzx13wwvf]] adds a diagnosis-aware NPD discourse version. [[GongXueping|龚学平]] argues that people often say "NPD" when trying to name being used, controlled, ignored, or emotionally drained, but she keeps the clinical term separate from casual labeling. The recognition threshold here is not a visible injury; it is the repeated loss of [[SelfFeelingTrust|trust in one's own discomfort]] and judgment.

The episode makes professional witnessing important. Amy's therapist tells her she is experiencing abuse, and the source treats that naming as part of her exit route. Dave's objection that the therapist has only heard Amy's side is itself part of the recognition problem: the victim is pushed back into proving, explaining, and doubting her own fear.

This concept is narrower than [[ComplexTraumaRecognition]] and more specific than [[ProtectionAsControl]]. It focuses on the threshold moment when nonphysical harm becomes legible enough that a person can stop treating fear, shaking, secrecy, and self-doubt as private overreaction.

## Key Claims
- Abuse can operate through shouting, intimidation, humiliation, religious shame, and reality manipulation without leaving visible injury.
- A victim may be highly educated, feminist, professionally competent, and still unable to see the relationship clearly from inside it.
- A trusted outside witness can help separate fear-based self-doubt from the abusive partner's account of reality.
- Recognition is not instant liberation; it often arrives before the person has the emotional, practical, or relational capacity to leave.
- Popular diagnostic language can help people name harm, but it still needs observation, boundaries, and sometimes professional support rather than becoming a universal insult.
