# Psychological Abuse Recognition / 精神虐待识别

## Definition
Psychological abuse recognition is the threshold practice of naming intimidation, humiliation, shame, reality distortion, and coercive control as harmful even when there is no clear physical assault.

## Current Synthesis
Across the current sources, recognition is hard because psychological harm often arrives through relationship roles that already claim legitimacy: romantic love, family care, expertise, workplace evaluation, or education. The victim may be capable, educated, competent, or high-performing and still struggle to accept that fear, shaking, secrecy, depletion, or self-doubt count as evidence.

VOL.214 expands the concept from intimate and diagnostic-label contexts into schools and workplaces. Humiliation by teachers, parent harassment of teachers, workplace PUA, boss surveillance, and coercive overwork all show that nonphysical harm becomes recognizable only when behavior, power, and effects are examined together.

## Key Claims
- Abuse can operate through shouting, intimidation, humiliation, religious shame, reality manipulation, or repeated evaluative pressure without leaving visible injury.
- Recognition often depends on trusting fear, discomfort, self-doubt, or depletion as evidence without treating those feelings as automatically complete proof.
- A trusted outside witness, therapist, clinician, friend, or institutional record can help separate the victim's reality from the abuser's explanation.
- Popular diagnostic or self-help language can help name harm, but it should not replace observation of repeated effects, power, and behavior.
- Competence does not immunize a person against coercive relationships; high achievement can even intensify self-blame when abuse is framed as performance correction.
- Recognition is an early step, not a full exit plan; safety, resources, evidence, jurisdiction, and professional support still matter.

## Evidence
- Intimate-abuse threshold: [[84-binglu-kuanghua-hao-guniang-ni-zhishi-wangle-yonggan-699443676]] treats Amy Butcher's fear, intimidation, religious shame, and reality distortion as abuse even before a simple physical-violence account is available.
- Diagnostic-language boundary: [[ep273-npc-haishi-npd-dou-bie-xiang-nazou-women-rensheng-de-yaokongqi-lqqnd-e29xvhx2mtf6uuzx13wwvf]] treats popular NPD language as a way people name being used, controlled, ignored, or emotionally drained while still keeping clinical diagnosis bounded.
- Workplace and school extension: [[vol-214-weishenme-yue-youxiu-de-ren-yue-rongyi-zai-zhichang-bei-pua-dao-huaiyi-ziji-cong-tiequan-jiaoyu-liaodao-tiequan-zhichang-lmx4ewrw7kvcqpyh-q3bur35ybgz]] adds teacher-student humiliation, parent-teacher harassment, workplace PUA, and supervisor pressure as nonphysical harm patterns that need power-aware recognition.
- Witness and support: [[84-binglu-kuanghua-hao-guniang-ni-zhishi-wangle-yonggan-699443676]], [[ep273-npc-haishi-npd-dou-bie-xiang-nazou-women-rensheng-de-yaokongqi-lqqnd-e29xvhx2mtf6uuzx13wwvf]], and [[vol-214-weishenme-yue-youxiu-de-ren-yue-rongyi-zai-zhichang-bei-pua-dao-huaiyi-ziji-cong-tiequan-jiaoyu-liaodao-tiequan-zhichang-lmx4ewrw7kvcqpyh-q3bur35ybgz]] all make outside support important, whether through therapy, self-worth recovery, talking with others, or institutional complaint.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Recognition is not the same as diagnosis, proof, or legal finding. The concept should not flatten every painful relationship, harsh manager, parental disagreement, or teacher criticism into abuse. Its useful boundary is repeated nonphysical harm plus coercive effect, especially when power imbalance makes self-defense costly.
