## Definition

任官适任性国家能力 / Appointment Quality as State Capacity is the governance pattern where the state succeeds or fails according to whether the right person, with the right method and authority, is placed into the right office at the right moment.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 779 adds an early An-era layer before the Shun and Huan cases. [[FanZhunLateHan|Fan Zhun]]'s education memorial shows appointment quality upstream: a ruler's future capacity depends on whether learned and upright instructors can be found before court learning decays. [[LuGongLateHan|Lu Gong]] shows procedural fit: a senior minister can save labor and lives by aligning litigation timing with agriculture and death-penalty review. [[RenShangLateHan|Ren Shang]] supplies the negative frontier case: a famous commander can be the wrong appointment when a role requires patience, local knowledge, and indirect governance after [[BanChaoEasternHan|Ban Chao]].

The Shun-era sequence then turns appointment quality into a formal selection problem. Hanji 811-812 use [[ZuoXiongLateHan|Zuo Xiong]] to tie local tenure, inspection truth, discipline, grassroots staffing, Xiaolian age thresholds, skill testing, and exception proof to state capacity. Hanji 815 adds [[ZhangHengLateHan|Zhang Heng]]'s warning that criteria can drift away from the office's original purpose and that mass removals can waste capacity.

The later cases show that office fit is crisis-specific. Hanji 819 replaces a 40,000-soldier Jiaozhi expedition with Zhang Qiao and Zhu Liang's fitted appointments. Hanji 821 shows [[MaXianLateHan|Ma Xian]] as a negative command choice against ignored warnings. Hanji 832 adds palace pressure on local appointments through Hou Lan, while Hanji 842 and Hanji 854 show reputation, relief, battlefield improvisation, pacification, and humane mediation as different kinds of fit. The concept is therefore broader than "use good people": it includes role diagnosis, political insulation, implementation method, and accountability after success.

## Key Claims

- Appointment quality is state capacity because tenure, inspection, eligibility thresholds, exception handling, placement, retention, and removal decide whether ability reaches the right office.
- The right officeholder can replace larger mobilization, while the wrong appointment can destroy capacity before performance is tested.
- Fit is role-specific: education, justice, frontier balancing, battlefield pursuit, local relief, pacification, and mediation need different officials.
- Personnel failure travels downward when palace pressure, weak evaluation, or superior preference overrides suitability.
- Correct appointment is visible in outcomes such as submission, returned captives, refugee shelter, surrender, pacification, reduced litigation, preserved labor, and restored order.
- The pattern is fragile when the system cannot protect, inspect, retain, and fairly remove fitting officials.
- Appointment quality can become morally complicated when status is priced, pressure overrides fit, or effective methods violate rules.

## Evidence

- Early education and justice fit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-779-lishi-gaosu-ni-yongcuo-ren-jing-hui-wangguo-miejia-liwe-syvny7vfmn4xpy24mgvd-z6|Hanji 779]] shows Fan Zhun reviving the scholar-instructor pipeline and Lu Gong correcting litigation timing to reduce agricultural disruption and rushed capital punishment.
- Frontier role mismatch: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-779-lishi-gaosu-ni-yongcuo-ren-jing-hui-wangguo-miejia-liwe-syvny7vfmn4xpy24mgvd-z6|Hanji 779]] shows Ren Shang failing in the Western Regions because military reputation does not substitute for Ban Chao's local-governance method.
- Local tenure and inspection: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-811-gudai-huangdi-jing-kao-chouqian-xuan-huanghou-lghfih9xcrjyrvdlb4obdxvwhl1y|Hanji 811]] says Zuo Xiong links local government failure to frequent transfers, fake reputation, bribery, amnesty loopholes, and weak grassroots staffing.
- Xiaolian standards and flexibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-812-guren-40-sui-caineng-dangguan-lordzuaqhzghyd93tpm0fql1cigv|Hanji 812]] records Zuo Xiong's age and skill tests, Hu Guang's flexibility objection, and the Xu Shu case where an exception must be proved rather than merely invoked.
- Criteria and removal risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-815-kebei-gudai-zhongchen-de-jiafeng-zhishang-llkhbxo-63pjw-gk9pihnbfr94v|Hanji 815]] says Zhang Heng warns that Xiaolian criteria can drift from filial conduct and that mass local-official dismissal can waste effective capacity.
- Positive appointment instead of scale: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-819-2-ren-gong-bi-4-wanren-tade-quanmou-lingren-paianjuejue-lsslj3y-krnd8h-c8x5pgsqeinff|Hanji 819]] says Li Gu replaces a 40,000-soldier expedition with the appointment of Zhang Qiao and Zhu Liang, whose reassurance and single-carriage entry induce surrender.
- Negative command choice: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-821-budong-laoban-xinsi-haixiang-jinsheng-meimen-lmjxr8fdxgw-colrlinmujba74fh|Hanji 821]] says Shun chooses Ma Xian against objection and ignores Ma Rong and Huangfu Gui before Ma Xian's delayed Qiang command ends in defeat and death.
- Patronage pressure against fit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-832-wei-lingdao-beiguo-ta-shi-sha-haishi-zhi-lmfpuixmi4odrf6lu31rf6-66-lq|Hanji 832]] says Hou Lan's request pressures Gao Lun toward an unsuitable appointment, while Chen Shi absorbs visible responsibility.
- Reputation, relief, and tactical fit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-842-shenme-shi-haoguan-ta-wei-baixing-gan-pinming-lj2okcbcvcmrfqrj3d-k1ylqdi2si|Hanji 842]] shows Li Ying's reputation lowering frontier conflict cost, Han Shao sheltering refugees, and Duan Jiong winning through battlefield improvisation with legal risk.
- Explicit personnel lesson: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-854-lishi-gaosu-ni-xuanguan-yongguan-hen-zhongyao-lhin-iwk6y1xr8vvzdmnjch3s2um|Hanji 854]] frames Duan Jiong, Xia Fang, Zong Zi, Huangfu Gui, Liu Ju, Huang Qiong, and Liu Chong as different appointment fits under disaster, rebellion, frontier, and fiscal pressure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not reduce every historical outcome to personnel. Hanji 779's Western Regions failure also depends on distance, local political ecology, and fiscal pressure; Hanji 819's southern success also depends on Li Gu's diagnosis, temporary retrenchment, reward policy, and rebel willingness to submit. The Shun-era selection reforms show the other risk: rigid standards may miss unconventional talent if exceptions are too narrow. Effective appointments can also create legal or moral problems, as Duan Jiong's forged-order success shows.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 779 as the earliest current layer, moving the concept from Shun-era reform back to early An-era education, justice, and Western Regions appointment fit.
- Added Ren Shang as a clear case that military talent can be misfit for indirect frontier governance.
- Compressed the evidence into representative groups while preserving the complete source inventory.
