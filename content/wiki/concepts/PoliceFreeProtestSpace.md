# Police-Free Protest Space

Police-free protest space is the protest-zone pattern added by [[20260721-pmoney-new-npr-series-we-keep-us-safe-from-the-embedded-podcast-trailer-20260721-pmoney-new-npr-series-we-keep-us-safe-from-the-embedded-podcast-trailer]]. The trailer describes [[CapitolHillOrganizedProtest|CHOP]] as a place formed after police abandoned a precinct during anti-police-brutality protests, creating an occupied area with food, tents, mutual aid, speeches, and music.

The concept is not simply absence of police. In the source, it is an attempt to make safety and community legible without ordinary policing. That makes the safety problem more demanding: people still have to decide who protects the space, how threats are interpreted, and what happens when fear and weapons enter the community.

## Key Claims
- A police-free space can be framed as both protest and positive model-building.
- Mutual aid and public gathering can create early legitimacy, but they do not automatically solve threat assessment or accountability.
- The CHOP trailer turns the ideal into a tragic question through [[AntonioMaysJr|Antonio Mays Jr.]]'s death.
