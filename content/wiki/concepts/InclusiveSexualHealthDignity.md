# Inclusive Sexual-Health Dignity / 包容性性健康尊严

## Definition
Inclusive sexual-health dignity is the principle that age, disability, illness, or need for support does not erase sexuality, consent, privacy, health needs, or the right to be included in respectful education and care.

## Current Synthesis
VOL.84 argues that public sex education commonly centers able-bodied younger adults and leaves older and disabled people outside the frame. Its first intervention is visibility: these groups have sexual feelings, relationship needs, health questions, and dignity claims even when social discomfort makes them easy to ignore.

Visibility is not permission to generalize or override consent. Support must remain person-led and attentive to communication, capacity, privacy, safety, law, access, and possible power imbalance. The episode's anecdote about assisted access to an adult-product venue illustrates the dignity claim, but it does not establish one service model for every person or setting.

## Key Claims
- Sexuality and intimacy are not limited to young, nondisabled adults.
- Disability or age does not by itself eliminate consent, agency, privacy, or relationship needs.
- Sexual-health education and services should account for communication, mobility, support, and access barriers.
- Recognition should avoid both desexualization and voyeuristic treatment.
- Assistance should support the person's choices rather than substitute a helper's preferences.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides only a brief discussion and anecdote, not a service framework, safeguarding protocol, or evidence review. Capacity, communication support, exploitation risk, caregiver power, privacy, local law, health status, and institutional policy require case-specific handling. Recognition of sexuality must never be used to weaken consent or safeguarding.
