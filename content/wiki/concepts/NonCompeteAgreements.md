# Non-Compete Agreements

## Definition

Non-compete agreements are employment restrictions that limit where workers can work after leaving a job, reducing outside options by contract rather than by natural market geography.

## Current Synthesis

In [[the-real-horror-of-alien-and-how-it-explains-why-were-not-paid-enough]], [[ArinDube|Arin Dube]] treats non-competes as [[MonopsonyByArtifice]] because they can weaken credible outside offers even where other employers want to hire. Their use beyond senior employees, including in low-paid work, shows that protection of specialized secrets cannot fully explain the institution.

A cross-doctrine substitution risk emerges from [[nooks-crannies-nooks-crannies|Nooks & Crannies]]: as states restrict non-competes, employers may rely more heavily on trade-secret claims to obtain similar mobility constraints. The mechanisms are legally distinct, but both require scrutiny of whether protection is tied to a specific legitimate interest or functions as broad labor-market control.

## Key Claims

- Non-competes reduce worker mobility and the credibility of outside offers.
- Broad use among ordinary workers exceeds a narrow trade-secret-protection rationale.
- Mobility restrictions can create employer power even without a geographically concentrated labor market.
- Limits on non-competes may shift some enforcement pressure into trade-secret litigation.
- Contract form, worker bargaining power, protected interest, scope, and jurisdiction matter to evaluation.

## Evidence

### Labor-market mechanism

- [[the-real-horror-of-alien-and-how-it-explains-why-were-not-paid-enough]] uses fast-food and summer-camp examples to argue that non-competes can strengthen monopsony beyond highly specialized roles.

### Substitution into secrecy claims

- [[nooks-crannies-nooks-crannies]] attributes to [[JeannieFromer]] the warning that employers may lean more on trade-secret claims as states restrict non-competes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The sources are structured episode summaries rather than a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction legal survey, representative contract dataset, or causal labor-market study. Non-compete and trade-secret doctrines differ, and a claim protecting specific misappropriated information is not automatically equivalent to a contractual ban on competition. The Thomas case concerns a senior executive with access to confidential information and should not be generalized directly to every worker.
