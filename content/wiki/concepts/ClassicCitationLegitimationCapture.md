# Classic Citation Legitimation Capture / 引经据典式欲望包装

Classic citation legitimation capture is the court-rhetoric pattern in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-919-xiang-huode-hao-xian-tikai-shanliang-de-ziji-lrqxfzir4bqxt0v3ccvfef8faade|Hanji 919]] where learned attendants cite authoritative classical precedent to legitimize a ruler's desire while removing the precedent's limiting conditions. In the episode, [[EmperorLingOfHan|Emperor Ling]] hesitates after [[YangCiLateHan|Yang Ci]] warns that new gardens will destroy fields and displace residents, but attendants use the [[MenciusText|Mencius]] contrast between [[ZhouWenwang|King Wen of Zhou]] and [[QiXuanWang|King Xuan of Qi]] to make the project sound compatible with "shared pleasure."

The capture lies in separating the attractive citation from the public-access rule that gives it meaning. The episode's critique is not that the classics are useless, but that a cultivated courtier can turn them into decoration for power unless the citation is tested against livelihood, access, and institutional context.

## Key Claims
- Citation becomes manipulation when it supplies prestige while discarding the conditions of the precedent.
- Learned flattery is more dangerous than blunt flattery because it can sound principled.
- Classical rhetoric needs an external governance test, such as public access and livelihood protection.
