# Shared Political Legitimacy Vocabulary / 共享政治正当性词汇

## Definition

Shared political legitimacy vocabulary is a repertoire of state-approved, patriotic, moral, or people-centered language that opposing groups reuse to present private interests and cultural conflict as publicly authoritative action.

## Current Synthesis

[[yi-chang-wuli-yiyi-shang-de-nannv-duili-chongqing-shidai-fengjun-shijian]] identifies this mechanism in the confrontation around [[ShidaiFengjun|时代峰峻]]. Male participants sang political songs, displayed flags, invoked "the people," and called for state action; female fans cited official recognition, national broadcasts, hard work, public service, and Lei Feng-style morality to defend idols.

These are not wholly separate ideologies. The episode argues that both sides compete inside a common legitimacy system shaped by two coexisting official narratives: patriotic youth idols can be promoted as models, while fan-circle disorder and "effeminate" aesthetics can be treated as governance problems. Cultural antagonists select different parts of the same repertoire and turn them against one another.

## Key Claims

- Political language can convert taste conflict into claims about public order, citizenship, and national value.
- Opposing groups may draw legitimacy from the same mainstream repertoire rather than from separate value systems.
- Official endorsement becomes a portable defense for entertainment products and fan identity.
- Appeals to "the people," the nation, or state intervention can elevate antagonism without proving representative authority.
- Coexisting official messages can supply both sides with selective but recognizable legitimacy resources.

## Evidence

- Male political performance: [[yi-chang-wuli-yiyi-shang-de-nannv-duili-chongqing-shidai-fengjun-shijian]] reports songs, flags, people-centered slogans, and appeals for state action.
- Fan respectability claims: [[yi-chang-wuli-yiyi-shang-de-nannv-duili-chongqing-shidai-fengjun-shijian]] reports fans citing national-broadcast appearances, official recognition, hard work, and Lei Feng-style morality.
- Shared-repertoire interpretation: [[yi-chang-wuli-yiyi-shang-de-nannv-duili-chongqing-shidai-fengjun-shijian]] places patriotic idol promotion beside regulation of fan circles and "effeminate" aesthetics.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Using patriotic or moral language does not by itself show cynicism, coordination, or bad faith. Participants may sincerely hold these values. The source interprets how language supplies legitimacy; it does not establish every speaker's motive or reduce the physical conflict to discourse alone.

## What Changed

- Initial concept created from the opposing camps' use of overlapping official-value language.
