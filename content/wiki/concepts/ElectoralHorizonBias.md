# Electoral Horizon Bias

## Definition

Electoral Horizon Bias is the tendency for governments to prioritize crises, budgets, and constituencies that matter before the next election over investments whose largest benefits or failures will emerge beyond that political horizon.

## Current Synthesis

The French protest source applies a “tragedy of the horizon” frame to education and youth politics. Governments must survive immediate demonstrations, fiscal negotiations, confidence threats, and elections, while missing teachers, poor facilities, and opaque admissions decisions can alter a student's opportunities for decades.

Unequal electoral voice intensifies the mismatch. Most secondary-school students cannot vote, younger voters form a comparatively small bloc, and older voters are both more numerous and more directly protected by politically salient spending. Street protest can make youth grievances visible, but subsequent violence can redirect attention from long-run education reform toward immediate order and punishment.

## Key Claims

- Election cycles can privilege visible short-run stabilization over long-run institutional investment.
- People who bear the longest consequences of education policy may have the weakest immediate electoral leverage.
- Budget rules and debt pressure narrow the set of politically available long-term commitments.
- Demographic voting power can protect older-cohort benefits while youth-facing services remain under-resourced.
- Protest can compensate for weak formal voice by creating attention, but escalation may displace the original policy agenda.
- Acknowledging a long-run problem does not ensure that a government can or will fund a durable response.

## Evidence

- Competing time scales: [[faguo-50-wan-ren-shangjie-yichang-gaozhong-kangyi-ruhe-shaobian-quanguo-400332274631798b431d5bcde6337d90]] contrasts immediate protest, budget, and confidence pressures with lifelong consequences from educational failure.
- Unequal political voice: [[faguo-50-wan-ren-shangjie-yichang-gaozhong-kangyi-ruhe-shaobian-quanguo-400332274631798b431d5bcde6337d90]] says most high-school students lack voting rights and reports a much larger older than younger electorate.
- Distributional protection: [[faguo-50-wan-ren-shangjie-yichang-gaozhong-kangyi-ruhe-shaobian-quanguo-400332274631798b431d5bcde6337d90]] contrasts underinvestment in teachers with inflation-linked pension protection.
- Agenda displacement: [[faguo-50-wan-ren-shangjie-yichang-gaozhong-kangyi-ruhe-shaobian-quanguo-400332274631798b431d5bcde6337d90]] argues that riot imagery may move debate from education and living costs toward law and order.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source supplies one national episode rather than a comparative causal test. Older voters do not have uniform interests, long-term policy can also benefit current voters, and immediate public order is a legitimate government responsibility. Debt, administrative capacity, labor supply, legal design, and policy disagreement can constrain education spending independently of electoral calculation. The reported demographic shares and spending comparisons remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the source's explicit conflict between election time and educational life time.
- Added weak youth voting power and protest-driven agenda displacement as mechanisms.
