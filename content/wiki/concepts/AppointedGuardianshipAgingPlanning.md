# Appointed Guardianship Aging Planning / 意定监护养老规划

## Definition
Appointed guardianship aging planning / 意定监护养老规划 is the use of a written pre-incapacity arrangement to designate who will make care, medical, residential, and related decisions when an adult later loses civil capacity.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents appointed guardianship as a slow-growing legal answer to family-structure change. Its need appears when older adults live alone, adult children live abroad, couples have no children, people never marry, or daily care is provided by friends, neighbors, partners, classmates, or paid/non-kin carers rather than default legal relatives. In those situations, affection and proximity may not automatically produce authority to sign, arrange care, enter institutions, manage property, or handle death-related affairs.

The current judgment is that appointed guardianship expands [[ElderCareStateCapacity]] only when legal paperwork, institutional knowledge, ethical safeguards, and usable local practice align. The episode values the system because it can recognize chosen trust relationships, but it also shows its limits: undocumented wishes can lose to inheritance rules, public understanding remains thin, and suspicious moral readings can arise when guardianship intersects with sex, caregiving, or property.

## Key Claims
- Appointed guardianship responds to a real decision-maker gap created by aging, family thinning, overseas children, DINK households, nonmarriage, and solitary living.
- Legal capacity planning is part of [[CareSocialization]] because it moves elder support beyond filial defaults into documented, accountable arrangements.
- The institution is not enough by itself: wills, guardianship documents, care records, and estate rules must fit the older person's actual preferences.
- Non-kin guardians or carers can be legitimate, but the arrangement requires safeguards because care, intimacy, money, and dependency can overlap.
- Local practice matters: Shanghai's developing appointed-guardianship regulations and notary cases show slow institutional growth rather than immediate nationwide maturity.

- System definition and timing - [[women-ruhe-laoqu-cong-qianlang2-kuijian-zhongguo-laonian-shenghuo-de-zhenshi-tujing-lkybur9dgim4vxxyjuq1eav4i-up]] defines appointed guardianship as a written arrangement made while the adult still has full civil capacity for use after incapacity.
- Family-structure pressure - [[women-ruhe-laoqu-cong-qianlang2-kuijian-zhongguo-laonian-shenghuo-de-zhenshi-tujing-lkybur9dgim4vxxyjuq1eav4i-up]] links demand to overseas children, DINK couples, nonmarriage, declining births, and older people who need signatures or care decisions when relatives are absent or unavailable.
- Estate and intention tension - [[women-ruhe-laoqu-cong-qianlang2-kuijian-zhongguo-laonian-shenghuo-de-zhenshi-tujing-lkybur9dgim4vxxyjuq1eav4i-up]] describes a lonely elder who expressed a wish to donate property but left no will, forcing legal-heir handling despite the notary's knowledge of her preference.
- Local institutional growth - [[women-ruhe-laoqu-cong-qianlang2-kuijian-zhongguo-laonian-shenghuo-de-zhenshi-tujing-lkybur9dgim4vxxyjuq1eav4i-up]] says appointed guardianship entered the Civil Code in 2021 and was followed by Shanghai local regulation and notary practice from 2022 to 2026.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode gives a documentary discussion, not a procedural legal guide. Regional rules, notarization requirements, supervision mechanisms, conflict-of-interest safeguards, and court practice remain outside the bounded evidence. The source also uses "异定监护" in the summary, while the more standard legal term is "意定监护"; this page normalizes the concept to appointed guardianship without treating the wording variance as a substantive contradiction.
