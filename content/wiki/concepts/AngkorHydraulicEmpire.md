# Angkor Hydraulic Empire / 吴哥水利帝国

Angkor hydraulic empire / 吴哥水利帝国 is the source's synthesis for how [[Angkor|吴哥]] and the [[KhmerEmpire|吴哥王朝]] could sustain monumental construction and dense settlement in tropical [[Cambodia|柬埔寨]]. [[ep267-tafang-meigonghe-yuji-hunfeiyi-yu-shuili-diguo-lkq2vxaatn4wxue7y5q1fc3-8yfk]] links the empire to [[TonleSapLake|洞里萨湖]] flood pulses, rainy-season water storage, dry-season irrigation, rice surplus, labor mobilization, and divine kingship.

The concept is not only about engineering. The episode treats water control as a political-religious system: kings could claim sacred authority through temples, but their power also depended on the ability to organize reservoirs, canals, agricultural production, and maintenance across seasons.

## Key Claims
- Angkor's abundance depended on turning seasonal water variation into stored agricultural capacity.
- [[TonleSapLake]] connected flood, nutrient, fishery, and irrigation systems.
- Hydraulic infrastructure supported state capacity, but also required knowledge, labor, and maintenance.
- Temple building and water management reinforced one another as forms of imperial authority.
- The concept should remain source-scoped: the episode uses archaeology, hydrology, inscriptions, and [[ZhouDaguan|周达观]] without claiming every detail of Angkor history is settled.
