# Gossip As Social Grooming / 八卦作为社会性梳毛

Gossip as social grooming is the source's revaluation of gossip, small talk, and talk about people as social-maintenance labor. [[83-shumao-bagua-ji-yuyan-de-jinhua-renji-guanxi-dengbashu-zan-bu-fu-bu-xing-698132861]] argues through [[RobinDunbar|Robin Dunbar / 罗宾·邓巴]] that much everyday language is not primarily about transferring formal information; it is about mapping relationships, sharing attention, checking trust, and creating belonging.

The page is broader than [[LiteraryGossipAsContext]]. Literary gossip uses author anecdotes to interpret cultural works. Gossip as social grooming treats gossip itself as a general human relationship mechanism, with both useful and harmful forms.

## Key Claims
- Gossip can carry relationship information: who is trusted, who broke norms, who belongs, and what the group notices.
- Talking about absent people can create shared social maps, but it can also become exclusion, cruelty, or reputational harm.
- Casual talk often matters because the interaction itself signals time, attention, and mutual recognition.
- The source pushes back against treating gossip as feminine, unserious, or merely wasteful.
- A social-grooming frame should not excuse harassment, privacy violation, or malicious rumor.
