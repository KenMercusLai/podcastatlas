# Skipping Plyometric Progression

## Definition
Skipping plyometric progression is the use of rhythmic skipping as a lower-intensity bridge toward the posture, coordination, stiffness, hip extension, and eccentric control required for striding and sprinting.

## Current Synthesis
The source positions skipping between ordinary locomotion and more demanding bounding or maximal sprinting. Many adults may retain cardiovascular ambition while lacking the tendon, joint, tissue, balance, or eccentric capacity to sprint safely. Skipping offers a scalable way to reintroduce flight, rhythm, alternating limbs, cross-body coordination, and ground-force acceptance without pretending it is risk-free.

The example on-ramp alternates 30 seconds of skipping with 30 seconds of walking or jogging. That is a source example, not a universal prescription. Progress should remain governed by movement quality, recovery, symptoms, surface, and the person's training and medical context.

## Key Claims
- Skipping is a plyometric gait, not merely a childlike activity.
- It can train hip extension, knee-behind-body position, ground stiffness, rhythm, posture, and cross-body coordination.
- It is less demanding than bounding and can precede striding or sprinting.
- Alternating skipping with walking or jogging provides a simple way to scale exposure.
- Jump-rope work can support coordinated ankle, knee, and hip flexion-extension when the whole body bounces rather than only the toes.
- Quality and symptom response should govern progression more than fatigue accumulation.

## Evidence
- Adult on-ramp: [[how-to-increase-your-speed-mobility-longevity-with-plyometrics-sprinting-stuart-mcmillan-scim1307532744]] recommends skipping and hills for adults not yet ready to sprint and gives a 30-second alternating example.
- Trained qualities: [[how-to-increase-your-speed-mobility-longevity-with-plyometrics-sprinting-stuart-mcmillan-scim1307532744]] links skipping to hip extension, stiffness, posture, rhythm, coordination, and eccentric control.
- Plyometric classification: [[how-to-increase-your-speed-mobility-longevity-with-plyometrics-sprinting-stuart-mcmillan-scim1307532744]] describes skipping as a regression from harder bounding and distinguishes it from mainly concentric box jumping.
- Rope variation: [[how-to-increase-your-speed-mobility-longevity-with-plyometrics-sprinting-stuart-mcmillan-scim1307532744]] uses a trampoline-like bounce and coordinated lower-limb flexion-extension as jump-rope cues.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not establish injury rates, minimum capacity tests, progression intervals, or comparative effectiveness against other plyometric regressions. Skipping can still be inappropriate or require modification for people with balance impairment, recent injury, pain, osteoporosis, cardiovascular risk, neurological conditions, pregnancy, or limited impact tolerance. The 30-second pattern is not individualized clearance.
