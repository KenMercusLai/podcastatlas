# Agarwood Industrialization / 沉香产业化

Agarwood industrialization / 沉香产业化 is [[ep263-xianxiang-jueqi-faxian-zhongshi-xiangqi-de-meili-lq1lsqcprqyr7auo5gslwyymuiv]]'s account of [[Agarwood|沉香]] moving from wild collection and high-end cultural consumption toward planted trees, artificial结香, [[Qinan|奇楠]] grafting, standardized processing, and mass-market line incense. The episode follows Guangdong Dianbai and Hainan-related aroma reputation to show that the recent incense boom is not only aesthetic; it also depends on raw-material production changing.

The source frames industrialization as both opportunity and constraint. Planted qinan and artificial结香 can lower prices and create new products, but export proof, wild-versus-planted legality, quality standards, consumer recognition, and source trust remain bottlenecks.

## Key Claims
- Wild agarwood scarcity and forest protection made old collection routes harder or impossible inside China.
- Planting white-wood incense trees requires long time horizons; the episode describes roughly ten years of growth plus years of artificial结香 work.
- Qinan grafting around 2015-2016, with usable fragrance emerging around 2019-2020, is presented as a major technical turning point.
- Industrialization changes the product ladder: beads and high-end play remain, but line incense, blended incense, essential oil, and daily fragrance products become more plausible.
- Export compliance is a structural issue because businesses must prove planted origin and avoid illegal wild-agarwood flows.
- Industrialization does not remove taste training; consumers still need to distinguish raw-material quality, blending, and credible origin claims.
