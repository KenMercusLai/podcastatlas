# Substantive Choice Capability / 实质选择能力

## Definition
Substantive choice capability is the practical ability to understand, select, and carry through an option under adequate material, institutional, social, and cognitive conditions, rather than the mere presence of formal permission or multiple alternatives.

## Current Synthesis
[[308-nvquan-shifou-yiweizhe-zhengqu-xuanze-ziyou-lgesbs0sd76ciib1v1fw8szvulrt]] distinguishes a right to choose from the capability to make that right real. A person may formally be allowed to work, leave, parent, avoid parenthood, obtain care, or pursue education while wages, childcare, medical access, discrimination, violence, time, or dependency make the nominal option unusable. Public policy and care infrastructure are therefore part of freedom rather than only external corrections after private choice.

Capability is also cognitive and social. Advertising, expectations, peer discipline, addiction, uncertainty, and option overload can shape desires or consume the attention needed to evaluate them. This does not prove that autonomy is an illusion, but it makes choice quality dependent on information, reflection, manageable complexity, and the ability to revise or exit without catastrophic cost.

## Key Claims
- Formal permission is weaker than a genuinely usable option.
- Income, childcare, health care, education, anti-discrimination protection, and safety can determine whether choice exists in practice.
- The ability to choose includes self-understanding and evaluative capacity, not only a larger menu of alternatives.
- Socially shaped desire does not erase agency, but it weakens claims that preference alone proves freedom.
- More options can increase freedom up to a point while also increasing attention costs, anxiety, indecision, and exposure to manipulation.
- Collective institutions can enlarge autonomy when they make options safe and feasible, even though paternalistic control can also suppress it.

## Evidence
- **Material conditions:** [[308-nvquan-shifou-yiweizhe-zhengqu-xuanze-ziyou-lgesbs0sd76ciib1v1fw8szvulrt]] names decent wages, childcare, health care, education, protection from discrimination, and freedom from violence as conditions for real choice.
- **Unequal starting points:** [[308-nvquan-shifou-yiweizhe-zhengqu-xuanze-ziyou-lgesbs0sd76ciib1v1fw8szvulrt]] presents left feminist criticism that a choice-centered frame most readily fits people who already possess broad social and economic options.
- **Desire formation:** [[308-nvquan-shifou-yiweizhe-zhengqu-xuanze-ziyou-lgesbs0sd76ciib1v1fw8szvulrt]] uses advertising, social expectation, peer discipline, compulsion, and addiction to question whether observed preference alone establishes autonomous endorsement.
- **Cognitive burden:** [[308-nvquan-shifou-yiweizhe-zhengqu-xuanze-ziyou-lgesbs0sd76ciib1v1fw8szvulrt]] links expanding technological options to greater demands on time, energy, judgment, and tolerance for uncertain consequences.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides a normative and interpretive framework rather than a measurement model for capability. It does not establish a universal threshold for sufficient resources, information, or option complexity. Social influence affects all preference formation and cannot by itself prove coercion or false consciousness. Public provision can expand real freedom, but intervention still requires limits so that capability language does not become a general license for authorities to override competent adults.

## What Changed
- Established a distinction between nominal options and usable freedom.
- Joined economic and care infrastructure to cognitive and social conditions of decision-making.
- Added option overload as a limit internal to choice expansion rather than evidence against autonomy itself.
