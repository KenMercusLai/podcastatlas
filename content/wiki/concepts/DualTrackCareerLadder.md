# Dual-Track Career Ladder / 专业与管理双通道

## Definition
A dual-track career ladder lets employees advance through deep professional contribution or through people management without treating team leadership as the only legitimate route to pay, status, and continued employment.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep157-kun-zai-pinduoduo-de-xitong-li-weida-de-gongsi-bu-yinggai-rang-ren-gandao-haipa-ckwriued61wwabaaaacbzp2q|EP157]] argues that tying advancement primarily to team size can produce two losses at once: skilled specialists spend less time on their craft, while people who do not want or cannot perform management work become managers anyway. Employees who prefer expert work may then be read as lacking ambition even when their contribution remains strong.

The dual-track alternative does not claim that expert work requires no coordination or that titles should rise automatically. It requires comparable reward, explicit competency standards, real decision scope, and movement between tracks so that “individual contributor” is not merely a lower-status holding category.

## Key Claims
- Excellence in a specialist role does not prove fit for people management.
- Promotion systems can destroy expert capacity when management is the only rewarded route.
- A credible expert track needs compensation, status, scope, and evaluation comparable to management.
- Employees should be able to prefer stable expert contribution without being treated as unmotivated.
- Movement between tracks should depend on demonstrated role fit rather than a one-way hierarchy.

## Evidence
- Specialist displacement - [[ep157-kun-zai-pinduoduo-de-xitong-li-weida-de-gongsi-bu-yinggai-rang-ren-gandao-haipa-ckwriued61wwabaaaacbzp2q]] argues that strong practitioners can lose craft time when promotion requires supervising others.
- Management mismatch - [[ep157-kun-zai-pinduoduo-de-xitong-li-weida-de-gongsi-bu-yinggai-rang-ren-gandao-haipa-ckwriued61wwabaaaacbzp2q]] says organizations can create weak managers by promoting people for technical strength rather than leadership ability.
- Status pressure - [[ep157-kun-zai-pinduoduo-de-xitong-li-weida-de-gongsi-bu-yinggai-rang-ren-gandao-haipa-ckwriued61wwabaaaacbzp2q]] reports that workers who do not seek leadership may be judged as lacking ambition by company and society.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a general workplace argument rather than comparative evidence on specific ladder designs. Some senior expert roles necessarily include mentoring, cross-team coordination, risk ownership, or technical leadership. A nominal dual ladder can also fail if expert titles lack pay parity, authority, promotion slots, or executive sponsorship.

## What Changed
- Established role-fit and status parity as the basis for separating expert and management advancement.
