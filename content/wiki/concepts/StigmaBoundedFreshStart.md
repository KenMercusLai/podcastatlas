# Stigma-Bounded Fresh Start / 污名约束的重新开始

## Definition

A stigma-bounded fresh start is an attempted identity or life change whose credibility remains limited by past conduct, group association, rumor, and another person's legitimate uncertainty about future risk.

## Current Synthesis

[[YouSanjie|尤三姐]] treats marriage to [[LiuXianglian|柳湘莲]] as acceptance of her changed conduct and an exit from the sexualized 贾-household world. Liu Xianglian instead treats her connection to 宁国府 and [[JiaBaoyu|贾宝玉]]'s evasiveness as evidence that the risk persists. The episode's dispute shows why neither internal reform nor elapsed time automatically produces interpersonal trust.

The concept separates permission to change from entitlement to another person's confidence. A person should not be frozen forever at the worst interpretation of a past identity, but a prospective partner also cannot be compelled to absorb uncertain relational risk. The tragedy intensifies when reputation substitutes for direct inquiry, when changed conduct has no credible nonfatal proof, and when one relationship bears the entire burden of social re-entry.

## Key Claims

- Changed intention and behavior do not automatically generate verifiable trust.
- Group association can become a shortcut that confuses environment, coercion, prior conduct, and present identity.
- Refusing a relationship is not inherently immoral, but judgment without direct inquiry can reproduce stigma and cause harm.
- A fresh start becomes fragile when one promise is made responsible for dignity, safety, livelihood, and social recognition at once.
- Death or self-destruction must not become the imagined standard of proof that change was sincere.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode analyzes fictional characters, not a general rule for evaluating real partners or rehabilitation. It does not settle the content of You Sanjie's past, Liu Xianglian's dominant motive, or the level of future risk. Gender-neutral application is a stated aspiration of the discussion, but apparently symmetrical choices can occur within unequal reputational and material structures.

## What Changed

- Established a distinction between the right to change and entitlement to intimate trust.
- Identified direct inquiry, distributed support, and noncatastrophic evidence as missing conditions in the episode's tragedy.
- Preserved the moral gap between understandable caution and associative condemnation.
