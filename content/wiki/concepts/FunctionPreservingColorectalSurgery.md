# Function-Preserving Colorectal Surgery

## Definition
Function-preserving colorectal surgery is the treatment frame that combines oncologic safety with preservation of bowel, sphincter, bladder, sexual, vascular, valve, and quality-of-life functions where clinically appropriate.

## Current Synthesis
The episode describes a shift from surgery judged mainly by whether the tumor is removed cleanly toward surgery judged by cure plus postoperative living quality. Ding Kefeng still prioritizes safe cancer removal, but he emphasizes sphincter preservation, precise anastomosis, blood supply, valve function, bladder and sexual-function nerves, and selected ileocecal preservation in right-colon surgery when margins and anatomy allow. The concept stores a modern surgical tradeoff: preserving function is valuable only when it does not compromise oncologic safety.

## Key Claims
- Colorectal-cancer treatment quality depends on stage, cure probability, and life after surgery.
- Sphincter, bladder, sexual-function, valve, bowel-continuity, and blood-supply protection can matter to postoperative quality of life.
- Laparoscopy, robotic systems, and precise dissection can expand preservation possibilities in selected cases.
- Traditional right hemicolectomy favors safety and reliable blood supply, but some cases may allow ileocecal preservation.
- Function preservation must remain bounded by tumor location, safe margins, complete resection, and complication risk.

- Right-colon example: [[vol-203-kao-bingmeishi-tongbian-yi-jinzhang-jiu-fuxie-nide-cuipi-changdao-xuyao-zhefen-shiyongshu-lvtxc2z3hn5ukh3va9rzijremcih]] describes Ding's team's ileocecal-preserving right-colon surgery research for selected hepatic-flexure or near-hepatic transverse-colon cases.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page is not a surgical indication guide. Tumor site, stage, margins, nodes, vessels, patient condition, surgeon experience, emergency setting, and complications can all make a more extensive operation necessary.
