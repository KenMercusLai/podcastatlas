## Definition

权力中心接近优势 / Power Center Proximity Advantage is the institutional and career advantage created when a role sits close to real decision makers, information flow, document routing, and promotion visibility, even if its formal rank or immediate jurisdiction is smaller than an alternative role.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 769 defines the concept through [[HuangXiangLateHan|Huang Xiang]]'s refusal to leave the [[ShangshuTaiImperialAccess|Shangshu Tai]] for [[DongCommandery|Dong Commandery]]. A commandery taishou has high rank and real local authority, but the source argues that a Shangshu post near the emperor and senior court offices can carry denser information, stronger elite ties, and better visibility to the people who decide future promotion.

The current synthesis is not that local office lacks power. The source explicitly treats local commandery authority as large. Its sharper claim is comparative: when a person's future depends on central recognition and access to high-quality information, distance from the power center can reduce opportunity even when the new office looks larger on paper.

## Key Claims

- Formal rank, salary, and jurisdiction do not fully measure an office's practical value.
- Roles near the ruler and central document flow can produce information advantages unavailable to distant local posts.
- Visibility to senior decision makers is itself a career resource because future trust and promotion require being known.
- High-end networks around the court can outweigh local elite networks when the relevant game is central advancement.
- The advantage is conditional: role fit, historical period, and the real distribution of power determine whether proximity helps.

## Evidence

- Formal-promotion contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-769-gongwuyuan-gai-zenme-kao-guren-gaosu-ni-ltgxekmkpioy11b2-q2kkj93ja0i|Hanji 769]] says Huang Xiang's planned move from shangshu ling to Dong Commandery taishou looks like a higher-ranking promotion.
- Proximity and information: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-769-gongwuyuan-gai-zenme-kao-guren-gaosu-ni-ltgxekmkpioy11b2-q2kkj93ja0i|Hanji 769]] argues that the Shangshu Tai is closer to the emperor, Three Excellencies, and high-quality information.
- Visibility and network: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-769-gongwuyuan-gai-zenme-kao-guren-gaosu-ni-ltgxekmkpioy11b2-q2kkj93ja0i|Hanji 769]] says being known by the "big boss" and connected to central actors changes promotion prospects.
- Conditional scope: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-769-gongwuyuan-gai-zenme-kao-guren-gaosu-ni-ltgxekmkpioy11b2-q2kkj93ja0i|Hanji 769]] also notes that local taishou power is real and that later Qing office valuation changes after Yongzheng, limiting the claim's universality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not be used as a universal rule that headquarters or court posts are always better. Local officials can hold substantial authority, build local coalitions, and matter more in periods when local power rises. Hanji 769's modern corporation analogy and Qing-period comparison are source-scoped explanatory frames, not a complete theory of every bureaucracy.
