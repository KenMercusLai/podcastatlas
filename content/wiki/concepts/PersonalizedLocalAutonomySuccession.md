## Definition

个人化地方自治继承断裂 / personalized local autonomy succession is the failure of a negotiated local settlement to pass automatically from a powerful broker to an heir when the center recognizes the person, but not a hereditary right to office, territory, or armed capacity.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1119-ta-shengqian-guishun-sunquan-sihou-weihe-jiazu-bei-ganjin-shajue-ll4mblhk7ydn3pdxg-5zucstokzz|Hanji 1119]] supplies the founding case. [[ShiXie|士燮]] possesses deep family roots, governs [[JiaozhiCommandery|交趾郡]], coordinates brothers across [[Jiaozhou|交州]], and remains indispensable to successive outside inspectors. He nevertheless avoids the highest provincial title, submits to [[SunQuan|孙权]], sends a son as hostage, and pays tribute. These actions allow high practical autonomy to coexist with formal subordination during his lifetime.

The settlement breaks at succession because its two sides understand continuity differently. Sun Quan treats Shi Xie's death as an opening to divide the province and appoint replacement officials. [[ShiHui|士徽]] treats inherited family capacity as grounds to claim the Jiaozhi post. His resistance removes ambiguity and lets [[LyuDai|吕岱]] dismantle the family leadership through induced surrender and execution.

The concept therefore separates three things often collapsed into “loyalty”: personal recognition, tolerated local capacity, and hereditary entitlement. A broker can be rewarded and left in place without the center conceding the third. Conversely, an heir can inherit real followers and wealth without inheriting formal legitimacy.

## Key Claims

- Practical local autonomy may rest on a personal bargain rather than a durable institution.
- Formal submission and large independent local capacity can coexist while a trusted broker remains alive.
- Hostages, tribute, rank, and reciprocal gifts stabilize a relationship without settling hereditary succession.
- The death of the broker reopens disputes over appointment, territory, revenue, and force.
- Inherited capacity is not the same as inherited legitimacy, even when an heir can mobilize kin and followers.
- Open resistance can convert an ambiguous succession dispute into a center-versus-rebel conflict.
- Personal prudence matters, but structural pressure to reclaim authority can persist regardless of an heir's temperament.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept currently rests on one podcast reconstruction. It does not prove that Sun Quan had decided on total extermination before Shi Hui resisted, nor that continued restraint would have preserved meaningful Shi autonomy. The host's father-versus-son morality may overstate temperament and understate the center's standing interest in appointments, territory, and revenue. Conversely, structural centralization does not erase Shi Hui's agency in claiming office and taking up arms.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the Shi Xie–Shi Hui succession contrast.
- Separated personal recognition, tolerated capacity, and hereditary entitlement.
- Added a structural qualification to the episode's prudence-versus-rashness explanation.
