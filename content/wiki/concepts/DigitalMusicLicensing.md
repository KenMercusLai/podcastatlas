# Digital Music Licensing

## Definition
Digital music licensing is the rights, registry, compensation, and industry-coordination system required to distribute or generate music legally through networked services.

## Current Synthesis
The complete source set traces three stages of the problem. Napster showed that overwhelming consumer demand and plausible revenue ideas could not substitute for rights-holder agreement. Snowcap showed that a technically credible registry and payment mechanism could still fail when labels would not align. Spotify's 2021 account adds the platform-building response: product development and label negotiation proceeded together, and usage-based settlement replaced some bulk catalog arrangements that paid without regard to actual listening.

Generative AI adds a new layer because licensing now concerns training data, model construction, and generated output as well as track distribution. Litigation and partnership can proceed simultaneously, as shown by different label responses to Suno. Across all stages, legal access depends on institutional coordination, and compensation metrics introduce new gaming and allocation problems.

## Key Claims
- Consumer demand and technical distribution do not create legal rights to music.
- Registries and clearing systems can fail through coordination even when technically plausible.
- Platform viability improves when licensing and product design develop together.
- Usage-based settlement links payment to consumption but creates measurement and manipulation risks.
- Failed negotiations can deepen distrust and delay later licensing structures.
- AI music extends the problem from distributing recordings to training models and governing outputs.

## Evidence
- Napster negotiation failure: [[tsr-ronconway-part4-v2-tsr-ronconway-part4-v2]] describes proposed revenue sharing, data, commerce, concert, and sponsorship routes that did not produce a settlement before shutdown.
- Registry coordination: [[tsr-ronconway-part4-v2-tsr-ronconway-part4-v2]] presents Snowcap as a licensed/unlicensed identification and compensation attempt that could not align labels.
- Product-and-rights co-development: [[ep166-spotify-yuanhe-chengwei-dibiao-zuiqiang-yinle-liumeiti-pingtai-ckwriueepmnaaabaaacarjjd]] describes Spotify building the product while negotiating with labels and moving toward payment based on actual plays.
- AI-era divergence: [[tech-20260817-tech-pod-128-tech-20260817-tech-pod-128]] reports continuing Universal and Sony litigation alongside Warner's settlement and work with Suno.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Napster history is Ron Conway's participant account, the Spotify details come from a 2021 conversation, and the Suno-label status is a dated news snapshot. The sources do not provide contract text, audited royalty flows, full rights-holder perspectives, or proof that usage-based settlement is fair across artists. The reported label equity, play threshold, and device-specific licensing costs remain source-scoped.
