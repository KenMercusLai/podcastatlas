# Cognitive Reappraisal Early Intervention

## Definition
Cognitive reappraisal early intervention is the regulation strategy of reframing or redesigning a situation before attention intensifies an emotional state into rumination or automatic action.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats reappraisal as useful but double-edged. Thinking differently about an event can reduce distress, yet repeatedly attending to sadness or anxiety can also deepen it. Adolphs' practical emphasis is therefore timing: earlier monitoring, environmental design, and avoiding predictable traps often work better than trying to reason with a state after it has already taken priority.

## Key Claims
- Reappraisal can regulate emotion by changing how a situation is interpreted.
- Attention can also feed the emotion, especially when reappraisal turns into rumination.
- Earlier intervention usually has more leverage than late-stage control.
- Environmental design is part of regulation when it avoids predictable emotional traps.
- Reappraisal works best as one tool inside a broader regulation toolkit, not as constant analysis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not claim reappraisal is universally effective. It explicitly preserves the risk that analysis can become rumination, especially for sadness or anxiety.
