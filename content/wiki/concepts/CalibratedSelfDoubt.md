# Calibrated Self-Doubt

## Definition
Calibrated self-doubt is uncertainty that remains proportionate enough to support preparation, humility, and revision without becoming a global judgment of worth, belonging, or capability.

## Current Synthesis
The source rejects both total confidence and chronic self-condemnation. Too little doubt can obstruct reasoned judgment and make corrective evidence easier to ignore. Too much can feed anxiety, depression, withdrawal, opportunity avoidance, over-preparation, and destructive effort. The useful middle is functional: doubt should direct attention toward evidence and controllable preparation, then yield when it threatens health or turns one setback into an identity.

Calibration also depends on context. Competitive institutions, racialized expectations, exaggerated praise, and unsafe responses to error can raise the personal cost of ordinary imperfection. Individual reframing works better when leaders make mistakes survivable and when evidence of competence is recorded before selective memory erases it.

## Key Claims
- The value of self-doubt depends on what it does, not merely whether it is present.
- Useful doubt prompts evidence review, preparation, humility, and willingness to revise.
- Harmful doubt converts mistakes into identity judgments, discourages opportunity, or demands work that compromises health and rest.
- Concrete achievement records can counter the confirmation bias that remembers failures while discounting success.
- Institutions affect calibration by making ordinary error either survivable or evidence that a person does not belong.
- Excessive praise can be destabilizing when it creates a standard that ordinary setbacks seem to disprove.
- The healthy threshold is person- and context-dependent; the source does not supply a universal dose of doubt.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not identify a measurable threshold at which doubt becomes beneficial or harmful, and Cokley's own over-preparation may not transfer safely to other people. Public figures and the Iraq-war example illustrate the two extremes but do not establish causal symmetry between private insecurity and political hubris. Persistent or severe distress may require qualified support rather than achievement tracking or reframing alone.

## What Changed
- Established a functional middle between disabling self-condemnation and uncorrectable certainty.
- Added institutional error climate, evidence records, rest, and health as conditions of useful doubt.
