# Fund Advisory Fee Transparency / 基金投顾费率透明

Fund advisory fee transparency is the [[145-jijin-tougu-zhide-xinren-ma-lrckug0zjqolcczni8ajikb0k5mi]] problem created when advisory fees become visible to investors instead of being partly funded through hidden distribution economics. The source says Chinese fund advisory fees vary widely and that investors have reacted strongly when platforms begin charging explicit advisory fees.

The episode does not treat visible fees as inherently worse. Its point is that explicit fees force the service to prove itself: client profiling, rebalancing, reports, education, consultation, and [[InvestmentBehaviorCoaching|behavior coaching]] must be clear enough that the client sees advice rather than another product shelf charge.

## Key Claims
- Visible advisory fees can be healthier than hidden sales-service fees, but only if they clarify who pays whom and for what.
- A direct fee creates sharper expectations for post-purchase service, market explanation, and client support.
- Advisory fees should be compared with U.S. robo-advice and hybrid-advice costs only after accounting for service scope and local market structure.
- Fee reform can expose [[FinancialPlatformIncentives]] that were previously buried inside trail commissions, C-share fees, or product-company payments.
- Fee transparency fails if explicit client fees coexist with undisclosed commercial sponsorship from fund companies.
