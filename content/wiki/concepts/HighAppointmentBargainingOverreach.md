## Definition

高层任命越权交易 / High appointment bargaining overreach is the political and organizational failure in which actors without appointment authority privately trade praise, recommendations, access, or factional support as if they can allocate senior posts.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 479 creates the concept from [[JiaJuanzhiWesternHan|贾捐之]] and [[YangXingWesternHan|杨兴]] under [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]. The pair's scheme is not merely flattery. It bundles praise of [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]], proposed rewards for Shi and his brothers, Jia's imagined Shangshu Ling role, and Yang's Jingzhao Yin ambition into one visible bargain.

The source's management translation is that personnel power is a core authority of rulers and bosses. Touching it without a mandate can offend every side at once: the ruler sees appointment authority being bypassed, the favored intermediary fears being suspected of private manipulation, and the schemer loses credibility because praise looks purchased rather than principled.

## Key Claims

- Senior appointment power is politically sensitive because it signals who controls future access, rewards, and organizational direction.
- Private praise becomes dangerous when it appears tied to a concrete office bargain.
- A prior public stance increases risk: reversing from criticism to praise without credible reason makes self-interest visible.
- Favored intermediaries may punish excessive praise if it makes them look like they are manipulating the ruler.
- Workplace analogies are useful only when they preserve the core hierarchy: not every recommendation is overreach, but unauthorized bargaining over top posts is.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not condemn all recommendations, sponsorship, or advocacy for appointments. Its boundary is the source's bundled exchange: people without authority appear to trade favor and personnel outcomes while bypassing the ruler's decision right. The modern workplace analogy is interpretive; the historical legal outcome remains source-scoped to Hanji 479.
