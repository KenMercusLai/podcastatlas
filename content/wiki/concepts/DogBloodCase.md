## Definition

Dog-Blood Case / 狗血案 names the 3 CE Wang Mang-era incident in which [[LuKuanDogBloodCase|吕宽]] spilled dog blood at [[WangMang|王莽]]'s gate as part of a failed attempt by [[WangYuXinPrince|王宇]] and [[WuZhangWesternHan|吴章]] to frighten Wang Mang into allowing [[EmpressDowagerWeiPingdi|卫太后]] access to [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]].

## Current Synthesis

The case is a small operational failure with large political consequences. The grievance begins with maternal access: Wang Mang blocks Pingdi's Wei birth relatives from entering Chang'an, and Wang Yu fears direct remonstrance. A deferential Wei Taihou memorial fails because Wang Mang substitutes material grants for access. Wu Zhang then proposes a "变怪" tactic that treats Wang Mang's fear of spirits and signs as a pressure point.

The tactic misreads the regime. Wang Mang already operates in a political world where signs, portents, and ritual claims matter, so a crude staged anomaly is easy to convert into guilt. Once Lu Kuan is discovered, the case reaches Wang Yu, destroys Wu Zhang, harms the Wei clan, and lets Wang Mang turn family punishment into public virtue through "大义灭亲." Hanji 617 adds the next step: that virtue language becomes authorization for [[CaseExpansionPoliticalPurge|案件扩大化政治清洗]], drawing [[JingwuPrincessWesternHan|靖武公主]], [[LiuLiWesternHan|梁王刘丽 / 刘立]], Wang-family relatives, marquises, senior officials, and hundreds of elite figures into a year-long purge.

As a concept, the dog-blood case connects [[AuspiciousOmenPolitics|祥瑞政治]] to [[WuguPoliticalPanic|巫蛊政治恐慌]] and then to case-expansion purge. It is not a settled proof that all participants intended curse magic. Its importance is that staged hidden ritual action could be read inside the same danger field as厌胜 or wugu and then amplified into state violence through elite relationship networks.

## Key Claims

- Maternal-family exclusion, not dog blood by itself, is the case's political trigger.
- Indirect persuasion becomes dangerous when direct remonstrance is too risky.
- Staged anomaly tactics can backfire when the target already controls sign interpretation.
- Wugu-adjacent fear lets a symbolic act become a purge trigger.
- Wang Mang converts the punishment of his own son into moral-political capital.
- Hanji 617 shows that this moral-political capital then authorizes wider purge against indirectly connected royal, Wang-family, and ministerial figures.
- The case weakens the Wei consort-kin network and clarifies the later memory of Wang Yu's imprisonment.

## Evidence

- Blocked access and first memorial: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-you-xinji-de-ren-you-duo-kepa-lnhkk70905eqg4lqwh3mp2sxzamc|Hanji 616]] says Wang Yu wants to help Wei Taihou reach Pingdi, first by arranging a deferential memorial.
- Failed compensation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-you-xinji-de-ren-you-duo-kepa-lnhkk70905eqg4lqwh3mp2sxzamc|Hanji 616]] says Wang Mang increases Wei Taihou's fief and gifts but still blocks her Chang'an access.
- Staged anomaly: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-you-xinji-de-ren-you-duo-kepa-lnhkk70905eqg4lqwh3mp2sxzamc|Hanji 616]] says Wu Zhang proposes a "变怪" to frighten Wang Mang and Lu Kuan spills dog blood at Wang Mang's gate.
- Punishment chain: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-616-you-xinji-de-ren-you-duo-kepa-lnhkk70905eqg4lqwh3mp2sxzamc|Hanji 616]] records Lu Kuan's death, Wang Yu's forced death, Lu Yan's imprisonment and postpartum execution order, Wu Zhang's execution, student禁锢, and Wei-clan destruction.
- Moral authorization: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-617-wangmang-dusha-gongzhu-lishi-shimo-lqvzejfm1sd8k0amporgi_mqhsrd|Hanji 617]] says Wang Mang compares his handling of Wang Yu to Zhougong's suppression of disorder and receives an edict praising him for placing state hierarchy above kinship.
- Expanded purge: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-617-wangmang-dusha-gongzhu-lishi-shimo-lqvzejfm1sd8k0amporgi_mqhsrd|Hanji 617]] says the case expands to Jingwu Princess, Liang King Liu Li/Liu Li, Wang-family figures, ministers, and hundreds of elite victims over more than a year.
- Later household memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-634-huozai-nanren-yingzi-beihou-de-ta-can-can-can-lm3wub0l70yxwwyvwi15j93i9lwk|Hanji 634]] later refers back to Wang Yu's earlier imprisonment and his wife's prison death.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source set does not prove that the act was a real curse or that all participants understood it as wugu in a strict legal sense. It supports a narrower claim: in a palace environment already sensitive to spirits, anomalies, and hidden ritual harm, dog blood at the regent's gate could be treated as grave political danger. Hanji 617 supports the purge-expansion aftermath but keeps Jingwu Princess's full biography, Xue Kuang's exact conduct, Wang Li and Wang Ren's actual case connection, victim counts, and Wang Mang's inner motives source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 617 as the purge-expansion aftermath, connecting the dog-blood case to Zhougong-style moral authorization, Jingwu Princess's forced poisoning, and wider elite terror.
- Preserved Hanji 616 as the origin case around Wei maternal access, staged anomaly, wugu-adjacent panic, and Wang Mang's family-punishment performance.
