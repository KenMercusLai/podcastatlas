# Omen Countermeasure Mobility / 游徙避命

Omen countermeasure mobility / 游徙避命 is the Qinji 126 part 2 pattern in which a death omen is answered through movement. After "祖龙死" makes [[QinLateOmenCrisis|秦始皇晚年异象危机]] personal, [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]] consults divination and receives the result "游徙吉." The episode reads that as a recommendation to move rather than stay fixed under the predicted fate.

The concept has two linked forms in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-2-ln15p5qb7lc20goni1ibcpmn3wca|Qinji 126 part 2]]. One is population movement: Qin Shi Huang sends thirty thousand households toward the northern frontier around 北河 and 榆中, compensating them with a one-rank promotion. The other is ruler movement: the following year he begins his fifth and final tour, which the episode reads as an attempt to change qi or timing rather than only a display of rule.

This concept is a subcase of [[ProphecyTriggeredPolicy|预言触发政策]], but it differs from the "亡秦者胡" branch. There, a prophecy identifies an external danger and helps frame a military campaign. Here, the danger is the emperor's own death, so the response becomes itinerary, migration, and symbolic motion. It also overlaps with [[QinFrontierBufferManagement|秦国后方边患管理]] because the population movement has a frontier-settlement effect even if the immediate narrative motive is omen avoidance.

## Key Claims
- Movement can become a ritual-political countermeasure when a ruler interprets a prophecy as personally threatening.
- The same divination result can generate both state population transfer and ruler itinerary change.
- Omen-driven movement may still have practical state effects, such as frontier settlement and rank incentives.
- The concept should remain source-scoped because the episode reconstructs Qin Shi Huang's motive from transmitted omen narrative and [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]], not from direct access to his psychology.
