# Omen Countermeasure Mobility / 游徙避命

## Definition
Omen countermeasure mobility / 游徙避命 is the use of movement—of populations, rulers, or itineraries—as a ritual-political response to a threatening prophecy. In the Qin case, the divination result "游徙吉" is interpreted as a way to act against the personal death warning "祖龙死."

## Current Synthesis
The concept has two linked forms. Qin Shi Huang first moves thirty thousand households toward the northern frontier, joining fate avoidance to settlement and rank incentives. He then begins his fifth and final tour, where movement becomes inseparable from imperial display, sacrifice, omen interpretation, and the hope of changing fate. Qinji 127-1 confirms the tour sequence but also establishes the limit of the strategy: the emperor's route ends in grave illness and death at [[Shaqiu|沙丘]]. Mobility can reorganize people and political theater, but the source does not show it overcoming mortality or stabilizing succession.

## Key Claims
- A ruler can translate a personal omen into state movement rather than treating it as passive prediction.
- The same divinatory instruction can generate both coerced population relocation and ruler travel.
- Omen-driven mobility can have practical frontier and administrative effects even when its stated narrative motive is fate avoidance.
- The final tour fuses religious countermeasure, imperial inspection, sacrifice, and political performance.
- The Shaqiu outcome exposes the strategy's limit: movement does not produce a durable succession settlement or avert the foretold death.

## Evidence
- Divination and dual movement: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-126-qinshihuang-siyin-zhimi-2-ln15p5qb7lc20goni1ibcpmn3wca|Qinji 126 part 2]] connects "祖龙死" and "游徙吉" to the movement of households and the launch of the final tour.
- Tour implementation and limit: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-1-jiemi-qinshihuang-weili-chujun-shi-shenme-yuanyin-lniz32t2r2d3thgqnbfarrqeq2a2|Qinji 127-1]] follows the route through mountain-and-river sacrifices, illness, and Qin Shi Huang's death at Shaqiu.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources reconstruct motive from transmitted omen narratives, divination, and the Zhao Zheng Shu context; they do not provide direct access to Qin Shi Huang's psychology. Imperial tours also had independent political, administrative, and ceremonial purposes, so fate avoidance should not be treated as the sole cause. The relocation's practical frontier effects do not make its human costs or omen rationale independently verified.

## What Changed
- Added the actual final-tour sequence as evidence that the mobility response was implemented beyond the initial divination.
- Added the conceptual endpoint: the tour ends in death and an exposed succession gap.
- Distinguished the strategy's practical state effects from its unproven fate-altering aim.
