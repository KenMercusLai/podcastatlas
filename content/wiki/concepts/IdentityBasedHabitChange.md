# Identity-Based Habit Change

## Definition
Identity-based habit change is the use of repeated action as evidence for a chosen, revisable self-concept, so that behavior and identity reinforce one another without treating either as fixed essence.

## Current Synthesis
[[best-ways-to-build-better-habits-break-bad-ones-james-clear-scim2778708637]] describes each repetition as a vote for the kind of person someone wants to become. Identity can stabilize action when momentary motivation varies because the question shifts from one isolated outcome toward what a repeated choice says about the person being practiced.

The same source supplies an important limit. Athlete, soldier, CEO, founder, parent, or author identities can become painful or restrictive when a role ends. Useful traits can be carried into the next season without preserving every behavior or making one public achievement the whole self. Identity therefore works best as accumulated, defeasible evidence rather than a purity test.

## Key Claims
- Repeated actions provide stronger identity evidence than aspiration or self-description alone.
- Identity can support consistency by connecting a small action with a broader direction of becoming.
- One action is only one vote; a lapse need not erase the larger evidence base or define the whole person.
- Social groups influence identity by making some behaviors normal, admired, embarrassing, or costly.
- A past role can be translated through transferable traits rather than copied unchanged into a new season.
- Identity should remain revisable when health, responsibilities, values, or evidence change.

## Evidence
- Votes for identity - [[best-ways-to-build-better-habits-break-bad-ones-james-clear-scim2778708637]] presents repeated actions as evidence for the person someone wants to become.
- Motivation boundary - [[best-ways-to-build-better-habits-break-bad-ones-james-clear-scim2778708637]] uses identity as a stabilizer after novelty or motivation becomes unreliable.
- Transition through-lines - [[best-ways-to-build-better-habits-break-bad-ones-james-clear-scim2778708637]] recommends carrying traits from athletics, military service, leadership, entrepreneurship, or parenting into a new role.
- Rigidity warning - [[best-ways-to-build-better-habits-break-bad-ones-james-clear-scim2778708637]] says identity helps early habit formation but can restrict growth when held too tightly.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Identity labels can intensify shame, perfectionism, exclusion, or all-or-nothing thinking when a person misses an action. Behavior is also constrained by health, disability, resources, discrimination, work, family, and environment, so failure to perform does not prove a defective identity. The source does not establish that identity framing is superior to other behavior-change methods or sufficient for clinical conditions.

## What Changed
- Established an identity model based on accumulated action evidence and explicit revisability.
- Added role-transition through-lines and social norms as identity mechanisms.
- Made lapse shame and identity rigidity explicit boundaries.
