# Science Trust Politicization

## Definition
Science trust politicization is the pattern where scientific authority loses credibility because disputed policy judgments are presented as settled science, dissent is coded as anti-science, or grant criteria and public messaging appear tied to political commitments.

## Current Synthesis
The Kratsios interview generalizes the wiki's public-health trust material into a wider science-policy claim. In this account, COVID authority, DEI grant requirements, and climate-emergency language are not isolated disputes; they are examples of institutional science becoming too dogmatic or politically sorted. The current synthesis keeps that claim source-scoped while treating it as important because public trust determines whether science policy can survive democratic contestation.

## Key Claims
- The episode argues that public trust falls when questioning a prominent scientific authority is treated as anti-science rather than as inquiry.
- DEI-related grant criteria are framed as politicization when applicants must signal social or demographic commitments unrelated to the research question.
- Climate debate is presented as a severity and modeling dispute rather than a binary question of whether humans affect climate.
- COVID-era school masking and restriction inconsistencies are used as the main public-health example of institutional overconfidence.
- Trust repair requires separating scientific evidence, policy tradeoffs, political values, and uncertainty instead of collapsing them into one authority claim.

## Evidence
- Anti-science labeling: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-michael-kratsios-trumps-science-agenda-anti-science-claims-faucis-damage-dei-china-42543770]] says people who asked COVID questions or questioned parts of IPCC climate reports were criticized as anti-science.
- DEI politicization: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-michael-kratsios-trumps-science-agenda-anti-science-claims-faucis-damage-dei-china-42543770]] argues that required DEI factors in grant applications are not gold-standard science.
- Climate framing: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-michael-kratsios-trumps-science-agenda-anti-science-claims-faucis-damage-dei-china-42543770]] accepts human CO2 contribution while disputing climate-emergency rhetoric and the use of RCP 8.5.
- COVID trust: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-michael-kratsios-trumps-science-agenda-anti-science-claims-faucis-damage-dei-china-42543770]] uses school masking, protest restrictions, hospital visits, and Fauci criticism as trust-loss examples.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is built from a single administration-friendly interview. It does not independently evaluate the empirical merits of DEI research, IPCC scenarios, COVID interventions, or Fauci's career; it records the episode's trust-politics mechanism and the need to keep scientific evidence distinct from political messaging.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to generalize public-health trust repair into broader science-policy trust.
- Added DEI, climate, and COVID as one source-scoped politicization cluster rather than treating them as resolved factual disputes.
