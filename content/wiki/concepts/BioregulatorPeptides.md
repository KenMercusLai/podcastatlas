# Bioregulator Peptides

## Definition
Bioregulator peptides are the episode's category for small peptides such as pinealon and epithalon, discussed through Soviet-era research, sleep or cognition anecdotes, longevity claims, and unresolved receptor or U.S.-standard evidence.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents bioregulators as intriguing but evidence-constrained. Pinealon is described as the tripeptide EDR with no known receptor and a Soviet-era research lineage. Huberman reports increased REM sleep from pinealon, while Bakri says dose and timing may matter for sedation, cognition, or performance. Those reports create hypotheses, not protocols.

Epithalon is treated as the more longevity-salient branch. Bakri discusses Russian nursing-home data involving epithelamine and thymic peptides and reports mortality signals, while Huberman connects epithalon to DNA repair and retinal or optic-nerve-related neurodegeneration interest. The page keeps those claims source-scoped because the strongest evidence cited is not presented as U.S.-gold-standard clinical proof.

## Key Claims
- Pinealon and epithalon sit outside the clearer receptor-defined peptide category in the episode.
- Soviet-era and Russian literature can make the compounds worth investigating without settling efficacy or safety for broad users.
- Sleep, cognition, sedation, DNA-repair, longevity, and retinal or neurodegeneration claims remain hypothesis-generating.
- Dose, timing, and formulation may matter, but the source does not establish a validated protocol.
- Bioregulator enthusiasm should be separated from regulated peptide-drug evidence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page should not be read as validating pinealon or epithalon for sleep, cognition, longevity, cancer prevention, immune function, DNA repair, or neurodegeneration. The source supplies mechanistic interest and caution, not a clinical recommendation.
