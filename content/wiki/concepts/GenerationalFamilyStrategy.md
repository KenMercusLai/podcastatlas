# Generational Family Strategy / 家族代际战略

Generational family strategy / 家族代际战略 is the frame [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-1-lq3t7lwpmq1oyb8zmmmn2tywnu8d]] draws from [[HanState|韩国]]'s destruction of [[ZhengState|郑国]]. The episode argues that major early Warring States outcomes should sometimes be read at the lineage scale rather than as one ruler's isolated achievement.

In the source, the Han case is a slow "ants eating an elephant" pattern. [[HanKangzi|韩康子]], [[HanWuzi|韩武子]], [[HanJinghou|韩景侯]], [[HanLiehou|韩烈侯]], [[HanWenhou|韩文侯]], and [[HanAihou|韩哀侯]] keep pressure on Zheng through war, succession, and [[StrategicCapitalRelocation|capital relocation]] until absorption becomes possible.

## Key Claims
- A ruling lineage can preserve a strategic direction across several generations even when individual rulers differ.
- Slow accumulation can matter more than a single decisive event in explaining state expansion.
- The source links Han's conquest of Zheng to other long-horizon family projects such as the three families' partition of [[JinState|晋国]] and [[TianHe|田和]]'s line replacing Jiang-line [[QiState|齐国]].
- Reading at the family scale should not erase geography; it works here because Han's line repeatedly moves closer to Zheng.
