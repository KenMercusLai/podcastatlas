# Competitive Wine Blind Tasting / 竞技葡萄酒盲品

## Definition

Competitive wine blind tasting is a scored practice in which participants infer grape variety, country, region, vintage, or related attributes from controlled sensory evidence while labels, bottle shapes, people, and other outside clues are withheld.

## Current Synthesis

The source separates competition from informal blind tasting. Social tasting can prioritize conversation, pleasure, and learning, while competition supplies fixed answers, point weights, time pressure, rankings, and deliberate control of external cues. Success depends less on recognizing one bottle than on joining visual, aromatic, structural, geographic, varietal, and vintage evidence into a narrowing classification process.

Training is both sensory and organizational. Repeated comparison builds [[TasteTraining|sensory vocabulary]]; representative samples improve transfer; controlled serving reduces accidental clues; feedback calibrates confidence; and event rules shape preparation. In team formats, four people must translate different reasoning styles into a shared language and one submitted answer.

## Key Claims

- Competitive blind tasting is classification under uncertainty, not simple bottle memorization.
- Removing labels is insufficient if bottle shape, food, smoke, pourer identity, facial reaction, or sponsor context leaks information.
- Stable improvement depends on regular, targeted comparison and feedback more than on a fixed gift.
- Representative samples and event-specific preparation matter because no competitor can memorize the full wine universe.
- Variety usually carries more score weight than vintage in the formats described.
- Team success requires shared vocabulary, calibrated confidence, and resolution of divergent judgments.
- Blindness reduces prestige cues but does not make evaluation perfectly objective.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source does not quantify training effects, talent, inter-rater reliability, sample representativeness, or the predictive value of sponsor information. A correct answer can reflect sensory evidence, learned priors, event strategy, or residual leakage. Competition rewards typicity, while consumer or critic evaluation may reward quality, pleasure, originality, or aging potential instead.
