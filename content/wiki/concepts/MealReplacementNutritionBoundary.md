# Meal Replacement Nutrition Boundary / 代餐营养边界

## Definition
Meal replacement nutrition boundary is the distinction between filling a nutrient gap and replacing an actual meal with a product whose energy, protein, carbohydrate, fiber, micronutrient, and satiety profile must be checked.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats meal replacements as a market category that can easily be confused with protein powder, collagen, vegetable powder, cereal bars, or outdoor energy products. The decision hinge is whether a product is replacing a meal, supplementing a missing nutrient, or solving an abnormal scenario such as travel, hiking, camping, or temporary access constraints.

This makes label reading central. A meal replacement is not safer because it looks lighter, has "zero sugar" language, or is sold in health packaging. Some powders, bars, cereal products, or energy bars can be calorie-dense, carbohydrate-heavy, or poorly suited to blood-sugar control. The source therefore frames meal replacement as occasional context support rather than a routine substitute for ordinary meals.

## Key Claims
- Meal replacement products should not be treated as interchangeable with protein powder or collagen because their purposes and nutrient profiles differ.
- Replacing a meal requires checking energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat, fiber, micronutrients, and satiety rather than only checking one headline nutrient.
- "Zero sugar" or healthy-looking packaging does not prove low carbohydrate, low energy, or suitability for blood-sugar control.
- Energy bars and meal bars may be designed for outdoor or high-demand scenarios and can be counterproductive as casual snacks.
- Vegetable powders and meal replacements can help in abnormal travel or outdoor contexts but should not become default substitutes for regular meals.
- Collagen is protein after digestion, but the source does not treat it as guaranteed face, skin, or joint rebuilding.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide a formal standard, full product comparison, or individualized meal-replacement plan. The concept is a public-literacy boundary: read the label, ask what meal or nutrient gap the product is replacing, and keep diabetes, kidney disease, pregnancy, eating disorders, weight-management distress, and clinical nutrition inside qualified care.
