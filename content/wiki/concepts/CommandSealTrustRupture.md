# 将印信任破裂 / Command-Seal Trust Rupture

将印信任破裂 / command-seal trust rupture is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-135-mingyun-tai-shenqi-cong-yufu-zuodao-wang-lrh1lhuxxikx4m6prpi6qm0jcafb|Qinji 135]] draws from [[ZhangEr|张耳]] and [[ChenYu|陈馀]] after [[JuluBattle|巨鹿之战]]. A relationship once described as close as father and son collapses when resentment over failed rescue turns into suspicion, then into a struggle over the general's seal and troops.

The episode's sequence matters. Zhang Er first accuses Chen Yu of not rescuing him from [[Julu|巨鹿]] and then doubts Chen Yu's explanation that Zhang Yan and Chen Ze died with the five thousand troops Chen Yu gave them. Chen Yu, enraged by the accusation, throws his general's seal to Zhang Er as proof that he is not clinging to command.

The seal gesture backfires. Zhang Er's retainers advise him to accept what has been offered, and when Chen Yu leaves briefly, Zhang Er takes the seal and the army. Chen Yu returns to find both command symbol and troops gone, then leaves with only about one hundred followers. The old bond is not merely emotionally damaged; command control has changed hands.

This differs from [[OathBoundCommandTrust|盟誓约束下的统帅信任]], where a public commitment protects a commander from later abandonment. Here the prior bond lacks a working constraint. Once accusation enters the scene, the symbol that should prove sincerity becomes the instrument of dispossession.

## Key Claims
- Trust rupture in wartime can become irreversible when it attaches to command symbols and troop ownership.
- A gesture meant to prove innocence can become dangerous if the other side treats it as an actual transfer of authority.
- Old affection or sworn friendship cannot substitute for clear command boundaries after a crisis.
- The episode turns Chen Yu's Julu caution into a post-battle command conflict: even if his military caution had reasons, Zhang Er experiences it as betrayal.
