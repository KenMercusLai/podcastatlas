## Definition

[[NonRetaliatoryAdversityConduct|逆境中的不迁怒]] is the discipline of not redirecting pain, criticism, or loss toward uninvolved people and of retaining the ability to identify value in an opponent's position.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1161 defines the pattern negatively through a wolf whose rage at a thorn escalates the injury, then positively through [[LuXunDongWu|陆逊]]. Lu Xun can acknowledge that military action burdens civilians, distinguish [[ChunyuShiLateHan|淳于式]]'s complaint from the complainant's usefulness, and recommend him. The concept therefore combines emotional restraint with information preservation and public-interest judgment; it does not require passivity, denial of harm, or agreement with every criticism.

A contemporary negative case shows [[IrvGotti]] receiving a racist provocation from one executive and, before cooling down, directing the resulting anger toward [[JenniferLopez]] during an unrelated interview. His later accountability clarifies the concept's boundary: identifying the source of anger explains the transfer but does not excuse the person who imposed its cost on someone else.

## Key Claims

- Secondary retaliation can magnify an initial injury and spread its cost to others.
- Self-command under pain matters more than politeness displayed only in favorable conditions.
- Criticism can contain useful institutional information even when personal rivalry is present.
- Non-retaliation is compatible with acknowledging conflict, burden, and different roles.
- Recommending a critic can serve the wider organization when the critic protects a neglected interest.
- Accountability after displacement requires naming both the real trigger and the uninvolved person who bore its cost.

## Evidence

Escalation through anger:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1161-caocao-wei-shenme-yao-cisi-erxifu-lhv36g01hxpxaabh77bbyosdjzfr|Hanji 1161]] uses the wolf-and-thorn fable to show anger becoming more destructive than the initiating wound.

Non-retaliatory personnel judgment:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1161-caocao-wei-shenme-yao-cisi-erxifu-lhv36g01hxpxaabh77bbyosdjzfr|Hanji 1161]] says Lu Xun acknowledges civilian disruption, credits Chunyu Shi's contribution, and recommends him after the complaint.

Displaced anger and lasting harm:
- [[why-would-i-do-that-to-jennifer-lopez-the-mistakes-series-why-would-i-do-that-to-jennifer-lopez-the-mistakes-series]] reports that Gotti's interview followed immediately after a racist confrontation and that his redirected outburst permanently damaged his relationship with Lopez.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The wolf story is a moral fable, not behavioral evidence. Lu Xun's reported response is one episode-attributed case and does not prove the absence of self-interest, conflict, or coercion elsewhere. The Gotti account is retrospective, and the exact argument, emotional state, intoxication, interview wording, and causal sequence are not independently verified. Non-retaliation should not be used to demand endurance of abuse, suppress legitimate anger, excuse unsafe conditions, or replace accountability and protective boundaries.
