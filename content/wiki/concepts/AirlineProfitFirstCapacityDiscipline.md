# Airline Profit-First Capacity Discipline

## Definition
Airline profit-first capacity discipline is the choice to protect yield and contribution margin by limiting discounts, leaving some seats unsold, or slowing expansion instead of maximizing passenger count or load factor at any price.

## Current Synthesis
The source contrasts seasonal Chinese route reallocation with U.S. carriers' reported resistance to heavy discounting after fuel costs rose. Both cases treat capacity as an economic portfolio: place aircraft where timing and demand support returns, and reassess growth when filling incremental seats would dilute margin.

## Key Claims
- Full aircraft are not automatically more profitable when the marginal fare fails to cover incremental or opportunity cost.
- Route scheduling can pursue business demand and seasonal tourism without implying indiscriminate network expansion.
- Higher fuel cost strengthens the case for yield protection but can also reduce consumer access.
- Premium products and passenger mix become more important when airlines prioritize revenue per traveler over raw volume.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The reported five-airline revenue and traffic comparison does not isolate fares, mix, ancillary revenue, capacity, or route composition.
- Persistent capacity restraint may weaken [[AirlineMarketPriceDiscipline]] and raise fares for price-sensitive travelers.
