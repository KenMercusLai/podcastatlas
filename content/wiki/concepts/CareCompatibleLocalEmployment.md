# Care-Compatible Local Employment / 照护兼容型本地就业

## Definition
[[CareCompatibleLocalEmployment]] is paid work whose location, schedule, leave, and predictability allow workers to remain responsible for children or other dependents.

## Current Synthesis
In Yonghe, annotation's value came from more than wages. Office conditions, weekends and holidays, and relatively regular hours allowed mothers to collect children and remain in the county, while local retail and food-service work often involved lower pay, rotating shifts, or nights.

Care compatibility can expand real choice, but it can also become an employer-selection mechanism. Workers whose responsibilities prevent migration may be stable and less likely to quit, increasing their attractiveness to firms while weakening their alternatives when a project ends.

## Key Claims
- Schedule and location can be as important as hourly or monthly pay.
- Care constraints segment labor markets and can trap qualified workers in narrow local options.
- A stable maternal workforce may benefit employers as well as workers.
- Job loss has asymmetric effects when workers cannot migrate or accept nights and rotating shifts.
- Employment policy should evaluate childcare, scheduling, portability, and transition support alongside job counts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode does not compare household arrangements, childcare availability, partner income, commuting, or worker preferences across a representative sample. It shows why the job fit several interviewees; it does not imply that all mothers share identical constraints or that care-compatible work is inherently exploitative.

## What Changed
- Added schedule and location compatibility as a distinct dimension of job quality.
- Added the dual effect by which care constraints produce both employment fit and reduced exit options.
