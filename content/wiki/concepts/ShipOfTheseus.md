# Ship of Theseus

## Definition

The Ship of Theseus is a persistence problem: if an object's components are replaced one at a time until none of the originals remain, what makes the resulting object the same object, a different one, or an unresolved case?

## Current Synthesis

Across the bounded sources, the puzzle is most useful as a diagnostic rather than a universal answer. It exposes competing criteria for identity—material composition, continuity through time, function, name, ownership, or organized relations—and forces an argument to state which criterion matters in context.

[[introducing-revisionist-history-season-two]] links the puzzle to [[Heraclitus]]' river and uses it as a tentative frame for stories about change. The golf episodes make the stakes legal and fiscal: a club can retain one legal identity while every member-owner turns over. The [[KimiK3|Kimi K3]] discussion applies the metaphor technologically, asking when a system with heavily replaced attention mechanisms should still be called a Transformer.

## Key Claims

- Gradual replacement can preserve temporal continuity while eliminating original components.
- Different identity criteria can yield different but internally coherent answers.
- The puzzle makes hidden continuity rules visible but does not choose the correct rule for every domain.
- Legal identity, institutional ownership, and technical taxonomy may each require different persistence criteria.
- A metaphor of replacement can clarify an argument without proving that the compared cases share one mechanism.

### Technical taxonomy

- [[xiangjie-kimi-k3-qiangdao-chongji-anthropic-guzhi-de-moxing-shenmeyang-1-177-1]] calls modern attention a Ship of Theseus after repeated architectural substitutions, using the puzzle to challenge a simple “plain Transformer” label.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The puzzle does not establish one metaphysical verdict, legal standard, or technical taxonomy. The two golf sources share a reporting lineage; the trailer only proposes a theme; and the K3 comparison is an expert metaphor rather than a formal classification rule. Component lists can also understate continuity in function, training lineage, governance, or causal organization, while continuity language can hide changes that matter for accountability or policy.

## What Changed

- Established a cross-domain synthesis separating the puzzle's diagnostic value from any claim that it supplies one universal identity rule.
