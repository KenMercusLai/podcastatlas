# Kleos Heroic Glory / 克莱奥斯英雄声名

## Definition

Kleos heroic glory is the Homeric value of gaining enduring fame through great deeds, especially martial and heroic action.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses kleos to explain the distance between Homeric epic and Nolan's modern moral frame. In the hosts' reading, [[TheOdyssey]] already complicates Iliadic glory by showing [[Achilles]] in the underworld preferring ordinary mortal life to dead renown. Nolan pushes that suspicion further: his film rejects glory-seeking and treats heroic stories themselves as morally unstable after war.

## Key Claims

- Kleos makes heroic action durable by turning deeds into sung or remembered fame.
- The Odyssey can already be read as reflecting on the cost of Iliadic glory rather than simply repeating it.
- Nolan's adaptation is strongly anti-kleos because it treats fame, victory, and war success as morally compromised.
- Deconstructing kleos shifts the return story from heroic restoration toward repentance and survival after violence.
- The concept remains ancient and source-specific; modern anti-war feeling should not be retrojected as Homer's own settled position.

- Glory definition: [[dang-xila-lishi-meiguo-zhengzhi-nuolan-meixue-zai-aodesai-li-da-qilai-gkwrijiov2xkbtdknqthj-ue]] defines kleos as immortal fame won through great heroic deeds.
- Homeric complication: [[dang-xila-lishi-meiguo-zhengzhi-nuolan-meixue-zai-aodesai-li-da-qilai-gkwrijiov2xkbtdknqthj-ue]] points to Achilles' underworld speech as a challenge to simple heroic glory.
- Nolan's rejection: [[dang-xila-lishi-meiguo-zhengzhi-nuolan-meixue-zai-aodesai-li-da-qilai-gkwrijiov2xkbtdknqthj-ue]] says the film opposes achievement, war fame, and the pursuit of glory.
- Return-story shift: [[dang-xila-lishi-meiguo-zhengzhi-nuolan-meixue-zai-aodesai-li-da-qilai-gkwrijiov2xkbtdknqthj-ue]] links anti-glory to Odysseus' decision to leave rather than simply restore rule.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page follows one film-analysis episode and does not settle the full Homeric relationship between kleos, nostos, death, and social honor. It should remain separate from a general theory of all heroic fame.
