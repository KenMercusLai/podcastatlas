# AI Academic Evaluation Signal Collapse

## Definition
AI academic evaluation signal collapse is the loss of usefulness in paper counts, conference submissions, and low-selectivity review when content volume grows faster than qualified attention and formal outputs become easier to generate than genuine understanding.

## Current Synthesis
The source offers a participant's diagnosis rather than a system-wide measurement. It argues that low-quality AI-generated submissions and weak review participation make publication activity less informative for hiring or research judgment. The proposed alternative combines work-sample understanding, coherent research reports, live questioning, problem selection, and judgment by credible peers; this may improve signal but also increases dependence on subjective taste, networks, and evaluator quality.

## Key Claims
- Cheap content generation can increase submission volume without increasing research contribution.
- Paper counts become weaker hiring signals when authorship and review quality are heterogeneous.
- Effective peer evaluation depends on knowledgeable reviewers with sufficient time and research judgment.
- Demonstrated understanding, logic, question answering, and current problem judgment can complement publication records.
- Replacing formal metrics with taste and reputation creates its own bias and access risks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- One researcher's withdrawal from submission and reviewing does not establish field-wide institutional collapse.
- The source supplies no conference statistics, acceptance-quality study, authorship audit, reviewer-performance data, or hiring-outcome comparison.
- Live discussion, reputation, and “taste” can favor insiders, fluent speakers, dominant paradigms, or prestigious networks.
- Publication remains useful evidence when contribution, provenance, replication, and review quality can be assessed directly.

## What Changed
- Established a bounded framework for distinguishing output volume from evaluation signal under AI-assisted research production.
