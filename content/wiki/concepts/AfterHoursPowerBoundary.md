## Definition

下班后权力边界 / After-hours power boundary is the management norm that a leader should treat employee off-work time as power-sensitive, because invitations that look voluntary may be hard to refuse when they come from a boss.

## Current Synthesis

The source develops the concept through [[LuoYonghao|罗永浩]]'s self-critique. Luo says he once often took employees to meals after work, but his wife pointed out that their apparent happiness did not prove they wanted to attend. Because he was the boss, employees might act pleased while feeling unable to say no.

The episode connects this to broader "势利" because unequal treatment is not only about subordinates flattering superiors. It can also be the invisible pressure a superior creates without intending harm. A leader's social needs, loneliness, or habit of building team feeling can consume employee time because rank changes the meaning of an invitation.

## Key Claims

- Boss invitations after work are not neutral social offers because employment power changes refusal costs.
- Employee enthusiasm can be unreliable evidence of consent when the leader controls opportunity, evaluation, or atmosphere.
- Leaders may create pressure without intending exploitation.
- Respecting after-hours boundaries can require the leader to wait for employees to initiate rather than repeatedly inviting them.
- The concept separates genuine workplace warmth from rank-conditioned obligation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not claim all after-work meals are coercive. The source's narrower point is that leaders need to account for the power attached to their invitations and should not treat visible politeness as sufficient evidence of free preference.
