# Airport Approval Airspace Constraint / 机场审批与空域约束

## Definition
Airport approval airspace constraint is the planning problem in which a city's airport ambition depends on central approval, controlled airspace, nearby airport layout, military coordination, and airline route participation.

## Current Synthesis
The Shengdong Jixi roundtable uses Suzhou to show why airport construction cannot be inferred from local GDP alone. Even a wealthy city may struggle to build an airport if nearby airports already serve the region, if airspace is crowded, and if multiple national and military approval layers are required.

The concept also separates hard and soft infrastructure. A city may win permission to build an airport, but it still needs airlines, routes, subsidies or demand, and enough passenger or cargo flow to make the airport function as a network node.

## Key Claims
- Airport construction is constrained by regulatory approval as well as local economic demand.
- Airspace and nearby airport layout can block or delay airport ambitions for otherwise strong cities.
- Chinese airport development involves central agencies and military-related coordination in the source's framing.
- Physical airport construction is insufficient without airline entry, route allocation, and passenger or cargo demand.

## Evidence
- Suzhou case: [[403-cong-bashi-nian-feixing-shi-kan-chengshi-shehui-he-women-ziji-de-gaibian-guotai-hangkong-x-shengdongjixi-53eaa77d]] says Suzhou's lack of airport is linked to nearby Shanghai Hongqiao, Wuxi Shuofang, and complex airspace conditions despite strong economic scale.
- Approval layers: [[403-cong-bashi-nian-feixing-shi-kan-chengshi-shehui-he-women-ziji-de-gaibian-guotai-hangkong-x-shengdongjixi-53eaa77d]] names central approvals involving development, transport, civil aviation, and military-related bodies.
- Soft infrastructure: [[403-cong-bashi-nian-feixing-shi-kan-chengshi-shehui-he-women-ziji-de-gaibian-guotai-hangkong-x-shengdongjixi-53eaa77d]] stresses that airports still need airline entry and route opening after construction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives a high-level public-discussion view, not a legal map of Chinese airport approval procedure. Specific agency names, current approval status, and military airspace details should remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Created a planning concept for airport approval and airspace limitations.
- Distinguished city demand from the institutional ability to create a usable airport.
