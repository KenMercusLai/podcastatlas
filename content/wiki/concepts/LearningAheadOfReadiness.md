# Learning Ahead of Readiness

Learning ahead of readiness is the episode's idea that a person can sometimes begin a role or project before feeling fully prepared, then learn the missing material just ahead of need. In [[ep-3-demystifying-the-imposter-syndrome]], [[StephenMathis|Stephen Mathis]] tells a story about his mother being assigned to teach 18th-century French literature even though she did not already know the subject; the practical lesson was that she could read ahead of the students and still guide them.

The concept does not say every gap can be ignored. It works with [[CapabilityGapSelfDiagnosis]]: if the missing knowledge can be acquired during the task and the risk is manageable, waiting for total readiness may be unnecessary. If the gap is too large or unsafe, the answer is support, scope change, or more preparation.

This is a career-learning counterpart to [[SelfDirectedLearning]]. It turns "I do not know this yet" from a stopping condition into a planning question.

## Key Claims
- Readiness can be built while doing the work, not only before starting it.
- The method depends on a gap being learnable within the available time and risk boundary.
- Learning ahead can reduce [[ImpostorSyndrome|imposter feelings]] by making ignorance ordinary and actionable.
- The practice requires honest pacing rather than pretending the gap does not exist.
