## Definition
Procedureless political purge is the pattern in which a regime removes a defeated power network through criminal punishment without producing visible proof, charge specificity, interrogation, or proportional settlement. The core problem is not that political cleanup is always illegitimate, but that punishment presented as law becomes unstable when it cannot show how guilt was tested.

## Current Synthesis
The Deng case now has two layers. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] shows the purge beginning from succession insecurity: [[WangShengPalaceActorLateHan|Wang Sheng]], [[LiRunLateHan|Li Run]], and [[JiangJingLateHan|Jiang Jing]] slander [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]], and after her death the alleged plan to install [[LiuYiPingyuanWangLateHan|Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi]] becomes the basis for rapid punishment of the [[DengFamilyAnEraLateHan|Deng family]].

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] then supplies the procedural critique. [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] and anti-Deng actors have an intelligible reason to reduce [[OuterRelativeCourtPower|outer-relative power]] after a regency, but [[ZhuChongLateHan|Zhu Chong]] argues that the actual process rests on one-sided palace testimony, no confrontation, unclear evidence, and excessive household destruction.

The result is a partial legitimacy failure. The purge is not overturned, but public mourning and Zhu Chong's coffin memorial force Emperor An to limit its aftermath by permitting burial and survivor return. The same sequence shows a second failure: the Deng vacuum is filled by Geng, Song, Yan, eunuch, wet-nurse, and favorite networks, so procedureless cleanup does not restore public appointment order.

## Key Claims
- Political cleanup after a regency can be structurally understandable while still failing as lawful punishment.
- Succession-rumor accusations are especially dangerous because the alleged crime threatens the ruler's throne before evidence has been tested.
- Evidence defects become politically dangerous when the accused household is punished through death, burial disruption, confiscation, and kinship ruin.
- Public remonstrance can force limited correction without producing full rehabilitation.
- Procedureless purge is especially unstable when the winners immediately reproduce similar access problems through new kin and favorites.
- The concept links legal-process critique to court-feedback failure: evidence problems surface only after costly, symbolic, and risky protest.

## Evidence
- Succession-rumor trigger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] says the anti-Deng charge centers on obtaining old deposed-emperor files and installing Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi.
- First punishment wave: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-794-yidai-nvjun-dengsui-qushi-hou-jing-quanjia-zaoyang-llmlhabdd09opca6-pr80q4mfoxi|Hanji 794]] reports abolished titles, office removals, confiscation, exile, pressured suicides, and starvation deaths before any later public correction.
- Deng purge as defective adjudication: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] reports that Zhu Chong challenges the anti-Deng case because it relies on palace testimony without confrontation or clear proof.
- Household punishment and ritual damage: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] says multiple Deng family members die and burial/survivor arrangements become part of the remonstrance.
- Partial correction under pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] links public mourning and Zhu Chong's memorial to Emperor An's limited retreat.
- New favorite replacement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-795-dengjia-miewang-hou-weihe-juchao-wei-tamen-mingyuan-lqvmbqrrookphb4becaqbp7h-wi1|Hanji 795]] shows Geng, Song, Yan, Jiang Jing, Li Run, Wang Sheng, Bo Rong, and Fan Feng gaining access after the Deng purge.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current evidence is one connected case. It does not prove that every purge lacking transparent process is identical, nor does it show that the Deng household had no political liability at all. Hanji 794 makes the Liu Yi replacement plan an accusation with consequences, not a settled fact; Hanji 795 makes Zhu Chong's defense politically and procedurally important without requiring full exoneration. The concept should be applied narrowly: the marker is the combination of power-transfer punishment, weak visible evidence, blocked confrontation, and disproportionate kinship or ritual consequences.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 794 as the first punishment-wave layer before Zhu Chong's Hanji 795 procedural critique.
- Added succession-rumor danger around Pingyuan Wang Liu Yi as a trigger for procedureless punishment.
- Preserved Hanji 795's judgment that public remonstrance wins partial correction but not full rehabilitation.
