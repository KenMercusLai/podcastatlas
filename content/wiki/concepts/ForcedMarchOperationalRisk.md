## Definition

Forced-march operational risk / 急行军作战风险 is the danger that an army gains contact or surprise by moving faster than its command cohesion, supplies, formation, terrain knowledge, or rear security can support. Speed is therefore neither inherently reckless nor inherently superior: its value depends on what is sacrificed, whether the route is passable, and whether the opponent has already shaped the pursuit.

## Current Synthesis

The Maling sources provide the clearest failure mechanism. [[SunBin|孙膑]] exploits [[PangJuan|庞涓]]'s contempt, grievance, and desire for credit through [[DecreasingStovesDeception|减灶诱敌]]. Pang Juan interprets apparent desertion as weakness, leaves the slower main force behind, doubles his pace, and arrives at [[MalingBattle|马陵之战]] under conditions the opponent selected. The march delivers contact but breaks concentration and transfers initiative to the rested defender.

The late-Han evidence broadens the pattern beyond a prepared lure. [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] turns victory at Shimen into an over-deep pursuit and is surrounded for more than two hundred days, while [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]]'s anger-driven advance sacrifices rear security and strategic depth. In both cases, psychological momentum converts movement into exposure.

Hanji 1077 adds the productive side of the same tradeoff. [[GuoJia|郭嘉]] recommends that [[CaoCao|曹操]] shed baggage and accelerate against an allegedly unprepared [[Wuhuan|乌桓]], making surprise the reason for accepting lower logistical resilience. Rain, mud, and a guarded road then immobilize the force. Because the transcript ends at [[TianChou|田畴]]'s appearance, this source establishes the risk exchange but not its later solution: deliberate speed can be rational while remaining acutely dependent on terrain and route intelligence.

## Key Claims

- Speed is operationally useful only while command, supply, formation, route access, and rear security remain adequate to the objective.
- Forced-march failure can be induced by deception, victory momentum, anger, or a commander's own decision to trade baggage resilience for surprise.
- Psychological confidence becomes physical vulnerability when it causes a force to outrun support or accept terrain it has not verified.
- A light, fast force may seize a narrow surprise window, but the decision remains high-risk rather than retroactively safe because it later succeeds.
- The decisive comparison is not fast versus slow in isolation, but the moving force's condition versus the opponent's preparation and control of time and place.
- Route knowledge and contingency capacity determine whether reduced baggage creates usable tempo or a stranded army.

## Evidence

- Deception-driven pursuit: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-19-maling-zhi-zhan-lhbapt09x2ptdqx0uopkp8apbada|Zhouji 19]] defines the long-distance pursuit risk, while [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-20-pangjuan-si-ci-shu-xia-ls-8-3jhz5q1c5ca11mhdzmz4wg4|Zhouji 20]] shows Pang Juan leaving the main force and doubling his pace into Sun Bin's prepared ambush.
- Victory-driven overextension: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-942-yue-youxiu-de-ren-huode-yue-lei-heli-ma-lkwlrcgnt-kxxc2qqz6z6wkg7e5z|Hanji 942]] shows Gongsun Zan pursue beyond support after Shimen and become trapped at Guanzi City.
- Anger-driven loss of depth: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac|Zhouji 51]] shows Chu Huaiwang drive deeper after defeat, stretch supply, and expose the rear to Han and Wei.
- Surprise-driven calculated exposure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1077-caocao-du-shang-quanbu-shenjia-jiusi-yisheng-tu-ge-sha-lp7dgomh7hilbtcw0rmwerjgqgsk|Hanji 1077]] has Guo Jia recommend discarding baggage to surprise Wuhuan, then records rain, mud, and a guarded road stopping the force before the transcript reaches Tian Chou's solution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not imply that rapid movement is usually a mistake. Hanji 1077 supplies a case where speed is deliberately chosen to exploit surprise, and later episodes—not part of this page's new source evidence—continue the campaign successfully. The narrower judgment is that speed transfers risk: less baggage and a lighter column can improve surprise while making route failure, weather, and resupply more consequential. Maling is also an intentionally shaped trap, not proof that every separated mobile force will fail. The dramatic tree inscription and some exact battlefield detail remain source-scoped. Hanji 1077 is incomplete and cannot independently establish Tian Chou's plan or the campaign outcome.
