## Definition
Crisis withdrawal self-protection is the deliberate lowering of exposure or exit from public contention when direct moral action cannot be protected under deteriorating power conditions.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence makes withdrawal one disciplined answer to disorder rather than a synonym for fear. [[ChenXianXinOfficial|陈贤]] withdraws from Wang Mang's usurpation field, orders his sons out of office, and preserves Han ritual and legal memory privately; Yuan Hong withdraws from Yuan-family wealth and visibility; Shentu Pan leaves the capital before party-prohibition repression falls; [[XiaFuLateHan|夏馥]] changes appearance and hides through rough labor; and [[DuGenLateHan|杜根]] avoids old friends so that discovery will not implicate them. Hanji 793 now supplies Du Gen's original An-era case: after direct return-power remonstrance is almost punished to death under [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|Empress Dowager Deng]], he survives through feigned death, hides for fifteen years as a mountain tavern waiter, and returns only when [[EmperorAnOfHan|Emperor An]] begins personal rule.

Hanji 894 adds [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] as a constrained public-intellectual version: he survives because he avoids fierce political speech, grieves the destruction of名士, and turns to closed-door teaching when the court order looks beyond repair. Chen Shi, He Yong, and [[ZhangJianLateHan|张俭]] provide contrasts in which protection comes from reputation, quiet rescue, or flight through networks instead of full exit. The concept qualifies [[PoliticalSurvivalFirst|安全第一政治生存]] by focusing on timing, lowered exposure, and preservation of moral autonomy.

## Key Claims
- Withdrawal is most defensible when direct denunciation lacks power, protection, or timing.
- Timing matters: seeing danger but remaining in the admiration field differs from leaving before crackdown.
- Withdrawal can coexist with moral seriousness and social care; it avoids becoming decoration or casualty for stronger actors.
- Concealment can be active survival work, including disguise, low-status labor, feigned death, and deliberate non-contact.
- Family withdrawal and document preservation can make exit an institutional-memory tactic, not only individual risk reduction.
- Family and livelihood constraints bound the tactic, as Yuan Hong's mother and Shentu Pan's labor show.
- Non-contact can be protective when accepting aid would convert friends, kin, or hosts into collateral targets.

## Evidence
- Direct-speech danger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-896-jiaru-luanshi-jianglin-ni-gai-ruhe-zibao-lsuvt-ksp-gfggc9riqqovjswkg|Hanji 896]] cites Sima Guang's contrast between orderly times for punishing evil and bad times when unsupported public confrontation endangers wider networks.
- Yuan Hong's household withdrawal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-896-jiaru-luanshi-jianglin-ni-gai-ruhe-zibao-lsuvt-ksp-gfggc9riqqovjswkg|Hanji 896]] presents Yuan Hong refusing Yuan-family wealth and living in a doorless courtyard hut for eighteen years.
- Shentu Pan's early exit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-896-jiaru-luanshi-jianglin-ni-gai-ruhe-zibao-lsuvt-ksp-gfggc9riqqovjswkg|Hanji 896]] presents Shentu Pan leaving the capital and surviving by rough labor before repression falls.
- Contrasting tactics: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-896-jiaru-luanshi-jianglin-ni-gai-ruhe-zibao-lsuvt-ksp-gfggc9riqqovjswkg|Hanji 896]] contrasts withdrawal with Chen Shi's courtesy-derived protection and He Yong plus Yuan Shao's quiet rescue network.
- Collateral-avoidance withdrawal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] uses Xia Fu and Du Gen to show hiding, disguise, and refusal of aid as ways to avoid sending disaster toward helpers.
- Original Du Gen case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-793-lishi-zhuming-nvhuang-dengsui-zenme-si-de-lszfgj7waiuusrddzjmv5mbrp1i|Hanji 793]] shows Du Gen surviving a lethal punishment order by feigning death, then hiding for fifteen years in tavern labor before later restoration.
- Chen Xian's family withdrawal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-645-bidu-zhichang-quanmou-mingzhebaoshen-or-niliu-ershang-lvdqcakza0zqextrhympnpswxyjy|Hanji 645]] says Chen Xian retires, refuses renewed summons, and tells his three sons to resign when Wang Mang's court becomes dangerous.
- Chen Xian's memory preservation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-645-bidu-zhichang-quanmou-mingzhebaoshen-or-niliu-ershang-lvdqcakza0zqextrhympnpswxyjy|Hanji 645]] says Chen Xian keeps Han ritual dates and hides Han legal texts in a wall.
- Fugitive contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] contrasts withdrawal with Zhang Jian's flight through host networks, which leads to the Kong-family punishment case.
- Scholar-teacher withdrawal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-894-yi-chang-taowang-yinfa-de-da-tusha-lhulp9hqu07uayyfl3eg-xwrtkeu|Hanji 894]] presents Guo Tai as surviving by guarded speech, then closing his door to teach thousands after recognizing the party-prohibition disaster as a sign of dynastic exhaustion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is not a universal rule against action. Hanji 896's Sima Guang frame allows direct punishment of evil under ordered conditions, and Hanji 895 treats the Kong family's sheltering courage as morally serious rather than foolish. Hanji 793 also shows that Du Gen first acts publicly before withdrawing; the withdrawal becomes justified only after punishment power makes further exposure lethal and collateral-producing. Hanji 645's Chen Xian case is cleaner as regime-exit timing but thinner on later outcome, so the source supports principled withdrawal and preservation more than any direct causal claim about restoration.
