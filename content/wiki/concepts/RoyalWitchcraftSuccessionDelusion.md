## Definition

Royal witchcraft succession delusion / 宗室巫术夺位幻想 is the pattern in which a royal kinsman treats ritual harm, curses, or occult manipulation as a substitute for open succession struggle, then misreads unrelated political outcomes as confirmation that the hidden method works.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-456-1-yi-ming-ji-huangwei-jiemi-hanwudi-zui-qipa-erzi-ltdqudgpmgkulrhhxkzvjsvgjlo5|Hanji 456-1]] supplies the pattern through [[LiuXuGuanglingKingWesternHan|广陵王刘须]]. Liu Xu wants the throne but lacks the will to claim it openly. He therefore employs a witch to curse successive emperors or emperor-candidates. Liu Fuling's death and Liu He's deposition become false evidence in his mind, turning coincidence into a reinforced political superstition. The pattern becomes most dangerous when resentment joins the fantasy: after family punishment and territorial loss, Liu Xu returns to curses against [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]], and the later investigation pushes him into killing witnesses and suicide.

## Key Claims

- The pattern is a succession behavior, not only a superstition story: the occult action substitutes for direct claim-making.
- Confirmation bias is central because ordinary dynastic events are misread as proof that ritual harm caused political outcomes.
- The secrecy of the method creates evidence-suppression pressure once investigation begins.
- The pattern can shift from ambition to revenge when punishment, demotion, or fief loss turns desire into resentment.
- In the Liu Xu case, the final harm is less the curse itself than the poisoning, destruction of evidence, and suicide triggered by exposure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is currently source-bounded to Liu Xu. It should not be generalized to all Han witchcraft cases, all royal-kin treason cases, or the earlier witchcraft disaster without additional sources. The concept uses "delusion" descriptively for the source's pattern of false causal belief, not as a clinical diagnosis.
