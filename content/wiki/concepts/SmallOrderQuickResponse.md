# Small-Order Quick Response / 小单快反

Small-order quick response / 小单快反 is the manufacturing pattern where firms accept smaller and more varied orders, insert them into production quickly, and respond to demand signals before the market window closes. In [[ep271-tanfang-rouxing-zhizao-gongchang-shi-shui-rang-ni-xiangyao-jiu-neng-maidao-lqyrioptggnbb8hl-llej0-5ow0v]], [[WuLiwei|吴立伟]] uses apparel and [[YagorGroup|雅戈尔]] to explain why Chinese flexible-manufacturing debates often pass through clothing supply chains and e-commerce pressure.

The concept also extends beyond apparel. [[ShenzhouInternational|申洲国际]]'s championship-jersey case shows burst response to sports events, while [[DeliGroup|得力]]'s stationery factories show small runs and IP-linked variants moving through order scheduling, material checks, and production status systems. In all cases, speed is useful only if quality, material coordination, and delivery remain controlled.

## Key Claims
- Small-order quick response reduces the risk of overproducing a forecasted hit and missing a real-time demand signal.
- It requires production scheduling, material visibility, work-station allocation, and fast switching, not just workers moving faster.
- The pattern is especially valuable when demand is volatile, seasonal, IP-driven, event-driven, or customized.
- It can raise manufacturing value added, but it also raises management and system requirements.
- For smaller manufacturers, basic scale, stable quality, and low cost may still be more viable than building high-cost quick-response infrastructure.
