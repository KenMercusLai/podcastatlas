# Work From Home Evidence

## Definition
Work from home evidence is the empirical evaluation of remote, hybrid, and in-person arrangements by job type, workflow, organizational outcome, worker context, and household effect rather than by one universal workplace rule.

## Current Synthesis
The job-specific frame resists a universal location rule: some roles work better in person, many fit hybrid arrangements, and some, including call centers in [[would-you-trust-an-economist-with-your-economy]]'s account of [[NickBloom]], can perform better remotely. The management problem is evidentiary and political because executives may overweight the conditions under which they personally succeeded or use office mandates for objectives beyond productivity.

The household branch adds family logistics as an outcome. Bloom reports in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] that couples in which both partners work from home at least one day per week have more children on average, especially additional children, possibly because flexibility eases childcare and logistics. A narrower working paper points in the opposite direction, and selection into remote-compatible work remains unresolved.

## Key Claims
- Remote-work performance depends on the job and coordination design.
- Hybrid work is an evidence-bearing category, not merely a rhetorical compromise.
- Productivity is not the only outcome; satisfaction, attrition, family logistics, and fertility may also matter.
- Executive intuition can overweight office-formed career experience.
- Remote-work fertility associations do not by themselves establish causation.
- Evidence persuasion improves when experts listen to objections rather than treating disagreement with contempt.

## Evidence
- Job heterogeneity: [[would-you-trust-an-economist-with-your-economy]] presents Bloom's in-person, hybrid, and fully remote distinctions.
- Management interpretation: [[would-you-trust-an-economist-with-your-economy]] places the evidence inside [[EconomistTrustCrisis]] and [[ExpertTrustRepair]].
- Household association: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] reports larger average family size when both partners work remotely at least weekly.
- Conflicting population: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] reports a slight decrease among married, college-educated women working from home in another paper.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither podcast summary supplies full study designs, effect identification, job composition, or all outcome measures. Remote work may facilitate family growth, but people who want children may also select flexible jobs. One population-level result should not be generalized across occupations, genders, household types, career stages, or countries.
