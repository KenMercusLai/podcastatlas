# Work From Home Evidence

## Definition
Work from home evidence is the empirical evaluation of remote, hybrid, and in-person arrangements by job type, workflow, organizational outcome, worker context, and household effect rather than by one universal workplace rule.

## Current Synthesis
The job-specific frame resists a universal location rule. Some roles work better in person, many fit hybrid arrangements, and measurable routine work can perform better remotely. In the randomized [[Ctrip]] experiment described by [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9cf0c3dfc0e043244126a9eac2eb0f8-49651044-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9cf0c3dfc0e043244126a9eac2eb0f8-49651044]], home-based call-center workers produced more, quit less often, and reduced company cost, but many later returned to the office and remote staff received fewer promotions even after performance was controlled.

The result makes hybrid work an organizational design problem rather than a location compromise. Routine individual production, creative collaboration, mentoring, informal learning, social connection, career visibility, home space, connectivity, schedule intensity, and worker choice can move in different directions. [[would-you-trust-an-economist-with-your-economy]] adds that executives may overweight the office conditions under which they personally succeeded, so evaluation requires several outcomes rather than presence or output alone.

The household branch adds family logistics as an outcome. Bloom reports in [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] that couples in which both partners work from home at least one day per week have more children on average, especially additional children, possibly because flexibility eases childcare and logistics. A narrower working paper points in the opposite direction, and selection into remote-compatible work remains unresolved.

## Key Claims
- Remote-work performance depends on the job, measurability, and coordination design.
- Hybrid work is an evidence-bearing category shaped by worker choice, schedule intensity, and deliberate in-person use.
- Productivity is not the only outcome; promotion, learning, isolation, satisfaction, attrition, family logistics, and fertility may move differently.
- Remote eligibility and success are unequally distributed across occupations, housing, connectivity, and household conditions.
- Executive intuition can overweight office-formed career experience.
- Remote-work fertility associations do not by themselves establish causation.

## Evidence
- Job heterogeneity: [[would-you-trust-an-economist-with-your-economy]] presents Bloom's in-person, hybrid, and fully remote distinctions.
- Randomized call-center evidence: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9cf0c3dfc0e043244126a9eac2eb0f8-49651044-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9cf0c3dfc0e043244126a9eac2eb0f8-49651044]] reports higher output, lower company cost, lower quits, and a promotion penalty among Ctrip's home-based workers.
- Preference and isolation: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9cf0c3dfc0e043244126a9eac2eb0f8-49651044-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9cf0c3dfc0e043244126a9eac2eb0f8-49651044]] reports that roughly half of the experimental home-working group later chose to return to the office and that preferences varied widely.
- Access and task limits: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9cf0c3dfc0e043244126a9eac2eb0f8-49651044-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-b9cf0c3dfc0e043244126a9eac2eb0f8-49651044]] distinguishes routine work from creative or large-team collaboration and reports gaps in suitable internet, rooms, and remote-compatible occupations.
- Management interpretation: [[would-you-trust-an-economist-with-your-economy]] places the evidence inside [[EconomistTrustCrisis]] and [[ExpertTrustRepair]].
- Household association: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] reports larger average family size when both partners work remotely at least weekly.
- Conflicting population: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-58f17fc3725ef3102277ae962324f7dd-59875813]] reports a slight decrease among married, college-educated women working from home in another paper.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The new source describes random assignment and objective output, but the experiment involved volunteers doing measurable call-center work and an intensive four-home-day schedule. It should not be generalized to creative work, large-team coordination, involuntary pandemic arrangements, or workers without suitable space and connectivity. Remote work may facilitate family growth, but people who want children may also select flexible jobs. Forecasts about cities, migration, and property conversion remain pandemic-era projections rather than settled outcomes.

## What Changed
- Added randomized evidence that output and retention gains can coexist with isolation and a promotion penalty.
- Reframed hybrid design around task fit, choice, in-person purpose, home resources, and multiple outcomes.
- Added unequal occupational, housing, and connectivity access as a boundary on remote-work benefits.
