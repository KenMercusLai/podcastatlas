# CPR/AED Response Boundary / 心肺复苏与AED使用边界

## Definition
CPR/AED response boundary / 心肺复苏与AED使用边界 is the public first-aid distinction between suspected cardiac arrest that warrants chest compressions and AED use, and other collapse or illness states where the priority is assessment, emergency calling, and professional guidance.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-222-aoye-xintiao-kuangbiao-shi-cusi-qianzhao-aed-hui-dian-cuo-huoren-dai-ni-bikai-zhiming-changshi-shijie-jijiu-ri-teji-lnemogq8gb9o3e2835jvtfx3emiy]] frames CPR and AED use as public skills that still require a threshold: a person is collapsed, unconscious, and not breathing normally. For lay rescuers, the episode does not require pulse diagnosis; it emphasizes "拍喊," looking for breathing, calling 120, starting compressions when the arrest pattern fits, and dividing work.

AED use is presented as safer and more guided than many people fear. The device prompts pad placement, analyzes rhythm, advises shock only when appropriate, and requires bystanders to avoid touching the person during analysis and shock delivery.

The wider defibrillation boundary also distinguishes implanted from public rescue systems. An [[ImplantableCardioverterDefibrillator|ICD]] can continuously detect and treat selected dangerous rhythms in a clinically selected patient, whereas an AED depends on a bystander response after collapse. The two layers complement one another and serve different populations, as illustrated by [[vol-152-nazha-wo-ming-you-wo-bu-you-tian-xianshi-zhong-de-gaiming-shenqi-ni-zhidao-ma-zhen-neng-yufang-cusi-ma-lvt24r7x6bppx7e5gqhas1yn-jru]].

## Key Claims
- Chest compressions are for suspected cardiac arrest, not for any person who falls or feels unwell.
- Lay judgment can focus on unconsciousness and absent or abnormal breathing rather than difficult pulse checks.
- High-quality response needs task distribution because one rescuer cannot sustain compressions, call, search for an AED, and manage the scene alone for long.
- AEDs are public-use devices that analyze rhythm and advise whether defibrillation is needed.
- AED-supported rescue and implanted ICD protection are complementary rather than interchangeable approaches.
- Bystanders should stop touching the person during AED rhythm analysis and avoid body contact during shock delivery.
- Wet skin, poor pad contact, dense chest hair, or metal objects near the chest can interfere with AED setup and should be handled pragmatically.

## Evidence
- Arrest threshold: [[vol-222-aoye-xintiao-kuangbiao-shi-cusi-qianzhao-aed-hui-dian-cuo-huoren-dai-ni-bikai-zhiming-changshi-shijie-jijiu-ri-teji-lnemogq8gb9o3e2835jvtfx3emiy]] says public rescuers should check response and breathing, and should not perform chest compressions on someone who wakes and can speak.
- AED process: [[vol-222-aoye-xintiao-kuangbiao-shi-cusi-qianzhao-aed-hui-dian-cuo-huoren-dai-ni-bikai-zhiming-changshi-shijie-jijiu-ri-teji-lnemogq8gb9o3e2835jvtfx3emiy]] explains ventricular fibrillation, electrode placement prompts, automatic rhythm analysis, charging, shock precautions, and continued compressions after device-guided steps.
- Setup constraints: [[vol-222-aoye-xintiao-kuangbiao-shi-cusi-qianzhao-aed-hui-dian-cuo-huoren-dai-ni-bikai-zhiming-changshi-shijie-jijiu-ri-teji-lnemogq8gb9o3e2835jvtfx3emiy]] mentions wiping water or sweat, improving pad contact, dealing with chest hair, and removing metal items from the front chest area.
- External-versus-implanted protection: [[vol-152-nazha-wo-ming-you-wo-bu-you-tian-xianshi-zhong-de-gaiming-shenqi-ni-zhidao-ma-zhen-neng-yufang-cusi-ma-lvt24r7x6bppx7e5gqhas1yn-jru]] contrasts public AED access and bystander timing with continuous ICD monitoring for selected high-risk patients.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are public medical education, not certified CPR/AED training, an implantation guideline, or a complete resuscitation guideline. Local device models, emergency numbers, dispatcher instructions, legal protections, formal training standards, and ICD indications vary.

## What Changed
- VOL.222 creates a dedicated CPR/AED concept that sharpens the older public emergency-preparedness page's collapse-response boundary.
- VOL.152 distinguishes bystander-dependent AED rescue from continuous ICD protection in selected high-risk patients.
