# Puer Aeternus / 永恒少年

[[PuerAeternus|Puer aeternus / 永恒少年]] is the Jungian "eternal youth" pattern used in [[07-yong-rongge-lilun-fenxi-xiaowangzi-yongheng-shaonian-ji-qita-539091165]] to reread [[TheLittlePrince|《小王子》]]. The source defines it as an adult psychic state that remains stuck near adolescence: attracted to imagination, transcendence, ideal love, heroic professions, and future greatness, while resisting routine work, commitment, practical tasks, and ordinary responsibility.

The episode keeps the pattern double-sided. Eternal youth is not only childish failure; it carries creativity, hope, renewal, sensitivity, and refusal to let adult utility flatten the soul. But when it cannot integrate the ground, it turns into dependency, laziness, romanticized escape, self-centeredness, and sometimes death-seeking.

## Key Claims
- The little prince is charming because he preserves wonder against a narrow adult world, but he is dangerous because he keeps leaving relations and duties behind.
- Eternal youth is distinct from a simple "巨婴" frame. The source's emphasis is not just entitlement, but flight from adulthood through fantasy, idealism, and refusal of grounded work.
- The figure often appears in literary and mythic forms: child-gods, romantic youths, sensitive rebels, and beautiful self-destructive boys.
- The repair is not to kill childlike imagination; it is to bind it to suffering, labor, relation, and responsibility.
