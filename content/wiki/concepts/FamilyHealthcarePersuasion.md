# Family Healthcare Persuasion / 家庭就医劝导

## Definition
Family healthcare persuasion is a barrier-matched approach to helping a relative accept screening, clinical review or treatment through understanding, agency, trust, logistics, accompaniment and explicit escalation thresholds rather than expertise or command alone.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat persuasion as relational care. Medical correctness may fail when a clinician child is still heard as “the child,” when arrangements are made without a parent's participation, or when urgency is expressed as blame. The first task is to discover whether the obstacle is price, pain, a procedure, bad news, restrictions, queues, unfamiliar technology or the wish not to need help.

Useful responses differ by barrier. Cost can be discussed transparently; hospital friction can be reduced through accompaniment; fragmented beliefs can be addressed patiently; and autonomy can be preserved through choices and agreed thresholds for qualified care. Trust is longitudinal: accurate small judgments, respectful tone, visible follow-through and emotional presence can matter more than one appeal to authority.

VOL.75 adds a concrete, bounded invitation: help a parent obtain a comprehensive oral examination and understand the maintenance task, rather than arguing abstractly about “good teeth.” The visit can turn invisible plaque, decay, residual roots, mobility, poor restoration fit or mucosal change into specific options, while the family can support navigation and follow-through without making the diagnosis or medication decision itself.

## Key Claims
- Professional expertise does not automatically create authority inside a family relationship.
- Persuasion should begin by identifying the relative's actual fear, constraint or unmet emotional need.
- Commands, shame, threats and fully prearranged care can intensify resistance when they reduce agency.
- Cost transparency, practical navigation and accompaniment can resolve barriers that medical explanation alone cannot.
- Clear symptom, change or time thresholds can convert a vague recurring argument into an agreed escalation plan.
- A concrete first assessment can be easier to act on than a broad demand to become healthier.
- Family support should enable qualified care, not replace examination or medication coordination.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
No communication technique guarantees agreement, and family persuasion must not become coercion, deception, diagnosis or unauthorized treatment. Urgent warning signs may require emergency escalation rather than prolonged negotiation, while non-urgent informed refusal still deserves respect. These experience-based strategies are not a validated protocol; usefulness depends on disease risk, cognition, family history, finances, service access and the person's preferences.
