## Definition
Synthetic voice identity authentication verifies who authorized speech independently of whether the audible voice was produced biologically, restored through assistive technology, or generated by AI.

## Current Synthesis
The source identifies an accessibility failure in conventional voice authentication. A laryngectomee may legitimately speak through converted or synthesized audio, while the same technical surface can be used by an autonomous agent or impersonator. A detector that only labels audio as AI-generated therefore risks excluding the real augmented speaker without establishing who actually controlled the utterance.

The proposed direction separates three claims: the audio is synthetic, a specific person is authenticated, and that person authorized this interaction. A secure identity token, voiceprint, or comparable credential could carry the second and third claims, but the source does not settle the protocol. The durable requirement is modality independence: identity cannot continue to rely on biological vocal texture as synthetic voices become ordinary.

## Key Claims
- Audible similarity is no longer reliable proof of identity.
- AI-audio disclosure and human-identity verification solve different problems.
- Authentication must include legitimate users of assistive synthetic speech rather than treating generation as fraud.
- A separate credential should bind the speaking person to the interaction and authorization context.
- Voiceprints alone may remain vulnerable when conversion changes output or cloning reproduces expected features.
- Systems should distinguish a human using augmentation from an autonomous agent or impersonator.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a problem statement and proposal rather than a deployed protocol, security analysis, or accessibility evaluation. Tokens can be stolen, voiceprints spoofed, devices compromised, and disclosure metadata removed. Strong identity requirements can also create surveillance, exclusion, recovery, and privacy burdens, so implementation needs risk-based and accessible alternatives.

## What Changed
- Established modality-independent identity as the authentication requirement for legitimate synthetic speech.
