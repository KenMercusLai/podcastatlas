# Sovereign Housing Development

Sovereign housing development is the episode's case where a government or nation with land-use sovereignty can build housing outside the usual municipal zoning veto points. In [[the-skyscrapers-that-nimbys-and-zoning-couldnt-stop]], the [[SquamishNation|Squamish Nation]] uses control over [[SinakVillageSite|returned land]] near [[Vancouver]] to pursue 11 towers and roughly 6,000 rental units.

The concept is not a simple argument against coordination. The source still includes public infrastructure negotiation, utility connections, traffic and transit upgrades, and a Canadian government low-interest loan. Its distinctive claim is that when ordinary zoning vetoes do not dominate, land value, housing supply, and long-term Indigenous wealth can be evaluated on a different scale.

## Key Claims
- Sovereignty changes the default: the project does not need to clear normal Vancouver height and density limits.
- Land-use control lets the Squamish evaluate the site through long-term nation-building rather than only neighborhood preference.
- Public infrastructure coordination remains necessary even when municipal zoning authority is limited.
- Sovereign development can increase regional housing supply while also creating revenue for the landholding nation.
- The concept extends [[HousingAffordabilitySupplyMechanics]] by showing how approval power, not only construction technology or landlord ownership, shapes supply.
