# Pork Cycle / 猪周期

## Definition
Pork cycle / 猪周期 is the recurrent price-and-capacity pattern in pig farming: high pork prices encourage herd expansion, expanded supply pushes prices down, losses force capacity reduction, and tighter supply later supports prices again.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that China's current pork cycle is becoming flatter and slower to clear. Earlier cycles could swing sharply because small producers exited quickly during losses, but the latest cycle has been reshaped by scale farming, efficiency gains, and demand substitution. The result is not the disappearance of cycles; it is a weaker link between short-term losses and fast supply contraction.

## Key Claims
- China's historical pork cycles were usually understood as three-to-four-year supply-price loops.
- The current cycle has had a shorter price-up phase and a longer low-price phase after late 2022.
- Scale farming makes producer exit slower because larger firms can absorb losses longer than small households.
- Productivity improvements make breeding-sow counts less predictive of final pork supply.
- Demand growth is weaker because pork faces substitution from other proteins and health-oriented diet changes.
- Downstream storage, processing, and export channels are too limited to absorb oversupply quickly.

## Evidence
Price-cycle change:
- [[zhurou-jiage-jingli-man-chang-xiadie-weishenme-zhuzhouqi-buzai-zunxun-laojiezou-1012895922]] says earlier cycles since 2002 lasted roughly three to four years, while the current sixth cycle entered a prolonged low-price phase after November 2022.

Producer-exit mechanism:
- [[zhurou-jiage-jingli-man-chang-xiadie-weishenme-zhuzhouqi-buzai-zunxun-laojiezou-1012895922]] links the slower clearing process to [[ChinaPigFarmingScaleConsolidation]], financing access, and lower unit costs among larger producers.

Supply and demand offsets:
- [[zhurou-jiage-jingli-man-chang-xiadie-weishenme-zhuzhouqi-buzai-zunxun-laojiezou-1012895922]] says efficiency gains can keep output rising even when breeding-sow counts fall, while [[PorkConsumptionSubstitution]] and [[DownstreamPorkAbsorptionLimits]] reduce demand-side and downstream buffering.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an explanatory podcast based on cited media, official data, and analyst commentary rather than a full commodity-market model. It supports a claim that the pork cycle is changing, not that pork prices can no longer rise or that all producer losses are sustainable.
