# Viking Age Worldview

## Definition

Viking Age worldview is a reconstruction of how Scandinavian people may have experienced gods, local beings, dead people, animals, landscape, magic, danger, and social practice as parts of one reality rather than separate natural and supernatural domains.

## Current Synthesis

[[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] argues that modern “religion,” “mythology,” and “supernatural” categories can distort the evidence. Stories varied locally and organically, children could learn practical warnings about forests and unseen beings, and everyday negotiation with elves or other local powers may have mattered more frequently than formal attention to distant gods.

## Key Claims

- Belief is better approached as practical orientation to a populated world than as assent to doctrine.
- Gods, spirits, magic, animals, landscape, and the dead could belong to different kinds of nature within one lived reality.
- Oral stories were variable, local, and contradictory rather than one fixed “Norse mythology.”
- Local beings and dangers may have structured ordinary conduct more directly than elite gods did.
- Christianization could replace official divine structures faster than it erased everyday practices and fears.

## Evidence

- Category boundary: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] compares asking whether someone believed in Odin to asking whether they believed in a mountain.
- Local practice: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] describes warnings about forests, bears, elves, and an invisible population negotiated in daily life.
- Persistence: [[358-viking-sorcery-glt1200485419]] notes legal prohibitions and long folkloric survival after formal conversion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The surviving evidence is regionally uneven and often late, Christian, literary, archaeological, or analogical. “Viking Age worldview” is itself a modern synthesis and should not imply one uniform Scandinavian mentality across three centuries.

## What Changed

- Created the concept as a category-sensitive synthesis of lived divine, magical, local, and environmental reality.
