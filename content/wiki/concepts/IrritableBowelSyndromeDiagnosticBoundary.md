# Irritable Bowel Syndrome Diagnostic Boundary

## Definition
Irritable bowel syndrome diagnostic boundary is the source's caution that IBS-style functional labels should follow exclusion of organic disease rather than replace evaluation.

## Current Synthesis
The sources do not deny functional gastrointestinal disorders. They argue that repeated diarrhea, constipation, bowel spasm, pain, bloating, or discomfort can be interpreted as functional only after clinicians have reasonably assessed organic causes through history, examination, testing, and sometimes endoscopy. VOL.53 adds the complementary point that a normal or mildly abnormal scope does not make symptoms unreal: sleep, anger, work pressure, and anxiety may influence symptoms through a brain-gut pathway after structural danger has been assessed.

## Key Claims
- IBS-style explanations should not be the first stop for every repeated bowel symptom.
- Functional symptoms and organic disease can look similar early, especially when the bowel signals indirectly.
- Stool testing and colonoscopy can be appropriate before settling on a functional diagnosis.
- Functional bowel disorder is not itself presented as a direct colorectal-cancer cause.
- Long-term diarrhea or inflammation can still matter as risk context even when the original symptom pattern seems functional.
- Normal endoscopy can narrow structural explanations without proving that pain or bloating is imaginary or clinically irrelevant.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not give formal Rome criteria, a complete organic-disease workup, treatment protocols, or individualized advice. “Brain-gut axis” is not a shortcut for psychologizing symptoms, and a prior normal test does not permanently exclude later disease. The durable principle is sequencing: functional interpretation requires context and proportionate assessment, while persistent or changing warning signs can reopen evaluation.
