# Reusable Packaging Durability / 可复用包装耐久性

Reusable packaging durability / 可复用包装耐久性 is the engineering shift required when a package that used to be discarded after one product cycle must survive multiple refill cycles. In [[lvdian-xiaoyang-yao-tuiguang-tihuanxin-de-ba-meizhuang-chongzuo-yibian-1006757937]], this is the hidden R&D consequence of [[RefillableBeautyPackaging]].

The source emphasizes that a beauty package has to protect the formula and feel good in use. Once refills are introduced, the shell, pump head, seal, snap-in, screw-in, or liquid refill interface may need to work two, three, or four times rather than once. That makes durability a product requirement, not an after-sales nicety.

## Key Claims
- Reusable packages need higher material, seal, and actuation standards than disposable packages.
- A pump head that only had to last one full product may need more demanding press tests if the customer keeps reusing the outer container.
- Different refill mechanisms create different risks: press-in, screw-in, snap-in, and pour-in formats all need low mess and low failure.
- Durability affects [[RefillAdoptionFriction]] because a single bad refill experience can stop the habit.
- Reusable packaging turns package design into part of [[CPGManufacturingScaleUp]], since the package must be produced consistently enough for repeated consumer handling.
- The concept complements [[PackagingAsProductExperience]] by adding time: the package has to feel good across the second, third, and fourth use, not only at unboxing.
