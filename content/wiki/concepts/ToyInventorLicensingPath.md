# Toy Inventor Licensing Path

Toy inventor licensing path is the route where a game or toy creator licenses an idea to a publisher or toy company rather than building the whole consumer-products business. In [[advice-line-with-carlton-calvin-of-razor-c0de1a84-3d35-45f3-921c-6144693ad86e]], [[CarltonCalvin]] presents licensing as a traditional path for inventors, while warning [[SeanBarassa]] that game buyers see many submissions and choose very few.

The source's practical boundary is proof. [[EulogyGame|Eulogy]] may have a distinctive premise, but just under 1,000 units sold is not enough to assume a publisher will take it on. Carlton and [[GuyRaz]] recommend conventions, small stores, game nights, and social or media attention as ways to convert an interesting mechanic into market evidence.

## Key Claims
- Licensing can let an inventor avoid becoming the full operator, but it shifts the hurdle toward buyer-visible demand proof.
- A clever game premise still needs evidence that strangers play, recommend, reorder, gift, or ask retailers for it.
- Retailers and publishers care about comparative opportunity cost because every accepted game displaces another product or development slot.
- Grassroots play can become licensing evidence when it creates visible reactions and repeatable customer language.
