# Child Communication Development Signals

## Definition
Child communication development signals are the episode's observable markers for whether a young child is building communication capacity across speech, comprehension, gesture, emotion, and social response.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates communication development from simply counting spoken words. [[LihuaSpeechTherapist|梨花医生]] asks caregivers to watch whether a child responds to caregivers, understands voices, faces, emotions, and gestures, uses pointing or other body language to express wants, and gradually moves from sounds into dialogue and sentence-level replies. The practical value is screening attention: weak signals do not by themselves diagnose a condition, but they should stop families from passively waiting when a child appears disconnected from language, emotion, or social interaction.

The concept also ties observation to family practice. If adults provide every object before a child attempts to gesture or speak, or replace reciprocal interaction with one-way media, the child may have fewer reasons to practice communication.

## Key Claims
- Communication development includes speech, understanding, gestures, gaze, emotional response, social interaction, and practical expression.
- Early markers named by the source include caregiver-directed smiling or crying, simple syllables, "bye-bye," head-shaking, pointing, and using language or gesture to request objects.
- Two-to-three-year-old development is framed through growing dialogue, polite expressions, and sentence-level answering rather than isolated word repetition alone.
- Absence of gesture language is a serious signal in the episode because gesture is a basic fallback communication channel.
- Emotional and facial-response indifference can be a care-seeking signal when the child does not appear to understand social or affective cues.
- Age signals are prompts for professional consultation and family adjustment, not standalone diagnosis.

## Evidence
- Multi-channel observation: [[vol-35-kangfu-yixueke-dui-guiren-yuchi-de-wujie-hui-danwu-ertong-yanyu-de-fazhan-lnmoqgfmshs-zzt1gxkutv06w8aj]] tells caregivers to look at understanding of language, expression, emotional response, gestures, and social communication before age two.
- Age-linked examples: [[vol-35-kangfu-yixueke-dui-guiren-yuchi-de-wujie-hui-danwu-ertong-yanyu-de-fazhan-lnmoqgfmshs-zzt1gxkutv06w8aj]] names simple syllables around one year, pointing or requesting between one-and-a-half and two, and more dialogue and sentence replies after two.
- Gesture warning: [[vol-35-kangfu-yixueke-dui-guiren-yuchi-de-wujie-hui-danwu-ertong-yanyu-de-fazhan-lnmoqgfmshs-zzt1gxkutv06w8aj]] says a child who cannot use gesture language should quickly raise a professional-consultation question.
- Home context: [[vol-35-kangfu-yixueke-dui-guiren-yuchi-de-wujie-hui-danwu-ertong-yanyu-de-fazhan-lnmoqgfmshs-zzt1gxkutv06w8aj]] links over-complete caregiving and passive screens to fewer active communication opportunities.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives practical public-facing examples rather than normative milestone tables, validated screening instruments, or diagnostic thresholds. Hearing, neurodevelopmental, language, motor, and environmental assessments remain professional questions.
