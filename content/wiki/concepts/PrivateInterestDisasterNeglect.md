## Definition

私利优先的灾害搁置 / private-interest disaster neglect is the governance failure in which public repair is delayed or abandoned because the ruler's family, factional, reputational, or property interest is safer if the public harm remains displaced elsewhere.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-645-bidu-zhichang-quanmou-mingzhebaoshen-or-niliu-ershang-lvdqcakza0zqextrhympnpswxyjy|Hanji 645]] grounds the concept in Wang Mang's handling of a Yellow River breach. Flooding harms eastern commanderies, but [[WangMang|王莽]] worries first about whether the water will threaten Wang-family ancestral graves at Yuancheng. Once he learns that Yuancheng is safe because the flood runs east, he chooses not to repair the dike, fearing repair could redirect water west.

The episode's durable claim is not that every delayed repair is corrupt. The narrower pattern is priority inversion: a public disaster is tolerated because the distribution of harm protects the ruler's private sacred interest. Disaster governance then becomes a legitimacy problem, not only an engineering problem.

## Key Claims

- Disaster response can fail because the harmed population is not the decision-maker's protected constituency.
- Private ancestral, property, or factional interests can distort technical infrastructure choices.
- Non-repair is still an active allocation of risk when officials understand where the harm is going.
- The moral failure is sharper when relief would be possible but is avoided to keep danger away from the ruler's own assets.
- This pattern converts environmental crisis into political credibility loss.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source note does not quantify repair feasibility, engineering constraints, or the full hydrology of the breach. The concept therefore preserves the episode's political reading while leaving exact flood-control counterfactuals source-scoped.
