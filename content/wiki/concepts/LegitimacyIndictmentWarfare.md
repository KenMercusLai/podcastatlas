# Legitimacy Indictment Warfare / 正当性控诉战

Legitimacy indictment warfare / 正当性控诉战 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-1-liubang-geqiang-chaofeng-qi-de-xiangyu-shejian-touxi-lhs6miq2aa3avnny-prxtvkfxgvy|Hanji 164-1]] gives to [[LiuBang|刘邦]] at [[GuangwuChuHan|广武]]. Faced with [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s repeated desire for single combat, Liu Bang does not answer as a private rival. He publicly recites Xiang Yu's crimes and turns the confrontation into a prosecution of illegitimate rule.

The content of the indictment matters. Liu Bang cites broken promises under the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]], coercive entry into Qin, burning Qin palaces, killing surrendered people, unequal partition, driving out and later killing Yi Emperor [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]], annexing land, and violating covenants. The source presents the speech as a move from "who is stronger" to "who has the right to mobilize the world."

The concept therefore sits beside [[MoralizedWarPretext|道德化战争借口]] but is narrower. It is not only a pretext for starting a war; it is a battlefield speech act that refuses the enemy's preferred contest format and reclassifies personal conflict as collective punishment of a public wrongdoer.

## Key Claims
- A commander can reject single combat by changing the audience and issue: from personal courage before the enemy to legitimacy before allied states and soldiers.
- Accusations work politically when they assemble remembered grievances into a coherent right-to-punish frame.
- The tactic depends on an opponent who can be made to look like a breaker of covenants, not merely a strong enemy.
- In Hanji 164-1, Xiang Yu's crossbow response shows that physical force can interrupt the speech but does not answer the legitimacy frame.
