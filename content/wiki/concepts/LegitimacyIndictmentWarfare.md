## Definition
Legitimacy Indictment Warfare / 正当性控诉战 is the tactic of publicly assembling an opponent's alleged crimes into a right-to-punish frame, so a conflict is heard as legitimate prosecution of public wrongdoing rather than as private rivalry or naked force competition.

## Current Synthesis
The concept now covers spoken battlefield indictment, written campaign proclamation, and anti-usurpation mobilization. In the Chu-Han case, [[LiuBang|刘邦]] refuses [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s single-combat frame at [[GuangwuChuHan|广武]] by reciting broken covenants, settlement violence, and the killing of Yi Emperor [[MiXin|芈心 / 楚怀王]]. In the late-Han Guandu case, [[ChenLinLateHan|陈琳]] writes [[YuanShao|袁绍]]'s anti-[[CaoCao|曹操]] proclamation, listing Cao's alleged injuries to loyal officials, emperor-control, Xuzhou violence, tomb-robbing offices, and lineage-based ingratitude. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-630-jiahaoren-sidiao-mianju-nengyou-duo-choue-li9bkjm4dphuq9l-sjyk-qnwfeyl|Hanji 630]] adds [[ZhaiYiRebellion|翟义起义]]: Zhai Yi's side accuses [[WangMang|王莽]] of poisoning [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]] and using "假皇帝" regency to dismantle Han rule, then presents Liu Xin's elevation as Heaven-backed punishment of a usurper.

The shared pattern is audience conversion. The indictment moves attention away from the enemy's preferred measure, whether duel courage, battlefield scale, court office, or regency legality, and toward the question of who has authority to mobilize punishment. The limit is equally important: indictment can sharpen morale and reputation pressure, but Cao Cao's response in the White Horse source and Wang Mang's emergency organization in Hanji 630 show that strong accusation still has to be matched by talent, strategy, command, and durable coalition capacity.

## Key Claims
- A commander or patron can reject the enemy's preferred contest by changing the audience and issue from personal strength to public right.
- Accusations work politically when they assemble remembered grievances into a coherent right-to-punish frame.
- The tactic depends on an opponent who can be made to look like a breaker of covenants, public order, or accepted legitimacy.
- Written proclamations extend the same logic beyond battlefield speech into pre-campaign public-opinion and morale work.
- Anti-usurpation indictment can attach a rebel force to a dynastic restoration banner by naming the powerholder as a traitor rather than a lawful regent.
- Rhetorical force has limits: legitimacy indictment can wound reputation, but it does not by itself solve command, logistics, or execution.

- Indictment material: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-164-1-liubang-geqiang-chaofeng-qi-de-xiangyu-shejian-touxi-lhs6miq2aa3avnny-prxtvkfxgvy|Hanji 164-1]] uses the Huaiwang agreement, entry into Qin, killing of surrendered people, unequal partition, and Yi Emperor's death as legitimacy anchors against Xiang Yu.
- Campaign proclamation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1043-yuanshao-tongma-caocao-jing-zhihaole-tade-piantouteng-ltibjj6xizsrynpgr9oyf-q3ydna|Hanji 1043]] says Yuan Shao orders Chen Lin to write and circulate a proclamation accusing Cao Cao of five major categories of wrongdoing.
- Rhetorical impact and limit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1043-yuanshao-tongma-caocao-jing-zhihaole-tade-piantouteng-ltibjj6xizsrynpgr9oyf-q3ydna|Hanji 1043]] frames the proclamation as powerful enough to shock Cao Cao, but also has Cao Cao answer that writing must be backed by real ability and military command.
- Anti-usurpation accusation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-630-jiahaoren-sidiao-mianju-nengyou-duo-choue-li9bkjm4dphuq9l-sjyk-qnwfeyl|Hanji 630]] says Zhai Yi's side circulates charges that Wang Mang poisoned Han Pingdi and is gradually removing Han rule.
- Restoration banner: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-630-jiahaoren-sidiao-mianju-nengyou-duo-choue-li9bkjm4dphuq9l-sjyk-qnwfeyl|Hanji 630]] says the proclamation declares a Han son of Heaven already established and calls for punishing Wang Mang under Heaven's mandate.
- Force-capacity limit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-630-jiahaoren-sidiao-mianju-nengyou-duo-choue-li9bkjm4dphuq9l-sjyk-qnwfeyl|Hanji 630]] shows Wang Mang panicking but still rapidly arranging armies, passes, capital defenses, and a Zhougong counterframe.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Legitimacy indictment is not neutral fact-finding. The source claims may be selective, exaggerated, or polemical, so the concept tracks the political function of accusation rather than verifying every charge; Hanji 630's poisoning claim against Wang Mang remains a rebel accusation, not a settled finding. The tactic also differs from ordinary slander because it needs a public authority frame and a usable audience. Xiang Yu's crossbow response, Cao Cao's command critique, and Wang Mang's counter-mobilization all show that force can interrupt, outlast, or answer the rhetorical moment.

## What Changed
- Added Hanji 630 as an anti-usurpation case: Zhai Yi's side uses poisoning and regency-usurpation charges to turn rebellion into Han-restoration punishment.
- Broadened the limit from rhetoric-versus-command to rhetoric-versus-coalition capacity: indictment can scale response, but it must survive the ruler's counterframe and military organization.
