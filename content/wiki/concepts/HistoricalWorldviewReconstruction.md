# Historical Worldview Reconstruction

## Definition

Historical worldview reconstruction is the dramatic practice of making a past society's incentives, beliefs, status codes, and moral assumptions govern the story rather than using period costume around largely contemporary characters.

## Current Synthesis

In [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]], [[RidiculeFilm|Ridicule]] is the central case. Its provincial nobleman cannot obtain a practical marsh-drainage project merely by proving public benefit; he must enter a Versailles system where wit, access, humiliation, and performance allocate attention. The film makes that unfamiliar code causally active, allowing viewers to understand both its internal logic and its exclusionary consequences.

The same criterion helps explain praise for austere material settings in Joan the Maid and for the cultural world rebuilt in [[LesEnfantsDuParadisFilm|Les Enfants du Paradis]]. Historical accuracy here includes social and emotional structure, not only factual detail or visual decoration.

## Key Claims

- Period detail becomes historically meaningful when it constrains character action rather than merely decorating it.
- Institutions and status codes should shape what characters can plausibly know, want, say, and accomplish.
- A reconstructed worldview can remain morally criticizable without converting historical characters into modern spokespeople.
- Material restraint can aid reconstruction when performance, setting, and social logic carry more weight than spectacle.

## Evidence

- Court logic: [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] presents Ridicule's Versailles as a system in which verbal performance determines access to royal power.
- Medieval belief: [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] praises Joan the Maid for internalizing visions through performance rather than resolving spiritual experience through spectacle.
- Cultural environment: [[242-french-history-on-film-glt2672520929]] values Les Enfants du Paradis for rebuilding the theatrical, class, criminal, romantic, and popular worlds of nineteenth-century Paris.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

No film can recover a past consciousness without interpretation, and apparent strangeness is not proof of accuracy. The source offers critical judgments rather than archival validation of each production, so worldview reconstruction should complement rather than replace factual and source criticism.

## What Changed

- Established the concept through court, medieval, and urban-cultural cases in French historical cinema.
