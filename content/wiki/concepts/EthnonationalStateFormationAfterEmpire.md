# Ethnonational State Formation After Empire

## Definition

Ethnonational state formation after empire is the replacement of plural imperial jurisdictions by territorial states that claim legitimacy through a more exclusive national people, often amid war, expulsion, or minority insecurity.

## Current Synthesis

The episode uses the collapse of the [[OttomanEmpire|Ottoman Empire]] and the Greek-Turkish war as its sharpest case. Older cities such as Smyrna and Constantinople contained overlapping Greek, Turkish, Jewish, Armenian, Arab, and other communities, but postwar territorial projects pushed toward national homogeneity. Greek expansion failed under [[MustafaKemalAtaturk|Mustafa Kemal]]'s forces; Smyrna burned amid mass death and flight; and the resulting order displaced populations while British and French mandates reorganized other former Ottoman lands under international authorization.

## Key Claims

- Imperial collapse turns mixed cities and borderlands into contested evidence for rival national claims.
- Military victory can determine which national project gains state power, but not erase civilian suffering or prior pluralism.
- Population displacement is not merely a side effect when successor states define security through ethnic-national alignment.
- International mandates can claim moral or legal authority while carrying forward imperial promises and strategic interests.
- New national orders may increase sovereignty for one population while creating minority, refugee, and territorial crises for others.

## Evidence

- **Plural imperial baseline:** [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]] describes Alexandria, Smyrna, and Constantinople as multiethnic cities within a wider Ottoman order.
- **War and homogenization:** The source connects Greek overreach, Turkish victory, atrocities by both armies, Smyrna's destruction, and population transfer to the rise of more ethnically defined states. [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]]
- **Mandate reordering:** The episode links the Palestine Mandate to the Balfour Declaration, Arab promises, Transjordan, Jewish immigration, and League authorization. [[137-1922-the-birth-of-the-modern-world-part-2-glt5281126494]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The page does not treat imperial pluralism as peaceful or egalitarian, nor national sovereignty as inherently illegitimate. The source's Smyrna death estimate, chronology, atrocity allocation, and account of mandate motives are not independently adjudicated here. Ireland and the former Ottoman lands share a post-imperial mechanism but differ substantially in institutions, demography, external power, and violence.

## What Changed

- Created a comparative frame for mixed imperial space becoming nationally bounded territory through war, mandate, partition, and displacement.
