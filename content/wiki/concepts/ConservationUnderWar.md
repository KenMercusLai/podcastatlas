# Conservation Under War

Conservation under war is the source's pattern where ecological protection work continues inside a landscape shaped by bombardment, checkpoints, displacement, and fear. In [[the-elon-game-musks-vision-of-the-future-6a633594d19896314260e5c4]], [[MonaKhalil]]'s turtle-conservation work at the [[OrangeHouseLebanon|Orange House]] is repeatedly interrupted by conflict in southern [[Lebanon]].

The episode says the Orange House was hit during the 2006 war after [[Israel]] invaded Lebanon, that a checkpoint later sat beside it, and that Khalil refused to leave during renewed Israeli attacks. Conservation is therefore not framed as separate from war; the beach, house, volunteers, hatchlings, and access rules all sit inside the security environment.

## Key Claims
- War can damage conservation infrastructure directly through shelling, displacement, and checkpoints.
- Conflict can also reduce visitor access, volunteer continuity, and local attention to environmental rules.
- Refusing to abandon a site can become part of conservation identity, but it also increases personal risk.
- Conservation under war still needs ordinary interventions: habitat protection, local rules, education, and monitoring.
