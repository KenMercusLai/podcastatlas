# Organizational Long-Termism / 组织长期主义

## Definition
[[OrganizationalLongTermism|Organizational long-termism / 组织长期主义]] is the capacity to make mutually reinforcing governance, talent, investment, product, and resource-allocation choices that preserve future capability when short-term incentives point elsewhere.

## Current Synthesis
[[lossad1trayjdp11pbjiywrxy43o|The Capital Group episode]] treats long-termism as an operating system rather than patience rhetoric. Ownership must let current contributors participate without encouraging rapid extraction; hiring must support long relationships; evaluation and attribution must tolerate informative error; portfolio architecture must distribute judgment; and product and capacity decisions must protect clients from the firm's own growth incentives. The test is behavior at conflict points: whether the organization restrains profitable expansion in euphoric markets, keeps investing in weak markets, transfers opportunity to later generations, and communicates through periods when results are uncomfortable.

## Key Claims
- Long-term intent becomes organizational capability only when ownership, incentives, hiring, evaluation, information, and product policy reinforce one another.
- Intergenerational continuity requires incumbents to surrender some economic control and opportunity to current and future contributors.
- Long evaluation horizons require systems that distinguish decision quality from lucky or unlucky outcomes.
- Restraining asset gathering when strategies are capacity-constrained or customers are likely to enter at bad times is a substantive client-alignment test.
- Downturn investment and continued communication preserve future option value even when they reduce current profit.
- Surface imitation fails when a practice such as relaxed exploration time or co-management is detached from strategy, talent selection, and accountability.

## Evidence
### Ownership and succession
- [[lossad1trayjdp11pbjiywrxy43o]] uses Capital Group's private ownership and equity transfer toward current professionals as evidence that continuity requires incumbent restraint.

### Talent and learning
- [[lossad1trayjdp11pbjiywrxy43o]] describes lengthy reciprocal hiring, internal development, multi-year evaluation, and attribution systems that make patient learning operational.

### Client and capacity discipline
- [[lossad1trayjdp11pbjiywrxy43o]] cites cautious product launches, bull-market restraint, bear-market investment, and conflict decisions favoring clients as observable tests.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- One successful company history cannot establish that private ownership or long evaluation automatically produces superior client outcomes.
- Founder wealth, historical period, regulation, market growth, and organizational culture may be enabling conditions that later firms cannot reproduce.
- Long horizons can conceal weak accountability if attribution, review, and exit mechanisms are poor.
- The source is a structured podcast summary rather than comparative governance research, primary ownership records, or audited performance attribution.

## What Changed
- Defined long-termism as a linked organizational system rather than a holding-period preference.
- Made costly conflict decisions and intergenerational transfer central tests of the concept.
- Added outcome attribution and capacity restraint as necessary complements to patient evaluation.
