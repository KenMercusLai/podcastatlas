# Human-Dependent Safety System

Human-dependent safety system is the safety pattern named from [[04-qieernuobeili-de-wuye-woyao-jin-jianyu-le-530594843]]'s account of the [[ChernobylNuclearPowerPlant]]. The episode argues that the reactor's safety relied too heavily on operators following complex procedures under pressure, even when they did not fully understand the design features that could make those procedures dangerous.

The concept does not excuse operator decisions. It explains why individual judgment becomes brittle when the system asks people to compensate for design opacity, incomplete training, production demands, and the psychological background of [[NuclearSafetyPropaganda]].

In the source, [[AnatolyDyatlov]] illustrates the danger of concentrating authority in a high-pressure control room, while the graphite control-rod detail shows how a shutdown action could behave differently under extreme conditions than operators expected.

## Key Claims
- Safety is weaker when technical protection depends on perfect human interpretation of rare edge conditions.
- Procedures cannot substitute for understanding if the design itself has hidden or counterintuitive failure modes.
- Hierarchical authority can make frontline correction difficult exactly when warning signs matter most.
- Operator blame and design blame often interact rather than excluding each other.
