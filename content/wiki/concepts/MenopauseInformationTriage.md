# Menopause Information Triage / 更年期信息辨别

## Definition
Menopause information triage is the health-literacy practice of filtering menopause and hormone claims across books, social media, experts, products, tests, and personal stories against evidence, date, clinical context, and the person's actual symptoms.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence moves from silence to overload. EP274 describes a public field where menopause stories, books, expert claims, social-media posts, products, and hormone messages have become more visible but unevenly reliable. The Gottfried episode intensifies that problem by adding many plausible but decision-sensitive topics: hormone testing, dried urine metabolomics, micronutrients, magnesium, oral contraceptive risks and benefits, perimenopause brain metabolism, hormone therapy, and coronary artery calcium scoring.

The shared answer is not one authority replacement. Triage means comparing claims, noticing product incentives or outdated science, bringing outside information into clinical discussion, and refusing both blanket hormone optimism and blanket medication fear. It also means asking whether a symptom is being dismissed too quickly or whether a proposed test or intervention has jumped beyond the patient's actual risk.

## Key Claims
- More menopause information is useful only when claims are sorted by evidence, recency, source, incentives, and personal relevance.
- Personal stories and influencer advice can reduce shame while also flattening menopause into one dramatic remedy or identity.
- Hormone and supplement claims need clinical context because symptoms overlap with thyroid, metabolic, sleep, mental-health, and medication factors.
- Oral contraceptive and hormone-therapy discussions require risk-benefit framing rather than a single pro- or anti-hormone story.
- Avoiding medication or testing is not automatically safer if symptoms, bones, cardiovascular health, sleep, or mental state are worsening.
- Personal baseline data can support triage, but only when interpreted against cost, uncertainty, and professional guidance.

## Evidence
- Information overload - [[ep274-ruhe-zhaodao-shihe-ziji-de-gengnianqi-daan-li2bp1rtitdnvc04rmizyfg1txfh]] describes menopause books, expert claims, social-media advice, products, and treatment messages becoming visible but unevenly reliable.
- Multi-source checking - [[ep274-ruhe-zhaodao-shihe-ziji-de-gengnianqi-daan-li2bp1rtitdnvc04rmizyfg1txfh]] recommends comparing claims, checking dates and sources, and asking menopause clinics or professional doctors when symptoms affect life.
- Hormone-claim expansion - [[essentials-how-to-optimize-female-hormone-health-for-vitality-longevity-dr-sara-gottfried-scim5131677929]] adds hormone testing, metabolomics, micronutrients, oral contraceptives, perimenopause brain metabolism, and coronary calcium screening to the triage field.
- Risk-benefit boundary - [[ep274-ruhe-zhaodao-shihe-ziji-de-gengnianqi-daan-li2bp1rtitdnvc04rmizyfg1txfh]] says avoiding medication is not automatically risk-free, while [[essentials-how-to-optimize-female-hormone-health-for-vitality-longevity-dr-sara-gottfried-scim5131677929]] treats contraception and hormone symptoms as informed-consent topics.
- Symptom context - [[essentials-how-to-optimize-female-hormone-health-for-vitality-longevity-dr-sara-gottfried-scim5131677929]] frames hot flashes, night sweats, anxiety, sleep problems, brain fog, painful periods, and bowel patterns as signals that should be interpreted rather than ignored.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Triage cannot establish medical truth by itself. Some claims in the Gottfried source are strong and source-scoped, while some outside menopause information may be useful but incomplete, outdated, product-driven, or overgeneralized. Professional clinical discussion remains part of the safety boundary.
