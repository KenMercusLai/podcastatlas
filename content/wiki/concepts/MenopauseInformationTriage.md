# Menopause Information Triage / 更年期信息辨别

Menopause information triage is the source's response to the shift from silence to overload. In [[ep274-ruhe-zhaodao-shihe-ziji-de-gengnianqi-daan-li2bp1rtitdnvc04rmizyfg1txfh]], [[XiaoChuzhou|萧楚舟]] and [[NanxiWanxiang|南希]] describe a public field where menopause stories, books, expert claims, social-media posts, products, and hormone messages have become much more visible, but not equally reliable.

The concept extends [[InformationOverloadKnowledgeTrap]] into health literacy. Correct-sounding facts, personal anecdotes, translated books, and product claims can all be useful signals or bad shortcuts depending on how they are tested against clinical context, date, source, and the person's actual symptoms.

EP274's practical answer is multi-source checking rather than one authority replacement. A person should compare claims, notice whether a recommendation is outdated or product-driven, ask a menopause clinic or professional doctor when symptoms affect life, and remember that avoiding medication is not automatically risk-free.

## Key Claims
- More menopause information is not automatically better if it collapses into one product, one hormone story, or one influencer's experience.
- Books can be useful but may contain outdated science, translation artifacts, or source-context gaps.
- Social-media visibility can reduce shame while also flattening menopause into dramatic before-after stories or quick fixes.
- Medical uncertainty should push people toward structured questions, not toward either blind trust or total refusal.
- Risk and benefit have to be evaluated together; "not using medicine" can still carry risk if symptoms, bones, cardiovascular health, sleep, or mental state are worsening.
