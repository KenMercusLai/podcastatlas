# Progression-Free vs Overall Survival

Progression-free vs overall survival is the clinical-endpoint distinction emphasized in [[156-shengwu-yiyao-de-2026-dang-shichang-bu-zai-wei-bd-zaodong-zhongguo-yaoqi-de-xingchen-dahai-cai-ganggang-zhankai-lil-ugrzq8uvzviq3f8i-wm9ilup]]. [[XiaoPTeacher|小P老师]] explains PFS as time before disease progression, while OS asks whether patients live longer overall.

The distinction matters because a drug can shrink tumors or delay progression without yet proving a dramatic survival extension. The source applies this to [[Akeso|康方生物]], [[SummitTherapeutics|Summit]], and [[PD1VEGFBispecific|PD-1/VEGF bispecific]] interpretation: strong PFS can justify attention, but OS is harder to read and more decisive for the market's strongest claims.

## Key Claims
- PFS can respond more directly to tumor shrinkage and progression delay.
- OS is more patient-direct but can be confounded by follow-on treatment, patient condition, and trial design.
- A trial whose primary endpoint is PFS should not be interpreted as if it had already proven exceptional OS.
- Endpoint interpretation is central to [[BiotechBDDataValidationPhase]] because BD assets need more than one attractive number.
