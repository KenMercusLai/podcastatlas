# Freedom As Dignity / 自由作为尊严

Freedom as dignity / 自由作为尊严 is the source's reading of [[OrianaFallaci|Oriana Fallaci]] in [[77-ta-shi-ziyou-de-yegou-ta-shi-falaqi-685710187]]. The episode says Fallaci does not offer a final theory of freedom, and even admits absolute freedom cannot exist because humans live with management, love, dependence, and other people. Still, the pursuit of freedom remains necessary because giving it up means giving up dignity.

The concept appears in several scales. As a child, Fallaci rejects empty homeland language because dictators and torturers also claim to represent a homeland. As a journalist, she resists rulers' frames. As a woman, she rejects marriage, credentials, clothing rules, and reproductive expectation as automatic measures of value. In love, [[AlexandrosPanagoulis|Alexandros Panagoulis]] matters because his refusal makes dignity concrete.

## Key Claims
- Freedom is not treated as unlimited self-will; the source repeatedly keeps constraint and obligation in view.
- Dignity depends on being able to say no to power, even when refusal does not guarantee victory.
- The concept connects private life, journalism, political resistance, and gendered bodily agency rather than separating them into different moral vocabularies.
- A freedom vocabulary can be abused by institutions and factions, so the source tests freedom by what it permits people to do and say under pressure.
