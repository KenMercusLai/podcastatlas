# 救灾物资贪腐 / Disaster Relief Corruption

救灾物资贪腐 / disaster relief corruption enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-940-de-minxin-zhe-de-tianxia-ni-xin-bu-xin-loipf7b0p5a-yh0rj2njnsml8a1j|Hanji 940]] as the negative half of a late-Han flood-relief contrast. The episode says floods hit seven commandery-states, leaving homes, fields, and households damaged; some officials work to rescue people, while others steal relief materials and profit from disaster.

The concept names a specific failure inside [[DisasterResponseStateCapacity|disaster response state capacity]]. Aid has to exist, move, and reach people; if officials divert it, the disaster becomes a legitimacy and trust crisis as well as a material crisis. The source's moral language treats this as a test of character, but the institutional implication is that relief systems need accountability under stress.

This page also links the flood segment to [[NaturalHazardSocialDisaster|natural hazard as social disaster]]. Water damage starts the emergency, but official action determines whether the disaster becomes protection, abandonment, or predation.

## Key Claims

- Disaster relief can fail after resources are mobilized if distribution channels are corrupt.
- Corruption after disaster compounds harm because people are least able to replace stolen aid.
- Crisis exposes both administrative capacity and personal incentives: some officials become protectors, others become predators.
- Anti-corruption and public legibility are part of disaster response, not separate good-government add-ons.
