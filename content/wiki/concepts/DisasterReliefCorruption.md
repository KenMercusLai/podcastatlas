## Definition

救灾物资贪腐 / disaster relief corruption is the failure mode where disaster aid is diverted, falsified, or converted into private advantage by officials, turning relief into a state-capacity and legitimacy crisis.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-940-de-minxin-zhe-de-tianxia-ni-xin-bu-xin-loipf7b0p5a-yh0rj2njnsml8a1j|Hanji 940]] grounds the concept in a late-Han flood-relief contrast. Floods hit seven commandery-states; some officials work to rescue people, while others steal relief materials and profit from disaster. The source treats this as both a moral test and an administrative test: aid that exists on paper only matters if it reaches people under stress.

An earlier late-Xin famine branch appears in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-660-2-yi-ji-kan-dong-chimei-jun-de-dansheng-lgqk1gkvh-0cwder6g5-wryi6mce|Hanji 660-2]]. [[WangMang|王莽]] creates relief channels for refugees entering Guanzhong, but envoys and clerks steal grain, and the source says many refugees die. The same episode broadens corruption into information failure through [[WangYeXinOfficial|王业]], who extracts goods through market authority and then stages polished rice and meat soup to convince Wang Mang that ordinary people are eating well.

Across the two branches, the concept now names more than theft. Relief corruption includes diversion of supplies, predatory administration around scarcity, and false reporting that prevents the center from seeing need. It sits inside [[DisasterResponseStateCapacity]] because mobilization, delivery, monitoring, and truthful feedback are all part of actual relief.

## Key Claims

- Disaster relief can fail after resources are mobilized if distribution channels are corrupt.
- Corruption after disaster compounds harm because people are least able to replace stolen aid.
- Crisis exposes both administrative capacity and personal incentives: some officials become protectors, others become predators.
- False reporting can be part of relief corruption when it hides hunger and blocks correction.
- Anti-corruption, monitoring, and public legibility are part of disaster response, not separate good-government add-ons.

## Evidence

- Late-Han flood contrast: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-940-de-minxin-zhe-de-tianxia-ni-xin-bu-xin-loipf7b0p5a-yh0rj2njnsml8a1j|Hanji 940]] says floods hit seven commandery-states and that some officials rescue people while others steal relief materials.
- Disaster profiteering: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-940-de-minxin-zhe-de-tianxia-ni-xin-bu-xin-loipf7b0p5a-yh0rj2njnsml8a1j|Hanji 940]] uses the moral frame of profiting from national disaster to characterize relief theft.
- Xin famine relief theft: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-660-2-yi-ji-kan-dong-chimei-jun-de-dansheng-lgqk1gkvh-0cwder6g5-wryi6mce|Hanji 660-2]] says Wang Mang sets up grain distribution for refugees, but envoys and clerks steal grain and many refugees die.
- Market-predation branch: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-660-2-yi-ji-kan-dong-chimei-jun-de-dansheng-lgqk1gkvh-0cwder6g5-wryi6mce|Hanji 660-2]] says Wang Ye uses market authority to buy cheaply from civilians.
- False reassurance branch: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-660-2-yi-ji-kan-dong-chimei-jun-de-dansheng-lgqk1gkvh-0cwder6g5-wryi6mce|Hanji 660-2]] says Wang Ye shows Wang Mang polished rice and meat soup as if it represents ordinary food conditions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept should not imply that every failed relief effort is corrupt. Some failures come from mismatched supplies, transport breakdown, disease exposure, debt, or insufficient capacity. Hanji 940 supports a flood case where officials steal relief materials; Hanji 660-2 supports a famine case where relief grain is stolen and market information is falsified. Exact quantities, reporting channels, and the full institutional design of either relief system remain source-scoped.
