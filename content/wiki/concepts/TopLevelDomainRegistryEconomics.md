# Top-Level Domain Registry Economics

## Definition
Top-level domain registry economics is the system in which an operator pays to pursue and run an internet extension, then seeks value through public registrations, private use, brand control, community goals, or resale while bearing technical, legal, and demand risk.

## Current Synthesis
A top-level-domain string is not just a label. Control can create a wholesale registration business, a proprietary namespace, a defensive brand asset, or community infrastructure. Entry is expensive and technically demanding, contention can add auction cost, and winning does not guarantee delegation or adoption. `.com` retains a credibility and scarcity advantage, while alternatives compete through meaning, availability, identity, and wordplay.

## Key Claims
- Registry control and ownership of one ordinary domain are economically different positions.
- Application fees, technical operation, and auctions create a strong capital and capability filter.
- Public sale, private use, defensive control, community operation, and national revenue are distinct value models.
- Semantic fit can create demand, as AI did for `.ai`, but cultural salience can change faster than registry cost is recovered.
- Auction price is an input cost rather than evidence of launch, usage, or profitability.
- `.com` scarcity supports alternatives without eliminating `.com`'s trust and status advantage.

## Evidence
- Cost and contention - [[tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128]] cites a roughly $250,000 application cost, technical requirements, Google's $25 million `.app` auction, and the $135 million `.web` auction.
- Business-model variety - [[tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128]] contrasts public `.app` registrations, private `.google`, city and niche extensions, and [[DotMeowFoundation|`.meow`]].
- Failure and delay risk - [[tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128]] says `.web` remains unavailable after roughly a decade of legal dispute.
- Incumbent advantage - [[tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20261119-1119-mp-tech-pod-128]] describes `.com` as a credibility marker whose desirable names can be costly or unavailable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode provides examples, not registry financial statements or comparative renewal data. A memorable extension may fail to attract registrars or repeat users; a private brand extension may create control without direct registration revenue; and a high auction price may reflect strategic defense rather than expected standalone profit.

## What Changed
- Established a unified framework separating registry control from ordinary domain ownership.
- Added application, auction, delegation, distribution, adoption, renewal, and litigation as distinct economic stages.
