# Fragrance Product Storytelling / 香气产品叙事

Fragrance product storytelling / 香气产品叙事 is the work of making an invisible and hard-to-sample smell understandable through naming, packaging, retail space, social content, historical reference, and use scene. In [[ep263-xianxiang-jueqi-faxian-zhongshi-xiangqi-de-meili-lq1lsqcprqyr7auo5gslwyymuiv]], [[WuLiwei|吴立伟]] and the other speakers stress that fragrance products cannot rely on "酒香不怕巷子深"; customers need ways to imagine the smell before they buy or visit.

The concept explains why line incense and perfume brands invest in boxes, gifts, offline shops, Xiaohongshu stories, poetic names, lifestyle imagery, and product narratives such as "1934林徽因" or [[Guanxia|观夏]]'s mood-oriented names. The story does not replace smell, but it creates an entry point for a sensory product that cannot be fully transmitted through a screen.

## Key Claims
- Fragrance products need story because the core experience is hard to digitize and hard to judge from ingredient lists.
- Offline experience matters: customers often need a shop, counter, hotel lobby, or event to test whether a scent fits their body, room, or mood.
- A strong fragrance story can connect material, formula, character, place, season, or emotional promise.
- Storytelling has to be constrained by product proof; poetic copy cannot compensate for weak scent quality or unclear raw-material trust.
- Gift suitability raises the importance of packaging and named meaning because the giver needs to explain why the scent fits the recipient.
