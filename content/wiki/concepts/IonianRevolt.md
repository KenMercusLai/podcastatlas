# Ionian Revolt

## Definition

The Ionian Revolt is the late sixth-century BC uprising of Ionian Greeks against Persian rule that draws in [[Athens]] and Eretria, provokes [[DariusI]], and sets the road to the [[BattleOfMarathon]].

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents the revolt as a chain of local failures and imperial consequences. Ionian cities are under Persian authority because [[CyrusTheGreat]] conquered Lydia and [[Sardis]], but local rulers such as [[Histias]] and [[Aristagoras]] still operate through Greek elite ambition, tyrannical patronage, and pressure for democracy. Aristagoras's failed Naxos expedition leaves him exposed, so his turn against tyranny and toward democracy becomes a survival tactic with explosive results.

Once Athens and Eretria join, the revolt ceases to be a purely Ionian problem. The burning of Sardis and a temple of Cybele in 498 BC gives Darius a symbolic and religious affront to avenge. The Persian destruction of [[Miletus]] in 494 BC then shows the cost of failed rebellion and turns Marathon into the next stage of retaliation.

## Key Claims

- The revolt begins from local Ionian elite politics as much as from abstract freedom against empire.
- Aristagoras's democratic conversion is presented as tactical, but it still mobilizes real anti-tyrant action.
- Athenian intervention internationalizes the revolt and creates Darius's later revenge target.
- The burning of Sardis is the episode's decisive symbolic provocation.
- Miletus's destruction makes Persian punishment concrete for Athens.
- The revolt explains Marathon as retaliation after a longer imperial crisis, not as a sudden Persian attack.

## Evidence

- Local trigger: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] connects Aristagoras's failed Naxos expedition to his fear of Persian dismissal and local instability.
- Democratic mobilization: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] says Aristagoras abdicated tyranny and urged Ionian cities to overthrow tyrants.
- Athenian escalation: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] describes Aristagoras's appeal to Athens and the sending of twenty ships.
- Sardis provocation: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] treats the burning of Sardis as the event that made Persian retaliation unavoidable.
- Miletus punishment: [[668-greece-vs-persia-the-rise-of-the-first-superpower-part-1-glt8965800182]] describes Lade, the storming of Miletus, and the city's traumatic afterlife in Athens.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is source-scoped to one narrative-history episode. The motives of Aristagoras, Histias, Athens, Eretria, and Darius are interpretive, and the episode's vivid punishment details remain bounded to the source note.

## What Changed

- Created the Ionian Revolt as the pre-Marathon chain linking Persian client rule, democratic revolt, Sardis, Miletus, and Darius's revenge.
