# Decision Speed-Accuracy Control

## Definition
Decision speed-accuracy control is the deliberate matching of evidence accumulation, arousal, and decision ownership to whether a situation rewards rapid action or careful correctness.

## Current Synthesis
The episode describes choice as gathering evidence, integrating it with prior experience, assigning expected value, acting, and updating through prediction error. Faster thresholds can be useful under genuine time pressure but produce more mistakes when accuracy requires more evidence. High arousal and fatigue can increase noise or shift behavior toward speed, making breath-based down-regulation, extra time, checklists, or prepared external decision authority useful when the cost of error is high.

## Key Claims
- Value-based decisions integrate current evidence with stored experience and expected outcomes.
- Speed and accuracy are competing priorities rather than universally compatible goals.
- High arousal may amplify both useful signal and irrelevant noise.
- Fatigue can push decisions toward speed and away from accuracy.
- Offloading late-stage decisions can help when the executor is depleted but a coach or protocol remains prepared.

## Evidence
- Evidence accumulation - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] describes expected-value choice followed by prediction-error updating.
- Arousal control - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] recommends slowing down, breathing, and lowering arousal when accuracy matters.
- Fatigue study - [[how-to-make-better-decisions-dr-michael-platt-scim9606820714]] reports wrestlers favoring speed over accuracy as exertion accumulated and proposes coach-led late-period decisions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Slower is not always better: emergencies and practiced environments can reward rapid decisions, while an external authority can also be wrong or poorly informed. The source does not supply a universal arousal threshold or decision-offloading protocol.

## What Changed
- Created a state-sensitive framework for matching decision speed, accuracy, and ownership.
