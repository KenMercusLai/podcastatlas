# Film-to-Festival Feedback

## Definition

Film-to-festival feedback is the process by which a fictional or stylized screen representation becomes a model for later real-world public ritual, tourism, and civic performance.

## Current Synthesis

The episode's central case is the opening of *Spectre*. Its Mexico City Day of the Dead parade combined recognizable festival symbols into a large procession that did not yet exist in that form. After the film's release, authorities organized a real parade, and repetition turned cinematic expectation into a recurring public event.

The mechanism is circular rather than one-way. Film borrows cultural signs to create plausible spectacle; global audiences and tourism make that spectacle feel authentic; institutions then stage a version of it; and the new event becomes material for later memory and media.

## Key Claims

- Screen fiction can create expectations about what an authentic public tradition should look like.
- Tourism and international visibility give institutions incentives to materialize those expectations.
- The resulting event can become socially real despite its recent origin.
- Media-created ritual usually borrows older symbols, making novelty easy to mistake for continuity.
- Repetition can stabilize a feedback product into civic tradition.

## Evidence

- Fictional model: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] says the large central Mexico City procession in *Spectre* did not exist when filmed.
- Institutional adoption: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] says the government launched a real parade after the film's release.
- Cultural plausibility: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] shows the film drawing on skulls, costumes, flowers, and other already recognizable Dia de Muertos imagery.
- Repetition: [[264-mexico-day-of-the-dead-glt9022174156]] reports that the parade became an annual Mexico City event.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source gives a striking causal sequence but does not isolate film influence from tourism planning, local initiative, sponsorship, or earlier processional practices. The claim concerns the specific large parade form, not the invention of Dia de Muertos itself.

## What Changed

- Created a media-feedback mechanism that distinguishes a newly staged parade from the older festival it represents.
