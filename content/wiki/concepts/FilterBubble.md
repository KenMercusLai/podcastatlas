## Definition
A filter bubble is an information environment narrowed by algorithmic personalization around inferred preferences. It differs from an echo chamber, which emphasizes social reinforcement among similar people, and from the broader [[InformationCocoon]] frame, which can include deliberate user choice and cross-platform habits.

## Current Synthesis
Filter bubbles can exist without sealing users inside uniform ideology. Platform ranking may narrow particular content paths, including high-risk recommendation loops, while [[IncidentalExposure]], multi-platform use, and arguments with strangers make the wider environment leaky. The newest source adds a regulator's preliminary account: the [[EuropeanCommission]] says [[TikTok]]'s watch-time-led recommendations and ineffective controls can keep users in repetitive content loops, while TikTok disputes that characterization.

The central question is therefore not whether every user is isolated, but when platform preselection, user feedback, and social interaction produce measurable narrowing, harmful repetition, antagonistic exposure, or diversion of public attention. Product controls and regulatory remedies should be evaluated by whether they change exposure, not merely whether a setting exists.

## Key Claims
- Filter-bubble claims should be separated from polarization, misinformation, echo chambers, and hostile discussion.
- Algorithmic personalization can narrow some paths while exposing users to unexpected information or opposing views.
- User behavior and platform design interact: clicks, skips, follows, comments, content pools, ranking signals, and interface affordances all shape exposure.
- Weak evidence for universal ideological isolation does not make platforms neutral; [[AlgorithmicAmplification]] and [[AlgorithmicEntertainmentRedirect]] can still reshape attention and emotion.
- Known high-risk loops can justify product safeguards even when population-level bubble effects remain mixed.
- Cross-cutting exposure can increase conflict rather than understanding, so diversity of exposure is not identical to deliberative quality.
- User-control tools are not sufficient evidence of autonomy when the ranking objective continues to recreate the same exposure pattern.

## Evidence
- Conceptual and empirical distinction: [[164-suanfa-de-tuzi-dong-weishenme-ni-zong-zai-kanwan-xinwen-hou-huaxiang-yule-duitan-huangshengchun-jiaoshou-lo6ik8cj2njvu-cy2jdj-kl1jpuq]] separates bubbles from echo chambers, stresses mixed evidence, and adds [[IncidentalExposure]] and cross-platform leakage.
- Platform shaping without total enclosure: [[164-suanfa-de-tuzi-dong-weishenme-ni-zong-zai-kanwan-xinwen-hou-huaxiang-yule-duitan-huangshengchun-jiaoshou-lo6ik8cj2njvu-cy2jdj-kl1jpuq]] shows that preselected pools, ranking, feedback, and entertainment redirection remain powerful even when users encounter difference.
- High-risk recommendation loops: [[tech-20260812-0812-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260812-0812-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports that [[TikTok]] developed a safeguard against repeated extreme-dieting and self-harm content but withheld it from a test group.
- Regulatory product-effect claim: [[yingweida-qidong-dui-huangrenxun-de-diaocha-xiaofei-pinpai-jiti-kuajie-chongwu-shengyi-gkwrijiox3zoafmb9at4mvyn]] reports the European Commission's preliminary view that TikTok's similar-content loops may harm mental health and that available controls do not adequately reduce repetition; TikTok denies the allegation.
- Offline-versus-online comparison: [[94-silicon-valley-part-2-glt3075342575]] records Andreessen's argument that affinity groups coexist with unusually frequent exposure to strangers and disagreement online.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Chinese-language research discussion cautions against universal or stable filter-bubble claims but does not show that narrowing never occurs. The two TikTok sources concern a reported high-risk safety intervention and a preliminary regulatory finding, not a general measure of political isolation or a final legal decision. TikTok disputes the European Commission's claim. Andreessen's offline comparison is an interpretive claim rather than population evidence and does not distinguish algorithmic exposure from voluntary argument, harassment, or attention capture. Exposure diversity, belief change, emotional response, and safety should be measured separately.

## What Changed
- Added the European Commission's preliminary TikTok “rabbit-hole” allegation while preserving TikTok's denial.
- Distinguished nominal user controls from demonstrated changes in recommendation outcomes.
- Preserved the broader evidence boundary against universalizing platform-specific harmful loops.
