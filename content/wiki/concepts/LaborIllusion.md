## Definition

Labor illusion is the increase in perceived value or appreciation that can occur when customers can see evidence that work is being performed for them.

## Current Synthesis

Digital services often hide the activity between request and result. The [[DominosPizzaTracker|Domino's Pizza Tracker]] restores a simplified view of that activity, making the order feel attended to rather than lost in an opaque system. This mechanism can explain why a progress display improves an unchanged wait, but it does not justify displaying effort that did not occur.

## Key Claims

- Visibility of effort can change perceived value without changing the underlying output or duration.
- Progress stages can substitute for the physical-world experience of watching a worker perform a service.
- The effect depends on credibility: invented effort may produce short-term reassurance while damaging longer-term trust.
- Labor illusion and uncertainty reduction overlap but are not identical; one concerns perceived effort, the other concerns orientation and control.

## Evidence

### Visible work and appreciation

- [[the-dominos-pizza-tracker-theory-of-everything]] explicitly uses labor illusion to explain why watching an order advance can increase appreciation.

### Digital replacement for physical observability

- [[the-dominos-pizza-tracker-theory-of-everything]] contrasts tracker interfaces with physical transactions where customers can directly watch work.

### Credibility boundary

- [[the-dominos-pizza-tracker-theory-of-everything]] finds that the tracker mixed real events with fixed timing and imprecise attribution, showing that displayed effort can be partly representational.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The episode summarizes the research mechanism but does not present the underlying study design or effect size.
- Greater appreciation is not proof of better service quality.
- A display can make work visible and reduce uncertainty simultaneously, so the causal contribution of labor illusion may be difficult to isolate.

## What Changed

- Added labor illusion as the behavioral mechanism connecting work visibility to perceived service value.
- Added truthfulness as an explicit qualification on the use of visible-effort cues.
