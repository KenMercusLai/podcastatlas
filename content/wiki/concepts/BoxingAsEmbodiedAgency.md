# Boxing As Embodied Agency

Boxing as embodied agency is the body-practice concept extracted from [[88-bie-renshu-huizi-zai-ting-budao-hecai-de-shijie-li-huiquan-708510803]]. For [[KeikoBoxer|惠子]], boxing is not introduced as a childhood dream or abstract empowerment slogan. It begins as an affordable martial-arts choice and becomes a disciplined way to convert anger, fear, and sensory attention into repeatable practice.

The source's hearing-impaired boxing details make the concept specific. 惠子 cannot rely on ring sounds or wear hearing aids while fighting, so she reads faces, eyes, timing, and body motion. Her body is not merely overcoming disability; it is learning another perceptual route into the same sport.

The episode also resists triumphalism. 惠子's first professional win validates years of refusal and training, but her second fight forces her to admit pain, fear, and the desire to quit. Embodied agency therefore includes choice, defense, rest, and the possibility of stopping, not only endurance.

## Key Claims
- Body practice can turn diffuse anger into rule-bound attention.
- A sport can become a language when ordinary verbal or auditory channels are unreliable.
- Agency grows through training, coaching, and feedback rather than through self-belief alone.
- Winning is not the whole measure; standing in the ring, being assessed, learning defense, and choosing whether to continue all matter.
- The concept extends [[ActionAgainstAnxiety]] because action here is physical, paced, and continuously corrected by the body.
