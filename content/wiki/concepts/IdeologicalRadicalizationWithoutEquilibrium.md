# Ideological Radicalization Without Equilibrium

## Definition

Ideological radicalization without equilibrium is the pattern in which a regime's core goals reward continuing escalation and provide no internally legitimate stopping point at which a durable moderate order can emerge.

## Current Synthesis

The episode applies the pattern to [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]]. Hitler's worldview treated the First World War as unfinished, imagined racial and international enemies in apocalyptic terms, and connected national recovery to rearmament, territorial conquest, eastern expansion, removal, and mass killing. Early success did not satisfy that project; it increased authority and opened space for grander aims.

This does not mean every measure followed a fixed timetable or that strategy disappeared into ideology. The move against the Soviet Union had strategic as well as ideological reasoning, including British resistance and possible American entry. The narrower claim is that tactical calculation operated inside objectives that made stable moderation implausible. Delegated initiative under [[WorkingTowardsTheFuhrer|working towards the Führer]] could further intensify the process.

## Key Claims

- Some ideological systems treat success as permission for further escalation rather than completion.
- Broad leader direction and competitive implementation can radicalize policy without a detailed master schedule.
- Tactical calculation can coexist with objectives whose endpoint is structurally expansionary and violent.
- Temporary restraint or institutional sophistication does not by itself create a moderate equilibrium.
- Counterfactual stable-regime scenarios must confront the content of the regime's governing aims.

## Evidence

- Expansionary endpoint: [[64-hitler-with-ian-kershaw-part-2-glt5766781968]] presents war as unfinished business and Nazi enemies as international and apocalyptic.
- Success and escalation: [[64-hitler-with-ian-kershaw-part-2-glt5766781968]] links economic, military, and foreign-policy gains to increased authority and more radical ambition.
- Eastern program: [[64-hitler-with-ian-kershaw-part-2-glt5766781968]] cites General Plan East as evidence against a stable postwar Nazi Europe.
- Strategy inside ideology: [[64-hitler-with-ian-kershaw-part-2-glt5766781968]] gives the Soviet invasion both ideological and strategic purposes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept does not claim that every action was predetermined, that Nazi institutions never restrained timing or method, or that all participants understood the same endpoint. It also does not prove that every authoritarian ideology lacks equilibrium. General Plan East numbers, precise causal weights, counterfactual succession, and claims about what another German nationalist leader would have done remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Established a distinction between tactical calculation and an ideological project lacking a stable moderate endpoint.
- Added success-driven escalation and anticipatory implementation as mutually reinforcing mechanisms.
