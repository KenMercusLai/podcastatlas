# Heavy Agriculture and Light Commerce Policy / 重农轻商政策

Heavy agriculture and light commerce policy / 重农轻商政策 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-179-lishishang-heqin-zenme-laide-lslks-ovbomneornk4j-q6-ns0x1|Hanji 179]] as an early [[WesternHanDynasty|西汉]] governing move. The episode says Gaozu eighth-year policy restricted merchants' clothing, weapons, vehicles, and status display, making commerce a managed and degraded occupation relative to farming.

The source's interpretation is skeptical of simple suppression. It argues that commercial behavior is difficult to stop by decree; when official policy marks merchants as low-status but still needs goods, money, and exchange, trade may continue through official-merchant collusion or forms of "official commerce" instead of disappearing.

This concept differs from [[TradeHubStatecraft|商贸枢纽型治国]], where the state strengthens itself by attracting merchants and circulation. It also differs from [[StateResourceMonopoly|国家资源专营]], where private actors may still operate inside state-controlled resource rents. Here the source emphasizes status suppression and unintended adaptation under an agrarian political preference.

## Key Claims
- The policy raises agriculture as the favored productive base while lowering merchants' social and political standing.
- Restricting merchant display does not necessarily reduce exchange; it can drive commerce into official channels or collusive arrangements.
- Anti-merchant ideology can slow commercial development if it confuses moral hierarchy with economic function.
- The episode treats the policy as a source-scoped early Han example, not as a complete account of all Chinese commercial regulation.
