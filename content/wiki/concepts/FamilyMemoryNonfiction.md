# Family Memory Nonfiction / 家族记忆非虚构

Family memory nonfiction / 家族记忆非虚构 is the source's mode for turning family absence into historical inquiry. In [[05-ta-laizi-maliwuboer-ruguo-ni-kanjian-guo-wo-ceng-jiandao-de-533375925]], [[NataschaWodin|纳塔莎·沃丁]]'s search for her mother [[YevgeniaSheCameFromMariupol|叶弗根尼亚]] becomes a reconstruction of [[Mariupol]], Soviet repression, [[OstarbeiterForcedLabor|Eastern forced labor]], postwar displacement, and inherited silence.

The concept differs from memoir that relies only on remembered scenes. It combines [[PersonalArchiveAsHistory]] with literary narration: forum messages, relatives, state lists, photographs, notebooks, names, and songs are assembled so that the reader can see how large events broke a particular family.

## Key Claims
- Family history can become a legitimate route into political history when it stays grounded in traces, documents, and named people.
- The method restores agency to people who survive in archives only as victims, categories, or missing names.
- Fragmentary evidence does not remove narrative responsibility; it makes careful distinction between fact, inference, and silence more important.
- The strongest family-memory nonfiction connects private grief to public systems without dissolving either one.
