## Definition

Textual omission attribution shift / 史书删节与责任归属转移 is the source-critical pattern in which a later history's removal or compression of motive, knowledge, or responsibility changes which actor appears prudent, selfish, guilty, or victimized even when the remaining event sequence is broadly similar.

## Current Synthesis

The current evidence compares [[Shiji|《史记》]] with [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] across three cases. In the anti-Qin coalition, removal of [[ZhangEr|张耳]] and [[ChenYu|陈馀]]'s grievance makes their Zhao-centered advice look less privately motivated and shifts blame toward [[ChenSheng|陈胜]]. In the Jingxing campaign, shorter treatment of Chen Yu's repeated righteous-army language weakens the link between rejected tactics, moralized command, and concern for interstate reputation.

The family-responsibility case extends the pattern from motive to prior knowledge. The episode says the Shiji account makes [[LiuBang|刘邦]] aware of [[LiuChangHuainanKing|刘长]]'s mother's pregnancy but describes Zizhi Tongjian as omitting that knowledge. In the host's reading, the shorter version directs more responsibility toward [[LuZhi|吕雉]]'s jealousy and [[ShenYiji|审食其]]'s failed intercession; restoring Liu Bang's knowledge makes his refusal to act central and further weakens Liu Chang's later revenge rationale against Shen Yiji. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-1-loi3nr4qfrtssqayn-8qb7yjx1xx|Hanji 213 part 1]]

The concept does not treat every omission as falsification. Chronicle compression may serve genre, chronology, or selection goals. The operational rule is narrower: when an omitted clause concerns prior knowledge, grievance, stated principle, or agency, compare the fuller text before assigning motive or moral responsibility.

## Key Claims

- Omitted motive clauses can make interested political action appear more neutral or principled.
- Omitted statements of prior knowledge can shift responsibility away from a decision-maker and toward intermediaries.
- Restored language can change interpretation even when dates, actions, and outcomes remain unchanged.
- A later chronicle's compression should trigger comparison, not an automatic accusation of deliberate distortion.
- Source criticism should separate what the texts differ on from the host's further causal or moral judgment.

Coalition grievance and fragmentation:

Military principle and tactical refusal:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-156-bingxian-hanxin-de-fengshen-zhizhan-1-ltkz7bwqgkcqessdfbn9unf5e05l|Hanji 156 part 1]] restores fuller Shiji wording about Chen Yu's repeated refusal of deceptive or strange stratagems, changing how his rejection of [[LiZuoChe|李左车]]'s plan is read.

Knowledge and family responsibility:
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-213-gei-liubang-dai-lvmao-de-shenyiqi-xiachang-ruhe-1-loi3nr4qfrtssqayn-8qb7yjx1xx|Hanji 213 part 1]] says the fuller account includes Liu Bang's knowledge of the pregnancy and argues that omission shifts responsibility toward Lu Zhi and Shen Yiji.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

These are claims made in popular-history episode notes, not a complete collation of editions, manuscript traditions, or compiler principles. Exact wording, source dependence, chronology, and whether a clause was deliberately omitted rather than inherited through another textual path require direct textual comparison. Restoring a motive or knowledge clause does not by itself settle total culpability: Zhang Er and Chen Yu may have both grievance and strategic reasons; Chen Yu may combine moral language with military error; Liu Bang, Lu Zhi, Shen Yiji, and other intermediaries may bear different kinds of responsibility. The concept tracks attribution effects rather than proving authorial intent.

## What Changed

- Added prior knowledge, rather than motive alone, as a mechanism that reallocates responsibility.
- Extended the concept from coalition and battlefield interpretation into family and court culpability.
- Migrated the page to synthesis-v1 while preserving the complete source order.
