# Pancreatic Risk Communication Boundary / 胰腺风险沟通边界

## Definition
Pancreatic risk communication boundary separates modifiable risk context and symptom awareness from unsupported causal inference, diagnosis, and individualized treatment.

## Current Synthesis
The episode connects smoking, obesity, diabetes, recurrent or chronic pancreatitis, alcohol, and heavy or high-fat eating with pancreatic health. Those factors can motivate risk reduction or clinical review, but they do not show that a particular symptom is cancer or that one habit caused one person's disease.

The sharpest boundary concerns late-night eating. The guest reports seeing frequent night eating in some patients and offers an “organ overwork” explanation, while explicitly acknowledging that he lacks substantial evidence. The observation is therefore retained as a source-scoped hypothesis and general routine reminder, not as a pancreatic-cancer mechanism or screening rule.

## Key Claims
- Risk-factor discussion should support prevention and assessment without turning association into diagnosis or individual causation.
- Smoking, obesity, diabetes, and recurrent or chronic pancreatitis are risk contexts named by the episode, not proof of pancreatic cancer.
- Heavy eating and alcohol can matter to acute pancreatic symptoms, but a possible trigger does not confirm pancreatitis.
- Late-night eating as a cause of pancreatic cancer is an explicitly unsupported source observation.
- Persistent upper-abdominal or back pain, vomiting, fever, jaundice, weight change, or glucose abnormality needs symptom-specific clinical interpretation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is derived from one structured podcast summary and is not a risk calculator, screening guideline, or diagnostic algorithm. The source does not quantify risk, distinguish new-onset from longstanding diabetes, establish the guest's claimed increase or age shift in pancreatic surgery, or demonstrate that night eating, “organ rest,” chilling, food oiliness, or any single behavior causes pancreatic cancer. Symptoms overlap with many gastrointestinal, hepatobiliary, cardiac, vascular, metabolic, musculoskeletal, and surgical conditions.

## What Changed
- Added a boundary separating pancreatic risk awareness from diagnosis and causal attribution.
- Classified the late-night-eating hypothesis as explicitly source-scoped rather than established.
- Connected lifestyle discussion to symptom-based escalation instead of supplement or “organ overwork” narratives.
