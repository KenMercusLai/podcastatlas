## Definition

Fair allocation as political skill / 公平分配式政治能力 is the ability to turn contested benefits into accepted outcomes by weighing contribution, rank, relationship, place, quality, and public feeling. It treats distribution as a visible governance act, not a neutral arithmetic task.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 150 part 1 grounds the concept at village scale through [[ChenPing|陈平]]'s role in a local社祭. After the sacrifice, the community has Chen Ping divide the meat. He distributes it fairly enough that the villagers praise him as good at performing the sacrificial steward's role, and the host reads the scene as an early sign that Chen Ping can allocate contested benefits without alienating participants.

Hanji 150 part 2 carries that ability from local trust into camp and court politics. The episode connects Chen Ping's meat distribution to later reward and fief distribution among [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s followers. The mechanism is the same at a larger scale: internal cohesion depends on making benefits feel properly ranked, intelligible, and acceptable to the people comparing their shares.

Hanji 683 now supplies the Eastern Han founding analogue through Feng Qin. After [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] decides to reward merit holders, Feng Qin handles fief allocation by evaluating each person's merit, the distance of assigned land, and the land's fertility. The episode says true fairness is impossible, but Feng Qin's work leaves many people satisfied and makes Liu Xiu entrust him with further Shangshu affairs.

The current synthesis is therefore cross-scale. Chen Ping shows that allocation skill can make local reputation visible before office; Feng Qin shows that founding reward order needs administrative mediation after battlefield merit has been recognized. Fairness here means credible, accepted adjustment among unequal claims, not equal shares.

## Key Claims

- Allocation is a test of political capacity because it turns abstract fairness into visible outcomes.
- Fairness is not sameness; it requires comparing contribution, rank, location, quality, and social expectations.
- Small public tasks can reveal whether someone understands hierarchy, feeling, and practical execution.
- Large reward programs need administrative mediators because ruler intent alone does not decide whether recipients accept the details.
- Allocation skill builds trust when participants can see that differences follow legible reasons rather than private favoritism.
- The concept is the positive counterpart to reward allocation backlash: the same distribution arena can stabilize insiders or create resentment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept should not be read as a formula for perfect justice. Hanji 683 explicitly qualifies that there is no true perfect fairness, and neither the Chen Ping nor Feng Qin evidence gives a complete allocation table. The current sources show that public acceptance can be improved through visible judgment, not that resentment disappears. Feng Qin remains source-scoped here because the episode gives his allocation role but not a full biography.
