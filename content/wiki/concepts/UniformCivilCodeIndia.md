# Uniform Civil Code in India

Uniform civil code in India is the state-level legal project described in [[settler-bet-a-west-bank-flashpoint-6a842bb539e73ea8f3c13a2d]], where citizens are supposed to follow the same civil laws regardless of religion. [[KiraHuyu|Kira Huju]] says the idea has a liberal constitutional appeal but, in current BJP-ruled state practice, often makes minorities conform to rules rooted in Hindu legal traditions.

The source's first concrete case is [[Uttarakhand]], where [[IndianMuslims]] have been barred from following many customary family-law practices since January 2025. The episode then broadens to [[Assam]], [[Gujarat]], [[MadhyaPradesh]], and possible future tests such as [[WestBengal]].

## Key Claims
- Uniform law can carry a rights-based promise, especially around inheritance and polygamy.
- The source treats current state-level adoption as politically asymmetric because many [[IndianMuslims|Muslims]] must change marriage, divorce, and inheritance practices while the majority population changes less.
- Tribal exemptions weaken the claim that the codes are fully uniform.
- [[LiveInRelationshipRegistration]] turns the policy from family-law reform into a surveillance question.
- The politics of implementation can push minorities away from the legal mainstream if they experience the code as coercive conformity.
