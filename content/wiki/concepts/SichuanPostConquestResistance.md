# 川蜀征服后反抗 / Sichuan Post-Conquest Resistance

Sichuan post-conquest resistance / 川蜀征服后反抗 is the episode's explanation for why the Song conquest of Shu produced recurring unrest. [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115]] argues that the problem was not simply local refusal to accept unification, but the interaction of military abuse, inherited tax burdens, and state monopolies on necessities such as tea.

The source centers the violence of commanders such as [[WangQuanbin|王全斌]] and the escalation around [[QuanShixiong|全师雄]]. Plunder, killing, family destruction, and coercive sexual violence are presented as political causes because they made rebellion an intelligible response to conquest.

This concept keeps the wiki's [[SongUnificationStrategy|先南后北]] branch from becoming only a top-down strategy story. A conquest can be rational for the central state and still disastrous for a region when victory is followed by extraction and indiscipline.

## Key Claims
- Local rebellion after conquest can come from material injury rather than cultural backwardness or mere separatism.
- Military indiscipline converts strategic victory into governance failure.
- State monopolies and inherited tax burdens can continue conquest violence in administrative form.
- A dynasty's unification story should include the costs borne by conquered populations.
