# Dementia-Friendly Home Design / 认知症友好居家环境

## Definition
Dementia-friendly home design is the adjustment of lighting, visibility, contrast, layout, and physical hazards so a person with cognitive and sensory change can recognize objects, move more safely, and retain more independence.

## Current Synthesis
The episode treats environmental change as a form of cognitive support. A home that is dim, visually ambiguous, or cluttered can convert sensory and recognition difficulty into avoidable dependence or falls. Better lighting is therefore the first priority when only one change is possible.

The remaining practices are simple but functional: remove loose rugs, cables, and low furniture from walking paths, and increase visual contrast between important objects and their surroundings. A dark bowl can make white rice easier to see; a contrasting toilet seat can make the fixture easier to identify.

The household-care episode extends the environment beyond visibility. Hazardous objects, exits, and wandering risk may need adaptation; familiar routine and bright, recognizable clothing or tableware can also support mood, appetite, and orientation. The aim remains supported independence rather than confinement, with safeguards matched to the person's actual behavior and household.

## Key Claims
- Environmental difficulty can amplify cognitive and sensory impairment.
- Better lighting is a high-priority first change for visibility and orientation.
- Removing trip hazards reduces avoidable physical risk.
- Strong color contrast can make food, fixtures, and everyday objects easier to recognize.
- Design should support remaining independence rather than only contain risk.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not compare intervention studies, building standards, wandering safeguards, bathroom modifications, or occupational-therapy assessment. Home changes should be individualized for vision, mobility, behavior, housing, and caregiver constraints; contrast and lighting are not substitutes for clinical or professional safety review.

- Added routine, appetite and mood cueing, dangerous-object control, and wandering safeguards while preserving the independence goal.
