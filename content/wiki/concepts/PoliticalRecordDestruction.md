# Political Record Destruction

## Definition

Political record destruction is the deliberate attack on charters, court rolls, debt instruments, administrative files, or institutional archives because records make hierarchy, obligation, ownership, and legal authority enforceable.

## Current Synthesis

During the [[PeasantsRevolt1381|Peasants’ Revolt]], rebels repeatedly burned charters, court rolls, chancery material, and other writings. The pattern appeared at Lambeth Palace and continued beyond London, including the destruction of Cambridge university records. These were not merely symbolic objects: documents carried claims about servitude, rent, debt, jurisdiction, and privilege.

The tactic therefore sits between material and symbolic politics. Destroying records could obstruct enforcement while dramatizing rejection of the institutions that produced them. The episode’s account of rebels destroying luxury goods while punishing looters strengthens this interpretation, though it does not prove that every fire had a single disciplined purpose.

## Key Claims

- Administrative documents are instruments of power as well as repositories of information.
- Destroying records can target enforceability of status, debt, rent, jurisdiction, and privilege.
- Repetition across sites makes documentary destruction a recognizable tactic rather than incidental riot damage.
- Anti-looting discipline can distinguish political destruction from private appropriation without making the violence benign.

## Evidence

- Lambeth and London: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] links the sack of Lambeth Palace and repeated burning of charters, rolls, and writings to hostility toward lordship and authority.
- Cambridge extension: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] records destruction of pre-1381 university documents during the wider rising.
- Destruction versus theft: [[414-the-peasants-revolt-londons-burning-part-2-glt6726049231]] reports the destruction of Savoy luxury goods and punishment of looters.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The political reading is an inference from repeated target selection and the functions of the documents. It should not imply uniform planning, full understanding of every record, or a clean boundary between principled action, intimidation, revenge, and disorder. Destroying evidence could also harm people whose claims depended on those archives.

## What Changed

- Established record burning as an attack on the operational infrastructure of lordship rather than treating it as undifferentiated vandalism.
