# Go Fever Organizational Risk / 硬干狂热

## Definition

Go fever is an organizational escalation pattern in which accumulated investment, public commitment, schedule pressure, and prior momentum make continuing feel safer or more legitimate than pausing, even when unresolved warnings are multiplying.

## Current Synthesis

In [[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]], Challenger is the central case. NASA and its contractors had reasons to protect a promised launch cadence and public mission narrative, while engineers lacked a sufficiently authoritative path from uncertainty to a stop decision. Go fever converts those conditions into action bias: prior success and investment narrow the perceived option set until delay is misread as institutional failure rather than safety control.

## Key Claims

- Sunk investment can make stopping feel more costly even though it cannot reduce the underlying technical risk.
- Public schedules and promotional commitments can turn an engineering decision into a reputational test.
- Repeated partial anomalies can be normalized when earlier missions avoided catastrophe.
- Warning systems fail when engineers must prove an exact failure threshold before decision-makers will pause.
- A credible stop rule must assign authority, evidence thresholds, and consequences before schedule pressure peaks.
- Post-accident component fixes are insufficient if cadence, incentives, communication, and decision rights remain unchanged.

## Evidence

### Commitment and schedule pressure
- [[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]] connects Challenger's launch to prior investment, promised launch frequency, mission windows, and public messaging.

### Warning-to-decision failure
- [[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]] describes low-temperature seal warnings and ice concerns that did not receive controlling weight because uncertainty was treated as insufficient proof of failure.

### Systemic rather than component-only correction
- [[502-yuzhou-chaoji-gongcheng-shi-ruhe-kua-diao-de-liuyi-tan-tiaozhanzhehao-shigu-sishi-zhounian-loylfmnxyc-cuajgcn12-v7-ybd]] uses the later Columbia disaster to argue that replacing or inspecting one component does not automatically repair the institutional pattern.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- Proceeding under uncertainty is not automatically go fever; complex missions cannot eliminate all risk before action.
- A delay can itself carry safety, financial, or mission costs, so the concept does not make stopping universally correct.
- The source does not quantify how much each political, publicity, contractor, or schedule factor changed the final launch decision.
- “Go fever” is an interpretive organizational label, not a substitute for reconstructing the actual decision chain and technical evidence.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from the Challenger episode's synthesis of commitment escalation and suppressed stopping decisions.
