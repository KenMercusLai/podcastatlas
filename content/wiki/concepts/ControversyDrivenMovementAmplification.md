# Controversy-Driven Movement Amplification

## Definition
Controversy-driven movement amplification occurs when criticism, protest, or investigative coverage intended to expose a movement also increases its visibility, strengthens its grievance narrative, and helps it recruit supporters or sell movement-aligned products.

## Current Synthesis
The episode presents a two-sided publicity problem. National reporting and local opposition make [[DougWilson]]'s teachings, institutions, and alleged harms more visible to scrutiny. The same attention reaches people attracted to the movement's certainty, hierarchy, or resistance to mainstream culture; Wilson says media attention has been highly beneficial, and the source connects pandemic-era protest coverage to incoming families.

[[CanonPress]] supplies a conversion mechanism between attention and durable capacity. Provocative media can point audiences toward books, curricula, podcasts, events, and a broader community. Amplification is therefore not the claim that journalists cause the movement or should ignore it. It is a reason to distinguish exposure, audience growth, persuasion, revenue, recruitment, accountability, and harm when evaluating coverage.

## Key Claims
- Adverse attention can increase reach without producing agreement among most viewers.
- A movement can reinterpret criticism as evidence of persecution and use it to strengthen internal solidarity.
- Owned products and media channels make it easier to convert attention into audience, revenue, and recruitment.
- Local backlash can repel some observers while attracting others who identify with the targeted group.
- Public-interest reporting and movement benefit can occur at the same time.
- Evaluation should separate exposure from persuasion, conversion, accountability, and net social effect.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies participant claims and observed sequence rather than audience analytics or causal estimates. Growth could also reflect family networks, housing, schooling, national politics, dissatisfaction with other churches, or pre-existing online reach. Coverage may deter recruits, inform neighbors, surface allegations, or improve accountability even when it also expands awareness. The concept should not become a blanket argument against reporting controversial movements.

## What Changed
- Established a movement-level amplification model that preserves both accountability benefits and recruitment risk.
- Identified owned media and products as the bridge between transient attention and durable capacity.
