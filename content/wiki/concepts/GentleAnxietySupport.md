# Gentle Anxiety Support / 温柔靠近焦虑

## Definition
Gentle anxiety support is the relational posture of approaching an anxious person slowly enough that help does not become another stimulus, while still offering presence, grounding, naming, and repair.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that anxious people often cannot use advice until their body and relational field feel less threatened. A helper who rushes to fix, persuade, or demand recovery may be reacting to their own contagious anxiety and may increase the pressure on the anxious person.

Gentleness here is not passivity. It includes concrete behaviors: slowing speech, reducing urgency, sitting with the person's reality, helping the problem become smaller, noticing deeper shame or hurt beneath surface anger, and repairing later when the first response was too rushed.

## Key Claims
- Fast help can become additional stimulation when the anxious person is already overloaded.
- The helper's anxiety can drive premature advice, impatience, or pressure for the other person to improve.
- Gentle support begins by reducing threat and increasing felt companionship before analysis.
- Intimate anxiety often hides deeper shame, anger, grievance, loneliness, or fear beneath visible irritability.
- Repair after a bad response can still matter because no partner, family member, or helper supports perfectly every time.
- Supportive attention can be accumulated through daily self-affirmation, warm witnesses, and relationships that do not only criticize.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Gentle support should not become avoidance of real risk, enabling harmful behavior, or substitute therapy in severe distress. The episode is a public conversation, so crisis risk, abuse, panic disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder, eating disorders, and medical issues still require appropriate professional boundaries.
