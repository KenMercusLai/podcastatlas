# Private Medical Acquisition Incentives / 民营医疗获客激励

## Definition
Private medical acquisition incentives describe how advertising, lead generation, referral commissions, brand borrowing, rent, equipment, and staffing costs can shape what a provider must earn from each acquired patient.

## Current Synthesis
Acquisition is not itself misconduct, but the episode shows a dangerous configuration: expensive traffic meets severe information asymmetry, weak credential visibility, high-margin procedures, and limited continuity. In that setting, false diagnosis, excessive testing, prolonged treatment, low-price inducement, and operating-table escalation can become revenue tactics rather than isolated bedside errors.

## Key Claims
- Early street notices and television advertising evolved into measured source attribution and online lead collection.
- Public-hospital names, facilities, clinicians, and equipment could lend private operators institutional authority while obscuring commercial control.
- Large acquisition, rent, contracting, equipment, and referral costs increase the pressure to raise revenue per patient.
- Privacy-sensitive or anxiety-heavy conditions make patients especially vulnerable to authoritative claims and rapid treatment decisions.
- Low advertised prices can function as an entry mechanism when the true revenue event occurs after examination or during a procedure.
- Search ranking and hospital branding can be mistaken for clinical validation, turning platform design into a patient-safety issue.

## Evidence
- **Measured traffic:** Zhan Guotuan's television-ad experiment and monthly lead-source accounting show acquisition as an operational discipline rather than incidental promotion. [[gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox-gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox]]
- **Institutional authority:** Department contracting and equipment cooperation joined private incentives to public-hospital facilities and brands. [[gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox-gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox]]
- **Revenue escalation:** Infertility, gynecology, and male-hospital cases show excessive treatment, guided payment, misleading low-price promises, and charges added while a patient was on the operating table. [[gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox-gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox]]
- **Platform amplification:** The [[WeiZexiIncident|魏则西事件]] connected paid search, unclear promotional labeling, hospital cooperation, and misleading treatment claims. [[gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox-gkwrimaorinia5ejiwtsfhox]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- High advertising spend or private ownership does not prove overdiagnosis, fraud, or clinical harm; case-level evidence and current control still matter.
- The source does not provide a comparative dataset measuring whether acquisition cost predicts misconduct across private hospitals.
- Public hospitals, platforms, regulators, intermediaries, and clinicians can each contribute to or constrain the incentive chain; it is not reducible to one hometown network.

## What Changed
- The wiki now treats patient acquisition cost as a clinical-governance variable, not only a marketing metric.
- Hospital branding and search ranking are separated from evidence of provider quality.
- Operating-table escalation is placed at the extreme end of a broader lead-to-revenue funnel.
