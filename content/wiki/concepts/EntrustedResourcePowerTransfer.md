# Entrusted-Resource Power Transfer / 受托资源反向转移

## Definition

Entrusted-resource power transfer occurs when a ruler gives an invited ally troops, supplies, territory, or delegated command for defense, but those resources help the ally build an independent local position capable of displacing the ruler.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 1124 supplies the warning stage. As fear of [[CaoCao|曹操]] taking [[Hanzhong|汉中]] narrows [[LiuZhang|刘璋]]'s perceived options, [[ZhangSongLateHan|张松]] presents [[LiuBei|刘备]] as the necessary defender. [[HuangQuanLateHan|黄权]] identifies the structural dilemma: Liu Bei is too substantial to remain content as an ordinary subordinate, but honoring and securing him creates a rival center. [[WangLeiLateHan|王累]] also protests, yet the invitation proceeds.

Hanji 1125 presents the material stage. Liu Zhang funds Liu Bei's entry, provides additional troops and military supplies, assigns Baishui forces to his command, and gives him a northern mission. Liu Bei then pauses at Jiameng, distributes favor, and cultivates support instead of immediately completing the assigned attack.

The transfer is “reverse” because the principal's attempt to buy security strengthens the agent's autonomy. The risk is highest when the invited force already has independent leadership, a portable reputation, internal sponsors, and a territorial objective of its own.

## Key Claims

- Defensive delegation can shift practical power even when formal titles remain with the host ruler.
- Threat framing can compress the host's decision space until a structurally dangerous ally appears to be the only usable option.
- Troops, supplies, command authority, and local access reinforce one another more strongly than any single transfer.
- An invited ally is especially dangerous when insiders already support its succession or entry.
- The agent can convert an assigned frontier mission into time and space for local coalition building.
- The pattern is a risk framework, not proof that every external military delegation ends in takeover.

## Evidence

- Pre-delegation warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1124-liuzhang-qing-liubei-ru-shu-shi-qiusheng-haishi-songsi-lkzau4jsd4ocgkgw8cg9jjwshhlm|Hanji 1124]] says Huang Quan warns that Liu Bei cannot be safely treated as either an ordinary subordinate or an honored guest, while Wang Lei also opposes the invitation.

- Material delegation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1125-bing-bu-xueren-zhuo-liuzhang-liubei-weihe-bu-yuanyi-lsaakh0scmfvorjhhtjv5j4m0kcb|Hanji 1125]] says Liu Zhang provides Liu Bei with entry support, troops, supplies, and command over Baishui forces.
- Mission repurposing: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1125-bing-bu-xueren-zhuo-liuzhang-liubei-weihe-bu-yuanyi-lsaakh0scmfvorjhhtjv5j4m0kcb|Hanji 1125]] says Liu Bei reaches Jiameng but cultivates support rather than immediately attacking Zhang Lu.
- Recognized danger: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1125-bing-bu-xueren-zhuo-liuzhang-liubei-weihe-bu-yuanyi-lsaakh0scmfvorjhhtjv5j4m0kcb|Hanji 1125]] attributes to [[YanYanLateHan|严颜]] the warning that Liu Zhang is releasing a tiger to defend himself.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episodes compress threat chronology, dialogue, offices, force totals, supply arrangements, and northern command. They do not show which transfers were reversible, how much direct control Liu Zhang retained, or whether Liu Bei's Jiameng activity displaced all military preparation against Zhang Lu. External assistance may still be rational when the host has no stronger defense option, and correct warnings do not by themselves prove that a feasible safer alternative was available.

## What Changed

- Added the pre-delegation stage in which threat framing makes a dangerous protector appear necessary.
- Added Huang Quan's dual-power warning and Wang Lei's protest as evidence that the structural risk was recognized before resources moved.
- Preserved the material mechanism linking troops, supplies, command, local coalition building, and eventual displacement.
