## Definition
中国职业足球联赛扩军 / Chinese football league expansion is the policy question of whether and how Chinese professional leagues should increase club counts, especially moving [[ChineseSuperLeague]] and [[ChineseLeagueOne]] from 16 toward 18 teams.

## Current Synthesis
The source supports expansion but treats it as a system-design problem. Expansion should be announced before the relevant season, paired across the top two tiers, and implemented through transition rules that preserve competitive pressure. Its value is not only more teams; it is a way to reduce late-season dead zones, improve promotion-relegation incentives, and give recovering city markets more access to the top-flight platform.

## Key Claims
- Expansion is plausible because the source says Chinese professional football has moved out of its low point and top-flight attention is recovering.
- The top two tiers should be considered together; expanding only [[ChineseSuperLeague]] risks weakening the promotion-relegation bridge.
- Rule timing matters: sudden mid-cycle changes to promotion and relegation would damage fairness and credibility.
- Playoff or mini-tournament transition designs can preserve pressure better than a simple one-down, three-up formula.
- Expansion needs finance, calendar, foreign-player, and lower-tier reforms alongside the headline club-count change.

## Evidence
- Market recovery: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] frames 2025 as a clear rebound year and cites stronger ball-market attention.
- Two-tier linkage: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] proposes synchronized Chinese Super League and China League One expansion to 18 clubs.
- Fairness rule: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] rejects immediate ad hoc expansion because clubs should compete under known rules.
- Transition design: [[vol-226-zhongchao-kuojun-yu-kuanianzhi-liansai-xiayibu-gai-zenme-zou-ft-chenyong-gkwrimaoef2yadmp9qtvpacu]] favors involving top-flight relegation candidates and second-tier promotion candidates in playoffs or round-robin competition.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a policy conversation, not an adopted regulation. Expansion is qualified by unresolved club finances, incomplete lower-tier match supply, uncertain B-team policy, stadium and city-market variation, and the possibility that more teams could dilute competition if governance does not improve.
