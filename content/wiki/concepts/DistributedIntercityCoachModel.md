# Distributed Intercity Coach Model / 分散站点城际大巴模式

## Definition
The distributed intercity coach model replaces dependence on one traditional bus terminal at each end with multiple pickup and drop-off points near urban transit, combining low fares with shorter access legs and broader origin-destination coverage.

## Current Synthesis
The Pearl River Delta case in [[gkwrijiorooxafjycwtsvi3u-gkwrijiorooxafjycwtsvi3u]] treats route design as the real product. Coaches between Shenzhen and Guangzhou reportedly charge as little as RMB 9.9, offer free Wi-Fi, and stop near several metro stations. Their advantage is not necessarily the fastest trunk journey: it comes from reducing the time and cost passengers spend reaching a central coach station and from matching increasingly dispersed travel demand. The model is therefore a network redesign response to competition from rail, private cars, carpooling, and ride-hailing.

## Key Claims
- Low fares attract attention, but distributed stops can be the more durable convenience advantage.
- Door-to-door travel time should include access, waiting, transfer, and final-mile legs rather than only scheduled trunk speed.
- Multiple metro-adjacent stops let a fixed-route service imitate some point-to-point convenience without becoming a private car service.
- Traditional terminal, route, and timetable structures can become less competitive as passenger origins and destinations disperse.
- The model trades terminal concentration for more complex curb access, stop coordination, reliability, and local compliance requirements.

## Evidence
- **Fare and service proposition:** [[gkwrijiorooxafjycwtsvi3u-gkwrijiorooxafjycwtsvi3u]] reports Shenzhen-Guangzhou fares from RMB 9.9 to the low twenties plus free Wi-Fi.
- **Network design:** [[gkwrijiorooxafjycwtsvi3u-gkwrijiorooxafjycwtsvi3u]] says newer routes reduce reliance on traditional coach stations and place stops near multiple metro stations in both cities.
- **Competitive context:** [[gkwrijiorooxafjycwtsvi3u-gkwrijiorooxafjycwtsvi3u]] attributes pressure on traditional coaches to high-speed rail, private cars, carpooling, and ride-hailing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide operator economics, load factors, traffic reliability, terminal fees avoided, curb-use rules, safety records, labor conditions, or evidence that promotional fares persist. A two-hour all-in trip may be attractive for some origin-destination pairs but not others, so competitiveness remains route- and passenger-specific.
