# Local Cinema Community Scaling

## Definition
Local cinema community scaling is the organizational problem of expanding a theater brand while preserving locally meaningful programming, audience relationships, and creative autonomy through a shared operational backbone.

## Current Synthesis
The Alamo case separates two kinds of capability that expansion requires. Local cinema identity grows through staff taste, unusual programming, creator relationships, and events that may pay back through loyalty and press rather than immediate margin. Multi-location survival requires accounting, legal agreements, kitchen design, service systems, lease discipline, and repeatable operations.

Neither side is sufficient alone. The founders' independent Austin operations protected creative freedom but created comparison and contract conflict with franchise locations; the outside team supplied systems but reportedly struggled to reproduce the Austin identity. Recombination became plausible only after an investor understood the difference between short-term event profit and long-term brand investment. The resulting ideal is not total uniformity but a network with clear shared standards and bounded local discretion.

## Key Claims
- Local programming can create durable brand and community value even when individual events have thin direct margins.
- Shared systems are necessary for scale, especially where one venue combines exhibition, kitchens, alcohol, staffing, and leases.
- Franchising can expand capital and operating reach while creating incentives to standardize away the local qualities customers value.
- Local autonomy needs explicit contractual and operating boundaries rather than dependence on founder preference.
- Crisis can expose whether expansion rests on resilient economics or on debt, expensive leases, and continuing traffic growth.

## Evidence
- Community investment: [[alamo-drafthouse-cinema-tim-and-karrie-league-2023-7bc6885b-e29f-4ae8-a587-2e17b1d8bea3]] describes theme meals, roadshows, festivals, and creator events that often needed to sell out for direct profit but generated identity and coverage.
- Organizational complementarity: [[alamo-drafthouse-cinema-tim-and-karrie-league-2023-7bc6885b-e29f-4ae8-a587-2e17b1d8bea3]] contrasts the founders' brand and programming strengths with the franchise team's systems, accounting, and operations.
- Scale fragility: [[alamo-drafthouse-cinema-tim-and-karrie-league-2023-7bc6885b-e29f-4ae8-a587-2e17b1d8bea3]] links pre-pandemic expansion, debt, staffing, and leases to the severity of shutdown and Chapter 11 restructuring.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence comes primarily from the founders' side of a franchise dispute and does not include franchisee records, location-level results, creditor analysis, or customer comparisons. Recombination and later survival do not prove that the autonomy-versus-standardization balance was solved, and pandemic shock should not be treated as evidence that every earlier expansion decision was unsound.

## What Changed
- Created a cinema-specific scale framework that treats local programming and central systems as complementary capabilities.
- Added contract design, financial resilience, and crisis exposure to the community-brand account.
