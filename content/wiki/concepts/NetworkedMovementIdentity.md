## Definition
Networked movement identity is the pattern in which an online formation becomes more than an information channel by supplying participants with belonging, purpose, practices, leaders, allies, enemies, and routes into political, consumer, or financial action.

## Current Synthesis
The concept links weakened offline sources of meaning with digitally coordinated participation. Political narratives, cryptocurrencies, founders, and companies can all acquire movement-like qualities when following, posting, buying, holding, recruiting, and defending become identity performances. This does not make every enthusiastic community a cult or prove that collective belief determines financial value; the useful question is which social mechanisms convert attention into durable commitment and real-world behavior.

## Key Claims
- Online groups can supply community and moral orientation as well as information.
- Participation becomes movement-like when members enact identity through repeated social and material practices.
- Digital narratives can spill into elections, legislation, consumption, and asset markets.
- Founder or brand identification can turn customers and investors into message carriers.
- Movement language requires evidence of social commitment beyond popularity or fandom.

## Evidence
- Meaning and belonging: [[94-silicon-valley-part-2-glt3075342575]] connects online movements to weakened family, tribal, cultural, and religious sources of cohesion.
- Political spillover: [[94-silicon-valley-part-2-glt3075342575]] describes QAnon as an online participatory narrative with real-world political action.
- Market and company examples: [[94-silicon-valley-part-2-glt3075342575]] treats [[Bitcoin]] and [[Tesla]] as movements whose supporters follow, advocate, purchase, invest, and engage politically.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode uses “movement” and “cult” provocatively rather than through a defined comparative method. It does not measure participant motives, distinguish loose fandom from costly commitment, or establish that movement dynamics explain [[Tesla]]'s valuation. Older brands and political communities also generated identity before the internet, so networking may change scale and coordination without creating the underlying social need.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded account of how online belonging can become political, consumer, or financial action.
- Added a gate separating movement identity from ordinary popularity or brand enthusiasm.
