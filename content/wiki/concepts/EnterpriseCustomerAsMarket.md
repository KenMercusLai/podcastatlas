# Enterprise Customer As Market

Enterprise customer as market is the view that one large organization can contain enough teams, workflows, document types, approvals, and expansion paths to behave like a market in itself. In [[enterprise-sales-with-no-product-landing-a-big-four-customer]], [[ChristianLund]] says [[Templafy]] pushed toward broad company-wide adoption rather than treating a small team pilot as the natural starting point.

The concept explains why [[EnterprisePOCDiscipline]] matters. If the security and approval burden can be similar for 10 users and many thousands, a startup should make sure the first proof is tied to a real expansion path. The target is not only "land and expand" in the abstract, but landing in a way that opens the internal market rather than trapping the company in a low-scope pilot.

## Key Claims
- A large enterprise can contain multiple internal use cases, buyer groups, compliance needs, and workflow variants.
- Initial rollout scope should be judged against the approval and implementation burden, not only against user count.
- Broad adoption can create a reference surface that later supports deeper team-level workflows.
- Small pilots are dangerous when they absorb enterprise complexity without proving enterprise-scale intent.
