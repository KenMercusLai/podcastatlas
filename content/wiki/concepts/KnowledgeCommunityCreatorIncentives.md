# Knowledge Community Creator Incentives / 知识社区创作者激励

Knowledge Community Creator Incentives is the tension the source uses to explain [[Zhihu|知乎]]'s shift from invitation-based expert Q&A toward paid knowledge products, creator conflict, and later story-oriented paid reading. In [[no-212-cengjing-de-jingshen-jiaoluo-douban-zhihu-tieba-hupu-zhongguo-hulianwang-gushi-25-1003510980]], early Zhihu worked because recognition, expertise, invitation, and shared room quality were themselves rewards.

As the platform opened and scaled, those rewards weakened. Creators wanted attention, reputation, outside monetization, and platform compensation, while the platform wanted to preserve the room, prevent external diversion, and build paid products without looking too commercial. The Wukong Q&A subsidy battle showed that cash could move creators, but it did not prove that a healthy knowledge community could be bought quickly.

## Key Claims
- Elite knowledge communities begin with status, peer recognition, and room quality as incentives.
- Scale can reduce the emotional return of answering because the audience becomes less legible and norms become noisier.
- Paid knowledge products need creator alignment; otherwise the platform can look like it values the room more than the people supplying knowledge.
- Subsidy attacks can expose weak creator incentives without solving the deeper community-governance problem.
