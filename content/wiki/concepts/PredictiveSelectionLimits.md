# Predictive Selection Limits

## Definition

Predictive selection limits are the gaps between observable preselection signals—such as charisma, confidence, speeches, scores, and polished applications—and later performance in the role or activity being selected for.

## Current Synthesis

The episode's strongest cross-domain claim is that selection systems often evaluate a performance about future performance. Campaigning can reward visibility and certainty even when governing requires listening, collaboration, and follow-through; grant scores can express expert judgment without finely predicting later citation impact; and admissions files can reward presentation without resolving future potential. This does not make all prediction impossible. It supports matching evidence to the actual task, testing calibration, using thresholds where rankings are weak, and preserving feedback and revision after selection.

## Key Claims

- Selection performance and role performance can require different traits.
- Confidence and charisma are ambiguous signals because they can aid communication while masking ego or weak collaboration.
- Expert evaluation can improve screening without supporting precise outcome rankings.
- Hidden capability can emerge only after responsibility, training, and team context are supplied.
- Prediction quality should be tested against later outcomes rather than inferred from evaluator status.
- Weak ranking evidence favors thresholds, portfolios, trials, or reversible decisions rather than fatalism.

## Evidence

### Campaigning versus governing
- [[the-powerball-revolution-the-powerball-revolution]] reports that confident students sometimes failed to complete terms while shy or initially disengaged students became important collaborators.

### Expert review versus later impact
- [[the-powerball-revolution-the-powerball-revolution]] reports no strong relationship between NIH peer-review scores and later citation impact within the funded set.

### Legitimacy and practical design
- [[the-powerball-revolution-the-powerball-revolution]] records objections about public speaking and earned authority, then develops a qualified-pool lottery as a compromise rather than treating uncertainty as proof that standards are useless.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The cases do not show that charisma, speeches, expert review, grades, or work samples contain no information. The NIH result is subject to range restriction because only funded applications can produce funded outcomes, and citation impact is incomplete. School anecdotes cannot estimate error rates, while post-selection support may partly create the performance later attributed to hidden ability.

## What Changed

- Connected campaign, grant-review, and admissions uncertainty without treating the domains as identical.
- Distinguished useful screening from unsupported fine ranking.
- Added responsibility and institutional support as possible causes of capability becoming visible.
