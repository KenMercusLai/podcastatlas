# Big Science Technology Spillover / 大科学技术外溢

Big science technology spillover is the source's practical defense of spending on basic research infrastructure. In [[ep248-zai-gaoyuan-yu-dixia-tanxun-zhongguo-da-kexue-xiangmu-lidn7xxkcw4-66fw-y50fqqacwo]], [[MiaoQian|苗千]] argues that extreme scientific requirements can force advances in materials, detectors, semiconductors, data systems, and manufacturing.

The episode gives three source-grounded examples. [[JiangmenUndergroundNeutrinoObservatory|江门中微子实验]] needed unusually strong acrylic material and photomultiplier tubes; [[AliPrimordialGravitationalWaveObservatory|阿里原初引力波探测站]] depends on detector and semiconductor-device upgrades; and the source invokes internet history as a broader example of research communication needs producing infrastructure with later civilian value.

The concept is not a promise that every basic-science project will directly commercialize. Its more careful claim is that demanding instruments create capability ladders: if a project needs something no supplier can cheaply provide, scientists and firms may learn to make it, improve it, or localize it.

## Key Claims
- Technology spillover is strongest when the scientific requirement is extreme, measurable, and tied to an actual apparatus.
- Spillover can occur through materials, sensors, cryogenics, cleanrooms, electronics, computing, and data transmission.
- The public value argument for big science includes intrinsic discovery, shared knowledge, and industrial learning; none of these alone fully explains the investment.
- [[SemiconductorSupplyChain|semiconductor capability]] can be pulled by scientific instruments as well as by consumer electronics, AI, or defense.
