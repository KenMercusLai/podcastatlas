# Crime Statistics Comparability

Crime statistics comparability is the methodological problem highlighted in [[spring-then-fall-a-weakened-muslim-brotherhood-6a7aef1b616564bf00d9b354]] when the episode tests claims about whether Europe or America is safer. Definitions, willingness to report, city boundaries, population density, and category-specific denominators can all change what a cross-country crime comparison appears to show.

[[MattAshby]]'s [[UniversityCollegeLondon|University College London]] dataset is the source's constructive answer. It selects large cities, adjusts boundaries to make density more similar, and harmonizes definitions using Britain as the benchmark, while still being described as the "least worst" option rather than a clean global truth machine.

## Key Claims
- Homicide is more comparable than many other crime categories because definitions are more stable and deaths are harder to miss.
- Robbery, burglary, rape reporting, vehicle crime, and shoplifting-with-force are harder to compare because legal definitions and reporting behavior differ.
- City boundary choices can make a dense urban core and a wider metropolitan jurisdiction look artificially similar or different.
- Comparing a city with itself over time can be more reliable than ranking cities across countries.
