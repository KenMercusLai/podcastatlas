# Subordinate Success Non-Jealousy / 下属成就不嫉妒

Subordinate Success Non-Jealousy / 下属成就不嫉妒 is the leadership pattern introduced by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-994-genzhe-dongwu-zhongchen-lvfan-xuexi-zhichang-zhihui-lgqp2vqmh1mss96gcwmflp-ccpfu|Hanji 994]] through [[SunCe|孙策]]'s reaction to [[ZhangZhaoLateHan|张昭]]. When northern士大夫 credit Zhang Zhao for Jiangdong's achievements, Sun Ce is pleased rather than threatened, treating Zhang Zhao's reputation as proof of organizational success.

The source generalizes the lesson through [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]] and [[GuanZhong|管仲]], [[LiuBei|刘备]] and [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]], and [[CaoCao|曹操]] and [[XunYu|荀彧]]. A leader's job is not to keep every capable person below his own cognitive ceiling, but to identify, trust, and benefit from people who can supply judgment or execution the leader lacks.

The concept also has a self-cultivation side. The episode uses [[ZengGuofan|曾国藩]] and [[YanHui|颜回]] to argue that jealousy and boasting are linked interpersonal problems: people resent others' success, but also make others uncomfortable by insisting on their own goodness or labor. Anti-jealous leadership therefore requires both using able subordinates and moderating one's own comparison reflex.

## Key Claims
- A subordinate's external reputation can strengthen the leader when the relationship and institution are aligned.
- Jealousy converts talent from an asset into a perceived threat, damaging delegation and trust.
- Real talent may exceed the leader's own cognition; reducing talent to task execution wastes it.
- Anti-jealousy is not only managerial technique but also self-cultivation around comparison, boasting, and merit display.
