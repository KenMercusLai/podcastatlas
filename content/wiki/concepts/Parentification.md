# Parentification / 亲职化

## Definition
Parentification / 亲职化 is a childhood family-role reversal in which a child assumes adult caregiving, emotional management, mediation, or household-survival responsibility while still needing developmentally appropriate care and protection.

## Current Synthesis
Parentification can arise through crisis, abandonment, adult incapacity, domestic violence, substance use, or routine family expectation. It may look like maturity, leadership, loyalty, or exceptional competence while teaching the child that personal needs are unsafe or secondary. The current sources distinguish helpful age-appropriate contribution from a role reversal in which the child becomes a substitute parent, emotional protector, or physical caretaker, then may judge an impossible burden as a personal failure.

## Key Claims
- Parentification is defined by role reversal and disproportionate burden, not by every instance of a child helping at home.
- Crisis-driven caregiving can force a child to secure food, protect siblings, and manage danger without adult support.
- Emotional and physical parentification can make a child comfort a battered parent, manage intoxication, mediate adult conflict, or care for a parent's stability.
- Competence and leadership may coexist with shame, exhaustion, suppressed need, and difficulty asking for help.
- Adult over-rescuing can repeat the earlier equation of love with carrying another person's responsibilities.
- Recovery involves locating adult failure accurately, restoring boundaries, and separating care from self-erasure.

## Evidence
### Survival role reversal
- [[151-zao-an-guaiwu-zhu-ni-zhansheng-kongju-zhu-ni-huode-kangfu-900179286]] grounds the concept in Laura, who feeds and protects siblings after maternal death and paternal abandonment, yet later treats her inability to be a perfect substitute mother as shameful failure.

### Family mediation and inherited duty
- [[vol-188-fengkuang-dongwucheng-2-beihou-cangzhe-de-chengnianren-xinlike-lltxpvji9zeewoygycdyvrnjbn4c]] broadens the role pattern to children who become a parent's parent or a household mediator, placing it within family expectations and structural family-role confusion.

### Domestic violence and parental incapacity
- [[the-alabama-murders-part-6-the-porterfield-sessions]] describes Smith comforting his mother after his father's violence and, by eight or nine, cleaning up after her, placing cold cloths on her head, and helping her to bed when she drank heavily.

### Adult repetition and boundaries
- [[151-zao-an-guaiwu-zhu-ni-zhansheng-kongju-zhu-ni-huode-kangfu-900179286]] links Laura's later rescuing of weak or exploitative partners to early responsibility and love hunger; [[vol-188-fengkuang-dongwucheng-2-beihou-cangzhe-de-chengnianren-xinlike-lltxpvji9zeewoygycdyvrnjbn4c]] distinguishes ordinary care from taking over another adult's debts or life tasks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The three sources are podcast interpretations, with detailed but different cases rather than comparative clinical evidence. Household contribution varies by age, culture, necessity, support, and duration; responsibility alone does not establish trauma or parentification. The Zootopia episode's “dispatch theory” and structural-family-therapy language remain source-scoped. Smith's childhood burden may contextualize later alcohol use and violence, but the source does not establish a deterministic causal path or excuse the murder.

## What Changed
- Added domestic violence and parental substance use as conditions that can force early caregiving.
- Extended the synthesis from sibling care and mediation to a child comforting and physically tending a parent.
- Clarified that developmental context does not establish a deterministic path to adult violence.
