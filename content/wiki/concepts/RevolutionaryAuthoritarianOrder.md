# Revolutionary Authoritarian Order

## Definition

Revolutionary authoritarian order is the preference for radical political transformation under concentrated, disciplined authority rather than either restored monarchy or uncontrolled popular violence.

## Current Synthesis

The episodes use the young [[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon]] as an emerging case. He supported the [[FrenchRevolution]], remained hostile to Bourbon weakness, and benefited from the collapse of aristocratic military careers. Yet the June and August 1792 Tuileries crises convinced him that crowds and a passive king represented intolerable disorder. His response was not royalism: it was a republican preference for authority capable of directing force. [[383-young-napoleon-the-shadow-of-the-guillotine-part-2-glt2658259929|Part 2]] later shows that disposition becoming politically consequential at Toulon and Vendémiaire.

## Key Claims

- Revolutionary commitment and authoritarian preference can coexist rather than cancel each other.
- Revulsion at disorder does not necessarily imply revulsion at violence itself.
- Weak incumbents can appear culpable when they cannot command institutions or control crowds.
- Military discipline offers a model for converting revolutionary energy into organized state force.
- Political openings created by revolution can reward actors who promise both transformation and order.

## Evidence

### Tuileries and the judgment of disorder

- [[382-young-napoleon-teenage-revolutionary-part-1-glt6500252244]] connects Napoleon's contempt for Louis XVI's weakness, horror at the Tuileries aftermath, continuing republicanism, and preference for order.

### Career opportunity and organized force

- [[382-young-napoleon-teenage-revolutionary-part-1-glt6500252244]] connects aristocratic officer emigration to new advancement paths for an ambitious artillery officer.

### Organized state violence in practice

- [[383-young-napoleon-the-shadow-of-the-guillotine-part-2-glt2658259929]] connects Napoleon's preference for strong government to the organized force used at Toulon and Vendémiaire.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept rests partly on the hosts' interpretation of remembered scenes and dialogue. The episode does not prove a fully formed later doctrine in 1792, nor does dislike of crowd violence alone establish support for dictatorship. Royal weakness, fear, ambition, republican conviction, and professional military culture may all contribute to the response.
