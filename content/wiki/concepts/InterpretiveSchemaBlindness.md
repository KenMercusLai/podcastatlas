## Definition

Interpretive schema blindness / 解释框架遮蔽 is the failure to perceive relevant information because a familiar representational system directs attention toward the wrong layer of an object.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded [[XiakeXingNovel|《侠客行》]] discussion distinguishes lack of knowledge from misapplied knowledge. Learned martial artists see poems, annotations, and unusual writing on 侠客岛 and reasonably assume that the task is semantic interpretation. That expertise organizes sustained inquiry, but it also makes the strokes' visual forms—figures, weapon movements, and circulation routes—hard to notice.

[[ShiPotian|石破天]] escapes the shared frame because he cannot read. His success does not show that ignorance is generally superior: he also possesses the internal power needed to enact what he sees, and his discovery may be lucky rather than evidence of uniquely high wisdom. The reusable lesson is narrower. Knowledge improves perception when its schema fits the object; when it silently fixes what counts as signal, it can make experts systematically overlook another representational layer.

The episode extends the problem beyond reading without collapsing into anti-intellectualism. Concepts can reveal the source of suffering and enable action even when awareness increases distress. The appropriate response is therefore not to discard language or explanation, but to notice when a tool of understanding has become an exclusive filter.

## Key Claims

- Expertise selects relevant features as well as supplying answers, so a mismatched schema can hide evidence in plain sight.
- Semantic content, visual form, and embodied instruction can coexist in one inscription without being interchangeable.
- Shared training can produce correlated error when every expert asks the same kind of question.
- Freedom from one interpretive frame can create discovery without proving general ignorance or superior intelligence.
- Direct perception still requires enabling capacity: 石破天 needs deep internal power as well as illiteracy.
- Knowledge can both burden and emancipate; recognizing injustice may intensify pain while making dignity and change possible.

## Evidence

### Semantic attention hiding visual form

- [[lkwyqt4w5g-uet_vckex3f33nu8n]] reports that learned visitors pursue poetic meaning and commentary while the martial diagrams remain embedded in the shapes of the strokes.

### Frame escape plus enabling capacity

- [[lkwyqt4w5g-uet_vckex3f33nu8n]] gives two conditions for 石破天's success: inability to read the inscriptions conventionally and sufficient internal power to understand and perform their movements.

### Knowledge as burden and resource

- [[lkwyqt4w5g-uet_vckex3f33nu8n]] distinguishes basic emotion from concept-mediated awareness and argues that painful recognition can be a prerequisite for resisting indignity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept comes from a fictional puzzle and a conversational philosophical extension, not an empirical study of expertise. The hidden-diagram premise is deliberately constructed, so it cannot establish that novices generally outperform experts, that direct perception is interpretation-free, or that literacy produces suffering. An alternative reading is that 石破天 succeeds by rare coincidence rather than insight. Expertise often reveals patterns unavailable to novices; the warning applies when a schema monopolizes attention and alternatives are not tested.

## What Changed

- Created a concept separating misapplied knowledge from ignorance.
- Joined semantic, visual, and embodied representational layers in one account of expert error.
- Preserved knowledge's emancipatory role alongside its capacity to intensify suffering.
