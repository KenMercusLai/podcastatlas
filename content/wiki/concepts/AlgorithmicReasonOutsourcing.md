# Algorithmic Reason Outsourcing / 算法理性外包

Algorithmic reason outsourcing is [[167-bailatu-lusuo-hayeke-alunte-sida-zhexuejia-hui-ruhe-jieshi-suanfa-shidai-chuantai-dushu-buchenglin-lmbsdbfeqqfz5vm3g67b-wjgdjfja]]'s Enlightenment critique of recommendation systems. The episode uses [[ImmanuelKant|Kant]]'s call to use one's own reason and [[JeanJacquesRousseau|Rousseau]]'s suspicion of rationalist progress to ask whether platforms keep reason active while moving it out of the user's own judgment.

The concept does not claim that algorithms are irrational. Its worry is almost the opposite: ranking, search, recommendations, hot lists, and machine calculation can look like more reason, more knowledge, and more efficiency while the person becomes less practiced at active comparison, source evaluation, and independent judgment.

## Key Claims
- The anti-Enlightenment risk is not the death of reason; it is reason performed elsewhere on the user's behalf.
- Information abundance can make filtering necessary while also making filtering politically and morally important.
- Recommendation dependence can turn the early internet's active-search ideal into passive intake.
- The user's task is not to reject all filters, but to notice when ranking has become a substitute for thinking.
- [[AlgorithmicMediaLiteracy]] and [[FeedCuration]] are practical counterweights because they return some judgment to the user.
