# Quantified Parenting

Quantified parenting is the extension of score-driven self-tracking into child care. [[tech-20260811-0811-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260811-0811-mp-tech-pod-128]] adds it through [[Nanit]] sleep scores and the comparison to adult wearables such as [[OuraRing|Oura Ring]] and [[Whoop]].

The source's key move is not just that parents receive more data. It is that a baby can become part of a visible metric system where sleep is scored, compared, optimized, and sometimes posted beside the parent's own wearable data. That connects parenting to [[SleepAsDailyHealthAccount]] while also raising [[ParentingDataHypervigilance]] and [[ChildBedroomDataPrivacy]] concerns.

## Key Claims
- Baby sleep scores borrow the language and social behavior of adult health wearables.
- The child's data can become part of the parent's self-tracking environment even though the child is the monitored subject.
- Scores can make real patterns visible, but they can also turn ordinary variation into performance pressure.
- Quantified parenting is not automatically overcontrol, but it needs boundaries around interpretation, sharing, and action.
