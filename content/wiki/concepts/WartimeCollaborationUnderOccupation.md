# Wartime Collaboration under Occupation

## Definition

Wartime collaboration under occupation is a framework for distinguishing elite client rule from the varied coercive, pragmatic, and survival-based accommodations made by people living under foreign military domination.

## Current Synthesis

[[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] grounds the concept in [[WangJingwei]]'s Japanese-backed Nanjing government. Wang's regime was subordinate to Japan, yet justified itself through peace, reduced bombing, order, and continuity with the Republic of China. That legitimacy claim competed directly with [[ChiangKaiShek]]'s continued-resistance government.

The episode resists transferring one judgment about Wang to every person in occupied territory. Residents and local officials could prioritize physical safety, family, property, routine, or order. Recognizing those constraints does not erase Japanese control or turn the client regime into an independent government.

## Key Claims

- Collaborationist governments can claim national continuity while depending on an occupying power.
- Harm reduction and order can serve as both sincere rationales and legitimating language.
- Elite political collaboration differs from everyday accommodation under coercion.
- Local participation does not prove uniform ideological support for occupation.
- Continued resistance and collaboration competed over both survival strategy and state legitimacy.

## Evidence

- Client regime: [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] describes Wang's negotiation with Japan and installation in Nanjing in 1940.
- Claimed legitimacy: [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] says the regime presented itself as the true Republic and heir to Sun Yat-sen.
- Local ambiguity: [[236-china-and-world-war-ii-part-2-glt5290070743]] emphasizes mixed attitudes shaped by order, survival, property, and ordinary life.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Attention to survival does not negate the regime's subordination to Japan or excuse officials' conduct. The source offers a broad interpretive warning rather than local case studies sufficient to classify particular collaborators, resisters, or residents.

## What Changed

- Created a distinction between elite client-government collaboration and constrained everyday accommodation.
- Added legitimacy competition and harm-reduction claims to the wiki's occupation framework.
