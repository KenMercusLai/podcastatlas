# Classroom Magazine Circulation / 班级杂志传阅

Classroom magazine circulation is the school-age pattern in which a few purchased magazines become shared objects across a class, dorm, or friend group. In [[ep272-shui-de-qingchun-li-meiyou-yi-ben-bei-chuanyue-dao-juanbian-de-zazhi-lmwyj84wfdgnlgsovkihulfailu]], the speakers remember expensive magazines such as 《看天下》 being bought by only a few students and then passed around, while comics, youth-literature magazines, and entertainment issues moved through desks, schoolbags, toilets, and teacher confiscation.

The circulation mattered because magazines were both content and social infrastructure. Reading the same issue created conversation, status, shared jokes, fandom, waiting for serials, and small conflicts with adult authority. The source therefore treats "卷边" as more than wear and tear: the curled edge marks how many hands, places, and social permissions a magazine passed through.

## Key Claims
- Price and scarcity made one issue socially productive: shared access could matter more than individual ownership.
- Teacher bans and confiscation did not simply stop circulation; they sometimes redirected it through other adults, teacher children, or informal exchanges.
- Serial publication made classmates wait together, speculate together, and care about a magazine's editors or authors over time.
- The social object could include inserts, posters, stickers, DVDs, and other gifts, not only articles.
- Classroom circulation connected reading to friendship, hidden autonomy, taste formation, and mild rebellion against exam-centered school life.
