# Cross-Border Family Reunification After Separation / 长期分离后的跨国家庭团聚

## Definition
Cross-border family reunification after separation is the rebuilding problem that begins when parents and children resume living together after migration has kept them apart through much of childhood.

## Current Synthesis
Physical reunion and legal admission are necessary changes, but neither supplies shared memory, mutual knowledge, trust, or a settled division of care and authority. In Xiaorui's case, years of video calls preserved a relationship while allowing mother and daughter to form abstract versions of one another. Once together, each interpreted conflict through a different history: the mother saw newly acquired rebellion, while Xiaorui saw continuity in a self her mother had never known closely.

Reunification also occurs inside current material constraints. Debt, shop hours, housing, health, education, gender expectation, and residence timing shape the household into which the child arrives. Repair therefore depends on repeated ordinary care, credible recognition of past absence, explicit labor and money boundaries, and room for the reunited young person to develop autonomy.

## Key Claims
- Remote contact can preserve connection without replacing shared daily life or mutual knowledge.
- Legal and physical reunion begins relational rebuilding rather than completing it.
- Parents and children may explain post-reunion conflict through incompatible memories of the child's earlier character.
- Debt and work organization can crowd out the attention needed to repair attachment.
- Economic opportunity may coexist with alienation from both the destination and the parental home.
- Autonomy, wages, health recognition, and freedom from coerced marriage are part of a durable reunion boundary.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One family cannot establish a universal sequence. Some separated families may develop secure remote attachment or reunite with more resources, while some children may prefer the caregivers and educational route already established at home. The source gives limited paternal perspective and does not provide clinical assessment or longitudinal follow-up.
