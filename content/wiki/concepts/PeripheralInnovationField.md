# Peripheral Innovation Field / 边域式创新场域

## Definition
A peripheral innovation field is a social and spatial environment at the edge of established disciplines, institutions, or status centers where heterogeneous people can define new problems with less pressure from dominant authority.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents Dartmouth as a "边域" because participants came from many disciplines and because Dartmouth was not the strongest mainstream center of computing, cybernetics, or elite institutional power. Everyone was somewhat foreign to everyone else, which made the workshop less captive to one discipline's standards.

The source then generalizes the pattern through University of Chicago, Silicon Valley, climbing culture, and Patagonia. Its core claim is not that marginality automatically creates innovation, but that edge positions can lower prestige pressure and leave more room for new combinations.

## Key Claims
- Edge locations can reduce the authority pressure that comes from established centers.
- Cross-disciplinary participation makes every person partly an outsider, which can weaken one field's dominance.
- Peripheral fields are most valuable when they preserve contact, exchange, and enough shared direction.
- Such fields can appear in universities, startup regions, hobbyist communities, or outdoor/counterculture scenes.
- Peripheral status is not sufficient by itself; the field still needs talent density, shared values, and sustained interaction.

## Evidence
- Dartmouth as edge: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] calls Dartmouth a "边域" and contrasts it with MIT, Harvard, and Stanford.
- Heterogeneous participants: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] says participants came from neural physiology, mathematics, computing, and language learning.
- Distance from cybernetics: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] says keeping distance from mainstream cybernetics and Norbert Wiener was treated as helpful.
- Analogical cases: [[401-bianyu-manju-manzou-women-haineng-yinglai-lingwai-yige-datemao-si-de-xiatian-ma-f7495e05]] compares Dartmouth with University of Chicago, Silicon Valley, Yosemite climbing culture, and Patagonia.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source warns indirectly against romanticizing small groups. Yang says small teams are not automatically strong; they still require intellectual density and value alignment. The concept should not treat marginality alone as a causal guarantee.
