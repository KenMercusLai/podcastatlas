# Politically Connected Banking Crisis

## Definition

A politically connected banking crisis occurs when lending privileges tied to political access produce large bad-loan stocks, weaken bank balance sheets, freeze ordinary credit, and force a contested choice over recapitalization and loss allocation.

## Current Synthesis

The [[Bangladesh]] case links crony lending to development reversal. State-owned or state-backed banks reportedly lent heavily to connected figures under [[SheikhHasina]], while delayed recognition made non-performing loans appear closer to 10% before newer valuations placed them around 30%. Once losses become visible, transparency is necessary but insufficient: banks still need capital, borrowers and owners face losses, and governments may continue granting selective payment holidays.

The macroeconomic consequence runs through credit. Banks burdened by bad loans cannot finance otherwise viable activity, so slower growth, job pressure, poverty, and protest complicate the reforms needed to restore lending. External recapitalization can supply scarce funds, but lender conditions and subsidy cuts shift costs onto politically sensitive groups.

## Key Claims

- Politically favored credit can hide private transfers inside bank balance sheets until losses become system-wide.
- Accurate bad-loan recognition improves diagnosis but can expose a recapitalization need too large for domestic banks or the state to absorb easily.
- Weak bank balance sheets transmit past corruption into present credit scarcity, slower growth, and poverty.
- Payment holidays for favored firms can reproduce the original governance problem after a change of government.
- External assistance can restore capital only by confronting loss recognition, conditionality, subsidy policy, and public opposition.

## Evidence

### Connected lending and delayed recognition

- [[strait-draw-irans-hot-war-cools-a9cfdd53e637fbdfdb684cf03b8222f4]] attributes much of Bangladesh's bad-loan stock to politically connected lending and reports that revised valuations raised the apparent non-performing share from about 10% to roughly 30%.

### Credit and development transmission

- [[strait-draw-irans-hot-war-cools-a9cfdd53e637fbdfdb684cf03b8222f4]] links impaired lending capacity to annual growth falling from about 6% to roughly 2% and to renewed poverty.

### Recapitalization and reform politics

- [[strait-draw-irans-hot-war-cools-a9cfdd53e637fbdfdb684cf03b8222f4]] reports a recapitalization estimate near 10% of GDP and presents the IMF, World Bank, or India as possible funding routes whose conditions could provoke further protests.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The episode does not provide bank-level loan tapes, provisioning rules, recovery values, audited accounts, or a counterfactual separating corruption from COVID-19, fuel costs, political unrest, and wider war effects. The percentages and recapitalization estimate are source-scoped. Private banks are described as healthier, so the crisis is not presented as uniform across every institution.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Bangladesh's connected-lending, recognition, credit-freeze, and recapitalization sequence.
