## Definition

The simulation–understanding boundary distinguishes a convincing representation of a person, event, or world from restored life, verified identity, causal explanation, or historically grounded understanding.

## Current Synthesis

In [[DoctorFaustusMarlowe|*Doctor Faustus*]], apparitions of Alexander the Great and Helen can satisfy courtly curiosity or desire without reviving the dead. The episode extends that distinction to AI-generated voices, faces, and language styles: resemblance can make the past experientially vivid while leaving biography, context, motives, and historical conditions unexplained.

Simulation can still support learning, art, memory, or inquiry. Its value depends on transparent identity claims, provenance, contextual framing, and whether surface fidelity is treated as an entry into investigation or a substitute for it.

## Key Claims

- Perceptual or stylistic fidelity does not establish identity or restored life.
- A vivid encounter can produce real emotion without becoming historical proof.
- Representation becomes misleading when an apparition or model is treated as an authoritative witness to a life it reconstructs.
- Historical understanding requires context, evidence, causation, and uncertainty beyond recognizable mannerisms.
- Simulation can aid inquiry when its synthetic status, sources, and limits remain legible.
- Immersion and explanation are different achievements and should be evaluated separately.

- Desire and illusion: [[372-dang-keji-nenggou-manzu-yiqie-yuanwang-women-hai-zhidao-ziji-xiangyao-shenme-ma-maluo-fushide-boshi-de-beiju-ltehzo-lehi9vpgy5ukexbcrtcpr]] uses Helen's final apparition as comfort that cannot alter Faustus's actual condition.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Simulation and understanding are not mutually exclusive. Reconstruction, reenactment, visualization, and interactive models can reveal patterns or motivate research when evidence and uncertainty are made explicit. The current source offers a literary analogy, not an evaluation of a specific historical simulation or AI system.

## What Changed

- Created a boundary separating experiential resemblance from identity, resurrection, and contextual historical knowledge.
