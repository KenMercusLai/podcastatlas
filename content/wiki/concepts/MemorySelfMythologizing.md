# Memory Self-Mythologizing / 记忆的自我神话化

[[MemorySelfMythologizing|Memory self-mythologizing]] is the pattern [[12-man-dang-milan-kundela-yi-ju-zhengjing-meiyou-549335675]] draws from the Czech entomologist in [[SlowKundera|《慢》]]. The source says his post-[[PragueSpring|1968]] choice was not pure brave resistance: he yielded under pressure and fear of contempt, then later remembered the resulting cost as evidence of free will and noble courage.

The concept is not a denial that genuine courage exists. It names the way memory can edit motive after the fact so that embarrassment, passivity, coercion, or accidental consequence becomes destiny. In the episode, even a failed conference speech can be absorbed into a grander self-story.

## Key Claims
- Political suffering can be real even when the sufferer's later account of motive is self-flattering.
- Public sympathy can help a person convert shame into heroic narrative.
- Memory may protect dignity, but it can also hide the weakness or contingency that produced an action.
