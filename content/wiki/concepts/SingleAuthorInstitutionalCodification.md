## Definition

单人负责式制度编纂 / single-author institutional codification is the decision pattern where a ruler assigns one responsible compiler to turn inherited precedents and current principles into usable rules when broad committee deliberation is expected to stall implementation.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 757 creates the concept through [[EmperorZhangOfHan|Emperor Zhang]]'s commission to [[CaoBaoEasternHan|Cao Bao]]. Cao Bao asks to create a Han ritual code, but Taichang Chao Kan says such a major code exceeds one scholar's status. [[BanGuEasternHan|Ban Gu]] recommends gathering scholars from multiple Confucian lineages for joint discussion.

Zhangdi rejects that process because he expects scholar caution and mutual rivalry to produce delay. His "roadside house" analogy frames a design problem: too many commentators can keep a public project from becoming a usable institution. He therefore gives Cao Bao [[ShusunTong|Shusun Tong]]'s twelve `Han Yi` chapters and asks him to revise, extend, and fix the rites for current Han use.

The concept does not claim one-person codification is always better. It identifies a tradeoff: single authorship can increase speed, accountability, and coherence, but it may reduce buy-in and later durability if the ruler or institution cannot defend the result.

## Key Claims

- Broad consultation can improve legitimacy and coverage but can also stall codification when participants are cautious, rivalrous, or unable to agree.
- Single responsible authorship can make institutional projects faster and more coherent.
- Codification still depends on inherited material; Cao Bao works from Shusun Tong's `Han Yi` rather than beginning from nothing.
- A ruler choosing single authorship is making an execution tradeoff, not eliminating the need for later acceptance.
- Later enforcement risk remains: a code produced under one sponsor may weaken if successors cannot defend it.

- Precedent revision: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-757-rensheng-xiang-shao-zou-wanlu-xian-zhaozhun-dingwei-lri1zyiq8210ymqjakznyp3v4k6x|Hanji 757]] says Zhangdi gives Cao Bao Shusun Tong's twelve `Han Yi` chapters and asks him to edit additional rites and fix the text.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Hanji 757 favors Zhangdi's execution logic but also preserves the counterargument that ritual codes may require broader scholarly authority. This concept should therefore be used as a tradeoff frame, not a general rejection of consultation. Hanji 758 and Hanji 768 later show that completion and acceptance do not guarantee durability once later court resistance appears.
