# Deworming as Human Capital Investment

Deworming as human capital investment is the source's health-education example in [[piles-of-cash-and-a-town-of-solutions-in-kenya-nigeria-summer-school]]. [[TedMiguel]] evaluated free medicine for parasitic worms among schoolchildren near [[BusiaKenya|Busia]], and the episode says treated children attended school more often.

The longer-run claim is that early health treatment can affect later work and income. In the source, a follow-up study linked deworming to better jobs and higher incomes, making the intervention part of [[HumanCapitalDevelopment]] rather than only disease control.

## Key Claims
- Health can be an education intervention when illness keeps children from school.
- Randomized evidence can make low-cost health interventions visible to governments.
- Follow-up studies matter because human-capital returns can appear years after treatment.
- Deworming also illustrates [[PositiveExternality]] because reducing infections can benefit untreated people nearby.
