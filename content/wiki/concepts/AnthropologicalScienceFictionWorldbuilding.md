# Anthropological Science Fiction Worldbuilding / 人类学式科幻世界建构

Anthropological science fiction worldbuilding is the method [[36-heian-de-zuoshou-liufang-yu-ai-de-weilai-wangshi-605254522]] identifies in [[UrsulaKLeGuin|Le Guin]]'s [[TheLeftHandOfDarkness|《黑暗的左手》]]. The source says the novel's opening and structure can feel like a dossier: a report on [[Gethen|格森星 / Gethen]] built from travel, political description, myths, social terms, religion, kinship, climate, and outsider observation.

The concept extends [[FieldworkAsKnowledgeMethod]] into fiction. The point is not that [[GenlyAi|金利·艾 / Genly Ai]] is a real anthropologist, but that the narrative makes knowledge depend on situated contact, misunderstanding, correction, hospitality, local categories, and embodied travel. The reader learns the world by watching an observer fail and revise his assumptions.

## Key Claims
- A speculative world becomes credible when its customs, myths, politics, language, and bodily conditions mutually constrain one another.
- Alien societies are not only scenery; they are social systems that can make the reader's own assumptions visible.
- Fictional documents, legends, and field-note-like description can create [[FictionalHistoriography]] even without a conventional archive plot.
- The method works best when the outsider's misunderstanding remains part of the evidence rather than being erased by exposition.
