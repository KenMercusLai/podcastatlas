# Cat As Civilizational Infrastructure

Cat as civilizational infrastructure is the episode's practical claim that [[Cat|猫]] helped human systems work before becoming primarily visible as pets or symbols. In [[34-maomi-mishi-he-yuchun-you-keai-de-renlei-601590607]], cats protect granaries, households, books, temples, ships, military supplies, and expedition camps by controlling rodents and other dangerous small animals.

The concept deliberately echoes [[HorseAsCivilizationalInfrastructure]] but at a different scale. Horses transform speed, war, and trade; cats protect stored value and fragile living spaces. Grain stores, manuscript collections, silkworm production, ship provisions, polar food supplies, and wartime logistics all depend on keeping destructive animals under control.

## Key Claims
- Cat usefulness is strongest where stored food, texts, textiles, or ship provisions are vulnerable to rodents.
- Cats are infrastructure partly because their labor is easy to naturalize and forget once the system works.
- The episode's ship-cat and war-cat stories are culturally useful even when treated as anecdotes: they show how human survival logistics can depend on nonhuman workers.
- The concept links public health and material history because rodent control affects food security, disease anxiety, and household safety.
- [[Felicette]] extends the infrastructure arc into modern state science, where cats become experimental participants rather than only pest-control collaborators.
