# Cable Landing Point Security

Cable landing point security is the risk and governance layer around the places where [[UnderseaDataCables]] come ashore and connect into terrestrial networks. [[tech-20260820-tech-pod-128-tech-20260820-tech-pod-128]] adds the concept by stressing that cable trust depends not only on the fiber in the ocean but also on the hardware, software, and vendors at the landing points.

The episode treats landing points as intelligence-sensitive infrastructure. If sensitive data moves through cable systems, then equipment choice, maintenance access, and operational control can affect whether traffic is exposed, tapped, decrypted, interrupted, or routed through trusted alternatives.

## Key Claims
- Cable security is a system problem: the wet segment, landing stations, network equipment, software, operators, and repair channels all matter.
- Landing-point equipment can turn a connectivity project into a national-security question even when the cable itself is commercially useful.
- Vendor trust connects [[CableLandingPointSecurity]] to [[TrustedNetworkGeopolitics]], especially when governments worry about suppliers tied to rival states.
- The source does not prove a specific landing-point compromise; it uses landing points to define a risk surface that policymakers and operators evaluate.
