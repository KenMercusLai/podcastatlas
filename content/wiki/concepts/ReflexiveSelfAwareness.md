# Reflexive Self-Awareness / 反身性自我意识

## Definition
Reflexive self-awareness is the capacity to step out from one's immediate intention, see how one appears from another person's position, and return to oneself with a revised understanding of one's own speech or behavior.

## Current Synthesis
The episode uses "反身性" to explain why habitual preachers often do not notice their own intrusiveness. They may believe they are helping, teaching, or improving the other person, while lacking the ability to imagine how their advice sounds from the listener's side. This failure is not tied to education, class, age, or professional status; the episode treats it as a cross-cutting self-knowledge problem.

The concept also has an opposite edge. The host contrasts low self-awareness with young people whose self-consciousness is so strong that they over-anticipate others' judgments and become anxious before acting. That contrast makes the target clearer: useful reflexivity is neither crude self-certainty nor paralyzing self-surveillance.

## Key Claims
- Reflexive self-awareness lets a speaker notice not only what they meant, but how their speech positions the listener.
- Habitual preaching often lacks this capacity, so the speaker misses their own awkwardness, rudeness, or overreach.
- Education, achievement, leadership, fame, or age do not guarantee self-awareness.
- Too little reflexivity can produce crude intrusion, while too much self-consciousness can produce anxiety and paralysis.
- The episode suggests a possible generational link: over-self-conscious young people may have been shaped by adults with weak reflexive awareness and strong didactic habits.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not turn reflexive self-awareness into a clinical measure. It is a communication and self-observation frame derived from everyday examples. Some anxiety, caution, or request for feedback can be adaptive, and some direct advice can be welcome when invited, competent, and sensitive to context.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the episode's use of "反身性" to distinguish self-observation from both intrusive certainty and anxious self-surveillance.
