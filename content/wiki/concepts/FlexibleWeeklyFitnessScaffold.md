# Flexible Weekly Fitness Scaffold

## Definition
A flexible weekly fitness scaffold is a source-defined planning framework that sets weekly resistance, cardiovascular, and rest targets while allowing individual sessions to move, combine, or occasionally be skipped when recovery and real-life constraints require it.

## Current Synthesis
The source's ideal week contains three resistance sessions, three cardiovascular sessions, and one full rest day. The resistance branch covers legs, torso pushing and pulling, and smaller body parts; the cardiovascular branch covers a longer outdoor session, a moderate session, and a short high-intensity or VO2-max-oriented session. This creates broad weekly coverage without requiring every workout to be long.

The more reusable idea is not the exact split but the relation between structure and flexibility. Weekly targets guide decisions, while sleep, illness, stress, travel, soreness, family time, nutrition, and recent training determine whether a session should proceed, move, combine with another, change modality, or be skipped. Consistency therefore means a high long-run completion rate rather than perfect obedience to a calendar.

## Key Claims
- Weekly targets can provide enough structure to cover resistance work, moderate cardiovascular work, high-intensity conditioning, longer easy movement, and deliberate rest.
- The source's specific scaffold is three resistance sessions, three cardiovascular sessions, and one complete rest day.
- Session order is adjustable; recovery state can justify switching resistance and cardiovascular days or moving a workout forward or backward.
- Combining moderate cardio with short high-intensity work can recover schedule coverage, but only when sleep, stress, illness risk, nutrition, and recovery support it.
- Consecutive resistance days increase the importance of a subsequent rest day, and injury-aware exercise selection overrides the nominal split.
- Long-run adherence can survive occasional missed workouts; the source proposes roughly 85-95% completion rather than perfection.
- Fitness should increase capacity for the rest of life rather than routinely displacing sleep, health, travel, or family time.

## Evidence
- Weekly coverage - [[ama-11-improve-task-switching-productivity-and-reduce-brain-fog-scim5878324891]] describes three resistance sessions, three cardiovascular sessions, and one complete rest day, distributed across legs, torso, smaller body parts, longer movement, moderate cardio, and short high-intensity work.
- Flexible ordering - [[ama-11-improve-task-switching-productivity-and-reduce-brain-fog-scim5878324891]] says workouts can shift by a day, swap order when the legs are not recovered, or occasionally combine when conditions permit.
- Recovery gate - [[ama-11-improve-task-switching-productivity-and-reduce-brain-fog-scim5878324891]] makes sleep, illness, stress, soreness, nutrition, time, and recent session intensity part of the decision to train or rest.
- Adherence boundary - [[ama-11-improve-task-switching-productivity-and-reduce-brain-fog-scim5878324891]] says an occasional skipped workout does not collapse the program and proposes completing roughly 85-95% of scheduled work.
- Life-fit boundary - [[ama-11-improve-task-switching-productivity-and-reduce-brain-fog-scim5878324891]] treats family vacation and broader life vigor as reasons not to make workout completion the overriding objective.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is one public-education scheduling example, not evidence that six training days are necessary, sufficient, or optimal for every person. The source does not compare the scaffold with other frequencies or provide study methods, progression rules, individualized intensity targets, or outcomes. Training age, disability, injury, cardiovascular risk, illness, pregnancy, medication, sport goals, time, access, and recovery capacity can all change an appropriate plan. VO2-max intervals, weighted walking, heat or cold exposure, caffeine, exercise combinations, and periodization require their own safety and goal context.

## What Changed
- Added a weekly planning layer that integrates resistance, cardiovascular, and rest targets without making calendar placement rigid.
- Made recovery and ordinary life explicit gates for moving, combining, changing, or skipping sessions.
- Defined consistency as high long-run adherence rather than perfect workout completion.
