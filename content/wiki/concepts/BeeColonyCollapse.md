# Bee Colony Collapse

Bee colony collapse is the beekeeping risk where worker bees disappear or colonies fail, leaving beekeepers with sudden hive losses. In [[jiufan-zhongting-mifeng-jingjixue-956460448]], the topic appears after the episode explains commercial pollination: the more bees are mobilized for services such as almond pollination, the more relevant disease, pesticide, winter-rhythm disruption, extreme weather, and replacement costs become.

The source treats colony collapse as both ecological uncertainty and supply-chain problem. Causes remain contested in the episode's account, but the business response is clearer: beekeepers can split surviving hives, buy queens, buy packaged bees, and price expected losses into [[PollinationServiceMarket]] fees. That supports [[ExternalityInternalization]] as a business mechanism while also marking its limit: pricing a loss does not prove the underlying system is biologically healthy.

[[ep261-mifeng-weiji-ruguo-shiqu-mifeng-shijie-jianghui-zenyang-lqp-cwcdxx1ziixb35ijmmvd6cv]] sharpens the boundary between managed-colony risk and [[WildPollinatorCrisis|wild-pollinator crisis]]. A beekeeper may rebuild or replace managed colonies after poisoning, disease, or weather shock, but solitary and native pollinators can disappear from local habitats without the same replacement supply chain or market visibility.

## Key Claims
- Commercial pollination can raise beekeeper revenue while also increasing hive stress and disease exposure.
- Almond pollination is especially intense because it happens early and concentrates a large share of commercial hives in one region.
- Pesticide exposure, extreme weather, food scarcity, and disease transmission are all treated as possible risk contributors in the source.
- Mature bee replacement supply chains can make colony loss financially manageable for some operators.
- A risk can be costed, insured against, or repaired through supply chains without becoming harmless.
- Managed-hive recovery should not be mistaken for recovery of wild pollinator diversity.
