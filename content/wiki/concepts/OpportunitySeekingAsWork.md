# Opportunity-Seeking as Work

## Definition
Opportunity-seeking as work is the recognition that finding intermittent creative employment can itself require sustained information gathering, travel, waiting, applications, auditions, follow-up, and relationship maintenance before any paid assignment exists.

## Current Synthesis
[[VarunSharma]]'s acting account separates visible paid work from the labor required to become selectable. Before social platforms made leads easier to circulate, he visited studios, spoke with other actors, located offices, distributed photographs and introductions, waited in lines, auditioned, and followed projects through repeated shortlists. Six months leading to one advertisement therefore contained continuing work even though only the shoot was paid and publicly visible.

The concept complements [[ActingOpportunityReadiness]] without collapsing search into skill. Training and casting-room observation can improve performance, while information and relationships increase exposure to possible roles; neither guarantees that gatekeepers select the actor. Treating every rejection as a possible future connection can keep a search network open, but it also carries financial, psychological, and access costs.

## Key Claims
- Paid creative assignments may rest on long periods of unpaid or underpaid search, travel, waiting, preparation, and follow-up.
- Information about which projects exist and where decisions occur is a career resource, not merely background knowledge.
- Repeated contact can turn an immediate rejection into future familiarity, but it does not create an entitlement to selection.
- Search labor and performance readiness are complementary: opportunity exposure cannot substitute for craft, and craft cannot allocate access by itself.
- Family resources, geography, mobility, time, money, and psychological support affect how long a person can sustain the search.
- Visible outcomes systematically understate the labor and uncertainty preceding them.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept rests on one successful actor's retrospective and does not estimate selection odds, typical search duration, or the value of any particular networking tactic. Search intensity cannot eliminate discrimination, gatekeeping, unsafe practices, financial exclusion, geographic barriers, or weak demand. Continued pursuit also needs boundaries: a future-contact interpretation must respect refusal and must not become harassment, sunk-cost escalation, or denial of deteriorating health and finances.

## What Changed
- Established opportunity search as a distinct labor process from both paid performance and craft preparation.
