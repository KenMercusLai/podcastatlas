## Definition

Maternal status succession leverage / 子以母贵式继承杠杆 is the mechanism where a prince or heir candidate's value changes because he is attached to a higher-status biological, adoptive, or foster maternal figure.

## Current Synthesis

The concept now has five main shapes. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]] adds the precondition layer for [[LiuQiangDonghaiKing|刘强]]: [[GuoShengtong|郭圣通]]'s empress status grows from a founding political marriage tied to [[LiuYangZhendingKingEasternHan|真定王刘杨]], and her later deposition threatens the maternal foundation under Liu Qiang's heir position. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-720-donghan-kaiguo-taizi-weihe-fanfu-da-cizhi-baogao-lslizspo8rywnf3cebyhincdhvz0|Hanji 720]] gives the clearest biological status-loss consequence: Liu Qiang is already crown prince, but after Guo is deposed, his heir position becomes unsafe enough that he repeatedly asks to withdraw. [[LiuXiu|Guangwu]] then makes Liu Yang, the son of the current empress line, crown prince and renames him Liu Zhuang.

In the Zhouji 97 sources, [[LuBuwei|Lu Buwei]] sees that [[QinZhuangxiangwang|Yiren]]'s low rank can change if [[HuayangFuren|Huayang Furen]], a favored but childless wife of [[QinXiaowenwang|Anguo Jun]], adopts him. The carved jade tally then turns maternal affiliation into a [[WrittenSuccessionCredential|written succession credential]].

Hanji 751 adds a coercive Eastern Han variant. [[EmpressDowagerDouHeLateHan|Empress Dou]] has no son and raises [[EmperorHeOfHan|Liu Zhao]], the son of [[LiangGuirenHeLateHan|Liang Guiren]], as her own. Once Liu Zhao becomes useful as Dou's foster-maternal route, [[LiuQingQingheKing|Liu Qing]] and [[SongGuirenZhangHeLateHan|Song Guiren]] become the rival maternal line to remove.

Hanji 918 and Hanji 943 add a late-Han factional variant. [[EmpressDowagerHe|Empress He]]'s rise from a Nanyang butcher household and the birth of [[LiuBianHongnongWang|Liu Bian]] prepare a maternal-household base before the succession crisis. After [[EmperorLingOfHan|Emperor Ling]] dies without naming an heir, Liu Bian's living queen-mother line and Liu Xie's birth/foster-maternal associations become competing factional labels, even without a deliberate Lu Buwei-style adoption strategy.

The current synthesis is therefore broader than the original "under-ranked son gains a mother" model. Maternal status can be produced by political marriage, uplift an heir candidate, destabilize an already established heir after his mother falls, expose a rival mother-child pair, or preserve court factions around maternal and foster-maternal labels after the ruler's decision is incomplete.

## Key Claims

- A prince's heir value can rise or fall through biological, adoptive, foster, or politically installed maternal affiliation.
- A mother's empress status may depend on upstream alliance value, so succession leverage can depreciate when the alliance behind her loses force.
- Loss of a mother's rank can endanger even an already established crown prince.
- A childless high-status consort can become a succession channel by adopting or raising an otherwise weaker candidate.
- Maternal leverage becomes stronger when formalized by recognition, credentials, title, or an actual crown-prince decision.
- The mechanism creates family-power incentives because the maternal household gains status with the son and fears rival maternal lines.
- Later factional labels can preserve maternal or foster-maternal ties as succession camps when the ruler has not clearly designated an heir.

## Evidence

- Political-marriage precondition: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]] says Guo Shengtong's empress standing begins from a political marriage tied to Liu Yang's Hebei support.
- Biological status loss setup: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-719-guangwudi-liuxiu-suanbusuan-zhanan-lk8eme3wwyk5qkjjfxkp0zsc77ix|Hanji 719]] says Guo is deposed and replaced by [[YinLihua|阴丽华]], creating the maternal-status change that matters for Liu Qiang.
- Biological status loss consequence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-720-donghan-kaiguo-taizi-weihe-fanfu-da-cizhi-baogao-lslizspo8rywnf3cebyhincdhvz0|Hanji 720]] says Liu Qiang becomes uneasy after Guo Shengtong's deposition because his mother had been his support, then repeatedly asks to resign as crown prince.
- Replacement through current maternal rank: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-720-donghan-kaiguo-taizi-weihe-fanfu-da-cizhi-baogao-lslizspo8rywnf3cebyhincdhvz0|Hanji 720]] says Liu Xiu justifies the replacement by naming Liu Yang as the empress's son and installs him as crown prince under the name Liu Zhuang.
- Adoption as status channel: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-3-lvbuwei-lishishang-fengtou-di-yi-ren-lswa9b864oc-s8vaxtdu15arydu2|Zhouji 97-3]] identifies Huayang Furen's childlessness as the opening for Yiren's repositioning, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-751-dou-huanghou-gaosu-ni-nvren-bu-hen-diwei-bu-wen-luvqadctbfuzit2ackrqifour2c6|Hanji 751]] shows Empress Dou using Liu Zhao as her foster-maternal succession route.
- Formalization: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-4-shishang-wei-shiye-song-laopo-diyi-ren-lo8omg01gkwlojkpiflaep99x-vz|Zhouji 97-4]] makes the jade tally the durable proof of adoption, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-751-dou-huanghou-gaosu-ni-nvren-bu-hen-diwei-bu-wen-luvqadctbfuzit2ackrqifour2c6|Hanji 751]] shows Zhangdi's crown-prince replacement converting foster-maternal positioning into succession fact.
- Rival-line removal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-751-dou-huanghou-gaosu-ni-nvren-bu-hen-diwei-bu-wen-luvqadctbfuzit2ackrqifour2c6|Hanji 751]] says the Song sisters' accusation leads to Liu Qing's demotion and Liu Zhao's promotion.
- Household rise prehistory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-918-shiqing-bu-shunli-shi-weishenme-yao-bizui-lh-tbohgo96qw8bjqu3x1yp6czv5|Hanji 918]] shows Empress He's palace selection, Liu Bian's birth, and the He household's political rise before the later succession split.
- Factional labels: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-943-quanshi-kaoyan-renxing-beihou-cangzhe-juda-xiongxian-lg7ia5xok-swdmhml35yv9qqkvt|Hanji 943]] presents Liu Bian and Liu Xie through queen-mother, birth-mother, and foster-grandmother associations in a succession crisis without a named heir.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The cases should not be collapsed into one tactic. Hanji 719 is an upstream political-marriage and empress-deposition setup, while Hanji 720 is the direct biological status-loss consequence for Liu Qiang. The Zhouji 97 case is a deliberate entrepreneurial adoption strategy backed by a credential. Hanji 751 is a coercive harem-succession struggle in which accusation and maternal-line removal matter as much as adoption. Hanji 918 and Hanji 943 show a factional label structure built from existing maternal and foster ties, not a manufactured adoption transaction.

The concept also does not mean maternal status alone determines succession. Ruler approval, palace access, written proof, outer-relative offices, military alliance value, and the rival side's weakness all mediate whether maternal affiliation becomes politically decisive.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 719 as the political-marriage precondition for Guo Shengtong's maternal status and Liu Qiang's later vulnerability.
- Reframed the Eastern Han Guo-Liu Qiang case as a two-step sequence: alliance-backed empress status first, biological status loss after deposition second.
- Preserved Hanji 720 as the direct biological status-loss variant where Guo Shengtong's deposition destabilizes Crown Prince Liu Qiang.
- Preserved Hanji 751 as the coercive Eastern Han foster-maternal variant.
- Preserved Hanji 918 and Hanji 943 as factional-label variants rather than forcing them into the adoption model.
