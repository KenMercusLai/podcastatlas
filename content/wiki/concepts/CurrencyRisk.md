# Currency Risk

[[176-niuyue-yinian-yige-beiguanzhuyizhe-de-huofa-725-shalong-shilu-lte-k8lw8e1txxw3nfxvo3wal8ah]] adds [[BaseCurrencyThinking]] as a household implementation rule. [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] argues that ordinary investors should decide their real base currency from future spending and living needs before treating RMB, dollars, or any other currency as an investment thesis; he also warns that exchange rates are especially hard to forecast.

Currency risk is the reminder that final value depends on the currency a household spends or reports in, not only the asset's local-currency yield or illustrated return. [[ep39-feng-man-lou-xiaji-quanqiu-shuaitui-manman-bijin-yanfang-sishou-bubu-weiying-manliao-xiabannian-meigu-meizhai-huilv-lgz718un4sjvgxqbydfikyxz0hcj]] applies this to U.S. Treasuries, dollar deposits, QDII products, RMB/USD moves, and yen/carry-trade volatility. [[ep18-dou-shi-huang-quan-yu-yue-ke-baoxian-mai-dui-xin-an-le-lly-wx9zecfmov5cbmufmynlosw]] extends it to [[OverseasInsuranceRisk]] by warning that foreign-currency insurance can add uncertainty for households whose income, spending, education, and retirement plans remain domestic. [[ep89-haiwai-quanshang-da-dizhen-kuajing-touzi-xinshidai-li8ya-r5cpz3sifdjby73vh9-rxs]] adds that overseas equity returns must be judged after FX movement and access-route friction, not only by looking back at winning U.S. stocks.

[[129-huobi-de-benzhi-yiji-huangjin-de-zhenzheng-jiazhi-chuantai-shifen-xiyin-lsjbfttqxf58uk-a4g8-srretkwb]] deepens the concept from exchange-rate volatility into measurement-unit risk. If money is [[MoneyAsFlow]] and a currency's value depends on its payment, credit, and economic network, then investment returns are always denominated in a unit whose own credibility can change. In that frame, gold, Bitcoin, RMB, dollars, and Treasuries are not just assets or currencies; they are competing units for measuring wealth during [[CurrencyAnchorTransition]].

## Key Claims
- A U.S. bond or deposit yield can be partly erased if the dollar weakens against the investor's home currency.
- Investors should exchange currency for a real use case such as travel, education, long-term allocation, or specific assets, rather than treating FX as a simple short-term trade.
- Professional FX trading often uses high leverage, making it unsuitable as casual retail speculation.
- The episode links currency movements to [[FederalReserve]] cuts, [[BankOfJapan]] policy, [[YenCarryTrade]] changes, Asian currency moves, and [[RMBExchangeRatePolicy]].
- Foreign-currency insurance can create the same mismatch problem when premiums, dividends, surrender value, or claims are measured in a currency the family does not normally use.
- Overseas stock gains can look different after RMB/USD movement, especially if the underlying asset return is modest or the investor later needs RMB liquidity.
- Episode 129 adds that currency risk is not only conversion risk; it is also the risk that the accounting unit used to report wealth loses credibility or stops feeling like the right anchor.
- Thinking in gold, RMB, dollars, or Bitcoin can produce different pictures of the same portfolio, which makes [[MoneyIllusion]] a practical investing problem.
