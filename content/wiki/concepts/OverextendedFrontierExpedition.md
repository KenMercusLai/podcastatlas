## Definition

Overextended frontier expedition / 边境远征过度 is the strategic failure pattern where a real frontier threat is answered by an attack whose scale, timing, pursuit depth, or support conditions exceed what the state or commander can actually sustain.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 909 supplies the original large-campaign case. Repeated [[Xianbei|Xianbei]] raids are real, but [[CaiYong|Cai Yong]] argues that state capacity, border discipline, enemy strength, internal disorder, and campaign cost do not support a deep punitive offensive. The court still launches three routes under [[XiaYuLateHan|Xia Yu]], [[TianYanLateHan|Tian Yan]], and [[ZangMinLateHan|Zang Min]], each going more than two thousand li beyond the frontier before [[TanshihuaiXianbei|Tanshihuai]] concentrates Xianbei forces and destroys the expedition.

Hanji 757 adds a smaller Eastern Han Qiang-frontier version through [[FuYuEasternHan|Fu Yu]]. Fu Yu requests fifty thousand troops from the commanderies but attacks [[MiWuQiangLeader|Mi Wu]] before the forces assemble. He then sends three thousand elite cavalry in urgent pursuit, reaches Sandou Valley at night with relaxed caution, and is killed with more than eight hundred subordinates.

Hanji 651 adds a late-Xin southwest version under [[WangMang|王莽]]. The Quting campaigns keep scaling after failure: Feng Mao's army suffers severe epidemic losses and civilian seizure, then Lian Dan and Shi Xiong mobilize a much larger multi-commandery force that runs short of grain, starves, and sickens. The concept therefore includes court-level deep-war overreach, command-level tempo overreach, and escalation lock-in after earlier failure.

## Key Claims

- A frontier threat can be real while the chosen campaign still exceeds actual capacity.
- Overextension can come from depth and scale, as in Hanji 909, or from premature timing and pursuit, as in Hanji 757.
- Force requests matter only if the commander waits for the requested support to exist.
- Repeating a failed campaign at larger scale can deepen overextension when the underlying supply, disease, and civilian-burden problems remain unresolved.
- Enemy withdrawal can bait overconfident pursuit when intelligence, discipline, and rest conditions are weak.
- Strategic restraint is not equivalent to ignoring danger; it can preserve force until conditions support action.
- Failed overextension can worsen the frontier problem by creating deaths, revenge pressure, fiscal strain, and credibility loss.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is not pacifism and does not deny frontier danger. Hanji 909 starts from real Xianbei raids, Hanji 757 starts from an unstable Qiang-frontier situation, and Hanji 651 starts from a southwest rebellion and court desire for control. The concept applies when the response outruns conditions. Hanji 909 gives a systemic court and strategy dossier, Hanji 757 gives a compressed command-failure case, and Hanji 651 gives an escalation-after-failure case; the three should be compared by pattern, not treated as identical in scale.
