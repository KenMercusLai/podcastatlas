## Definition

Overextended frontier expedition / 边境远征过度 is the strategic failure pattern where a real frontier threat is answered by an attack whose scale, timing, pursuit depth, or support conditions exceed what the state or commander can actually sustain.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 909 supplies the original large-campaign case. Repeated [[Xianbei|Xianbei]] raids are real, but [[CaiYong|Cai Yong]] argues that state capacity, border discipline, enemy strength, internal disorder, and campaign cost do not support a deep punitive offensive. The court still launches three routes under [[XiaYuLateHan|Xia Yu]], [[TianYanLateHan|Tian Yan]], and [[ZangMinLateHan|Zang Min]], each going more than two thousand li beyond the frontier before [[TanshihuaiXianbei|Tanshihuai]] concentrates Xianbei forces and destroys the expedition.

Hanji 757 adds a smaller Eastern Han Qiang-frontier version through [[FuYuEasternHan|Fu Yu]]. Fu Yu requests fifty thousand troops from the commanderies but attacks [[MiWuQiangLeader|Mi Wu]] before the forces assemble. He then sends three thousand elite cavalry in urgent pursuit, reaches Sandou Valley at night with relaxed caution, and is killed with more than eight hundred subordinates. The concept therefore includes both court-level deep-war overreach and command-level tempo overreach.

## Key Claims

- A frontier threat can be real while the chosen campaign still exceeds actual capacity.
- Overextension can come from depth and scale, as in Hanji 909, or from premature timing and pursuit, as in Hanji 757.
- Force requests matter only if the commander waits for the requested support to exist.
- Enemy withdrawal can bait overconfident pursuit when intelligence, discipline, and rest conditions are weak.
- Strategic restraint is not equivalent to ignoring danger; it can preserve force until conditions support action.
- Failed overextension can worsen the frontier problem by creating deaths, revenge pressure, fiscal strain, and credibility loss.

## Evidence

- Capacity warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-909-2025-ruhe-yangcheng-yi-shen-guiqi-lomndmphrybunjbbbbn9ebafcsj|Hanji 909]] says Cai Yong opposes the Xianbei campaign by weighing national capacity, enemy strength, border leakage, internal unrest, and cost.
- Deep expedition failure: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-909-2025-ruhe-yangcheng-yi-shen-guiqi-lomndmphrybunjbbbbn9ebafcsj|Hanji 909]] says three Han commanders each advance more than two thousand li before Tanshihuai's forces destroy them.
- Command-selection risk: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-909-2025-ruhe-yangcheng-yi-shen-guiqi-lomndmphrybunjbbbbn9ebafcsj|Hanji 909]] says Tian Yan reaches command through Wang Fu's palace access, linking patronage to campaign risk.
- Premature Qiang attack: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-757-rensheng-xiang-shao-zou-wanlu-xian-zhaozhun-dingwei-lri1zyiq8210ymqjakznyp3v4k6x|Hanji 757]] says Fu Yu requests fifty thousand troops but attacks Mi Wu before the commandery troops assemble.
- Pursuit trap: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-757-rensheng-xiang-shao-zou-wanlu-xian-zhaozhun-dingwei-lri1zyiq8210ymqjakznyp3v4k6x|Hanji 757]] says Fu Yu sends three thousand elite cavalry in pursuit, reaches Sandou Valley at night, relaxes caution, and is killed when Mi Wu attacks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This concept is not pacifism and does not deny frontier danger. Hanji 909 starts from real Xianbei raids, and Hanji 757 starts from an unstable Qiang-frontier situation. The concept applies when the response outruns conditions. Hanji 909 gives a systemic court and strategy dossier, while Hanji 757 gives a compressed command-failure case; the two should be compared by pattern, not treated as identical in scale.
