# Migraine Recognition and Triage

## Definition
Migraine recognition and triage is the process of identifying a recurring migraine-like symptom pattern while separating it from other primary headaches and potentially dangerous secondary causes.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-157-zui-touteng-de-wenti-jiushi-touteng-ft-dawu-shiye-banmajiang-lrkqhlcwhxomw_aegpd_rzco8vio|VOL.157]] rejects the shortcut that migraine simply means pain on one side. Location can contribute, but recurrence, duration, severity, functional disruption, nausea or vomiting, light and sound sensitivity, and aura-like symptoms provide a more useful pattern. Tension-type, cluster, cervicogenic, and secondary headaches can overlap in location while differing in associated signs, history, and risk.

The same framework avoids two opposite errors. Not every headache implies hemorrhage, aneurysm, stroke, infection, mass disease, or a cardiac cause, yet severe, unusual, rapidly changing, or neurologic-deficit-like presentations should not be normalized through a self-diagnosed migraine label. History, examination, selectively used imaging, and cross-specialty review belong to qualified assessment. VOL.42 adds that PFO can be considered in selected persistent or otherwise unexplained cases, but its commonness and variable response to closure prevent a simple PFO-causes-migraine rule.

## Key Claims
- One-sided pain is neither necessary nor sufficient for a migraine diagnosis.
- Recurrent pattern, duration, severity, disability, nausea, sensory sensitivity, and aura are more informative together than any one symptom.
- Visual or sensory aura can precede pain, but atypical neurologic loss requires cautious assessment.
- Other primary headaches and cervicogenic pain require different pattern recognition.
- Secondary-headache exclusion depends on clinical history, neurologic examination, and context-dependent testing.
- A discovered PFO can be relevant to selected cases but does not by itself explain migraine or create a closure indication.

## Evidence
- Diagnostic pattern: [[vol-157-zui-touteng-de-wenti-jiushi-touteng-ft-dawu-shiye-banmajiang-lrkqhlcwhxomw_aegpd_rzco8vio]] distinguishes unilateral pain from the broader recurring migraine syndrome and names nausea, vomiting, light sensitivity, sound sensitivity, and aura.
- Differential diagnosis: [[vol-157-zui-touteng-de-wenti-jiushi-touteng-ft-dawu-shiye-banmajiang-lrkqhlcwhxomw_aegpd_rzco8vio]] contrasts tension-type, cluster, cervicogenic, primary, and secondary headache patterns.
- Escalation boundary: [[vol-157-zui-touteng-de-wenti-jiushi-touteng-ft-dawu-shiye-banmajiang-lrkqhlcwhxomw_aegpd_rzco8vio]] connects serious or unusual presentations to history, examination, and imaging rather than podcast-based diagnosis.
- Cross-specialty causal boundary: [[vol-42-xinxueguan-neike-tingshuo-ni-zongshi-touteng-keneng-xuyao-zuo-yijian-du-xin-shi-lai-jiejue-lvw85wqzb0kvpyk5uipsagy13b11|VOL.42]] places PFO after broader headache assessment and reports variable rather than guaranteed improvement following closure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are conversational public education, not diagnostic guidelines. Their brief screening questions, attack descriptions, aura categories, imaging practice, vascular-risk statements, named subtypes, PFO prevalence, and closure-response figures should not be converted into a self-diagnosis rule, emergency checklist, or procedure indication without current clinical guidance.

## What Changed
- Added PFO as a selected cross-specialty differential without promoting it to a default explanation.
- Added variable response to closure as a reason to preserve causal and treatment uncertainty.
