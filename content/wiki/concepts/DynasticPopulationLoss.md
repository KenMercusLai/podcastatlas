## Definition

王朝更替人口损耗 / dynastic population loss is the large-scale demographic damage associated with regime change, civil war, conquest, famine, epidemic, migration, and administrative collapse during Chinese dynastic transitions.

## Current Synthesis

Hanji 388-3 frames Chinese dynastic transition as a demographic catastrophe for ordinary people rather than only a change in ruling house. The episode lists repeated population peaks and troughs from Qin-Han through Taiping-era Qing, arguing that even corrected estimates still leave losses on the scale of tens of millions and, in later high-population eras, up to roughly one hundred million.

The concept must be paired with [[HistoricalPopulationRegistrationCaution|户口数字解读谨慎]]. Population registers do not equal deaths one-for-one, but the correction does not erase the underlying pattern: large wars and state breakdown repeatedly translate political conflict into mass civilian loss.

## Key Claims

- Dynastic transition should be evaluated through civilian demographic loss, not just territorial unification or elite succession.
- Population loss includes death but also displacement, hidden households, unregistered groups, and administrative failure.
- Corrected estimates still show severe declines across Qin-Han, late Eastern Han and Three Kingdoms, Sui-Tang, Song-Yuan, Ming-Qing, and the Taiping era.
- Han Wudi's reign is used as an imperial-achievement entry point because war frequency and late risky campaigns make rulerly grandeur inseparable from cost.
- The source's moral focus is ordinary people's suffering behind the numbers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The source offers a high-level population-history summary, not a period-by-period demographic reconstruction. Exact figures, causal allocation, regional variation, and the distinction between death, flight, concealment, and registry failure remain source-scoped. The concept should also not imply that all dynastic transitions had identical causes or mortality structures.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Hanji 388-3 to capture cross-dynastic demographic loss as a civilian-cost frame.
