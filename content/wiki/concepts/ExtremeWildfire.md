# Extreme Wildfire

Extreme wildfire is the category used in [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]] for fires that stand out by intensity, size, speed of spread, or damage. [[OliverMorton]] distinguishes these fires from ordinary fire-season expansion: the question is not only whether more land burns, but whether more events overwhelm ordinary expectations and institutions.

The episode's examples include [[Australia]]'s Black Summer fires, the 2023 fires in [[Canada]], and destructive Los Angeles fires in [[California]]. The concept extends [[NaturalHazardSocialDisaster]] because extreme fires become disasters through exposure, settlement, smoke vulnerability, insurance, suppression capacity, and whether [[LandscapeFirePrevention]] has been sustained before ignition.

## Key Claims
- Extreme fire should be measured by intensity, size, speed, and damage, not only by whether a fire occurred.
- Longer fire seasons and more very-high-risk fire-weather days make extreme events more likely.
- Extreme fires can produce global effects through smoke, CO2 release, ocean fertilization, and atmospheric feedbacks.
- Direct flame deaths are only one harm channel; [[WildfireSmokeHealthBurden]] may dominate the population cost.
