# Allergy Immune Imbalance / 过敏免疫失衡

## Definition
Allergy immune imbalance is the VOL.201 frame that allergic symptoms come from a dysregulated immune response to otherwise harmless substances, not from immunity being simply too strong.

## Current Synthesis
[[vol-201-weishenme-jinjinian-guomin-de-ren-yuelaiyue-duo-guomin-tongshike-lgmmw7ejv8ulj0y5gokkv7wieu0z]] uses [[WangHuiyingAllergyDoctor|汪惠英]]'s explanation to correct two common simplifications at once: allergy is neither proof of superior immunity nor just bodily weakness. In normal tolerance, the body can fight bacteria and viruses while ignoring pollen, smell, dust, food particles, or other harmless exposures. In allergy, that discrimination fails, so sneezing, mucus, itch, rash, wheeze, congestion, and inflammation become excessive defenses. [[vol-154-chuntian-yang-dao-naoxin-poju-guomin-jie-de-shengcun-zhinan-lnxaxys733qzi9rkcwvgqt6uzfur]] supplies an earlier, less precise version by calling allergy an over-sensitive protective response rather than low immunity.

Across the sources, symptom expression is multi-causal. Microbial exposure, over-cleaning, new industrial allergens, infection, stress, sleep loss, diet change, hormonal fluctuation, season, household humidity, dust mites, pollen, mold, pets, and individual susceptibility can all shift whether exposure becomes symptomatic. The older episode's wind, heat, cold, dampness, “发物,” and organ-system explanations remain TCM interpretations rather than interchangeable immunological mechanisms.

## Key Claims
- Allergy should be understood as failed tolerance or immune imbalance rather than simply strong immunity.
- Harmless environmental substances can become clinically burdensome when the body's response over-defends against them.
- Reduced microbial exposure and heavy disinfection may plausibly weaken immune training, but the source treats this as one factor rather than a sole cause.
- Adult-onset or worsening allergy can reflect accumulated allergen exposure plus infection, stress, sleep, diet, and environmental change.
- Hormonal fluctuations, especially around menstrual cycles, can modulate allergic inflammation in some patients.
- “Over-sensitive protection” is a useful public-language approximation, but failed immune tolerance is the more specific clinical claim in the available sources.

## Evidence
- Immune-tolerance correction: [[vol-201-weishenme-jinjinian-guomin-de-ren-yuelaiyue-duo-guomin-tongshike-lgmmw7ejv8ulj0y5gokkv7wieu0z]] says allergy is not immune strength but an imbalanced response to harmless external substances.
- Multi-factor emergence: [[vol-201-weishenme-jinjinian-guomin-de-ren-yuelaiyue-duo-guomin-tongshike-lgmmw7ejv8ulj0y5gokkv7wieu0z]] links adult allergy and post-infection flare patterns to allergen exposure, antibodies, work stress, sleep loss, diet change, viral infection, and COVID-era conditions.
- Hormonal modulation: [[vol-201-weishenme-jinjinian-guomin-de-ren-yuelaiyue-duo-guomin-tongshike-lgmmw7ejv8ulj0y5gokkv7wieu0z]] explains menstrual-period worsening through estrogen, progesterone, and TH2-skewed inflammation.
- Earlier public explanation: [[vol-154-chuntian-yang-dao-naoxin-poju-guomin-jie-de-shengcun-zhinan-lnxaxys733qzi9rkcwvgqt6uzfur]] rejects the idea that allergy is low immunity and instead describes an excessive protective reaction, while also adding source-scoped TCM pattern language.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are public conversations, not mechanistic reviews or individualized diagnoses. Hygiene, COVID infection, stress, hormones, lifestyle, season, diet, and climate are contributors or observations, not universal explanations. The VOL.154 TCM categories do not establish immunological mechanism, and broad food-avoidance or “constitution-changing” claims are not validated by the transcript.

## What Changed
- Added the earlier episode's accessible “over-sensitive protective response” explanation while retaining failed tolerance as the more precise clinical synthesis.
- Separated seasonal and TCM interpretations from established immunological mechanism.
