# Naval Breakthrough Tactics

## Definition

Naval breakthrough tactics are an approach that accepts concentrated danger while closing with an enemy formation in order to penetrate it, disrupt mutual support, and create local close-range advantages.

## Current Synthesis

At the [[BattleOfTrafalgar|Battle of Trafalgar]], the concept joins geometry, timing, morale, technology, and delegated command. [[HoratioNelson]]'s two columns were vulnerable as they approached the allied line, but penetration converted that risk into stern-raking fire and confused ship-to-ship fighting where the episode says British gunnery and preparation were strongest. [[CuthbertCollingwood]]'s early isolated engagement shows that success depended not on safety or perfect coordination but on prior intent, trusted captains, and rapid exploitation of [[PierreCharlesVilleneuve|Villeneuve]]'s disordered line.

## Key Claims

- Breakthrough tactics trade approach vulnerability for positional advantage after penetration.
- Formation disorder in the defending line increases the attacker's opportunity to isolate local targets.
- Close-action success depends on crew preparation, firing speed, seamanship, and damage tolerance, not geometry alone.
- Clear pre-battle intent and subordinate trust become more important as smoke and collision reduce central control.
- Heroic narratives can obscure how much the method depends on institutional capability and extreme human cost.

## Evidence

### Accepted exposure and positional payoff

- [[245-trafalgar-victory-part-3-glt4217731619]] describes the slow British approach under fire, followed by Royal Sovereign and Victory penetrating and raking the allied line.

### Delegated execution under confusion

- [[245-trafalgar-victory-part-3-glt4217731619]] emphasizes Nelson's prior communication, captain-level trust, Collingwood's initiative, smoke, collision, and the loss of centralized tactical visibility.

### Capability and cost

- [[245-trafalgar-victory-part-3-glt4217731619]] connects close action to British firing systems and practiced crews while documenting severe casualties on both sides.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

One successful battle does not establish a universally superior method. The episode's explanation is British-centered and may understate weather, coalition constraints, allied skill, chance, and survivorship in judging the plan. Casualty figures and technological comparisons remain source-scoped, while the outcome does not erase the approach's genuine risk.

## What Changed

- Established a tactical synthesis separating the plan's deliberate exposure from the institutional capabilities that made the gamble work.
