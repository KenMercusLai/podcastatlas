## Definition

A narrative bridge protagonist is a character whose mobility, sociability, viewpoint, and relationships connect semi-independent story regions, making structural linkage—not only heroic centrality or screen time—a criterion for protagonist status.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded episode develops the concept through [[DuanYu|段誉]] in [[TianLongBaBu|《天龙八部》]]. He opens the novel in Dali, enters the Gusu Murong network, crosses into Qiao Feng and the Beggars' Sect crisis, participates in the Zhenlong-board sequence, befriends Xuzhu, links the three sworn brothers, and supplies the viewpoint for Murong Fu's closing image. His lack of rigid factional or status attachment makes movement between groups plausible.

This does not settle who is the novel's “first protagonist.” [[XiaoFeng|萧峰]] may dominate heroic memory, and a three-protagonist model may better capture the novel's segmented architecture. The concept instead clarifies the criterion: narrative centrality can arise from opening paths, transferring attention, connecting people, and closing the map even when another character bears the largest tragic weight.

## Key Claims

- Protagonist status can depend on connective function as well as heroic magnitude, viewpoint duration, or adaptation prominence.
- A bridge character makes transitions legible by entering multiple social, geographic, and plot networks.
- Sociability across rank and faction can be a structural capacity rather than only a personality trait.
- Relationship brokerage can convert parallel heroes into an ensemble, as with the sworn-brother triad.
- Opening and closing viewpoint positions strengthen the sense that one character conducts the reader through the work.
- The framework can coexist with plural protagonists and does not require a single winner in a hierarchy debate.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The concept is extracted from one podcast's structural interpretation, not a formal narratological study. Counting appearances or links would not by itself establish emotional, thematic, or causal primacy. Screen adaptations may redistribute prominence, and readers can reasonably prioritize Xiao Feng's tragic force or the three-brother ensemble. A mobile character also needs causally meaningful links; mere travel does not make a protagonist a bridge.

## What Changed

- Created a criterion for protagonist centrality based on linkage, transition, brokerage, and viewpoint position.
- Preserved heroic-centrality and plural-protagonist alternatives to the Duan Yu claim.
