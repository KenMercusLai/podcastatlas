# Southern Conservative Realignment Signal

## Definition

The Southern conservative realignment signal is the interpretation of Wallace's 1968 support as early evidence that white Southern and working-class voters were becoming available to a Republican coalition organized around race, order, federal power, cultural identity, and regional change.

## Current Synthesis

Wallace's five states and roughly ten million votes mattered beyond the failed third-party bid. The source uses Kevin Phillips's post-election analysis and later voting patterns to argue that political gravity was moving toward the Sun Belt and that parts of the Democratic white working-class and Southern base could be absorbed by Republicans. Wallace did not single-handedly cause that transition; he made an emerging coalition unusually visible.

## Key Claims

- Third-party performance can reveal a coalition before a major party fully incorporates it.
- Wallace demonstrated that a segregation-rooted appeal could travel through national cultural and class language.
- Later Republican success in Wallace states is consistent with realignment but does not establish one-candidate causation.
- Sun Belt growth, Democratic fracture, race, class, religion, crime, and federalism belong to the same transition without collapsing into one cause.

## Evidence

- Contemporary interpretation: [[511-america-in-68-george-wallace-the-first-donald-trump-part-4-glt8855008412]] presents Kevin Phillips reading Wallace as evidence of a changing political map and white working-class movement away from Democrats.
- Electoral sequence: [[511-america-in-68-george-wallace-the-first-donald-trump-part-4-glt8855008412]] notes later Nixon, Reagan, George W. Bush, and Trump victories across the Wallace states.
- Coalition mechanism: [[511-america-in-68-george-wallace-the-first-donald-trump-part-4-glt8855008412]] connects race, law and order, schools, anti-elitism, conservative evangelical networks, and Sun Belt opportunity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

Later voting continuity does not prove that the same individuals, motives, or organizations moved intact from Wallace to later Republican candidates. Demography, migration, party strategy, candidate effects, turnout, religion, economics, and policy changed across decades. The episode's lineage is a strong interpretive claim rather than a complete causal model.
