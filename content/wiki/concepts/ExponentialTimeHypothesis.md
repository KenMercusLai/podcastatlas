# Exponential Time Hypothesis

## Definition

The exponential time hypothesis is a complexity-theory conjecture that 3SAT cannot be solved in subexponential time in the worst case; it is stronger and more specific than the claim that P differs from NP.

## Current Synthesis

The bounded record contains one secondary account of an unverified OpenAI paper. The episode says the paper claims a subexponential algorithm for a class of 3SAT problems and therefore challenges the exponential time hypothesis without proving P equals NP. The distinction between a restricted class and worst-case 3SAT is decisive: a class-specific improvement may be important without refuting the full hypothesis.

The episode uses the claim mainly as an [[AIForMath|AI-for-math]] case. If independent review confirms a meaningful result, it would show AI accelerating search in theoretical computer science; until then, the correct wiki state is an open validation question rather than a breakthrough finding.

## Key Claims

- The exponential time hypothesis concerns subexponential worst-case algorithms for 3SAT.
- Challenging the hypothesis is not equivalent to proving P equals NP.
- A result for a restricted class does not automatically refute the general hypothesis.
- Complexity-theory claims require precise problem definitions, asymptotic bounds, and independent proof review.
- AI can accelerate mathematical exploration without making its outputs self-validating.
