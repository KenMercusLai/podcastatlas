# Regicide as Republican Founding

## Definition

Regicide as republican founding is the interpretation that killing a deposed monarch can serve as a constitutive act through which a republic demonstrates that monarchy has ended and makes its own sovereignty publicly credible.

## Current Synthesis

The episode argues that the French Republic lacked one universally decisive founding proclamation, so the execution of [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] could become its irreversible threshold. The act destroyed the king as both person and symbol and led [[JeanPaulMarat|Marat]] to describe belief in the Republic as real only after the tyrant's head fell; yet royalist martyrdom and coming civil conflict show that constitutive violence can found one political identity without producing shared legitimacy.

## Key Claims

- A new regime may seek irreversibility by physically eliminating the office embodied in a former ruler.
- Legal punishment and sacrificial political meaning can coexist even when official language denies sacrifice.
- Public spectacle converts abstract sovereignty into a visible claim about who may judge and punish.
- Founding through violence draws a boundary between loyal citizens and adherents of the displaced order.
- An act can consolidate republican belief among supporters while deepening civil conflict.
- Rival martyr narratives prevent execution from fixing one permanent meaning.

## Evidence

### The king as political symbol

- [[547-the-french-revolution-the-execution-of-the-king-part-4-glt2184509954]] presents Louis as accused of conduct and as the embodiment of monarchy and worldwide despotism.

### Constitutive spectacle

- [[547-the-french-revolution-the-execution-of-the-king-part-4-glt2184509954]] connects the renamed square, controlled crowd, military presence, guillotine, displayed head, and republican acclamations to a founding act.

### Unsettled legitimacy

- [[547-the-french-revolution-the-execution-of-the-king-part-4-glt2184509954]] contrasts Marat's republican certainty with royalist martyrdom, Louis XVII, and the prospect of internal war.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- This is the hosts' interpretive synthesis rather than proof that France had no other constitutional or symbolic founding moments.
- The execution did not create consensus, end royalism, or by itself explain the Republic's later trajectory.
- “Sacrifice” describes political meaning, not a settled legal category or shared contemporary theology.

## What Changed

- A canonical concept now captures the episode's central distinction between execution as sentence and execution as republican foundation.
