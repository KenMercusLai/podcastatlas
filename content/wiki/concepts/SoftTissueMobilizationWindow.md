# Soft-Tissue Mobilization Window

## Definition
A soft-tissue mobilization window is a temporary change in discomfort, range, or movement tolerance created by rolling, compression, massage, or related self-treatment and then tested through purposeful movement.

## Current Synthesis
[[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] presents foam rolling and targeted compression as means rather than ends. The source's D2R2 mnemonic—desensitize, decongest, reperfuse, and restore—organizes the proposed effects, but the actionable standard is simpler: choose a relevant stiff or uncomfortable area, use pressure that still permits breathing and voluntary contraction, relax gradually, and then reassess range or movement.

The durable judgment is transfer. Symptom relief or a range change is useful when it enables better movement, sleep, or training; it does not prove that fascia was remodeled, an injury healed, or a diagnosis was established. Extreme pain, indiscriminate whole-body rolling, and repeated symptom suppression without examining load or clinical causes fall outside the concept.

## Key Claims
- Rolling, massage, and compression are best judged by whether they create a useful short-term change in symptoms or movement.
- Targeted work on a relevant area is more informative than mindless whole-body treatment.
- Pressure should remain compatible with full breathing, voluntary contraction, and the ability to stop.
- A mobility window should be followed by movement that tests or uses the changed range.
- Symptom modulation does not establish tissue healing, structural correction, or diagnostic safety.
- Trauma, systemic red flags, neurological change, major swelling, bowel or bladder change, or persistent functional loss require appropriate assessment.

## Evidence
- Temporary-window claim - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] says rolling can reduce pain, restore range, or reduce soreness and is most useful when it opens movement.
- Technique boundary - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] recommends pausing on a relevant area, breathing, contracting briefly, and relaxing with long exhales rather than chasing maximal discomfort.
- Tolerability boundary - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] says self-mobilization should permit full breathing and voluntary control.
- D2R2 frame - [[how-to-improve-your-mobility-posture-flexibility-dr-kelly-starrett-scim3685343544]] attributes symptom and range change to desensitization, congestion reduction, reperfusion, and restoration while also favoring controlled movement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The structured source summary does not provide effect sizes, duration, mechanism comparisons, or condition-specific evidence for foam rolling, fascia work, D2R2, abdominal compression, or delayed-onset soreness. Short-term range or pain change does not demonstrate tissue remodeling or faster healing. Deep abdominal or pelvic work may be inappropriate in pregnancy, recent surgery, acute illness, bleeding, hernia, vascular disease, unexplained pain, or other medical contexts; intimate or persistent pelvic symptoms warrant qualified care.
