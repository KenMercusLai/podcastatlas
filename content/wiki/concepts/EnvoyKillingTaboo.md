# 不斩来使 / Envoy Killing Taboo

Envoy killing taboo / 不斩来使 is the diplomatic norm [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-53-qinhuiwang-yong-zhangyi-huan-dipi-lvmo2cwbafcg2gnxliuo18fewimk]] uses to explain why killing [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] could help [[QinState|秦国]] even if [[ChuState|楚国]] satisfied [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]]'s desire for revenge. The host says states at war generally did not kill envoys, so Zhang Yi's death in Chu would supply Qin with a stronger claim to punish Chu.

The norm matters because Zhang Yi is both the hated deceiver from the earlier [[UnverifiedTerritorialPromiseTrap|Shangyu promise trap]] and a Qin envoy in the later exchange. Chu's private revenge impulse therefore runs into public diplomatic cost.

The concept is useful for reading why Qin Huiwen Wang's calculation can be two-sided. If Zhang Yi survives and is used, Qin may gain [[Qianzhong|黔中]]; if Zhang Yi is killed, Qin gains a war pretext. The norm turns a personal death into interstate legitimacy.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-3-hanfei-yu-yaojia-lunzhan-qinwang-dang-caipan-ljxxlok4uh-ajjirmolybodlspv9]] adds a boundary case through [[HanFei|韩非]]. The host treats Han Fei's death as a late-Warring-States tragedy because an older "do not kill envoys" norm no longer reliably protects him once [[LiSi|李斯]] frames him as a [[HanState|韩国]] royal whose loyalty cannot be trusted. The case differs from Zhang Yi: Han Fei is imprisoned and poisoned inside Qin rather than openly executed by a hostile receiving court, but the episode uses it to mark the norm's weakening under conquest politics.

## Key Claims
- A hostile court may want revenge against an envoy, but killing him changes the diplomatic and military justification available to the sending state.
- Diplomatic norms can protect individual messengers while also being exploited as pretexts by stronger states.
- The norm makes Zhang Yi's risk legible as both personal danger and Qin strategic leverage.
- The norm's protection weakens when the envoy can be recoded as an internal security threat rather than treated as a protected messenger.
