## Definition

The immersive experience throughput tradeoff is the tension between giving each guest a detailed, interactive, well-paced encounter and moving enough guests through a fixed attraction to control queues and serve mass demand.

## Current Synthesis

In Universal's haunted-house case, richer lighting, sound, scenery, actors, opening performances, and close-range interaction can increase dwell time and reduce hourly capacity. Continuous guest streams raise throughput, but later visitors may see a scare before reaching it and everyone has less time to inspect the environment. The tradeoff is not a claim that immersion and capacity can never improve together; it identifies a recurring design frontier requiring choices about group size, pacing, repeatability, staffing, layout, and the acceptable loss of personalization.

## Key Claims

- Interaction time and individualized pacing consume scarce attraction capacity.
- Continuous flow can reduce queues while weakening suspense, scene inspection, and the isolation needed for a personal scare.
- Detailed environments have limited value when operating pressure moves guests past them too quickly.
- More actors can enrich an encounter but also raise labor cost and make timing more complex.
- Capacity should be treated as part of experience design, not as a separate downstream logistics problem.

## Evidence

### Interaction and dwell time

- [[gaojiesi-kafeidou-cong-wanshengjie-xianding-dao-changnian-xiangmu-huanqiu-yingcheng-weihe-yazhu-jingxia-tiyan-1024529992]] describes rooms with multiple actors, opening performances, and two-to-three-hour waits, linking longer guest interaction to fewer guests served per unit of time.

### Flow and immersion loss

- [[gaojiesi-kafeidou-cong-wanshengjie-xianding-dao-changnian-xiangmu-huanqiu-yingcheng-weihe-yazhu-jingxia-tiyan-1024529992]] reports that sending guests through in continuous lines improves flow while reducing time with scenic detail and exposing upcoming scares to people farther back.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- One haunted-house format does not establish a universal capacity curve for all immersive entertainment.
- Better layout, timed entry, modular scenes, automation, variable routing, or demand pricing may improve both flow and perceived immersion.
- Queue length can reflect novelty, pricing, poor capacity, or high demand; it is not a direct measure of experience quality.
- The source supplies no measured hourly throughput, satisfaction, abandonment, staffing cost, or comparison among designs.

## What Changed

- Established capacity as an intrinsic constraint on personalized immersive design.
- Added continuous guest flow as a concrete operational compromise with suspense and scenic attention.
- Preserved simultaneous design improvement as possible rather than treating the tradeoff as fixed.
