# Home Improvement Warehouse Model

## Definition

The home improvement warehouse model is the retail format in [[home-depot-1]] where a store combines warehouse-scale space and low prices with enough assortment and expertise for customers to complete entire repair, renovation, and building projects.

## Current Synthesis

The model borrows from warehouse retail but diverges from warehouse clubs. Home improvement requires many more SKUs, project adjacency, bulky goods, urgent replacement demand, and associates who can explain how a job is done. Its moat therefore comes from selection, price, service, supplier volume, and logistics density working together.

## Key Claims
- The model works when customers can solve an entire project in one place.
- High assortment makes the format different from [[LimitedSKUOperatingModel]] even if both use warehouse economics.
- Service expertise is part of the model, not decoration.
- Supplier financing and fast inventory turns make capital scarcity survivable.
- At scale, logistics density becomes as important as the store box.

## Evidence
- Project completion: [[home-depot-1]] says Home Depot aimed for roughly 25,000 SKUs so customers could complete whole projects from one store.
- Warehouse difference: [[home-depot-1]] contrasts Home Depot with Price Club and Costco because home improvement needed expertise and breadth rather than only limited assortment.
- Service: [[home-depot-1]] says associates with trade knowledge helped customers learn decks, blinds, floors, and similar projects.
- Capital and logistics: [[home-depot-1]] links early supplier terms to inventory velocity and later distribution networks to rapid bulky-goods fulfillment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The model is not universally copyable. [[home-depot-1]] says several copycats imitated the warehouse shell but failed to reproduce service culture, volume, supplier relationships, and operating discipline.
