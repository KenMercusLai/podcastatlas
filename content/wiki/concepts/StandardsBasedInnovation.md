# Standards-Based Innovation

Standards-based innovation is the source's broader claim that modern technology often advances through shared rules and licensing institutions, not only through isolated invention. In [[the-invention-invention]], [[MPEG]] works because many devices and companies agree on a common video-compression language.

The concept depends on legal plumbing. [[PatentPool|Patent pools]], [[EssentialPatents]], and [[FRANDLicensing]] let rivals implement shared standards without every user negotiating separately with every patent owner. But standards also require antitrust guardrails because coordination can become [[PatentPoolCartelRisk|cartel risk]].

## Key Claims
- Interoperability can be as important as the underlying technical trick.
- Standards turn isolated inventions into usable ecosystems.
- Licensing institutions decide whether a standard becomes widely implementable or trapped behind rights conflicts.
- [[CollaborationVsCollusion]] is the central governance problem: the same coordination can expand markets or suppress competition.
