# Procrastination Self-Regulation Failure

Procrastination self-regulation failure is the source's research framing of procrastination as more than harmless delay. In [[wake-up-haul-an-ozempic-moment-for-the-brain-6a85798d1ad8716bb6078744]], [[BenjaminSutherland]] says procrastination can reduce income, delay medical treatment, increase pain risk, reduce self-esteem, and predict future depression.

The concept overlaps with [[PerfectionismAsAvoidance]] but is not the same. The earlier perfectionism page treats delay as a defense against visible failure; this source treats delay as a broader self-control and executive-function problem affected by working from home, smartphones, sleep loss, planning methods, exercise, diet correlations, and medication-adjacent ADHD evidence.

## Key Claims
- The source cites a 2013 study linking each one-point procrastination-scale increase with a $15,000 annual-earnings drop.
- It cites a 2023 study connecting frequent procrastination with delayed medical treatment and debilitating pain.
- It cites an 88-study analysis linking procrastination to lower self-esteem and future depression.
- Working from home, smartphones, and delayed bedtime are presented as plausible modern contributors.
- [[MentalContrastingImplementationIntentions]] and [[ExerciseSelfMasteryForProcrastination]] are stronger action candidates than diet correlations in this segment.
