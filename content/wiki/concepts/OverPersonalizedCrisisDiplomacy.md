# Over-Personalized Crisis Diplomacy

Over-personalized crisis diplomacy is the risk that international attention to one famous figure displaces attention to a broader conflict. [[the-case-of-the-missing-totem-aung-san-suu-kyi-6a560fc4f96471dde4821d2f]] adds it through [[Myanmar]], where proof of life for [[AungSanSuuKyi]] matters but cannot represent the suffering of the country's wider population.

The source's warning is not that individual advocacy is wrong. [[ProofOfLifeDiplomacy]] can be a legitimate humanitarian demand. The problem arises when the external narrative treats a single flawed figure as the whole crisis, leaving war, mass displacement, deaths, and ordinary civilian suffering less visible.

## Key Claims
- Famous detainees can become useful focal points because they make repression legible to outsiders.
- The same focal point can distort priorities if diplomats treat access or release as a substitute for wider settlement or accountability.
- A figure's flawed record should neither erase their detention conditions nor become the only moral frame for the crisis.
- The discipline is scale: keep individual verification, regime legitimacy, opposition dynamics, and population-level harm connected.
