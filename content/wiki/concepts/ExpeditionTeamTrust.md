# Expedition Team Trust

## Definition
Expedition team trust is the source's leadership frame for turning a high-skill group into a team that can operate under fatigue, danger, uncertainty, and moral pressure through generosity, communication, empathy, belonging, and diversity of perspectives.

## Current Synthesis
In [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757]], [[AraceliSegarra]] insists that a group of strong climbers is not automatically a team. [[DavidBreshears]]' selection logic matters because the [[EverestIMAXExpedition]] needed people who could film, climb, share resources, speak under pressure, recover from exhaustion, and redirect the mission when rescue became more important than summit ambition.

The concept translates the Everest case into organizational terms without losing the mountain constraints. Trust is not softness; it is the operating condition that lets people report needs, make mistakes, repair quickly, and combine different backgrounds when the environment is changing faster than the plan.

## Key Claims
- A team is built, not assembled by collecting high-performing individuals.
- Generosity, communication, empathy, and attention to others' needs become operational capabilities under stress.
- Diversity matters because difficult problems on a mountain are solved by perspectives, not only muscle.
- Psychological safety is practical when people must speak, admit mistakes, and repair quickly.
- Shared belonging can make people redirect resources toward rescue or humanity when the original mission changes.

## Evidence
- Group versus team: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757]] has Segarra say a group of people is not automatically a team.
- Selection logic: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757]] says Breshears wanted people able to work together at the highest level, not only the best climbers.
- Diversity and gender equity: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757]] describes intentional gender equity and a team spread across countries, ages, backgrounds, and production roles.
- Safety and rescue: [[default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757-default-mp3-ywr3ahjkcgo-842831df1049b1024b0dfd8bf11ec723-108598757]] links safe teams to speaking, mistakes, quick fixes, and the IMAX team's willingness to risk its summit for humanity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not offer a complete team-science model. It gives a mountaineering case where trust, diversity, and safety were visible under extreme conditions; ordinary organizations still need role clarity, decision rights, and performance standards in addition to belonging.

## What Changed
- Initial concept created from the Everest IMAX team's selection, diversity, safety, and rescue behavior.
