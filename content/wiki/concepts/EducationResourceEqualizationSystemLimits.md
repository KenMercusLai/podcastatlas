# Education Resource Equalization System Limits / 教育资源均衡的系统边界

## Definition
Education resource equalization system limits are the constraints that prevent personnel movement, school branding, or tutoring restriction alone from equalizing opportunity when finance, staffing by subject and employment status, teaching teams, student composition, family resources, materials, routines, incentives, and long-term institutional capacity remain uneven.

## Current Synthesis
[[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] supports the fairness goal of teacher and principal rotation but treats teaching quality as institutional rather than portable in one person. A rotating teacher enters an established timetable, preparation system, curriculum archive, peer group, student body, and family environment while bearing commute and adaptation costs. The same systems view explains how overall hiring competition can coexist with severe subject shortages: supply can be abundant in aggregate and mismatched by discipline, place, and timing.

Together, the sources extend the system boundary to district finance, school-group branches, employment status, and family purchasing power. A famous school name does not ensure that a branch has the original campus's accumulated staff and practice; low-paid substitute turnover can weaken continuity while permanent teachers carry excess classes; and families with money may purchase private alternatives when lotteries or tutoring restrictions narrow visible routes. Equalization therefore requires durable local capacity and distributional evidence, not only formal access to a brand or rule.

## Key Claims
- Teacher quality matters, but it is embedded in school routines, materials, teams, student composition, and family context.
- Rotation can spread experience while also imposing commute, adaptation, and continuity costs.
- Incentives, professional advancement, and time horizons shape whether rotating staff invest in long-term local capacity.
- Aggregate teacher competition can coexist with subject-specific or regional scarcity, so changing student demand requires matching workforce planning rather than only higher recruitment standards.
- School-group branding may widen formal access without reproducing the original campus's team, practice, or accumulated capacity.
- Employment conditions and substitute turnover affect whether staffing produces continuity rather than nominal headcount.
- Equity reform should track institutional capacity, household substitution, and student opportunity, not only personnel movement or formal school assignment.

## Evidence
- Rotation boundary: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] links school quality to student intake, family investment, teaching systems, materials, and accumulated practice, while noting commute and adaptation costs.
- Incentive and continuity: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] asks whether short-term rotating teachers will invest in local resources without title, compensation, and continuity support.
- Subject mismatch: [[ep188-jiaoshijie-duihua-zuotijia-yangchengshi-dangdai-zuotijie-xianzhuang-dayi-ckwriasfcgevabaaaadf6hi3|EP188]] describes increased geography selection producing overloaded teachers and early recruitment even as other teaching posts attract elite-degree applicants.
- District and school-group capacity: [[ep180-feidianxing-beijing-mama-de-foxi-jiwa-women-jiujing-xiang-peiyang-zenyang-de-haizi-ckwridoewbb3abaaaadgpbgf|EP180]] links uneven provision to district finance and distinguishes branches that share staff or practice from those relying mainly on a famous name.
- Staffing continuity: [[ep180-feidianxing-beijing-mama-de-foxi-jiwa-women-jiujing-xiang-peiyang-zenyang-de-haizi-ckwridoewbb3abaaaadgpbgf|EP180]] describes permanent teachers carrying excess classes while low-paid substitutes turn over frequently in one local setting.
- Household substitution: [[ep180-feidianxing-beijing-mama-de-foxi-jiwa-women-jiujing-xiang-peiyang-zenyang-de-haizi-ckwridoewbb3abaaaadgpbgf|EP180]] argues that families with greater means can pursue private branches or hidden tutoring when lottery or training rules limit standard routes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not evaluate completed equalization programs or provide comparative outcome data, and their staffing examples come from two teachers' environments. Rotation, group schools, lotteries, and tutoring restrictions may still improve access, professional exchange, or family wellbeing. Branch quality, substitute pay, turnover, district differences, private-school access, and tutoring substitution remain source-scoped. The concept sets an implementation test: formal access should be evaluated together with incentives, finance, team capacity, subject demand, employment quality, household substitution, continuity, and student outcomes.

## What Changed
- Expanded the system boundary from rotation and subject mismatch to district finance, branch-campus capacity, employment quality, staffing continuity, and household substitution.
