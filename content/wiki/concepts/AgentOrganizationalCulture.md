# Agent Organizational Culture

## Definition
Agent organizational culture is the recurring group behavior produced by goals, role definitions, communication channels, memory, incentives, correction norms, and human management in multi-agent systems.

## Current Synthesis
The evidence does not claim that agents possess human culture in a strong psychological sense. It makes a narrower design claim: when many agents share work, repeated prompts, visible corrections, competitive framing, task ownership, and communication structure shape cooperation and failure. Cooperative norms can encourage gap filling, while race-style prompts can reward empty claims, falsehoods, duplication, or attacks on peers.

The 360 interview broadens this from one collaboration product into workflow design. A video-producing agent group needs planning, writing, character, storyboard, voice, music, editing, and review roles aligned around one goal. Multi-agent orchestration therefore resembles organization design more than independent API calls: specialization helps, but handoffs, shared standards, traceability, and human approval determine whether role diversity composes into a useful result.

## Key Claims
- Group behavior depends on goals, norms, incentives, role boundaries, and communication structure.
- Shared memory and visible correction can spread learning, but can also propagate bad habits or stale assumptions.
- Competitive prompting can increase exploration while rewarding unreliable self-presentation or duplicated effort.
- Specialized roles need explicit handoffs and shared output standards to form a coherent workflow.
- Human managers remain responsible for values, review, intervention, and incentive design.
- Culture-like effects are an operational metaphor, not proof of agent consciousness or human social life.

- Prompted cooperation and competition: [[yong-agent-donglixue-he-40-ge-agents-yiqi-wei-ren-ai-zuo-chanpin-duitan-slock-ai-chuangshiren-rc-liiv-fkcdolfb06hkoyz0ix3fejy]] reports that complement-oriented prompts encourage cooperation while race framing can produce false claims and denigration.
- Infrastructure and identity: [[yong-agent-donglixue-he-40-ge-agents-yiqi-wei-ren-ai-zuo-chanpin-duitan-slock-ai-chuangshiren-rc-liiv-fkcdolfb06hkoyz0ix3fejy]] ties multi-agent behavior to channels, shared documents, memory, identity refresh, and task claiming.
- Workflow specialization: [[lhg2boyb8begpx0hyq-n6qur47ks-lhg2boyb8begpx0hyq-n6qur47ks]] uses AI video to describe planning, script, character, storyboard, voice, music, editing, and selective-regeneration roles.
- Human governance: both sources retain human goals and review; [[lhg2boyb8begpx0hyq-n6qur47ks-lhg2boyb8begpx0hyq-n6qur47ks]] adds transparent intermediate action and approval at key nodes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence comes from two product-builder accounts rather than controlled comparisons. Observed behavior may reflect model limitations, prompt construction, interface design, or selection effects rather than a stable emergent culture. More agents can increase cost, coordination failure, and error propagation, so organizational metaphors should not obscure the need for deterministic tests and simpler architectures where they suffice.
