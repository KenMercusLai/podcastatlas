# Process Knowledge / 过程知识

Process knowledge is the hard-to-codify know-how that comes from building, operating, debugging, repairing, and repeating real technical systems. In [[150-zhongguo-ruhe-tiaochu-pinkun-xianjing-xiandaihua-zhi-lu-you-hui-zouxiang-hefang-lgpvp04rngagkpubte2ac6i5ji3g]], [[DanWang]]'s [[Breakneck]] helps the episode argue that engineering strength is not only drawings, formulas, or tools; it is the memory embedded in workers, suppliers, factories, sites, and practice communities.

The source uses [[Shenzhen]], construction craft, aircraft, chips, and nuclear safety examples to show that losing process knowledge can be more serious than losing a visible product design. This extends [[EngineeringState]] from "can build fast" into "can retain and reproduce tacit industrial memory."

[[ep255-chang-erdai-de-jicheng-zhi-zhan-nianqingren-jieban-hui-chongsu-qiye-ma-likvzmpgwxnetpaezxclsx4s-hkw]] adds a management-side extension through [[TacitManagementKnowledge|默会知识]]. The [[FactorySecondGeneration|厂二代]] problem is partly that successors may have formal education and international exposure while lacking the practice-based judgment founders built from suppliers, factories, dealers, employees, and customers.

## Key Claims
- Technical capability includes tacit routines that are difficult to write down completely.
- Manufacturing and infrastructure ecosystems preserve knowledge through repeated practice and dense interaction.
- A society can lose capability if it stops building, outsources too much, or lets experienced communities disappear.
- Process knowledge is an advantage for [[China]] but not sufficient without service, culture, and feedback institutions.
- Family-business succession shows a managerial version of process knowledge: authority depends on repeated situated judgment, not only strategy language or credentials.
