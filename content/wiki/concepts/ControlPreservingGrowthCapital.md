## Definition
Control-preserving growth capital is outside funding or partial liquidity that gives a founder scale resources, expertise, or risk relief while leaving the founder with enough ownership and authority to keep directing the company.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded evidence now shows both the promise and the failure mode of minority growth capital. [[KimVaccarella]] rejected a majority acquisition offer, then accepted a 40% investor stake that the source says preserved control while adding experienced investors for [[BoggBag|Bogg Bag]]'s next scale stage. [[RossAndrewPaquette]] accepted investors for roughly 25% of [[Maropost]] despite not needing operating cash because he expected professionalization help; conflict later led him to repurchase the position for about $37 million. Formal majority control therefore does not by itself ensure strategic alignment, useful assistance, or low governance friction.

## Key Claims
- A founder may rationally reject a larger immediate payout if the transaction requires surrendering control before the founder believes the company is finished.
- Partial investment can be attractive when it pairs capital with operating expertise and preserves majority authority.
- Control-preserving capital still changes the company because hiring, titles, systems, and investor expectations enter the operating model.
- The right capital structure depends on founder goals, company stage, investor fit, and the cost of scaling without support.
- Minority ownership can still create major operating and strategic conflict; diligence must test the investor's usable capabilities, rights, expectations, and behavior under disagreement.

## Evidence
- **Rejected majority sale:** [[bogg-bag-kim-vaccarella-the-100-million-business-she-almost-abandoned-e1aadd32-1dc4-43fb-b654-32148b3f6cee]] says Kim turned down a majority acquisition offer that she believed would have paid her more than $100 million.
- **Investor-fit search:** [[bogg-bag-kim-vaccarella-the-100-million-business-she-almost-abandoned-e1aadd32-1dc4-43fb-b654-32148b3f6cee]] says she later connected with investors including Andrew Rosen, Hussein, and Lou Frankfurt.
- **Minority/control balance:** [[bogg-bag-kim-vaccarella-the-100-million-business-she-almost-abandoned-e1aadd32-1dc4-43fb-b654-32148b3f6cee]] says the investor group bought 40%, allowing Kim to maintain control.
- **Operating consequence:** [[bogg-bag-kim-vaccarella-the-100-million-business-she-almost-abandoned-e1aadd32-1dc4-43fb-b654-32148b3f6cee]] says the company then grew from about five or six employees to close to 100, creating new management challenges.
- **Minority-capital failure mode:** [[bff406f4]] says Maropost sold investors roughly 25% despite not needing operating cash, expected help professionalizing the company, and found the relationship increasingly exhausting.
- **Control restoration:** [[bff406f4]] says Ross later bought the investors out for approximately $37 million, restoring concentrated ownership at substantial cost.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source discloses full transaction documents, board rights, preferences, or governance controls. Control preservation is therefore based on founder accounts of retained ownership and authority. Bogg Bag does not yet establish that its investor relationship will remain aligned over time, while Maropost shows that preserving a majority stake cannot substitute for reference checking, explicit role definition, or verified operating value from investors.

## What Changed
- Added Maropost as a counterexample where minority investment preserved formal founder control but failed the expected professionalization and alignment test.
- Distinguished ownership percentage from practical governance friction and investor usefulness.
