# Cat Religious Mythology

Cat religious mythology is [[34-maomi-mishi-he-yuchun-you-keai-de-renlei-601590607]]'s cross-cultural frame for treating [[Cat|猫]] as sacred protector, soul vessel, divine companion, or magical threshold animal. The episode's strongest case is ancient Egypt, where [[Bastet]], cat amulets, household protection, and legal reverence turn cats into religious and civic beings.

The source then moves through Islamic purity stories, Indian and Southeast Asian soul beliefs, Burmese and Siamese cat legends, Buddhist library-cat tensions, Japanese courtly cats, and northern or Mediterranean goddess associations. These examples do not prove one universal cat cult; they show how feline behavior, night vision, hunting, independence, and household closeness repeatedly become religious material.

## Key Claims
- Cat mythology often begins from practical protection but becomes a story about divine or spiritual protection.
- The same cat traits can support opposite religious readings: sacred guardian, soul container, witch familiar, or demonic sign.
- Soul-container and temple-cat legends extend [[HumanAnimalContinuityInMyth]] because cat and human status can become porous after death.
- The cross-cultural pattern belongs near [[StoryMotifTransmission]] and [[MythAsHistoricalEvidence]]: resemblance opens comparison, not proof of one origin.
