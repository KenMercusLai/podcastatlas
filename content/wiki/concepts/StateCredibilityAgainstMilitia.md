# State Credibility Against Militia

State credibility against militia is the strategy in [[hard-cedar-lebanon-seeks-trumps-help-6a5f41e97bddc0bea5542de5]] of reducing an armed group's support by making the state visibly more protective and useful. The source case is [[Lebanon]], where [[JosephAoun]]'s government wants to show Shia citizens that [[Hezbollah]] dragged the country into war on [[Iran]]'s behalf while the Lebanese state can secure and rebuild the south.

The concept matters because disarmament is not only a military question. If the state cannot provide security, reconstruction, bank reform, and services, Hezbollah remains a social and political provider even after war damage.

## Key Claims
- A state can weaken a militia only if citizens see a better source of protection and recovery.
- Foreign military aid is not enough when the state lacks money to rebuild, pay forces, and restore trust.
- The strategy depends on sequencing: ceasefire credibility, army capacity, reconstruction finance, and banking reform have to reinforce one another.
