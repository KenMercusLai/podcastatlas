# Advanced Market Commitment

An advanced market commitment is a demand-creation mechanism where buyers commit purchasing power before a market is mature enough to support ordinary scale. [[peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted-peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted]] adds the concept through [[FrontierCarbonRemoval|Frontier Carbon Removal]], which [[PeterReinhardt]] describes as a [[Stripe]]-organized pool of about $1 billion for carbon-removal purchases.

The source uses the mechanism to explain why early [[CarbonRemoval]] companies can raise and build despite high costs. If buyers credibly commit to future purchases, suppliers such as [[CharmIndustrial|Charm Industrial]] can finance learning, equipment, permitting, and deployment before the [[CarbonRemovalCostCurve]] has fallen enough for broad demand.

## Key Claims
- Advanced market commitments reduce demand uncertainty for technologies that are socially valuable but not yet cheap.
- The mechanism is most useful when the buyer wants learning and market formation, not only the cheapest current unit.
- Commitments do not remove the need for verification, delivery, permanence, or cost reduction.
- In carbon removal, advanced commitments connect [[ProductLedWillingnessToPay]] to [[ClimateStartupCommercializationGap]] because customers pay partly to pull future supply into existence.
