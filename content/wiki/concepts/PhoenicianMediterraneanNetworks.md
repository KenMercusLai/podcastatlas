# Phoenician Mediterranean Networks / 腓尼基地中海网络

## Definition

Phoenician Mediterranean networks are the overlapping routes, skills, settlements, trading posts, goods, religious ties, and writing practices through which Levantine city communities helped connect the Mediterranean.

## Current Synthesis

The episode presents maritime reach as the product of city geography, shipbuilding, navigation, specialized production, and exchange rather than a single centrally administered empire. [[Tyre]] and neighboring cities processed materials and supplied high-value goods; merchants sought metals and other commodities; and settlements from North Africa to Iberia supported movement, storage, market access, and local relationships.

[[Carthage]] began within this field and later became its strongest western center. Network language matters because posts, colonies, kinship, commercial influence, and direct territorial rule were not identical. It also constrains sensational diffusion claims: the episode accepts extensive Mediterranean and Atlantic-facing navigation while rejecting North American claims and treating Cornwall and route details as unproven.

## Key Claims

- Maritime specialization grew from geographic constraint, defensible harbors, and regional opportunity.
- Ships, navigation, night sailing, production, and market knowledge worked as a combined capability.
- Colonies and posts connected the Levant, North Africa, Sicily, Sardinia, Iberia, and the Strait of Gibraltar.
- Metals, timber, purple dye, luxury goods, and other commodities sustained the network.
- Settlements varied from exchange nodes to colonies and later territorial possessions.
- Extensive reach does not validate every ancient or modern voyage claim.

## Evidence

- Eastern foundations: [[421-ancient-carthage-lords-of-the-sea-part-1-glt1311066631]] links Tyre's site, Bronze Age survival, ship design, and production to maritime expansion.
- Western reach: [[421-ancient-carthage-lords-of-the-sea-part-1-glt1311066631]] traces posts and colonies through Carthage, Sardinia, Sicily, southern Spain, and beyond Gibraltar.
- Navigational reputation: [[421-ancient-carthage-lords-of-the-sea-part-1-glt1311066631]] describes fast bitumen-coated ships, Pole Star navigation, Herodotus's Africa story, and the later Hanno and Himilco traditions.
- Evidential limit: [[421-ancient-carthage-lords-of-the-sea-part-1-glt1311066631]] rejects supposed North American evidence and finds no secure archaeological or explicit written proof for Cornwall.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

“Network” should not romanticize commerce or erase coercion, settlement conflict, local agency, or later Carthaginian annexation. Ancient voyage reports, the location of the Cassiterides, Africa circumnavigation, Hanno's route, ship performance, settlement dates, and the division between trade and political control remain source-scoped or contested.

## What Changed

- Established a network model separating maritime connection from uniform empire.
- Added ship technology, navigation, production, colonies, and evidential restraint as interacting parts.
- Positioned Carthage as a later western center rather than the origin of the whole system.
