# Federal Data Practice Trust

Federal data practice trust is the public belief that federal agencies collect, use, share, and retain personal data in legitimate, accountable ways. [[tech-20260409-0409-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260409-0409-mp-tech-pod-128]] introduces the concept through [[ElizabethLaird]] of the [[CenterForDemocracyAndTechnology|Center for Democracy and Technology]], whose polling discussion says concern about what the federal government knows is widespread across demographic and political groups.

The concept differs from narrow data-security fear. The episode frames the problem as a mix of concern, helplessness, and unclear recourse: people may believe the government has substantial information about them, but also believe they cannot control or correct how it is used. That turns privacy into an institutional-trust problem linked to [[GovernmentDataAccountability]].

## Key Claims
- Public concern can remain high even when people are used to breach notices and routine data exposure.
- Trust depends on use and sharing rules, not only on whether data is initially collected.
- Helplessness can intensify backlash because people may see data systems as powerful but unaccountable.
- Trust is operational: it can affect whether people use public services, not only how they answer surveys.
