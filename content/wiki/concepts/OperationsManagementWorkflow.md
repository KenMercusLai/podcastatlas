# Operations Management Workflow

## Definition
Operations management workflow is the practice of treating organizations and personal work as designed processes that transform inputs into finished outputs under constraints of cost, speed, quality, and variety.

## Current Synthesis
The EP86 Mini MBA episode makes operations management a bridge between factory logic and personal productivity. The host's process view says a company is not just a set of departments; it is a dynamic flow that turns resources into products, services, and customer satisfaction. The same lens is applied to individual work, where output depends less on a declared goal than on how tasks, attention, information, and recovery move through a system.

The episode's strongest contribution is integration. Bottlenecks, WIP, queueing slack, critical paths, information amplification, TPS waste removal, agile sprinting, and MVP validation are treated as one operating vocabulary rather than separate management-school terms.

## Key Claims
- Workflow design explains stable output better than moralizing about discipline or effort.
- Cost, response time, quality, and variety create tradeoffs for both companies and individuals.
- Local efficiency can harm total output when it ignores bottlenecks, WIP, queues, and feedback delays.
- Personal core competence can be understood as a long-optimized process matched to a specific demand.
- Operations tools become more useful when they diagnose the current system before prescribing habits.

## Evidence
- Process-view evidence: [[ep86-dianfu-gongzuoliu-xiaolu-tisheng-3000-gkwrimaoad7vbjtrsqtotc4k]] defines enterprises and individuals through input-output flows rather than static roles or ability labels.
- Tradeoff evidence: [[ep86-dianfu-gongzuoliu-xiaolu-tisheng-3000-gkwrimaoad7vbjtrsqtotc4k]] uses cost, response time, quality, and variety to explain why every system needs choices.
- Integration evidence: [[ep86-dianfu-gongzuoliu-xiaolu-tisheng-3000-gkwrimaoad7vbjtrsqtotc4k]] connects constraints, WIP, queues, critical paths, bullwhip effects, TPS, Scrum, and Lean Startup into one workflow toolkit.
- Personal-system evidence: [[ep86-dianfu-gongzuoliu-xiaolu-tisheng-3000-gkwrimaoad7vbjtrsqtotc4k]] applies operations language to writing, reading, learning, schedule design, boss decisions, and project review.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The episode is a practitioner synthesis, not an empirical productivity trial. Its "3000%" title should be treated as promotional framing, while the operational concepts remain useful as diagnostic metaphors and management tools.
