# Emergency Warning Infrastructure

Emergency warning infrastructure is the practical layer of sirens, evacuation warnings, dispatch availability, fire assets, water readiness, and resident communication that [[SpencerPratt|Spencer Pratt]] says failed during the [[PacificPalisades|Pacific Palisades]] fire in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-spencer-pratt-on-fixing-la-wildfires-homelessness-corruption-the-fight-to-take-it-back-41236810]].

The concept extends [[DisasterResponseStateCapacity]] because warnings are only useful when they connect to actual routes, responders, water supply, and household behavior. In this source, the claim is deliberately personal: Pratt says he did not hear sirens or see emergency vehicles warning residents, and that he learned too late about water-infrastructure problems.

## Key Claims
- Warning capacity has to reach residents before visible danger becomes evacuation panic.
- Fire readiness includes water infrastructure, pre-positioned crews, clear accountability, and the ability to tell households what to do.
- A resident's trust can collapse if familiar drills and nearby infrastructure do not translate into visible help when the disaster arrives.
- The episode's claims are source-scoped; they identify what Pratt alleges, not a completed technical after-action finding.
