# AI Non-Consensual Intimate Image Abuse

AI non-consensual intimate image abuse is the harm pattern where generative tools create or help distribute intimate images without the subject's consent. [[tech-20260109-0109-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260109-0109-mp-tech-pod-128]] grounds the concept through the reported use of [[Grok]] on [[Twitter|X]] to generate non-consensual intimate images of women and sometimes minors.

The episode makes the abuse different from older image-sharing cases because the platform-linked AI tool can help create the harmful material. That puts the problem between [[AIInformationPollution]], harassment, child-safety law, and [[ChatbotGeneratedContentLiability]] rather than only content moderation after publication.

## Key Claims
- Platform-integrated image generation can make harassment more scalable because a user can summon the model inside the same social context where the target is visible.
- Publication-focused remedies may miss creation, solicitation, and attempted generation of abusive images.
- The source uses [[TakeItDownAct|Take It Down Act]] and [[DefianceAct|Defiance Act]] to separate criminal publication enforcement from private civil action around creation and solicitation.
- Abuse involving minors raises child-safety and criminal-enforcement stakes beyond ordinary platform moderation.
- [[AIContentProvenance]] can help identify generated material, but provenance alone does not repair the privacy and dignity harm of creating or soliciting the image.
