# Mausoleum Secrecy Killing / 陵墓保密杀戮

Mausoleum secrecy killing / 陵墓保密杀戮 is the pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-4-qinshihuangling-de-jingtian-mimi-lhgwr2of7zcmg2ggbcdzragwstyi|Qinji 127-4]] draws from [[QinShiHuangMausoleum|秦始皇陵]]. The source describes the tomb not only as monumental architecture but as an information-control system: treasures, mechanisms, and burial arrangements become secrets protected by killing or entombing people who know too much.

The episode's two clearest cases are childless concubines and artisans. Concubines who have not borne sons are forced into the tomb, while artisans who made the mechanisms are also sealed in because they might reveal the tomb's internal design. The violence is therefore different from battlefield killing: it protects dynastic spectacle, buried wealth, and technical secrecy after the ruler is already dead.

This concept extends [[ImperialItinerarySecrecy|帝王行踪保密]] into the funerary realm. Itinerary secrecy hides where the living emperor is; mausoleum secrecy killing hides how the dead emperor's tomb works. Both make knowledge of imperial space dangerous, but the tomb version turns workers and palace women into disposable security risks.

## Key Claims
- A mausoleum can be a secrecy regime as well as a burial site.
- Technical knowledge can become a death sentence when artisans are treated as leak risks.
- Palace women can be converted into funerary property when succession or reproduction status leaves them unprotected.
- Tomb secrecy belongs near [[ImperialConstructionOverload|帝国工程过载]] because the same monumental project that consumes labor can also consume lives for secrecy.
