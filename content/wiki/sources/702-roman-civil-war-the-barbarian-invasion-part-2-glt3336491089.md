# 702. Roman Civil War: The Barbarian Invasion (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode continues the Roman Civil War sequence from [[701-roman-civil-war-rise-of-the-general-part-1-glt1262857909|Part 1]] by moving [[GaiusMarius]] from promising outsider to emergency savior of the [[RomanRepublic|Roman Republic]]. It follows his repeated consulships, disciplined campaign against the [[TeutonesAndCimbri]], and later entanglement in Italian citizenship politics, the [[SocialWar]], and the struggle over command against [[MithridatesVI]].

The episode's strongest wiki contribution is to turn foreshadowed [[LateRomanRepublicCrisis]] into a direct command crisis. [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]] emerges from Marius's former subordinate into a rival commander whose Social War prestige, consulship, eastern command, and humiliation by [[PubliusSulpiciusRufus]] and Marius bring [[RepublicanCommandTransferCrisis]] to the edge of armed rupture.

## Key Claims

- Rome is presented as a chaotic republican city whose disorder was interpreted by Romans as liberty, even as crowd politics, public insult, voting, and ambition made the city volatile.
- [[GaiusMarius]] becomes indispensable after Arausio and the terror Cimbricus because even enemies accept that his military competence is needed against the [[TeutonesAndCimbri]].
- Marius's repeated consulships during the northern crisis stretch republican tradition and show emergency prestige overriding normal office constraints.
- Marius defeats the Teutones near Aix-en-Provence and the Cimbri in northern Italy by discipline, fortified camps, refusal of theatrical challenges, and separate engagement.
- The source treats Marius's military method as practical and disciplined, while keeping the "military revolution" label contested.
- The [[RomanAlliedCitizenshipCrisis]] sharpens through Italian wealth, provincial profit, second-class legal status, Marius's selective citizenship grant, and [[MarcusLiviusDrusus]]' failed reform.
- [[SocialWar]] turns the citizenship dispute into armed revolt across Asculum, Campania, Nola, Pompeii, and Samnium.
- [[LuciusCorneliusSulla]] emerges from the Social War as the standout Roman commander, winning the 88 BC consulship and the command against [[MithridatesVI]].
- [[ManiusAquillius]]'s actions help provoke the eastern crisis, while Mithridates's invasion of Asia exposes how much Greek communities resent Roman and Italian exploitation.
- [[PubliusSulpiciusRufus]] and Marius trade support: Italian citizenship politics becomes the vehicle for transferring the Mithridatic command from Sulla to Marius.
- Street violence in Rome, the killing of Pompeius Rufus's son, and Sulla's forced refuge in Marius's house show [[PopularisPolitics]] crossing into coercive urban power.
- The episode ends when a plebiscite removes Sulla from the eastern command and sends a messenger to take over his six legions, making the army-command question immediate.

## Key Quotes

> "terror Cimbricus" - Roman memory label for panic after the northern defeats.

> "military revolution" - a debated label for Marius's army changes and methods.

> "favored by Venus" - the source's shorthand for Sulla's self-image around luck and divine favor.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction found. The source resolves Part 1's source-scope caution by narrating Marius's northern victories, Social War role, and Sulla's rise to the eastern command.
- The episode qualifies the earlier crisis pages by showing that the Roman breakdown is not only military personalization: Italian citizenship exclusion, provincial extraction, street organization, and plebiscitary command transfer all help produce the rupture.
- Source-scope caution: casualty figures for the Teutones and Cimbri are presented as enormous but likely exaggerated, and the "barbarian invasion" label remains a Roman/narrative framing rather than neutral ethnography.
- Source-scope caution: the episode stops at the moment Sulla's command is taken; Sulla's march on Rome remains the next unresolved threshold rather than an event narrated here.
