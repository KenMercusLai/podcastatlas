## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three cases where formal openings do not remove deeper power constraints. [[TomGardner]] reports that [[Sudan]]'s war remains a foreign-backed civil conflict even after the [[SudaneseArmedForces|SAF]] retook [[Khartoum]], because the [[RapidSupportForces|RSF]] still controls much of [[Darfur]], drone attacks continue around [[ElObeid]], and outside sponsors have strategic interests in Sudan's coastline, gold, land, and political future. [[AnnieCrabill]] then uses the [[GordieHoweBridge]] to show how mutually useful cross-border infrastructure can be caught in [[DonaldTrump]]'s tariff and ownership politics, while [[AnneRowe]] closes with [[BetyeSaar]]'s assemblage art as a way of making slavery, race, and liberation visible through discarded objects.

## Key Claims
- The episode says [[Sudan]]'s war is the world's worst humanitarian crisis, with millions displaced and possibly hundreds of thousands killed.
- [[TomGardner]] describes [[Khartoum]] after the army's return as devastated, quiet, dark, and methodically looted rather than recovered.
- The source says the [[SudaneseArmedForces|SAF]] controls Khartoum and much of eastern and northern Sudan, while the [[RapidSupportForces|RSF]] still controls much of western Sudan, especially [[Darfur]].
- Gardner says evidence points to significant [[UnitedArabEmirates|UAE]] backing for the RSF, while noting that the UAE denies this.
- The source describes the army as supported by [[SaudiArabia]], [[Egypt]], and especially [[Turkey]], whose drones are linked to recent battlefield gains.
- [[LowCostDroneWarfare]] appears as a countrywide safety problem: both sides have drone arsenals, and civilian infrastructure such as hospitals, markets, and gas stations has been hit.
- [[ElObeid]] is presented as a major hotspot; an RSF capture could reconnect western fronts and threaten [[Khartoum]] from the west again.
- Foreign backers are described as interested in Sudan's [[RedSea]] coastline, gold reserves, arable land, and future political alignment.
- The Sudan "quad" of the [[UnitedStates]], [[Egypt]], [[SaudiArabia]], and the [[UnitedArabEmirates]] is presented as a possible diplomatic channel, but not a guarantee of enforcement.
- [[FragmentedCeasefireEnforcement]] is the key implementation problem: both the SAF and RSF are coalitions whose components may not obey a single command.
- [[AnnieCrabill]] says the [[GordieHoweBridge]] connects [[Detroit]] and [[WindsorOntario|Windsor, Ontario]], after more than 14 years of development.
- The bridge is presented as commercially useful because the Detroit-Windsor crossing handles about $94bn in annual trade and gives trucks an alternative to the [[AmbassadorBridge]].
- The source says [[Canada]] paid the C$6bn construction cost upfront, while a later agreement in principle would route half of net profits into an American fund for 15 years.
- The bridge dispute is tied to broader [[TradeReciprocityProtectionism]] after Trump attacked the project, demanded a larger U.S. stake, and later announced new tariffs on Canadian goods.
- [[BetyeSaar]] is presented as an assemblage artist who gave new political force to discarded objects carrying traces of earlier owners, slavery, race, and constrained Black life.
- Saar's [[TheLiberationOfAuntJemima]] is described as a 1968 work that transformed a racist commercial figure by adding a rifle, a hand grenade, and a Black Power fist.
- The art segment links Saar's late work to journeys, captivity, and liberation through materials such as canoes, cages, branches, stones, and pleading hands.

## Key Quotes
