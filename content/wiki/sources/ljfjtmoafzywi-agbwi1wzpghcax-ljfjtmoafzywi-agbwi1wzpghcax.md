## Summary
This [[42Zhangjing|42章经]] conversation with [[HeJiachen|何嘉琛]] traces his path through [[AFFiNE]] and [[AFK]], then contrasts consumer personal agents with enterprise agents. Its central thesis is that an enterprise agent should learn across employees, software, and workflows while retaining person-specific memory and execution boundaries; repeated successful work may then be cached or distilled into cheaper task models instead of being rebuilt independently by every employee.

## Key Claims
- [[AFFiNE]] correctly anticipated a multimodal workspace, but He argues that fixed editors and canvases become constraints as agents gain autonomy across chat, collaboration software, phone, browser, CLI, MCP, and operating-system environments.
- [[AFK]] is presented as one highly concurrent company-wide agent with individual memory and runtime separation, not a collection of isolated personal agents.
- [[OrganizationalAgentLearning]] can reduce duplicate context loading, spread effective work methods, and turn distributed employee-Agent activity into shared operational learning.
- [[ModelDistilledTaskMemory]] separates expensive first-time reasoning from cheap repeated execution: frontier-model trajectories, tool use, results, and preferences can train a routing model that reproduces stable work.
- Enterprise adoption may be easier to monetize because management can connect permissions, token spend, labor cost, output, and business results, although the episode supplies no audited customer ROI or deployment data.
- [[AINativeRoleConvergence]] is illustrated by designers modifying real code and product managers specifying functional logic, while a shared codebase and live artifacts replace long handoffs among isolated role tools.
- The source expects fixed workflows, skills, prompts, and traditional software to lose value as models improve, but this is a strong forecast rather than established market evidence.
- Consumer personal agents face privacy, platform access, supply-chain, subsidy, and incumbent-retaliation problems when they attempt to become the user's primary transaction interface.
