# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》208｜以退为进，陈平的厚黑学（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s accession into the first reordering of the post-Lü court. [[ChenPing|陈平]] yields the senior chancellorship to [[ZhouBo|周勃]], explaining that Zhou's recent anti-Lü merit now exceeds his own; the host interprets the move as strategic risk transfer because the new emperor both needs and fears the veterans who installed him. The observable sequence supports deliberate status management, while the claim that Chen intended Zhou to enter danger in his place remains an inference.

The episode then shows Liu Heng resetting ruler-minister distance after [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] warns that excessive deference has made Zhou visibly proud, abolishing punishment of innocent relatives for another person's crime, and accepting the court's argument that his eldest eligible son [[HanJingdi|刘启 / later Emperor Jing]] should become crown prince. Together, these episodes connect coalition management, embodied hierarchy, individualized legal responsibility, and succession settlement in the opening of the Wen reign.

## Key Claims

- Chen Ping asks to rank below Zhou Bo before Liu Heng's accession ceremony, grounding the request in Zhou's greater merit in the destruction of the Lü clan.
- The host reads the concession as [[StrategicPositionYielding|strategic position yielding]]: Chen lowers his visibility and lets Zhou occupy the more exposed place between an insecure new ruler and accession-making veterans.
- Liu Heng appoints Zhou Bo right chancellor, Chen Ping left chancellor, and [[GuanYing|灌婴]] grand commandant while rewarding participants in the anti-Lü settlement.
- Yuan Ang distinguishes a meritorious minister from a minister of the altars of soil and grain, arguing that Liu Heng's deferential send-off has disrupted ruler-minister protocol and encouraged Zhou's pride.
- Liu Heng responds by adopting a more solemn audience posture; Zhou becomes apprehensive, while Yuan does not answer Zhou's later accusation that he spoke against him.
- Liu Heng abolishes rules that arrest, confiscate, or enslave innocent parents, spouses, siblings, and other relatives for an offender's crime.
- When officials request an early crown-prince designation, Liu Heng raises both abdication rhetoric and collateral royal candidates before accepting the dynastic-continuity and direct-line case for Liu Qi.
