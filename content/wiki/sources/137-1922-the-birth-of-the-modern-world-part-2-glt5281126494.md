# 137. 1922: The Birth of the Modern World Part 2

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode presents 1922 as a hinge between post-[[FirstWorldWar|First World War]] breakdown and recognizable modern life. It links American film and jazz, Tutankhamun news, radio and the [[BBC]], the Irish treaty split, Ottoman collapse, Greco-Turkish violence, dominion autonomy, and British party realignment rather than treating cultural modernity and geopolitical change as separate stories.

Its strongest synthesis is that mass media and nation-state formation moved together. Radio and Hollywood could create large shared audiences while imperial settlements in Ireland and the eastern Mediterranean produced new borders, civil conflict, population displacement, and more independent dominions; the episode closes by asking whether later platform fragmentation has ended the shared mass culture born in this period.

## Key Claims

- The [[UnitedStates]] could retreat from formal international leadership while gaining cultural power through Hollywood, jazz, animation, Chaplin, and rapidly expanding commercial radio.
- Tutankhamun's tomb became a worldwide news craze whose exclusivity, reporting competition, and invented curse story illustrate the new scale of mass-media attention.
- British radio moved from Marconi experiments and Peter Eckersley's Writtle broadcasts to the licensed [[BBC]] in 1922, contrasting with the more commercial and initially less regulated American system.
- The Washington Naval Treaty and postwar economy constrained the [[UnitedKingdom]] despite its large empire, while Canada's refusal to promise automatic support against Turkey exposed weakening imperial command.
- The Anglo-Irish Treaty created a workable but divisive settlement: dominion status, the royal oath, and partition split Irish nationalism and helped turn anti-British war into the Irish Civil War.
- [[MichaelCollinsIrishRevolutionary|Michael Collins]] moved from treaty negotiation to military action against the Four Courts occupiers before his death in an August 1922 ambush.
- Ottoman collapse replaced a multiethnic imperial order with mandates, nationalist armies, the destruction of Smyrna, displacement, and more ethnically defined states; [[MustafaKemalAtaturk|Mustafa Kemal]] was central to the Turkish victory.
- The Carlton Club decision and November election ended Lloyd George's coalition, weakened the divided Liberals, and established a Conservative-Labour axis in British national politics.
- The episode treats today's fragmented media and weakened cultural gatekeepers as a possible end point for the shared broadcast-and-studio culture that expanded in the 1920s.

## Key Quotes

> "return to normalcy" - Warren Harding's slogan for politically inward-looking postwar America.

> "Americanization of the world" - the episode's shorthand for the cultural reach of film and jazz.

> "How long did the modern world last?" - the closing question about broadcast culture, institutions, and fragmentation.
