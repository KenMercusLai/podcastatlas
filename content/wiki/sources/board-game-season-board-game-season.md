# Board Game Season

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode follows [[GeorgeRohrbacher]] and [[AnneRohrbacher]] from the 1970s back-to-the-land movement through drought, debt, and the American farm crisis. Their deliberately difficult [[TheFarmingGame|Farming Game]] turned lived agricultural risk into a product that helped preserve Breaks Ranch, taught farm economics, and gave distressed rural families a shared language for uncertainty.

## Key Claims

- Credit expansion, rising land values, strong grain demand, and pressure to scale encouraged farmers to take on obligations that became dangerous when drought, falling income, and high interest rates arrived together.
- [[GeorgeRohrbacher]] converted the family's near-failure into game mechanics involving inherited land, debt, harvest variability, operating costs, borrowing, bankruptcy, and off-farm work.
- [[GeorgeRohrbacher]] and [[AnneRohrbacher]] tested the game until its economics remained harsh without making success impossible, then financed an initial 10,000-copy production run by selling cattle and borrowing more.
- A reported Paul Harvey mention, agricultural advertising, farm shows, small-town stores, word of mouth, and direct phone orders helped the game reach its core rural audience.
- Educators, farm families, mental-health programs, and an agricultural advocacy group used the game to explain agricultural economics, discuss financial strain, and create relief through shared play.
- Game revenue allowed the family to retain Breaks Ranch and sustain [[WeekendFarmerCompany]], while the game remained in print and sold counter-cyclically during difficult periods.
- The episode uses farming to argue that preparation cannot remove chance and that companionship can create a meaningful form of success even when outcomes are unfavorable.
