## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[DavidBuss]] about human mating through evolutionary psychology. Buss separates long- and short-term strategies, mutual choice, mate value, deception, infidelity, jealousy, stalking, coercion, and intimate-partner violence while repeatedly qualifying population averages with overlap, cultural variation, changing evidence, and individual exceptions. The episode is most useful as a framework for competing mating interests and defensive behavior, not as a deterministic account of any person or as relationship or safety advice.

## Key Claims
- [[EvolutionaryMatingStrategies]] frames human mating as mutual preferential choice plus competition, with both sexes choosing and competing in long- and short-term contexts.
- [[MateValueAndMutualChoice]] treats desirability as multidimensional, socially comparative, partly self-monitored, and dependent on both broadly valued and person-specific traits.
- [[MateSwitchingHypothesis]] is Buss's preferred account of many women's affairs, replacing his earlier confidence in a dual-mating strategy after larger ovulation studies and reports of emotional involvement weakened that view.
- [[JealousyAndMateRetention]] treats jealousy as a pair-bond defense that can motivate reassurance and vigilance but can also escalate into surveillance, isolation, denigration, stalking, or violence.
- [[MatingConflictRisk]] joins predictable deception, sexual overperception, dark-triad traits, coercion, stalking, and intimate-partner violence while stressing that a minority of repeat perpetrators may account for much serious harm.
- Dating deception and app-era evaluation are easier when reputation and face-to-face cues are missing; Buss recommends moving from extended messaging to in-person evidence rather than relying on optimized profiles.
- Claims about sex differences are averages rather than rules, and the episode itself marks cultural variation, weak replication, measurement difficulty, and areas outside Buss's expertise.
