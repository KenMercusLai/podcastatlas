# 246. The Fall of Liz Truss

## Summary

Recorded immediately after [[LizTruss]] announced her resignation, this [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode interprets her 45-day premiership as an exceptional collapse of policy, authority, and party confidence rather than an ordinary short tenure caused by illness or a failed attempt to form a government. The hosts connect her fall to [[KwasiKwarteng]]'s mini-budget, [[PoliticalAuthorityCollapseCascade]], and [[PartyMembershipLeadershipSelection]], while treating the broader crisis as a product of both external shocks and Britain's unsettled post-[[Brexit]] governing strategy.

Historical comparisons with George Canning, Bonar Law, the Earl of Bath, Roman emperors, Australian leadership spills, Italy, interwar France, and 1970s Britain separate superficial tenure records from institutional causes. The episode also links Westminster instability to mortgages, business finance, energy costs, and [[EuropeanEnergySecurityDependence]], but its forecasts and personal judgments remain contemporary commentary recorded during the crisis.

## Key Claims

- Truss's 45-day premiership was historically unusual because political and policy failure, rather than illness or inability to form a ministry, destroyed her authority in public.
- Kwarteng's dismissal and Jeremy Hunt's reversal of the economic programme exposed a [[PoliticalAuthorityCollapseCascade]] in which sacrificing a close ally did not restore control.
- Opening leadership selection to party activists can produce a leader preferred by members but poorly aligned with MPs, voters, or governing constraints.
- Brexit's referendum victory did not supply an agreed governing programme: Johnson's levelling-up emphasis and Truss's deregulatory "Singapore on the Thames" approach represented competing projects.
- COVID-19 and Russia's invasion of [[Ukraine]] created severe external pressures, but those pressures do not erase domestic failures of policy design, conduct, communication, and political discipline.
- Political chaos has material consequences when fiscal credibility and expected interest-rate changes affect mortgages, households, and businesses.
- Europe's energy-price disadvantage could weaken industry relative to the United States, although the deindustrialization forecast is presented as a possibility rather than a demonstrated outcome.

## Key Quotes

> "Brexit without a plan" - the episode's shorthand for a referendum coalition that did not settle the governing model to follow.
