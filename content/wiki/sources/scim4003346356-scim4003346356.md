# Essentials: Healthy Eating & Eating Disorders - Anorexia, Bulimia, Binging

## Summary
This condensed [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] explain healthy and disordered eating through energy balance, appetite and satiety signaling, reward, habit, inhibitory control, perception, development, and social context. It reinforces [[EatingDisorderMultisystemModel]], [[AnorexiaHabitRewardDysregulation]], and [[BingePurgingImpulseControl]], while extending the clinical boundaries in [[AppetiteHormoneRegulation]], [[LossOfControlEating]], and [[CircadianEatingWindowAlignment]]. The release condenses [[scim4349715199-scim4349715199|the full 2021 episode]] and is overlapping provenance rather than independent confirmation.

## Key Claims
- Healthy eating has no single universal schedule: energy balance, metabolic and performance measures, mood, culture, family context, enjoyment, and individual needs all matter.
- [[AppetiteHormoneRegulation]] joins stomach distension, glucose and nutrient signals, gut-to-brain communication, hypothalamic POMC and AGRP neurons, and leptin without reducing eating disorders to one appetite defect.
- [[AnorexiaHabitRewardDysregulation]] frames restrictive food selection as a reinforced habit that can persist despite explicit knowledge of danger and can cause severe cardiovascular, skeletal, reproductive, gut, immune, and muscular harm.
- [[BingePurgingImpulseControl]] distinguishes bulimia's binge-purge cycle from binge eating without typical purging while linking both to loss of control, overridden satiety, reward, shame, and impaired inhibition.
- Cognitive and family-based approaches, medication, and behavioral care address different mechanisms and are presented as condition-specific clinical tools rather than self-directed protocols.
- Self-diagnosis is unreliable; concerning restriction, bingeing, purging, distorted self-perception, fainting, or other symptoms require qualified professional assessment.
