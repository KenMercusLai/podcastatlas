# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》173｜韩信逃过胯下之辱 没躲过鸟尽弓藏（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the question opened in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-173-hanxin-taoguo-kuaxia-zhiru-mei-duoguo-niaojin-gongcang-1-ljowpzxo9u0evorhzbdyi2o0shlx|Hanji 173 part 1]]: why [[HanXin|韩信]] did not break with [[LiuBang|刘邦]] when his military leverage was greatest. It then shifts from individual political judgment to the founding settlement. [[TianKenWesternHan|田肯]] praises Han Xin's arrest, describes [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] and [[QiState|齐国]] as strategic counterweights, and argues that Qi should go only to the emperor's son or brother. The host treats this advice as an early rationale for [[SameSurnameKingEnfeoffment|同姓王分封]] and the emerging division between Liu-family kings and meritorious marquises.

## Key Claims

- The host identifies two strong moments when Han Xin might have detached from Liu Bang: while Chu and Han were balanced at Honggou, and when [[ZhongliMo|钟离眜]] sought refuge in Chu.
- Han Xin is presented as a military genius with weaker political judgment, especially in assuming that past merit would continue to guarantee personal safety.
- After conquering Qi, Han Xin held enough leverage to aid Han, aid Chu, or attempt a third pole, but the source attributes his failure to choose partly to indecision.
- Tian Ken congratulates Liu Bang on both capturing Han Xin and establishing the capital in the Qin heartland, linking personnel control to defensible geography.
- Tian Ken describes Qi as an eastern counterpart to Qin because of its size, resources, defensibility, and mobilization potential.
- His recommendation that only a son or brother of the emperor should receive Qi turns strategic geography into a kinship criterion for royal appointment.
- The source interprets Tian Ken's surname and regional knowledge as possible evidence of descent from the Qi Tian lineage, but does not establish that genealogy.
- Han Xin's transfer from Qi to Chu and later removal show that territorial reassignment can reduce a meritorious commander's strategic base before formal elimination.
- The host extends the Qi rule to Chu and reads the ensuing settlement as a move toward Liu-family kingships alongside marquisates for meritorious officials.
- The closing “divide the cake” metaphor treats postwar allocation as a distinct political skill and contrasts Liu Bang's settlement challenge with [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s failed partition.

## Key Quotes

> “非亲子弟，莫可使王齐者。” - Tian Ken's kinship boundary for the Qi kingship as rendered by the episode.

> “东有琅邪、即墨之饶，南有泰山之固。” - the episode's geographic case for Qi's strategic weight.

> “分蛋糕是一门艺术。” - the host's summary of postwar allocation as a political problem distinct from conquest.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found.
- The claim that Han Xin had exactly two optimal rebellion opportunities and failed because of political insensitivity is the host's interpretation; gratitude, risk, uncertainty, and the existing [[TripartiteBalanceStrategy|三分天下式制衡]] evidence prevent a single-cause conclusion.
- Tian Ken's descent from the Qi Tian lineage is an inference from surname and regional knowledge, not an established genealogy in the supplied source.
- The episode calls Tian Ken's advice the starting point of the “非刘氏不王，非功臣不侯” pattern, but the broader rule emerged through a sequence of settlements and later enforcement; this source supports an early rationale rather than sole institutional authorship.
