# Science & Tools of Learning & Memory | Dr. David Eagleman

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[DavidEagleman]] about experience-dependent brain change, skill learning, self-control, mental imagery, subjective time, sensory substitution, dreaming, memory reliability, and group identity. Its central synthesis is that [[Neuroplasticity]] is selective and goal-directed rather than unlimited: novelty, attention, reachable difficulty, repetition, and recovery can reshape useful circuits, but the same adaptive machinery also supports addiction, distorted memory, and group-conditioned response. The episode adds [[UlyssesContract]], [[SensorySubstitution]], [[MentalImagerySpectrum]], and [[GroupIdentityEmpathyGating]] while extending [[ReconstructiveMemory]], [[MemoryContaminationRisk]], [[DreamFunctionAndMeaning]], [[TimePerceptionAndNeurochemicalState]], and [[GroupPolarization]].

## Key Claims
- Adult [[Neuroplasticity]] is presented as selective circuit change shaped by input, attention, novelty, practice, and goals; total infant-like flexibility would also destabilize accumulated skills, memories, and identity.
- Practice can make specialized performance faster and more energy-efficient, while broad development and creativity require varied inputs, critical thinking, and challenges that remain difficult but achievable.
- [[UlyssesContract|Ulysses contracts]] let a present self constrain a predictably tempted future self by removing access, adding social stakes, or lowering friction for the preferred action.
- [[MentalImagerySpectrum]] separates aphantasia, ordinary imagery, hyperphantasia, and inner speech without treating vivid visualization as necessary for visual creativity.
- [[TimePerceptionAndNeurochemicalState]] is extended by the freefall account: fear did not increase moment-to-moment visual sampling, but denser later memory made the frightening event seem longer in retrospect.
- [[SensorySubstitution]] treats cortex as able to learn structured information delivered through atypical channels, including sound represented as wrist vibration and visual information represented through touch.
- The proposed visual-cortex-defense theory of dreaming links REM activity to competition for cortical territory during darkness; the episode presents this as Eagleman's hypothesis rather than settled dream science.
- [[ReconstructiveMemory]] and [[MemoryContaminationRisk]] are reinforced by weapon focus, post-9/11 memory drift, recall-driven updating, and suggestion-based false-memory work.
- [[GroupIdentityEmpathyGating]] describes lower-level empathic and reward responses changing with religious, political, sporting, or arbitrarily assigned group labels without treating those responses as inevitable action.
- [[GroupPolarization]] is framed as older than social media: platforms may expose and amplify identity conflict, while dehumanizing labels and zero-sum models can narrow empathy and enable violence.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode's claim that fear-related slow motion is retrospective memory density rather than faster online perception narrows simple high-arousal “frame-rate” explanations without denying that arousal can alter duration judgments.
- The visual-cortex-defense account is retained as a hypothesis alongside emotional-processing, associative, and stage-dependent dream accounts rather than replacing them.
