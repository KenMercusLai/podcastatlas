# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》236｜七国之乱平定，汉景帝杀疯了（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows the military defeat of the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]] into its political settlement. An unnamed Qi envoy refuses a coerced surrender message and dies after telling besieged [[Linzi|临淄]] that the Han court has defeated the Wu-Chu force, but Qi's earlier secret contact with the coalition later exposes its king to investigation and suicide.

The second half centers on [[LiuAngJiaoxiKingWesternHan|胶西王刘昂]] and [[HanTuidangWesternHan|韩颓当]]. Liu Ang argues that the kings mobilized only to punish Chao Cuo and had withdrawn once he died; Han Tuidang answers that they neither petitioned the emperor nor possessed an edict or tiger tally and had attacked a neighboring kingdom that refused rebellion. The episode therefore frames [[PostRebellionAccountability|post-rebellion accountability]] around unauthorized force and conduct, not the rebels' declared slogan alone.

## Key Claims

- After [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] is killed, the episode treats the three-month rising as militarily finished and turns to the surviving royal houses.
- The commanders praise [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] for avoiding an immediate decisive engagement and severing the Wu-Chu supply line, while [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]] retains a grievance over delayed relief.
- An unnamed Qi envoy, captured outside besieged Linzi, rejects an order to announce that the Han court has lost and instead urges the city to hold because relief is coming; the coalition kills him.
- Qi survives the siege, but [[LuanBu|栾布]] later uncovers its earlier contact with the rebels, and the unnamed Qi king dies by poison before the threatened attack is resolved.
- Liu Ang rejects his son Liu De's proposal for a last attack on tired Han troops and approaches Han Tuidang's camp in a ritualized posture of guilt.
- Han Tuidang offers surrender under an imperial mandate but rejects Liu Ang's claim that the rising was only an effort to kill Chao Cuo.
- The rebuttal turns on procedure and conduct: the kings did not first petition the throne, lacked an edict and tiger tally, and attacked a neighboring kingdom that would not join them.
- Liu Ang dies by suicide after the imperial order is read; the episode also reports the deaths of his mother, his heir Liu De, and the kings of Jiaodong, Zichuan, and Jinan.

## Key Quotes

> “朝廷已经打败吴楚叛军，援军很快就会来。” - the message the unnamed envoy delivers to Linzi despite capture and coercion.

> “为什么不早早上奏天子？” - Han Tuidang's procedural challenge to Liu Ang's “诛晁错” defense.
