# 一份来自病床上的相关书单

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode is a sickbed [[ReadingListPointerEpisode|reading-list pointer episode]] rather than a single-book close reading. The hosts move from their own infection symptoms, hydration, nutrition, mood, and cautious medication into a set of books on infectious disease, medical images, historical body concepts, pandemics, public-health systems, geopolitics, and cardiac surgery.

The episode's durable contribution is a public-health frame: disease is not only a clinical event inside an individual body. It can expose [[InfectiousDiseasePublicLiteracy]], [[MedicalKnowledgeBoundary]], [[PandemicAsHistoricalForce]], [[PublicHealthGeopolitics]], and [[PublicHealthInvisibility]] at the same time.

## Key Claims
- Illness can become a reading prompt: the source explicitly uses the hosts' recovery state to choose books that are lighter to introduce but still connected to the immediate pandemic context.
- The self-care opening emphasizes drinking fluids, supplementing energy and electrolytes, eating protein and sugar, resting, and avoiding casual medication; the wiki stores this as source-scoped experience, not medical advice.
- [[ZhangWenhongShuoChuanran|《张文宏说传染》]] is positioned as the most practical, low-threshold entry into ordinary [[InfectiousDiseasePublicLiteracy]].
- [[BingMeigui|《病玫瑰》]] and the related "手术剧场" books show how [[MedicalVisualHistory]] made disease, anatomy, surgical knowledge, and bodily abnormality visible through drawings, wax models, and images.
- [[ZhongshijiDeShenti|《中世纪的身体》]] shifts the episode from modern medical common sense toward [[HistoricalBodyConcept]]: earlier people organized skin, blood, bathing, heart, limbs, and illness through very different frameworks.
- The discussion of Black Death and [[PaleRiderBook|《苍白的骑士》 / Pale Rider]] treats pandemics as social events that can change labor value, mechanization pressure, war, nationalism, expert trust, and public emotion.
- [[DongbeiBoyi|《东北博弈》]] and [[WuLiande|Wu Liande / 伍连德]] add the China and Northeast Asia version of [[PublicHealthGeopolitics]]: plague response, railways, empire, and foreign influence were entangled in the 1910-1911 Northeast plague.
- [[PlagueNights|《瘟疫之夜》 / Nights of Plague]] is treated as a fictional mirror for the same plague-politics pattern: an island outbreak, Ottoman imperial weakness, foreign powers, murder, and public order interact.
- [[APlagueTale|《瘟疫传说》 / A Plague Tale]] appears as a brief media extension, showing that plague imagination also travels through games and popular visual storytelling.
- [[ShixinPublicHealth|《失信：公共卫生体系的崩溃》]] anchors the public-health-system critique: epidemics recur, lessons are forgotten, and the systems that keep water, food, sewage, and disease control boringly functional are easy to underfund.
- [[DakayiKexin|《打开一颗心》 / Open Heart]] closes the episode by moving from system fragility back to medical practice: doctors remain limited and fallible, but surgical courage, restraint, and accumulated skill still matter.

## Key Quotes
> "精美又恶心" - the episode's description of [[BingMeigui|《病玫瑰》]] and its medical imagery.

> "不能只把它看成医学问题" - the source's implied frame for disease as social, political, and historical.

> "多保重身体、多喝水" - the closing return from disease history to the hosts' immediate sickbed situation.
