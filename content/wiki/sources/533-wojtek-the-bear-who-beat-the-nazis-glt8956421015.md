# 533. Wojtek: The Bear Who Beat the Nazis

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses [[Wojtek]], a Syrian brown bear adopted by displaced Polish soldiers, to connect an unusual animal biography with the destruction and exile experienced by [[Poland]] during the Second World War. His route through Iran, the Middle East, the [[BattleOfMonteCassino|Battle of Monte Cassino]], and postwar [[Scotland]] follows [[AndersArmy|Anders' Army]] from Soviet captivity into Allied service and then into a return many soldiers could not safely or willingly make.

Wojtek was formally enlisted so he could travel with the 22nd Artillery Supply Company, reportedly carried ammunition during the battle, and became the company's shell-carrying-bear emblem. The episode's durable interpretation is that his military role and comic behavior mattered because he gave uprooted soldiers affection, continuity, and a less direct way to hold collective grief, making him a case of [[WartimeMascotMemory]] rather than merely a novelty mascot.

## Key Claims

- Nazi Germany and the [[SovietUnion|Soviet Union]] divided Poland in 1939; Soviet imprisonment, the Katyn massacre, and mass deportation formed the traumatic background from which the Polish force later led by [[WladyslawAnders|Władysław Anders]] emerged.
- Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union enabled an agreement that released Anders and other Polish captives; more than 100,000 soldiers and civilians then left the Soviet Union through Iran, and the military formation became the Second Polish Corps.
- Accounts differ over the exact circumstances in which the orphaned bear cub was bought near Tehran, but Polish soldiers named him Wojtek and Piotr Prendys became his principal carer.
- Wojtek's play, swimming, wrestling, beer, coffee, and cigarette-eating made him a source of morale and affection among soldiers marked by displacement and loss.
- The army formally enlisted Wojtek as a private because transport rules barred ordinary animals from the ship carrying his company from Egypt to Italy.
- During the [[BattleOfMonteCassino|Battle of Monte Cassino]], Wojtek reportedly moved shells or ammunition crates for the 22nd Artillery Supply Company; the company adopted a bear carrying a shell as its badge.
- After the war, Wojtek went with Polish soldiers to Scotland, helped mediate contact with local communities, and entered Edinburgh Zoo after demobilization in 1947.
- Former comrades' visits, public commemoration, and statues in Duns and Edinburgh turned Wojtek into a symbol of Polish endurance, exile, and Polish-Scottish friendship.
