# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》370-1｜揭秘汉朝第一位与匈奴和亲的公主

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens the Yuanfeng 6 / 105 BCE marriage-alliance sequence before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-370-2-yeye-ba-jiaoqi-jiagei-qinsunzi-nainai-yiye-bian-xifuer-lopw_h3yzk6qtcpksqdm9l2egrvp|Hanji 370-2]]. Under pressure from [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] and impressed by Han's reach into the Western Regions, [[Wusun|乌孙]] requests a Han princess; after Wusun supplies one thousand horses as bridewealth, [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] creates [[LiuXijunPrincessWesternHan|刘细君]] a princess and sends her to the elderly ruler [[LiejiaomiWusun|猎骄靡]].

The durable synthesis is that the alliance transfers strategic adjustment costs onto a politically vulnerable royal woman. Xijun has genuine imperial lineage, but her father [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘建]]'s suicide after a rebellion case has weakened her household position; the episode therefore places her selection at the intersection of royal authenticity, frontier strategy, and reduced personal protection. It stops at her childhood rupture and marriage setup, leaving the later Wusun household and remarriage sequence to Hanji 370-2.

## Key Claims

- The episode presents Xijun as the first named heqin princess in its historical frame and as the first Western Han case involving genuine imperial-clan blood rather than a substitute bride.
- Wusun seeks the marriage after Han-Wusun contact grows and Xiongnu threatens Wusun for moving closer to Han.
- Han makes receipt of bridewealth a condition; Wusun sends one thousand horses, and Han answers with Xijun's princess title and a large dowry.
- Xijun is Liu Jian's daughter. His suicide after a rebellion case causes the family to lose status while she is still a child.
- Liejiaomi's origin legend, early dependence on a Xiongnu chanyu, later attack on the Yuezhi, and rule over a major north-Tianshan nomadic polity explain both Wusun's strength and its prior Xiongnu connection.
- The episode interprets the marriage as a state bargain in which alliance value outweighs the bride's unrecorded personal preference.
- The opening mentions Xijun's later cross-generational remarriage, but this installment does not narrate or verify that sequence in detail.

## Key Quotes

> “他们只看到了自己国家的利益” - the host's judgment on rulers using heqin while overlooking the bride's fate.

> “第一次真正割舍皇室骨肉去和亲” - the episode's source-scoped distinction between Xijun and earlier substitute-princess practice.

## Contradictions

- Title/content mismatch: the title calls Xijun a princess who married “with Xiongnu,” but the episode itself says she marries the Wusun ruler Liejiaomi; Xiongnu is the rival pressure shaping the alliance, not the destination polity.
- The episode calls Xijun the first named heqin princess and the first genuine imperial-blood bride in the Western Han frame. These priority claims are retained as source-scoped rather than adopted as universal historical firsts.
- The claim that Xijun was eleven when Liu Jian died, the family's exact post-case status, Liejiaomi's age, the divine-nurture legend, Wusun's relative size, and the actors' private motives remain source-scoped.
- Scope boundary: this installment does not develop Xijun's Wusun life or later remarriage. Those details are synthesized only from Hanji 370-2, not retroactively attributed to this source.
