# 696. Elizabeth I vs The Catholics: Killing the Queen of Scots (Part 6)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the final phase of [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]]' captivity in [[England]], focusing on the [[BabingtonPlot]], [[FrancisWalsingham]]'s intelligence operation, and the ciphered correspondence that gave Elizabethan ministers a legal basis to move against her. It presents Mary as a dynastic and confessional threat to [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]], but also emphasizes Elizabeth's reluctance to authorize the public killing of another anointed queen.

The episode then traces Mary's arrest, trial at [[FotheringhayCastle]], conviction, warrant politics, and execution. Its core interpretation is that espionage, law, dynastic fear, and symbolism all mattered: [[SpyNetworkEntrapment]] made the evidence usable, the [[ActForTheQueensSafety|Act for the Queen's Safety]] made prosecution possible, and Mary's final conduct turned defeat into [[MartyrdomStaging|martyr performance]] before the crisis widened toward the [[SpanishArmada]].

## Key Claims

- [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary]] was dangerous to Elizabethan Protestants because she combined hereditary claim, Catholic identity, continental sympathy, and the possibility of a succession crisis under [[TudorSuccessionCrisis]].
- [[AnthonyBabington]]'s proposal linked six assassins, Mary's release, a Catholic rising, foreign invasion, and restoration of Catholicism in [[England]].
- [[FrancisWalsingham]]'s network turned [[GilbertGifford]] and used the beer-barrel channel at [[ChartleyManor]] to make Mary believe she had secure communication with supporters.
- [[ThomasPhillips]] decrypted Mary's reply, copied it for Walsingham, and treated the endorsement of "the six gentlemen" as decisive evidence against her.
- [[WilliamCecil|William Cecil / Lord Burleigh]] and Walsingham wanted a public execution because it would remove Mary, brand her a traitor, and consolidate Protestant support.
- [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]] hesitated because executing an anointed queen could inflame Europe, strengthen Spanish justification, and create a dangerous precedent for lawful royal killing.
- The 1585 [[ActForTheQueensSafety|Act for the Queen's Safety]] let ministers frame Mary's fate as legal consequence rather than personal vengeance.
- [[AmiasPaulet]] enforced Mary's harsher captivity at [[ChartleyManor]] but refused to murder her privately without law or warrant.
- The [[FotheringhayCastle]] trial used procedure and staging, including Elizabeth's empty throne, to make Mary's judgment visible as [[PoliticalTrialTheatre]].
- Mary defended herself by denying responsibility for Babington's assassination plan, rejecting English jurisdiction over a queen, and accusing Walsingham of letter tampering.
- Mary's final black-and-crimson costume, Catholic prayers, English prayer for Elizabeth, and scaffold conduct allowed supporters to read her execution as [[MartyrdomStaging]].
- The episode links Mary's death to [[ConfessionalWarEscalation]], especially the path from English anti-Marian security politics toward Philip II's 1588 [[SpanishArmada]].

## Key Quotes

> "the six gentlemen to work" - phrase treated by the hosts as Mary's fatal endorsement of the assassination plan.

> "bloody letter" - Thomas Phillips's label for the decrypted Mary-Babington reply.

> "her death the life of it" - Kent's source-quoted Protestant warning to Mary before execution.
