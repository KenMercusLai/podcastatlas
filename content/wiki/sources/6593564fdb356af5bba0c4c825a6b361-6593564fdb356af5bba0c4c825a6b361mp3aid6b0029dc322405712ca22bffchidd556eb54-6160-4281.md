## Summary
This [[EconomistPodcasts]] episode joins three reports about powerful systems whose effects outrun ordinary accountability. It presents the [[InternationalCourtOfJustice|International Court of Justice]] judgment against [[Myanmar]] as legal recognition for the [[Rohingya]] but stresses the gap between a ruling and enforceable return or reparation; it then examines how frontier models can give cyberattackers persistent, parallel, increasingly autonomous capability; and it closes with [[VladimirUglev]]'s work on [[Novichok]] and his later public criticism of the secret Soviet programme.

## Key Claims
- The episode says the International Court of Justice found by nine votes to six that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, including killing, sexual violence, attacks on children, forced flight, and intent to destroy at least part of the community.
- The judgment ordered return and reparation, but continuing conflict, destroyed homes, distrust between Rohingya and ethnic Rakhine communities, and the court's lack of independent enforcement make practical remedies uncertain.
- Survivors treated the ruling, and the court's use of the name “Rohingya,” as recognition that restored dignity and validated a history of persecution.
- The cyber segment argues that AI's present offensive advantage comes especially from persistence and scale: thousands of instances can probe known weaknesses continuously, chain exploits, and sometimes search for unknown vulnerabilities.
- Defensive and offensive requests can be operationally indistinguishable, so model providers have not solved the dual-use problem merely by classifying a prompt's apparent purpose.
- The episode expects attackers to hold a near-term advantage, but its benchmark scores, regulatory description, incident examples, and forecast are episode-attributed rather than independently audited here.
- [[VladimirUglev]] is described as a Soviet chemist who helped synthesize four secret agents, identified the Salisbury substance as A234, and later spoke publicly about the programme and alleged state use.
- Uglev's reported heart-attack death is juxtaposed with A234's possible cardiac effects, but the supplied summary provides no evidence that poisoning caused his death.
