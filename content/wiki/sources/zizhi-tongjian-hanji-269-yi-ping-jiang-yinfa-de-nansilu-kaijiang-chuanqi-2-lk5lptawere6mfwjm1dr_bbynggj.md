# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》269｜一瓶酱引发的“南丝路”开疆传奇（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] sequel follows [[TangMengWesternHan|唐蒙]]'s negotiated opening of [[YelangStateWesternHan|夜郎]] into attempted rule. It says a new commandery and a road from Bodao toward the Zangke river are built through large compulsory labor drafts; deaths, flight, and military-law executions then turn conditional cooperation into revolt. The sequence makes [[CoerciveFrontierLaborBackfire|强制边疆劳役反噬]] the central qualification to the first episode's route-and-expansion plan.

[[HanWudi|汉武帝]] responds by sending [[SimaXiangru|司马相如]] rather than a punitive army. The episode credits 《喻巴蜀檄》, meetings with local elites, gifts, and persuasion with calming the crisis, after which Sima recommends accepting additional western groups, leads an embassy, moves the frontier gates outward, and helps open the road described as a component of the Shu-Shendu or southwestern overland Silk Road. The durable synthesis is that a corridor is not created by discovery alone: labor, local consent, communication, administrative capacity, and the coercive costs of construction determine whether strategic access becomes governable territory.

## Key Claims

- Tang Meng reports his mission, after which the episode says the Han court establishes a southwestern commandery and drafts labor from Ba-Shu and Ye Lang to build a road toward the Zangke river.
- Heavy mortality, escape attempts, and executions under military law frighten local communities and trigger an uprising against project supervisors.
- Wudi does not initially answer with a large army; he sends Sima Xiangru, whose 《喻巴蜀檄》 combines imperial authority, justification of the mobilization, and a loyalty-and-honor appeal.
- The episode says the proclamation and follow-up meetings calm the crisis, but it provides no independent measure of consent, casualties, or the settlement's durability.
- Additional western groups then request Han recognition and officials, and Sima argues that proximity, passable roads, and earlier Qin administration make renewed incorporation feasible.
- Sima's embassy uses Ba-Shu resources and gifts, moves old frontier gates outward, establishes new ones, opens a road, bridges a river, and supports a commandant-and-counties administrative package.
- The road is presented as part of a Sichuan-Yunnan-Myanmar-India corridor later remembered as the southwestern overland Silk Road.
- The closing material shifts north: Wudi drafts ten thousand laborers for Yanmen defenses, and the episode reviews Yanmen's strategic association with Li Mu, Li Guang, Wei Qing, and Huo Qubing.

## Key Quotes

> “哪里有压迫，哪里就会有反抗。” - the host's causal judgment linking coercive construction to the Ye Lang uprising.

> “以柔克刚。” - the episode's characterization of sending Sima Xiangru rather than immediately dispatching a punitive army.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was adopted. The episode qualifies Hanji 269-1's successful negotiation by showing how coercive implementation could reverse local cooperation, then adds a reported de-escalation and renewed expansion phase.
- The supplied document repeatedly writes “乾维郡” and “藏科江.” These likely point toward 犍为郡 and the 牂柯/牂牁 river corridor, but this note does not silently normalize them without textual apparatus.
- The forms rendered as “穷人和染人,” “临关道,” “孙水,” and “穹都” likely contain transcription errors involving southwestern peoples, Lingguan Road, a river, and Qiongdu. They remain source-scoped rather than being promoted into canonical identities.
- The episode's labor totals, mortality, exact execution sequence, claim that the proclamation ended violence and rumor, gifts, border extent, administrative count, bridge location, and road continuity require comparison with the transmitted histories and historical geography.
- Describing the route as a continuous Sichuan-to-India “Southwestern Land Silk Road” is a later corridor synthesis. The episode does not establish that one Han project created or continuously controlled the entire route.
- The Yanmen survey and palace preview are adjacent chronology and program transitions, not evidence for the southwestern project's outcome.
