# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》216｜汉朝第一首富，竟然是文帝的男宠？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode treats the 175 BCE revival of four-zhu coin and permission for private minting as a combined monetary, legal, and political problem. [[JiaYi|贾谊]] argues that profit-seeking minters have incentives to adulterate coin, that punishment cannot suppress an offense generated by the policy itself, and that mining and charcoal production can draw labor away from agriculture; his proposed lever is upstream control of copper rather than reliance on criminal penalties alone.

[[JiaShanWesternHan|贾山]] instead emphasizes sovereignty: if ordinary people can create the medium that purchases rank and wealth, they share an authority the emperor claims as his own. [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] rejects both warnings while granting [[DengTong|邓通]] a copper mountain and minting privilege; [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] likewise uses [[WuKingdomEarlyHan|吴国]]'s copper and salt resources to accumulate wealth and reduce ordinary taxation. The episode thereby complicates an unqualified austerity portrait of Liu Heng, but its sexualized title and interpretation of his attachment to Deng Tong go beyond what the supplied summary independently establishes.

## Key Claims

- In 175 BCE the court reportedly issued four-zhu coin marked “half liang” and removed the prohibition on private minting.
- Jia Yi argues that legal private minting creates a profit incentive to mix inferior metals into coin, producing widespread offenses that daily punishment cannot eliminate.
- He links decentralized minting to inconsistent coin weights, weak official authority in market exchange, labor diversion from farming into copper extraction and charcoal, and growth in bad coin without corresponding food output.
- Jia Yi also recognizes a prohibition dilemma: scarcity can raise the reward for illicit minting, so he recommends controlling copper supply at its source rather than relying on the death penalty.
- Jia Shan frames mint authority as a political monopoly because money can purchase wealth and status even if the material itself has little intrinsic value.
- Liu Heng rejects the two remonstrances and gives Deng Tong a copper mountain and permission to mint, turning personal favor into monetary and economic privilege.
- Liu Bi's copper mining, coin production, and salt manufacture give Wu an exceptional resource base; the episode says both Deng coin and Wu coin circulate widely.
- The episode treats Deng Tong as an intimate male favorite, but the exact nature of the relationship is interpretive rather than established by the supplied note.

## Key Quotes

> “有些法令，会诱导百姓违法。” - the episode's rendering of Jia Yi's argument that policy can manufacture the offense later punished.

> “钱本身没有价值，却可以换取富贵。” - the sovereignty premise attributed to Jia Shan.

## Connections

- [[JiaYi|贾谊]] - adviser who connects minting incentives, criminalization, agricultural labor, coin quality, and upstream copper control.
- [[JiaShanWesternHan|贾山]] - remonstrant who treats mint authority as part of the emperor's control over wealth and rank.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - ruler who permits private minting, rejects both warnings, and grants Deng Tong a copper mountain.
- [[DengTong|邓通]] - favorite whose access to copper and minting turns personal attachment into extraordinary wealth.
- [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] and [[WuKingdomEarlyHan|吴国]] - royal ruler and polity whose copper, coin, and salt resources support fiscal autonomy.
- [[PunitiveCurrencyEnforcementBackfire|惩罚性货币执法反噬]] - earlier Western Han argument that punishment fails when policy preserves the underlying monetary incentive.
- [[CentralizedWuzhuCoinage|五铢钱中央统一铸造]] - later institutional answer combining exclusive mint authority with copper control and production standardization.
- [[CurrencyCredit|货币信用]] - coin-weight variation and adulteration weaken the relationship between official denomination and market acceptance.

## Contradictions

- The source complicates, rather than overturns, the wiki's restraint portrait of Liu Heng: public frugality and burden reduction coexist with exceptional private enrichment of Deng Tong.
- Jia Yi's warning is not reducible to a simple ban. The episode explicitly says prohibition can increase illicit profit and attributes to him an upstream copper-control solution.
- The title's “男宠” and the host's stronger sexual or romantic language are interpretive. Favor, household visits, office, gifts, and minting privilege support exceptional intimacy and patronage but do not independently establish a modern sexual identity or a specific sexual relationship.
- Coin weights, crime counts, price examples, exact legal provisions, circulation reach, and the causal effects on agriculture and prices require comparison with transmitted histories and numismatic evidence.
