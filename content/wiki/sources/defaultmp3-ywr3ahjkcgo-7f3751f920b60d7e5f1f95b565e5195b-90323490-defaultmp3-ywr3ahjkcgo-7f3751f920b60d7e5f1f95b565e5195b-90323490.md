# Adah Sharma on The Kerala Story, Backlash, Threats & Handling Rejection | Shekhar Natarajan | Tomorrow Today

## Summary
This [[TomorrowToday]] episode has [[ShekharNatarajan]] interview actor [[AdahSharma]] about childhood difference, family values, dance training, auditions, typecasting, cross-language film work, [[TheKeralaStory]], public backlash, stress, and AI's effects on creative labor. Sharma presents preparation as necessary but insufficient for an acting career: skill helps an actor use an opportunity, while access, networks, typecasting, intuition, and psychological recovery shape whether opportunities arrive and remain workable.

The AI discussion holds empowerment and displacement together. Cheaper tools may let outsiders make and distribute films without traditional capital or connections, yet increasingly convincing digital voices and faces raise consent, authorship, and livelihood questions for actors, writers, artists, musicians, and traditional craftspeople.

## Key Claims
- [[AdahSharma]] describes childhood conformity as exhausting real-life performance and says adulthood brought greater calm when she stopped continually auditioning for other people's approval.
- [[ActingOpportunityReadiness]] separates preparation from access: dance and acting discipline can make an actor ready, but cannot guarantee auditions, casting, networks, or roles that break typecasting.
- The success of *1920* did not immediately create abundant work; Sharma attributes the gap partly to weak industry networking and being categorized as a foreign-looking horror-film actor.
- [[TheKeralaStory]] produced far greater public recognition while also bringing screening uncertainty, threats, controversy over the trailer's “32,000” claim, and targeted online harassment before release.
- Sharma treats rejection recovery as a learnable capacity: criticism still affects her, but experience has shortened how long she remains low after rejection or negative events.
- Stress can remain physically costly despite quick psychological recovery; she reports increased heart rate, acne, and hives, and uses exercise and yoga without claiming to eliminate anxiety.
- AI may democratize filmmaking for people without money or relationships, but digital replication of a performer's voice or expressions requires consent and may displace creative workers.
- Sharma's fire analogy makes AI neither inherently good nor bad, while her handmade Lucknowi-kurta example shows that consumer education and purchasing choices affect whether cheaper automation destroys traditional livelihoods.

## Key Quotes
