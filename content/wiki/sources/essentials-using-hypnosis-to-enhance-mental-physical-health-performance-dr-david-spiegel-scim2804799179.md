# Essentials: Using Hypnosis to Enhance Mental & Physical Health & Performance | Dr. David Spiegel

## Summary
This condensed [[HubermanLab]] conversation has [[DavidSpiegel]] present [[ClinicalHypnosis]] as voluntary, highly focused attention that can increase cognitive flexibility and mind-body control rather than remove agency. It revisits neural correlates, stress and sleep exercises, phobia and trauma work, pain and pediatric procedures, performance states, and the role of [[Hypnotizability]].

The edit also describes the [[Reveri]] self-hypnosis app and directs serious medical or psychiatric concerns toward licensed, hypnosis-trained professionals. Because it condenses the previously ingested full interview, its overlap adds provenance and accessibility rather than independent confirmation of the clinical or mechanistic claims.

## Key Claims
- Hypnosis is framed as focused absorption that reduces distractibility and ordinary self-evaluation while preserving voluntary control.
- The reported neural pattern combines reduced dorsal anterior cingulate activity, stronger dorsolateral-prefrontal-to-insula connectivity, and inverse dorsolateral-prefrontal-to-posterior-cingulate connectivity.
- Stress, sleep, phobia, trauma, pain, and medical-procedure exercises pair bodily regulation with focused imagery, controlled approach, and revised meaning.
- State-dependent access may help a person work with traumatic material, but vivid recall is not proof of historical accuracy.
- [[Hypnotizability]] varies substantially; the eye-roll test is presented as a correlate rather than a complete assessment or outcome guarantee.
- [[Reveri]] offers brief goal-directed exercises, but self-hypnosis does not replace medical evaluation or trained clinical care.
