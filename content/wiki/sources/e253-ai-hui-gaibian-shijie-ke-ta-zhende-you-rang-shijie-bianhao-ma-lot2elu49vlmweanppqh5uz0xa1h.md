# E253 AI 会改变世界，可它真的有让世界变好吗？

## Summary

This [[ZhixingXiaojiuguan|知行小酒馆]] episode with [[LiZhilin|李志林]] asks who receives the capability, opportunity, and wealth created by AI. It argues that technical progress does not automatically become broad welfare: electricity, devices, trained staff, transport, trust, and local follow-through still determine whether rural health, education, and employment tools reach the people they are meant to help.

The episode joins that implementation problem to distribution and personal action. It treats ownership, taxation, and philanthropy as complementary allocation layers, applies [[EffectiveAltruism|effective-altruist]] reasoning to donation choices, and presents recurring giving, public attention, and ordinary care as imperfect but actionable ways to widen the benefits of progress.

## Key Claims

- AI access is uneven, and urban technology discourse can mistake the experience of a small active user group for universal adoption.
- In rural cataract programs, the procedure may be brief, but language, transport, screening, family consent, trust, infrastructure, and clinical capacity make the human last mile decisive.
- Donated AI psychology devices can sit unused when schools lack time, trained staff, teacher incentives, and curriculum priority; training ordinary teachers to deliver [[SocialEmotionalLearning|social-emotional learning]] can be more operationally useful.
- Effective giving asks where a given amount of money produces the most benefit, accepts that delivery has losses, and still evaluates outcomes rather than donor intention alone.
- AI can extend rural clinicians' diagnostic support, personalize student practice, widen college-choice information, and help remote women earn income, but each case still requires training, supervision, local outreach, or human judgment.
- If automation removes low-cost labor and outsourcing work, poorer regions may lose a development ladder before they have accumulated capital or moved into higher-value industries.
- AI-era distribution involves ownership and income, taxes and transfers, and charitable giving; no one layer by itself guarantees that productivity gains become broad welfare.
- Small acts can create a ripple effect when donations, issue-sharing, or referrals reach people able to continue the work.
