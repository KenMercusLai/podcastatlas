## Summary
This [[RevisionistHistory]] feed episode introduces [[ValleyOfShadowsPodcast|Valley of Shadows]] through the unresolved 1998 disappearance of [[JohnAjay]], a Los Angeles County sheriff's deputy and ultramarathon runner, from [[DevilsPunchbowlNaturalArea|Devil's Punchbowl]]. [[BetsyShepherd]] and [[HaleyFox]] reconstruct a partial timeline and ask why the [[LosAngelesCountySheriffsDepartment|Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department]] stopped its search after six days, advanced a suicide explanation without conclusive physical evidence, and allegedly restricted access to the case file. The episode supports scrutiny of possible [[PrematureInvestigativeClosure|premature investigative closure]] and [[RestrictedCaseFileOpacity|institutional opacity]], but does not establish whether Ajay suffered an accident, died by suicide, deliberately disappeared, or was killed.

## Key Claims
- Ajay's Army service, survival skills, wilderness familiarity, and ultramarathon ability made ordinary assumptions about a missing hiker's location and survival window unreliable.
- Witnesses reportedly placed Ajay near the Punchbowl before noon and later moving through a campground toward the parking area, but the episode does not establish a complete final route.
- Searchers examined trails, gullies, mines, and other rugged terrain; bloodhound interest, a reported gunshot, footprints, and a sealed mine produced no confirmed physical trace.
- The operation expanded to include military and specialized LASD resources and treated foul play as possible, then ended on the sixth day without consultation with some search specialists and helicopter crews, according to participants.
- Internal reports reportedly attributed the disappearance to suicide despite no recovered body, blood, bullet, or note; colleagues disagree over whether Ajay's remarks, marriage difficulties, and personality support that interpretation.
- [[MikeBauer]] says a suicide theory circulated by the third day, responsibility for the case remained unclear, and homicide personnel later excluded or misled him.
- [[DaveRathbun]] says unusual permission was required to access the file and that a retired cold-case detective declined to examine it because it was considered a professional risk.
- Restricted access, early hypothesis narrowing, and an unusually short search create accountability questions, but none independently proves a cover-up or resolves Ajay's fate.
