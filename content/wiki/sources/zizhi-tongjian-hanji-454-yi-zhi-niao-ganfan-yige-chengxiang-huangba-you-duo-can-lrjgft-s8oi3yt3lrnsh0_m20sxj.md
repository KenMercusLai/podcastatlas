# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》454｜一只鸟干翻一个丞相！黄霸有多惨？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses [[HuangBaWesternHan|黄霸]]'s mistaken "divine bird" report to connect self-cultivation, reputation hunger, court evaluation, and omen politics under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]. After [[BingJiWesternHan|丙吉]] dies and Huang Ba becomes chancellor, the host contrasts Bing Ji's quiet merit with Huang Ba's insecure search for auspicious proof after moving from local governance into central office.

The durable synthesis is that a superior's visible preference can distort subordinate reporting even when no explicit order is given. Huang Ba's questions about yielding fields, gender-separated travel, lost objects, filial sons, and chaste women signal a taste for exemplary moralized indicators; [[ZhangChangWesternHan|张敞]] then uses the escaped heque bird to warn that such preferences can make local officials abandon law, manufacture surface virtue, and pursue reputation instead of substantive governance.

## Key Claims

- The episode frames Huang Ba's failure through Zhuangzi, the Great Learning, and Laozi: attachment to reputation, comparison, and visible success can make judgment shallow.
- Bing Ji's death in 55 BCE supplies a contrast figure: a meritorious chancellor tied to Emperor Xuan's survival who does not advertise his own credit.
- Huang Ba is praised as a strong local official but portrayed as mismatched in the central chancellor role, where he looks for auspicious signs rather than substantive state help.
- Zhang Chang keeps heque birds at home; when one flies to Huang Ba's residence, Huang misidentifies it as a "divine bird" and prepares to report it as an omen.
- Zhang Chang's memorial argues that Huang Ba's evaluation questions make local officials believe the chancellor wants impressive moralized examples, even if Huang never orders fabrication.
- The memorial rejects surface indicators such as yielding fields, men and women traveling on separate roads, lost objects left untouched, filial sons, and chaste women as insufficient tests of clean versus corrupt or chaste versus immoral governance.
- Emperor Xuan accepts Zhang Chang's warning and has attending officials instruct the reporting local officials accordingly.
- The host treats Huang Ba as a good official destabilized by wrong-position pressure, desire for recognition, and dependence on external validation.

## Key Quotes

> "凡外重者，内必拙" - the Zhuangzi line used to frame Huang Ba's reputation-driven failure.

> "去子之骄气与多欲，态色与淫志" - Laozi's advice to Confucius used in the episode's closing self-cultivation frame.
