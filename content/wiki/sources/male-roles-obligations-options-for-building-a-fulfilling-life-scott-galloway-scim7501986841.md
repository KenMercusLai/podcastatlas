# Male Roles, Obligations and Options for Building a Fulfilling Life | Scott Galloway

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[ScottGalloway]] about masculinity, young men, dating, Big Tech, social media, service, health, education, and intergenerational policy. Galloway argues that young men need a code organized around provision, protection, procreation, service, strength, work, and repeated exposure to rejection, while Huberman presses for nuance around [[ElonMusk]], public role models, alcohol, dating fear, and online judgment. The episode's central synthesis is that male flourishing requires both personal agency and institutional repair: phone audits, physical training, work outside the house, volunteering, respectful approaches, mentorship, national service, platform regulation, and youth economic on-ramps all matter.

## Key Claims
- [[PositiveMasculinityCode]] names Galloway's provider, protector, procreator, and service frame, with masculinity treated as usefulness and responsibility rather than domination.
- [[YoungMenOfflinePractice]] captures the concrete advice to audit phone use and redirect time into exercise, paid work outside the home, group service, and social exposure.
- [[RejectionTolerancePractice]] links dating, friendship, entrepreneurship, and public work to learning how to survive being told no.
- [[DigitalSexualSubstituteRisk]] records the source-scoped concern that porn and lifelike digital sexual stimulation can reduce motivation to date, socialize, train, work, and endure rejection.
- [[MaleMentorshipResponsibility]] captures the closing claim that adult men can help boys through ordinary presence, invitations, questions, and support for sons of single mothers or absent fathers.
- [[NationalServiceSocialIntegration]] records Galloway's preferred policy of mandatory service, military or civilian, as a structure for purpose, class mixing, and public obligation.
- [[IntergenerationalYouthEconomicBurden]] captures his claim that deficits, health care, education, tax policy, housing, and older-generation benefits can shift costs onto younger people and worsen male instability.
