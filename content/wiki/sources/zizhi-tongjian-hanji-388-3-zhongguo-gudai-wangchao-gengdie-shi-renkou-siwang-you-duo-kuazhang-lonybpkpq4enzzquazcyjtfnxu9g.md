# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》388-3｜中国古代王朝更迭时，人口死亡有多夸张？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] short episode uses Chinese historical population drops to ask how much death and social loss sat behind dynastic transition. It starts from [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] as a ruler whose military achievement and imperial grandeur came with war, fangshu obsession, fiscal strain, and a near-crisis for the dynasty.

The episode then uses [[GeJianxiong|葛剑雄]]'s [[ZhongguoRenkoushi|《中国人口史》]] to caution against reading registered households as direct death totals. Elite concealment, missing military-agricultural households, unregistered minority populations, migration, and administrative breakdown mean the records need correction; even after correction, [[DynasticPopulationLoss|王朝更替人口损耗]] remains severe across Qin-Han, late Eastern Han and Three Kingdoms, Sui-Tang, Song-Yuan, Ming-Qing, and the Taiping era.

## Key Claims

- Han Wudi is presented as a compressed example of imperial achievement with high human and institutional cost: long reign, frequent war, late risky campaigns, fangshu credulity, and pressure on the Western Han order.
- The episode says Han Wudi reigned for 53 years and launched 26 wars, with late northern expeditions often marked by poor intelligence, insufficient preparation, and deep-distance risk.
- [[HistoricalPopulationRegistrationCaution|户口数字解读谨慎]] is necessary because historical population lows can reflect missing registration, hidden households,屯田 producers outside county household rolls, uncounted minority populations, migration, and state-capacity collapse.
- Using Ge Jianxiong's correction for the Three Kingdoms period, the episode gives a population trough of roughly 23 million rather than the much lower official 7.67 million figure.
- Even corrected demographic estimates still show extreme population loss: the episode cites drops around Qin-Han transition, Wang Mang and early Eastern Han war, late Eastern Han to Three Kingdoms, Sui-Tang transition, An Lushan aftermath, Song-Yuan transition, Ming-Qing transition, and the Taiping era.
- The moral conclusion is that dynastic change and large-scale war should be read through ordinary people's suffering, not only through conquest, unification, or rulerly accomplishment.

## Key Quotes

> "人口损失动辄千万甚至上亿" - the episode's concluding frame for war and dynastic-transition cost.

> "不能简单等同于实际死亡人数" - the source's caution around reading official population records as mortality totals.
