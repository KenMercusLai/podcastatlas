# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》798丨东汉时期的百姓有多苦？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 121 CE [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]] layer immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-799-donghan-mingchen-yangzhen-ruhe-jujue-zou-houmen-lmufb7lvb-kzsvjtn6iu-zrpiptf|Hanji 799]]. It opens with source-scoped advice about avoiding self-consuming relationships, then turns to [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] warning that disasters, poverty, empty treasury, and palace missions through [[BoRongLateHan|伯荣]] are turning imperial ritual travel into [[PalaceEnvoyLocalExtraction|宫使扰民式地方抽取]].

The second half pairs this court diagnosis with a reputation story from Runan. [[WangGongTaiweiLateHan|王龚]] honors [[YuanLangLateHan|袁朗]], [[HuangXianLateHan|黄宪]], and [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]], while [[XunShuLateHan|荀淑]], [[DaiLiangLateHan|戴良]], Chen Fan, and [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] treat Huang Xian as a deep moral presence who never converts reputation into office. The durable synthesis is that Hanji 798 shows An-era suffering through both institutional burden and moral contrast: official power is misallocated, commoners bear the cost, and the most admired person in the episode remains outside the governing machine.

## Key Claims

- The opening self-care lesson about avoiding inner depletion is a source-scoped frame and is not promoted as a historical claim.
- In 121 CE, Chen Zhong describes a crisis field of heavy rain, seawater intrusion, locusts, rice shortfall, Qiang rebellion, poor commoners, and an empty treasury under Emperor An.
- Emperor An sends Huangmen Changshi and palace emissary Bo Rong between Ganling and the capital for repeated ritual missions.
- Chen Zhong says Bo Rong's passage shakes commanderies and counties: princes, marquises, and senior officials bow before him, while local people are conscripted for roads, stations, supplies, and gifts without a clear limit.
- Chen Zhong frames the burden as more than one emissary's misconduct. Palace proximity lets servants, attendants, and women envoys blur imperial authority, so local officials treat their preferences as dangerous commands.
- Emperor An does not accept Chen Zhong's first warning, leaving the emissary-burden and palace-channel problem unresolved.
- Chen Zhong's second memorial says the Three Excellencies have rank without practical authority because Shangshu offices control confidential affairs, selection, rewards, punishments, and legal decisions.
- The same memorial makes [[ThreeExcellenciesBlameWithoutAuthority|三公无权背责]] visible: disasters are used to remove or reprimand the Three Excellencies even though the decision power has shifted elsewhere.
- Wang Gong's Runan administration is shown as talent-honoring local governance: he appoints Yuan Lang as gongcao, recommends Huang Xian and Chen Fan, and treats worthy men respectfully.
- Huang Xian is presented as low-born and office-refusing yet morally transformative; Xun Shu, Dai Liang, Chen Fan, and Guo Tai all use him as a benchmark for character and depth.
- The closing Northern Xiongnu and Cheshi incursion into Hexi sets up the Yumen Pass and Yangguan closure debate continued in Hanji 799.

## Key Quotes

> "百姓贫穷、国库空虚" - Chen Zhong's fiscal and social background for opposing the missions.

> "皇帝权柄掌握在臣仆卑妾手中" - Chen Zhong's warning about palace-channel power.

> "您就是我的老师" - Xun Shu's praise after meeting the young Huang Xian.

> "无法说清是澄清还是混浊" - Guo Tai's image for Huang Xian's depth.
