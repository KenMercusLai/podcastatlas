## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] Bytes episode has [[StephanieHughes]] interview [[JoannaStern]] about three governance-facing technology stories: [[Anthropic]] restricting access to [[ClaudeMethosPreview|Claude-Methos Preview]], residents of [[PortWashingtonWisconsin|Port Washington, Wisconsin]] voting for direct approval of some large data-center incentives, and [[NASA]] turning [[Artemis2|Artemis 2]] into an always-visible livestream and social-media event.

The episode's through line is public trust in powerful infrastructure. AI vulnerability discovery creates [[FrontierModelReleaseGovernance]] and [[FrontierModelAccessRestrictions]] problems, data centers create local consent and benefit-sharing fights, and space exploration increasingly depends on [[SpaceMissionLivestreaming]] to keep distant missions politically and culturally present.

## Key Claims
- [[Anthropic]] announced [[ClaudeMethosPreview|Claude-Methos Preview]], described in the episode as especially strong at finding security vulnerabilities.
- The model was not released to the general public; Anthropic instead gave access to more than 40 companies and technology organizations through [[ProjectGlasswing]].
- The named access recipients include [[Google]], [[JPMorganChase|JPMorgan Chase]], and [[Cisco]].
- [[JoannaStern]] frames the capability as useful for finding old exploits and vulnerabilities that may otherwise remain hidden.
- The same capability is treated as frightening because a model that can find exploits could also help attackers compromise systems.
- Stern says [[OpenAI]] has also indicated that a future model may receive a similar staged or restricted release path.
- The episode links restricted model rollout to wider AI concerns including mass surveillance and weapons of mass destruction.
- Stern says cybersecurity work may evolve like coding work, with humans managing and directing AI agents rather than performing every task by hand.
- Residents in [[PortWashingtonWisconsin|Port Washington, Wisconsin]] voted for a measure requiring voter approval before some large tax incentives for developers can proceed.
- The measure could restrict future large data-center development, but the episode says an existing large data-center project in the city would not be affected.
- The data-center segment ties local pushback to environmental impact, grid strain, higher power costs, water use for cooling, taxes, and local burdens.
- The episode frames data centers as nationally strategic for U.S. AI competition but locally disruptive when communities do not control costs, incentives, and benefits.
- The [[Artemis2|Artemis 2]] segment says four astronauts on the test flight around the [[Moon]] traveled about 250,000 miles from Earth, farther than any humans before.
- NASA's continuous [[YouTube]] coverage, viral iPhone photos, and astronaut social-media clips are framed as ways to renew public interest in space programs.
- The closing promo for [[HowWeSurvive]] connects climate solutions to geoengineering, stratospheric balloons, sunshades, and space-economy questions.

## Key Quotes
> "self-edit" - the episode's frame for AI companies deciding what capability not to release broadly.

> "anti-data center referendum" - the Politico description quoted for the Port Washington vote.

> "floating microphone" - one example Stern gives of astronaut social-media moments traveling beyond the mission feed.
