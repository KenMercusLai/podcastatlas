# AMA #11: Improve Task Switching & Productivity and Reduce Brain Fog

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] premium AMA excerpt has [[AndrewHuberman]] answer how to schedule enough resistance and cardiovascular work for health. Its practical contribution is a [[FlexibleWeeklyFitnessScaffold]]: aim for three resistance sessions, three cardiovascular sessions, and one complete rest day, while allowing sessions to move, combine, or occasionally drop when sleep, illness, stress, travel, family obligations, or recovery make the ideal week a poor fit.

## Key Claims
- The source's default week contains three resistance sessions, three cardiovascular sessions, and one complete rest day, with most sessions lasting no more than about 60-75 minutes.
- Resistance work covers legs, torso pushing and pulling, and smaller body parts; cardiovascular work covers longer outdoor movement, moderate work, and a short high-intensity or VO2-max-oriented session.
- [[FlexibleWeeklyFitnessScaffold]] treats those weekly targets as a planning scaffold rather than a rigid day-by-day rule: sessions can shift by a day or occasionally be combined.
- [[ExerciseRecoveryReadiness]] constrains the schedule because sleep deprivation, illness, major stress, soreness, recent training, nutrition, and available time can justify rest or a different session.
- Training on consecutive days is paired with later recovery, and injury-aware movement selection takes precedence over preserving a nominal split.
- Morning training is a preference rather than a universal optimum; afternoon training is acceptable, and a regular workout time is described as potentially creating anticipatory energy.
- Consistency is defined as completing roughly 85-95% of planned sessions over time, not preserving every workout at the expense of sleep, health, travel, or family life.
