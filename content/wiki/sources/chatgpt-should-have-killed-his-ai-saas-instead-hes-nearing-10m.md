# ChatGPT Should Have Killed His AI SaaS. Instead, He's Nearing $10M

## Summary

This [[TheSaaSPodcast]] interview has [[OmerKhan]] speak with [[AlanTalmor]] about moving [[AskAIMosaic|Ask AI]] from a general enterprise assistant toward [[MosaicAI]]'s specialized technical-support platform after ChatGPT made the broader category look increasingly commoditized. The case connects [[AIApplicationSurvivalStrategy]], [[EnterpriseDesignPartnerValidation]], [[FounderLedSales]], [[EnterprisePOCDiscipline]], and [[EnterpriseTechnicalSupportAutomation]]: narrow around an expensive and technically demanding workflow, validate beyond one design partner, sell measurable cost and productivity outcomes, and use historical tickets to make the initial proof concrete.

## Key Claims

- Talmor says Ask AI connected roughly 100 enterprise systems, sold to companies including [[MondayCom|monday.com]], and approached $1 million in ARR before the team concluded that general-purpose enterprise assistants would be difficult to defend against model providers.
- The company selected technical support because the work joins product knowledge, code, logs, customer context, troubleshooting, and cross-system coordination while carrying a large and measurable operating cost.
- Talmor reports that Mosaic serves about 100 customers, has seven-figure revenue approaching eight figures, and employs roughly 70-100 people; these are founder-reported figures rather than independently verified financials.
- Yotpo acted as an early design partner after an executive described fragmented knowledge across CRM, support, Confluence, Jira, Slack, and other systems; the prototype period reportedly lasted about a year and included stated willingness to pay around $30,000 annually.
- Talmor recommends testing a design partner's problem with several other companies quickly and prefers mid-market access when possible because very large enterprises can pull a startup into non-transferable custom work.
- Talmor personally drove calls, email, relationships, and outsourced SDR support for roughly the first 25 customers and continued founder-led selling until the company neared $1 million in ARR.
- Mosaic positions against internal development by selling an end-to-end business result rather than a feature comparison, including source-reported targets such as 50-70% support-cost reduction.
- A five-day pilot can analyze roughly 1,000 historical tickets, estimate automation opportunities and savings, and assemble case context, evidence, logs, and a proposed response for engineer approval.
- The pilot is diagnostic rather than a complete rollout; integrations, adoption, IT work, and change management remain after initial proof.
- Mosaic favors tickets handled per support representative over resolution time or raw ticket volume because those alternatives can move for reasons unrelated to productivity.
- Talmor says disclosing a prospective co-founder's departure during fundraising cost some investor interest but helped preserve trust; the company ultimately raised a reported $6.5 million seed round.

## Key Quotes

> "Automate wherever possible." - Talmor's concise operating advice across product, internal work, and the customer offering.
