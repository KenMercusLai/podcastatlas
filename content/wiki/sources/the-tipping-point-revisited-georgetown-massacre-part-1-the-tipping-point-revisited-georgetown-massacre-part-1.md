# The Tipping Point Revisited: Georgetown Massacre Part 1

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode opens [[USVKhoury|U.S. v. Khoury]], the final case associated with the Varsity Blues investigation. [[TimothyDonovan]] described an agreement under which [[AymanKhoury]] paid $200,000 for a Georgetown tennis recruiting slot for [[CatherineKhoury]], while Khoury maintained that the payment followed the relevant events and was an act of generosity rather than a bribe.

The defense changed the trial's moral frame through [[BrendaSmithGeorgetown|Brenda Smith]]'s cross-examination. Emails discussing property values, wealthy families, and donor potential undermined a simple claim that [[GeorgetownUniversity]] kept fundraising wholly separate from athletic recruitment and admissions, sharpening both [[AdmissionsFundraisingEntanglement]] and the [[BriberyInstitutionalInfluenceBoundary|boundary between bribery and institutionally accepted financial influence]].

## Key Claims

- Prosecutors portrayed [[GordonErnst]] as a Georgetown tennis coach who sold recruiting slots and alleged that the Khoury payment bought one of them.
- [[TimothyDonovan]] testified that he collected $180,000 in cash from Khoury, retained $20,000, and delivered the remainder to Ernst's wife.
- Donovan said Catherine was not qualified for Georgetown-level tennis and did not intend to play, creating an evidentiary conflict with the expectations she later described in Part 2.
- [[BrendaSmithGeorgetown]] initially denied lobbying admissions or sharing prospective recruits' family wealth, but emails discussed home values, donor capacity, and affluent athletic prospects.
- The emails weakened Georgetown's claimed separation of fundraising from admissions without showing that the university authorized a private cash payment to a coach.
- [[LeoKatz]] used the distinction between *malum in se* and *malum prohibitum* and a tennis-camp hypothetical to ask why some wealth-mediated admission paths are accepted while a direct exchange is criminalized.
- [[TrialNarrativeReframing]] began with institutional context in Part 1; Part 2 later supplied a more direct alternative explanation of disputed intent.
