# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》689-2丨古今对话：解锁《鬼吹灯》中精绝古国的史学之谜

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses popular interest in Jingjue from fiction and screen adaptations as an entry point into the historical [[JingjueKingdom|精绝古国]]. It ties Jingjue's reality to Han textual records, the [[NiyaSite|尼雅遗址]], [[KharosthiNiyaDocuments|尼雅佉卢文文书]], and a Han wooden-slip identification, then treats [[SupiRegime|苏毗政权]] pressure as a stronger disappearance explanation than a simple deforestation-and-water-loss story. The episode ends by setting up the [[FiveStarsOutOfTheEastBrocade|五星出东方织锦]] episode that follows.

## Key Claims
- Jingjue was a real small Western Regions oasis polity, not only a modern fantasy setting; the episode cites a Han-record scale of 480 households, 3,360 people, and 500 soldiers.
- Its strategic importance came from its position on the Silk Road southern route and from its placement inside the [[WesternRegionsProtectorate|西域都护府]] frame.
- The [[NiyaSite|Niya Site]] anchors the historical argument because Kharosthi and Han wooden documents connect the ruins to the ancient Jingjue polity.
- [[AurelStein|Aurel Stein]] appears as both a discovery figure and a cultural-loss figure: his collecting made Niya known to foreign scholarship while also removing large batches of documents and artifacts.
- The episode challenges a single-cause ecological-collapse story by foregrounding Kharosthi letters that report attacks, population seizures, and horse seizures by a force identified as [[SupiRegime|Supi]].
- Supi is described as a powerful West Qiang polity associated with the Eastern Women's Kingdom tradition, which the episode links to [[Xuanzang]] and later [[JourneyToTheWest|《西游记》]] memory.
- The episode treats Supi's role in Jingjue's disappearance as a high-probability inference from documents and power comparison, not as a fully proven conclusion.

## Key Quotes
> "汉精绝王承书从" - the reported Han wooden-slip wording used to identify Niya with Jingjue.

> "Surpiece" - the Kharosthi-document enemy name the episode associates with Supi.

> "五星出东方利中国" - the brocade text used as the bridge to Hanji 689-3.

## Contradictions
- Textual discrepancy: Hanji 689-2 gives the brocade phrase as "五星出东方利中国," while the existing [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-689-3-wuxing-chu-dongfang-li-zhongguo-yige-gulao-yuyan-de-xiandai-jiedu-lqccxzbvg-jziurfxm5tr5k4jlvq|Hanji 689-3]] source note uses "五星出东方，立中国." The wiki keeps the exact character unresolved at the source-note layer.
- No settled contradiction was identified on Jingjue's disappearance. Hanji 689-2 narrows emphasis toward Supi invasion, while Hanji 689-3 keeps Jingjue's disappearance multi-causal through lost Han protection, regional pressure, and environmental stress.
