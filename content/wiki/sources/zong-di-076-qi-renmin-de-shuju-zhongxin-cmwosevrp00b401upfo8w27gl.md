# 总第076期：人民的数据中心

## Summary

This [[DuBaoTeleread|读报teleread / 独报]] episode traces data centers from a relatively specialized environmental issue into a household-affordability and electoral issue. Its central synthesis is distributional: the same facility can create construction work, land appreciation, and public revenue while exposing nearby residents and wider ratepayers to noise, water use, grid expansion, tax concessions, and electricity-price risk.

The episode strengthens [[DataCenterBacklash]], [[DataCenterCostShifting]], and [[DataCenterCommunityConsent]] without reducing them to a technology-company-versus-public binary. People occupy overlapping roles as workers, residents, taxpayers, electricity customers, and users of digital services; a shared material setting does not create one shared interest. Those interests become politically consequential through organizing, public review, journalism, regulation, and voting.

## Key Claims

- Low-frequency cooling noise and water-intensive cooling make an abstract digital service physically legible to host communities.
- Construction can create substantial temporary work and secondary local spending, while the completed facility may require relatively few permanent employees.
- Tax incentives should be evaluated against the jobs, tax base, public facilities, and other benefits that actually persist after construction.
- Data-center load can require generation, transmission, distribution, and reserve capacity before future demand is certain, creating stranded-infrastructure risk if anticipated AI use does not materialize.
- Household electricity exposure depends less on raw consumption alone than on how utilities allocate the cost of new and potentially underused capacity.
- Secrecy and accelerated legislation weaken [[DataCenterCommunityConsent]] even when a project later produces real local gains.
- Data-center opposition can affect candidate choice without fully replacing voters' prior ideology or other concerns such as inflation, immigration, and government performance.
- A resident can simultaneously benefit from data-center work or services and oppose higher bills, pollution, noise, weak disclosure, or excessive subsidies.
