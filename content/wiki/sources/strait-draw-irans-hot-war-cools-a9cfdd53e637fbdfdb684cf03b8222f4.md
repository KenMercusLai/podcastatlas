# Strait draw: Iran’s hot war cools

## Summary

This [[EconomistPodcasts]] episode describes an [[IranUnstableStalemate]] in which active fighting has subsided but the [[StraitOfHormuz]] blockade, nuclear dispute, proxy attacks, and escalation risks remain unresolved. It then presents [[Bangladesh]] as a case of [[PoliticallyConnectedBankingCrisis]], where bad loans and frozen credit threaten earlier development gains, before reporting [[Vals]] research that associates profanity in hidden model reasoning with failed rather than improved task performance through [[AIReasoningProfanityFailure]].

## Key Claims

- The Iran conflict is described as an unstable stalemate: active attacks have eased, but the Hormuz blockade, limited negotiations, Iranian nuclear threats, and pressure from the [[Houthis]] leave several routes back to open war.
- The source says war damage and lost oil revenue give [[Iran]] reasons to seek an end, while the regime's continued operation and public defiance reduce the immediate pressure to compromise.
- It portrays the [[UnitedStates]] as bearing higher oil prices, inflation, enforcement costs, and electoral damage while still preferring the impasse to a bargain it considers unnecessary.
- Gulf states are said to prefer a damaging cold-war-like standoff to renewed combat after absorbing infrastructure, economic, and reputational harm from a war they did not initiate.
- The episode attributes roughly 30% of Bangladeshi bank loans being non-performing largely to politically connected lending under [[SheikhHasina]], with state-owned and Islamic banks carrying much of the burden.
- It says frozen bank credit has accompanied a fall in annual growth from about 6% to roughly 2% and renewed poverty, while recapitalization could require resources equivalent to around 10% of GDP.
- External support from the [[InternationalMonetaryFund]], [[WorldBank]], or possibly [[India]] is presented as a possible route to recapitalization, but stricter loss recognition and subsidy cuts could intensify near-term protests.
- The [[Vals]] segment reports more profanity in the hidden reasoning of some models trained after July 2026 and says runs containing profanity usually fail, making swearing a possible symptom of a model becoming stuck rather than a reasoning aid.
