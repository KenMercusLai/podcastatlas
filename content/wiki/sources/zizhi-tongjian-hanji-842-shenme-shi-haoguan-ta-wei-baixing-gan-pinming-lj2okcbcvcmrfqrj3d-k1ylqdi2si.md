# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》842丨什么是好官？他为百姓敢拼命！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 156 CE [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Huan-era]] layer immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-843-gudai-nongmin-pinkun-de-genyuan-shi-shenme-lutep8yyzanhlb4ao6sope-8edvy|Hanji 843]]. It centers [[HanShaoLateHan|韩韶]] as a crisis-fit county magistrate: thieves avoid Ying county after his appointment, displaced households flee there, and he opens public granaries despite procedural risk.

The wider episode links this local "good official" standard to frontier and military appointment fit. [[TanshihuaiXianbei|檀石槐]] is introduced as an organized Xianbei leader, [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]] is appointed Duliao general after a Yunzhong attack, and [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] defeats Xianbei through a forged-recall ambush before later suppressing the Gongsun Ju and Dongguo Dou rebellion.

## Key Claims

- The episode defines a "good official" by crisis conduct: saving people and accepting personal risk matter more than title or procedural self-protection.
- Han Shao's reputation functions as local security capacity because bandits avoid Ying county and refugees treat it as a safe destination.
- Emergency grain release is presented as legitimate when war and hunger create immediate survival risk, especially once the Taishan administrator supports the decision.
- The [[YingchuanFourMagistrates|颍川四长]] label turns Han Shao's case into a wider county-level ideal of virtuous, people-benefiting governance.
- Tanshihuai's early Xianbei authority rests on law, dispute settlement, trust, and organized expansion rather than raiding alone.
- Li Ying and Duan Jiong show two other crisis-fit modes: frontier prestige that lowers conflict cost, and battlefield improvisation that works while carrying legality risk.
- The closing disasters, Southern Xiongnu rebellion, and Liang-family rewards keep the local relief story inside a broader late-Han field of state stress.
