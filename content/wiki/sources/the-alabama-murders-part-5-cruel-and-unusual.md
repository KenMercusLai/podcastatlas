# The Alabama Murders - Part 5: Cruel and Unusual

## Summary

This episode of [[TheAlabamaMurders]] reconstructs Alabama's failed 2022 attempt to execute [[KennethSmith]] by lethal injection after his jury recommended life imprisonment without parole and the trial judge imposed death instead. It uses [[JoelZivot]]'s medical analysis, earlier access failures, protocol secrecy, and Smith's reported experience to argue that [[MedicalizedExecutionOpacity]] can make an execution appear calm while concealing uncertain competence, invasive procedures, pain, and panic.

The episode follows Smith through hours of restraint, repeated arm punctures, and attempted central-line placement before the death warrant expired. Alabama then paused executions but, in the episode's account, principally responded by expanding the execution window rather than resolving the transparency and competence concerns that had accompanied the failures.

## Key Claims

- Smith's jury recommended life imprisonment without parole by an 11-1 vote, but the trial judge overrode that recommendation and sentenced him to death.
- Alabama's protocol called for ordinary intravenous access and then a central line, while withholding the execution team's identities, qualifications, training, and experience.
- Zivot says dehydration, illness, terror, long imprisonment, and non-cooperation can make intravenous access difficult, while professional ethics deter highly skilled clinicians from participating in executions.
- A second autopsy of Joe Nathan James reportedly found repeated punctures and an unauthorized surgical cutdown performed while he was alive; officials later failed to establish access before Alan Miller's warrant expired.
- After late litigation ended, three IV-team members repeatedly attempted arm access on Smith and another person attempted a central line beneath his collarbone.
- Smith reported searing pain, breathing difficulty, panic, and repeated movement of a long needle in his chest while the preparation remained outside ordinary witness view.
- The state abandoned the attempt near midnight after Smith had spent approximately three and a half to four hours restrained and was reportedly unable to walk or dress without assistance.
- Alabama paused executions and reviewed its procedures, but the principal change described by the episode was extending the time available to complete an execution.

## Key Quotes

> “prodding and poking” - Alabama Attorney General Steve Marshall's characterization of the intravenous-access attempts, as reported by the episode.

## Contradictions

- The episode contrasts Alabama's claim that Joe Nathan James's execution proceeded according to plan with reported autopsy evidence of repeated access attempts and an unauthorized cutdown.
- It contrasts the state's description of Smith's experience as ordinary IV “prodding and poking” with Smith's report of prolonged restraint, severe pain, breathing difficulty, panic, and repeated central-line attempts.
- No settled contradiction is adopted about the legality or competence of specific personnel. The source does not disclose their identities or qualifications, and the episode's inference that the team recognized its own inadequacy is explicitly speculative.
- The account is openly critical of Alabama and gives limited space to the state's defense beyond the attorney general's comments. Medical findings, protocol details, chronology, legal rulings, Smith's symptoms, dialogue, staff competence, and institutional motives remain episode-scoped because the supplied file is a structured summary rather than a transcript, autopsy, protocol, court record, medical report, or developed state response.
