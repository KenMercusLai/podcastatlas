# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》333-2｜他爱打皇帝的脸，为何能善终

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-333-1-men-ke-luoque-de-youlai-ni-zhidao-ma-lkoajhp2b_k32aurnk8rs-ukxaib|Hanji 333-1]] by moving [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] from social reversal into office, remonstrance, and local governance. After private minting troubles follow the replacement of three-zhu coin with five-zhu coin, [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] recalls Ji An and sends him to Huaiyang; Ji asks to remain near court as a zhonglang, but the emperor instead uses his reputation to settle strained official-popular relations.

Before leaving, Ji gives [[LiXiWesternHan|李息]] a severe warning about [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]]: Zhang allegedly blocks correction, decorates error, follows imperial preference, and uses legal manipulation and cruel officials to build authority. Li does not report the warning, while the later Zhang Tang sequence in Hanji 338-2 through 339-2 supplies the downfall only anticipated here. The closing branch introduces Chancellor [[ZhuangQingdiWesternHan|庄青翟]] as the nominal superior whom Zhang's direct access and practical influence hollow out, grounding [[NominalOfficePracticalPowerMismatch|名位—实权错位]] as a distinct court-risk pattern.

## Key Claims

- Private coin casting and disorder in Huaiyang form the immediate setting for Han Wudi's recall and outward assignment of Ji An.
- Ji asks to remain at the imperial center as a zhonglang who can correct omissions, while Wudi insists that Ji's reputation is enough to stabilize Huaiyang without exhausting labor.
- The episode presents Ji's reluctance as both a health concern and awareness that departure from court reduces his access to state deliberation.
- Ji warns Li Xi that Zhang Tang uses intelligence and rhetoric to reject advice, follows the ruler's preferences rather than public principle, and enlarges punishment through legal manipulation.
- Li Xi stays silent from fear; the episode treats Zhang's later fall as confirming Ji's diagnosis, though this source does not narrate the intervening case.
- Ji reportedly settles Huaiyang, is promoted to a kingdom chancellor, dies in office seven years later, and leaves a clean reputation that benefits his family.
- Zhuang Qingdi becomes chancellor by inherited status and prior office, but Wudi's direct reliance on Zhang Tang leaves the formally subordinate censorial official with greater practical influence.

## Key Quotes

> “高枕而卧” - the episode's compact description of Han Wudi's claim that Ji An's reputation could govern without constant exertion.

> “足智以拒谏、言足以饰非” - Ji An's attributed diagnosis of Zhang Tang's political and legal method.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. This episode anticipates Zhang Tang's fall; Hanji 338-2, 339-1, and 339-2 later supply the accusation, coalition, suicide, and reversal sequence.
- Ji An's condemnation of Zhang as treacherous and legally manipulative does not by itself contradict Hanji 339-1 and 339-2's claims that Zhang was personally frugal and left a documented modest estate. Financial cleanliness, legal severity, political alignment, and interpersonal conduct are separate questions.
- Chronology caution: this source places the initial five-zhu change and Huaiyang private minting before the later centralized mint system represented in Hanji 340-2; it should not be read as evidence that central monopoly had already eliminated local production.
