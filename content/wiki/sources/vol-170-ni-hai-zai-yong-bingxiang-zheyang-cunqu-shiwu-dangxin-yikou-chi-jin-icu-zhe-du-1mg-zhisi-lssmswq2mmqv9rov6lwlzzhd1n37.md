# VOL.170你还在用冰箱这样存取食物？当心一口吃进ICU！这毒1mg致死？

## Summary
This [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode has host [[Atang|阿汤]] and intensive-care physician [[ZihanDoctor|子涵医生]] explain why refrigeration delays rather than eliminates food-safety risk. Its main contribution is [[HouseholdRefrigeratorFoodSafety|household refrigerator food safety]]: time and temperature, raw-versus-ready separation, sealed storage, refrigerator thawing, portioning, cleaning, and timely disposal work together, while appearance and smell alone cannot exclude pathogens or preformed toxins. The closing barbecue discussion links heavy eating and alcohol to [[AcutePancreatitisEmergencyEscalation]] but remains general public education rather than individualized medical guidance.

## Key Claims
- Refrigeration and freezing slow many microbial processes but do not sterilize food or make storage indefinite; the episode names cold-tolerant organisms including Listeria, Salmonella, and Yersinia as examples.
- Household storage works as a system: keep raw meat and seafood away from cooked or ready-to-eat food, use sealed containers, account for warmer door zones and more stable colder areas, and clean accumulated spills and expired food.
- Frozen meat and seafood should preferably thaw in the refrigerator rather than at room temperature or in warm water; portioning before freezing reduces the need for repeated thawing and refreezing.
- “Overnight” is treated as a time-and-temperature problem rather than a calendar label. Prompt cooling and short storage matter more than whether food literally crossed midnight.
- The episode gives stronger discard advice for leafy vegetables, seafood, long-soaked wood ear mushrooms, fermented rice or starch products, and opened coconut water, but the exact time limits and food-specific mechanisms remain source-scoped.
- Sensory inspection is insufficient for some hazards because contaminated food can look and smell normal and some toxins may survive reheating; prevention therefore cannot be replaced by a final smell test or boiling step.
- Children, older adults, pregnant people, and immunocompromised people are described as more vulnerable to severe foodborne illness, while fever, persistent vomiting or diarrhea, dehydration, altered consciousness, cyanosis, or shock require prompt professional assessment.
- The closing summer-night-food branch warns that alcohol, heavy or high-fat meals, sugary drinks, and sleep disruption can coincide with pancreatitis, glucose crises, and cardiovascular emergencies, without making one meal diagnostic proof.
