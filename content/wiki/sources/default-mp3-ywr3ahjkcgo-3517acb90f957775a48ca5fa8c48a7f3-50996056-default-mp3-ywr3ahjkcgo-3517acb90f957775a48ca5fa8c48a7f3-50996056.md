# Group Think

## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode has social psychologist [[JayVanBavel|Jay Van Bavel]] explain [[GroupIdentity|group identity]] as a context-sensitive lens on preference, perception, reward, threat, and belonging. Minimal-group experiments, sports judgments, political conflict, pandemic behavior, and social-media feedback show that even thin categories can produce sincere favoritism and selective attention, while the same flexibility allows leaders, representative institutions, admired members, and shared goals to redraw who counts as “us.”

The constructive branch develops [[SuperordinateIdentityCooperation]] through [[NelsonMandela]]'s embrace of the [[Springboks]], Mohamed Salah's visibility at Liverpool, and mixed Christian-Muslim football teams in northern Iraq. These cases do not show that symbols or contact automatically eliminate prejudice; they suggest that common membership works best when it includes cooperation, interdependence, credible leadership, and visible success.

## Key Claims
- [[GroupIdentity]] can become salient when context makes a category distinctive, then shape consumer preference, sensory judgment, attention, reward, and social belonging.
- Arbitrary assignment can produce in-group liking and resource favoritism even when the category has no prior history or substantive meaning.
- Identity-biased observers can sincerely interpret the same smell, disputed goal, distance, threat, or body-camera footage differently rather than knowingly falsifying what they see.
- Political identity can become moralized and personal, increasing out-group dislike, relationship avoidance, selective risk perception, and support for distrusted leaders opposed to a feared rival.
- [[AlgorithmicAngerEngagement]] can reinforce division when moral-emotional language and attacks on an out-group receive more sharing and visible feedback.
- Crises can create solidarity, but common threat alone does not guarantee unity; leadership and cooperative framing help determine whether boundaries widen or harden.
- [[SuperordinateIdentityCooperation]] can reduce prejudice when former outsiders become valued teammates, institutions visibly include different groups, and people depend on one another for a shared goal.
- [[NelsonMandela]]'s Springbok strategy illustrates how a leader can transform an exclusionary symbol into a broader national invitation without proving that symbolic unity resolves structural inequality.
