## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty-Corino]] interview [[KayleeRobbins]] after an unexpected [[MetaAI|Meta AI]] exchange made Robbins investigate how social platforms could connect information about her family. Her response is neither total withdrawal nor unchanged use: she removes identifiable child information from public social media, limits app permissions, continues using AI more cautiously, and moves family sharing toward [[Tinybeans]].

The episode's strongest contribution is the distinction between isolated disclosure and [[NetworkedFamilyPrivacy]]. Information about a child can be distributed across parents, relatives, friends, and groups, while [[AIDataAggregationPrivacyRisk]] arises when a system assembles those fragments much faster than an individual investigator could.

## Key Claims
- [[KayleeRobbins]] says a built-in Meta AI interaction led her to investigate social-media privacy; [[Meta]] said the interaction missed the mark and that the responsible issue was fixed.
- Robbins received both community support and criticism that blamed her for posting family material, and she accepts some responsibility while describing much of the backlash as harsh.
- Her request that relatives remove her children's photos exposed a conflict between child privacy and family members' use of [[Facebook]] as a long-distance digital scrapbook.
- Robbins recommends withholding names, addresses, schools, landmarks, and other details that could identify or locate a child rather than insisting that every parent stop posting all child photos.
- She plans to keep using AI, but with stricter limits on uploads and app access to resources such as her camera and phone records.
- Robbins says Meta AI surfaced information from friends, family members, and groups within seconds, making aggregation speed—not merely the existence of public fragments—the central risk.
- Robbins entered a paid partnership with [[Tinybeans]], where she continues sharing family material in a more restricted setting.

## Key Quotes
> "There has to be boundaries on what it can access." — Robbins on continuing to use AI after the incident.

> "It took it seconds." — Robbins on the speed with which Meta AI assembled information from other accounts and groups.
