# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》229｜汉文帝之死遗诏藏着什么秘密？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode closes [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s reign through his 157 BCE death and a will that treats death as natural while converting mourning, burial, mobilization, and palace-household arrangements into bounded public policy. The title's “secret” is therefore not an assassination claim: the source explicitly says Liu Heng died after illness and locates the durable meaning in short mourning, thin burial, limited ceremony, and avoidance of popular disruption.

The retrospective then connects the will to Liu Heng's refusal of an expensive terrace, plain clothing, restricted court display, modest Baling grave goods, acceptance of remonstrance, lenient treatment of officials, agrarian priority, and light burdens. These anecdotes make [[FrugalImperialBurialRestraint|节俭帝陵约束]] part of a wider governing posture that the episode credits with laying foundations for [[WenJingProsperityTradeoff|文景之治]], while exact wording, chronology, causal reach, and character judgments remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- The episode dates Liu Heng's death at Weiyang Palace to the first day of the sixth month in 157 BCE and attributes it to illness rather than murder, usurpation, or forced removal.
- His will begins from the naturalness of death and argues that lavish burial and prolonged mourning can ruin households and injure ordinary life.
- The will reportedly limits formal wailing to three days, relaxes food and ritual prohibitions, avoids mass popular attendance, vehicles, and troops, and sharply shortens later mourning periods.
- Baling is to retain its natural terrain, and palace women are to return to their families; Liu Heng is buried there six days after his death in the episode's chronology.
- Refusal to build a terrace costing one hundred jin of gold, plain clothing, unembroidered furnishings, and ceramic rather than precious-metal burial goods are presented as evidence that the will continues a long-standing governing style.
- The episode associates Liu Heng's frugality, agricultural emphasis, lighter corvée and taxation, receptiveness to criticism, and non-punitive correction with the recovery later remembered as Wen-Jing rule.
- [[HanJingdi|刘启 / 汉景帝]] succeeds at age thirty-one in the episode's account; the final notice about the Changsha kingdom is incomplete because the supplied transcript breaks off.

## Key Quotes

> “万物之萌生，靡不有死。” - the natural-death premise attributed to Liu Heng's will.

> “毋禁取妇、嫁女、祠祀、饮酒、食肉者。” - the will's reported refusal to suspend ordinary social and ritual life broadly.

## Connections

- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - dying ruler whose will joins personal restraint to limits on state and household burden.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - crown-prince successor who inherits the Wen-era settlement after Liu Heng's death.
- [[FrugalImperialBurialRestraint|节俭帝陵约束]] - broadened here by a Western Han case that regulates mourning time, public mobilization, grave form, and burial material.
- [[WenJingProsperityTradeoff|文景之治的繁荣代价]] - later outcome frame whose recovery side gains a direct Wen-era frugality and light-burden antecedent.
- [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] - general recalled in the opening through the preceding Xiliu-camp inspection episode.
- [[DengTong|邓通]] - favored official from another Liu Heng source, qualifying any portrait of the ruler as uniformly detached from personal patronage.
- [[WesternHanDynasty|Western Han]] - dynastic setting for the death, succession, and recovery claims.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode's death account explicitly rejects an intrigue explanation and presents illness as the immediate cause.
- Text and chronology caution: the will's exact wording, mourning categories, group size, dates, age, burial sequence, terrace price, household-wealth comparison, Baling construction, grave goods, and quotations require comparison with transmitted texts and archaeological evidence.
- Character caution: the claim that the examples prove a consistently frugal, benevolent, and remonstrance-receptive life is the episode's retrospective synthesis; the existing [[DengTong|邓通]] patronage branch prevents treating restraint as uniform across every relationship or resource decision.
- Name caution: the transcript's “张五” is not normalized into a canonical official page because the intended identity and the bribery anecdote require source comparison.
- Scope caution: the source does not quantify how much Liu Heng's policies caused Wen-Jing prosperity, and its closing Changsha-king passage is incomplete.
