## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[JaredRutter]] about mitochondria, metabolism, and cellular energy allocation. Rutter argues that whole-body metabolism is a constellation of cell-specific metabolic programs, where nutrients can be routed toward ATP production, biomass construction, lactate export, fat use, or disease-linked growth depending on cell type and state. The episode adds [[CellularMetabolismMosaic]], [[MitochondrialEnergyAllocation]], [[MitochondrialPyruvateCarrier]], [[LactateMetabolicShuttle]], [[CancerMetabolicReprogramming]], and [[ReactiveOxygenSpeciesMetabolicStress]] while keeping diagnostics, aging, cancer-treatment, fuel-prioritization, and scent-based disease-detection claims source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- [[CellularMetabolismMosaic]] reframes "metabolism" as the combined output of many cell-level programs rather than a single body-wide calorie rate.
- [[MitochondrialEnergyAllocation]] captures the episode's burn-versus-build frame: nutrients such as glucose-derived pyruvate can be oxidized for ATP or diverted into cellular material.
- [[MitochondrialPyruvateCarrier]] records Rutter's MPC1/MPC2 work showing how pyruvate entry into mitochondria shapes glucose oxidation and disease-linked cardiac growth.
- [[LactateMetabolicShuttle]] qualifies the older waste-product story by treating lactate as an exportable and burnable fuel, including for heart cells.
- [[CancerMetabolicReprogramming]] explains cancer metabolism as resource reallocation by mutated self cells rather than simply broken mitochondria.
- [[ReactiveOxygenSpeciesMetabolicStress]] links excess mitochondrial energy to reactive oxygen species, protein and genome damage, aging hypotheses, and disease risk.
- The episode repeatedly preserves uncertainty around aging mechanisms, cellular-resolution metabolic imaging, breath or scent diagnostics, and personalized combination cancer therapy.
