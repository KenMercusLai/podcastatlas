# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》336-1｜战神霍去病为何24岁突然暴毙？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places [[HuoQubing|霍去病]]'s death in 117 BCE beside [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s royal enfeoffments, widespread illicit coin production, and a six-inspector circuit sent to investigate land annexation and unlawful officials. Its central contribution is evidentiary rather than diagnostic: a disease report survives without a named illness, while epidemic, accident, murder, and imperial-involvement stories lack direct support in the compact record.

The episode treats Huo's youth, victories against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]], high office, imperial favor, and sudden death as reasons later audiences seek a dramatic explanation. It nevertheless rejects certainty where the record is silent, making the case an example of [[SparseRecordDeathCauseInference|史料稀缺下的死因推断]] rather than proof of a specific disease or killer.

## Key Claims

- The episode dates Huo Qubing's death to the autumn of 117 BCE and calls him twenty-three in the body, while the title calls him twenty-four.
- It reports that Han Wudi grieved and had Huo's tomb built in a form modeled on the Qilian Mountains.
- Huo's compressed military arc is given as first command and fame at seventeen, appointment as Piaoqi general at nineteen, and renewed victory with [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] followed by Dasima rank at twenty-one.
- The most widespread and official account is illness, which the episode attributes to Chu Shaosun's supplement to the *Jianyuan yilai houzhe nianbiao* through a statement associated with [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]]; no disease is named.
- The epidemic theory is challenged because the cited Huo and Xiongnu biographies do not report a corresponding large outbreak and because the proposed contaminated-water exposure precedes Huo's death by about two years.
- Accident and murder remain unproved: the episode argues that a violent death of such a prominent commander would be unusually difficult to reconcile with total narrative silence, but silence itself does not establish natural death.
- The opening political-economic material says private minting had become widespread despite mass executions and that imperial inspectors were sent to investigate annexation and official illegality; its currency terminology and totals remain source-scoped.

## Key Quotes

> "没有一个人能说得很详细。" - the episode's boundary around Huo Qubing's cause of death.

> "瘟疫是有潜伏期的，但是绝对不会潜伏两年。" - the host's chronological objection to the epidemic story.

## Connections

- [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] - central commander whose early death is reported more securely than its specific medical cause.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler presented as grieving Huo and commissioning a Qilian-shaped tomb.
- [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - military relationship and opponent used to explain why Huo's short life attracted later speculation.
- [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] - figure through whom the episode says the disease report is transmitted in Chu Shaosun's supplementary text.
- [[SparseRecordDeathCauseInference|史料稀缺下的死因推断]] - method for separating a death notice, a broad illness report, rejected hypotheses, and an unknown specific cause.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-336-2-huoqubing-yunluo-wudi-zhen-shi-muhou-heishou-ltfkwalc48bj1hav65bg-8qotrf5|Hanji 336-2]] - sequel that considers imperial involvement but explicitly labels it speculation.

## Contradictions

- The title says Huo died at twenty-four, while the supplied body says twenty-three; this may reflect age-counting convention or editorial framing, but the source does not resolve it.
- No settled contradiction is adopted with Hanji 336-2. This episode's illness report does not name a disease, and the sequel supplies no direct evidence of murder; together they support uncertainty rather than either a precise diagnosis or a幕后黑手 conclusion.
- The epidemic story's contaminated-water mechanism, timing, and absence from the named biographies are reported through the host's summary rather than independently verified here.
- The royal-enfeoffment procedure, private-minting death toll, currency names, inspector names, and claim that unpunished minting was nearly universal remain source-scoped because the compact transcript provides no textual apparatus.
