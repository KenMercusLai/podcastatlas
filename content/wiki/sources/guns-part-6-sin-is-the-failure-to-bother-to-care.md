# Guns Part 6: “Sin is the failure to bother to care”

## Summary

This final installment of [[RevisionistHistory]]'s gun series follows [[AbdullahPratt]], an emergency physician who returned to Chicago's South Side after gun violence killed his older brother and 15 close friends. His account connects childhood trauma, interpersonal status, weapon access, and long-running retaliation to a crisis whose routine deaths receive less national attention than mass shootings.

The episode frames indifference as [[IndifferenceAsMoralFailure|a moral failure to use one's capacity to help]]. Pratt's response is [[CommunityGunshotFirstAid]]: he and his team reportedly trained more than 7,000 young people to alert emergency services, locate bleeding, and compress a gunshot wound before professional care arrives.

## Key Claims

- [[AbdullahPratt]] combines lived bereavement, emergency-medicine experience, and community teaching in the same South Side neighborhoods where he grew up.
- [[IndifferenceAsMoralFailure]] extends responsibility beyond causing harm to cases where people and institutions have the capacity to help but do not act.
- Persistent community gun violence can be sustained by childhood trauma, grief, status competition, easy weapon access, and [[ExpressiveViolenceRetaliation|retaliation]] across small territories.
- Mass shootings receive disproportionate national attention relative to the much larger routine burden described by trauma-center workers.
- Background checks, licensing, age limits, and sensitive-place rules may address parts of the problem but cannot by themselves resolve trauma, retaliation, status, or emergency-survival mechanisms.
- [[CommunityGunshotFirstAid]] turns care into a practical bystander capacity through a simple alert, bleeding-location, and compression sequence.
- The training may improve action after a shooting, but it does not replace primary prevention, professional emergency care, or institutional responsibility.

## Key Quotes

> “Sin is the failure to bother to care.” — James Keenan's moral formulation as presented by [[MalcolmGladwell]].

## Connections

- [[AbdullahPratt]] - emergency physician, bereaved community member, and educator at the center of the episode.
- [[IndifferenceAsMoralFailure]] - moral frame connecting capacity, attention, and action.
- [[CommunityGunshotFirstAid]] - practical training response for the period before professional help arrives.
- [[ExpressiveViolenceRetaliation]] - grief, status, and revenge pathway described by Pratt.
- [[MultiInterventionGunViolencePrevention]] - portfolio frame in which law, trauma care, community action, and primary prevention address different mechanisms.
- [[VisibilityDrivenPolicyUrgency]] - mismatch between routine community harm and the shootings that dominate national attention.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The claim that laws alone are insufficient qualifies single-policy accounts without showing that firearm regulation is irrelevant.
- Training bystanders to reduce blood loss is a downstream survival intervention, not evidence that communities should bear responsibility for preventing shootings or replacing professional services.
- The annual murder total, mass-shooting share, South Side trauma-center history, number trained, childhood-bereavement prevalence, firearm modifications, and causal claims remain episode-scoped because the supplied document is a structured summary rather than a transcript, dataset, program evaluation, or policy study.
