# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》388-2｜因谗言错杀太子，汉武帝晚年最大的悔恨！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode joins two late-[[HanWudi|汉武帝]] scenes. First, it narrates the failed [[GongsunYongWesternHan|公孙勇]] and Hu Qian rebellion attempt, where impostors try to exploit local fear of [[XiuyiEnvoyImperialSurveillance|绣衣使者]] authority but are stopped by alert officials such as [[TianGuangmingWesternHan|田广明]] and [[WeiBuhaiWesternHan|魏不害]].

The second half returns to the [[WuguIncidentWesternHan|巫蛊之祸]] aftermath. After investigation shows many accusations were false, [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]] gives [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] a politically usable way to recognize [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]]'s grievance; Wudi then punishes [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]]'s clan, burns [[SuWenWesternHan|苏文]], and builds 思子宫 and 归来望思之台 as material signs of remorse.

## Key Claims

- The Gongsun Yong and Hu Qian plot is dangerous less because it already controls large forces than because it weaponizes official ceremony, forged identity, and local fear.
- [[XiuyiEnvoyImperialSurveillance|绣衣使者]] are presented as emperor-direct special investigators with secret-police-like power, able to intimidate local officials and bypass ordinary administrative hierarchy.
- [[GongsunYongWesternHan|公孙勇]]'s failure depends on low-ranking officials who resist prestige panic and verify the claim before surrendering authority.
- The episode treats [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]] as a famous绣衣 figure whose special mandate in the [[WuguIncidentWesternHan|巫蛊之祸]] helped force [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|刘据]] into desperate rebellion.
- By 90 BCE, review of the Wugu cases reveals extensive false accusations, allowing Han Wudi to recognize Liu Ju as wrongly driven into fear rather than simply rebellious.
- [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]]'s memorial works because it reframes Liu Ju's action as a father-son matter and gives Wudi a supernatural-political bridge through the dream of a white-haired elder.
- Han Wudi's destruction of Jiang Chong's clan, burning of Su Wen, pursuit of anti-Liu-Ju attackers, and mourning buildings express delayed remorse but cannot undo the deaths produced by the Wugu panic.
