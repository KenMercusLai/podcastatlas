## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] episode follows [[JesseMerrill]] from film production and experiential marketing through Teany, Honest Tea, and Activate Drinks to co-founding [[GoodCulture]] with [[AndersEisner]]. Its strongest contribution is a [[CategoryRevival]] case: a declining cottage-cheese category became newly legible through cleaner formulation, thicker texture, clearer packaging, intensive sampling, retailer-level [[SalesVelocity]], and years of creator outreach. It also sharpens [[PreparedVirality]] and [[CPGManufacturingScaleUp]] by showing that a viral TikTok recipe accelerated demand only after brand, distribution, and influencer groundwork existed, while limited dairy capacity still prevented supply from meeting demand.

## Key Claims
- Merrill learned grassroots sampling, brand storytelling, sales, operations, and supply-chain work across Teany, Honest Tea, and Activate Drinks before starting Good Culture.
- The founding insight was that cottage cheese already offered high protein and relatively low sugar but had lost relevance through limited innovation, dated presentation, and inconsistent texture.
- Merrill and Eisner positioned Good Culture around a short ingredient list, thicker texture, live cultures, responsible production, and a name connecting fermented cultures with organizational culture.
- Laboratory recipes became soupy on full-scale equipment, illustrating that formulation and industrial process had to be developed together.
- Whole Foods, Sprouts, and other Expo West buyers validated retailer interest, but Good Culture initially limited its rollout so it could support stores closely.
- Early packaging looked too much like yogurt, so the company enlarged “cottage cheese” and added a bowl image to clarify the category at shelf.
- With roughly 45 days of shelf life and retailer expectations of about eight to ten weekly units per store, sampling, expiration monitoring, and targeted brand-ambassador support were essential.
- Revenue reportedly grew from about $2 million in 2016 to about $70 million in 2021 as distribution expanded.
- Years of authentic creator seeding positioned Good Culture to benefit when Lainey Cate’s high-protein cottage-cheese ice-cream recipe spread on TikTok.
- The episode reports that Good Culture exceeded $200 million in annual revenue by the end of 2025 while manufacturing served less than half of demand.
- [[LCatterton|L Catterton]] acquired a majority stake in a transaction closing in 2026; Merrill remained CEO and described operating expertise as the main reason for the partnership.
- Merrill presents persistence and preparation as controllable inputs and virality as “the right message meeting the right moment.”

## Key Quotes
> “the right message meeting the right moment” - Merrill’s description of virality after years of preparation.

> “clean label” - the company’s shorthand for milk, cream, salt, and cultures without gums, starches, or chemical preservatives.
