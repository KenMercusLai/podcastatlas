## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode explains [[California]]'s building-code requirements for [[EmbodiedCarbonBuildingCode|embodied carbon]] in large non-residential construction. Using [[MalibuHighSchool]], [[VerticalGroup]], [[BNBuilders]], and [[EmilyEnglish]], it shows embodied-carbon compliance as a documentation and review problem that is presented as doable rather than prohibitively costly. The source's main contribution is a market-design lens: a California mandate can create visible demand for [[LowCarbonConstructionMaterials]] and push manufacturers beyond voluntary green-building niches.

## Key Claims
- Buildings account for more than a third of global greenhouse gases, and the episode distinguishes [[OperationalCarbon]] from emissions created during construction.
- [[EmbodiedCarbonBuildingCode]] shifts attention from energy use after occupancy toward materials and construction choices such as roofing, floors, steel, walls, paint, and insulation.
- [[California]] became the first state to regulate embodied carbon in its building code, and the episode says large non-residential buildings over 50,000 square feet must now comply.
- [[MalibuHighSchool]] is used as the concrete project example for reviewing lower-carbon materials.
- Builders can comply by reusing at least 45% of an existing building, showing a project emits 10% less carbon, or using materials that meet lower global-warming thresholds.
- The episode frames [[ConstructionCarbonCompliance]] as an added documentation and review hurdle, not as an inherently expensive or schedule-breaking requirement.
- [[EmilyEnglish]] of [[BNBuilders]] says clear, actionable goals make the requirement achievable.
- [[BenStapleton]] of the [[USGreenBuildingCouncilOfCalifornia|U.S. Green Building Council of California]] says mandates help manufacturers see real demand for sustainable options.
- California's market scale could make lower-carbon construction materials easier to source outside the state if manufacturers adjust to serve its code.
- The closing [[HowWeSurvive]] promo connects the building-materials discussion to broader climate-solutions reporting by [[AmyScott]].
