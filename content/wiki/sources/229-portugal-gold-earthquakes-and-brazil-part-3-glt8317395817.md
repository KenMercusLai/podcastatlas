# 229. Portugal: Gold, Earthquakes, and Brazil (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[Portugal]] from rule under the Spanish monarchy through the 1640 restoration, Brazilian mineral wealth, the 1755 Lisbon earthquake, Napoleonic invasion, and [[BrazilianIndependence]]. It presents empire as both rescue and constraint: Brazil restored wealth and global reach while slavery, commodity dependence, British commercial leverage, and weak domestic development narrowed Portuguese choices.

The episode's strongest contribution is a sequence of forced adaptation. [[MarquisDePombal|Pombal]] used catastrophe to centralize and rebuild Lisbon, while [[DomJoaoVI]] moved the court to Rio under French and British pressure, reversing the normal relation between metropole and colony and making [[DomPedroI]]'s later break with Lisbon more plausible.

## Key Claims

- The Dutch-Portuguese War exposed the vulnerability of Portugal's overseas possessions under Spanish rule and helped deepen support for the 1640 restoration.
- Catherine of Braganza's marriage to Charles II renewed the Anglo-Portuguese alliance and transferred Bombay to England, giving a dynastic settlement global consequences.
- Brazilian gold, diamonds, sugar, and enslaved labor revived Portuguese fortunes while [[ColonialCommodityDependency|discouraging broader domestic development and strengthening British commercial influence]].
- [[MarquisDePombal|Pombal]] combined Enlightenment reform with coercive centralization, weakening privileged institutions while preserving colonial slavery and harsh political methods.
- The [[LisbonEarthquake1755|1755 Lisbon earthquake, tsunami, and fire]] became both a European challenge to providential optimism and a state-capacity test answered through planned reconstruction.
- Napoleon's pressure and British naval leverage made Portugal's 1807 decision a survival calculation rather than a freely chosen alliance policy.
- [[ImperialCenterRelocation|Moving the royal court and governing apparatus to Brazil]] elevated the colony into an imperial center and helped create the dynastic route to independence under [[DomPedroI]].
- British mediation around independence joined anti-slave-trade pressure to continuing commercial advantage, while Brazil's weakly enforced 1831 law illustrates the gap between formal reform and implementation.

## Key Quotes

> "Bury the dead and heal the living" - the episode's summary of Pombal's response to the Lisbon disaster.

> "Independence or death" - the episode's rendering of Pedro's 1822 break with Lisbon.

> "A law for the British to see" - the episode's explanation of a nominal reform adopted for external appearance but weakly enforced.
