## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode extends [[ZhangJiCaoOfficialLateHan|张既]] from post-Hanzhong relocation planning into a wider frontier-governance profile. It joins military suppression and strategic patience to salt-and-iron development, agriculture, local livelihoods, and personal restraint, while keeping the reported migration scale and long-run ethnic consequences source-scoped.

The second branch follows [[LiuBeiHanzhongKingship219LateHan|刘备称汉中王后的政治与军事布局]]. [[MengDaLateHan|孟达]] and [[LiuFengLateHan|刘封]] move through Fangling and Shangyong, [[ShenDanLateHan|申耽]] submits, and [[WeiYan|魏延]] receives the Hanzhong command; the episode then argues that expansion across Yizhou, Hanzhong, Jingzhou, and Shangyong has outrun rapid mutual support, creating [[FragmentedTheaterSupportRisk|分隔战区支援风险]] beneath Liu Bei's apparent high point.

## Key Claims

- Zhang Ji is presented as combining rebellion suppression and western defense with salt-and-iron development, encouragement of farming and sericulture, and measures intended to give frontier communities a viable livelihood.
- The episode attributes to Zhang Ji and Cao Cao the relocation of more than fifty thousand Wudu households toward the Fufeng–Tianshui area, extending the buffer proposal described in Hanji 1164 into an asserted implementation scale.
- Zhang Ji reportedly advises Cao Cao not to intervene immediately in fighting among Liangzhou local powers; their mutual conflict then removes several leaders at low direct Cao-side military cost.
- Meng Da attacks Fangling, Liu Feng assumes control of the force and joins the advance on Shangyong, and Shen Dan submits while retaining his local office.
- Liu Bei declares himself King of Hanzhong in Mianyang after taking Hanzhong, reports the act to Emperor Xian, and returns the seals of offices previously granted through Cao Cao's regime.
- Liu Bei names Liu Shan heir, assigns Wei Yan to Hanzhong, elevates Fa Zheng, and appoints Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, Ma Chao, and Huang Zhong to senior military ranks.
- The episode treats separated territory and dispersed forces as a structural weakness: Guan Yu in Jingzhou faces Cao Cao and Sun Quan while rapid support from Yizhou or Hanzhong is difficult.
- Zhuge Liang anticipates that Huang Zhong's elevation may anger Guan Yu, making status management part of the post-victory political settlement continued in Hanji 1166.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. Hanji 1165 expands the relocation account from Hanji 1164, but its figure of more than fifty thousand households, six-month timing, exact destination, voluntariness, hardship, and strategic effect remain unverified.
- Hu Sansheng is cited for linking population dispersal in Qin-Chuan to the much later Five Barbarians upheavals. The episode supplies a long-run interpretation, not sufficient evidence of direct or monocausal responsibility.
- Zhang Ji's twenty-year tenure, geographic jurisdiction, command-post placement, rebellion sequence, death in office, and “家无余财” characterization require comparison with the historical texts before use as a complete biography.
- The decision to let local powers exhaust one another may reduce direct military cost while imposing violence and insecurity on local populations; the source does not measure that burden.
- Liu Bei's legal justification, ceremony, ranks, Wei Yan's words, Guan Yu's anticipated reaction, and the claim that the *Longzhong Plan* was basically realized remain episode-framed and institutionally compressed.
- The source's “low profile” title and modern complex-decision lesson are interpretive frames rather than independently demonstrated causes of Zhang Ji's survival or success.
