## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode moves from [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]]'s death to the conclusion of the [[HanzhongCampaign218LateHan|218–219 Hanzhong campaign]]. It treats Xiahou Yuan's personal boldness as a [[RoleTransitionCapabilityTrap|role-transition capability trap]], then contrasts it with [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s patient refusal of battle, [[ZhaoYun|赵云]]'s open-gate bluff, and [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s decision to abandon an increasingly costly position.

The closing branch follows [[ZhangJiCaoOfficialLateHan|张既]]'s proposal to move residents eastward and reduce the supplies available between Hanzhong and Guanzhong. The episode presents this as a [[PopulationRelocationBufferStrategy|population-relocation buffer strategy]], while the supplied file's structured-summary form leaves the degree of voluntariness, population scale, exact geography, dialogue, and causal effect source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- The episode interprets Xiahou Yuan's strength in rapid, personal front-line action as dangerous after his role shifts from mobile commander to system-preserving theater leader.
- Cao Cao reportedly advances cautiously toward Hanzhong and protects vulnerable points on his route, while Liu Bei declines battle and expects distance, internal pressure, time, and morale loss to force a withdrawal.
- [[HuangZhong|黄忠]] leaves to attack a grain convoy, and Zhao Yun rides out with a small force to meet him before encountering and escaping a much larger Cao-side formation.
- Zhao Yun leaves the camp gate open; Cao Cao suspects an ambush and orders withdrawal, after which arrows and pursuit turn caution into disorder and losses.
- Continued refusal of decisive battle erodes Cao-side patience and morale, and Cao Cao uses the reported “鸡肋” order before withdrawing to Chang'an in 219.
- Liu Bei's acquisition of Hanzhong creates defensive depth and a northern strategic position, but the episode's statement that the Three Kingdoms balance was thereby basically formed is an interpretive compression.
- Zhang Ji proposes facilitated eastward relocation toward the Fufeng–Tianshui area so that a low-population corridor will make a Liu-side advance into Guanzhong harder to supply.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends the campaign beyond Hanji 1161–1162 and repeats Zhao Yun's open-camp episode already supplied by Hanji 1147.
- The “past strengths become future failure” frame is a management interpretation, not proof that Xiahou Yuan lacked caution in every command or that one trait alone caused his death.
- Liu Bei's account of Cao Cao's age, psychology, internal pressures, and lack of confidence is episode-attributed strategic interpretation rather than direct access to private motive.
- Zhao Yun's troop total, Huang Zhong's initiative, the camp layout, Cao Cao's presence and order, casualties, dialogue, and Liu Bei's praise require primary-text comparison.
- The “鸡肋” episode comes through the source's account of *Jiuzhou Chunqiu* and is not treated here as a complete explanation of Cao Cao's withdrawal.
- Calling the relocation voluntary does not by itself establish free consent under wartime authority; scale, implementation, hardship, destination, and strategic effect remain unverified.
