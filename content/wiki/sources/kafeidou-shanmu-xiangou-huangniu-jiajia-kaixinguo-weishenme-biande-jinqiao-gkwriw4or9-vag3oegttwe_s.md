# 咖啡豆｜山姆限购、黄牛加价，开心果为什么变得紧俏？

## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode uses warehouse-club purchase limits and resale markups to explain a [[PistachioSupplyShock|pistachio supply shock]] produced by simultaneous U.S. and Iranian harvest pressure, constrained shipping, China's import dependence, and the long maturation cycle of new orchards. It adds a demand-side layer through [[ViralFoodIngredientDemand]]: health positioning, visual distinctiveness, food-and-drink launches, and Dubai chocolate turned one nut into a socially amplified cross-category ingredient before supply could respond.

Short business items also report that a British sportswear brand rendered as “CastWall” is seeking buyers, AI systems found longstanding PHP vulnerabilities, [[MusePersonalAgent|Muse]] is pursuing transaction commissions and smart-glasses distribution, and beauty companies are testing products for facial changes associated with rapid weight loss.

## Key Claims
- The episode says China produced roughly 300 tonnes of pistachios in 2024 but imported more than 130,000 tonnes, with the United States and Iran supplying about 68% and 31% of imports respectively.
- Iranian output is reported near 120,000 tonnes, roughly half the prior year, after heat, drought, and irrigation interruptions; conflict and [[StraitOfHormuz]] shipping risk add time and cost.
- California heat is reported to have disrupted pollination and increased empty shells, while groundwater restrictions may constrain longer-term supply.
- New growing regions cannot close the gap quickly because pistachio trees are described as taking about eight years to bear and about twelve years to reach maximum production.
- [[SamsClub|Sam's Club]] and [[Costco]] reportedly limited selected pistachio purchases; relative warehouse-club price advantages and scarce availability created resale incentives.
- Pistachio demand predates the latest shortage, but drinks, ice cream, desserts, and especially viral Dubai chocolate widened its role from snack to highly visible ingredient.
- The episode attributes Dubai chocolate's acceleration to a late-2023 TikTok tasting video and says confectionery procurement reduced supply available to other buyers.
- Firms can raise prices or substitute almonds and cashews in some recipes, but substitution is harder when pistachio is the defining ingredient, so [[CommodityConsumerPricePassThrough]] can appear through price, recipe, or availability.
- Two cybersecurity companies' AI models reportedly found severe PHP vulnerabilities dating to 2004; European government, school, and hospital sites temporarily went offline as a precaution, while no known exploitation was reported in the episode.
- [[MusePersonalAgent|Muse]] reportedly passed ten million U.S. and Canadian users, routed more than a quarter of users into shopping with an average purchase above $200, and plans a commission model plus smart-glasses access; these figures remain company-reported.
- Beauty companies are described as testing treatments for facial laxity after rapid weight loss, while the episode stresses that the change is not unique to GLP-1 drugs and that topical skin care cannot fully replace lost subcutaneous fat.
