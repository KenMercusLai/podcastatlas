# 144.女侠段四合和她的家乡马坪关：“我们曾以为有电有路只是梦”

## Summary
This [[QizhulouYanBinke|起朱楼宴宾客]] field conversation records [[DuanSihe|段四合]] at a firepit in [[Mapingguan|马坪关]], using her growth from shy village woman to homestay host and public organizer to explain how a mountain village changed through electricity, roads, matsutake management, tourism, education, and women's organizing. The episode's main argument is not that outside helpers "saved" a backward village, but that [[VillageSelfGovernance|village self-governance]] already existed and became more legible when [[HuangYunwu|黄云武]], [[KakaGugu|卡卡姑姑]], and other visitors supplied methods, contacts, and confidence. It adds a grounded rural-development branch around [[RuralInfrastructureTransition]], [[MatsutakeCommonsManagement]], [[RuralWomenPublicParticipation]], [[RuralParentChildReading]], and [[SlowRuralTourism]].

## Key Claims
- The source was published on 2025-10-21; its village-history, organization, and project details should be read as source-dated oral-history claims unless independently corroborated elsewhere.
- [[DuanSihe|段四合]] says she initially expected life after junior high to follow marriage, childbirth, and childcare, but later became a mediator between outside teams and villagers and a host capable of public reception work.
- The episode places [[Mapingguan|马坪关]] near [[ShaxiAncientTown|沙溪古镇]] in [[Yunnan|云南]], with a village-name memory tied to Qing salt-tax collection and horse-caravan traffic.
- [[RuralInfrastructureTransition]] is central: the source says 马坪关 received electricity in 2009 and a road in 2015, making "having electricity and roads" the remembered threshold between dream and ordinary infrastructure.
- The source preserves ambivalence about modernization. Older firepit, visiting, and daylight rhythms are remembered warmly, but the episode does not treat no-electricity life as better than infrastructure access.
- [[HuangYunwu|黄云武]] and [[KakaGugu|卡卡姑姑]] are presented as external facilitators who helped residents practice meetings, reception, communication, and project work rather than as substitute decision-makers.
- [[VillageSelfGovernance]] appears through long village meetings, rule-setting, collective listening, proposal integration, and a decision threshold described in the episode as "自主制."
- Toilet renovation becomes a practical change-management case: villagers who resisted courtyard flush toilets were persuaded partly by experiencing renovated homes, showers, and actual use.
- [[MatsutakeCommonsManagement]] is the strongest commons case. Villagers reportedly organized group rotation, entrance guarding, rules against destructive digging, village-only contracting, per-capita revenue distribution, and a reserve fund for public and emergency needs.
- The episode says eight matsutake mountains brought total contract revenue of more than 1.3 million RMB in the prior year, but this figure remains source-scoped and oral.
- [[AiWawaReadingGroup|爱娃娃亲子阅读小组]] responds to school consolidation, lack of preschool preparation, Mandarin gaps, and mountain-child stigma by gathering parents and children around picture books and outside interaction.
- [[MamaHui|妈妈会]] and related dance or performance groups are presented as important ways for village women to speak, learn Mandarin, leave household isolation, and enter public affairs.
- [[DuanSihe|段四合]] says she advocated to education officials for a village parent to become school life administrator after renewed school-consolidation pressure, turning anxiety about children's schooling into a concrete institutional request.
- Government funding and support are named for roads, village-road repair, the old opera stage, wind-rain bridge, and temple restoration, making the episode a mixed case of village initiative, external facilitation, and state-backed infrastructure.
- The "守官人" identity is presented as a place-keeping story: villagers are said to treat staying in 马坪关 as an obligation across generations rather than only a fallback after failed migration.
- [[SlowRuralTourism]] is source-defining. 段四合's homestay began renovation in 2018 and only opened formally much later; she says the village wants tourism and income, but not at a pace that overwhelms local rhythm or capacity.
- The episode closes with a future museum idea: displaying village costumes, images, books, and post-2014 change records so children understand how the village moved from darkness and isolation into the present.
