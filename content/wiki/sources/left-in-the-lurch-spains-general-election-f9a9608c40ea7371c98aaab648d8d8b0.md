# Left in the lurch: Spain’s general election

## Summary
This [[EconomistPodcasts]] episode examines three institutions trying to adapt under pressure. In [[Spain]], strong aggregate growth has not protected [[PedroSanchez]] from housing scarcity, migration pressure, infrastructure failures, corruption allegations, or a likely rightward shift led by [[AlbertoNunezFeijoo]] and conditioned by [[SantiagoAbascal]]; [[Mattel]] is trying to turn toy brands into entertainment as birth rates fall and children spend more time on screens; and [[RamayanaRiseOfALegend]] tests whether a sacred Indian epic can become an international franchise without losing narrative force or domestic legitimacy.

## Key Claims
- Spain's above-euro-area growth, higher minimum wage, and lower unemployment did not produce broad electoral satisfaction because population and labor-force growth diluted felt gains while housing supply remained inadequate.
- The source presents Sanchez's election as a referendum on eight years of Socialist government, with Ceuta migration, two deadly high-speed-train crashes, and corruption cases involving aides compounding the housing problem.
- If polling holds, Feijoo would lead the next government, but the role and demands of Abascal's populist-right Vox could shape both domestic policy and perceptions of Europe's broader rightward shift.
- [[Mattel]] faces cyclical pressure from inflation, private-label competition, and input costs alongside structural pressure from lower birth rates and screen-based leisure; adult collectors are one partial growth market.
- Under [[RogerLynch]], Mattel proposes to use brands such as Barbie across films, games, experiences, and theme parks, but the mixed performance of its projects shows that intellectual property does not automatically become repeatable entertainment profit.
- [[RamayanaRiseOfALegend]] combines a reported $430 million budget, international creative talent, and franchise ambitions, yet mixed reviews suggest that visual scale and fidelity cannot by themselves solve episodic plotting, characterization, or global-audience challenges.
- Because many Indians treat the *Ramayana* as sacred, adaptation choices face a dual constraint: deviation can provoke backlash, while excessive reverence can weaken dramatic tension.

## Key Quotes
> "kidult" — the episode's label for adults who buy toys such as Hot Wheels or complex Lego sets for themselves.

> "Marvel-style cinematic universe" — the source's description of the producers' ambition for the new *Ramayana* adaptation.

> "faithful retelling" — the episode's characterization of the film's relative success in avoiding the controversy that followed *Adipurush*.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction was adopted. The Spain segment advances the earlier snap-election story from agenda-setting strategy to a late-campaign forecast, but polling, causes of dissatisfaction, corruption allegations, and the eventual result remain source-dated or unresolved.
- The Mattel segment qualifies the idea that established intellectual property reliably produces entertainment growth: *Barbie* was a hit, but the source says Mattel captured limited profit and later projects did not reproduce its success.
- The film segment holds fidelity and dramatic effectiveness apart. Avoiding an *Adipurush*-scale backlash does not establish international appeal, narrative coherence, or eventual profitability.
