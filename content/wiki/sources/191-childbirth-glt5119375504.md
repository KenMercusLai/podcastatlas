# 191. Childbirth

## Summary
This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode with [[SaraRead|Sara Read]] treats [[ChildbirthHistory|childbirth history]] as social as well as medical history. Its early-modern English focus reconstructs a female birth community of licensed or informal midwives, relatives, neighbours, prayer, household work, and practical knowledge, while showing how male-authored texts can distort the surviving record.

The source's central synthesis is that childbirth's physiology is broadly continuous while its authority, setting, ritual, and meaning change. [[EarlyModernMidwifery]] joined experiential skill to religious and parish institutions; later [[MedicalizationOfChildbirth|medicalisation]] brought new interventions and hospital settings but also created infection risks before hygiene, anaesthesia, and antisepsis improved.

## Key Claims
- The historical record overrepresents male medical writers even though women performed much routine childbirth care and transmitted practical knowledge through experience.
- Early-modern English birth was commonly a household and community event involving a midwife, female relatives, neighbours, and “gossips,” rather than a private clinical encounter.
- Licensed midwives could be trained through long deputy or apprenticeship-like arrangements, swear church-administered oaths, lead prayer, serve rich and poor, and manage burial or baptism-related duties.
- [[JaneSharp]] argued that competent midwifery required both theoretical learning and practical experience.
- The episode rejects several popular stories as weak or false: routine persecution of midwives as witches, Julius Caesar's surgical birth, and Louis XIV causing supine delivery practice.
- Pre-modern Caesarean intervention was usually associated with a dying or dead mother; modern survivability depended on later anaesthesia, antisepsis, and surgical change.
- The shift toward physicians and hospitals changed authority around birth and could worsen infection exposure before effective hygiene, even as later medicine made difficult interventions safer.
- Birth across time combines bodily continuity with culturally variable settings, institutions, risks, and social meanings.

## Key Quotes
> “gossips” — historical term for women who attended and supported someone during childbirth.

> “Mother's Legacy” — genre in which mothers left instructions for children in case they died.
