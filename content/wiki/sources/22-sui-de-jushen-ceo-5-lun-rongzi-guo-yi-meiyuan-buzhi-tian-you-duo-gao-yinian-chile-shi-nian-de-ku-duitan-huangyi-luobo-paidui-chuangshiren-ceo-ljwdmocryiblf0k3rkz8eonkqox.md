## Summary
This [[ShizilukouCrossing|十字路口Crossing]] episode interviews [[HuangYiRobotics|黄一]], founder and CEO of [[LuoboPaidui|萝博派对]], about building an open-source humanoid robotics company at age 22. The discussion covers five financing rounds, over-100-million-dollar fundraising, first-round terms, investor selection, industry bubble dynamics, [[RPORobot|RPO]] and [[RP1Robot|RP1]] product positioning, research-education demand, organization scaling, and why [[OpenSourceRobotPlatformStrategy]] can be commercial rather than charitable. Its core contribution is a founder-level view of how an embodied-AI hardware startup uses capital, open source, and platform adoption to buy iteration time before broader commercialization.

## Key Claims
- [[LuoboPaidui]]'s early fundraising was not only a bet on a young founder: [[HuangYiRobotics|Huang Yi]] says investors responded after the team had already built a robot prototype and put personal capital into the work.
- [[HardTechFinancingDiscipline]] matters early because seed terms, buyback clauses, valuation structure, lawyer quality, and investor mix can follow a hardware company through later rounds.
- The episode treats venture capital as a way to pull future cash flow into the present so a fast-iterating robotics company does not fall behind while waiting for a side cash-flow business.
- [[OpenSourceRobotPlatformStrategy]] is presented as a commercial adoption route: good open-source hardware lowers cooperation friction, attracts researchers and users, and creates ecosystem feedback around the company's body platform.
- [[RPORobot|RPO]] gives [[LuoboPaidui]] real orders and customer feedback from schools, startups, and labs, while [[RP1Robot|RP1]] is positioned as the more productized next platform for research and education.
- The company is keeping its short-term center of gravity on humanoid body hardware and low-level control, while exploring [[EmbodiedNativeFoundationModels]], reinforcement learning demos, and later model work.
- Huang says the embodied-AI market contains bubble dynamics, but argues that the cycle still helps with fundraising, supplier cooperation, and talent migration into robotics.
- The management story is part of the technology story: a roughly 140-person team with many interns can rely on talent density only up to the point where manufacturing, delivery, sales, and supply chain require process.
