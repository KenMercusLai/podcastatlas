## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode is a compact business-and-technology roundup dated to 17 September 2026, moving from [[Ctrip]]'s penalty-driven quarterly loss to [[ByteDance]]'s AI-heavy first half, [[Apple]]'s [[EmmyAwards|Emmy]] lead, the [[AITO]] cooperation reset between [[Seres]] and [[Huawei]], [[BYD]]'s European local-production push, [[Waymo]]'s Tokyo robotaxi plan, [[Meta]]'s camera-free glasses, [[NikeSkims]] entering China, and [[Luxihe|泸溪河]]'s executive hiring. Its throughline is that growth-stage companies are trading near-term profit for investment, control, or privacy headroom: fines, AI spending, content budgets, partner role changes, tariffs, and localization choices all show up as margin and control questions rather than isolated headlines.

## Key Claims
- [[Ctrip]] reported second-quarter 2026 revenue growth of about 6% but swung to a loss after the [[StateAdministrationForMarketRegulation]] antitrust penalty, described here as close to RMB 5.2 billion in total. Excluding the penalty, quarterly net profit was about RMB 2.7 billion, transport ticketing revenue fell about 1%, and international platform revenue rose more than 50%, with inbound travel and Asia-Pacific leading and Europe and the Americas growing from a low base. The episode adds an [[HSBC]] research view that weak summer travel demand plus antitrust rectification, international expansion, and AI spending may keep margins under pressure for two to three more quarters.
- [[ByteDance]] revenue rose roughly 30% year over year to about USD 120 billion in the first half, with [[Douyin]] still the main revenue engine and overseas business above 30% of the total, while profit fell because of heavier AI spending. [[TheInformation|The Information]] is used to argue that ByteDance lags on foundation models and is the only major Chinese model maker keeping most of its models closed, so enterprise customers reach them mainly through the ByteDance cloud. The episode reads [[Doubao]]'s integration into [[Feishu]] and the push into AI office agents as a search for growth outside consumer chat.
- [[Apple]] won 28 [[EmmyAwards|Emmy Awards]], the first time since 2003 that a company outside [[HBO]], [[Netflix]], and [[TheWaltDisneyCompany|Disney]]'s FX has led the awards, even as eligible titles across the industry fell about 40% from four years earlier to 212. The episode frames [[AppleTVPlus|Apple TV+]] as a deliberate counter-cyclical content investor, while separating awards success from audience scale: Nielsen data put Apple TV at 0.5% of total U.S. television watch time in July against close to 8% for [[Netflix]].
- [[Seres]] and [[Huawei]] changed the [[AITO|问界]] cooperation model on 15 September: product definition, brand marketing, and channel retail move to Seres leadership while Huawei Terminal continues as an enabler, and the other four [[HongmengZhixing]] brands keep Huawei's full-process model. The episode links the change to AITO sales falling more than 14% in the first eight months of 2026 and to a first-half loss at Seres, and adds a source-推测 layer that the shift lowers dependence and cost but leaves AITO's independent brand-building unproven.
- [[BYD]] plans its first European heavy truck next year and says all products sold in Europe will eventually be built there, after European truck makers asked the [[EuropeanUnion]] for tariffs comparable to those on electric cars. The episode cites a Deutsche Bank report raising BYD's 2026 overseas sales target from 1.3 million to between 1.9 and 2.0 million vehicles, names shipping capacity as a constraint, and describes an expanding owned car-carrier fleet with [[Indonesia]] and [[Brazil]] plants running and a Hungarian plant due for large-scale production next year.
- [[Waymo]] announced a Tokyo commercial robotaxi plan with [[NihonKotsu|日本交通]] and the GO ride-hailing app, with fares benchmarked to existing Tokyo taxi prices and the taxi operator handling vehicle maintenance, management, and operations. The source, citing Nikkei, notes that incumbent Japanese taxi companies may keep unusually strong control in robotaxi deployment, that the industry has opposed ride-hailing on safety grounds while treating robotaxis more positively, and that Waymo's roughly 15 fully driverless U.S. cities are the base for Tokyo, London, and Munich preparation.
- [[Meta]] plans an autumn launch of smart glasses without a camera, with six microphones and voice interaction with [[MetaAI]] and the [[MetaMuseModels|Muse]] agent, following a [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Ray-Ban]] line that is popular but privacy-contested. The episode describes the LED recording indicator problem and Meta's software response that disables the camera when interference is detected, and places the product next to a [[RealityLabs]] quarterly loss above USD 4.6 billion.
- [[NikeSkims]] announced its China entry on 16 September with a first Shanghai offline space opening at the end of the month and running to February 2027. The episode frames the brand as [[Skims]]' all-size body-to-athleisure path under [[KimKardashian]], plus [[Nike]]'s attempt to reach women's sportswear through a separate identity, and records an industry view that Chinese women's sportswear demand is stratifying into finer segments where overseas trend heat helps initially but localization, channels, and marketing decide durability.
- [[Luxihe|泸溪河]] hired Domino's China's former chief marketing officer as chief growth officer and a former McDonald's China executive as general manager after a March funding round, which the episode reads as [[ChainExecutiveImport|professional-manager import]] during a deep reshuffle in Chinese bakery chains from scale expansion toward fine-grained operations. Store count is described as above 650 with the previous pace of more than 100 openings a year no longer continuing, and insiders say the new management is testing a new store model while most stores remain mall-based.
