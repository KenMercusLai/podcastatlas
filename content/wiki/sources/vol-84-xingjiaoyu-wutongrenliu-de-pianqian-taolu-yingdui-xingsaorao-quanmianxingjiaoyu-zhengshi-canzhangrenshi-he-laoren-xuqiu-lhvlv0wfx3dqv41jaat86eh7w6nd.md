# VOL.84性教育｜“无痛人流”的骗钱套路｜硬怼性骚扰、全面性教育、正视残障人士和老人需求

## Summary
This [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode has host [[Atang|阿汤]], reproductive-medicine clinician [[WangYimingReproductiveDoctor|汪一鸣]], and sex educator [[CaiYeSexEducator|蔡夜]] frame sex education as a life-course health, relationship, consent, and dignity practice rather than a one-time anatomy lesson. It extends [[LifelongSexEducation]] through [[ConsentAndBodyBoundaryEducation]], [[ContraceptionSexualHealthRiskLiteracy]], [[GynecologicalExamDignity]], and [[InclusiveSexualHealthDignity]], connecting everyday boundaries with contraception, pain, infection prevention, clinical help-seeking, and the sexual agency of children, older adults, and disabled people.

The practical through-line is prevention without victim-blaming. People need usable knowledge about consent, condoms, lubrication, unintended pregnancy, examinations, and care pathways, but institutions and potential perpetrators also carry responsibility. The episode repeatedly directs pregnancy and symptom concerns toward qualified care while warning that low-price abortion advertising, shame, and medical misinformation can delay safer treatment.

## Key Claims
- [[LifelongSexEducation]] includes bodies, relationships, gender and identity, consent, contraception, infection prevention, help-seeking, and changing needs across the life course.
- [[ConsentAndBodyBoundaryEducation]] starts with bodily discomfort and the right to refuse; children should be allowed to reject unwanted kissing or touching even from familiar adults, and responsibility for harassment cannot be shifted entirely onto possible victims.
- [[ContraceptionSexualHealthRiskLiteracy]] treats condoms as both contraceptive and infection-risk tools, rejects withdrawal as reliable contraception, and places emergency contraception and method choice inside informed, time-sensitive care.
- Pain during sex can involve anatomy, dryness, insufficient arousal, fear, pelvic-floor response, or other causes; communication and preparation matter, but persistent pain needs qualified assessment rather than shame or endurance.
- [[GynecologicalExamDignity]] joins timely care with explanation, consent, privacy, and patient communication; abnormal discharge, itching, pain, pregnancy concerns, or other symptoms should not be hidden because an intimate examination feels embarrassing.
- Pregnancy termination should be handled through qualified institutions rather than low-price advertising that may conceal add-on charges, incomplete treatment, or unsafe care.
- [[InclusiveSexualHealthDignity]] makes older and disabled people's sexuality visible as an issue of agency, support, health, and dignity rather than treating adult able-bodied sexuality as the only legitimate case.
