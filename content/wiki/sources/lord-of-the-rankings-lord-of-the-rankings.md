# Lord of the Rankings

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode traces the [[USNewsAndWorldReport|U.S. News and World Report]] college ranking from a 1980s magazine differentiation strategy to a consequential higher-education institution. [[MalcolmGladwell]] and a [[ReedCollege]] statistics class led by [[KellyMcConville]] reconstruct the weighted model and find that Reed is the exceptional prediction failure: the published 2019 rank was 90th, while their model placed it around 36th. The episode then identifies peer assessment as a powerful but weakly informed input and connects circular reputation to material inequality through Dillard president [[WalterKimbrough]] and research on historically Black colleges' higher bond costs.

## Key Claims

- U.S. News discloses the variables used in its college rankings but not the complete procedure that combines them into a final score.
- After Reed stopped submitting data, its researchers used public government data to reconstruct a roughly 20-factor weighted model that closely predicted scores for other institutions.
- Reed was the only institution outside the model's reasonable prediction range in the reported test: U.S. News ranked it 90th in 2019, while the reconstruction placed it around 36th.
- The reconstruction identified peer assessment as the most influential modeled input, with a reported 6.58-point overall-score increase associated with a one-unit assessment increase while other factors were held constant.
- Presidents, provosts, and admissions leaders rate hundreds of institutions even though interviewed administrators acknowledge having little direct knowledge of many of them.
- Previous rankings, familiarity, scenery, age, athletics, personal anecdotes, and publicity can therefore enter judgments that are presented as measurements of academic quality.
- The episode uses higher bond spreads for historically Black colleges—20% overall and 37% in Louisiana, Mississippi, and Alabama in the cited research—as an independent example of racialized reputation carrying financial cost, not as direct proof of the ranking mechanism.
- [[InstitutionalReputationFeedbackLoop]] arises when prior status and visibility shape reputation scores that then reproduce status and visibility.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode anticipates and corroborates [[project-dillard-project-dillard|Project Dillard]], which adds a stronger model of wealth, student advantage, and access incentives.
- Reed's anomalous result is consistent with special treatment but does not establish how U.S. News produced the score or prove deliberate retaliation for nonparticipation.
- A trimmed mean can limit isolated strategic ratings but cannot by itself remove shared ignorance, inherited prestige, or correlated bias.
- The HBCU bond-market evidence demonstrates a separate reputational penalty; the episode presents its relationship to peer assessment as an analogy rather than a direct causal test.
