# 百万个景观社会：听说你也想当主播？

## Summary
This [[Mianji|面基]] episode with [[TianFeng|田峰]] and two 00后 researchers from [[ChineseAcademyOfSocialSciences|中国社科院]] uses fieldwork in [[Chengdu]] and [[Changsha]] guild backstages to examine [[TalentGroupLivestreaming|才艺团播]]. The strongest contribution is that the episode treats livestreaming as a live labor system: anchors, guilds, operators, hosts, cameras, lighting, makeup, data dashboards, AI moderation, and platform rules jointly produce the visible performance. It connects [[LivestreamGuildIndustrialization|直播公会工业化]], [[RealTimeLivestreamLabor|实时数据直播劳动]], [[PlatformModerationComplianceLabor|平台合规劳动]], and [[SocialProductionOfBeauty|美的社会生产]] into one platform-work account.

## Key Claims
- Talent group livestreaming is not just "people dancing on camera"; it is a coordinated stage, operations, data, and compliance system.
- Guilds have shifted from screening many individual streamers after they emerge to training, managing, styling, and coordinating anchors from the start.
- Real-time data changes livestream labor because operators can adjust songs, dance repetition, camera attention, and pacing during the session rather than waiting for later reports.
- Equipment and staging improved quickly, but when lighting, cameras, choreography, and studio techniques are copied, competition returns to durable talent, stamina, expression, and continuous invention.
- The livestream room produces beauty socially: makeup, styling, camera distance, lighting, platform inspection, audience response, and data review jointly decide what looks effective.
- Compliance is labor. Clothing, camera angle, body exposure, lighting color, and AI image-recognition mistakes can all affect traffic or trigger review.
- Anchors' workday extends beyond the two-hour visible session into makeup, fitting, rehearsal, livestreaming, review, peer feedback, new dance practice, and sometimes personal-account streaming after leaving the guild room.
- Group livestreaming can offer young performers new routes from art schools, trainee systems, variety shows, theaters, TV stations, and short-video experience, but it also produces high turnover and uncertain income.
- The episode qualifies simple creator-economy optimism: professionalization can improve support and production quality while also raising capital cost, work intensity, and organization control.
- The students' participant observation matters because dancing, standing under the lights, reading the public chat, tracking fans, and interviewing anchors in the practice room revealed bodily and cognitive labor that remote commentary would miss.

## Key Quotes
> "每个行业都有自己的算法" - the host's shorthand for hidden operational know-how across jobs.

> "美不是绝对的，而是由社会生产出来的" - Tian Feng's frame for makeup, camera, data, and platform feedback.

> "感觉到时间的存在" - Tian Feng's description of loneliness, which livestreaming and short video can help users consume or forget.

> "第二自我" - the student researcher's phrase for how livestream persona can leak into daily self-presentation.
