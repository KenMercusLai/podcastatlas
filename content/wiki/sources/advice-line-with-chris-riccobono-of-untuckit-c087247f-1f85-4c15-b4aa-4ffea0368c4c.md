## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] Advice Line episode pairs [[GuyRaz]] with [[ChrisRiccobono]] of [[UNTUCKit]] to advise founders from [[AeroShorts]], [[SnugSafety]], and [[HockeyNinja]]. Riccobono's update on [[UNTUCKit]] and [[GreatnessWins]] frames apparel growth around cash pressure, wholesale discovery, rising paid-social costs, and AI-mediated search. Its main wiki contribution is a founder-advice case where [[CoreTribePositioning]], [[WholesaleAsMarketing]], [[TrustBasedDistribution]], [[SafetyProductCredibility]], [[MeasuredChannelTesting]], and [[AthleteSponsorshipStrategy]] become channel-choice tools.

## Key Claims
- [[ChrisRiccobono]] says [[UNTUCKit]] is still working through COVID-related debt, high interest rates, tariffs, and a source-scoped roughly $9 million cash impact from tariff and de minimis rule changes.
- [[UNTUCKit]] is entering wholesale for the first time, adding department stores alongside e-commerce and its own stores.
- [[GreatnessWins]] has moved into wholesale earlier than expected, especially green-grass golf shops, because pro-shop placement can create revenue and discovery for later online purchases.
- Riccobono says paid social was easier for direct-to-consumer brands in 2011, while apparel founders in 2026 need non-obvious channels and should watch AI discovery surfaces such as ChatGPT-style recommendations.
- [[AdrianAlvarez]] built [[AeroShorts]] from high-school founder origins into a hybrid-shorts brand with about $100,000 in first-year sales, a second-year run rate around $400,000, and source-scoped 60% repeat purchase.
- Riccobono and [[GuyRaz]] advise AeroShorts to define its core tribe before trying to become mainstream, with volleyball and college/high-school customers treated as sharper early audiences than "shorts for everyone."
- [[PreetAnand]] says [[SnugSafety]] provides free daily check-ins for seniors living alone, with a paid plan that can call the user, emergency contacts, and emergency services if safety is not confirmed.
- [[SnugSafety]] has about $400,000 in annual revenue, 70% or greater growth, strong word of mouth, some Google and senior-YouTuber traction, and a limited marketing budget.
- For Snug, Riccobono recommends testing radio and audio reads while Guy emphasizes earned media, local publications, doctors, churches, and other trusted intermediaries because the product sells reassurance.
- Snug is positioned as complementary to or distinct from [[LifeAlert]] because Life Alert depends on an emergency button, while Snug can help when a person does not respond to daily check-ins.
- [[HockeyNinja]] sells protective hockey visors and full-face titanium hybrid shields, with average annual sales around $250,000 and a source-scoped high around $400,000.
- For Hockey Ninja, Riccobono and Guy advise prioritizing credible player use, equipment-manager relationships, and athlete partnerships over generic social media, because safety equipment needs visible professional validation.

## Key Quotes
> "free marketing" - Riccobono's frame for golf-shop placement that also drives later online discovery.

> "define the tribe" - source summary of Guy's first AeroShorts positioning move.

> "really, really hard" - Riccobono's closing reminder about entrepreneurship.
