## Summary
This [[YiLaoYongYi|一劳永逸]] episode reads [[Ctrip]] through the reported [[StateAdministrationForMarketRegulation]] penalty totaling 51.79亿元, or 5.179 billion yuan, then links that event to the company's twenty-plus-year rise as a Chinese [[OnlineTravelAgency]]. It extends the existing Ctrip history by making the alleged abuse mechanics concrete: [[TravelPlatformMerchantExclusivity]], [[TravelPriceParityEnforcement]], merchant ranking pressure, automatic repricing tools, and user-side price-disclosure disputes. The episode's broader synthesis is that OTA power is not only traffic scale; it is a bundle of inventory, customer service, data, acquisition history, trust, and possible [[AITravelPlanning]] capability.

## Key Claims
- The episode says [[StateAdministrationForMarketRegulation]] punished [[Ctrip]] for abusing market dominance, with confiscated illegal gains of 16.58亿元, a fine of 35.21亿元, and a total penalty of 51.79亿元 under [[CtripAntitrustPenalty]].
- It distinguishes the official penalty core from adjacent consumer complaints: the source says the punishment focused on merchant-side "二选一" and "全网最低价" constraints, while big-data price discrimination was discussed mainly as user experience and risk.
- The source says Ctrip's domestic core hotel-and-travel share was about 56% by the end of 2024, and potentially above 70% if related invested or acquired platforms are included.
- "特牌" hotel arrangements are presented as a [[TravelPlatformMerchantExclusivity]] mechanism: better traffic and display in exchange for not listing the same supply on rival platforms such as [[Fliggy]].
- "金牌" arrangements are presented as [[TravelPriceParityEnforcement]]: merchants allegedly had to keep Ctrip's price at least 20 yuan or 5% below competing channels, while platform tools monitored cross-platform effective prices.
- The episode names tools such as "调价助手" and "挂牌通" as alleged automation surfaces that could reduce the Ctrip-side price when another channel appeared cheaper, weakening hotel owner autonomy under [[HotelPlatformPricingPower]].
- User-side examples include cancellation-fee confusion and the Hu hotel-booking lawsuit, which the episode uses to connect [[TravelBookingHiddenFees]] and price-disclosure trust without treating them as the main legal basis of the penalty.
- The episode retells the founding mix around [[LiangJianzhang]], [[JiQi]], [[ShenNanpeng]], and [[FanMin]], emphasizing technology, execution, capital, and travel-operations complementarity.
- Early Ctrip's "鼠标加水泥" model is presented as a hybrid of online interface, offline sales, call centers, hotel contracting, ticketing qualifications, and business-travel service rather than a pure website story.
- The source repeats the importance of acquiring [[ModernExpressTravel]] and [[CoastAirService]] for room-booking volume, ticketing resources, and offline fulfillment capacity.
- The episode uses [[Elong]], [[Qunar]], and [[ZhuangChenchao]] to show how hotel focus, price subsidies, price-comparison search, mobile traffic, and public accusations pressured Ctrip before later capital integration.
- [[Meituan]], [[Fliggy]], [[Douyin]], [[Xiaohongshu]], and [[JDTravel]] are framed as later travel-booking challengers that can attack Ctrip from local services, ecommerce, content discovery, or existing traffic.
- [[Skyscanner]], [[MakeMyTrip]], and the Trip.com brand are presented as Ctrip's internationalization path, while the episode claims Southeast Asia and Belt-and-Road destinations are important growth surfaces.
- The source says Ctrip's self-operated 24/7 customer service, emergency contact ability, twenty years of transaction data, and [[CtripWendao]] create possible recovery assets if the company can rebuild trust.
- The user takeaway is practical rather than only regulatory: do not assume an OTA is always cheapest; compare hotel and airline official channels, loyalty benefits, cancellation rules, and platform alternatives under [[HotelDirectBookingChannels]].
