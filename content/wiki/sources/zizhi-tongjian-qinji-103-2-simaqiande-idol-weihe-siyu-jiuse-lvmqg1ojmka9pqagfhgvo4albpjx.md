# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》103-2｜司马迁的“idol” 为何死于酒色

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]]'s late arc after the [[GuanchengWarringStates|管城]] crisis by showing how [[QinState|秦国]] turns military failure into a [[CounterintelligenceRumorWedge|反间流言楔入]] against [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]] and Xinlingjun. It argues that Xinlingjun's "death by wine and women" is only the surface: the deeper cause is renewed slander, loss of command, political frustration, and self-consuming withdrawal.

The source also widens Xinlingjun's afterlife through [[LiuBang|刘邦]], who is presented as a young admirer who tried to join Xinlingjun, then turned to [[ZhangEr|张耳]] after arriving too late. The closing shifts from Wei to Qin: [[QinZhuangxiangwang|秦庄襄王]] dies, thirteen-year-old [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] succeeds, [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] governs as 仲父, and the episode tees up the puzzle of how Qin could unify despite internal unrest.

## Key Claims
- The source opens by revisiting [[SuGao|苏高]], [[AnlingJun|安陵君]], and Xinlingjun, arguing that all three were under hard constraints rather than reducible to a simple loyal/disloyal verdict.
- Xinlingjun's threat against Anling was harsh and helped produce Su Gao's suicide, but the host values his later apology as both moral recognition and a way to repair internal Wei tension.
- After [[MengAo|蒙敖]] is checked by Xinlingjun's coalition, Qin uses money, spies, and [[JinBi|晋鄙]]'s former retainers to make Xinlingjun look like a replacement king.
- Qin's envoys repeatedly "congratulate" Xinlingjun in language implying that he may have become Wei king; the message is designed to reach Wei Anxi Wang and activate royal suspicion.
- Wei Anxi Wang believes the slander, replaces Xinlingjun in command, and takes back military power.
- Xinlingjun understands that he has again been destroyed by slander, claims illness, stops participating in government, and spends the last four years in wine, women, and frustration.
- The source frames the stated "death by wine and women" as secondary to political disappointment and depression after being made unusable.
- Compared with [[LuZhonglian|鲁仲连]], Xinlingjun is criticized for not withdrawing cleanly after political defeat; he consumes himself rather than turning exit into principled freedom.
- After Xinlingjun dies, [[HanHuanhuiwang|韩桓惠王]] comes to Wei to mourn, but [[KongBin|孔斌]] advises Xinlingjun's son to decline because a foreign ruler's mourning must be hosted by Wei's own ruler.
- The episode asks why Liu Bang later honored Xinlingjun alone among the [[WarringStatesFourLords|战国四公子]], arguing that Liu Bang had admired him as a living hero in youth.
- Liu Bang reportedly went to [[Daliang|大梁]] hoping to enter Xinlingjun's retainer network, arrived after Xinlingjun's death, and then joined Zhang Er, a former Xinlingjun retainer.
- The source reads Liu Bang's later generosity and reputation-building among followers as partly modeled on Xinlingjun's patronage style.
- Qin Zhuangxiang Wang dies in the same year, Ying Zheng succeeds at thirteen, and Lu Buwei governs state affairs as Wenxin Hou and "仲父."
- The episode notes that Lu Buwei's power is exceptional because he is neither Qin royal kin nor a queen-mother's relative, but a former merchant with a huge household and guest network.
- Qin's establishment of [[TaiyuanWarringStates|太原]] commandery is followed by unrest at Jinyang after Qin Zhuangxiang Wang's death, setting up the next question: why Qin's internal disorder did not prevent rapid unification.

## Key Quotes
> "天下只知信陵君而不知魏王" - the slander frame used to make Xinlingjun's coalition prestige look like royal threat.

> "在人上要把别人当人，在人下要把自己当人" - the episode's ethical frame for judging both command pressure and defeat.

> "与其说他死于酒色，不如说是不得志、郁郁而终" - the source's interpretation of Xinlingjun's final years.
