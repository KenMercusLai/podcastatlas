# 397. JFK: A Conspiracy Unmasked (Part 6)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode traces how the killing of [[LeeHarveyOswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]] by [[JackRuby|Jack Ruby]], the creation of the [[WarrenCommission|Warren Commission]], declining trust after Vietnam and Watergate, disputed visual and acoustic evidence, and popular works such as Oliver Stone's *JFK* made the [[KennedyAssassination|assassination of John F. Kennedy]] a durable conspiracy object. It reviews proposed Soviet, Cuban, CIA, FBI, Johnson, military-industrial, right-wing, mafia, and Ruby-centered plots and argues that none withstands the hosts' tests of evidence, motive, and operational plausibility. The episode therefore supplies the historical clearing-away of alternatives that precedes Part 7's positive cumulative case for Oswald.

## Key Claims

- Dallas police and the FBI quickly believed the evidence pointed to Oswald, but Ruby's public killing of him eliminated a trial and created the lasting appearance that the accused assassin had been silenced.
- Johnson established the Warren Commission partly to counter rumor, foreign propaganda, and public destabilization; its prominent bipartisan membership assumed a degree of institutional trust that later decades would erode.
- The commission concluded that Oswald killed Kennedy and J. D. Tippit, Ruby acted alone, the two men were unconnected, and Oswald's precise motive could not be established.
- American anti-centralization traditions, Cold War suspicion, McCarthyism, civil-rights backlash, Vietnam, and Watergate gave assassination theories a wider culture of political distrust in which to grow.
- Mark Lane, Thomas Buchanan, Jim Garrison, the Zapruder film, grassy-knoll witnesses, and named photographic figures helped turn fragments, anomalies, and interpretive ambiguity into an expanding theory industry.
- The [[HouseSelectCommitteeOnAssassinations|House Select Committee on Assassinations]] preserved doubt by reporting possible acoustic evidence of conspiracy in 1979, although the episode says later specialists discredited the dictabelt analysis.
- The hosts reject Soviet, Castro, Cuban-exile, CIA, FBI, Johnson, military-industrial, right-wing, mafia, and Ruby-as-silencer explanations as unsupported or operationally implausible.
- Claims about Kennedy's intended withdrawal from Vietnam and hostility to the CIA are treated as uncertain or exaggerated rather than established motives for an institutional murder.
