# 553. The Last Viking: Warrior of the New Rome (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[HaraldHardrada|Harald Hardrada]] through his service in the [[VarangianGuard|Varangian Guard]], campaigns across the [[ByzantineEmpire|Byzantine Empire]], return to Kiev, and rise to Norwegian kingship after accommodation with [[MagnusTheGood|Magnus the Good]]. It argues that Byzantine warfare converted exile into military experience, wealth, reputation, and political leverage, while [[SagaSourceCriticism|saga source criticism]] is necessary to separate the career's plausible outline from its serpents, disguises, romances, and spectacular escapes.

The episode then traces how Harald used accumulated treasure to force a share of Norway, inherited sole rule after Magnus's death, fought Denmark, and imposed a more centralized but violent kingship. His severity, taxation, coinage, Christian patronage, and suppression of upland resistance complicate the heroic image and supply the political formation behind his final 1066 campaign.

## Key Claims

- Harald probably spent roughly 1035-1042 campaigning for Byzantium in the Aegean, Armenia, the Holy Land, Sicily, and the Balkans, although the itinerary is reconstructed from uneven Byzantine and later saga evidence.
- Service in the Varangian Guard supplied more than battlefield experience: imperial rank, command reputation, and accumulated treasure became transferable political capital when Harald returned north.
- The 1042 removal of Michael V and restoration of Zoe provide a secure court-crisis context, but Harald's imprisonment, romance, role in the blinding, serpent fight, and escape over the Golden Horn chain remain legendary or disputed.
- Harald's treasure and threat of alliance with Svein helped turn a weak demand into shared rule with Magnus; Magnus's death in 1047 then made Harald sole king of Norway.
- As king, Harald pursued war with Denmark, stronger taxation, coinage, Christianity, and centralized authority, combining state-building with coercion.
- His 1065 destruction of upland resistance grounds the “Hardrada” or severe-ruler profile more securely than a purely romantic “last Viking” image.
- The episode treats the Byzantine and Norwegian career as the essential prehistory to Harald's invasion of England and death at the [[BattleOfStamfordBridge|Battle of Stamford Bridge]] in 1066.
