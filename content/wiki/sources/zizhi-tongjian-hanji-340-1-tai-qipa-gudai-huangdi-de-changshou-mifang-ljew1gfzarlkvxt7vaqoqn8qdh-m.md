# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》340-1｜太奇葩！古代皇帝的长寿秘方

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses the construction of 柏梁台 and an仙人承露盘 under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] to show how an unnamed fangshi's longevity claim could become imperial architecture. The fangshi says dew collected from heaven and mixed with powdered jade can produce an immortality medicine; Wudi responds by commissioning a monumental bronze device in which an immortal figure holds a plate for catching dew.

The durable contribution to [[ImmortalityQuestPolitics|求仙政治]] is a material-conversion mechanism: water that has not touched the ground is treated as heaven-bestowed and ritually superior, an unverifiable recipe gives it a medical promise, and imperial authority converts that promise into specialized construction and repeated collection. The episode's modern hydration advice and its claims about dew, jade powder, aromatics, breathing practice, immunity, and longevity remain popular-health or cultural assertions rather than established medical evidence.

## Key Claims

- The episode places the construction of 柏梁台 in Han Wudi's Yuanding 2 and says the platform was named for its fragrant cypress beams.
- An unnamed fangshi tells Wudi that heavenly dew mixed with powdered precious jade can be made into a medicine of immortality.
- The claim assigns special value to water before it touches the ground; the host groups fresh rain, overnight plant dew, and clean melted snow as forms of imagined “heaven-bestowed water.”
- Wudi responds by commissioning an仙人承露盘 at 柏梁台, turning the prescription into a purpose-built collection device and a visible sacred tableau.
- The episode describes the apparatus as bronze, more than twenty zhang high, and so broad that seven people could encircle it; these dimensions remain episode-attributed.
- References to [[SuShi|苏轼]] collecting rain and to snow-, rain-, and dew-water tea in 《红楼梦》 broaden the cultural analogy but do not independently verify the Western Han recipe or apparatus.
- The opening advice to drink before thirst, together with claims about metabolism, endocrine function, organ function, immunity, Anapana practice, and lifespan, is not supported by clinical evidence in the supplied document.

## Key Quotes

> “天上的露水配上宝玉磨成的粉” - the episode's rendering of the fangshi prescription.

> “仙人承露盘” - the architectural device through which the claimed recipe becomes imperial material practice.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode backfills an early dew-collection and 柏梁台 layer before later sources on Luan Da, Gongsun Qing, Penglai search, high buildings, and Wudi's eventual dismissal of fangshi.
- Chronology and architecture caution: the dating and purpose of 柏梁台, the identity and intent of the fangshi, the recipe, the construction sequence, the material, dimensions, and seven-person circumference of the承露盘 remain episode-attributed pending comparison with primary histories and architectural evidence.
- Medical caution: the source does not demonstrate that dew or powdered jade prolongs life, that collected rain or snow is safe to drink, or that its hydration and breathing recommendations produce the stated metabolic, endocrine, immune, or longevity outcomes.
- Literary caution: the Su Shi and 《红楼梦》 comparisons show later cultural valuation of special waters but cannot authenticate the Western Han event or establish a continuous practice.
