# 525. Charlemagne: Emperor of the West (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs [[Charlemagne]]'s coronation by [[PopeLeoIII|Pope Leo III]] on Christmas Day 800 as the prepared settlement of overlapping Roman, papal, Frankish, and Byzantine crises rather than an improvised surprise. Leo's vulnerability in Rome, [[IreneOfAthens|Irene of Athens]]'s contested rule, [[Alcuin]]'s Christian-imperial argument, Charlemagne's itinerary, and his careful titulature made the crown a claim to sacred responsibility as well as geopolitical rank.

The second half follows imperial display at [[Aachen]], recognition from [[HarunAlRashid|Harun al-Rashid]] and Byzantium, succession under [[LouisThePious|Louis the Pious]], partition in 843, and the later recovery of western imperial authority by [[OttoTheGreat|Otto the Great]]. Its central synthesis is that Charlemagne's personal military, diplomatic, and learned networks were powerful but institutionally fragile: repeated division, external attack, and shrinking territorial control increased dependence on papal coronation before Saxon rulers reassembled the imperial project.

## Key Claims

- The 800 coronation was probably anticipated: Charlemagne's route through Tours, Paris, and Ravenna, his formal Roman entry, the legal settlement of accusations against Leo, and contemporary vacancy arguments point to preparation rather than surprise.
- Leo's weak aristocratic base and attack by Roman rivals made Frankish protection essential, while Charlemagne gained papal recognition for a western imperial title.
- Western advocates treated Irene's sex and violent accession as grounds for declaring the imperial office vacant, but this was a partisan legitimacy argument rather than an objective end to eastern Roman rule.
- Charlemagne avoided styling himself simply “emperor of the Romans,” preserving his new rank without forcing the titulature dispute with Constantinople into open war.
- [[Einhard]]'s physical and personal portrait mixes eyewitness memory with literary borrowing from Suetonius, so it is evidence for both recollection and deliberate imperial image-making.
- Aachen's palace, baths, road plan, and Ravenna columns made the Carolingian capital a material claim to Roman inheritance, while gifts from Harun and Byzantine recognition in 812 placed the court in a wider diplomatic system.
- Charlemagne crowned Louis joint emperor without papal participation, but later partition and weak institutions fragmented the realm and made papal coronation more important to rulers with less territorial power.
- West and East Francia contributed to later France and Germany, while the Saxon Otto's coronation in 962 revived the western imperial line that endured as the [[HolyRomanEmpire]].

## Key Quotes

> “would not have entered the cathedral” - [[Einhard]]'s retrospective claim about Charlemagne's supposed ignorance of the coronation, which the episode treats skeptically.

> “father of Europe” - the episode's qualified description of Charlemagne's learned and administrative network rather than proof of a unified European nation.

## Contradictions

- No settled thematic contradiction is adopted. The episode strengthens the existing wiki's skepticism toward a wholly spontaneous coronation and adds more circumstantial evidence for advance preparation.
- The supplied episode summary dates the attack on Leo to April 797, whereas [[12-days-coronation-of-charlemagne-and-the-collapse-of-the-soviet-union-glt9083951211]] and the current [[PopeLeoIII]] synthesis place it in 799; the chronology conflict is flagged rather than silently resolved.
- Irene's rule did not objectively leave the Roman throne vacant, and Byzantine recognition of Charlemagne as emperor in 812 did not concede the exclusive title “emperor of the Romans.”
- The reported attack injuries to Leo, Charlemagne's exact prior knowledge, Einhard's portrait, diplomatic motives, the depth of Carolingian institutions, and straight-line claims from the 843 partitions to modern France and Germany remain source-scoped or interpretive.
