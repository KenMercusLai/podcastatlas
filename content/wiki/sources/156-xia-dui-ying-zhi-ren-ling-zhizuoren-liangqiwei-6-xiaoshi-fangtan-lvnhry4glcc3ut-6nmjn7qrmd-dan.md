## Summary
This [[ZhangXiaojunCommercialInterviews|张小珺Jùn｜商业访谈录]] conversation with [[LiangQiwei|梁其伟]] uses the question “what is a xia?” to connect wuxia ethics, personal development, author-led game making, entrepreneurship, capital, and AI. Liang defines xia through extraordinary risk, capacity, and fidelity to one's inner moral center, while arguing that contemporary heroes should grow from credible personal motives rather than imposed slogans.

The career account follows Liang from architecture study and the independent [[RainBlood|《雨血》]] project into founding [[SGame|灵游坊]], mobile-game success, expansion and capital ambition, share repurchases, and the return to a large premium game through [[PhantomBladeZero|《影之刃零》]]. Its durable synthesis is that finance, technology, and organization should expand a studio's ability to make better products, while cultural roots, authorial identity, and player trust remain sources of differentiation that production efficiency alone cannot replace.

## Key Claims
- [[ModernWuxiaHeroism]] locates xia in choices made under serious risk: social order is weak, the protagonist is damaged or marginal, and moral value appears through preserving humanity rather than merely performing ordinary kindness.
- Contemporary heroic narrative can begin with self-rescue and personal growth. [[SoulPhantomBlade|魂]] starts from saving himself and may help the world, whereas [[ZuoShang|左殇]] begins with a world-saving plan that may become destructive.
- [[AuthorLedGameProduction]] makes the founder's experience, personality, aesthetic judgment, and structural habits visible in the finished game; organization design must therefore protect strong creative ownership without denying management needs.
- [[SGame|灵游坊]] learned commercialization, project management, financing, and team management through mobile games, but Liang says trend-following and capital ambition became counterproductive when they displaced the studio's creative strengths.
- [[ConservativeOperationsAggressiveProduct]] separates survival from creative ambition: protect the company's financial runway while allowing the product itself to take concentrated artistic and technical risk.
- [[CulturalRootedGenreModernization]] treats traceable martial arts, ancestral halls, old sounds, and weapon traditions as roots from which a modern “kung-fu punk” aesthetic can grow rather than as nostalgic decoration.
- The episode frames Chinese premium games as entering direct global competition after the market impact of *Genshin Impact* and *Black Myth: Wukong*, but it does not treat a few successes as proof that Chinese production theory and craft have caught up everywhere.
- AI was reportedly excluded from artistic creation on [[PhantomBladeZero|《影之刃零》]] and used mainly for logic, testing, and optimization; the source argues that buyers also value the accountable creators, world, brand, and accumulated relationship behind a premium game.
- [[ProductCapabilityAsCorporatePurpose]] makes better game-making ability the studio's governing end, with revenue, financing, reputation, and technology serving the next stronger work rather than replacing it.
