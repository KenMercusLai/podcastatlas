# Overcome Addictions & Obsessions of All Kinds | Dr. Anna Lembke

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview psychiatrist [[AnnaLembke]] about addiction as both reward adaptation and a narrowing attempt to control internal experience. Lembke extends [[PleasurePainBalanceAddiction]] through [[AddictionAsControlStrategy]], presents [[RadicalSurrenderRecovery]] as relinquishing failed control while taking the next available values-based action, and applies [[CodependencyControlLoop]] to enabling, reassurance seeking, parenting, and digital monitoring. The conversation also covers trauma, spirituality, psychedelic and ketamine risk, stimulant prescribing, GLP-1 drugs, and compulsive thought patterns while preserving clinical and evidentiary boundaries.

## Key Claims
- Addiction is defined through loss of control, compulsion, craving, and continued use despite harm across substances and potentially reinforcing behaviors.
- [[PleasurePainBalanceAddiction]] describes repeated reward as recruiting compensatory discomfort, tolerance, withdrawal, and a deficit state in which use may feel necessary merely to feel normal.
- [[AddictionAsControlStrategy]] explains why severe addiction can persist after pleasure declines: the substance, behavior, or thought remains a predictable lever for changing immediate internal experience.
- [[RadicalSurrenderRecovery]] means acknowledging that a repeated control strategy is not working, accepting what cannot be changed, seeking help, and doing the next right action rather than resigning agency.
- [[CodependencyControlLoop]] occurs when managing another person's behavior or response becomes a way to regulate one's own emotions, potentially converting help into enabling or enmeshment.
- Recovery is presented as biological, psychological, social, spiritual, and values-based change rather than abstinence alone; community, responsibility, meaning, and avoiding substitution remain important.
- Psychedelics, ketamine, stimulants, and GLP-1 drugs may have clinical relevance in selected settings, but benefit claims, mechanisms, monitoring, misuse risk, and severe adverse outcomes require qualified care and stronger evidence.

- The periaqueductal-gray spirituality network, serotonin-versus-dopamine contrast, executive-control inhibition, and surrender-related neuroplasticity are presented as proposed mechanisms rather than settled causal explanations.
- The episode's cultural claims, gender patterns, prayer effects, synchronicity interpretations, digital-generation hypotheses, and claims about private versus performed experience remain clinical, personal, philosophical, or source-scoped.
- The 10-to-14-day withdrawal range, third-to-fourth-week improvement estimate, 30-day abstinence experiment, simultaneous-cessation claim, GLP-1 response, and medication mechanisms are not individualized treatment rules.
