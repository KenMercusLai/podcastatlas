## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] roundup connects [[PopMart|泡泡玛特]]'s overseas inventory problems, AI-linked changes in wealth and technology spending, [[Apple]]'s control over motion-triggered ads, the possible European use of a China-developed vehicle architecture, celebrity-endorsement exits, beverage competition, and regional-airport economics. ## Key Claims
- The episode says Pop Mart's overseas operation can experience stockouts and excess inventory at the same time because long domestic-to-overseas replenishment cycles amplify demand-forecast errors and make cross-market correction expensive.
- The cited 2026 Hurun ranking presents [[ZhangYiming|张一鸣]] as China's richest person and describes AI, chips, optical modules, and compute infrastructure as major wealth-growth channels.
- A new iOS control reportedly lets China-region users deny specific apps access to motion data, preventing those apps from using a phone shake as an advertising jump trigger.
- [[Nvidia]]'s reported investment in [[Meta]] and Meta's capital spending above operating cash flow illustrate an AI financing loop in which suppliers can fund customers that buy more infrastructure, while platform companies increasingly use capital markets for data centers and chips.
- The reported [[Microsoft]] voluntary-departure plan, alongside earlier workforce reductions at Microsoft, [[Google]], and Meta, is presented as a shift from labor expansion toward AI infrastructure spending rather than proof that every departure was caused by automation.
- [[VolkswagenGroup|Volkswagen Group]] is considering using an electronic/electrical architecture co-developed with [[XPeng]] in Europe after the China route reached production sooner than the separate Rivian route; China-developed vehicles have also reportedly been exported to Australia and the Middle East.
- Chinese brands ended agreements with [[KylianMbappe|Kylian Mbappe]] after controversial remarks, illustrating how local legitimacy can rapidly override the value of global celebrity reach.
- [[CocaCola|Coca-Cola]] reportedly widened its recent growth advantage over [[PepsiCo]], while packaging, freight, and commodity costs remained a constraint.
- The loss of scheduled service at Jiuzhai Huanglong Airport illustrates why a tourism-dependent regional airport can be commercially fragile even when its time-saving and territorial-access value remains socially important.
