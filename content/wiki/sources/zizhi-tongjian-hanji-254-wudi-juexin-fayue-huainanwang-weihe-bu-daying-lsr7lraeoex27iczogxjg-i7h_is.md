# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》254｜武帝决心伐越，淮南王为何不答应？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the opening half of [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]]'s memorial against [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s intervention after [[MinyueKingYing|闽越王郢]] attacks [[Nanyue|南越]] and [[ZhaoHuNanyue|赵胡]] requests Han aid. Liu An argues that unfamiliar terrain, waterborne fighting, dispersed settlement, weak occupation value, and the prospect of repeated enforcement make victory and durable rule different problems. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-255-yiren-ke-di-shiwan-jun-huainanwang-liuan-de-huaiyi-zhice-lq1xob3iigegjaez9_zz5ddbzjy8|Hanji 255]] continues the same memorial with domestic distress, disease, logistics, Qin precedent, and an envoy-based alternative.

The episode also backfills the early chancellorship of [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]]. After [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] dies, Tian Fen is appointed chancellor, promotes allies, and seeks public land for a larger residence; Wudi's reported rebukes show that maternal kinship opens access without erasing the sovereign's control over appointments and state property. Coin reform, the Five Classics doctorates, royal deaths, disasters, and longevity claims provide chronology but remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- The episode places Zhao Tuo's death and Zhao Hu's succession before changes to coinage, establishment of Five Classics doctorates, royal deaths without heirs, fires at imperial ancestral sites, and the death of Dowager Dou.
- After Dowager Dou's death, Wudi dismisses Xu Chang and appoints maternal uncle Tian Fen as chancellor.
- Tian Fen uses his new position to recommend many associates and seeks land attached to the imperial workshop for residence expansion; Wudi reportedly rebukes both the patronage drive and land request.
- Ying attacks Nanyue's border, while Zhao Hu asks Han for aid rather than retaliating without imperial approval.
- Wudi sends [[WangHuiWesternHan|王恢]] from Yuzhang and [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] from Kuaiji against Minyue.
- Liu An argues that Yue customs, settlement patterns, terrain, waterways, vegetation, and naval skill make Central Plains administration and occupation unusually costly.
- He distinguishes battlefield entry from governable gain: even successful occupation may not support durable commandery administration, taxation, tribute, or useful labor obligations.
- He warns that treating every failure to obey as grounds for force could create an open-ended sequence of campaigns rather than a bounded rescue operation.

## Key Quotes

> “地图上相距几寸，实际距离可能是几百里、上千里。” - the episode's spatial summary of why map distance understates operational difficulty.

> “恐怕以后的战争会没完没了。” - the episode's rendering of Liu An's warning about repeated coercive enforcement.

## Contradictions

- The episode calls Liu An a younger brother of [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] and therefore Wudi's uncle. Existing Huainan evidence identifies Liu An as a son of [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]], while Jingdi descends from Liu Chang's brother Emperor Wen; the supplied kinship statement is not adopted as canonical.
- The transcript's “骆郢” is normalized to [[MinyueKingYing|闽越王郢]] from the event sequence continued in Hanji 256, rather than treated as a separate ruler.
- Hanji 256 says Wudi proceeds despite the memorial and that mobilization helps induce Ying's internal removal. This qualifies Liu An's argument without disproving his narrower distinction between coercive leverage and cheap occupation.
- The title's broad “伐越” is more specific in the body: the immediate expedition targets Minyue after an aid request from Nanyue, not an undifferentiated campaign against every Yue polity.
- The supplied file is a structured podcast summary rather than a primary-text collation. Emperor counts, Zhao Tuo's age and succession details, coin weights, doctorate chronology, disasters, dialogue, appointment motives, property request, military routes, Nanyue's authorization practice, terrain ratios, tribute status, and administrative feasibility remain episode-attributed.
