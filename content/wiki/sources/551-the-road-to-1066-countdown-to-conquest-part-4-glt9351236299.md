# 551. The Road to 1066: Countdown to Conquest (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces the [[GodwinEarlOfWessex|Godwin family]] from Godwin's rise under Cnut through [[HaroldGodwinson|Harold Godwinson]]'s emergence as England's dominant earl on the eve of 1066. It argues that the [[NormanConquestOfEngland|Norman Conquest]] grew from [[EdwardTheConfessor|Edward the Confessor]]'s childlessness, the unstable bargain between king and magnate, Norman influence, rival English noble houses, and the disputed succession promises later claimed by [[WilliamTheConqueror|William of Normandy]].

The episode ends in late 1065 after the Northumbrian revolt removes [[TostigGodwinson|Tostig Godwinson]]. Harold refuses to force Tostig back on the north at the cost of civil war, while Tostig interprets that settlement as betrayal; Edward is gravely weakened, Harold is positioned for power, and William can invoke Harold's relic-backed oath.

## Key Claims

- [[GodwinEarlOfWessex|Godwin]] rose from obscure Sussex thane to Earl of Wessex under Cnut, married into a Danish-connected family, and survived repeated succession changes through political flexibility.
- Godwin's involvement in Alfred's capture and death gave [[EdwardTheConfessor|Edward]] reason for resentment, yet each needed the other: Godwin helped restore Edward, and Edward confirmed the family through office and marriage to [[EdithOfWessex|Edith]].
- Edward and Edith's childlessness made succession structurally unstable; the failed 1051 attempt to break Godwin power increased Norman influence and may have opened the way for Edward's reported promise to William.
- The Godwins' armed return in 1052 restored Edith and the family, foreclosed divorce as a practical succession solution, and left Edward increasingly overshadowed by the house he had tried to remove.
- Harold inherited Wessex in 1053, built military reputation in Wales, and became the strongest English political and military figure, while Godwin expansion intensified rivalry with Mercia and Northumbrian resistance.
- Harold's 1064 or 1065 visit to William produced an oath over relics whose purpose and freedom remain disputed; the hosts treat reconnaissance, compulsion, and political bargaining as more plausible than an uncomplicated mission to surrender the succession.
- Tostig's absentee rule, taxation, appointments, and failure to protect Northumbria helped provoke the 1065 revolt; Harold's acceptance of Morcar avoided civil war but converted family conflict into an external threat.

## Key Quotes

> "politically interested rather than neutral" - the episode's treatment of the praise for Harold and Tostig in the Life of King Edward.

> "a bargaining chip" - the episode's proposed frame for Edward's use of the unresolved succession.
