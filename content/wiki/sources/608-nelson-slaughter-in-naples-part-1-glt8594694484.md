# 608. Nelson: Slaughter in Naples (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]] from his triumphant September 1798 arrival in Naples through the failed Neapolitan invasion of Rome, the royal court's flight to Palermo, and the violent restoration of [[FerdinandIVOfNaples|Ferdinand IV]]. It makes Naples a moral turning point: exhaustion, flattery, attachment to [[EmmaHamilton|Emma Hamilton]], royal orders, and counterrevolutionary vengeance converged when Nelson rejected a negotiated capitulation, used British ships to detain republicans, and rapidly executed [[FrancescoCaracciolo|Francesco Caracciolo]].

## Key Claims

- Nelson arrived after the [[BattleOfTheNile|Battle of the Nile]] as a wounded international celebrity; Emma and [[SirWilliamHamilton|Sir William Hamilton]] gave him care, admiration, and a household that became emotionally and politically influential.
- [[MariaCarolina|Maria Carolina]] drove the Neapolitan court's anti-French policy, while Nelson overestimated an inexperienced army whose brief occupation of Rome collapsed when French forces returned.
- The royal flight to Palermo placed the Hamiltons and Nelson inside the court's trauma, including the death of Prince Carlo Alberto during the storm crossing, while Nelson's exhaustion and dependence on Emma deepened.
- The [[ParthenopeanRepublic|Parthenopean Republic]] had a narrow reformist base and faced popular Catholic and royalist resistance; [[CardinalRuffo|Cardinal Ruffo]] nevertheless tried to end the reconquest through a capitulation rather than a final massacre.
- Nelson treated Ruffo's agreement as unauthorized, detained surrendered republicans who expected passage to Toulon, and rejected officers' objections that good faith required either honoring the terms or restoring the military status quo.
- Caracciolo's rapid court martial and hanging, followed by wider royal trials and executions, made Nelson's obedience to the court conflict with military honor and [[SurrenderGuaranteeIntegrity|surrender-guarantee integrity]].
- Historians and the hosts disagree over culpability, but contemporary criticism from British officers and admirers prevents the episode from being dismissed as purely retrospective condemnation.

## Key Quotes

> "right or wrong" - Nelson's reported formulation that he would obey his orders when officers challenged the treatment of surrendered republicans.

> "a stain upon the memory of Nelson, and upon the honour of England" - Robert Southey's later judgment on the Naples episode, as presented by the hosts.
