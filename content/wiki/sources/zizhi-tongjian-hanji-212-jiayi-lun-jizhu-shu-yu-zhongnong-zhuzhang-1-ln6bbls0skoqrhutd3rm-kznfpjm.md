# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》212｜贾谊《论积贮疏》与重农主张（1）

## Summary

Despite its title, this [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode contains no substantive discussion of [[JiaYi|贾谊]], [[LunJiZhuShu|《论积贮疏》]], or agrarian policy. Its supplied body instead presents two remonstrances by [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] to [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]: one stops the emperor from taking an avoidable riding risk, and the other restores rank distinction between Empress Dou and the favored [[ShenFurenWesternHan|慎夫人]] at a Shanglin banquet.

The durable synthesis joins [[ResponsibilityBoundRiskAvoidance|责任绑定风险回避]] to [[CourtCeremonialSpaceBoundary|宫廷礼制空间的公私边界]]. A ruler's body carries public obligations, while seating makes private favor publicly legible. The episode contrasts [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s attempted heir replacement around [[QiJi|戚夫人]] with Liu Heng and Shen Furen's eventual acceptance of rank restraint, but the broader claim that this alone secured Shen's lifelong safety remains an interpretation.

## Key Claims

- The episode portrays Liu Heng as unusually willing to receive petitions and stop his carriage to address useful proposals, although it supplies no complete institutional account of this practice.
- When Liu Heng wants to ride quickly down a steep slope near Baling, Yuan Ang restrains the horse and argues that a ruler should not gamble with danger because his safety also concerns the imperial house and state.
- The host extends that warning to modern corporate leaders, whose private risk-taking can impose losses on investors and organizations; the unnamed mountaineering example remains anecdotal.
- Shen Furen is presented as a Handan-born singer, dancer, and zither player who becomes Liu Heng's favored consort after entering palace life.
- At a Shanglin banquet, Yuan Ang has Shen Furen's seat moved below Empress Dou's because favor cannot erase the distinction between empress and concubine.
- Yuan Ang invokes Qi Ji's mutilation after Liu Bang's death to argue that elevating a favorite beyond rank can expose her to later retaliation; Liu Heng and Shen Furen accept the warning, and Shen reportedly rewards Yuan.
- The episode attributes Shen Furen's survival to restraint, deference toward Empress Dou and Empress Dowager Bo, the absence of a son, and Liu Heng's limits on luxury, but those causal claims are not independently established here.

## Key Quotes

> "千金之子坐不垂堂。" - the maxim Yuan Ang uses in the episode to connect high responsibility with avoidance of unnecessary danger.

> "尊卑有序，上下乃和。" - the episode-attributed principle behind separating the empress's seat from the favored consort's.
