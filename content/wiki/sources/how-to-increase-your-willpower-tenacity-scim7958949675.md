# How to Increase Your Willpower & Tenacity

## Summary
This solo [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] distinguish effortful willpower from habit and review competing accounts of why self-control sometimes declines across tasks. It connects [[EgoDepletionDebate]], [[AnteriorMidCingulateWillpower]], and [[AllostaticEffortAllocation]] to [[SlightlyHarderChoice]]: sleep, stress, pain, illness, beliefs, reward, and context affect available effort, while small safe acts of approach or restraint may provide practice without making discomfort, glucose, or one brain region a universal prescription.

## Key Claims
- [[EgoDepletionDebate]] preserves the conflict between [[RoyBaumeister]]'s limited-resource interpretation and [[CarolDweck]]'s evidence that beliefs about willpower and glucose can alter repeated-task performance.
- The episode treats autonomic state as a prerequisite rather than background: sleep loss, pain, emotional stress, distraction, and illness can reduce access to tenacity and willpower.
- [[AnteriorMidCingulateWillpower]] is presented as an integrative hub for autonomic, motor, reward, interoceptive, exteroceptive, and contextual information, not a simple on/off willpower switch.
- [[JoeParvizi]]'s direct-stimulation work is used as causal evidence for an anticipatory push-through state, while task, lesion, dieting, anorexia, exercise, and super-aging findings remain heterogeneous and source-scoped.
- [[AllostaticEffortAllocation]] explains why constant exertion is not the goal: effort must compete with rest, habits, strategy switching, recovery, and other bodily demands.
- [[SlightlyHarderChoice]] gains the episode's “micro-sucks” version: choose small, safe, genuinely unwanted actions or restraints, but do not turn food restriction, overtraining, or psychological harm into willpower practice.
- Occasional reward after challenge may reinforce future persistence, but the animal stress-relief evidence does not establish a universal reward schedule for people.

## Key Quotes
> "I absolutely will" and "I absolutely won't" - the episode's two directional forms of willpower.

> "micro sucks" - Huberman's label for small, safe, deliberately effortful challenges.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is recorded. The episode explicitly presents ego depletion as contested and synthesizes resource, glucose, belief, and body-state influences rather than declaring one complete explanation.
- Its willpower-training proposal is narrower than a willpower-only model: [[SelfControlStrategyToolkit]], [[StateDependentSelfControl]], and [[SystemDesignOverWillpower]] show that strategy, environment, recovery, and support can be better interventions than adding effort.
- Claims about anterior mid-cingulate size or activity, obesity, dieting, anorexia, achievement, aerobic exercise, super-aging, cross-domain transfer, stress relief, and a “will to live” remain source-scoped public neuroscience rather than individualized medical, psychiatric, eating-disorder, or exercise guidance.
