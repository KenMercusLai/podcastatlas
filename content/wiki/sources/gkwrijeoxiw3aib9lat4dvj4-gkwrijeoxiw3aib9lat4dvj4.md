# 体验经济火热升温，主打玩乐生意的 Klook 为何依然难赚钱？

## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode uses [[Klook]] to explain why strong demand and booking growth do not automatically produce profits in destination activities. Its durable contribution is [[DestinationExperienceMarketplaceEconomics]]: tours, attraction tickets, transport, and cultural activities combine fragmented local supply, weak standardization, late-forming demand, price comparison, cross-border localization, and exception-heavy support, so scale can expand operating and marketing work instead of rapidly diluting it.

The opening roundup adds source-scoped updates on media-driven destination demand, [[Airbnb]] expanding from accommodation toward services and hotels, U.S. AI-safety and copyright disputes involving [[OpenAI]] and [[Microsoft]], and [[Tesla]] reallocating proposed factory capacity from Cybertruck toward [[CyberCab]].

## Key Claims
- The source says [[Klook]] grew from a 2014 attempt to make hard-to-find local activities bookable across language and payment barriers into a platform covering more than 4,000 destinations.
- Klook reportedly completed more than 60 million bookings in the 12 months through September 2025, served more than 10 million users, and generated over $400 million of 2024 revenue, yet remained unprofitable and withdrew its U.S. listing application.
- The episode attributes the profitability gap partly to nonstandard supply: pickup, food, guides, meeting points, language, cancellation, weather, and local-provider capabilities vary by product and destination.
- Experience demand often forms later than demand for flights or hotels, so the platform spends on inspiration and creator-led discovery before users begin an explicit search.
- Transparent products such as theme-park tickets and rail passes invite comparison, discounts, and subsidies that can compress platform margin.
- The source reports that sales and marketing expense reached $179 million in the first three quarters of 2025, or 7.8% of gross booking value, versus an episode-cited 4.4% comparison for Booking.
- Klook reportedly automated about two-thirds of customer inquiries and outsourced some support, but the episode's complaint cases show how unclear listings, delayed ticketing, refunds, and changing entitlements can damage trust and add compensation cost.
- A source-reported concentration of roughly 80% of business in Asia-Pacific gives Klook local supply knowledge while increasing exposure to regional policy, currency, and travel-demand shocks.
- The opening news item says [[Airbnb]] is adding standalone photography, rental-car, grocery-delivery, and boutique-hotel services while planning an AI travel agent, although its CEO is reported to believe travelers still want to browse and compare.
- The Tesla item says the company plans to stop Cybertruck production around mid-2027 and expand [[CyberCab]] capacity; the production, sales, and fleet figures remain episode-reported rather than independently verified here.

## Key Quotes
> "即使订单持续增加，这类依赖线下履约的业务也难以像纯数字产品一样迅速摊薄成本。" - the episode's synthesis of the scale problem.

> "自动化和外包能够降低客服成本，但面对高度非标准化的体验产品时，也可能放大信息传递与责任协调的问题。" - qualification on support automation.
