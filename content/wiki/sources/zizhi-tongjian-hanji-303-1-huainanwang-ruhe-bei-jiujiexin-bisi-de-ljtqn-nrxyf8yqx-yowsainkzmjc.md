# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》303-1｜淮南王如何被“纠结心”逼死的？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the planning stage immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-303-2-saiweng-shima-de-zuozhe-shi-zenme-si-de-lp9u6yu1la2w6sfqwqfikcvgq9pt|Hanji 303-2]]'s disclosure and destruction of the Huainan case. [[WuBeiWesternHan|伍被]] reportedly tells [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]] that neither the princes nor the people support revolt, then offers a low-probability scheme built on forged orders, forced migration, arrests, and manufactured fear; Liu An instead cycles through forged seals, an attempted infiltration of [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]]'s household, palace fire, false border alarms, and the killing of kingdom officials without settling on an executable course.

When the central court sends officers to arrest crown prince [[LiuQianHuainanCrownPrinceWesternHan|刘迁]], the Huainan leadership cannot mobilize its own senior officials. Liu An releases the only chancellor who answers his summons, while Liu Qian proposes taking responsibility and attempts suicide. The durable synthesis is not that decisiveness would have made rebellion viable: the episode itself depicts a weak political base, implausible plans, and no reliable coalition, while its “纠结心” interpretation shows how continued planning added exposure without producing either action or withdrawal.

## Key Claims

- The episode opens with a `Huainanzi` teaching that things have different strengths and limits, then uses Liu An's conduct as a source-framed failure of self-knowledge.
- Wu Bei says the princes lack rebellious intent and the population lacks grievance; his proposed workaround is to forge high-level orders that create forced migration, elite arrests, fear, and resentment.
- Even Wu Bei gives the manufactured-crisis plan only a one-in-ten chance, making the proposal evidence of poor underlying feasibility rather than a sound plan awaiting execution.
- Liu An allegedly forges imperial and official seals and tallies, considers placing an assassin near Wei Qing, and judges [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] harder to corrupt than officials such as [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]].
- Liu An and Wu Bei consider drawing the kingdom chancellor into a palace fire, killing him, and using a fabricated Nanyue border alarm to justify troop mobilization, but none of the schemes becomes a settled operation.
- After court officers arrive to arrest Liu Qian, Liu An and his son summon senior kingdom officials with a plan to kill them and rise; only the chancellor appears, and Liu An lets him leave because the kingdom's 内史 and commandant do not come.
- Liu Qian then says the usable officials have already been removed, offers to bear responsibility for his father, and attempts suicide but survives within the supplied excerpt.

## Key Quotes

> “诸侯无异心，百姓无怨气。” - Wu Bei's diagnosis of the missing political conditions for rebellion.

> “只杀国相无益，内史、中尉都不来。” - the immediate reasoning attributed to Liu An when the attempted official roundup fails.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. This episode supplies the operational prelude to Hanji 303-2's confession, arrest, suicide, punishment, and abolition sequence.
- The title says Liu An was “逼死,” but the supplied excerpt stops after Liu Qian's failed suicide attempt and does not narrate Liu An's death; that outcome comes from the adjacent Hanji 303-2 source note.
- The episode's private dialogue, exact forged documents and seals, assassination plan, official attendance, internal motives, corruptibility judgments, and one-in-ten estimate remain source-scoped pending comparison with received historical texts.
- The host's “纠结心” diagnosis is useful for analyzing execution failure, but the same source says the political base was weak and the schemes implausible. It therefore does not establish that quicker rebellion would have succeeded.
- The opening `Huainanzi` passage is presented as a thematic paraphrase from 《说林训》; this ingest does not independently verify the wording, chapter context, or Liu An's personal authorship of that passage.
