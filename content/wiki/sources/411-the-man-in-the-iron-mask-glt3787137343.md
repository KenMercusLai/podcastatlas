# 411. The Man in the Iron Mask

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode moves from [[AlexandreDumas]] and [[Voltaire]]'s famous versions of the [[ManInTheIronMask]] to the surviving prison record. Three contemporary notices establish a masked state prisoner connected to [[SaintMars]] and eventually the Bastille, while correspondence from 1669 lets the hosts identify [[EustacheDege]] as the most plausible match and reject royal-twin, royal-father, Mattioli, and English-candidate theories.

The identification is stronger than the explanation. Degé can be tracked from arrest at Calais through Pignerol, Exilés, Sainte-Marguerite, and the Bastille, but the source cannot establish what he knew or why [[LouisXIV]] required such severe isolation. [[SecrecyDrivenHistoricalMyth]] captures the episode's larger point: concealment can advertise importance, attract conjecture, and give literary invention a more durable life than the surviving facts.

## Key Claims

- Contemporary reports from 1687, 1698, and 1703 support the existence of an unnamed masked prisoner, but disagree on whether the mask was steel or black velvet.
- The continuing presence of [[SaintMars]] across Pignerol, Exilés, Sainte-Marguerite, and the Bastille supplies the administrative thread connecting the prisoner records.
- Letters, a lettre de cachet, chronology, and prison transfers make [[EustacheDege]], a valet arrested in Calais in 1669, the episode's strongest identification.
- Degé's work as Nicolas Fouquet's valet argues against claims that he was a prince, king's brother, or other high-ranking noble.
- [[Voltaire]] amplified the story with claims of an iron mask, elaborate feeding mechanism, exceptional respect, and royal birth; [[AlexandreDumas]] later made the twin theory central to popular memory.
- The episode rejects Ercole Mattioli because newly found Saint-Mars correspondence indicates that he probably never reached the Bastille, and it rejects English candidates partly because no English or Italian accent was reported.
- Degé may have known something connected to the secret Anglo-French diplomacy that produced the Secret Treaty of Dover, but the episode presents this as conjecture rather than a documented cause of imprisonment.
- The extraordinary precautions used to suppress the prisoner's identity encouraged observers to infer importance and thereby helped manufacture the mystery they were meant to contain.

## Key Quotes

> “made everyone try to guess who he was” — the episode's paraphrased lesson from Saint-Mars about the effect of his own security measures.

## Contradictions

- The source corrects the familiar literal “iron mask” image: one early report says steel, but the 1703 Bastille record describes black velvet.
- It rejects royal-brother, twin, father, Mattioli, Cromwell-family, and Monmouth identifications as unsupported or inconsistent with the documentary trail.
- No settled contradiction with [[479-the-french-revolution-the-storming-of-the-bastille-part-5-glt5485693441]]: both distinguish real arbitrary detention from later imagery, while this episode supplies the prisoner's likely identity.
- The spelling “Eustache Degé,” exact reason for imprisonment, Secret Treaty of Dover connection, full implications of later marginal notes, and reported treatment remain source-scoped or unresolved.
