# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》209｜周勃，文帝夺权的牺牲品（2）

## Summary

Despite its title, this [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode does not discuss [[ZhouBo|周勃]]. It instead narrates [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s attempt to repair relations with [[ZhaoTuo|赵佗]] after an Empress Lü-era Han campaign fails and Zhao Tuo expands [[Nanyue|南越]], adopts imperial display, and openly contests Han hierarchy.

Liu Heng avoids another immediate expedition. He repairs Zhao Tuo's ancestral graves, honors and appoints his relatives, withdraws the reported border force, and sends [[LuJia|陆贾]] to deliver a letter. The letter combines face-saving blame of the Lü crisis, recognition of autonomy south of the Five Ridges, and an appeal to the human cost of war with a firm demand that two emperors cannot coexist. The host reads the family and grave measures as both benevolence and latent coercive leverage, making the settlement a second-stage case of [[RecognitionBackedFrontierDiplomacy|册封威慑式边疆外交]].

## Key Claims

- An Empress Lü-era Han force reportedly fails to cross the Five Ridges because of summer heat, humidity, and epidemic disease; after Lü's death it withdraws.
- Zhao Tuo then uses gifts to extend influence toward Minyue, Western Ou, and Luo Yue, enlarges Nanyue's field, and adopts imperial carriage, banner, and title symbolism.
- Liu Heng first changes the bargaining environment by repairing Zhao Tuo's parental graves, assigning officials to maintain sacrifice, and granting office and rewards to Zhao's relatives.
- Lu Jia returns to Nanyue as envoy and carries a letter that separates the new emperor from earlier Lü-family policy while answering Zhao Tuo's requests about relatives and border forces.
- Liu Heng argues that renewed war would kill soldiers, impoverish families, and cost Nanyue more than it gains; conquest would add little land or wealth worth the price to Han.
- The letter offers noninterference south of the Five Ridges and restored exchange, but treats Zhao Tuo's imperial title as an unacceptable two-emperor challenge.
- The host interprets the graves and relatives as dual-use measures: they preserve Zhao Tuo's dignity and kin connection while reminding him that Han controls valued people and sites.

## Contradictions

- Title/body mismatch: the title promises a second installment about Zhou Bo as a victim of Liu Heng's seizure of power, but the supplied body discusses Zhao Tuo, Nanyue, and Liu Heng's letter. No Zhou Bo claim is incorporated.
- Name normalization: the supplied summary repeatedly writes “南粤”; the wiki routes the historical polity to established [[Nanyue|南越]] rather than creating a duplicate identity.
- The host's “hostage” reading of Zhao Tuo's relatives and ancestral graves is an interpretation of leverage, not evidence that Liu Heng formally detained the relatives or threatened to destroy the graves in the supplied letter.
- The episode stops before Zhao Tuo's response, so it establishes the design and delivery of the diplomatic initiative but not its result from this source alone.
- Force names, dates, territorial scale, identities such as 周灶 and 陈濞, exact letter wording, boundary terms, family appointments, motives, and the comparative burden of war remain episode-attributed pending primary-text comparison.
