## Summary
This [[LongNow]] archive episode revisits [[JonathanKeats]]'s talk on [[PublicExperimentalPhilosophy]] and [[DeepTimeMentalProsthesis]]. Keats describes projects that turn thought experiments into public situations: paid time-card thoughts, relativistic time-management objects, epigenetic identity speculation, century and millennium cameras, and [[CenturiesOfTheBristlecone]]. The core contribution is that long-duration artworks can make accountability to future people and ecological time feel concrete before their final outputs exist.

## Key Claims
- [[JonathanKeats]] treats philosophy as an open-ended public experiment rather than only an academic argument with a predetermined conclusion.
- The early gallery time-card project exposes how difficult it is to commodify thought without reducing it to a receipt or representation.
- Relativistic time ingots, time-warp garments, and rotating-city proposals use absurd engineering to question how societies organize labor, lifespan, agriculture, and production.
- Keats's epigenetic "cloning" project uses [[NapoleonBonaparte]] as a speculative case for thinking about identity, environment, historical contingency, and time travel without literal genetic duplication.
- Century and millennium cameras convert future viewers into an imagined audience, making present-day city change and ecological change feel answerable to people not yet born.
- [[DeepTimeMentalProsthesis]] names the source's strongest claim: humans are not naturally equipped to think across millennia, so objects, rituals, and institutions can extend temporal imagination.
- [[CenturiesOfTheBristlecone]] challenges abstract calendar regularity by making a bristlecone pine's growth and climate exposure into a competing timekeeping system.
- The source values institutional support when it creates conditions for open inquiry, but it warns that top-down art-science programs can ossify or instrumentalize experimental work.
