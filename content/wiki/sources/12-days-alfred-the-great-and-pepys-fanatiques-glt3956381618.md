# 12 Days: Alfred the Great and Pepys' 'Fanatiques'

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode pairs two January crises separated by nearly eight centuries. The first follows [[AlfredTheGreat|Alfred the Great]] from [[Guthrum]]'s surprise attack at Chippenham through Athelney and the [[BattleOfEdington]], then connects his survival to London, Mercian cooperation, and the longer process of [[AngloSaxonStateFormation|English state formation]].

The second uses Samuel Pepys's diary to reconstruct [[ThomasVenner]]'s tiny but violent 1661 rising in Restoration London. It presents [[FifthMonarchism]] as an apocalyptic current carried out of the [[EnglishCommonwealth]] and into the reign of [[CharlesIIOfEngland|Charles II]], while arguing that Civil War-era dissent persisted after armed millenarian revolt became politically marginal.

## Key Claims

- The [[GreatHeathenArmy]] and later Viking forces destroyed or subordinated Northumbria, East Anglia, and Mercia before Wessex became the last independent Anglo-Saxon kingdom capable of sustained resistance.
- Guthrum's midwinter seizure of Chippenham brought Alfred close to political destruction, while Alfred's recovery at Athelney and victory at the [[BattleOfEdington]] created the opportunity for negotiated withdrawal and Guthrum's baptism.
- Alfred's later control and fortification of London, combined with dynastic cooperation in Mercia, connected military survival to a wider and more legitimate political project rather than a simple West Saxon annexation.
- The source treats Alfred's survival as a major contingency in the emergence of England, but its global counterfactual remains interpretive rather than established fact.
- [[ThomasVenner]] and roughly 30 Fifth Monarchist followers caused disproportionate panic in London by defeating or escaping larger forces during their January 1661 rising.
- [[FifthMonarchism]] turned the Book of Daniel's sequence of earthly empires into an imminent political expectation that Christ's kingdom would replace restored monarchy.
- The rising shows that the Restoration did not immediately erase Civil War religious radicalism; the episode argues that dissenting energy persisted even as armed apocalyptic politics declined.

## Key Quotes

> “King Jesus and the heads upon the gates” - the insurgents' reported motto joining millenarian expectation to regicide memory.

> “last gasp” - the hosts' characterization of violent religious enthusiasm after the Restoration.

## Connections

- [[AlfredTheGreat]], [[Guthrum]], and [[BattleOfEdington]] - collapse, recovery, victory, and negotiated conversion in 878.
- [[GreatHeathenArmy]] and [[AngloSaxonStateFormation]] - conquest pressure and the longer political consolidation made possible by Wessex's survival.
- [[ThomasVenner]] and [[FifthMonarchism]] - leader and apocalyptic ideology of the January 1661 London rising.
- [[EnglishCommonwealth]] and [[CharlesIIOfEngland]] - revolutionary setting and restored monarchy across which radical expectations persisted.
- [[AnnaTrapnel]] - earlier Fifth Monarchist visionary showing that the movement also operated through prophecy and opposition rather than armed revolt alone.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was found. The episode reinforces the fuller Alfred sequence in the existing wiki while compressing the chronology between Edington, London's recovery, Mercian division, and later territorial settlement; those developments should not be treated as one immediate treaty package.
- Alfred's survival as a necessary condition for England's later global influence, the number and performance of Venner's followers, their precise expectations, and the claimed redirection of radical energy into later dissent, abolitionism, and Atlantic revolution remain source-scoped interpretations.
