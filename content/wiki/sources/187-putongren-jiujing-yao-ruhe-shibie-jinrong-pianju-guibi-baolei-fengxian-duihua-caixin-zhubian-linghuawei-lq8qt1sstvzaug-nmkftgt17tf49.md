# 187.普通人究竟要如何识别金融“骗局”，规避暴雷风险？｜对话财新主编凌华薇

## Summary

This [[QizhulouYanBinke|起朱楼宴宾客]] conversation with [[LingHuaweiCaixin|凌华薇]] examines how ordinary investors can detect failure and fraud risk in P2P platforms, wealth managers, elderly-care projects, village-bank products, and cross-border schemes. Its central qualification is that a dangerous institution may have real operations, qualified staff, years of normal payments, and a polished interface: investors therefore need to verify the legal counterparty, license, product status, account, underlying assets, related-party flows, and commercial logic rather than treating reputation or modest yield as proof of safety. The practical frame joins [[InvestmentFraudRedFlags]] with position limits, diversification, and precommitted exit rules so that one mistake cannot destroy household finances.

## Key Claims

- A business failure, governance collapse, or liquidity crisis is not automatically proof that an institution was fraudulent from inception; the legal characterization and the investor's risk screen are related but distinct questions.
- Real services, long operating history, professional founders, small early benefits, normal early repayments, government-adjacent participation, and a bank-like interface can all transfer trust without establishing product safety.
- A non-financial company raising money from the public, self-financing, a shared controller over industrial expansion and financial risk approval, hidden related-party borrowers, or an opaque fund pool should raise scrutiny.
- A promised return of roughly 5% to 6% can still accompany serious risk; yield level alone cannot verify the counterparty, asset, custody, liquidity, or legal protection.
- Investors should confirm who issues, manages, sells, receives, and owes under the contract, because a bank employee, branch location, or familiar interface can conceal a private-account transfer, unauthorized product, or flying order.
- Product review should identify the underlying asset and distinguish deposits, insurance, equity, standardized securities, and non-standard credit rather than treating all stable-looking products as equivalent.
- Investigation should begin with public records, a timeline, and adverse hypotheses, then cross-check claims with insiders, peers, competitors, regulators, customers, and counterparties instead of collecting only confirming evidence.
- Cross-border investing adds legal, regulatory, account, and market-structure differences; regulated channels such as QDII are distinguished from informal schemes that borrow domestic assumptions.
- Sunk cost can include money, identity, trust, and self-image. Once warnings appear, demands for additional funds to recover old money are a reason to reassess rather than automatically double down.
- Diversification, position limits, and exit conditions should be set before purchase so that employment, income, and investments do not all depend on the same institution.
- The source presents fiduciary duty, client-interest priority, conflict avoidance, and institutional ethics as necessary foundations for expert financial management.
