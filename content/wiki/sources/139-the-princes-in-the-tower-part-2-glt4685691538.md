# 139. The Princes in the Tower Part 2

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[EdwardIV]]'s death through [[RichardIII]]'s seizure of [[EdwardV]], the destruction of rival protectors, the princes' delegitimation, Richard's coronation, and the boys' disappearance from the [[TowerOfLondon]]. It argues that Richard probably improvised rather than began with a settled plan to take the crown, but that each coercive step narrowed his options until usurpation and the likely murder of [[PrincesInTheTower|the princes]] became connected parts of regime survival.

The hosts judge Richard's responsibility overwhelmingly probable while preserving the lack of conclusive proof. Alternative suspects, later pretenders, reported confessions, discovered bones, and survival theories remain useful because they expose the evidentiary limits rather than displacing the strongest political inference.

## Key Claims

- Richard of Gloucester probably did not begin with a long-planned design for the crown; the episode presents his route to kingship as escalating improvisation under factional and succession pressure.
- Control of the twelve-year-old [[EdwardV]] was the decisive political resource after Edward IV's death, making custody, access, armed retinues, and the coronation timetable central to the crisis.
- The arrests of Anthony Woodville and Richard Grey, Elizabeth Woodville's flight to sanctuary, and Edward V's transfer to the Tower isolated the young king from his maternal protectors.
- The summary execution of [[WilliamHastings]] marks the transition from defensive control to open removal of opponents, followed by the acquisition of [[RichardOfShrewsbury]] from sanctuary and a public campaign against the princes' legitimacy.
- [[RichardIII]]'s control of the Tower, political motive, inability to produce the boys, and failure to blame Buckingham later make him the strongest suspect in their disappearance and probable deaths.
- [[HenryStaffordDukeOfBuckingham|Buckingham]] is the main alternative suspect considered, but the hosts doubt he could have acted inside the Tower without Richard's knowledge or authorization.
- Lambert Simnel and Perkin Warbeck show how unresolved royal identity remained politically useful after Bosworth, while the reported James Tyrrell story and the 1674 bones do not provide conclusive proof.
- The Missing Princes Project's proposed Devon survival theory is treated skeptically because it requires both Richard III and Henry VII to preserve and conceal a dangerous rival.
- The case endures because probability can be strong without becoming certainty, and because romance, dynastic memory, literature, material remains, and political incentive support competing narratives.

## Key Quotes

> "the overwhelming probability" — the hosts' confidence level for Richard's responsibility.

> "no conclusive answer is possible" — the episode's boundary against claiming proof.
