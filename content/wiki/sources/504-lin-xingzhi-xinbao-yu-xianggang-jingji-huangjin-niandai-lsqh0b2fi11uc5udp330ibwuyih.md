# 504 林行止、《信报》与香港经济黄金年代

## Summary
This [[HuzuoHuyou|忽左忽右]] episode has host [[ChengYanliang|程衍樑 / 成言梁]] and former Lin Xingzhi column editor [[ZhengShiliang|郑诗亮]] reconstruct [[LinXingzhi|林行止]]'s path from Chaoshan displacement and work at Ming Pao to founding the [[HongKongEconomicJournal|《信报》 / Hong Kong Economic Journal]]. It presents the newspaper as a public-knowledge institution that joined markets, public policy, international affairs, and readable Chinese for Hong Kong's expanding middle class. Its durable contributions are [[FinancialCommentaryKnowledgeTranslation|financial-commentary knowledge translation]] and two tests of newsroom independence: [[JournalisticIndependenceAgainstFaction|refusing factional alignment]] and [[AdvertiserPressureEditorialIndependence|resisting advertiser pressure while correcting factual error]].

## Key Claims
- [[LinXingzhi|林行止]] is presented as a refugee, self-taught economic writer, editor, newspaper founder, and unusually prolific columnist whose life offers one route through postwar [[HongKong|Hong Kong]] economic and political change.
- Work under [[JinYong|金庸]] at Ming Pao gave Lin a research and editing apprenticeship, but disagreements over newspaper direction later helped push him toward an independent financial paper.
- Lin and [[LuoYoumei|骆友梅]] founded the [[HongKongEconomicJournal|《信报》]] in 1973; its survival depended on her administration, staffing, financing, and media network as well as his writing and editorial vision.
- The paper filled more than a price-information gap. Through [[FinancialCommentaryKnowledgeTranslation]], it connected stocks and firms to economics, institutions, public policy, and international events in accessible Chinese.
- [[ZhangWuchang|张五常]]'s column shows the paper acting as an editorial bridge between specialist economics and a Chinese-reading public: Lin supplied a platform, editing, and encouragement while Zhang supplied theory and cases.
- Lin's market-liberal stance included skepticism toward large public spending and welfare intervention, but the episode frames his stable reference point as Hong Kong residents' interests rather than loyalty to Beijing, London, or the colonial administration.
- [[AdvertiserPressureEditorialIndependence]] is illustrated by the paper's refusal to retreat when criticism of [[LiKaShing|李嘉诚]] prompted a threat to withdraw substantial advertising.
- The same independence standard required correction rather than stubbornness: after learning that criticism of Wu Jinglian contained factual error, Lin apologized and stopped using that contributor.
- Lin and [[JinYong|金庸]] combined early mentorship, newspaper competition, and public disagreement over Hong Kong's constitutional future; the episode treats their continued private civility as compatible with sharp public conflict.
- The sale of the paper to [[LiTzarKai|李泽楷]] between 2006 and 2014 marks a shift from founder-led literati journalism to group-owned commercial media, while the episode argues that some early editorial character persisted.
- The closing frame defines Hong Kong literati journalism as a portable cultural practice: inherit local culture, absorb other knowledge systems, judge independently, care about public reality, and explain complexity in one's own language.
