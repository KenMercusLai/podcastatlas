# 271.唐朝都要不存在了？为什么伪史论会在今年大爆发？

## Summary
This [[Luanfanshu|乱翻书]] live episode has [[PangLuan|庞乱]], [[HeSenbao|何森堡]], [[MenggeTiancaiBushou|猛哥]], and [[SongFangjin|宋方金]] discuss the 2026 surge of [[PseudoHistoryConspiracy|伪史论]], from older claims that Greek or Roman civilization was fabricated to newer claims that the [[TangDynasty|唐朝]] did not exist. Its central claim is that pseudohistory is a self-expanding [[ConspiracyTheoryPatternSeeking|conspiracy mechanism]]: once a claim rejects ordinary evidence, it must reject more documents, archaeology, cross-cultural traces, and professional judgment to stay coherent. The episode links that evidentiary failure to short-video distribution, [[PublicRelevanceAlgorithms|algorithmic public relevance]], simple-causality comfort, [[ProfessionalCommunityTrust|professional community trust]], and the risk that false history turns into scapegoating.

## Key Claims
- [[PseudoHistoryConspiracy|伪史论]] is not the same as [[EvidenceBoundHistoricalRevision|evidence-bound historical revision]]: it begins with a desired reversal and then rejects the evidence system that could test the reversal.
- The episode treats the "唐朝不存在" claim as a late-stage escalation, because it moves pseudohistory from flattering nationalist stories about the West into denial of a central Chinese dynasty and its material traces.
- [[HeSenbao|何森堡]] argues that a fabricated civilization or dynasty would need coordinated sources, archaeological remains, institutions, scripts, cultural influence, and foreign contacts; once a society interacts with others, a total invention becomes increasingly hard to sustain.
- [[RosettaStone|罗塞塔石碑]] and [[JeanFrancoisChampollion|商博良]] are used as a compact example of cross-checked historical evidence: known Greek text, Egyptian scripts, dynastic context, and phonetic names make the decipherment more than a single unsupported story.
- The source presents denial of [[Aristotle|亚里士多德]] as a method error: a large corpus can involve students, school notes, later editing, and transmission history without proving that the thinker was invented.
- [[AnatolyFomenko|福缅科]] is presented as an international pseudohistory source whose Russian-centered framework is selectively imported while dropping the part that also denies Chinese history.
- Short-video platforms and [[AlgorithmicAmplification|algorithmic amplification]] help extreme claims travel farther than careful correction, especially when anger, comedy, and identity reward make pseudohistory easier to circulate than evidence.
- The discussion treats economic stress and life uncertainty as one soil for conspiracy belief, but not the only one; the broader mechanism is a desire for order, safety, and blame in a complex world.
- [[SongFangjin|宋方金]] frames pseudohistory as a failure to tolerate multi-causal reality: people often want one clean cause, one hidden actor, and one moral explanation for confusing outcomes.
- [[ProfessionalCommunityTrust|professional community trust]] is necessary because ordinary people cannot personally verify every claim in history, physics, mathematics, medicine, or spaceflight; the question becomes which communities and methods are trustworthy.
- The episode warns that pseudohistory can move from entertainment into harm: it starts with falsehood, then often needs a group to blame for national humiliation, personal failure, or social pain.
- Public refutation is framed as [[PublicArgumentForBystanders|argument for bystanders]]: the goal is less to convert hard-core believers than to leave evidence and judgment available for people who have not yet joined the closed group.
