# 19.荒野上的大师：“我辈于乱世求研究，本为逆流之妄举”

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] live conversation with [[ZhangQuan|张泉]] uses [[HuangyeShangDeDashi|《荒野上的大师》]] to revisit the formation of modern Chinese scholarship through four institutions: [[GeologicalResearchInstitute|地质研究所/地质调查所]], [[TsinghuaGuoxueInstitute|清华国学研究院]], [[InstituteOfHistoryAndPhilology|历史语言研究所]], and [[SocietyForResearchInChineseArchitecture|中国营造学社]]. The episode argues that figures such as [[DingWenjiang|丁文江]], [[LiangSicheng|梁思成]], [[LinHuiyin|林徽因]], [[ZhaoYuanren|赵元任]], and [[FuSinian|傅斯年]] mattered not only as famous scholars, but as people who rebuilt methods, archives, field practices, and institutions under war, poverty, migration, and political pressure. Its closing turns the historical question back toward the present: even when body or environment is constrained, intellectual and spiritual freedom can remain a live practice.

## Key Claims
- [[HuangyeShangDeDashi|《荒野上的大师》]] frames the birth of modern Chinese scholarship through institutions and field practices rather than through isolated genius.
- The episode treats "荒野" literally and methodologically: mines, mountains, temples, archaeological sites, old buildings, and dialect villages become places where knowledge is made.
- [[DingWenjiang|丁文江]]'s rereading of [[XuXiake|徐霞客]] is presented as a way to give Chinese field inquiry a historical ancestor without reducing Xu to a mere eccentric traveler.
- The early geology branch joins state need, mining, paleontology, international expertise, and local labor into a modern research program.
- [[LiangSicheng|梁思成]] and [[LinHuiyin|林徽因]] are treated less as gossip figures than as builders of a field: their work depends on difficult travel, social negotiation, measurement, and institutional survival.
- [[ZhaoYuanren|赵元任]] makes language research a fieldwork practice through dialect listening, recording technology, and attention to local speech amid pressure for national-language unity.
- [[FuSinian|傅斯年]] and [[DingWenjiang|丁文江]] appear as institution designers whose administrative labor consumed their own research ambitions.
- [[InstituteOfHistoryAndPhilology|史语所]] and the [[Yinxu|殷墟]] excavations show how Chinese scholars used modern archaeology to push Chinese civilization history into globally legible evidence.
- The source presents worldliness and nationalism as compatible in this generation: scholars learned from Western methods and international collaborators while trying to know China's land, language, buildings, and past on Chinese terms.
- Wartime scholarship is shown as materially fragile: books burn, classrooms become tin-roofed huts, funds vanish, institutes migrate, and fieldwork competes with survival.
- The discussion of [[ChenYinke|陈寅恪]] and [[WangGuowei|王国维]] links scholarly institutions to [[AcademicFreedom|academic freedom]] and the defense of an independent desk.
- The closing [[QianLiqun|钱理群]] section turns the episode from commemoration into a question of present-day [[SpiritualFreedomUnderConstraint|spiritual freedom under constraint]].

## Key Quotes
> "我辈于乱世求研究，本为逆流之妄举" - phrase used to frame the generation's improbable scholarly work.

> "听雨" - the episode's image for makeshift wartime classrooms where rain on tin roofs could stop teaching.

> "汽油是人民的血汗钱" - Fu Sinian's reported public-resource ethic in his late Taiwan University period.
