# 677. USA: The Star-Spangled Banner (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode opens a World Cup-linked anthem series by treating [[StarSpangledBanner|The Star-Spangled Banner]] as a contested historical object rather than a simple patriotic song. It follows the [[WarOf1812]], the British attack on [[FortMcHenry]], [[FrancisScottKey]]'s poem, the borrowed [[AnacreonticSong|Anacreontic Song]] melody, and the song's eventual 1931 official adoption.

The episode's strongest wiki contribution is the U.S. case of anthem ambiguity. The hosts keep the third-verse "hireling and slave" controversy unresolved, but they place it beside British wartime recruitment of escaped enslaved people into the [[ColonialMarines]], Key's own slaveholding and anti-abolitionist record, and later anthem protests by [[ColinKaepernick]] and [[BlackLivesMatter]]-era activists.

## Key Claims

- [[StarSpangledBanner|The Star-Spangled Banner]] originated as Key's poem "The Defence of Fort McHenry" after the September 1814 British bombardment of [[FortMcHenry]] during the [[WarOf1812]].
- The song was first a broadside ballad set to [[AnacreonticSong|The Anacreontic Song]], a familiar English tune associated with the Anacreontic Club rather than a newly composed American melody.
- [[MaryPickersgill]] made the large Fort McHenry flag raised after the attack, while [[GeorgeArmistead]] had ordered a very large garrison flag for the fort.
- The anthem's third verse remains contested because its "hireling and slave" line may refer to British subjects and mercenaries, escaped enslaved people fighting with Britain, or both meanings in a liberty-slavery rhetoric already full of contradiction.
- British Chesapeake strategy relied on escaped enslaved people for intelligence and manpower, and the [[ColonialMarines]] made Black flight from slavery a direct military and symbolic threat to American slaveholders.
- [[FrancisScottKey]] was not just an anthem author but a slaveholder, colonization supporter, and later Washington, D.C. district attorney who opposed abolitionism.
- The song became official only in 1931 after long institutional use, veteran lobbying, and objections that its tune was British, its lyrics militaristic, and its range hard to sing.
- Modern disputes around Jose Feliciano, Jimi Hendrix, Tommie Smith, John Carlos, Borat, [[ColinKaepernick]], and [[BlackLivesMatter]] show that anthem conflict often turns on who may reinterpret national symbols and how reverently.

## Key Quotes

> "The Defence of Fort McHenry" - original publication title before the later anthem name.

> "hireling and slave" - disputed third-verse phrase at the center of the slavery controversy.

> "land of the free" - liberty formula the episode reads beside escaped enslaved people's flight to British lines.
