# 58. The World Cup of Gods - Part 1

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses the first-round losers in a listener-voted “World Cup of Gods” to compare [[Moloch]], [[XipeTotec]], [[IshtarInanna]], [[BrigidOfKildare]], [[OctavianAugustus|Augustus]], [[Loki]], [[Cybele]], and [[PrincePhilip]]. The comic tournament becomes a survey of how sacrifice, fertility, war, sex, political authority, sainthood, literary transmission, and modern media complicate any fixed definition of a god.

Its strongest contribution to [[AncientDivineIdentitySyncretism]] is the identity boundary itself. Moloch may name a sacrifice rather than a deity, Brigid may be a historical saint later given goddess-like traits, Augustus crosses from human rule into imperial cult, and Prince Philip shows modern worship developing around a living public figure. The episode also argues that Christian and Islamic expansion ended or absorbed many older cults, while names, motifs, rituals, and stories survived through changed institutions.

## Key Claims

- The tournament excludes continuously worshipped gods, which makes its field Eurocentric and turns religious discontinuity into an eligibility rule rather than a neutral global sample.
- [[Moloch]] remains evidentially ambiguous: biblical condemnation, classical testimony, Carthaginian archaeology, ritual vocabulary, and later literature do not settle whether the name identifies a god, an offering, or a rite.
- [[XipeTotec]] joins flaying, agricultural renewal, disease, healing, and priestly embodiment, showing that divine blessing and harm need not be separate domains.
- [[IshtarInanna]] combines love, war, kingship, ritual boundary-crossing, death, and seasonal return; her underworld myth and later reception resist a single functional label.
- [[BrigidOfKildare]] illustrates a disputed goddess-saint relationship in which pagan evidence, historical biography, hagiography, and later interpretation may run in more than one direction.
- [[OctavianAugustus|Augustus]] shows deification operating through peace, imperial gratitude, ruler cult, funeral spectacle, and succession politics rather than a strict mortal-immortal divide.
- [[Loki]], [[Cybele]], and [[PrincePhilip]] extend the identity problem through literary rewriting, ecstatic and gender-crossing ritual, modern media, and living cult formation.
