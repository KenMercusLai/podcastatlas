# 46 古人如何长生不老？东西方的炼金术、炼丹术与长生药

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] episode compares Chinese immortality practice and [[DaoistAlchemy|Daoist external alchemy]] with [[WesternAlchemy|Mediterranean, Islamic, and European alchemy]]. It traces Chinese immortal-island searches, cinnabar and gold theories, external and internal cultivation, and furnace ritual alongside Greek element theory, Arabic translation and experimentation, metal transmutation, the philosopher's stone, universal medicine, and artificial-life stories.

Its central synthesis is that alchemy combined unsupported supernatural goals with real material practice. Furnaces, metallurgy, distillation, pharmacy, and repeated manipulation of substances helped preserve useful techniques, but [[AlchemyChemistryContinuity|modern chemistry did not emerge merely by accumulating alchemical recipes]]: measurement, explicit records, repeatable conditions, public testing, and willingness to reject failed premises were decisive. The episode closes with mercury and mineral toxicity, imperial deaths, poetic criticism, and the judgment that accepting finite life is safer than turning mortality anxiety into dangerous medicine.

## Key Claims

- Chinese immortality belief and bodily cultivation preceded mature external alchemy; elixir making developed as one proposed route to transcendence rather than the origin of every immortal practice.
- Coastal sacred geography, imperial mortality anxiety, and specialist promises made [[XuFu|徐福]], [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]], [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], and [[LiShaojunWesternHan|李少君]] part of a political economy of search, ritual, materials, and patronage.
- External alchemy treats the furnace as a small cosmos that can accelerate transformations otherwise attributed to nature; internal alchemy relocates transformation into breathing, meditation, diet, and the body.
- [[WeiBoyang|魏伯阳]]'s death-and-revival legend illustrates how elixir danger could be recoded as a test of faith, while [[GeHong|葛洪]] preserves the story in the episode's account.
- Western alchemy moved through Hellenistic Egypt, Arabic-language scholarship, and Latin translation rather than developing inside one unchanged European tradition.
- [[JabirIbnHayyan|Jabir ibn Hayyan]] represents systematic material classification and laboratory technique, while [[IbnSinaAvicenna|Ibn Sina]] supplies an internal critique that apparent change does not prove genuine transmutation.
- European alchemy joined gold making and medicine to the philosopher's stone, universal cure, and homunculus, with [[Paracelsus]] emphasizing mineral and chemical medicine.
- [[RobertBoyle|Robert Boyle]] is used to mark a methodological turn toward measurement, recorded conditions, and reproducibility; [[IsaacNewton|Newton]] shows that experimental science, theology, and alchemy still overlapped in the early modern period.
- Mercury, lead, sulfur, and cinnabar could appear symbolically powerful because of color and phase changes, but their toxicity made some immortality medicines causes of injury and death.

## Key Quotes

> “人生非金石，岂能长寿考” - verse from *Nineteen Old Poems* used to reject the fantasy that a human body can share the durability attributed to metals and stone.

> “服食求神仙，多为药所误” - the episode's closing poetic warning that elixir seeking can shorten the life it is meant to preserve.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode reinforces existing pages that distinguish early court fangshi from later systematized Daoist alchemy and that treat alchemy-chemistry history as continuity plus methodological rupture.
- The claim that Chinese, Indian, and Mediterranean alchemy developed largely in parallel is presented as the episode's summary of a disputed scholarly question, not as proof that transmission never occurred.
- Dates, etymologies, authorship, translation routes, recipes, metal theories, imperial death counts, and individual biographies remain episode-attributed pending specialist verification.
- The supplied file is a structured podcast summary, not a primary-text or laboratory dossier. It does not verify transmutation, immortality, philosopher's stones, homunculi, mineral medicines, or supernatural revival.
- Similar materials and goals do not establish institutional equivalence among Chinese external alchemy, internal cultivation, Indian traditions, Arabic-language alchemy, and European natural philosophy.
