# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》644丨谁敢和皇帝使者讨价还价？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills [[WangMang|王莽]]'s 11 CE [[XinDynasty|新朝]] crisis between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-643-weihuo-zhengfa-xiongnu-weihe-hui-bei-bishi-lr-5-qxjm4fqlirvwhdwelo1elg5|Hanji 643]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-645-bidu-zhichang-quanmou-mingzhebaoshen-or-niliu-ershang-lvdqcakza0zqextrhympnpswxyjy|Hanji 645]]. It pairs frontier and interior extraction with [[GongShengXinScholar|龚胜]]'s refusal of Wang Mang's summons as师友祭酒, then contrasts fatal loyalist refusal with safer withdrawal strategies by [[XueFangXinRecluse|薛方]], [[GuoQinXinOfficial|郭钦]], and [[JiangXuXinOfficial|蒋诩]].

The durable synthesis is that Xin legitimacy is already brittle before later rebellions dominate the story. Military camps, frontier envoys, and local officials convert mobilization into predation; Wang Mang tries to borrow authority from respected scholars through elaborate summons; and former Han loyalists answer with a spectrum ranging from ritual-respectful martyrdom to citation-backed reclusion and illness-based exit.

## Key Claims

- Border troops waiting for mobilization, new taxes, and forced recruitment turn both frontier and interior regions into zones of civilian flight, theft, and disorder.
- Wang Mang's frontier control system multiplies generals, envoys, and inspectors, but posted officials exploit their positions through bribery, seizure, and disruption.
- The northern frontier's post-Han-Xuandi peace collapses after Xin-Xiongnu relations worsen, turning previously secure border life into death, surrender, captivity, and abandonment.
- Wang Mang's summons of Gong Sheng is both honor and pressure: imperial envoys, local officials, elders, students, an official carriage, edict, and seal try to make refusal difficult.
- Gong Sheng's conduct combines ritual respect with political nonrecognition: he receives the edict in illness posture but refuses office because he has received Han favor and cannot serve two dynastic houses.
- The episode treats Gong Sheng, Xue Fang, Guo Qin, Jiang Xu, Huan Xiang's family, and Tang Zun as a spectrum of Xin-era scholar responses, not a single model of resistance.
