# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》314-2｜弑杀的汉武帝，居然包容他的错？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-314-1-xiongnu-wang-guijiang-wudi-weihe-nusha-500-hanren-ljb-ocshiauhvyra88aykkqlxkak|Hanji 314-1]] by identifying ironware as the militarily sensitive object in trade between Han merchants and [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]]'s entourage. It presents [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s severe punishment as a nationwide border-security signal, while the earlier episode preserves [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]]'s objection that the merchants could not reasonably treat local Chang'an exchange as prohibited frontier export.

The main branch profiles Ji An as an educated hereditary official whose direct criticism concerned public affairs rather than private requests. Wudi repeatedly sends him away yet brings him back because his frankness is uncomfortable but useful; the episode treats that tolerated corrective role as a scarce constraint on imperial power and argues, without independently proving, that Ji's death contributed to Wudi's later loss of restraint.

## Key Claims

- The episode says the condemned merchants sold ironware to people associated with Hunye King and treats such trade as a possible route by which the Xiongnu could obtain weapons or military material.
- Wudi's severe response is framed as deterrence aimed at merchants across the empire, including trade with Xiongnu who had already surrendered.
- The person transcribed as “几岸” is identifiable from the career and Huaiyang sequence as Ji An, a multigenerational official who entered service through his father and served the future Wudi before and after accession.
- Ji An is portrayed as unusually direct: his advice could be mistaken, but the episode says his recorded exchanges with Wudi concerned public affairs rather than private favors.
- Ill and reluctant to govern Huaiyang, Ji asks to remain near Wudi so that he can correct omissions; Wudi nevertheless uses his reputation in local office.
- Wudi repeatedly distances and recalls Ji because he dislikes the affront of direct criticism but does not want to lose its governing value.
- The episode attributes Wudi's later excesses partly to the disappearance of this constraint, while also previewing that Wudi still chose several capable regents, including a Xiongnu-born minister.

## Key Quotes

> “又不能失去他。” - the episode's compact description of why Wudi recalls a critic he repeatedly sends away.

> “帮助他查漏补缺。” - Ji An's attributed reason for wanting to remain near the emperor despite illness.

## Connections

- [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] - normalized identity behind “几岸” and the central example of public-minded, tolerated direct criticism.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler presented as using severe deterrence at the frontier while retaining an adviser who repeatedly challenges him.
- [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]] - surrendered Xiongnu ruler whose entourage forms the trade context inherited from Hanji 314-1.
- [[SurrenderReceptionDomesticBurden|归降接纳的内外成本冲突]] - the merchant dispute joins a real security concern to the risk of imposing context-blind punishment on domestic subjects.
- [[PowerCenterProximityAdvantage|权力中心接近优势]] - Ji's request to remain beside the emperor treats corrective access as distinct from outward office.
- [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]] - failure contrast invoked by the episode's claim that Wudi became less constrained after Ji's death.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. Specifying ironware is compatible with Hanji 314-1's broader account of restrictions on weapons and money, but the exact goods, statute, location, and legal status still require primary-text comparison.
- The transcript's “几岸” is normalized to 汲黯 from the distinctive Han Jingdi, crown-prince attendant, imperial remonstrance, illness, and Huaiyang career chain; “魂噎王” is normalized to 浑邪王 from the direct continuation of Hanji 314-1.
- The supplied document is a short structured summary rather than a verbatim transcript or primary-source study. Ji's ancestry, seventh-generation claim, education, offices, exact dialogue, the completeness of the “no private requests” claim, Wudi's motives, and the number of removals and recalls remain episode-attributed.
- The episode says Ji's opinions were not always correct but gives no example here, so it does not establish a specific error. Its causal claim that Ji's death enabled Wudi's later excesses is interpretive, not a demonstrated counterfactual.
- The closing Xiongnu regent is not named in this source. The teaser is not used here as direct evidence for [[JinMidiWesternHan|金日磾]] or for the quality of Wudi's full regency arrangement.
