## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] Advice Line episode pairs [[GuyRaz]] with [[MichelleWahler]], co-founder and former CEO of [[BeyondYoga]], to advise [[KariAngoff]] of [[ElectiveStudyAbroad]], the founder of [[GraceAnnUpholstery]], and [[AngelRaymer]] of [[SacredPawsSoapCompany]]. Wahler opens by rejecting the idea that founders must raise money or start with an exit plan, arguing that motivation, control, and a business that supports the founder's life come first. The three calls then apply that operating bias to concrete conversion and delivery problems: qualification before the sales call, peer-group selling for high-ticket experiences, relationship repair with early clients, delivery buffers that create surprise, and a website that has to carry story, trust, and product at the same time.

## Key Claims
- [[MichelleWahler]] says the worst startup advice she hears is that founders must raise capital; she argues they should know their motivation and the business they want, avoid starting with an exit plan, and accept that investors make the exit the focus and change the company's direction.
- She treats a small, sustainable business that pays the founder and gives them freedom as a legitimate outcome, saying founders do not all need to become the next [[Anthropic]].
- [[BeyondYoga]] grew slowly for roughly a decade through wholesalers, yoga studios, and some department stores before [[Levis]] acquired it in 2021 for about $400 million; Wahler left the company in 2024.
- After the exit, Wahler taught a culture-building course at the [[UniversityOfSouthernCalifornia|University of Southern California]] and founded [[QuinceHill]] for investing and mentorship, and she now spends most of her time advising founders and matching them with people who understand web development, marketing, or inventory.
- [[KariAngoff]] reports strong interest in [[ElectiveStudyAbroad]] — high click rates, specific email questions, and steady calls — but low conversion from interest to enrolment, with deadlines and discounts doing little; courses average about $6,200 per person.
- Wahler argues Kari is selling an experience rather than a course, and that destination-specific imagery should replace generic photos of people looking at maps or sitting in a classroom.
- Wahler recommends screening questions before the booking call, including readiness for a seven-day immersive experience, willingness to invest around $6,000 for a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity, and commitment as a lifelong learner; [[GuyRaz]] adds that the peer group itself is what is being sold.
- Guy suggests testimonials that admit initial nervousness and a post-call follow-up email with the sample reading list, and Michelle suggests partnering with high-end custom travel agencies and upscale independent living communities for retirees.
- The [[GraceAnnUpholstery]] founder says early demand overwhelmed her, that she communicated poorly, and that she disappointed interior designers; Wahler says those relationships can be rebuilt if she first fixes her process, apologises briefly, and shows what she can do now.
- Wahler calls "under-promise, over-deliver" a core principle and recommends working back through the client list one at a time, starting with the smallest client, because a second disappointment would be genuinely damaging.
- Guy calls the approach a "second first impression": ask for a small project, over-communicate, photograph the work, and let current designers act as proof rather than asking to be trusted again.
- Wahler says service to designers is linked: good work makes the designer look good to their client, delays embarrass them, and small gestures such as turning leftover fabric into a pillow for the designer create outsized goodwill.
- For scheduling, Wahler recommends timing the actual work with a stopwatch and quoting with enough buffer that early delivery becomes a surprise rather than an expectation.
- [[AngelRaymer]] sells [[SacredPawsSoapCompany]] self-care kits with gemstone-embedded soap, affirmations, and a stone that drops into an exfoliating bag, but says the emotion that works at metaphysical fairs and music festivals does not transfer online.
- Wahler says the product page is attractive and plausibly priced at roughly $35-38, but the site has no story, the About page is empty, and the page repeats "self care kit" instead of naming rose quartz and tiger's eye and the meaning each stone carries.
- Wahler's website rule is that a product applied to skin has to tell the story, build trust through ingredients, and show the product at the same time; Guy adds that the ritual and the feeling of being taken elsewhere are what the page has to reproduce.
- Wahler's closing advice is to remember why you started and stay focused, quoting her husband Jesse's challenge about whether she had served every customer before chasing the next idea: what you say no to matters almost more than what you say yes to.

## Key Quotes
> "You don't have to raise money" - Wahler's summary of the most damaging advice founders receive.

> "Under-promise, over-deliver" - her rule for service businesses rebuilding trust.

> "A second first impression" - Guy's frame for returning to designers with proof instead of excuses.

> "What you say no to is almost more important than what you say yes to" - her closing focus discipline.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction with existing wiki content. The episode reinforces the wiki's pattern that consumer and service businesses still need proof, fit, and repeatable delivery even when the founder story is strong.
- Several figures are episode claims rather than disclosures: the roughly $400 million Levi's acquisition, the $50,000 annual revenue at Grace Ann Upholstery, the $6,200 average course price, and the roughly $35-38 kit price are recorded as source-scoped.
- The coaching is consistent with the wiki's [[SustainableGrowthPace]] and [[CapacityMatchedGrowth]] branches but sharper in tone: Wahler treats raising outside capital as an optional and often distorting choice rather than a default path, which is in tension with growth-first Advice Line episodes that treat capital as a tool for a larger brand.
