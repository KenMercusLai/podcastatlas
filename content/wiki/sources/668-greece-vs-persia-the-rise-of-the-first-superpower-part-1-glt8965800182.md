# 668. Greece vs Persia: The Rise of the First Superpower (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode sets up the [[BattleOfMarathon]] by telling the road from [[CyrusTheGreat|Cyrus]] and [[DariusI]]'s imperial consolidation to the [[IonianRevolt]] and Persian punishment campaign against [[Athens]] and Eretria. It presents the [[AchaemenidEmpire]] as a military, administrative, fiscal, and ideological order rather than a simple foil for Greek liberty.

The episode's strongest wiki contribution is to explain why Athens became a Persian target before Marathon: [[Aristagoras]]' failed Naxos gamble, tactical democratic conversion, Athenian and Eretrian intervention, the burning of [[Sardis]], the destruction of [[Miletus]], and Darius's geopolitical and cosmic-religious motive for retaliation all make the 490 BC expedition feel like an imperial consequence rather than an isolated invasion.

## Key Claims

- [[DariusI]] is presented as a possible usurper, ruthless consolidator, administrator, and ideological ruler who linked imperial order to truth against the lie.
- The [[AchaemenidEmpire]] combines conquest, tribute, provincial rule, royal infrastructure, terror, and religious-political legitimation.
- [[IonianRevolt]] begins from local Greek tyranny, failed Naxos expansion, elite self-preservation, and democratic mobilization rather than from pure idealism.
- The burning of [[Sardis]] in 498 BC turns Athenian involvement into a symbolic and religious affront that Darius cannot ignore.
- The destruction of [[Miletus]] becomes the warning example of what Persian punishment could mean for [[Athens]] and Eretria.
- [[Miltiades]] enters the Marathon story as an Athenian with prior Persian-war experience from the Chersonese, not merely as a battlefield commander.
- The 490 BC Persian campaign mixes terror and diplomacy: Naxos is punished, Delos is honored, Eretria falls through siege and betrayal, and Marathon is chosen for beach, bay, and cavalry terrain.

## Key Quotes
