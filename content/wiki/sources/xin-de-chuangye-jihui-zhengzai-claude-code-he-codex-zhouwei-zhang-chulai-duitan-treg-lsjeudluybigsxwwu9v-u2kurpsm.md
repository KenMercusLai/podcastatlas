## Summary

This [[ShizilukouCrossing|十字路口Crossing]] interview follows [[JasonTrigg|Jason]], [[ShiTianxin|石天鑫]], and [[Trigg]] through three startup directions: [[SuperDesign]], [[Coolit]], and an agent-facing data and tool marketplace. Their operating conclusion is that stronger general agents compress thin vertical wrappers but create new demand for real-time data, professional APIs, cross-agent tool portability, and interfaces for supervising many sessions.

The episode also qualifies full-automation narratives. The team reports that support, bug repair, monitoring, and other narrow deterministic work can be automated more readily than growth, innovation, channel discovery, or product judgment. Its practical thesis is therefore [[HumanAgentCollaboration]] rather than a near-term zero-human company, with [[ClaudeCode]] and [[Codex]] treated as distribution ecosystems on which smaller companies can build.

## Key Claims

- The team moved from the design agent [[SuperDesign]] to the growth agent [[Coolit]] and then to [[Trigg]], using short experiments to decide when model progress or weak results had invalidated a direction.
- Super Design reportedly gained more than 6,000 GitHub stars through open-source distribution, but the team later concluded that general models and coding agents were absorbing more frontend and design capability.
- A roughly two-month attempt to operate Super Design with AI found support and bug fixing substantially more automatable than growth, where channel timing, emerging keywords, competition, and taste still required human judgment.
- A [[Codex]] plugin reportedly changed Super Design's growth curve without additional promotion and brought lower-churn users, illustrating the distribution leverage and dependency risk of an early platform marketplace.
- Trigg aggregates real-time data, professional APIs, and generative-media models behind one key, usage-based billing, and reusable access from multiple agent harnesses.
- The marketplace thesis is that no single supplier is best on coverage, accuracy, and price; task-level feedback from agents could help route later requests toward better tools.
- Small, single-responsibility loops are presented as safer operational automation than broad autonomous management, with monitoring, recovery, and multiple loops used to contain failure.
- Heavy users already run many sessions in parallel, creating demand for task tracking, context transfer, cloud/local coordination, and a primary-agent or “captain and first officer” routing pattern.
- The guests argue that coding speed is becoming less scarce while problem selection, product judgment, cross-domain ability, experimentation, and result review remain differentiating human work.

## Key Quotes

> “零人公司不是现实场景” — the team's six-to-twelve-month boundary after trying to automate Super Design operations.

> “创新无法依靠自动 Loop 完成” — the distinction between open-ended innovation and narrower operational automation.

> “如果没有，就应该考虑转向” — Jason's test for pivoting when no credible experiment could change the current result.
