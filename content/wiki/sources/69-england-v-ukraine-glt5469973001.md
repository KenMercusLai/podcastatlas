# 69. England v Ukraine

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] Euro 2020 special uses England's quarter-final against [[Ukraine]] to survey uneven but revealing Anglo-Ukrainian connections. [[TomHolland]] and [[DominicSandbrook]] move from [[KievanRus|Kievan Rus]], Byzantine Christianity, possible Anglo-Saxon migration to Crimea, and the [[VarangianGuard|Varangian Guard]] through the [[CrimeanWar|Crimean War]], [[JohnHughesWelshIndustrialist|John Hughes]]'s industrial settlement at Huzovka, and [[GarethJonesJournalist|Gareth Jones]]'s reporting on the [[Holodomor]].

The episode's best-supported synthesis is that [[AngloUkrainianRelations]] are episodic rather than one continuous bilateral tradition: migration, war, industrial technology, journalism, and memory create distinct points of contact. Its football, etymological, and historical-character analogies are comic framing, while the proposed medieval “New England” in [[Crimea]] is treated as tantalizing but inconclusive.

## Key Claims

- Medieval river trade tied Scandinavian Rus to Byzantium, and later displaced Anglo-Saxons may have entered Byzantine service through the [[VarangianGuard|Varangian Guard]] after 1066.
- Vladimir's baptism in Crimea and the Christianization of Rus give the peninsula religious as well as strategic significance, but later Ukrainian and Russian memory should not be projected backward as exclusive medieval ownership.
- The [[CrimeanWar|Crimean War]] joined imperial rivalry and holy-place disputes to railways, telegraphy, photography, war reporting, military medical reform, and a British culture of heroic-failure memory.
- Welsh industrialist [[JohnHughesWelshIndustrialist|John Hughes]] and imported skilled workers helped build Huzovka into a coal-and-iron center that later became [[Donetsk]].
- [[GarethJonesJournalist|Gareth Jones]] used direct travel and journalism to publicize the Ukrainian famine later known as the [[Holodomor]], while Walter Duranty's denial illustrates how access and prestige can obstruct truthful reporting.
- Ukraine's twentieth century is presented as a succession of famine, occupation, mass killing, displacement, and war rather than as a simple extension of the episode's football premise.
- Football can channel national rivalry into symbolic competition, but the hosts qualify that optimistic view with cases where sport accompanied or intensified political conflict.
