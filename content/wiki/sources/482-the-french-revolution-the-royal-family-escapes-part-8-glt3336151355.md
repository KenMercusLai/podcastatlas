# 482. The French Revolution: The Royal Family Escapes (Part 8)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs [[FlightToVarennes|the royal family's failed flight to Varennes]] in June 1791 as both a badly executed escape and a political turning point in the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]]. Delays, a conspicuous coach, failed cavalry rendezvous, recognition by [[JeanBaptisteDrouet|Jean-Baptiste Drouet]], and municipal resistance stopped [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]] and [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]] from reaching loyal troops near the frontier.

Its wider argument is that the Revolution itself defeated the plan. Clubs, elected bodies, National Guards, newspapers, religious conflict, inflation, and mass political participation had produced a [[RevolutionaryRuralPoliticization|politicized countryside]] in which local officials treated national representation and public security as stronger claims than personal loyalty to the king.

## Key Claims

- Parisian political culture had become mass and daily by 1790-1791, while bread prices, inflation, strikes, religious conflict, and Assembly exhaustion made settlement difficult.
- Louis publicly accepted revolutionary change while privately rejecting concessions he regarded as coerced; the Civil Constitution of the Clergy sharpened the conflict between kingship, faith, and the new order.
- [[AxelVonFersen|Axel von Fersen]] proposed a more discreet escape, but the royal family's insistence on traveling together required a large, slow, conspicuous berline.
- The plan depended on a precise timetable, hidden identities, staged horse changes, and cavalry escorts, but late departure and repeated delays broke that coordination.
- Louis's confidence that provincial France remained personally loyal to him underestimated the spread of clubs, elected municipal authority, National Guards, and fear of counterrevolution.
- [[JeanBaptisteDrouet|Jean-Baptiste Drouet]] identified the king from his image on paper money and rode ahead to alert Varennes.
- Varennes officials initially delayed rather than immediately attacked the travelers, then confirmed the king's identity and refused passage as armed townspeople gathered.
- [[AntoineBarnave|Antoine Barnave]] developed sympathy for the royal family during the return journey and believed constitutional monarchy could still be stabilized, even as the political center was moving away from that compromise.
- The failed flight and Louis's declaration exposed his rejection of the revolutionary settlement, damaged monarchical legitimacy, and accelerated the path toward republicanism.

## Key Quotes

> "There is no longer a king in France." - Louis's reported response after the order to return to Paris.

> "The revolution itself stopped him." - the episode's concluding explanation for why the escape failed.
