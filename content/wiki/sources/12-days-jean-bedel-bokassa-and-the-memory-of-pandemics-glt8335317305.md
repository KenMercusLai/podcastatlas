# 12 Days: Jean-Bédel Bokassa and the memory of pandemics

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] New Year's Eve episode pairs [[JeanBedelBokassa|Jean-Bédel Bokassa's]] 31 December 1965 coup in the [[CentralAfricanRepublic]] with the first WHO notification of viral-pneumonia cases in Wuhan on 31 December 2019. The first half contrasts Bokassa's military status-seeking, early reforms, Napoleonic coronation, French support, and comic absurdity with arbitrary violence and the killing of schoolchildren; the second asks why COVID-19 might remain more visible in public memory than the 1918 influenza pandemic.

Its connecting argument is that historical importance and historical memory are not identical. Personalist spectacle can make a ruler memorable while obscuring victims, and pandemic memory depends not only on mortality but also on visual imagery, rival crises, government response, technology, connectivity, and the narratives available afterward.

## Key Claims

- Bokassa's French military career, medals, family connection to President David Dacko, command of a very small army, and sensitivity to ceremonial rank formed the immediate background to the 1965 coup.
- His rule combined selected modernizing measures with personal coercion, making reform and brutality simultaneous rather than mutually exclusive.
- The 1977 self-coronation converted scarce public resources and French patronage into [[PersonalistImperialSpectacle|personalist imperial spectacle]], while the diamonds given to [[ValeryGiscardDEstaing|Valéry Giscard d'Estaing]] later damaged the French president politically.
- The episode treats cannibalism allegations skeptically and notes Bokassa's acquittal on that charge; it centers the killing of protesting schoolchildren as the better-grounded atrocity behind France's withdrawal of support and his fall.
- The 1918 influenza pandemic's weak place in popular memory shows that mortality alone does not determine commemoration; World War I supplied a competing event, stronger imagery, and more established public narratives.
- The hosts expect COVID-19 to remain visible through lockdowns, empty-city images, digital communication, border closure, fiscal intervention, and accelerated online life, not only through the disease itself.
- The episode's December 2021 discussion of China and a possible laboratory accident records contemporary uncertainty and suspicion rather than resolving SARS-CoV-2's origin.

## Key Quotes

> "the bourgeoisie" was abolished - the episode's summary of Bokassa's coup broadcast and its improvised revolutionary language.

> "seismic historical event" - the episode's forecast for COVID-19's long-term significance and memory.

## Connections

- [[JeanBedelBokassa]] - soldier, coup leader, president, and self-crowned emperor at the center of the first anniversary.
- [[CentralAfricanRepublic]] - post-colonial state whose weak institutions, French relationship, and political violence structure Bokassa's rise and fall.
- [[PersonalistImperialSpectacle]] - use of coronation, medals, titles, gifts, and state spending to stage one ruler's authority.
- [[ValeryGiscardDEstaing]] - French president politically damaged by the Bokassa diamond affair.
- [[PandemicHistoricalMemory]] - framework comparing the remembrance of 1918 influenza and COVID-19.
- [[PandemicAsHistoricalForce]] - broader account of disease interacting with state action, connectivity, age structure, and political change.
- [[COVIDOriginsInvestigationUncertainty]] - later methodological framework that keeps origin hypotheses open without converting possibility into proof.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing pandemic or Giscard branches was found. The episode adds a memory-centered comparison and more direct Bokassa context.
- Cannibalism testimony is disputed and Bokassa was acquitted of that charge; it should not displace the documented political violence emphasized by the episode.
- Coronation cost, victim totals, French motives, modernization claims, influenza origins and mortality, and causal claims about COVID-era economic or geopolitical change remain compressed or source-scoped.
- The COVID-origin remarks reflect a December 2021 discussion. They do not establish equal probability between hypotheses, prove a laboratory accident, or justify the hosts' forecast that the truth will never be known.
