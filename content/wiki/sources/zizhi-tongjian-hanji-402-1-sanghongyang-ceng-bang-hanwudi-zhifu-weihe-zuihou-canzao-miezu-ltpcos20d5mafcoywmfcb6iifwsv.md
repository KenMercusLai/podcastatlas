# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》402-1｜桑弘羊曾帮汉武帝致富，为何最后惨遭灭族

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the Han Zhao-era sequence between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-401-suihong-zhiming-hanzhaodi-tuiwei-rangxian-huoguang-zaile-ll6pyzpjl0mlcfn0g1zjot2wf8s8|Hanji 401]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-402-2-shudiwushu-de-kuli-duzhou-weihe-zuizhong-deyi-shanzhong-lqrunv51se0w7_2fi2ifs_f7zub4|Hanji 402-2]]. It first records a 78 BCE frontier success against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] after Han intelligence warns Zhangye and Jiuquan defenders, then turns to [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]]'s final destruction after his conflict with [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] and alleged role in the [[LiuDanYanKingWesternHan|燕王刘旦]] rebellion.

The durable synthesis is that political cleanup expands through legal classification. Sang Hongyang's son [[SangQianWesternHan|桑迁]] flees and is hidden by [[HouShiWuWesternHan|侯史吴]]; [[WangPingWesternHan|王平]] and [[XuRenWesternHan|徐仁]] initially treat Hou Shiwu as eligible for amnesty, but Huo Guang pushes a stricter review that recasts harboring and judicial leniency as complicity with rebellion. [[DuYannianWesternHan|杜延年]] tries to keep the punishment boundary from reaching [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]], while warning that excessive law could damage Huo's public reputation.

## Key Claims

- In Yuanfeng 3 / 78 BCE, Xiongnu forces attack the Zhangye frontier after Liwu Wang reports weak Han defenses, but Han's own intelligence enables preparation and counterattack.
- The Zhangye taishou and dependent-state commandant defeat the invading Xiongnu units at Rile, Wulan, and Fanhe; an Yiqu leader in Han service kills Liwu Wang and receives reward and the Liwu Wang title.
- Sang Hongyang is remembered as a major fiscal contributor under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], but his later conflict with Huo Guang and ties to Liu Dan's anti-Huo action make him politically unsafe.
- Huo Guang has the seventy-four-year-old Sang Hongyang executed and his household exterminated, showing that old service does not protect a minister once rebellion and factional hostility combine.
- Wang Ping and Xu Ren initially judge Hou Shiwu's temporary sheltering of Sang Qian as pardonable under the new amnesty because Hou himself was not a rebel.
- Huo Guang rejects that narrow legal reading; the review defines Sang Qian as rebellion-equivalent because he knew his father's plot and did not stop it, and defines Hou Shiwu as an unpardonable harborer of rebels.
- Du Yannian's memorial tries to keep the case from swallowing Tian Qianqiu, arguing that overbroad punishment and accusations of fabricated charges would harm Huo Guang's reputation and public feeling.
