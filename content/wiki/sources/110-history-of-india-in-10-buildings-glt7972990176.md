# 110. History of India in 10 Buildings

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode uses ten buildings and sites to trace a selective history of [[India]] from the [[MohenjoDaro|Indus Valley city of Mohenjo-daro]] to post-independence Delhi. Its central method is [[ArchitectureAsHistoricalEvidence]]: ritual spaces, caves, temples, mosques, capitals, gardens, planned cities, colonial monuments, and demolished modern buildings make trade, religious change, kingship, conquest, empire, and public memory materially visible.

The episode resists a sealed or simply sequential account of Indian civilization. Maritime Islam in Kerala, Central Asian Mughal gardens, local building forms, temple spolia at the [[QutbMinarComplex|Qutb complex]], the colonial afterlife of the Gateway of India, and the destruction of the [[HallOfNationsIndia|Hall of Nations]] and [[BabriMasjid|Babri Masjid]] instead show architecture as a field of adaptation, appropriation, political legitimation, and [[ArchitecturalErasureAndRegimeMemory|regime memory]].

## Key Claims

- [[MohenjoDaro]] shows that the subcontinent's early urban history combined sophisticated water engineering, ritual space, commerce, and unresolved archaeological questions.
- Udayagiri, the Brihadeeswara Temple, Hampi, Jaipur, and the [[QutbMinarComplex]] show rulers using sacred imagery, scale, urban planning, reused material, and controlled space to make authority visible through [[MonumentalDynasticLegitimation]].
- Kerala's Cheraman Jama Masjid complicates an invasion-only account of Islam in India by foregrounding Arabian Sea trade, local conversion traditions, and a mosque expressed in regional architecture.
- Mughal gardens carried Central Asian memory into South Asia and made cultivated landscape part of [[MughalArchitecturalSynthesis|Mughal territorial and political expression]].
- The Gateway of India changed from a monument to imperial arrival into a site of ceremonial British departure, showing that architectural meaning can reverse without the structure changing.
- Partition's weak shared monumental presence, despite the Partition Museum's oral-history work, suggests that traumatic memory can remain politically and institutionally unresolved.
- Demolition is itself historical action: the [[HallOfNationsIndia|Hall of Nations]] and [[BabriMasjid|Babri Masjid]] became battlegrounds over secularism, modernity, Hindu nationalism, and which national past deserves material continuity.
