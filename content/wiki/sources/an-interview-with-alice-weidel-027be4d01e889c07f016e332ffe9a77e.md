# An interview with Alice Weidel

## Summary

[[ZannyMintonBeddoes]] interviews [[AliceWeidel]], co-leader of [[AlternativeForGermany|AfD]], about what the party would attempt in national government. Weidel links Germany's economic problems to energy costs, nuclear shutdowns, taxation, regulation, debt, and migration; advocates a looser [[EuropeanUnion|EU]], stronger German defence, renewed Russian energy access, and an end to German support for [[Ukraine]]; and resists the interviewer's challenges on fiscal arithmetic, Russian aggression, migration removals, social policy, extremism, and coalition compromise.

The interview's main contribution is scope clarification under sustained questioning. A narrow opening definition of [[RemigrationPolitics|remigration]] expands to include the return of Syrians, Afghans, and eventually Ukrainians, while Weidel's self-described libertarianism remains in tension with party positions on schools, family, gender, history, and national identity.

## Key Claims

- Weidel says Germany needs economic reconstruction through cheaper energy, restored nuclear power, lower taxes, less regulation, and spending cuts rather than incremental reform.
- She advocates reforming the EU into a looser community of sovereign states centered on free trade but does not clearly answer whether Germany should leave the euro if that reform fails.
- Her proposed tax and pension program relies on cuts to foreigner welfare, development aid, and climate-related subsidies, while the interviewer argues that the fiscal arithmetic remains unproven.
- She supports higher German defence spending for national capability but opposes military and financial support for Ukraine, favors immediate talks, and gives weight to Russian security objections to NATO expansion.
- Her explanation of remigration expands from people already legally required to leave to Syrians, Afghans, and postwar Ukrainians, which she estimates together at roughly four to five million people.
- She distinguishes asylum from skilled work migration and says Germany needs qualified foreign workers who speak the language, cooperate professionally, and accept equal treatment of women.
- She presents herself as personally libertarian while defending AfD positions favoring traditional family norms, stronger nationalist history education, and restrictions on queer and gender policy.
- She rejects accusations of extremist or neo-Nazi tendencies within AfD and says the [[BrandmauerCordonSanitaire|firewall]] is undemocratic, yet also rules out compromise on the central policies described.

## Key Quotes

> "common sense" - Weidel's description of AfD's positions on borders, families, public order, and schools.

> "no compromise" - her position on the central policies discussed when asked about coalition bargaining.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the wiki is adopted. The source deepens the existing AfD profile from state-election promises to a national governing program and makes the proposed scope of remigration more concrete.
- The interview exposes internal tensions rather than resolving them: national defence versus ending support for Ukraine, tax and pension promises versus uncertain savings, personal libertarianism versus state-backed traditional norms, and demands for coalition access versus refusal to compromise on core positions.
- Energy prices, bankruptcies, fiscal savings, debt projections, migration and crime effects, returnee counts, wartime judgments, polling, party classifications, and historical claims remain participant assertions or interview framing rather than independently verified findings.
