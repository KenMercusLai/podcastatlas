# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》249｜董仲舒：我不是儒家的罪人！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-248-dongzhongshu-ruhe-gaibian-zhonghua-minzu-de-mingyun-1-lnbdqi1evkkfbajdmhh_l5m70n0z|Hanji 248]] by moving [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] from imperial policy questioning into the Jiangdu chancellorship and by placing [[WeiWanWesternHan|魏绾]]'s exclusion proposal beside the rise of court Confucianism. Its interpretive claim is that “罢黜百家，独尊儒术” made Confucianism the dominant public framework without literally eliminating every other school: [[OuterConfucianInnerLegalistGovernance|外儒内法式帝国治理]] could preserve forceful, Legalist-style practice beneath Confucian political language.

The second half follows [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] and [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] as they recommend [[ZhaoWanWesternHan|赵绾]] and [[WangZangWesternHan|王臧]], who plan a Mingtang and invite their teacher [[ShenGongWesternHan|申公]] to court. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s padded carriage, four-horse escort, gifts, and appointment make the summons an early case of [[ImperialTeacherHonorRitual|帝王尊师礼制]], while Shen Gong's advice that government depends on action rather than abundant speech preserves a gap between ceremonial respect and policy substance.

## Key Claims

- The episode says Dong Zhongshu studied the Spring and Autumn tradition intensely, was a court scholar under Emperor Jing, and entered the Jiangdu chancellorship after Wudi valued his policy answers.
- It attributes the proposal to exclude candidates associated with Shen Buhai, Han Fei, Su Qin, and Zhang Yi to Chancellor Wei Wan, with Wudi approving it.
- The host treats “罢黜百家，独尊儒术” as the elevation of a dominant ideological frame rather than the literal disappearance of non-Confucian ideas.
- “Outer Confucian, inner Legalist” describes the resulting tension between ritual-moral presentation and Wudi's centralizing, expansionist, and coercive governing practice.
- Dou Ying and Tian Fen recommend Zhao Wan and Wang Zang, who pursue a Mingtang project and ask the emperor to summon their teacher Shen Gong.
- Wudi receives the elderly Shen Gong with gifts, a cushioned carriage, and four horses; Shen Gong answers the question of order and disorder by emphasizing action over speech.
- Shen Gong is appointed a court grandee and participates in discussions about Mingtang, imperial tours, the calendar, and official colors, although the episode says his plain answer initially disappoints the young emperor.

## Key Quotes

> “最初的罢黜百家、独尊儒术，不是说百家思想真的全部都不要了。” - the host's boundary between ideological predominance and literal intellectual erasure.

> “为治者不在多言，顾力行何如耳。” - Shen Gong's action-centered answer on government, as rendered by the episode.

## Connections

- [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] - scholar whose study, policy access, Jiangdu appointment, and disputed responsibility for later orthodoxy anchor the episode.
- [[WeiWanWesternHan|魏绾]] - chancellor to whom the episode attributes the explicit exclusion proposal.
- [[OuterConfucianInnerLegalistGovernance|外儒内法式帝国治理]] - interpretive frame separating Confucian public orthodoxy from the state's mixed operating methods.
- [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]], [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]], [[ZhaoWanWesternHan|赵绾]], and [[WangZangWesternHan|王臧]] - patronage chain behind the early Mingtang and court-Confucian program.
- [[ShenGongWesternHan|申公]] - elderly teacher summoned from Lu and consulted on government and ritual reform.
- [[ImperialTeacherHonorRitual|帝王尊师礼制]] - mechanism through which transport, gifts, audience, and office make scholarly authority publicly visible.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler who approves ideological exclusion, honors Confucian scholars, and still governs through a broader mixture of methods.

## Contradictions

- The episode defends Dong Zhongshu against responsibility for destroying the Hundred Schools, but it does not establish the formula's textual history, the exact division between Dong's policy answers and Wei Wan's proposal, or the institutional reach of either measure.
- “Outer Confucian, inner Legalist” is the host's retrospective synthesis. It helps distinguish ideology from practice but should not be treated as proof that every Wudi policy followed one coherent hidden Legalist program.
- The Jiangdu ruler is rendered as “易王” in the supplied transcript; the identity and exact title are not normalized here without further collation.
- The appointment dates, official titles, Mingtang plan, travel protocol, dialogue, and reform tasks are retained as episode-attributed details rather than independently verified chronology.
