# 307-为什么国内很多学术翻译质量这么差？

## Summary
This [[DushuBuchenglin|独树不成林]] discussion with [[ZhaoYufei|赵雨飞]] argues that [[AcademicTranslationReliability|academic translation reliability]] begins with fidelity, conceptual precision, and stable terminology rather than literary elegance. A translator of intellectual works must understand an author's wider conceptual system, investigate textual and biographical context, explain departures from established renderings, and preserve ambiguity instead of silently imposing a preferred interpretation.

The episode also develops [[TranslationLaborIncentives|translation labor incentives]] as an institutional constraint. Careful comparison, annotation, and research take substantial time, but low per-character fees, weak academic recognition, limited sales, and thin editorial support make reliable scholarly editions difficult to produce consistently.

## Key Claims
- Bad translations can transfer textual failure onto novice readers, who may interpret incomprehension as evidence that they are incapable of scholarly reading.
- Literary and scholarly translation have different priority structures: style and effect may matter in both, but academic translation must not sacrifice meaning, argument, ambiguity, or terminology for elegant Chinese.
- [[YanFu|严复]]'s “信达雅” belongs to a historical translation program rather than functioning as a self-explanatory modern rule; his “雅” meant an archaizing classical style, and his “信” allowed meaning-level intervention beyond literal correspondence.
- [[AcademicTranslationReliability]] depends on consistent key terms and on understanding an author's whole conceptual system, research tradition, works, correspondence, people, places, and historical setting.
- [[ConceptualTranslationJudgment]] requires translators to justify changes to established renderings and to avoid resolving ambiguities that the source author leaves open.
- A scholarly translator should maintain critical distance from the author: fidelity includes presenting unattractive, offensive, or politically inconvenient material rather than acting as a fan or advocate.
- [[TranslationLaborIncentives]] help explain uneven quality because research-intensive translation receives low fees, limited academic credit, and uncertain market demand.

## Key Quotes
> "译者首先应该是学者" - the episode's compact statement of its ideal for rigorous intellectual translation.

> "准确、忠实、完整" - the closing priority for presenting a thinker's meaning.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source complements the earlier AI-translation episode by specifying the human scholarly practices behind conceptual judgment rather than rejecting machine assistance.
- The episode criticizes some mainland and Taiwan translations from personal reading experience but does not provide systematic side-by-side textual analysis, industry data, or a representative comparison of regions and publishers.
- Fee levels, sales, edition quality, the interpretation of Yan Fu, and the suitability of particular renderings such as virtue, amour-propre, and amour de soi remain source-scoped pending publisher records, primary texts, and specialist scholarship.
