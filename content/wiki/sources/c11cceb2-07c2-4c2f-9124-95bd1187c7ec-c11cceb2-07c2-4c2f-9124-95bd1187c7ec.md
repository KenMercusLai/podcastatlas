# 当 AI 研发下一代 AI，人类研究员还剩下什么？｜S10E36

## Summary

This [[WhatsNextKejiZaozhidao|What's Next｜科技早知道]] episode interviews [[BianJiang|边疆]] about [[AIForAI|AI for AI]], [[RecursiveSelfImprovement|recursive self-improvement]], and the [[RDAgent]] research system. It distinguishes present-day AI-assisted research from stronger recursion: models can already propose ideas, write implementations, run experiments, analyze results, and preserve useful or failed trajectories, but RSI requires the resulting model or research capability to improve the next research loop itself.

The practical center is a verifiable feedback loop. Humans specify objectives, resource constraints, and the broad research frame; agents parallelize research, development, monitoring, and evaluation. The source argues that this can change research organization and increase the speed and breadth of experimentation, but it does not automatically reduce compute demand or prove that AI can make distribution-breaking scientific discoveries.

## Key Claims

- [[AIForAI|AI for AI]] is the broader use of models throughout model and agent research; [[RecursiveSelfImprovement|RSI]] is the stronger condition in which the improved system raises the capability of the next research cycle.
- [[BianJiang|边疆]] reports that roughly a dozen researchers coordinated more than 300 agents over four to five months on post-training a 400B model; the supplied file provides no benchmark, model identity, or independent project record.
- [[RDAgent]] connects research ideation, engineering implementation, experimental execution, result analysis, and another proposal cycle.
- Research automation is most tractable when a proposed solution can receive sufficiently clear, inexpensive, and repeatable feedback through [[AIVerification|verification]].
- Compared with traditional AutoML, model-led research can use prior experiments and broad knowledge to change the search direction rather than only optimize human-fixed hyperparameters.
- Post-training and agent systems offer more manageable cost and feedback cycles than pretraining, which requires large datasets, compute, infrastructure, and expert monitoring of training behavior.
- Successful and failed experimental trajectories can become skills or memory that improve later search, but retaining the wrong lesson can also misdirect later work.
- Faster research becomes another scaling dimension: agents can run many branches in parallel, although more experiments may increase total compute unless the system also improves experiment selection.
- Human researchers currently remain responsible for goals, budgets, broad framing, and judging whether proposed experiments are meaningful; [[ResearchTaste|research taste]] may be partly learnable where evaluation criteria can be articulated.
- The source leaves open whether statistical learning can produce Newton- or Einstein-scale discoveries outside known data distributions and evaluable search spaces.
- General-model AI-for-AI competition favors organizations with strong models, compute, data, and accumulated research systems; smaller teams may have better openings in vertical workflows with proprietary data and closed feedback loops.
- AI-for-science loops become harder when feedback depends on slow, expensive physical experiments, regulation, or long drug-development processes.
