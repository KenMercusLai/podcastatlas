## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] Bytes episode has [[MariaCurie|Maria Curi]] of [[Axios]] connect three AI-policy and consumer-hardware stories: [[Grok]] misuse on [[Twitter|X]] to generate non-consensual intimate images, the [[DonaldTrump]] administration's executive-order pressure against state AI laws, and strong demand for [[Meta]]'s updated [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Ray-Ban smart glasses]]. The episode's throughline is that generative AI products are moving faster than liability doctrine, state-federal governance, and bystander privacy norms. It adds [[AINonConsensualIntimateImageAbuse]], [[ChatbotGeneratedContentLiability]], [[TakeItDownAct|Take It Down Act]], [[DefianceAct|Defiance Act]], [[StateAIRegulationPatchwork]], [[NeuralBand]], and [[SmartGlassesBystanderPrivacy]].

## Key Claims
- Users are reportedly tagging [[Grok]] on [[Twitter|X]] to create non-consensual intimate images of women and sometimes minors, making [[XAI|xAI]]'s tool part of the abuse surface rather than only a passive host.
- [[XAI|xAI]] and Grok are described as already facing investigations in Europe and India, while X and [[ElonMusk]] say illegal content on the platform will not be tolerated.
- The [[TakeItDownAct|Take It Down Act]] is framed as a publication-focused law taking effect in May 2026, with criminal enforcement by authorities.
- The [[DefianceAct|Defiance Act]] is presented as potentially more applicable to AI image abuse because it covers creation and solicitation and lets individuals sue.
- [[Section230]] may not apply cleanly when a user prompts a company chatbot and the company tool generates the harmful output, especially when the output is redistributed on the same platform.
- [[DonaldTrump]]'s December executive order aims to push back against state AI laws by creating an AI litigation task force, examining burdensome state rules, and tying pressure to federal grants.
- The source says legal experts question whether agencies such as the FCC or FTC have authority to perform parts of the executive order's requested analysis.
- States are still moving ahead: California is described as regulating chatbot safeguards for minors and self-harm contexts, Illinois as limiting employer AI use in hiring, and Texas as requiring consumer disclosure for AI interactions.
- Updated Meta Ray-Ban smart glasses with the [[NeuralBand]] are described as supply constrained after strong demand, helped by marketing, AI features, celebrity advertising, better technology, and normal-looking design.
- Smart-glasses privacy remains unresolved because recording indicators may be hard for bystanders to notice and [[Meta]]'s data collection practices remain a concern.
