# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》233｜晁错被腰斩，揭露出职场中的大忌

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]]'s death inside the opening political crisis of the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]]. Chao's territorial-reduction program concentrates princely and court resentment on him; after he proposes that [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] campaign personally and tries to prosecute [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]], Yuan gains a private audience and recommends killing Chao, pardoning the rebels, and restoring the reduced lands.

The episode treats Jingdi's decision as a political sacrifice intended both to remove the coalition's declared grievance and to reduce resistance inside the court. Its modern workplace lesson is narrower than the historical claim: ability and loyalty do not replace attention to authority boundaries, colleague support, crisis priorities, and personal exposure, but those traits alone do not establish why the rebellion began or why the state executed Chao.

## Key Claims

- Chao Cuo defends territorial reduction as necessary for court authority and royal security, while his father predicts that the resulting hatred will endanger the Chao family.
- After the seven kingdoms rise and demand Chao's death, Chao reportedly advises Jingdi to campaign personally while he remains in Chang'an, a proposal the host treats as politically alarming.
- Chao tries to use the crisis to prosecute Yuan Ang over his ties to [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]], but the investigating official argues that Yuan's corruption does not make him a rebel.
- Yuan reaches Jingdi through [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]], first minimizes the military quality of Liu Bi's following, then excludes Chao from the private discussion.
- Yuan proposes killing Chao, pardoning Wu and Chu, and returning reduced territory; Jingdi deliberates before accepting the recommendation and later has senior officials impeach Chao.
- Chao is taken from court business without advance warning and executed by waist-cutting; the episode presents the formal charges and collective-family punishment as the legal-political packaging of a sacrifice.
- The host argues that killing Chao may have seemed useful even without guaranteeing peace because his continued protection could leave other officials passive, hostile, or open to defection.

## Key Quotes

> “臣何敢爱一人以谢天下。” - Jingdi's episode-attributed formulation of the choice between protecting one favored official and answering a realm-wide crisis.

> “锐于为国远虑，而不见身害。” - the Ban Gu appraisal used by the episode to join Chao's public purpose to his failure to perceive personal danger.

## Connections

- [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]] - central official whose territorial policy, crisis choices, isolation, and execution structure the episode.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - ruler who deliberates, authorizes impeachment and execution, and then sends a mission seeking rebel withdrawal.
- [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] - threatened rival who gains private access and proposes sacrificing Chao as the route to negotiation.
- [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|七国之乱]] - external crisis whose declared grievance and internal court effects make Chao expendable.
- [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] - leading rebel whose wealth, following, and public justification are assessed in Yuan's audience.
- [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] - intermediary who enables Yuan to reach the emperor after learning of Chao's proposed prosecution.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. This episode ends before the result of the concession is known; Hanji 234 supplies the immediate qualification that Liu Bi does not withdraw after Chao's execution.
- Causal boundary: the host's “职场大忌” explanation highlights Chao's treatment of colleagues, authority boundaries, and crisis priorities, but the same account also supplies structural causes in territorial reduction, royal autonomy, rebellion, and court survival. The page does not reduce the execution to personality alone.
- Motive boundary: Jingdi's ten-day deliberation and continued consultation with Chao support calculated concealment in the episode's telling, but do not independently establish every private motive or prove that the emperor expected the killing to end the war.
- Source-scope caution: the thirty-plus legal changes, Chao father's suicide, advice on personal campaigning, Yuan's alleged payments, private audience, speeches, offices, impeachment wording, family sentence, execution method and location, dates, and expected behavior of other ministers require comparison with transmitted records.
