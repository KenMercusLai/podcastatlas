# Vol.278 为什么银行门口会有石狮子？

## Summary

This episode of [[ShangyeJiushiZheyang|商业就是这样]] asks why some Chinese bank entrances display lions and traces one influential lineage to the [[HSBCLions|HSBC lions]] made for the bank's 1923 Shanghai headquarters. By comparing ancient and modern architectural use, Republican-era bank photographs, Bank of China buildings, street-view samples, and the stone-carving trade, it argues that bank lions are a mixed [[InventedTradition|invented tradition]] shaped by corporate imagery, auspicious reinterpretation, feng-shui practice, commercial copying, and branch-level choice rather than a continuous industry rule.

## Key Claims

- [[HSBC]] commissioned two reclining male bronze lions for its Shanghai headquarters; later Hong Kong and global copies, banknote imagery, and ritual installation turned them into a durable corporate symbol.
- Independent guardian lions in China historically clustered around tombs, temples, palaces, offices, and elite residences; ordinary homes more often used small carved threshold stones, so a large lion at a bank door should not be assumed to preserve an ancient universal custom.
- Surviving traditional remittance shops and most photographed Republican-era bank buildings did not place freestanding lions at their entrances; the Shanghai HSBC pair was an exceptional prestige commission.
- [[BankOfChina]] used Chinese architectural and mythical-animal motifs in Shanghai and Hong Kong, while [[LuQianshou]] helped translate institutional identity into modern bank architecture without establishing one mandatory animal form.
- The episode's non-rigorous street and street-view sample found entrance sculpture at only about 10-15% of ordinary bank outlets; headquarters and higher-level branches appeared more likely to use landscaped or animal symbols.
- [[BankAnimalSymbolism]] can combine authority, stability, trust, auspicious meaning, feng shui, and brand distinction, but the effect depends on building type, available space, local management, and procurement.
- Huian workshops market HSBC-like forms as “港币狮” or “汇丰狮”; copying and sales language can recast the original two male lions as a male-female pair or as “招财/守财” objects detached from HSBC history.

## Key Quotes

> “港币狮” — stone-trade name reported for HSBC-style reclining lions.

> “北狮” — contrasting trade label reported for more conventional Chinese guardian-lion forms.

## Contradictions

- The episode contradicts the common assumption that large stone lions have always been a normal feature of Chinese banks, money shops, or ordinary doorways. Its evidence instead supports selective elite use followed by modern recombination.
- It does not establish one origin for every bank lion. Traditional Chinese guardian lions, HSBC copies, Bank of China mythical beasts, local stone-carving supply, and independent branch decisions remain distinct and sometimes overlapping lineages.
- The 10-15% estimate, city comparisons, procurement prices, workshop origin, building-history motives, and feng-shui interpretations remain episode-reported or source-scoped because the sample was informal, street-view dates varied, and the supplied document is a structured summary rather than an audited survey.
