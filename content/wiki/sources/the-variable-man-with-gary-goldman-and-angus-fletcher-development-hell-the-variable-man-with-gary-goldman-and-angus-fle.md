# The Variable Man with Gary Goldman and Angus Fletcher | Development Hell

## Summary

In this [[RevisionistHistory]] installment of *Development Hell*, screenwriter [[GaryGoldman]] and narrative scholar [[AngusFletcher]] reconstruct their unproduced adaptation [[TheVariableManFilm|The Variable Man]], inspired by [[PhilipKDick]]. Their future society delegates food, gifts, romance, and major decisions to Plato, a predictive AI whose advice reliably outperforms individual judgment; the one person it cannot model may be either humanity's only hope or the cause of its destruction.

The screenplay turns prediction into [[AlgorithmicAutonomyParadox|an autonomy paradox]] rather than a simple anti-AI warning: obedience produces good outcomes, self-trust produces mistakes, and neither intuition nor calculation receives final moral authority. Its development history adds [[VisionBackedFilmFinancing]]: interest from stars and producers could not finance a roughly $120 million project without a director trusted to turn thematic complexity into an investable production. The last opportunity ended when a script-selection company failed after an algorithm-backed film underperformed.

## Key Claims

- Goldman and Fletcher expanded Dick's statistical war-prediction premise into a society where an AI predicts and optimizes ordinary desire, relationships, and life choices.
- Plato forecasts human self-destruction within twenty-four hours but cannot identify a solution among known variables, so the future retrieves the unpredictable Cole from the past.
- Cole repeatedly gets better outcomes when he follows Plato and worse outcomes when he trusts himself, preventing the story from reducing autonomy to “follow your heart.”
- Plato engineers Cole's romance and then asks him to kill the child Cole once saved, making love, responsibility, manipulation, and species survival inseparable.
- Fletcher argues that the story asks audiences to reason through the conflict rather than surrender judgment to either the AI or a familiar cinematic resolution.
- Actor interest did not solve the financing problem because the large-budget project lacked a director whose vision investors trusted.
- The final financing lead came through algorithmic screenplay selection, but the company collapsed after a previously selected film failed commercially.
