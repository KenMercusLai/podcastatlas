# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》405-2｜为什么说“一日为师，终身为父”？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] short episode steps sideways from the Liu He sequence into the social history behind "一日为师，终身为父." It argues that in a low-information, skill-scarce society, a teacher or master could function like a second parent because instruction, household dependence, work access, etiquette, and lifelong obligation were bound together.

The durable synthesis is that traditional apprenticeship was not simply education. It joined gratitude and moral hierarchy with food, lodging, labor, festival gifts, job introduction, and sometimes fee extraction, while the episode's comparison with medieval European guild rules highlights a contrast between Chinese master-servant dependence and more contract-like apprentice protections.

## Key Claims

- The teacher-as-father ethic rests on knowledge and craft scarcity: someone who transmits livelihood-changing knowledge becomes comparable to a second parent.
- Traditional拜师 included formal gifts such as `束脩`, household proximity, service work, and continuing seasonal visits after the initial learning period.
- The master-apprentice relation could preserve craft knowledge and industry networks, but it also bound the apprentice through labor, etiquette, personal dependence, and post-training obligation.
- Chinese apprenticeship is presented as historically moving from family craft transmission into private apprenticeship, official craft training, and later guild-linked mechanisms.
- Late imperial master-servant law and custom gave employers and masters strong disciplinary authority over hired workers and apprentices.
- The Western medieval guild comparison is used to show that apprenticeship could include food and discipline without erasing the apprentice's legal personhood or ability to seek another master.
- The episode keeps "respecting teachers" and "apprentice exploitation" together rather than treating the proverb as pure moral uplift.

## Key Quotes

> "一日为师，终身为父" - the proverb the episode unpacks as a social and ethical relation.

> "徒弟徒弟，三年奴隶" - the folk saying used to mark the coercive side of traditional apprenticeship.
