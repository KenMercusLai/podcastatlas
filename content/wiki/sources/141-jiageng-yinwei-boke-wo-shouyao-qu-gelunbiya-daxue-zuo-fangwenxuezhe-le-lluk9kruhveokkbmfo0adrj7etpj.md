## Summary
This [[QizhulouYanBinke|起朱楼宴宾客]] addendum is [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]]'s explanation of how podcasting led to his one-year visiting-scholar opportunity at [[ColumbiaJournalismSchool|Columbia Journalism School]]. The episode turns a personal announcement into a research agenda about [[CreatorDrivenFinancialNarrative|creator-driven financial narratives]], [[CreatorFactCheckingResponsibility|creator fact-checking responsibility]], algorithmic information cocoons, and how long-term podcast work can create paths beyond direct monetization.

## Key Claims
- [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]] says the visiting-scholar opportunity came to him unexpectedly, after he submitted a CV and research outline to [[ColumbiaJournalismSchool|Columbia Journalism School]] and waited through months of institutional and visa uncertainty.
- His research topic asks how macroeconomic, financial-market, and public narratives are reshaped when traditional media loses influence and individual creators, subscriptions, and algorithmic feeds become major information gateways.
- [[DonaldTrump]]'s podcast and social-media strategy during the 2024 U.S. election is treated as a signal that political persuasion can route through influential creators rather than only through legacy media.
- In China, the episode argues that individual creators, public-account writers, short-video commentators, podcasters, investment advisers, and [[Xueqiu|雪球]] investors increasingly shape how ordinary investors read markets.
- Traditional media's newsroom routines, editing, neutrality norms, and fact-checking do not automatically carry over to self-media; creator accuracy depends more heavily on personal ethics and rigor.
- The host's Japanese healthcare episode became a concrete lesson when [[QiongQiong|琼琼]] wrote a long fact-checking response to claims the host had found plausible, showing how persuasive weak sourcing can be even for informed listeners.
- Subscriptions and algorithms can both form [[InformationCocoon|information cocoons]]: subscriptions cluster similar worldviews, while algorithmic feeds reinforce event-level and opinion-level similarity through attention signals.
- Social platforms such as [[Xiaohongshu]], [[Douyin]], [[Kuaishou]], podcasts, public accounts, and investment communities can concentrate A-share narratives, strengthen winning themes, and make reversals more violent when the story breaks.
- The source frames going to the [[UnitedStates]] as a [[SituatedMediaObservation|situated media-observation]] method: living in a place, talking to people, and hearing local podcasts can reduce the risk of distributing secondhand bias.
- The personal-life section argues that [[PodcastEnabledCareerPath|podcast-enabled career paths]] can emerge from long, sincere work that was not optimized for immediate commercial return.
