# 394. JFK: Death in Dallas (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode reconstructs [[JohnFKennedy|John F. Kennedy]]'s political trip through Texas and the motorcade's final approach to [[DealeyPlaza|Dealey Plaza]] on 22 November 1963. It joins southern electoral pressure, Texas Democratic division, Kennedy's managed image of health and accessibility, Dallas's hostile reputation, unexpectedly warm crowds, and an open-car security problem to a minute-by-minute account of the shooting. The narrative follows the official account associated with the [[WarrenCommission|Warren Commission]] and Vincent Bugliosi while preserving witness, timing, and interpretive limits.

## Key Claims

- Kennedy's Texas trip sought to strengthen his position before the 1964 election, reconcile divisions between John Connally and Ralph Yarborough, and display the public appeal amplified by Jacqueline Kennedy's presence.
- Dallas's record of anti-Kennedy hostility and threats produced serious anxiety, yet the actual motorcade drew large, warm crowds and ended with Kennedy affirming Nellie Connally's observation that Dallas welcomed him.
- Clear weather removed the planned plexiglass bubble, while Kennedy's preference for handshakes, slow public movement, and visible campaign theater intensified the tradeoff between political accessibility and protection.
- Kennedy's severe back problems, concealed by a public image of vitality, required a brace that the hosts argue kept him upright after the first wound and exposed him to the fatal head shot.
- Witnesses noticed a rifleman or suspicious figure at the Texas School Book Depository before and immediately after the shots, although some initially assumed the figure was part of presidential security.
- The episode adopts the Warren Commission/Bugliosi sequence of three shots, with Kennedy and John Connally wounded before a final shot struck Kennedy's head.
- Within a minute, the presidential car was heading for Parkland Hospital and Officer Marion Baker briefly confronted Lee Harvey Oswald inside the Depository before a supervisor identified him as an employee.

## Key Quotes

> "We're heading into nut country today." - remark the episode attributes to Kennedy about Dallas.

> "You certainly can't say that Dallas doesn't love you, Mr. President." - Nellie Connally's remark immediately before the shooting, as quoted by the episode.
