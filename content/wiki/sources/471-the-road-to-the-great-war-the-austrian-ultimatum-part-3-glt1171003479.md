# 471. The Road to The Great War: The Austrian Ultimatum (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[JulyCrisis|July Crisis]] from the Franco-Russian summit in St Petersburg through Austria-Hungary's ultimatum to Serbia and Russia's first partial-mobilisation measures. It argues that Russian support for Serbia grew from strategic anxiety over the Straits, Black Sea access, grain exports, Ottoman naval strengthening, and repeated Balkan retreats as well as pan-Slav solidarity, while [[RaymondPoincare|Raymond Poincaré]] encouraged [[SergeiSazonov|Sergei Sazonov]] to believe that French alliance support made firmness safe.

The episode presents escalation as rival defensive reasoning. Austria-Hungary expected Russia to retreat again; Russia treated another retreat as intolerable humiliation; France expected a united front to deter Vienna; and Russian leaders described partial mobilisation as precaution and signal. Once the ultimatum arrived, [[MauricePaleologue|Maurice Paléologue]] reinforced Sazonov's hard line, Russia advised Serbia not to accept, and military preparation began to narrow the diplomatic choice that Parts 4-6 continue.

## Key Claims

- Russia's Balkan policy joined pan-Slav affinity to strategic concerns about the Bosphorus, Black Sea access, Ukrainian grain exports, and Ottoman naval power.
- Earlier Russian retreats over Bosnia and Serbian access to the Adriatic produced opposing lessons: Austria-Hungary expected another retreat, while Russia adopted a "never again" position.
- France's alliance with Russia combined security against Germany, financial and railway ties, military coordination, and revanchist memory after 1870.
- Poincaré's confrontational diplomacy and preference for firmness encouraged Russia to expect French support, while the less bellicose René Viviani failed to soften the summit line.
- Austria-Hungary's ultimatum mixed real concerns about Serbian-linked violent networks with demands for participation inside Serbia that directly challenged sovereignty.
- Paléologue encouraged Sazonov rather than restraining him while France's senior leaders were at sea and difficult to reach.
- Russia's assurance to Serbia and initial partial-mobilisation preparations were intended as support, defense, and warning but raised the danger of wider alliance escalation.

## Key Quotes

> "world inferno" - Sazonov's warning about the likely result if Austria-Hungary moved against Serbia.

> "Serbia has friends" - Poincaré's warning to the Austro-Hungarian ambassador during the St Petersburg visit.
