# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》711丨他助刘秀打下半壁江山，为何死法憋屈？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the Jianwu 11-12 Shu campaign immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-712-liuxiu-weihe-bei-yuwei-zui-hui-da-zhang-de-huangdi-lt7rx7tblodjfngqfdq33ikrcmyn|Hanji 712]]. It centers on [[CenPengEasternHan|岑彭]] breaking the Shu defensive line through speed from Jiangzhou to Wuyang and Guangdu, [[ZangGongEasternHan|臧宫]] defeating [[YanCenEasternHan|延岑 / Yan Cen]] on the Fu River, and [[GongsunShuEasternHan|公孙述]] answering collapse with refused surrender and assassination.

The durable synthesis is that Guangwu's western campaign turns on operational tempo and post-breakthrough legitimacy. Cen Peng's disciplined no-plunder command creates local memory even after his assassination, while [[MaYuanEasternHan|马援]] and [[GuoJiEasternHan|郭伋]] extend the same episode into frontier-resource governance and the transition from Nanyang-heavy founding networks toward nationally selected officials.

## Key Claims

- Cen Peng and Zang Gong divide the Shu campaign into fast mobile pressure and river-line containment: Zang Gong moves with 50,000 surrendered Ba-Shu troops up the Fu River while Cen Peng leads the Han navy from Jiangzhou.
- Cen Peng breaks Hou Dan's line, forces a long day-night march, captures Wuyang, and sends light cavalry toward Guangdu fast enough to shock Chengdu and expose Gongsun Shu's reliance on geography.
- Zang Gong converts a shaky force into victory by using Liu Xiu's horses and supplies, urgent supply authority, flags, drums, and night marching to defeat Yan Cen; the defeat causes heavy losses, Yan Cen's flight to Chengdu, and wider surrender.
- Liu Xiu writes to Gongsun Shu urging surrender, but Gongsun Shu refuses because he frames himself as a Son of Heaven who cannot submit.
- Gongsun Shu later sends assassins again, and Cen Peng is killed at Pengwang/Peng亡 before seeing the final Shu victory despite having created the decisive campaign advantage.
- Cen Peng's no-plunder discipline leads Shu people to remember him positively, including Qionggu king Ren Gui's submission tribute, a Wuyang temple, and the later Pengshan place-memory.
- Ma Yuan's Jincheng advice opposes abandoning fertile and fortified frontier territory; Liu Xiu adopts the plan, and Ma Yuan repairs walls, digs canals, restores officials, draws back residents, and reassures Qiang groups.
- Guo Ji tells Liu Xiu that appointments should draw from national talent rather than mostly from Liu Xiu's Nanyang compatriots, making personnel selection part of post-founding state capacity.
