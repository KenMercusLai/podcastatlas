## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty Carino]] interview [[WillCreeley]] of the [[FoundationForIndividualRightsAndExpression|Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression]] about [[Meta]]'s teen social-media addiction settlement. Creeley argues that the settlement's youth-safety promises may push Meta, [[TikTok]], and [[YouTube]] toward [[OnlineAgeVerification]] or identity checks that would be legally harder to impose directly through statute.

The source extends the wiki's platform-law branch by connecting [[SocialMediaProductLiability]] remedies to [[SocialMediaAgeGateSpeechBurden]], [[YouthOnlineSpeechRights]], and [[PlatformFirstAmendmentDefense]]. Its core warning is that a popular child-safety settlement can become [[SettlementInducedAgeVerification|settlement-induced age verification]], where private litigation pressure produces infrastructure that affects anonymous adult speech and dissent.

## Key Claims
- Meta agreed to a youth-safety settlement in a teen social-media addiction case, with promised restrictions such as time limits, overnight blackouts, and hidden likes.
- The episode says Meta offered to pay up to $17 billion, with part of the structure contingent on TikTok and YouTube adopting similar reforms.
- [[WillCreeley]] argues that separating children and teens from adults on major social platforms would likely require identity or age checks for everyone.
- Creeley says courts have protected both minors' First Amendment interests and adults' ability to access protected speech without first proving identity.
- The source treats [[FreeSpeechCoalitionVPaxton|Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton]] as a narrower adult-content precedent, not a general authorization for identity checks across broad social platforms.
- Creeley argues that Meta might have a strong First Amendment objection if the same reforms were imposed directly by a state law, because platforms have editorial rights over curation and presentation.
- The episode records a split between popular support for stronger child-safety limits and civil-liberties concerns from FIRE, the [[ElectronicFrontierFoundation|Electronic Frontier Foundation]], academics, and journalists.
- Creeley's preferred alternative is more user and family control, rather than one-size-fits-all identity checks or government-backed platform mandates.

## Key Quotes
> "show identification" - Creeley's shorthand for the likely enforcement step.

> "end run around the First Amendment" - Creeley's description of settlement pressure replacing direct legislation.
