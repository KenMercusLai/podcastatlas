# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1136丨他勇如韩信，却因一个决定全家被杀

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode reconstructs why relief failed to reach Ji during [[MaChao|马超]]'s eight-month siege. Distance, slow communication, [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s southeastern campaign, approval chains, Ma Chao's interception, and support from the Di leader Qianwan combine into a multi-constraint explanation rather than a single command failure.

Inside the city, [[YanWenLateHan|阎温]] is captured while seeking help and uses a false promise of imminent relief to sustain morale before Ma Chao kills him. [[YangFuLateHan|杨阜]] later opposes surrender, but [[WeiKangLateHan|韦康]] opens Ji; Ma Chao reportedly kills Wei Kang and his household, turning military capture into a [[BreachOfSurrenderTrust|surrender-trust breach]] that helps explain the local resistance developed in Hanji 1137 and 1138.

## Key Claims

- Ji's relief failure is attributed jointly to long-distance communication, Cao-side multi-front commitments, military authorization delay, interception by Ma Chao, and Di support for Ma Chao.
- Cao Cao orders Xiahou Yuan to advance from Chang'an, but Ma Chao attacks the relief force and Qianwan's rising adds pressure; [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]] withdraws before reaching Ji.
- Wei Kang sends Yan Wen through the siege lines to request aid; water traces expose Yan Wen after he swims out at night.
- Ma Chao orders the captive Yan Wen to tell the defenders that aid will not come, but Yan Wen instead claims a large army will arrive within three days and urges continued defense.
- After prolonged siege and failed relief, Wei Kang and the commandery administrator favor surrender despite Yang Fu's opposition.
- Ma Chao reportedly kills Wei Kang, the administrator, and their households after Ji opens, then claims the titles General Who Conquers the West and governor of Bingzhou.
- The episode interprets the killing of surrendered local elites as a political error that destroys trust, creates enemies through kinship and local networks, and makes Ma Chao's new rule unstable.
- Ma Chao's responsibility for the later execution of Ma Teng's household and the claim that he lacked political judgment remain the host's moral and psychological framing.
