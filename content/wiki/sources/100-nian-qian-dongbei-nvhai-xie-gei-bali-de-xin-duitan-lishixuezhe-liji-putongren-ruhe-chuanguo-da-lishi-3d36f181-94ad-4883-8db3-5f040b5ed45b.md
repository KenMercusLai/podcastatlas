# 100 年前东北女孩写给巴黎的信

## Summary
This [[FuyouTiandi]] episode interviews [[LiJiHistorian|李纪]] about three vernacular letters that girls from [[SantaiZiCatholicVillage|三台子]] wrote to [[FatherLinNortheastMission|林神父]] more than a century ago, and about the archival and fieldwork path that made those ordinary lives historically visible. It reframes late Qing and early Republican [[China|Chinese]] Northeast history through migration, Catholic mission villages, maps, letters, gendered religious space, oral memory, and the [[ParisForeignMissionsSociety|巴黎外方传教会]]. The episode also turns methodological: historical work needs source discipline, field presence, question consciousness, and resistance to one-line labels for complex people.

## Key Claims
- [[NortheastModernityTestSite|东北现代性试验场]] treats the late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century Northeast as an immigrant frontier where farmers, missionaries, treaty power, empire, land, and global modernity met.
- [[ParisForeignMissionsSociety|巴黎外方传教会]] missionaries created a rural knowledge infrastructure through village surveys, Catholic maps, family records, churches, letters, and long-running local relationships.
- [[MissionaryMapKnowledgeProduction|传教士地图知识生产]] explains why [[SantaiZiCatholicVillage|三台子]] looked central on church maps even when it was not equally prominent in administrative geography.
- The [[DuFamilyGirls|杜氏女孩]] letters show [[VernacularFemaleReligiousWriting|女性宗教白话书写]]: church literacy, catechism vocabulary, vernacular emotion, and gender constraint combined into a rare written trace of ordinary rural girls.
- [[FemaleReligiousLifeSpace|女性宗教生活空间]] is ambivalent: 守贞女 and 神女 roles could create room outside marriage, but they remained governed by church hierarchy, family economics, and moral discipline.
- [[OralHistoryMemoryAnalysis|口述记忆分析]] requires listening to descendants and elders while testing memory against context, archives, genealogy, and field evidence.
- [[HistoricalQuestionConsciousness|历史问题意识]] acts as the organizing discipline for large archives: without a question, even hundreds of letters and records become an undirected pile.
- [[HistoricalComplexityAgainstLabels|拒绝历史人物标签]] is the episode's strongest interpretive ethic: missionaries, believers, ordinary women, and historians should be understood inside structures without being reduced to final moral slogans.
