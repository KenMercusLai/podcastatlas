# 《资治通鉴·周纪》05丨光彩夺目的魏文侯（3）

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]'s rule as a three-part personnel problem: restore hierarchy after [[LeYang|乐羊]]'s conquest of [[ZhongshanState|中山国]], let specialists do specialist work, and treat mobile scholars with restraint. Through [[TianZifang|田子方]]'s exchanges with Wei Wenhou and crown prince [[WeiWuhou|魏击 / 后来的魏武侯]], it argues that leadership lies in selecting, supervising, and coordinating people rather than personally mastering every technical task. The episode then connects scholars' willingness to leave an unreceptive court with the competitive Warring States talent market, while treating Qin unification as a later shift toward more centralized career rules.

## Key Claims
- After Le Yang's three-year Zhongshan campaign, Wei Wenhou gives him a box of hostile memorials rather than treasure, showing that the general's victory depended partly on the ruler's sustained support against court opposition.
- Le Yang responds by returning credit upward; the episode reads the exchange as a way to reduce post-victory arrogance without discarding a successful commander.
- Wei Wenhou grants Le Yang 灵寿 and places his son Wei Ji in Zhongshan, while [[LiKe|李克]] is said to administer the conquered territory effectively.
- When Wei Wenhou detects an incorrect bell pitch, Tian Zifang says a ruler should understand how to appoint and supervise music officials rather than become a music specialist himself.
- The episode links that exchange to “君子不器”: managerial value lies in matching people to work, defining responsibility, and evaluating performance.
- Tian Zifang refuses to return crown prince Wei Ji's roadside bow and argues that powerful people cannot safely despise others, whereas a poor scholar whose advice is rejected can put on his shoes and leave.
- The source explains that confidence through the interstate demand for talent: rulers need reputations for honoring scholars, and mobile people can seek another patron when one court will not use them.
- The episode contrasts that competitive court market with Qin unification, claiming that centralized rules narrowed elite mobility and made advancement depend more heavily on state-prescribed channels.

## Key Quotes
> "为君者要善于任用乐官，而不用精通音乐。" - Tian Zifang's distinction between ruling and specialist performance as rendered by the episode.

> "贫贱者骄人。" - the compact claim behind Tian Zifang's answer to the crown prince.

> "穿上鞋子，离开你就是了。" - the episode's image of a mobile scholar's exit option.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The identification of the episode's crown prince “魏击” with [[WeiWuhou|魏武侯]] is consistent with the existing succession branch but remains source-scoped because another ingested supplement uses noisy forms such as “魏姬.”
- The claim that Qin unification required “real ability” and forced all talent into prescribed routes is the host's broad institutional contrast, not a complete account of Qin appointment practice or later imperial recruitment.
- The exact contents and number of anti-Le Yang memorials, the scope of the Lingshou grant, Zhongshan personnel arrangements, dialogue wording, and later Le-family continuity are episode-attributed pending fuller source comparison.
