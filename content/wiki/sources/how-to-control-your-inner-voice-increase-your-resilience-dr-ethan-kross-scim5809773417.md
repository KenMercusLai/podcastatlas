# How to Control Your Inner Voice & Increase Your Resilience | Dr. Ethan Kross

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview psychologist [[EthanKross]] about useful inner speech, repetitive negative "chatter," and flexible emotion regulation. The practical synthesis is a sequenced, context-sensitive toolkit: recognize when reflection has become looping, create distance through names or time perspective, shift attention or sensory state, seek advisers who combine validation with perspective, and pre-plan responses through WOOP-style if-then rules. The conversation treats unpleasant emotion, mind wandering, selective avoidance, and intrusive thoughts as potentially normal or functional while keeping persistent impairment, diagnosis, and treatment outside a one-size-fits-all self-help script.

## Key Claims
- Inner speech supports working memory, planning, simulation, self-control, and motivation; it becomes chatter when negative material repeats without producing understanding or action and consumes limited attention.
- Emotion regulation is a flexible portfolio rather than one universally effective technique. The episode groups sensory, attentional, perspective, relational, physical-space, and cultural "shifters" and says people often combine several.
- Distanced self-talk uses one's own name or second-person language to approach a problem as an adviser would, while temporal distancing asks how the problem may look tomorrow, next week, or years later.
- Music, mutually desired touch, movement, nature, awe, environmental order, cue removal, and deliberate transitions can shift state or restore capacity, but tool effects depend on person and situation.
- Support works best when validation is followed by perspective or problem solving; venting can strengthen connection but may become co-rumination when it repeatedly amplifies one interpretation.
- [[MentalContrastingImplementationIntentions|WOOP and if-then plans]] connect a wish and desired outcome to likely obstacles and pre-selected responses, making a regulation sequence easier to retrieve under stress.
- Strategic attention can alternate between engaging a problem and taking a break. Chronic avoidance is risky, but constant confrontation is not the only healthy response.
- Intrusive dark simulations are described as common and not equivalent to intent, action, or diagnosis; persistent, dangerous, or impairing symptoms still require qualified assessment.
