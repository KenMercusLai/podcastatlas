## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode uses a Menlo Park walk with [[PaulVixie]] to compare the dot-com fiber buildout with today's AI infrastructure boom. [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty-Carino]] frames old telecom manholes as evidence of infrastructure that helped bankrupt firms in the short term but later carried search, social media, streaming, cryptocurrency, and AI traffic. The source extends [[ProductiveBubbleSpillovers]] and [[TechnologyInstallationCycle]] by showing how unused [[DarkFiber]] can become long-term option value rather than pure waste.

## Key Claims
- [[PaulVixie]] is described as an early internet innovator who helped build the [[PaloAltoInternetExchange|Palo Alto Internet Exchange]] and later became a vice president at [[AmazonWebServices|AWS Security]].
- During the dot-com boom, telecommunications companies spent billions laying fast internet infrastructure under competitive pressure to build as quickly as possible.
- The immediate aftermath included a wave of telecom bankruptcies, so the episode does not portray infrastructure booms as painless or automatically rational.
- After the bust, large amounts of installed fiber sat unused as [[DarkFiber]].
- The [[FederalCommunicationsCommission|Federal Communications Commission]] estimated that by 2007 roughly two-thirds of 45 million miles of fiber were still dark.
- Later internet waves used that spare capacity for search engines, social media, streaming video, cryptocurrency, and AI.
- Vixie rejects the simple "glut" interpretation; he argues the fiber buildout ultimately paid off because it left enough capacity for future builders.
- The episode's AI analogy is suggestive rather than complete: today's data centers may also leave useful capacity after a bust, but the source does not deeply compare fiber with AI facilities' power, operating-cost, financing, or hardware-obsolescence constraints.
- The APM climate-podcast promotion at the end is not treated as part of the main Marketplace Tech source.
