# EP95 《FX战士久留美》：妈妈亏掉的钱，我要亲手赚回来

## Summary

This [[YiLaoYongYi|一劳永逸]] episode uses the first two episodes of [[FXSenshiKurumi|《FX战士久留美》]] to explain [[ForeignExchangeMarginTrading|retail FX margin trading]], currency pairs, long and short positions, floating profit and loss, margin, leverage, liquidation, and [[YenCarryTrade|yen-funded carry]]. Its deeper contribution is behavioral: an early lucky recovery can reward rule-breaking, so overconfidence, confirmation seeking, [[TradingOutcomeBias|outcome bias]], [[SunkCostEntrapment|sunk-cost chasing]], and moving a stop can combine with leverage before the trader recognizes that the process has failed.

The episode's risk synthesis is that a trade should be judged across repeated outcomes, position size, payoff ratio, and adherence rather than by one win or a claimed hit rate. Living-expense money should remain outside speculative accounts, borrowed money should not be layered on top of derivative leverage, and a planned [[StopLossDiscipline|stop loss]] is different from broker-imposed liquidation.

## Key Claims

- A currency-pair quote expresses one currency in another; a trader can open long or short exposure and usually settle profit or loss without taking physical delivery of the currencies.
- Leverage magnifies both directions, while a margin shortfall can let the broker close the position before the investor's hoped-for recovery arrives.
- The episode uses AUD/JPY in 2008 to show that interest-rate carry can be overwhelmed by exchange-rate loss and that buying back funding currency can intensify a [[CarryTradeUnwind]].
- Candlestick charts compress open, high, low, and close information, but the program treats the popular single-inventor story around Honma Munehisa cautiously.
- Kurumi's first profitable recovery rewards waiting, hoping, and abandoning an exit plan, which makes later risk-taking feel validated even though the decision process was weak.
- A more profitable trader's confidence and a bullish story do not substitute for independent sizing, evidence checks, or an invalidation rule.
- Moving or cancelling a stop near the loss limit remains a process failure even if price later rebounds; evaluating it only by the favorable result is [[TradingOutcomeBias|outcome bias]].
- Prior loss is already incurred; holding longer or adding money to “win it back” creates new exposure rather than recovering the past through logic alone.
- Hit rate is incomplete without payoff ratio: many small controlled losses can coexist with positive expectancy when winners are larger, while a high win rate can hide rare ruinous losses.
- Household bills, education costs, travel money, and other required spending should not depend on achieving a market return.
