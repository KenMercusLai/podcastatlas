# Micronutrients for Health & Longevity | Dr. Rhonda Patrick

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] interview has [[AndrewHuberman]] and [[RhondaPatrick]] connect micronutrients, plant compounds, marine omega-3s, vitamin D, magnesium, exercise, deliberate cold, and sauna through an [[IntermittentChallengeHormesis]] frame. Its practical center is measurement and context: food and supplement forms differ, biomarkers can be more informative than generic intake rules, and thermal protocols depend on dose, adaptation, health status, and evidence type. The episode adds [[MicronutrientStatusMeasurement]], [[Omega3StatusAndFormBoundary]], and [[HeatExposureDoseAndSafety]] while extending [[ColdExposureDoseAndSafety]].

## Key Claims
- [[IntermittentChallengeHormesis]] describes exercise, fasting, heat, cold, and some plant compounds as manageable stressors that may activate overlapping antioxidant, inflammatory, autophagy, heat-shock, and mitochondrial responses.
- Sulforaphane is presented as an NRF2 activator; broccoli sprouts are described as a concentrated source, while food preparation and myrosinase availability can affect formation.
- [[Omega3StatusAndFormBoundary]] distinguishes ALA from EPA and DHA, food and supplement sources, chemical form, oxidation quality, meal context, and the red-blood-cell omega-3 index.
- [[MicronutrientStatusMeasurement]] treats vitamin D and omega-3 testing as dose feedback because sun exposure, pigmentation, age, genetics, diet, formulation, and metabolism alter response.
- Magnesium is described as relevant to ATP use, vitamin D metabolism, and DNA-repair enzymes, but supplement form, gastrointestinal tolerance, food intake, and clinical context prevent one universal protocol.
- [[ColdExposureDoseAndSafety]] joins catecholamine arousal and thermogenic adaptation to cold-shock, cardiovascular, transition, and individual-dose cautions.
- [[HeatExposureDoseAndSafety]] treats sauna and hot baths as dose-dependent heat stress with plausible heat-shock and cardiovascular mechanisms, observational outcome evidence, fertility and hypotension tradeoffs, and explicit medical cautions.
