# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》223｜晁错为什么要劝文帝卖官换粮食？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]]'s 《论贵粟书》 as a food-security and incentive-design argument under [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. Chao says political order depends on grain production and reserves, yet irregular levies, forced sales, debt, and merchant bargaining power leave cultivators poor while nominal law claims to honor agriculture; his answer is to make grain more valuable by exchanging deliveries for rank and penal redemption.

The episode's title compresses this into “selling office,” but the supplied account supports a narrower mechanism: contributors receive rank or legal relief, not demonstrated control of a governing post. Liu Heng reportedly allows grain to be delivered to the frontier according to a rank schedule; Chao then proposes stopping once frontier stores cover five years, filling commandery and county stores to one year, and using the resulting capacity to reduce agricultural taxes. The policy therefore joins [[HeavyAgricultureLightCommercePolicy|agrarian priority]], [[RankSaleDisasterFinance|rank monetization]], frontier logistics, and eventual burden relief while leaving implementation and distributional effects unmeasured.

## Key Claims

- Chao treats food shortage as a problem of institutions, incentives, land and labor use, and public reserves rather than as natural disaster alone.
- Hunger can dissolve family attachment and political loyalty, so agriculture, sericulture, livestock, lighter taxes, and stored grain form a security system rather than a purely economic program.
- Gold and jewels receive value from official demand but cannot directly feed or clothe people; grain and cloth are slower to produce, less portable, and indispensable.
- Cultivators face seasonal labor, corvée, disaster, irregular requisitions, forced low-price sales, high-interest debt, and possible loss of land or family members, while merchants can profit from storage, price information, and urgent state demand.
- Chao argues that nominally honoring farmers is ineffective when actual rewards favor other activity; the state should raise grain's effective value by accepting it for rank and penal redemption.
- The proposed sequence is frontier supply first, then commandery and county granaries, then agricultural tax reduction after specified storage targets are met.
- Liu Heng reportedly accepts the frontier delivery scheme and later remits half the year's agricultural tax while criticizing officials for weak agrarian leadership.

## Key Quotes

> “民贫则奸邪生。” - the episode's compact link between material insecurity and political disorder.

> “粟者，王者大用，政之本务。” - the memorial's reported description of grain as a ruler's essential resource and the basis of government.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source adds an earlier and more explicit policy rationale to the late-Wen grain-for-rank measure already recorded in Hanji 228.
- The title's “卖官” is materially broader than the supplied content: the episode establishes rank and penal redemption, not the sale of an active governing office.
- The claim that merchants' gain causes farmer dispossession and flight is an attributed political argument, not a measured causal decomposition; storage and circulation can also perform useful exchange functions recognized elsewhere in the wiki.
- Ancient disaster counts, the Yao and Tang precedents, acreage and yield figures, tax timing, interest rates, merchant conduct, delivery quantities, rank schedule, penal-redemption scope, granary targets, implementation, price effects, and tax-relief outcomes require comparison with transmitted texts and specialist evidence.
