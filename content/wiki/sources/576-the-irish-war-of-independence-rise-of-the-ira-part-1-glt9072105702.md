# 576. The Irish War of Independence: Rise of the IRA (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode with [[PaulRouse]] follows Irish politics from the aftermath of the [[EasterRising|Easter Rising]] to Sinn Fein's victory in the 1918 general election. It argues that the Rising did not itself create an inevitable mass republican movement: executions, internment, commemoration, by-election organization, [[ThomasAshe|Thomas Ashe]]'s death and funeral, British policy drift, and the [[ConscriptionCrisis1918|1918 conscription crisis]] changed how defeat was interpreted and organized.

The episode's core synthesis is a transition from symbolic inheritance to practical political machinery. [[SinnFein|Sinn Fein]] had not organized the Rising, but official mislabelling gave it the rebel name; [[ArthurGriffith]] supplied an existing nationalist organization, [[EamonDeValera|Éamon de Valera]] led its republican turn, and [[MichaelCollinsIrishRevolutionary|Michael Collins]] connected prisoner support, electoral work, clandestine networks, and military organization. The enlarged 1918 electorate then gave abstention, an Irish parliament, and a republic a sweeping mandate while leaving unionist opposition concentrated in Ulster.

## Key Claims

- Irish nationalism cannot be reduced to an uninterrupted “800 years of oppression”; conquest, intermixture, religion, land, famine, empire, cultural revival, constitutional nationalism, and unionism created a layered political field.
- Sinn Fein did not organize the Easter Rising and was not formally republican before 1917, but British and public misidentification made its name the main political vehicle for the rebellion's afterlife.
- Executions, internment, films, handbooks, flags, prisoner collections, requiem culture, and commemorative use of the GPO converted a militarily defeated minority action into an expanding public memory.
- Internment connected activists and enabled organization, while by-election victories, prisoner funds, boycotts, land and food agitation, and volunteer reorganization turned sympathy into capacity.
- [[ThomasAshe|Thomas Ashe]]'s death after force-feeding during a hunger strike and his large Dublin funeral extended the martyrdom sequence; Collins's graveside role linked ritual memory to emerging leadership.
- [[ArthurGriffith|Arthur Griffith]] initially advocated national self-reliance and an Austria-Hungary-inspired dual-monarchy model, while de Valera's 1917 presidency accompanied Sinn Fein's commitment to seek international recognition for an independent republic followed by a referendum on government form.
- [[MichaelCollinsIrishRevolutionary|Michael Collins]] emerged as an organizer of prisoner support, by-election work, IRB networks, volunteer activity, and military planning rather than only as a later gunman.
- The attempt to impose conscription in 1918 united Sinn Fein, constitutional nationalists, Catholic bishops, and labour opposition, making British coercion appear to validate separatist warnings and further weakening the Irish Parliamentary Party.
- Arrests under the alleged German Plot removed many leaders but left Collins and Richard Mulcahy at liberty, strengthening the contrast between visible repression and adaptable organization.
- Expanded suffrage increased the Irish electorate from roughly 700,000 to nearly two million; Sinn Fein won 73 seats on abstention, a separate parliament, an independent republic, and international recognition, while unionists won 26 and the Irish Parliamentary Party six.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode supplies the missing bridge between the Easter Rising source and the already-ingested War of Independence sequence.
- Sinn Fein's later ownership of the Rising must not be projected backward into responsibility for planning it; the episode explicitly separates mistaken attribution from later political inheritance.
- The 1918 election established a powerful mandate under the electoral rules and geography of the time, but it did not create uniform consent across Ireland; unionist victories remained concentrated in Ulster.
- Claims about public mood, private motives, exact crowd and electorate figures, the alleged German Plot, the strategic effect of particular symbols, and whether conscription alone destroyed constitutional nationalism remain source-scoped.
