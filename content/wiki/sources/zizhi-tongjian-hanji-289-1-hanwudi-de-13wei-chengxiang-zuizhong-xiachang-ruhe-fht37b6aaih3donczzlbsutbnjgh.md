# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》289-1｜汉武帝的13位丞相，最终下场如何？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode introduces the high-risk chancellorship of [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] by contrasting dismissal, prosecution, suicide, execution, and a few deaths in office. Its main figure is [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]], whom it presents as rising from poverty and late study to become chancellor and Marquis of Pingjin in 124 BCE, then dying naturally in office despite the danger surrounding the post.

The durable contribution is comparative rather than biographically complete. Gongsun Hong's outcome adds a death-in-office route to [[GoodDeathPoliticalSurvival|乱局善终]], while the wider list reinforces [[StrongRulerChancellorshipDoubleBind|强势君主下的丞相双重约束]]. The supplied text stops as it previews criticism of Gongsun Hong, so it cannot yet explain his survival or resolve whether adaptation, competence, imperial trust, timing, or luck mattered most.

## Key Claims

- The episode says Han Wudi used thirteen chancellors and that most were dismissed, prosecuted, killed, or driven to suicide; the count and allocation of outcomes remain episode-attributed.
- Gongsun Hong and Lu Wenshu are introduced through poverty and improvised copying materials, making study under resource constraint the narrative prelude to later office.
- The source presents Gongsun Hong as tending pigs until late in life, entering politics from commoner status, and becoming an inspirational mobility case; its ages and early-life details are not independently verified here.
- In Yuanshuo 5 / 124 BCE, Han Wudi removes Xue Ze and appoints Gongsun Hong chancellor and Marquis of Pingjin; the host treats the combined appointment and enfeoffment as a precedent.
- Gongsun Hong, [[ShiQingWesternHan|石庆]], and [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]] are the episode's principal good-end contrasts, although Shi Qing is described as undistinguished and Tian Qianqiu's tenure extends into the reign of [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]].
- The episode names several adverse outcomes to show that incompetence, criminal accusation, family implication, and major political cases could all make the same office dangerous through different mechanisms.
- The source closes by saying Gongsun Hong's success attracted substantial criticism but does not supply that criticism or a full causal account of why he survived.

## Key Quotes

> “政坛长青树” - the host's shorthand for Gongsun Hong's unusual survival and continuity in office.

> “最终善终” - the episode's outcome category for Gongsun Hong, contrasted with the fates assigned to most other Wudi-era chancellors.

## Contradictions

It also appears to mistranscribe 卫绾, 许昌, 田蚡, 赵绾, 王臧, 刘屈氂, 刘据, 赵周, 田千秋, and 刘弗陵; the source note uses established canonical identities where the match is clear.
- Coverage gap: despite the title, the supplied four-minute text does not systematically enumerate all thirteen chancellors or establish a stable denominator for each outcome category.
- Causal gap: natural death, dismissal, suicide, execution, and clan punishment are outcomes rather than one explanation. The source does not show that the chancellorship double bind alone caused every adverse end or that one survival technique explains Gongsun Hong, Shi Qing, and Tian Qianqiu alike.
- Biographical caution: the improvised bamboo-copying story, age and duration of pig tending, exact appointment day, “first chancellor enfeoffed” formulation, judgments of inactivity or incompetence, and each compressed terminal account remain source-scoped pending broader textual comparison.
- The promised criticism of Gongsun Hong is absent from the supplied text. Later claims about flattery, rivalry, policy, or character cannot be inferred from this cutoff.
