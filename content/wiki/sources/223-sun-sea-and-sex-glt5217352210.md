# 223. Sun, Sea, and Sex

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode traces modern British holidays from First World War battlefield travel and interwar walking clubs through paid leave, holiday camps, charter flights, Mediterranean resorts, and internet booking. It presents [[ModernMassTourism]] as the result of several interacting changes rather than one invention: statutory time off, rising wages, cars, aviation, accommodation, state development policy, and businesses able to bundle unfamiliar travel into a purchasable experience.

The episode also emphasizes continuity. [[HolidayImprovementIdeology|Holidays remained coded as improving, healthy, respectable, or socially revealing]], while [[OrganizedHolidayIntermediation|clubs, camps, agents, brochures, representatives, and package operators]] managed uncertainty and participation. The internet weakened that older gatekeeper structure, but did not erase the demand for coordination, trust, or class distinction.

## Key Claims

- First World War battlefield visits mixed mourning, pilgrimage, curiosity, and spectacle, making the moral status of tourism difficult to separate from visitor motive.
- Interwar cooperatives, walking groups, and camping organizations treated leisure as education, fellowship, health, simplicity, and character formation rather than mere consumption.
- Tanning reversed an older class signal: pale skin had indicated freedom from outdoor labor, while travel and sun exposure increasingly signaled leisure, health, sexuality, and modernity.
- The 1938 Holidays with Pay Act widened access to time away, but class continued to shape destinations and ideals, from Blackpool and holiday camps to the Lake District and Mediterranean travel.
- [[BillyButlin]] turned the holiday camp into managed collective leisure, using inclusive pricing, programmed activities, and redcoat entertainers to reduce uncertainty and fear of boredom.
- [[VladimirRaitz]] adapted the guided-tour model associated with [[ThomasCook|Thomas Cook]] to charter aviation by bundling flights, basic accommodation, food, local clubs, and representatives.
- Spain under [[FranciscoFranco]] used tourism for hard currency and development while British demand for cheap, reliable sun helped transform places such as Benidorm into mass-resort landscapes.
- The internet and late-20th-century aviation changes weakened the brochure-and-agent gatekeeper model by allowing travelers to compare, book, and customize trips directly.
