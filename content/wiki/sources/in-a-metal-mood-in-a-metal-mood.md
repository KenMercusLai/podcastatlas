# In a Metal Mood

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode has [[MalcolmGladwell]] reconsider [[PatBoone]] through his 1950s covers of Black R&B and his 1997 lounge-style heavy-metal album. Comparisons with [[GeorgiaGibbs]], [[ElvisPresley]], and [[LittleRichard]] distinguish close imitation that can displace an originator from [[TransformativeCulturalAppropriation|transformative borrowing]] that remains recognizably different and may redirect listeners toward the source.

[[TacoBell]] supplies the cross-domain analogy. [[GlenBell]] borrowed the taco form, but the chain's Mexican-inspired menu and test-kitchen products depart so far from Mexican cuisine that the episode treats them as invention rather than faithful reproduction. The comparison yields a qualified consequential standard based on transformation, displacement, attribution, and whether the adaptation leaves room for the originator.

## Key Claims

- Boone's lounge treatment of heavy metal changes genre, mood, and performance identity rather than impersonating the original artists.
- [[GeorgiaGibbs]]'s close reproduction of [[LaVernBaker|LaVern Baker]]'s “Tweedle Dee” is presented as the clearest case of [[ImitativeCulturalDisplacement]] because a celebrated cover can replace a culturally rooted source without adding meaningful distance.
- The episode argues that [[ElvisPresley]] followed [[OtisBlackwell|Otis Blackwell]]'s demos so effectively that white audiences could lose a reason to seek the Black performers and styles behind the songs.
- [[LittleRichard]] first said Boone's “Tutti Frutti” blocked his version from white radio and stores, then later credited the cover with introducing white listeners who sought out the original.
- Boone's stylistic limitations become part of the defense: his smoother, on-beat version remains visibly unlike Little Richard and therefore cannot fully erase the source's distinctive performance.
- [[TacoBell]] describes itself as Mexican-inspired rather than authentic; products such as the Naked Chicken Chalupa and Doritos Locos Taco require substantial product development and are presented as transformations rather than copies.
- The episode's “right kind” of appropriation creates something recognizably new, does not present itself as the definitive source, and can widen attention to the originator.
