## Summary
This [[PlanetMoney]] episode visits [[ZydorChurch]] in [[OaklandCalifornia|Oakland, California]] to examine how a psychedelic church offers [[PsilocybinMushrooms]] and [[DMT]] as sacrament while operating around federal drug law. The reporting uses [[DaveHodges]] and attorney [[JohnRapp]] to explain [[PsychedelicChurches]], [[ReligiousControlledSubstanceExemption]], the [[ControlledSubstancesAct]], and the [[DEAExemptionProcess]]. Its central tension is whether Zydor is a sincere religion, a harm-reduction service, a legal protest, a business-like gray market, or some mixture of those categories.

## Key Claims
- [[ZydorChurch]] is presented as a large Oakland psychedelic church whose members obtain mushroom and DMT sacrament through cash donations rather than described sales.
- [[DaveHodges]] says Zydor has had more than 135,000 people physically come through the church and that busy weekends can bring more than 300 members a day into the sacrament room.
- The church requires an application, a statement of sincere religiosity, a referral answer, a law-enforcement question, a $10 initiation fee, and a $5 monthly fee.
- The episode says Zydor's sacrament room contains cannabis products, multiple strains and potencies of psychedelic mushrooms, mushroom edibles, [[DMT]], and DMT vape pens.
- Zydor's scale and low barrier to entry make it an outlier among [[PsychedelicChurches]] and sharpen the question of whether the organization is mostly religious, commercial, political, or harm-reduction oriented.
- [[JohnRapp]] advises psychedelic churches on legal risk, institutional design, and how to make sincere religious practice legible to courts and regulators.
- The [[NativeAmericanChurch]] peyote exemption and a 2006 Supreme Court ayahuasca decision are used as precedent signals that religious use of controlled substances can sometimes receive legal protection.
- The episode stresses that churches do not have full protection unless the government has granted an exemption; otherwise the [[ControlledSubstancesAct]] can still expose them to law enforcement and court action.
- The [[DrugEnforcementAdministration|DEA]] exemption route is described as slow and risky because applicants may have to pause controlled-substance use and disclose conduct that could be incriminating if the request is denied.
- [[MyersTest]] criteria, including spiritual leadership, ritual, theology, sacred texts, holidays, and regular ceremonies, become a practical checklist for making religion legible to outside authorities.
- Zydor began from the broader [[ChurchOfAmbrosia]] organization and moved from a marijuana-church origin toward a mushroom-centered, high-dose practice after Dave's claimed 2019 mushroom vision.
- The 2020 Oakland police raid confiscated mushrooms, marijuana, and cash, but Dave says the raid brought national attention and Zydor resumed serving sacrament the next day.
- Local decriminalization in cities such as Oakland lowers some practical risk for mushrooms, but it does not remove state or federal illegality, nor does it resolve liability after injuries or deaths in other psychedelic-church cases.
- Rapp defends Zydor as both a true church and [[PsychedelicChurchHarmReduction]], contrasting high-dose mushroom access with alcohol, cocaine, and opioids.
