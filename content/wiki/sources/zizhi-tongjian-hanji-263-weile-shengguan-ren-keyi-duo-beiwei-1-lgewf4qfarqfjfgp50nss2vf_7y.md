# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》263｜为了升官，人可以多卑微？（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the rise-of-[[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] prequel to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-264-tianfen-hunyan-xianchang-ruhe-chengle-guanfu-de-duantoutai-lux-kqkfsqpjldtocrk65j5pvl8p|Hanji 264]]. It presents Tian Fen as an initially undistinguished attendant in [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]]'s household who uses conspicuous deference to gain access to Dou Ying and, indirectly, [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]]'s power network.

After [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] succeeds Han Jingdi, the episode describes a layered court in which the senior Dowager Dou retains great authority while [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]] has closer maternal access to the young emperor. Tian Fen's Wang-family connection and familiarity with Dou Ying's network help him become grand commandant; he then recruits retainers for advice, information, and political execution. The source therefore treats humility, kinship, network knowledge, and retainer infrastructure as complementary mechanisms, while leaving appointment causation and private motive source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- During Han Jingdi's early reign, Tian Fen holds an undistinguished attendant role in Dou Ying's large household and is not presented as unusually capable.
- Tian Fen serves Dou Ying with extremely low posture; the episode interprets this conduct as a deliberate investment in access, protection, and advancement rather than simple affection.
- Because Dou Ying is close to the politically dominant Dowager Dou, deference toward Dou Ying also gives Tian Fen a route toward the senior court power center.
- After Wudi's accession, Dowager Dou's senior authority and Empress Dowager Wang's mother-son proximity create overlapping but unequal influence channels.
- Tian Fen's appointment as grand commandant is attributed jointly to his sister's position and his long familiarity with Dou Ying's people, rather than demonstrated merit alone.
- Tian Fen recruits retainers after taking office because they can provide advice, intelligence, contacts, and help with matters serving himself, Wang, and Wudi.
- The episode frames adaptation and reading changed power conditions as politically useful, but its moral evaluation of humiliation and opportunism does not establish Tian Fen's private intent.

## Key Quotes

> “事有常变，治政有大体，应世有权宜。” - the episode's opening frame for adapting conduct to changing political circumstances.

> “进庙先拜神。” - the episode's analogy for reaching Dowager Dou's power through deference to Dou Ying.

## Connections

- [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] - initially minor attendant whose deference, kinship access, network familiarity, and retainer recruitment form the episode's advancement case.
- [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] - powerful patron whose household and Dou-family access provide Tian Fen's early training and political bridge.
- [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] - senior power center approached indirectly through Dou Ying and still influential after Wudi's accession.
- [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后]] - Tian Fen's sister and the maternal channel that strengthens his position under the new emperor.
- [[StrategicPersonalDeference|策略性低姿态进身]] - deliberate lowering of status display to gain access, trust, or advancement.
- [[RetainerInformationInfrastructure|门客信息基础设施]] - household network used to supply advice, intelligence, contacts, and execution capacity.
- [[OuterRelativeBalanceUnderImperialPower|皇权下的外戚制衡]] - court structure whose earlier formation is visible in the coexistence of Dou- and Wang-family influence.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode backfills Tian Fen's ascent before Hanji 264-265's conflict with Dou Ying; early dependence and later rivalry describe different phases rather than incompatible relationships.
- Naming normalization: the supplied note uses 田芬、豆婴 and 豆太后; the canonical pages use the historical forms 田蚡、窦婴 and 窦太后.
- Office and chronology caution: the appointment rationale, exact balance among Dowager Dou, Empress Dowager Wang, and Wudi, and the claim that the emperor appointed Dou Ying and Tian Fen partly to please Dowager Dou require comparison with transmitted records.
- Motive caution: Tian Fen's deference, Dou Ying's response, retainer recruitment, and use of information are episode-attributed; outward humility does not independently prove calculated insincerity or exclusive motive.
- Scope caution: the source ends at Tian Fen's early consolidation and does not yet narrate the complete later conflict with Dou Ying.
