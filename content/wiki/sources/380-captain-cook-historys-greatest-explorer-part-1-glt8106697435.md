# 380. Captain Cook: History’s Greatest Explorer (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[JamesCook]] from a Yorkshire farm-labourer family through merchant-coal training, Royal Navy service, wartime survey work, and Newfoundland charting to command of the [[Endeavour]]. It presents the voyage as an Enlightenment project in practical seamanship, astronomy, natural history, and crew health that was also shaped from the outset by European rivalry, a search for Terra Australis, and possible British territorial acquisition.

The episode resists both the claim that Cook “discovered Australia” and a simple hero-or-villain verdict. Cook’s exceptional technical discipline, [[JosephBanks]]’s well-funded scientific party, and the transit-of-Venus mission coexist with imperial secrecy, European misconceptions about unfamiliar peoples and places, and evidence that Cook’s judgments of Indigenous societies remained hierarchical even as they later changed.

## Key Claims

- Cook’s rise from a labouring family depended on patronage, maritime apprenticeship, Royal Navy opportunity, and demonstrated merit rather than elite birth.
- Coal-trade seamanship, Seven Years’ War surveying on the St Lawrence, and four years charting Newfoundland and Labrador formed the practical navigation and cartographic skill that won Admiralty confidence.
- The 1769 transit of Venus gave the expedition a genuine international scientific purpose: observations from separated locations could improve estimates of astronomical distance.
- The voyage was never purely scientific; the Admiralty also wanted Cook to search for the conjectured Terra Australis and assess lands for British strategic or territorial use.
- Banks turned the Endeavour into a substantial natural-history expedition by bringing assistants, instruments, equipment, and an artist alongside the naval crew.
- Cook treated provisioning, diet, cleanliness, and discipline as operational science, although the episode’s claims about sauerkraut and scurvy prevention require historical and medical qualification.
- The episode’s opening rejects a European discovery narrative because Indigenous Australians had occupied the continent for tens of thousands of years and earlier European navigators had already reached parts of the region.

## Key Quotes

> “perhaps the greatest sea explorer of all time” - the episode’s attribution to Nicholas Thomas, presented as an evaluative judgment rather than a settled ranking.

> “I want to go to sea” - the answer attributed to the young Cook in the possibly apocryphal South Sea shilling story.
