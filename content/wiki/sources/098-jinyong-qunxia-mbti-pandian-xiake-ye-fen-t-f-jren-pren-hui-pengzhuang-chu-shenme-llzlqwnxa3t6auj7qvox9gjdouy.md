# 098 金庸群侠MBTI盘点：侠客也分T、F？J人P人会碰撞出什么？

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode uses T/F and J/P preferences to compare decision-making, emotional priority, planning, and adaptability across [[JinYong|金庸]] characters. Its strongest contribution is [[FictionalCharacterTypingBoundary|a boundary for fictional-character typing]]: recurring choices can organize comparison, but mixed motives, role demands, changing habits, disputed readings, and plot function prevent four-letter labels from becoming definitive character identities. The hosts also argue that logic, feeling, order, and flexibility have no intrinsic moral value, while acknowledging that wuxia convention often codes emotional responsiveness as heroic.

## Key Claims

- T/F is used as a decision-priority heuristic rather than an emotion/no-emotion binary: the discussion contrasts problem solving and impersonal principle with interpersonal feeling and value commitment.
- J/P is used to compare early closure, order, planning, and control with openness, improvisation, and adaptation; the hosts' own production workflow shows that planning and surprise can be deliberately combined.
- [[FictionalCharacterTypingBoundary|Fictional-character typing]] is strongest when it tests recurring choices across several situations and weakest when one dramatic act, moral judgment, or plot outcome is treated as proof of a fixed type.
- Kangxi and Huang Rong remain explicitly disputed cases, showing that office, circumstance, learned habit, and different readings can support more than one interpretation.
- T/F and J/P do not determine virtue: Guo Jing and Zuo Lengchan can both be planned and goal-directed, while emotional commitment can support chivalry, self-sacrifice, impulsiveness, or political misjudgment.
- The discussion suggests that wuxia often favors feeling-coded heroes because empathy, loyalty, and response to strangers' suffering help define “侠,” while calculation and long-range plotting are frequently assigned to antagonists.
- Wei Xiaobao, Shi Potian, and Linghu Chong separate flexibility from laziness: adaptation can involve opportunistic improvisation, sustained effort without a fixed life plan, or a martial style built around open response.
