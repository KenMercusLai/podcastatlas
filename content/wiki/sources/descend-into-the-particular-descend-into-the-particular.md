# Descend into the Particular

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode uses Jesuit casuistry to develop [[ParticularizedMoralDiscernment]]: moral judgment should suspend ideological reflex long enough to examine the facts, motives, institutions, and suffering within a concrete case. [[MalcolmGladwell]] applies that method to Ferguson, arguing that the Justice Department's insufficient-evidence finding about Darren Wilson and its finding of predatory, racially harmful municipal policing can both be true.

The central case is the police killing of [[AngelNavarro]], who approached officers with a cell phone after claiming to have a gun. [[RonMartinelli]] interprets Navarro's preceding conduct and recorded movements through [[SuicideByCopInference]], while [[FelipeNavarro]] must reconcile that reconstruction with the brother he knew. The episode ends with [[ConsolationBeyondLiability]]: Navarro, his family, and a visibly distressed officer may all warrant humane attention even though their roles, losses, and responsibilities are not equivalent.

## Key Claims

- Particularized moral reasoning delays categorical judgment so that inconvenient facts, institutional context, motive, and suffering remain visible together.
- The two Ferguson reports answer different questions: federal evidence did not support prosecuting Wilson, while the wider investigation found revenue-driven and abusive policing that had damaged police legitimacy.
- Reaction-time demonstrations and Martinelli's reconstruction show why officers may perceive a small movement as an immediate lethal threat, but tactical expertise can also favor preemptive interpretations.
- Navarro's attack, vehicle theft, claims to be armed, repeated stops, concealment of one hand, and presentation of a phone support Martinelli's suicide-by-cop interpretation without directly proving Navarro's intention.
- Video evidence can constrain a narrative without making its moral meaning self-interpreting; Felipe's view changes after he sees behavior that appears erratic and threatening.
- The crying officer's reaction widens the moral frame to include possible trauma without erasing Navarro's death, his family's grief, or questions about police conduct.
- Consolation and accountability answer different questions: recognizing several wounded parties does not make them causally or morally interchangeable.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with current wiki content was adopted. The Ferguson findings concern different levels of analysis and can coexist: evidence about Wilson's individual federal criminal liability does not resolve the legitimacy of Ferguson's policing system.
