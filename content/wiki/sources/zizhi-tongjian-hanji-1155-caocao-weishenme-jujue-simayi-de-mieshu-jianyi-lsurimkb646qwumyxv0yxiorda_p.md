## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows the aftermath of [[HanzhongCampaign215|曹操西征汉中（215年）]] from [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]]'s rewarded submission through the contest for Ba and [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s refusal to turn victory into an immediate invasion of [[YizhouLateHan|益州]]. It treats favorable surrender terms as a public signal, extends [[ZhangFei|张飞]], [[ZhangHeLateHan|张郃]], and [[HuangQuanLateHan|黄权]] into the Hanzhong-Ba frontier, and presents [[ZhaoYanLateHan|赵俨]] containing a Guanzhong troop mutiny through selective accountability, amnesty, and phased transfer.

The episode's distinct historiographical claim is that the *Zizhi Tongjian* groups events from different years to keep an actor and causal chain together. It therefore warns readers to distinguish narrative placement from event chronology and to compare annalistic arrangement with biographical histories such as the *Sanguozhi*; the proposed 217, 218, and 219 dates remain episode-attributed until checked against the underlying texts.

## Key Claims

- Cao Cao receives Zhang Lu personally, grants office and honors to Zhang Lu, his sons, [[YanPuLateHan|阎圃]], and associated surrenderers, and thereby makes negotiated submission appear safer than continued resistance.
- After Hanzhong changes hands, Huang Quan's earlier warning about its strategic value becomes immediate: he moves against other Ba-region actors after learning that Zhang Lu has already submitted.
- Zhang He advances into Ba, attempts to move residents toward Hanzhong, and is opposed by Zhang Fei; the episode credits Zhang Fei with reconnaissance, terrain use, and a night attack rather than treating him as merely impulsive.
- The host makes the Cao Pi-Cao Zhi succession struggle the principal explanation for Cao Cao's return north, but this is stronger than the evidence can establish and should remain one competing political constraint alongside logistics, frontier security, troop condition, and season.
- Zhao Yan faces a reported mutiny among troops drawn from former Han Sui and Ma Chao networks, first secures a small mounted group, limits punishment to ringleaders, and uses amnesty to bring ordinary participants back.
- After fear of replacement triggers renewed instability, Zhao Yan separates a trusted minority, transfers the rest, and later rotates out those temporarily retained, combining immediate reassurance with longer-term force separation.
- The episode says the Huang Quan action, Zhang Fei-Zhang He battle, and Zhao Yan troop-transfer case occurred in different later years even though the *Zizhi Tongjian* places them beside the 215 sequence.
- The opening parenting section argues that children learn from repeated parental conduct and need room to experience difficulty; it is interpretive framing rather than evidence for the historical claims.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode reinforces Hanji 1153's short-window invasion argument but ranks succession politics more confidently than the current evidence permits.
- Chronology caution: the episode assigns the Huang Quan, Zhang Fei-Zhang He, and Zhao Yan episodes to 217, 218, and 219 respectively. Those dates, and the claim that their placement is deliberate causal composition rather than transmission or summary complexity, require direct comparison with the *Zizhi Tongjian*, *Sanguozhi*, and relevant commentaries.
- Campaign caution: grouping Ba-region fighting and later Guanzhong troop transfer with the 215 Hanzhong aftermath is useful for reception history but does not make them phases of one continuous campaign.
- Source-scope caution: titles, troop and migration totals, fifty-day duration, battlefield route and tactics, kinship among the mutineers, pardon mechanics, the reported transfer of more than twenty thousand people, dialogue, and Cao Cao's motives remain episode-reported.
- The parenting, self-mastery, and “治未病” applications are motivational analogies rather than independent historical evidence.
