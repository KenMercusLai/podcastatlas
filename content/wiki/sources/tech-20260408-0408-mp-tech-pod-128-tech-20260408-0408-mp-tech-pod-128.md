## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[StephanieHughes]] interview [[SteveShaw]] of the [[WhartonSchool]] about [[CognitiveSurrender]], a pattern where people defer to AI answers instead of doing their own reasoning. Shaw argues that classic fast/slow cognition is no longer enough when tools such as [[ChatGPT]] supply [[ArtificialCognition|artificial cognition]], or "system three," inside the decision process.

The episode's core synthesis is that AI dependence is not only an accuracy problem; it is a timing and agency problem. Participants often adopted ChatGPT answers even when the system was wrong, and Shaw's practical response is close to [[FirstDraftThinking]] and [[AIUsePacing]]: think first, then prompt, and sometimes go offline enough to preserve [[HumanJudgmentUnderAI]].

## Key Claims
- [[SteveShaw]] says traditional behavioral economics often separates fast intuitive thinking from slower deliberative thinking, but AI introduces a third source of cognition into judgment.
- The source names this third layer [[ArtificialCognition|artificial cognition]] or "system three," because the AI can reason on behalf of the user.
- The Wharton studies had participants answer logic and reasoning questions, with some participants given optional AI access.
- Researchers manipulated [[ChatGPT]] accuracy in the background without participants knowing.
- Participants often adopted AI-provided answers even when those answers were incorrect.
- Time pressure made performance more tied to whether the AI was right or wrong, because users had less time to reason independently.
- Higher stakes led some participants to override AI more often, but the episode says performance still did not return to the level of participants without AI access.
- Shaw connects [[CognitiveSurrender]] to possible workplace and education de-skilling.
- In education, Shaw warns that if students defer the learning process itself to AI, they may never develop critical thinking skills in the first place.
- The same concern applies to [[AgenticWorkflow|agentic AI]], where autonomous tasks may create fewer natural moments for human checking.
- Shaw's own practice is not anti-AI: he allows students to use AI, uses it daily, and advises students to generate their own ideas before prompting.
- The closing [[HowWeSurvive]] promo mentions geoengineering, stratospheric balloons, space sunshades, and the space economy, but the main source contribution is AI decision-making.

## Key Quotes
