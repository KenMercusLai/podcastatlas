## Summary
This [[WhatsNextKejiZaozhidao|What's Next｜科技早知道]] episode interviews [[Chouxiangzai]] and [[Taitai]], two traditional film/TV practitioners who moved into [[AIShortDrama]] production. It adds a director-centered view of [[AIVideoProductionWorkflow]]: scripts, IP, asset libraries, storyboards, repeated generation, model selection, editing, platform submission, paid traffic, and overseas audience understanding all matter more than model access alone. The source's strongest contribution is connecting [[AIShortDramaOverseasLocalization]], [[AIDirectorCoreWorkflow]], and [[ShortDramaEconomics]] through real project examples including [[WuGeGeGeDouChongWo]], [[YiWanFuWengHuiGui]], and [[XinYuanYangHuDieMeng]].

## Key Claims
- The source reports that China's micro-short-drama market grew from 2020 to 2024 by more than 50x, reached 50.4 billion RMB in 2024, and exceeded Chinese box office revenue for the first time.
- The source reports that the 2025 micro-short-drama market exceeded 90 billion RMB, and that by February 2026 there were 127,800 AI short dramas on air, with AI comic dramas making up 30% of Spring Festival short-drama playback.
- [[Chouxiangzai]] moved from CCTV documentary and commercial film work into AI video after the pandemic weakened traditional film demand; his account produced a million-play breakout roughly three months after launch.
- Early AI short-drama production was slow and labor-intensive: [[WuGeGeGeDouChongWo]] had one sub-two-minute first episode that took almost ten days, with roughly 70 images converted into roughly 70 shots.
- The workflow has shifted from image-first "draw-card" labor toward [[AIDirectorCoreWorkflow]]: stronger multi-reference models such as [[Seedance]]/C-DANCE 2.0 can handle more character/background consistency, while people spend more time on script, IP, assets, storyboards, scene requirements, and editing judgment.
- [[Taitai]] and [[Chouxiangzai]] argue that the hard human capability is not only tool operation but directing the model: knowing how to describe performance, blocking, tone, emotion, and cinematic intent.
- [[AIShortDramaOverseasLocalization]] is not just translation. The source says [[WuGeGeGeDouChongWo]] localized domestic hit logic for overseas viewers by changing settings, appearances, status symbols, and fantasy scale.
- [[YiWanFuWengHuiGui]] is used as the commercial proof point: the source says it ranked first overseas in November and made money, despite visible AI flaws, because the story and target audience psychology worked.
- The source reinforces [[ShortDramaEconomics]]: story, genre, emotional hooks, and audience resonance still beat production polish at the current market stage, while better production can help only when it serves the target audience.
- [[Taitai]] estimates a 60-episode, 1.5-minute-per-episode modern romance live-action short drama may cost 300,000-500,000 RMB, while an AI version should be able to fall to about one-third of that; she says labor, not compute, remains the main cost.
- The commercial chain is described as production side, paid-traffic side, and platform side. The source presents ROI 120 as a working traffic-buying benchmark and says platform rules, prices, and settlement structure can change company survival.
- The source treats long-form AI film as technically possible but commercially immature: 60-minute-plus AI online films may seek approval paths, but long production cycles, fast model iteration, unclear budgets, and uncertain distribution make the route unsettled.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The source reinforces the wiki's existing [[AIShortDrama]], [[AIVideoProductionWorkflow]], and [[ShortDramaEconomics]] pages by adding a more director-centered and overseas-commercialization view.
- The source creates a productive tension with [[LiveActionShortDrama]] and [[LiveActionFilmUnderAI]] rather than contradicting them: it argues AI is well suited to short-drama spectacle and high-emotion beats, while still conceding that subtle, restrained, actor-driven performance can justify live action.
- Several market-size, ranking, ROI, platform-policy, and model-quality claims are source-reported practitioner observations. They should be treated as first-person industry evidence, not as independently verified market statistics.
