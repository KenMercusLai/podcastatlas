# 244. 书写给过去、未来的人与神明——中国文明中的书写传统与长期主义

## Summary

This [[BowuzhiPodcast|博物志]] episode uses visits to the National Museum of Classic Books and the First Historical Archives of China to ask whom writing addresses across time. It moves from [[OracleBoneDivination|oracle-bone divination]] and [[BronzeInscriptionPosterity|bronze inscriptions]] through [[ChineseClassicalInterpretationContinuity|classical reinterpretation]], Song editions, and [[ChineseWoodblockPrintingEconomics|printing economics]], then contrasts the enormous but restricted [[YongleDadian|《永乐大典》]] with the later scholarly and public uses of [[SikuQuanshu|《四库全书》]] and [[ImperialVeritableRecords|imperial veritable records]]. Its governing claim is [[LongHorizonKnowledgeStewardship]]: durable cultural memory requires not only costly inscription or collection, but repeated copying, organization, access, interpretation, and transfer to future users.

## Key Claims

- [[OracleBoneDivination|Oracle bones]] were specialized ritual records rather than ordinary Shang writing material. Their preparation cost, crack-making procedure, four-part inscription structure, and occasional verification records join uncertainty management to royal authority and archival evidence.
- [[WangGuowei|王国维]]'s comparison of oracle-bone names with the *Shiji* exemplifies mutually testing excavated and transmitted evidence, while the episode warns that success for Shang history does not automatically prove Xia history.
- [[BronzeInscriptionPosterity|Bronze inscriptions]] more explicitly address descendants: durable vessels record ancestry, awards, events, transactions, and ritual order through formulas of perpetual family possession.
- [[ChineseClassicalInterpretationContinuity|Classical interpretation]] creates continuity by repeatedly returning to a shared textual center. Han exegesis, Tang reassessment, Song philosophical construction, Ming latitude, and Qing evidential scholarship are presented as changing methods within a common civilizational conversation.
- [[AncientChineseBookMateriality|Song editions]] show that survival is shaped by calligraphy, carving, paper, layout, cost, use, and collecting; mass commercial production can expand readership even when cheaper objects survive less often.
- [[ChineseWoodblockPrintingEconomics|Woodblock and movable-type printing]] should be compared through the Chinese character inventory, duplicate sorts, skilled labor, layout quality, print run, and reuse rather than a simple hierarchy of invention.
- [[YongleDadian|《永乐大典》]] operated like a manually indexed knowledge database: its scale and extraction method preserved otherwise lost material, but narrow royal access, weak everyday use, physical vulnerability, dispersal, and an uncertain original copy made collection without readership precarious.
- [[SikuQuanshu|《四库全书》]] differed as a collection of books rather than a classified extraction system and used *Yongle Dadian* material for textual recovery, including reconstruction of lost works.
- [[ImperialVeritableRecords|Imperial veritable records and instructions]] were produced and stored with monumental authority. They are shaped accounts rather than transparent fact, yet their survival allows later historians to compare, criticize, and reuse dynastic memory.
- [[LongHorizonKnowledgeStewardship]] depends on active custody: preservation, recopying, classification, explanation, access, and future use matter more than a grand project merely existing.

## Key Quotes

> "子子孙孙永宝用" - formula used to characterize bronze inscriptions as writing explicitly directed toward descendants.

> "持续保存、整理有价值的东西，并把它交给未来的人" - the episode's closing definition of long-termism.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode extends the wiki's existing book-materiality, printing-economics, oracle-bone, and recopying frames with Chinese cases.
- The source distinguishes a strong Shang-era result for [[WangGuowei|王国维]]'s evidence comparison from broader claims about the Xia, limiting rather than rejecting the method.
- The episode's precise dimensions, totals, survival counts, reconstruction examples, and explanations for the missing original *Yongle Dadian* remain source-scoped; competing loss theories are not resolved.
- Imperial veritable records remain indispensable but politically edited evidence, so survival and official status do not guarantee neutrality.
