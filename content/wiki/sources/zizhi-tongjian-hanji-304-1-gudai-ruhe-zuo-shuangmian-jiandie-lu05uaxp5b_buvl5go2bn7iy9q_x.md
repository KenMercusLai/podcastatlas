# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》304-1｜古代如何做双面间谍？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode asks why [[WuBeiWesternHan|伍被]], despite expecting [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]]'s rebellion to fail, first advised the prince and later confessed the plot to a central envoy. It uses [[LeiBeiWesternHan|雷被]]'s earlier successful denunciation as a comparison and proposes that Wu Bei hoped to turn a dangerous conspiracy into personal advancement by changing sides at the decisive moment.

The durable synthesis is narrower than that motive reconstruction. The episode presents early denunciation and late confession as politically different acts: the court allegedly handled Lei Bei cautiously while war with Xiongnu made premature pressure on Liu An risky, but after the conspiracy was exposed [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] argued that an intimate royal retainer who cultivated outside political ties had to be executed for institutional deterrence. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] reportedly preferred to spare Wu Bei but accepted Zhang's argument, showing that cooperation can lose its protective value when authorities prioritize exemplary punishment over information.

## Key Claims

- The episode contrasts Liu An's association with the `Huainanzi` warning against desire and fame with his reported rebellion and suicide, using the gap between stated knowledge and action as moral framing.
- Wu Bei is presented as one of the “淮南八公” and as a close adviser who expected Liu An's project to fail but nevertheless contributed advice before confessing it.
- Lei Bei allegedly denounced Liu An and crown prince Liu Qian earlier, survived, and entered the central-court side; the host treats that outcome as a possible model for Wu Bei.
- The source attributes Lei Bei's survival partly to timing: while Wei Qing's Xiongnu campaign remained unresolved, the court supposedly avoided public treatment of Liu An's rebellion preparation that might trigger a princely uprising.
- After Liu Qian was arrested, Wu Bei allegedly feared that another confession would implicate him first and therefore gave the central envoy a detailed account of the plot.
- The host infers that Wu Bei may have hoped to encourage action, defect at the decisive moment, and exchange disclosure for office or reward, but repeatedly identifies this motive as conjecture.
- Han Wudi reportedly wanted to spare Wu Bei, while Zhang Tang insisted that a close court-connected retainer who privately served a prince had to die or future discipline would fail.
- The episode connects Wu Bei's admiration for the ancestral story of Wu Zixu and Prince Guang to ambition, but the psychological and genealogical inference remains source-scoped.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode supplies an internal Huainan-case layer before Hanji 304-2's account of Liu Ci's response and Hanji 316-1's downstream investigation of Liu Ji.
- The source is a compact structured summary rather than a primary-text dossier. Wu Bei's exact advice, confession, legal status, claimed entitlement to reward, the identities and fates of every “淮南八公” member, and Zhang Tang's quoted legal reasoning require direct source comparison.
- The central “double agent” explanation is explicitly the host's inference. Participation followed by confession establishes a change of position, not a proved long-range plan to manufacture rebellion and profit from suppressing it.
- Lei Bei's complete denunciation, the court's exact knowledge, the Xiongnu-war timing rationale, and the claim that his survival directly inspired Wu Bei remain episode-attributed.
- The Wu Zixu genealogy, Wu Bei's private admiration, the territorial description of King Helu's Wu, and their causal value for explaining Wu Bei's ambition remain source-scoped.
