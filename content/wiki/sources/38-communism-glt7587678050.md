# 38. Communism

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode treats [[Communism]] as both a modern political project and an older dream of shared property, equality, liberation, and earthly justice. Beginning with [[KarlMarx|Karl Marx / 马克思]] and [[FriedrichEngels]], it connects Christian and utopian precedents to [[VladimirLenin|Lenin's]] vanguard party, [[JosephStalin|Stalin's]] coercive state, Soviet disillusion, and the continued formal communist identity of states including [[China]], [[Cuba]], [[Vietnam]], and [[Laos]].

Its strongest interpretive claim is [[MarxismAsPoliticalReligion]]: Marxism can organize identity through prophets, canonical texts, heresy, sacrifice, redemption, and a promised classless future while presenting itself as scientific. Its political conclusion is skeptical and source-scoped: the hosts argue that repeated efforts to abolish private ownership and dissent create a [[UtopianCommunistCoercion|coercion problem]], even though ideals of justice and liberation retain emotional force.

## Key Claims

- Communist-like ideals predate Marx in early Christian communal practice, medieval and Reformation radicalism, Thomas More's utopian writing, and the [[Diggers]].
- Marx and Engels wrote from industrializing nineteenth-century Europe, admired capitalism's transformative power, and expected revolution in advanced industrial societies rather than agrarian Russia.
- [[VladimirLenin]] adapted Marxism to Russia through a disciplined vanguard party, turning historical expectation into a program of seizure and centralized rule.
- The hosts present Stalinism as developing from Leninist institutions and argue that confiscation, economic remaking, and intolerance of plural dissent help explain communist state coercion.
- [[MarxismAsPoliticalReligion]] captures the movement's sacred texts, prophetic figures, heresies, ritual symbols, and promise of earthly redemption without claiming that Marxism and Christianity are identical.
- Communist rule can fuse with nationalism and become a conservative ruling order concerned with institutional survival rather than continuous revolution.
- China illustrates the gap that can open between official communist identity and market practice, while continuing party control preserves a political lineage even when doctrine and everyday life diverge.

## Key Quotes

> “according to his ability” and “according to his needs” — the formula linked in the episode to both early Christian communal practice and Marx's classless ideal.

> “the classless society never arrives” — the episode's skeptical comparison between communist redemption and an earthly New Jerusalem.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki was adopted. The episode's broad claim that communism tends toward totalitarianism is an argued interpretation, not a conclusion independently established across every communist movement or state.
- Its account of [[JosephStalin]] as a committed Marxist developing Leninist logic adds an ideological explanation to existing wartime profiles; it does not displace evidence about institutions, nationalism, logistics, command adaptation, or coercive state capacity.
- The episode's claims about exploitation, global living standards, human nature, communist belief in contemporary China, and the inevitability of violence remain host-attributed and source-scoped.
