# 666. Wine and the Birth of Civilisation

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode treats [[Wine|wine]] as a long-running cultural system rather than only a drink. It follows wine from early Caucasus archaeology through Canaanite and Phoenician trade, Greek and Roman drinking culture, Christian ritual survival, Islamic legal and poetic ambivalence, English bottle technology, French classification, terroir language, and the 1976 [[JudgmentOfParisWine|Judgment of Paris]].

The episode's strongest wiki contribution is to connect wine's material base to institutions of prestige. [[WineTradeCivilizationNetwork]] explains how vines, amphorae, trade routes, ritual, and empire spread wine, while [[WinePrestigeClassificationSystem]] explains how estates, growth rankings, terroir, blind tasting, and New World competition made quality a social and commercial claim as much as a sensory one.

## Key Claims

- The earliest wine branch is placed around the south-of-Black-Sea world: Georgian pottery evidence near Tbilisi dates to around 6000 BC, while Areni in Armenia supplies the oldest known winery in the episode's account.
- Canaanites and Phoenicians made wine transportable at scale through export networks and amphorae, helping wine become a Mediterranean trade good rather than only a local agricultural product.
- Greek wine culture exported rules of sociable drinking through dilution and symposia, while Rome turned wine into an integrated imperial market and a marker of civilization.
- After western Roman collapse, wine survived through rivers, abbeys, and the Christian Eucharist even as northern beer cultures became more prominent.
- Islamic wine culture is presented as legally restrictive but culturally complex: prohibition, sanctions, Hanafi distinctions, Baghdad taverns, Abu Nuwas, Ibn Taymiyyah, and Rumi make wine a site of law, evasion, poetry, and metaphor.
- English coal-fired glassmaking, stronger bottles, Portuguese cork, port demand, and the corkscrew helped create the technological conditions for modern bottled wine and sparkling wine.
- French prestige emerged through Bordeaux estate branding, Haut-Brion, First Growth language, the 1855 classification, and terroir as a language of place, tradition, quality, and marketing.
- The 1976 Judgment of Paris showed Californian wines beating French wines in blind tasting, lifting New World prestige without eliminating French vocabulary or symbolic authority.
