# 723. The Charge of the Light Brigade: Crushing the Russians (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode follows the Allied invasion of Crimea from the poorly informed landing at Calamita Bay through the [[BattleOfTheAlma|Battle of the Alma]] and the missed opportunity to attack weakly defended Sevastopol. French initiative, British infantry discipline, the Minié rifle, and Russian errors produced victory at the Alma, but delay allowed the Russians to fortify Sevastopol and block its harbour, converting a proposed raid into a siege.

The episode then traces the exposed British supply system at Balaclava and the cavalry command tensions that preceded the famous charge. [[FitzRoySomerset1stBaronRaglan|Raglan]] repeatedly restrained the cavalry, [[GeorgeBingham3rdEarlOfLucan|Lucan]] and [[JamesBrudenell7thEarlOfCardigan|Cardigan]] remained divided, and [[LouisEdwardNolan|Louis Nolan]] attacked their passivity while promoting decisive cavalry action. The installment ends as Russian forces open the [[BattleOfBalaclava|Battle of Balaclava]], before the Thin Red Line or either cavalry charge.

## Key Claims

- The Allied expedition sailed without reliable intelligence about Russian strength, Crimean geography, landing sites, Sevastopol's defenses, or the likelihood of a winter campaign.
- An unopposed landing still exposed major coalition differences: the French landed rapidly, while British shortages of carts and difficulty unloading horses and equipment consumed several days.
- Cholera, dysentery, heat, thirst, and Russian destruction of supplies degraded the march south, while Raglan's caution limited cavalry reconnaissance.
- At the Alma, French troops exploited a flank [[AlexanderSergeyevichMenshikov|Menshikov]] considered impassable, while Campbell's Highland regiments used discipline and Minié-rifle fire against less effective Russian muskets.
- The Allies failed to exploit victory against weak landward defenses at Sevastopol; the delay enabled fortification, harbour obstruction, and a prolonged siege.
- British dependence on Balaclava rested on a single vulnerable road, rough tracks, understrength defenses, incompletely armed redoubts, and a cavalry force short of shelter and fodder.
- Nolan's doctrine of decisive cavalry action and contempt for passive or purchased command intensified the reputational pressure surrounding Lucan and the Light Brigade.
