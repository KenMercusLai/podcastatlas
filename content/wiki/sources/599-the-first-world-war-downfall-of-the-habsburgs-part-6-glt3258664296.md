# 599. The First World War: Downfall of the Habsburgs (Part 6)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses the two 1914-1915 sieges of [[Przemysl|Przemyśl]] to connect [[EasternFrontFirstWorldWar|Eastern Front]] operations with the weakening of [[AustriaHungary|Austria-Hungary]]. [[FranzConradVonHotzendorf|Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf]]'s overambitious Galician offensive, rail and supply failures, multilingual command friction, the failed Carpathian relief campaign, mass loss, surrender, and growing dependence on [[Germany]] turn the fortress from a shield for Hungary into a symbol of imperial breakdown.

The civilian branch is equally important. In [[Galicia]], Russian occupation joined Russification to violence against Jews, deportation of Polish elites, suppression of Ukrainian and Greek Catholic identity, and forced population movement. The episode presents these events as an early eastern-European pattern later intensified by revolution, nationalism, ethnic cleansing, Nazi occupation, and the Holocaust, while stopping short of describing the 1914-1915 violence itself as genocide.

## Key Claims

- [[Przemysl|Przemyśl]] mattered both as a fortress guarding routes through the Carpathians toward Hungary and as a symbol of Habsburg prestige.
- Conrad launched an eastern offensive despite Russian numerical superiority and severe Austro-Hungarian railway, supply, and command-language limits.
- The retreat from Galicia cost Austria-Hungary heavily in killed, wounded, and captured soldiers, after which Conrad left roughly 130,000 men to hold Przemyśl.
- The first siege ended when Russian forces shifted north, but symbolic attachment to the relief made evacuation before the second siege politically harder.
- The January 1915 Carpathian relief offensive exposed troops to extreme cold, inadequate clothing, broken supply, frostbite, disease, and losses that the episode links to declining loyalty to army, monarchy, and empire.
- Przemyśl surrendered in March 1915 after starvation, disease, a failed breakout, and demolition of military infrastructure; around 120,000 defenders entered Russian captivity.
- Russian rule in Galicia pursued political and cultural incorporation through Russian language, law, time, schooling, signs, festivals, and attacks on institutions associated with Polish and Ukrainian identity.
- Cossack and occupation violence against Jewish communities included looting, killing, rape, pogroms, and mass deportation, including the removal of Jews from Przemyśl.
- German forces retook the fortress in June 1915, but German rescue underlined rather than reversed Austria-Hungary's loss of autonomous military credibility.

## Key Quotes

> "too rotten and decayed to last" - the episode's rendering of a German liaison officer's judgment on the Habsburg Empire.

> "imperial honor" - the principle invoked when the trapped garrison was told not to surrender despite the failure of relief.
