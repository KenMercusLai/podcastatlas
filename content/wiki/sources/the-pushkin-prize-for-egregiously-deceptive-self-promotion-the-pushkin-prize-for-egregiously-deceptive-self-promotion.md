# The Pushkin Prize for Egregiously Deceptive Self-Promotion

## Summary

[[MalcolmGladwell]] gives the first satirical “Pushkin Prize” for egregiously deceptive self-promotion to [[ColumbiaUniversity]] after mathematics professor [[MichaelThaddeus]] compared the university's submissions to [[USNewsAndWorldReport|U.S. News and World Report]] with course, federal, faculty, and financial records. The episode reports substantial discrepancies in class size, part-time faculty, faculty qualifications, student–faculty ratio, and instructional spending, followed by Columbia's fall from second to 18th in the ranking.

The episode organizes its judgment around gratuitousness, remorselessness, and recklessness. [[LaurenLavelle]]'s model suggests some disputed inputs could improve Columbia's score but that the conspicuous instructional-spending difference produced only a modest modeled benefit. A comparison with [[TempleUniversity]] and [[MoshePorat]] distinguishes misconduct from institutional response, while the postscript records Columbia's withdrawal from the undergraduate ranking and [[MichaelThaddeus]]'s dispute with the university's account of its corrections.

## Key Claims

- [[MichaelThaddeus]] estimated that 62%–68% of Columbia classes enrolled fewer than 20 students, versus the 82.5% submitted to U.S. News, after extracting data for about 30,000 classes.
- The episode reports additional gaps between Columbia's U.S. News submissions and other records: 137 versus more than 1,000 part-time faculty, 99.5% terminal-degree faculty despite dozens of listed bachelor's-level instructors, and a reported 6:1 student–faculty ratio versus Thaddeus's estimate near 11:1.
- [[LaurenLavelle]] used a student-built reconstruction of the ranking algorithm to estimate that the disputed class-size, full-time-faculty, and student–faculty inputs together added about eight score points.
- Columbia reportedly counted roughly $1.2 billion in medical-center patient-care expense inside slightly more than $3 billion of instructional spending, while audited statements listed roughly $2 billion for instruction and educational administration.
- Lavelle's model estimated that reducing instructional spending from $3 billion to $2 billion would cost only about two or three score points, supporting Gladwell's “gratuitousness” framing without establishing who chose or approved the submission.
- [[InstitutionalRankingDataIntegrity]] requires submitted metrics to remain traceable to stable definitions, underlying records, and auditable inclusion rules rather than merely matching a ranker's requested field.
- Columbia's response is contrasted with Temple's external investigation, student compensation, and referral of former dean [[MoshePorat]] after false GMAT reporting in its online business school.
- Columbia later withdrew from the U.S. News undergraduate ranking and said it had corrected confirmed errors and changed collection methods, but Thaddeus disputed the completeness and specificity of that account.
- The episode argues that a university acts recklessly when its own institutional reporting violates the anti-falsification standards it imposes on students.
