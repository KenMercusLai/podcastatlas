# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》315-1｜汉武帝搞“强拆”，一举收复匈奴王

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]]'s surrender into a longer-term settlement policy. It says [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] dispersed Hunye King's former followers among five northwestern frontier commanderies, let them retain Xiongnu customs inside subordinate-state communities, and placed Han officers among them; the host interprets this as [[SurrenderedForceDispersal|降部拆分安置]], combining force fragmentation, surveillance, commitment testing, and use against the remaining [[Xiongnu|匈奴]].

The episode then connects reduced beacon activity and lower garrison demand to the opening of the [[HexiCorridorWesternHan|西汉河西走廊]] as a westward base. Its account of when Jiuquan, Wuwei, Zhangye, and Dunhuang became commanderies is explicitly contested: different *Hanshu* passages and the positions attributed to Zhang Weihua and Li Bingquan do not yield one settled chronology.

## Key Claims

- The surrendered followers of Hunye King are said to have been divided among Longxi, Beidi, Shang, Shuofang, and Wuyuan commanderies as five subordinate-state communities.
- The episode says those communities retained Xiongnu customs and lifeways while Han officers and commanders were stationed among them.
- The host interprets division into five groups as a way to reduce concentrated military risk, monitor loyalty, and place the surrendered population on the Xiongnu-facing frontier.
- Using surrendered Xiongnu against remaining Xiongnu is presented as an “以夷制夷” extension of Wudi's mix of force, inducement, and psychological pressure.
- Reduced Xiongnu activity is linked to fewer beacon alarms and an order halving garrison troops in Longxi, Beidi, and Shang commanderies.
- The surrender is presented as helping turn the Hexi Corridor into a base for Han movement toward the Western Regions.
- The dates and sequence for establishing the four Hexi commanderies remain disputed across transmitted records and the modern scholars cited by the host.

## Key Quotes

> “把几万兵马拆成了五份” - the host's compact explanation of the security logic attributed to the five-community arrangement.

> “学术界没有太多的定论” - the episode's express limit on the chronology of the four Hexi commanderies.

## Connections

- [[HunyeKingWesternHan|浑邪王]] - surrendered ruler whose former followers are dispersed and monitored in the episode's account.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler credited with joining settlement, surveillance, frontier defense, and westward expansion.
- [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - source population, continuing frontier opponent, and target of the “以夷制夷” interpretation.
- [[SurrenderedForceDispersal|降部拆分安置]] - synthesis of fragmentation, supervised cultural continuity, front-line placement, and commitment testing.
- [[HexiCorridorWesternHan|西汉河西走廊]] - territorial and logistical consequence attributed to the surrender, with commandery chronology kept contested.
- [[CrisisSurrenderStabilization|危机受降稳控]] - preceding phase that turns a reversible armed surrender into controlled movement.
- [[SurrenderReceptionDomesticBurden|归降接纳的内外成本冲突]] - preceding reception phase that records the transport, reward, labor, and legal costs borne inside Han.

## Contradictions

- The episode preserves rather than resolves a chronology conflict. It attributes one sequence to *Hanshu: Annals of Emperor Wu* (Jiuquan and Wuwei in 121 BCE; Zhangye and Dunhuang in 111 BCE) and a different sequence to *Hanshu: Treatise on Geography* (Jiuquan and Zhangye in 104 BCE, Wuwei in 101 BCE, and Dunhuang in 88 BCE).
- The Zhang Weihua position reported here places Jiuquan, Zhangye, and Dunhuang under Wudi but Wuwei in the Zhao-Xuan period; the Li Bingquan position keeps all four in Wudi's reign while allowing repeated boundary changes. The host prefers Zhang's view but expressly says the issue is unsettled.
- The “five commanderies were granted to Hunye King,” “several tens of thousands divided into five,” “以夷制夷,” 900-kilometre advance, 150,000-square-kilometre territory, exact monitoring arrangements, and causal link to reduced garrisons remain episode-attributed interpretations or figures requiring primary-source comparison.
- The opening teaser concerns an unnamed captive or enslaved Hu figure who later became an entrusted minister. The episode does not identify or complete that biography, so this note does not use it as direct evidence for [[JinMidiWesternHan|金日磾]].
