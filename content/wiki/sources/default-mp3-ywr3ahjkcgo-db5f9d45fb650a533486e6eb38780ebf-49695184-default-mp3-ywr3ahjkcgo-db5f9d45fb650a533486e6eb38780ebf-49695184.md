# Wellness 2.0: Who Do You Want To Be?

## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode has psychologist [[KenSheldon]] argue that the difficult part of goal pursuit is often choosing a destination that fits the person rather than merely learning how to reach it. [[SelfConcordantGoalSelection]] joins conscious choice to deeper inclinations through reflection, incubation, emerging insight, and real-world testing; [[IdentifiedMotivationPersistence]] explains why personally endorsed importance can sustain effort after intrinsic enjoyment declines. The episode also extends [[MotivationCrowdingOut]] and adds [[CommitmentDeliberationRubicon]], while keeping research examples, introspective signals, and the feasibility of major career change inside explicit evidential and material constraints.

## Key Claims
- [[SelfConcordantGoalSelection]] distinguishes goals that express a person's values and inclinations from goals adopted mainly through status, consumer culture, family expectation, or a fixed image of who one should be.
- Law students in an intensely competitive environment reportedly showed declining well-being and increasing depression, while some idealistic motives shifted toward grades, appearance, and prestige.
- A study of practicing lawyers is described as finding only a small association between income and happiness, with enjoyment, belief in the work, and perceived contribution showing larger associations.
- [[MotivationCrowdingOut]] can occur when payment, surveillance, public pressure, or loss threats make an enjoyable activity feel controlled; puzzle experiments and the later engagement of scholarship versus walk-on athletes are used as examples.
- Self-discovery is presented as a creative cycle: pose an important question, allow non-conscious incubation, notice a possible answer, and test it rather than treating intuition as revelation.
- [[CommitmentDeliberationRubicon]] describes the shift from weighing a goal to protecting and implementing it; commitment supports action but can also filter later evidence in favor of the chosen path.
- [[IdentifiedMotivationPersistence]] sustains difficult action because it remains important and value-expressive even when the activity is no longer enjoyable.
