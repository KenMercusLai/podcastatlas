# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》268｜河间献王刘德，如何与《诗经》结缘？（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens with 《关雎》 and credits [[LiuDeHejianKingWesternHan|河间献王刘德]] with helping preserve classical books through which later readers encounter the [[Shijing|《诗经》]]. It then presents Liu De as a learned and morally exceptional son of [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] who offered court ritual music, answered [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] on ritual institutions, received formal praise without practical adoption of his music, and was posthumously named “献”; the episode does not yet establish the specific textual-transmission mechanism or his cause of death.

## Key Claims

- The episode uses 《关雎》, the opening poem of the Shijing, to join literary appreciation to the preservation of ancient texts.
- It credits Liu De with a major role in protecting classical books, but does not identify manuscripts, collectors, editorial actions, or a specific Shijing lineage in the supplied installment.
- Liu De is introduced as Han Jingdi's second son and as an unusually studious, principled prince who did not use royal status to seek fame or profit.
- In the fifth year of Yuanguang, dated by the episode to 130 BCE, Liu De presented *yayue* ritual music and answered Han Wudi's questions about the Three Yong institutions, regulations, and Confucian principles.
- Han Wudi reportedly praised the answers and ordered court musicians to rehearse Liu De's music, but the host says it was not actually performed in the important ceremonies described.
- After Liu De's death in the following first lunar month, officials praised his disciplined, humane, modest, perceptive, and charitable conduct, and the title “献王” was proposed under the gloss “聪明睿智曰献.”
- Through [[BanGuEasternHan|班固]]'s judgment, the episode contrasts Liu De with privileged princes whose insulated upbringing and indulgence left rank unsupported by virtue.

## Key Quotes

> “关关雎鸠，在河之洲。” - the opening Shijing line used to introduce Liu De's cultural-preservation significance.

> “聪明睿智曰献。” - the posthumous-name formula reported for Liu De.

- [[Shijing|《诗经》]] - classic introduced through 《关雎》 and linked broadly, but not yet textually or institutionally, to Liu De's preservation work.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - Liu De's father and the emperor who questioned and formally praised him at court.
- [[PosthumousNamePolitics|谥号政治]] - framework through which the favorable character “献” condenses the official judgment of Liu De.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was adopted. Part 1 supplies the Shijing and classical-preservation connection that part 2's supplied text explicitly lacked.
- Part 1 stops at Liu De's death and favorable official assessment, whereas part 2 supplies a source-attributed suspicion-and-withdrawal explanation; the two notes are complementary, but neither establishes the true cause of death.
- The exact preservation work behind Liu De's relation to the Shijing, the textual lineage involved, the court use and technical nature of his ritual music, the meaning of the Three Yong institutions, and the date and wording of the court exchange require primary-text comparison.
- The medicinal properties attributed to 荇菜, the claim that *yayue* was simply music reserved for Han nobles, and the broad moral contrast between Liu De and other vassal kings remain episode-attributed.
