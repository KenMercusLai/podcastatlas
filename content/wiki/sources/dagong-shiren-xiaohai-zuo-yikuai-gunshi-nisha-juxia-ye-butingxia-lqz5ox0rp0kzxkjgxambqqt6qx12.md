# “打工诗人”小海：做一块滚石，泥沙俱下，也不停下

## Summary
This [[BuHeShiYi|不合时宜]] conversation has [[WangQingBuHeShiYi|王磬]] interview poet [[XiaoHaiPoet|小海]] and [[TonyXiaoHaiTranslator|Tony]] about the memoir [[YiKuaiGunShiBook|《一块滚石》 / A Drift in the South]], its expansion from 《深圳南流记》, and its Chinese and English publication. It connects factory labor, poetry, rock music, migration, translation, and reader response while resisting a reading of Xiaohai as only a suffering worker.

The episode's main contribution is a paired argument. [[WorkerArtistIdentityReduction|worker-artist identity reduction]] explains how a useful publishing label can flatten an artist into sociological evidence, while [[CreativeSelfPreservationUnderRepetitiveLabor|creative self-preservation under repetitive labor]] explains how reading, writing, music, performance, friendship, and modest control over time can preserve perception and agency without abolishing economic constraint.

## Key Claims
- [[XiaoHaiPoet|小海]] began writing during factory night shifts and later used Tang poetry, Hai Zi, rock music, Allen Ginsberg, and Bob Dylan to give loneliness, repetition, and worker life an artistic form.
- The episode rejects a hardship-only reading: factory pain and insecurity coexist with curiosity, first-wage pleasure, friendship, humor, artistic ambition, and a desire for sunlight, sunsets, and stars.
- [[WorkerArtistIdentityReduction]] occurs when “migrant worker poet” becomes not only context but the principal test of literary value, reducing a changing person and artist to a representative social type.
- [[TonyXiaoHaiTranslator|Tony]] describes translation as empirical work: unfamiliar factory terms required direct checking, and Chinese and English editorial processes produced different chapter structures and emphases.
- The English edition's iPhone-related chapter is intended to connect foreign readers' consumption to global production rather than leave Chinese factory labor as a distant spectacle.
- [[CreativeSelfPreservationUnderRepetitiveLabor]] does not mean art removes material pressure; Xiaohai continues to work, faces money and family expectations, and treats freedom as partial control over attention, time, movement, and expression.
- International publication and reader encounters show literature creating real cross-border relationships, but reception still divides between social-history evidence and an artist-development narrative.
