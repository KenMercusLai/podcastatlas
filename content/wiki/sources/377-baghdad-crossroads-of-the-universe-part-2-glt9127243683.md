# 377. Baghdad: Crossroads of the Universe (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[AbbasidRevolution]] from religious and political ferment in Khorasan through [[AbuMuslim]]'s mobilization, the destruction of Umayyad power, and the consolidation of the [[AbbasidCaliphate]]. It presents the movement as a coalition built through sacred lineage, black-banner symbolism, Persian memory, and promises of justice, then emphasizes the violence and disappointed expectations that accompanied Abbasid rule.

The second half centers on [[AlMansur]]'s foundation of [[Baghdad]]. The capital's location, [[RoundCityOfBaghdad|circular plan]], commercial access, Persian architectural inheritance, and proximity to Babylon, Seleucia, and Ctesiphon made it a practical seat of power and a claim to universal rule. The episode treats this designed center as the precondition for Baghdad's later growth into a plural, commercially connected, and intellectually powerful metropolis.

## Key Claims

- Late Umayyad weakness combined regional, sectarian, legal, and dynastic discontent, but the decisive Abbasid rising began in Khorasan rather than Iraq.
- [[AbuMuslim]] turned an ambiguous personal identity, Abbasid family claims, black banners, and Khorasan's mixed Islamic and Zoroastrian political expectations into a broad revolutionary coalition.
- Abbasid victory joined sacred legitimacy to military force and systematic violence against Umayyad people, tombs, and dynastic memory.
- The revolution's promises of justice and Quranic order were quickly qualified by continuity in coercive imperial rule under as-Saffah and [[AlMansur]].
- Al-Mansur chose a new Mesopotamian capital to escape Damascus's Umayyad associations, Kufa's factional politics, and Harran's links to Marwan and local paganism while remaining close to the dynasty's eastern base.
- [[RoundCityOfBaghdad|The Round City]] made hierarchy visible through walls, gates, radial roads, a central mosque, and the Palace of the Golden Gate, while Karkh's markets and Khorasani settlement connected the planned core to commerce and military support.
- Baghdad's Persian name, designers, circular precedents, architectural references, and location near older imperial cities marked an Abbasid turn toward Persian political culture without reducing the city to a simple Sasanian restoration.

## Key Quotes

> "crossroads of the universe" - the episode's description of al-Mansur's commercial and imperial ambition for Baghdad.

> "City of Peace" - the official name Madinat al-Salam, contrasted with the enduring Persian place-name Baghdad.
