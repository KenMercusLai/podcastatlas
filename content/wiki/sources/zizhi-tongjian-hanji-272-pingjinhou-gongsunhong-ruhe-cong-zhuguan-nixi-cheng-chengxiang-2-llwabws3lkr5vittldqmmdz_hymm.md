# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》272｜平津侯公孙弘，如何从猪倌逆袭成丞相（2）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-272-pingjinhou-gongsunhong-ruhe-cong-zhuguan-nixi-cheng-chengxiang-1-ltyeedn6elsv2ekulfgx096mxhug|Hanji 272 part 1]] by following [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] through a second recruitment and rapid rise under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. [[YuanGusheng|袁固生]] supplies the moral counterpoint: he warns Gongsun to keep inner judgment and outward speech aligned rather than bend learning toward worldly approval, while the host reads Gongsun's later caution as a response to dismissal, a rejected frontier report, and diminished confidence.

The episode's durable contribution is a ruler-facing form of [[AdviceFramingUnderHierarchy|等级场景中的进言包装]]. Gongsun reportedly lays out facts, reasons, and options, avoids direct court confrontation, and leaves the final choice visibly with Wudi. That method can preserve substantive input and the ruler's decision ownership at the same time, but the source also makes its moral cost explicit: hierarchy-aware presentation can shade into [[OpportunisticCourtAlignment|权势导向的机会主义站队]] when advisers change conviction rather than delivery.

## Key Claims

- Yuan Gusheng is presented as an elderly, upright Confucian recruited alongside Gongsun Hong; he warns Gongsun against separating outward conduct from inner conviction or distorting learning to gain worldly approval.
- The episode says flattering Confucian scholars attack Yuan as too old, after which he resigns and returns home; it uses this outcome as a contrast between principled candor and court adaptation.
- The host explains Gongsun's change through two setbacks: dismissal from a minor early post and Wudi's rejection of his report against southwest expansion. The first dismissal cause and the psychological chain are explicitly inferential.
- A new empire-wide recruitment in 130 BCE reportedly brings a reluctant, self-doubting Gongsun back to court, where Wudi again ranks him first and appoints him as boshi.
- Gongsun's advice method is to state the evidence and available choices without confronting Wudi publicly, preserving imperial decision ownership while still introducing policy considerations.
- Wudi reportedly values Gongsun's caution, argument, legal familiarity, administrative capacity, and ability to use classical learning, leading to promotion through Left Metropolitan Superintendent and Yushi Dafu to chancellor and Marquis of Pingjin.
- The host treats political maturity and moral compromise as inseparable in this ascent: experience improves Gongsun's effectiveness, while alleged flattery, concealed disagreement, and abandonment of scholarly independence remain contested character judgments.

## Key Quotes

> “务正学以言，无曲学以阿世。” - the episode's rendering of Yuan Gusheng's warning against bending learning toward worldly approval.

> “习文法吏事，而又缘饰以儒术。” - the source's compact account of why Gongsun Hong's legal-administrative competence was useful to Wudi.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source continues part 1's late-entry account and provides an earlier statement of the advice style later episodes illustrate through concrete debates and criticism.
- Chronology caution: the episode places the renewed call in 130 BCE, says Gongsun is already around seventy, describes a ten-year gap, and then compresses promotion, maternal mourning, return, Yushi Dafu service, chancellorship, and enfeoffment. Exact dates, ages, intervals, and office order remain source-scoped pending primary-text comparison.
- Yuan Gusheng's reported age of ninety, prior service as tutor to Qinghe king Liu Cheng, first-meeting speech, attacks by other scholars, and reason for resignation are episode-attributed rather than a complete independent biography.
- The cause of Gongsun's first dismissal, his loss of confidence, reluctance to answer the second call, and the claim that two setbacks caused a personality transformation are host reconstructions rather than demonstrated access to his inner state.
- “曲学阿世,” flattery, concealed disagreement, selling out associates, and abandonment of scholarly independence are moral interpretations. Listing facts and options can preserve honest counsel or conceal opportunism; the observable form alone does not settle which occurred.
- The episode invokes the *Shiji* for Gongsun's nonconfrontational court style, but this source note does not independently collate the quotation, full debate record, or causal link from speaking style to promotion.
