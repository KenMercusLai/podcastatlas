# 當新聞成為賭盤，記者可以大賺一筆嗎？

## Summary
This [[DuanwenNewsPodcast|端闻]] episode follows [[XiaoweiDuanwenReporter|小薇]] through a one-month [[PolySimulator]] experiment built on [[Polymarket]] live prices, asking whether a reporter's news habits can become tradable edge. The experiment ends with a simulated loss of 70.52 dollars and turns into a critique of [[PredictionMarketSettlementPower]], [[NewsAsTradingPosition]], and [[PredictionMarketGenderedAttention]]: event markets can express public expectations quickly, but they also compress complex realities into binary contracts and redirect attention from people and public meaning toward position risk.

## Key Claims
- [[Polymarket]] prices can behave like live probability signals, but a correct news judgment does not guarantee profit when the market has already priced most of the move.
- [[XiaoweiDuanwenReporter|小薇]]'s Hormuz trade shows the gap between news knowledge and trading edge: she understood the [[StraitOfHormuz]] event but entered after market confidence had shifted.
- Platform settlement wording can diverge from news reality: ceasefire declarations, passage rules, clothing definitions, and invasion thresholds may be harder than a Yes/No contract implies.
- Prediction-market advocates treat money-backed prices as potentially faster or less socially pressured than polls, but the episode keeps failed Polymarket forecasts beside the 2024 Trump-election example.
- The episode argues that Polymarket's user culture and market design can financialize gendered attention by turning female celebrities' pregnancy, clothing, relationships, and bodies into tradable events.
- The simulated trading month changed Xiaowei's first reaction to [[Reuters]] and [[Bloomberg]] alerts from news concern toward fear of portfolio loss.

## Key Quotes
> "现实世界不是简单的黑白" - the episode's closing contrast between journalism and binary markets.

> "第一反应变成担心交易亏损" - Xiaowei's description of how market exposure changed her news attention.
