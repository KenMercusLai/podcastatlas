# 562. Peter the Great: The Rise of Russia (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]] from birth through the [[MoscowUprising1682|1682 Moscow uprising]], joint coronation, and [[SophiaAlekseyevna|Sophia Alekseyevna]]'s regency to his effective seizure of power in 1689. It places his childhood trauma and practical curiosity inside a Muscovite state that was territorially vast but strategically constrained, then connects soldiers, ships, foreign officers, and technical craft to the ruler developed in the later Peter and Great Northern War episodes. [[VasilyGolitsyn|Vasily Golitsyn]] also complicates a simple Peter-versus-backwardness story by showing Westernizing reform within Sophia's government.

## Key Claims

- Late seventeenth-century Muscovy combined continental scale and sacral monarchy with weak maritime access, limited formal education and print culture, and strategic pressure from Sweden, Poland-Lithuania, and the Ottoman world.
- The 1682 succession dispute between the Miloslavsky and Naryshkin family networks turned real [[Streltsy]] grievances into armed palace intervention after rumors that Ivan had been killed.
- Ten-year-old Peter witnessed the killing of Artamon Matveyev, relatives, and ministers in and around the Kremlin; the episode treats this as a formative but not independently proven cause of his later hatred of the Streltsy and old Muscovy.
- Sophia converted the uprising into a joint monarchy under Ivan and Peter with herself as regent, overcoming the normal confinement of royal women while relying on court, military, and dynastic alliances.
- Sophia and Golitsyn were Westernizing rulers in their own right; Golitsyn pursued diplomatic, religious, military, and social reforms before failed Crimean campaigns weakened the regime.
- Away from the Kremlin, Peter developed mock regiments, practical craft, geometry, geography, and sailing, and insisted on learning from lower ranks rather than treating technical work as beneath a tsar.
- The 1689 confrontation ended when foreign officers and then the Streltsy aligned with Peter, Shaklovity was executed, Golitsyn was exiled, and Sophia was confined to a convent.

## Key Quotes

> "Grandfather of the Russian Navy" - the later name given to the English boat that helped turn Peter's practical curiosity toward sailing.

> "Third Rome" - the Muscovite claim to Orthodox and imperial succession after Constantinople's fall.
