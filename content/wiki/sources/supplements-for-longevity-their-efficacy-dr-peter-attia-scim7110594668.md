# Supplements for Longevity & Their Efficacy | Dr. Peter Attia

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] conversation has [[AndrewHuberman]] and [[PeterAttia]] evaluate intravenous NAD, NR, NMN, sirtuins, resveratrol, rapamycin, and related longevity claims. Its central conclusion is that NAD biology and age-related change do not establish that raising NAD extends human healthspan or lifespan; [[MechanismOutcomeEvidenceHierarchy]] requires tissue-relevant outcomes, controlled human evidence, and ideally survival or meaningful healthspan results. The episode uses the [[InterventionsTestingProgram]] and failed resveratrol and NR lifespan results to strengthen [[NADTherapyEvidenceBoundary]], while continuing to rank exercise, sleep, nutrition, and emotional health above speculative supplementation.

## Key Claims
- [[NADTherapyEvidenceBoundary]] separates NAD's essential metabolic and sirtuin-related roles from proof that intravenous NAD, oral NR, or oral or sublingual NMN improves human healthspan or lifespan.
- Age-related NAD decline varies by tissue, and one blood finding described rising NADH alongside falling NAD, making redox balance and mitochondrial function more specific hypotheses than a simple total-NAD deficiency story.
- [[InterventionsTestingProgram]] results are treated as a stronger longevity test than mechanism alone: the episode reports no lifespan or healthspan benefit from NR, while rapamycin, canagliflozin, acarbose, and 17-alpha estradiol produced positive results in mice.
- Resveratrol illustrates how a proposed sirtuin mechanism and an unusual high-fat-diet mouse result can attract broad claims that later lifespan testing does not support.
- Human NR and NMN studies discussed in the episode show null primary outcomes, subgroup-sensitive findings, or statistically significant but clinically small biomarker changes rather than demonstrated longevity benefit.
- Raising NAD in blood or liver does not establish a meaningful effect in skeletal muscle or another target tissue, and biomarker or biological-age movement does not automatically establish clinical benefit.
- A possible reduction in basal- and squamous-cell skin cancers is treated as a signal worth replication, not proof of general cancer prevention, melanoma prevention, or lifespan extension.
- [[SustainableHealthOptimization]] remains foundations-first: exercise, sleep, nutrition, emotional health, muscle, strength, and power are presented as more consequential than the NAD products discussed.

## Key Quotes
> "no" — both speakers' final answer to whether people should focus on NAD supplementation or infusions to extend life.

## Contradictions
- The episode materially qualifies the favorable NAD patient and self-reports in [[peptide-hormone-therapies-for-health-performance-longevity-dr-craig-koniver-scim6125190274]]: those observations may generate hypotheses, but negative NR lifespan testing and weak human outcome evidence leave longevity efficacy unestablished.
- Huberman reports energy and hair or nail changes after NMN and difficult symptoms during NAD infusion, but these uncontrolled observations do not establish efficacy, mechanism, or general tolerability.
- The skin-cancer signal, tissue NAD declines, NR/NMN pharmacology, human subgroup findings, biological-age-test criticism, radiation estimates, supplement regimens, and personal medication use remain source-scoped public medical discussion rather than individualized advice.
