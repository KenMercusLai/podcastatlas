# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》228｜从富可敌国到饿死街头，邓通经历了什么？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode gives the wiki's fullest current account of [[DengTong|邓通]]: a palace boat official becomes [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s favorite after matching a dream image, receives office, enormous gifts, a copper mountain, and minting privileges, but remains without an independent political base. Chancellor [[ShentuJiaWesternHan|申屠嘉]] can discipline him for disrespectful court conduct, yet only imperial intervention keeps that disciplinary encounter from becoming fatal.

The succession turn exposes the deeper dependence. After Deng Tong's intimate care of the ill emperor reportedly creates resentment in Crown Prince Liu Qi, [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] removes him from office, investigates illicit minting beyond the authorized area, confiscates his property, and leaves him in debt. [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]] can provide clothing and food but cannot restore his wealth or position, and the episode says he dies poor and hungry.

## Key Claims

- Deng Tong begins as a 黄头郎 associated with palace boats; Liu Heng notices him after a dream about a similarly dressed boatman helping the emperor ascend and after hearing “邓通” as a favorable echo of “登天.”
- The episode describes Deng Tong as cautious, socially withdrawn, and skilled at pleasing the emperor, but without administrative achievement or a record of recommending talent.
- Liu Heng's gifts, visits, promotion, copper-mountain grant, and authorization to mint “邓通钱” convert personal favor into office and extraordinary private wealth.
- Shentu Jia summons Deng Tong for arrogant behavior at court, threatens a capital penalty, and forces a bloody kowtow before Liu Heng sends an emissary to retrieve him.
- The episode attributes Crown Prince Liu Qi's hostility to an “吸痈” episode in which the prince reluctantly tends Liu Heng's abscess and learns that Deng Tong routinely did so.
- After succeeding as Jingdi, Liu Qi removes Deng Tong, investigates minting outside the authorized area, confiscates all property, and leaves him indebted.
- Liu Piao's later gifts are seized by officials when they are property, while clothing and food keep Deng Tong alive only temporarily; the source reports that he ultimately dies in poverty and hunger.

## Key Quotes

> “意愿谨，不好外交。” — the source's rendering of [[SimaQian|司马迁]] on Deng Tong's cautious, non-networking disposition.

> “力田不如逢年，善仕不如遇合。” — the episode's frame for fortune produced by favorable timing and ruler affinity rather than durable merit.

## Connections

- [[DengTong|邓通]] - central favorite whose rise, disciplinary exposure, succession reversal, confiscation, and death form the episode's arc.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - patron whose dream interpretation, gifts, visits, minting grant, and rescue create and protect Deng Tong's position.
- [[ShentuJiaWesternHan|申屠嘉]] - chancellor who asserts court discipline against a favorite but releases him after imperial intervention.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - successor whose earlier resentment and later investigation end Deng Tong's office and wealth.
- [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]] - princess whose limited relief cannot reverse official confiscation or restore political protection.
- [[PalaceFavoriteProtectionFragility|宫廷宠幸保护脆弱性]] - explains why enormous wealth and office remain reversible when both depend on one ruler's attachment.
- [[CourtIntimacyAsPoliticalResource|宫廷亲密关系作为政治资源]] - captures the conversion of bodily care and proximity into access, gifts, office, and privilege.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The fall and starvation account agrees with the earlier physiognomy-centered and male-favorite source notes, while this episode adds the dream, Shentu Jia, abscess, investigation, and relief sequence.
- Name normalization: the transcript's repeated “申图家” is routed to canonical [[ShentuJiaWesternHan|申屠嘉]].
- Motive boundary: the “吸痈” incident supplies the episode's explanation for Liu Qi's resentment, but it does not by itself establish that personal revenge was the sole cause of the later investigation and confiscation.
- Sexuality boundary: the episode places Deng Tong in a tradition of male favorites, but favor, physical service, gifts, and intimacy do not independently establish a modern sexual-orientation category.
- Source-scope caution: the dream, wordplay, offices, reward totals, court demeanor, summons procedure, threatened penalty, copper-mountain location, minting reach, abscess care, investigative charge, debts, gift seizures, final household, and judgments about talent or “德不配位” require comparison with transmitted records.
