# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》241｜梁孝王刘武：看我如何花样作死

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode fills the murder-case gap left by Hanji 240. After [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] and other ministers oppose a costly road requested by [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]], the episode says Liu Wu's favored advisers 羊胜 and 公孙诡 arrange the killing of Yuan and more than ten other opponents. Investigation reaches Liang, the suspects hide in the royal palace, and [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] persuades Liu Wu to surrender them by arguing that neither princely status nor [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]]'s protection can permanently defeat law and sovereign authority.

The settlement does not establish Liu Wu's innocence. [[ZouYangWesternHan|邹阳]] uses the interests of 王信 and the story of Shun and Xiang to lower [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s anger, while [[TianShuHan|田叔]] burns the Liang testimony and records after reporting that the king deserves death. Destroying the case file lets the court tell the dowager that guilty advisers have already died, but Liu Wu's theatrical submission restores only public form: Jingdi no longer invites him to share the imperial carriage. The episode therefore adds [[EvidenceSuppressionDynasticCompromise|证据切断式宗室妥协]] to the existing tension between [[RuleOverKinshipPower|法度压过亲缘特权]] and [[KinshipProtocolBoundary|亲情名分边界]].

## Key Claims

- Liu Wu requests a direct road from Suiyang toward Changle Palace; Yuan Ang and other ministers oppose it, and the episode makes that opposition the immediate source of his grievance.
- 羊胜 and 公孙诡 organize repeated assassination attempts against Yuan Ang and other opposing ministers; Yuan is killed outside the Anling gate after one reluctant assassin first warns him.
- Jingdi's investigation traces the attackers to Liang, where the two principal suspects hide inside Liu Wu's palace.
- Han Anguo cites Liu Rong's fate and the limits of dowager protection to persuade Liu Wu to make the suspects kill themselves and surrender their bodies.
- Zou Yang induces 王信 to intercede by showing that an execution of Liu Wu could also endanger the Wang family, then uses the Shun-Xiang story to reframe the conflict through sibling affection.
- Tian Shu tells Jingdi that Liu Wu is guilty of a capital offense but destroys the testimony and records so the emperor need not choose openly between law and his mother's survival-level grief.
- Liu Wu's disguised arrival, axe-bearing submission, and reunion restore visible family relations, but Jingdi's refusal to share a carriage marks lasting loss of political trust.

## Key Quotes

> “不伏诛，汉法安在？” - Tian Shu's legal side of the dilemma as rendered by the episode.

> “太后食不甘味，卧不安席。” - the family consequence used to argue against completing the prosecution.

- [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] - minister whose earlier succession opposition is followed here by road-policy opposition and assassination.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] and [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] - emperor and senior dowager caught between enforceable law, dynastic order, and family attachment.
- [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] - Liang official who converts a stalled search into surrender by making the prince confront the limit of kinship protection.
- [[TianShuHan|田叔]] - investigator who acknowledges capital guilt while deliberately eliminating the record needed to carry the prosecution forward.
- [[ZouYangWesternHan|邹阳]] - Liang advocate who builds an interest-based intercession channel and then supplies a sibling-affection analogy.

## Contradictions

- This source resolves Hanji 240's explicit evidence gap: the earlier episode announces that Yuan Ang's assassination is linked to Liang but does not narrate the plot; Hanji 241 supplies the alleged grievance, advisers, attackers, investigation, palace concealment, and political settlement.
- The result qualifies rather than flatly contradicts Hanji 266's favorable account of Han Anguo's earlier mediation. Han again prevents escalation, but here he does so by forcing Liu Wu to abandon protected subordinates after a lethal breach, not by merely reframing ceremonial excess.
- Liu Wu's legal culpability remains source-scoped. The episode says the advisers act after hearing his hatred and that he hides them, while Tian Shu calls the king capital-guilty; it does not provide a complete surviving order, evidentiary record, statute, or formal judgment because the narrative itself says the records were burned.
- The road's route and effects, number and identities of intended victims and attackers, exact sequence of Yuan's warning and death, search procedure, speeches, private motives, Wang Xin's intervention, Shun-Xiang analogy, destruction of records, submission choreography, appointment of Tian Shu, and carriage practice require comparison with transmitted records.
