# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》321｜皇帝跟商人抢饭碗，你见过吗？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode bridges [[WhiteDeerSkinCurrency|白鹿皮币]] and the later [[RewardedDenunciationFiscalEnforcement|算缗、告缗 enforcement]] by following [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s search for war finance through [[WhiteMetalCoinageWesternHan|白金币]], currency enforcement, and salt-and-iron state operation. It presents salt and iron as unusually attractive targets because demand is broad, private profits are concentrated, and iron also supports weapons and political-military power.

The durable institutional contribution is the formation of [[StateCommercialMonopolyExtraction|官营商业垄断式汲取]]. The court recruits salt merchant [[DongguoXianyangWesternHan|东郭咸阳]] and iron merchant [[KongJinWesternHan|孔仅]] into the agricultural-finance administration, combines their operating knowledge with [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]]'s calculating ability, and separates salt procurement from iron production: households boil salt with official equipment for exclusive official purchase, while iron production and sale are placed under direct official control. The episode further interprets monopoly as both revenue collection and a way to cut the resource base that had once strengthened vassal kingdoms.

## Key Claims

- The court issues three silver-tin white-metal denominations with dragon, horse, and tortoise designs and very high assigned values; the episode treats their near-five-year life as evidence of a short-lived fiscal currency experiment.
- High nominal value encourages private counterfeiting, while the death penalty turns monetary enforcement into mass lethal punishment in the episode's account.
- Private salt boiling and iron production had created powerful merchants, making those industries both a fiscal target and a source of expertise the court could recruit.
- Dongguo Xianyang and Kong Jin are appointed to salt-and-iron posts because the existing agricultural-finance administration lacks operating knowledge; Sang Hongyang joins the fiscal team through numerical skill and imperial trust.
- Salt operation uses recruited private producers, official boiling vessels, exclusive official purchase, and monopoly sale, whereas iron production, toolmaking, and sale are described as more completely state-run through local iron offices.
- Control of salt and iron captures stable demand and strategic production while also limiting the independent revenues from which vassal rulers could fund soldiers and patronage.
- Punishment and confiscation protect the monopoly, and the closing preview moves from control of industry profit to direct assessment of merchant and lender wealth under算缗.

## Key Quotes

> “募民自给费，因官器作煮盐，官与牢盆。” - the episode's quoted institutional formula for privately performed salt production under official equipment and exclusive purchase.

> “敢私铸铁器煮盐者，釱左趾，没入其器物。” - the quoted punishment-and-confiscation rule protecting the monopoly.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The transcript's “孔杰” is normalized to the existing [[KongJinWesternHan|孔仅]] because the iron-merchant role, pairing with Dongguo Xianyang, and salt-and-iron context align; this remains a transcription correction rather than evidence for a separate person.
- The episode calls the white-metal issue a virtual currency that devalued ordinary people's silver, but it does not establish circulation volume, acceptance, metal content, seigniorage, price effects, or the exact reason for abolition.
- The attribution of the first salt-and-iron proposal to Zhang Tang, the offices and individual responsibilities of Dongguo Xianyang and Kong Jin, the precise division between private and official labor, and the claim that monopoly deliberately targeted vassal finance require primary-text and institutional comparison.
- The punishment gloss, tool weight, number executed, private-merchant fortunes, Seven States comparison, military-control effect, and the closing moral description of merchant and lender wealth remain source-scoped.
