# 398. JFK: The Mystery is Solved (Part 7)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode closes its Kennedy-assassination series by arguing that [[LeeHarveyOswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]] was the most plausible killer of [[JohnFKennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and that the broad lone-gunman conclusion of the [[WarrenCommission|Warren Commission]] fits the presented evidence better than a large conspiracy. It reconstructs Oswald's childhood, Marxism, Marine training, Soviet defection, return to Dallas, Cuba activism, attempted shooting of Edwin Walker, access to the Texas School Book Depository, flight, killing of J. D. Tippit, and false statements after arrest. The conclusion uses [[OccamsRazor|Occam's Razor]] and assassin-profile comparisons while treating the cultural power of the case as an example of [[ConspiracyTheoryPatternSeeking|conspiracy theory pattern seeking]].

## Key Claims

- The hosts argue that Oswald's biography, weapons training, prior violence, rifle purchase, workplace access, movements, flight, killing of Tippit, and interrogation lies form a cumulative case that he killed Kennedy.
- The motorcade route was publicly reported only shortly before the assassination, so the episode interprets Oswald's Book Depository job as an opportunity rather than evidence of a long-prepared placement conspiracy.
- Oswald's Marxism is presented as both political conviction and a source of dignity, explanation, and imagined historical importance after childhood isolation and repeated personal frustration.
- The episode treats the Walker shooting attempt and Tippit's killing as evidence that Oswald was already willing to use political or personal violence, not merely an innocent intelligence-world bystander.
- The hosts defend the feasibility of the shooting and single-bullet trajectory, while acknowledging that the episode selects and interprets evidence rather than conducting a complete adversarial review of every disputed detail.
- The CIA-style explanation is rejected as lacking a convincing motive in the hosts' account of Kennedy's politics and as requiring more unsupported moving parts than the Oswald explanation.
- Kennedy-assassination conspiracy culture is presented as emotionally satisfying because it offers secret knowledge, historical totalization, and a hidden cause proportionate to the shock of the victim's death.

## Key Quotes

> "No other explanation carries as much evidentiary weight." - the episode's stated basis for preferring Oswald as the killer.

> "Occam's razor" - the hosts' shorthand for preferring the Oswald explanation over a larger hidden conspiracy.
