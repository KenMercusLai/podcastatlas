# The Science of Making & Breaking Habits

## Summary
This solo [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] frame habits as learned, plastic behavior sequences whose strength depends on initiation cost, context independence, reward learning, and sleep-supported consolidation. Its main contributions are [[HabitAutomaticityAndTaskBracketing]], a model for building flexible routines through procedural rehearsal and state-matched placement, and [[PostHabitReplacement]], a proposed way to disrupt an unwanted sequence by performing an easy positive behavior immediately afterward. The episode also extends [[DailyCircadianPerformanceRoutine]] and [[RewardPredictionErrorLearning]] while keeping its exact phase timings, 21-day cycle, and neural mechanisms source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- [[HabitAutomaticityAndTaskBracketing]] treats habit strength as a combination of low initiation friction and the ability to perform across times and contexts, rather than repetition alone.
- Mentally rehearsing the concrete steps of a desired behavior is proposed to reduce the threshold for starting it, while basal-ganglia activity at the beginning and end of a routine is used to explain task bracketing.
- [[DailyCircadianPerformanceRoutine]] gains a habit-placement branch: higher-friction behaviors are placed in a more alert early waking phase, lower-friction behaviors later, and sleep supports consolidation.
- [[RewardPredictionErrorLearning]] is applied to the whole habit envelope—preparation, effort, execution, and aftermath—rather than only to an endpoint reward.
- The episode proposes 21 days of attempting four or five of six listed habits, followed by 21 days without new additions to test which behaviors have become more automatic; this is a source-specific experiment, not a universal formation threshold.
- [[PostHabitReplacement]] uses the period immediately after an unwanted behavior as an intervention point for a small positive action intended to alter the learned sequence.
- Ordinary unwanted habits are distinguished from addiction, which can require broader clinical and psychological care.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The cited 18-to-254-day variability argues against reading the episode's 21-day build-and-test system as a universal habit-formation deadline.
- The episode's exact daily phase windows, neurotransmitter descriptions, supplement suggestions, procedural-visualization mechanism, task-bracketing interpretation, and post-habit replacement mechanism are condensed public-neuroscience claims rather than established individualized protocols.
- Reminders and self-monitoring may still help in some settings even though the episode presents them as weak predictors of durable change when used alone.
- Addiction, compulsive behavior, psychiatric symptoms, disordered eating, and medically risky routines should not be managed as ordinary habits solely through timing, rewards, punishment, or replacement behaviors.
