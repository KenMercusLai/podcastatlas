# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》429｜“骄兵必败”的故事怎么来的？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 64 BCE [[CheshiKingdomWesternHan|车师国]] crisis under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]. After [[ZhengJiWesternHan|郑吉]] is surrounded while trying to preserve Han's屯田 position near Cheshi, [[WeiXiangWesternHan|魏相]] persuades Xuan not to turn a rescue problem into a broad western strike against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]].

The durable synthesis is that "骄兵必败" begins as a political and moral test of war purpose, not only a battlefield slogan. Wei Xiang's typology of righteous, responsive, angry, greedy, and proud armies becomes [[MilitaryJustificationTypology|用兵名义分型]], while the second half uses Bing Ji's rediscovered prison-era protection of Xuan to deepen [[YindeRewardCausality|阴德福报因果]] and the show's broader warning against displaying power or virtue for status.

## Key Claims

- The Cheshi crisis begins when Han's control and屯田 position at Cheshi threaten Xiongnu because fertile land and stored grain could become a forward base against it.
- Zheng Ji leaves three hundred soldiers at Cheshi, later brings seven or eight thousand Quli屯田 troops to rescue it, and is surrounded by a larger Xiongnu force.
- Xuan considers attacking Xiongnu's western side while Xiongnu is occupied at Cheshi, but Wei Xiang argues that the court should first clarify what kind of war it would be waging.
- Wei Xiang distinguishes 义兵, 应兵, 忿兵, 贪兵, and 骄兵, and treats proud force-display by a strong state as the path behind "骄兵必败."
- Wei Xiang's anti-expedition case combines moral classification with practical state capacity: Xiongnu had recently released Han captives, border people were poor, and internal disasters and official failures deserved attention.
- Xuan accepts the remonstrance, sends Chang Hui with Zhangye and Jiuquan cavalry to recover Zheng Ji, makes Junxiu king of Cheshi, moves Cheshi's people to Quli, and leaves the old Cheshi territory to Xiongnu.
- Wei Xiang is profiled as a historically literate chancellor who studies earlier policy advice and asks officials about local anomalies, rebellions, and disasters that may not be reported upward.
- Bing Ji's old prison-era service becomes visible only after the Ye Ting bondwoman Ze claims nursing merit, prompting Bing Ji to identify Hu Zu and Guo Zhengqing as more deserving caregivers and finally report the full story to Xuan.
- The episode connects Bing Ji's long silence to Laozi's "上德不德," "积阴德," and later Buddhist/folk moral frames about doing good without display.

## Key Quotes

> "骄兵者灭" - Wei Xiang's core warning as the episode presents the idiom origin.

> "上德不德，是以有德；下德不失德，是以无德。" - the Laozi frame used for Bing Ji's hidden service.

> "没功德" - the Bodhidharma reply in the Liang Wudi analogy about visible merit-seeking.

## Connections

- [[WeiXiangWesternHan|魏相]] - central remonstrator whose war typology and state-capacity reasoning prevent a broader Xiongnu expedition.
- [[MilitaryJustificationTypology|用兵名义分型]] - the episode's core conceptual contribution: war must be classified by purpose before being judged.
- [[CheshiKingdomWesternHan|车师国]], [[ZhengJiWesternHan|郑吉]], [[QuliWesternRegions|渠犁]], and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - frontier crisis chain that turns Cheshi屯田 into a Han-Xiongnu pressure point.
- [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] - ruler who considers opportunistic attack but accepts Wei Xiang's restraint and chooses extraction plus settlement.
- [[BingJiWesternHan|丙吉]], Hu Zu, Guo Zhengqing, and Ze - hidden-service branch explaining how Xuan learns the details of his prison survival.
- [[YindeRewardCausality|阴德福报因果]] and [[VirtuePracticeInternalization|修养内化]] - moral-cultivation concepts extended by the episode's warning against self-advertised good deeds.
- [[Laozi|老子]], 梁武帝, 达摩, and 《金刚经》 - later interpretive frames the host uses to explain non-display virtue.
