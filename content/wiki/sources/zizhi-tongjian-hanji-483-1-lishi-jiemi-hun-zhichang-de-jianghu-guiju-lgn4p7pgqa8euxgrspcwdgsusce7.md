# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》483-1丨历史揭秘：混职场的“江湖规矩”

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses the Jianghu image from wuxia to read Yongguang 3-4 BCE court politics under [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]. It links disasters, fiscal strain, restored monopoly finance, reduced doctoral-student quotas, and shrine retrenchment with the continuing struggle between [[ZhouKanWesternHan|周堪]], [[ZhangMengWesternHan|张萌]], and [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]'s group. The durable synthesis is that court corruption can operate through moral language, cultural prestige, and information channels as much as through money: Zhou Kan and Zhang Meng return to office, but Shi Xian's control of access to Yuan keeps them politically exposed.

## Key Claims

- The episode frames the Western Han court as a Jianghu: wherever people, interest, resentment, and status meet, informal struggle appears beneath formal office.
- Yongguang-era disasters and fiscal pressure push the court toward retrenchment: salt-and-iron monopoly is restored, doctoral-student numbers are fixed at one thousand, and ancestral-shrine expense is later cut.
- Earlier blame against Zhou Kan and Zhang Meng becomes harder to sustain when worse eclipses, earthquakes, and disasters occur after their demotion.
- Shi Xian's party cannot defend the earlier omen-blame logic before Yuan, but Zhou Kan and Zhang Meng's return still lacks real power because the court channel remains hostile.
- Zhou Kan's nominal role handling memorials is hollowed out because Shi Xian supervises the channel and his allies mediate what can reach Yuan.
- The host treats this as a workplace information-war lesson: ability and title matter less when opponents control communication with the decision maker.
- "话语霸权式腐败" names a subtler corruption pattern in which moral, cultural, or scholarly authority can manipulate organizational judgment while appearing righteous.

## Key Quotes

> "有人的地方就有江湖" - opening frame for court and workplace struggle.

> "当面夸你容易，背后不毁谤你很难" - the episode's social-reputation warning after Zhou Kan and Zhang Meng's defeat.
