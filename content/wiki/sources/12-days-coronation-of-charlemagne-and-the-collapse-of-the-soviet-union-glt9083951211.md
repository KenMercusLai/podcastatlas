# 12 Days: Coronation of Charlemagne and the collapse of the Soviet Union

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] Christmas Day episode pairs [[Charlemagne]]'s coronation in Rome on 25 December 800 with [[MikhailGorbachev|Mikhail Gorbachev]]'s resignation on 25 December 1991. The first half explains how papal insecurity, Frankish military power, Carolingian legitimacy needs, [[IreneOfAthens|Irene of Athens]]'s rule in Constantinople, and Roman sacred geography combined in a new western imperial claim.

The second half compresses the [[SovietUnion|Soviet Union]]'s endgame into the failed August coup, [[BorisYeltsin|Boris Yeltsin]]'s rise, Ukraine's independence vote, republican agreement, Gorbachev's televised resignation, and the lowering of the Soviet flag. The pairing treats empire-making and state dissolution as institutional processes whose decisive ceremony became visible only after authority had already shifted.

## Key Claims

- The eighth-century papacy turned toward the Carolingians because Byzantine protection in Italy weakened while Lombard pressure increased, and the Frankish dynasty gained sacred legitimacy in return.
- After an attack by Roman opponents, [[PopeLeoIII|Pope Leo III]] sought Charlemagne's protection; Charlemagne later restored him and presided over a settlement that strengthened both men.
- Charlemagne's conquest, territorial scale, Frankish-papal alliance, Rome's prestige, and the contested status of Irene's rule were used together to justify a revived western imperial title.
- The Christmas Day coronation made papal ritual central to [[RomanImperialLegitimacy]], although Einhard's claim that Charlemagne was surprised is treated skeptically by the hosts.
- Gorbachev's simultaneous political and economic reforms weakened old controls without producing a stable replacement, while worsening material conditions reduced his domestic authority.
- The failed August 1991 coup left Gorbachev formally restored but enabled Yeltsin to ban the Russian Communist Party, dominate the center, and embody an alternative Russian sovereignty.
- Ukraine's independence referendum and the agreement among Russian, Ukrainian, and Belarusian leaders removed the institutional basis of the union presidency before Gorbachev formally resigned.
- The post-Soviet economic shock and loss of status shaped later Russian support for order, [[VladimirPutin|Putin]]'s collapse narrative, and Chinese Communist warnings against combining political and economic opening.

## Key Quotes

> "would not have entered the church" - Einhard's later claim about Charlemagne's supposed ignorance of the planned coronation, which the hosts doubt.

> "geopolitical tragedy" - the episode's compressed rendering of Putin's later description of the Soviet collapse.

## Connections

- [[Charlemagne]], [[PopeLeoIII]], and [[IreneOfAthens]] - ruler, pope, and eastern empress whose positions shaped the western coronation.
- [[HolyRomanEmpire]] and [[RomanImperialLegitimacy]] - later imperial order and the contested grounds of its Roman claim.
- [[MikhailGorbachev]], [[BorisYeltsin]], and [[SovietUnion]] - final union president, Russian rival, and dissolving state in the 1991 branch.
- [[DualSovereigntyStateDissolution]] - mechanism through which Russian and union authority became incompatible.
- [[FailedCoupAuthorityTransfer]] - process by which the August coup accelerated Yeltsin's rise and Gorbachev's eclipse.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The Soviet half broadly corroborates [[160-the-fall-of-the-soviet-union-glt9472343994]] but gives greater attention to the symbolism of Gorbachev's broadcast, signature, and the Kremlin flag change.
- Calling the coronation the dawn of the [[HolyRomanEmpire]] is useful as an origin claim but does not erase later fragmentation, the break before Otto I, or continuing Byzantine Roman identity.
- Charlemagne's prior knowledge, the timing and political use of the Jerusalem delegation, Leo's reported mutilation and recovery, leader motives, post-Soviet economic causation, and the influence of the collapse on later Russian and Chinese policy remain episode-attributed or source-scoped.
