# Show 68 - BLITZ Human Resources

## Summary

This [[HardcoreHistory]] episode presents the [[AtlanticSlaveTradeSystem|Atlantic slave trade]] as a commercial, maritime, imperial, and coercive system rather than a sequence of isolated atrocities. It follows captives from African warfare and inland transport through the Middle Passage, sale, family separation, plantation surveillance, and punishment, while arguing that European demand, state-backed companies, shipping, insurance, commodity profits, and colonial labor shortages enlarged older forms of enslavement into an Atlantic machine.

The later sections connect Enlightenment rights language, abolitionist mobilization, and the [[HaitianRevolution]] to the system's political breakdown. [[SaintDomingue]] shows how extreme wealth, hereditary racial hierarchy, free people of color, maroon resistance, revolutionary equality, and mass slave revolt could converge; Haitian independence destroyed colonial rule but also culminated in retaliatory massacre. Formal abolition therefore remains distinct from the longer [[RacializedSlaveryAfterlife|afterlife of racial hierarchy]], segregation, and constrained freedom.

## Key Claims

- Atlantic slavery expanded through the combination of older captive markets with oceanic transport, investors, chartered companies, insurance, naval power, and state support.
- Disease-driven Indigenous population collapse and continued demand for sugar, tobacco, rice, and later cotton intensified colonial demand for coerced African labor.
- African rulers and intermediaries participated in capture and sale, but that participation does not erase the demand, scale, finance, and transport system built by European buyers and empires.
- The captive journey began before the ocean crossing and continued through forced marches, confinement, shipboard crowding and sexual violence, cosmetic preparation for sale, and repeated family separation.
- [[PlantationSlaveryControlRegime|Plantation control]] joined patrols, passes, curfews, surveillance, exemplary punishment, and fear of revolt, corrupting slaveholding society as well as brutalizing the enslaved.
- Enlightenment universalism, print, petitioning, religious organization, and testimony from people such as [[FrederickDouglass|Frederick Douglass]] helped turn slavery into a mass political question without automatically overcoming its profitability.
- In Saint-Domingue, revolutionary rights claims and a complex racial order helped produce a successful slave revolution under leaders including [[ToussaintLouverture]], followed by independence under [[JeanJacquesDessalines]] and retaliatory killing.
- Legal abolition ended property ownership in people on different timetables but did not automatically dismantle inherited racial ranking, segregation, or coercive institutions.
