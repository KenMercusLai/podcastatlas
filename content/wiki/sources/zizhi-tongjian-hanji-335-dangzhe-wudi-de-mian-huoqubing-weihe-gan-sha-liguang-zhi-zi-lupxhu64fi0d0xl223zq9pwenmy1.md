# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》335｜当着武帝的面，霍去病为何敢杀李广之子

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode joins two Yuan狩六年 branches. The fiscal branch says [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] used [[YangKeWesternHan|杨可]] to intensify [[RewardedDenunciationFiscalEnforcement|告缗式奖励告发财政执法]], while [[YiZongWesternHan|义纵]] arrested Yang's subordinates in opposition to its disruption and was executed for resisting the imperial order.

The military-court branch follows [[LiGanWesternHan|李敢]] from battlefield merit and enfeoffment through the death of his father [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]], his assault on [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]], and his fatal shooting by [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] during an imperial hunt. The episode's durable synthesis is unequal accountability: Wei Qing privately contains Li Gan's assault, Huo turns kin loyalty into lethal retaliation without adjudication, and Wudi protects the favored killer through an official deer-accident explanation.

## Key Claims

- The episode says the court escalated from property reporting to Yang Ke-led告缗 after wealthy households did not voluntarily supply the hoped-for funds.
- Yang Ke's enforcement is said to have produced very large confiscations and broad commercial ruin, but the episode does not document the figures or legal scope in detail.
- Yi Zong arrests several of Yang Ke's subordinates because he sees their enforcement as damaging livelihood and public confidence; Wudi then executes him for resisting the edict.
- Li Gan is presented as Li Guang's surviving son, a daring cavalry officer who reassures an encircled force and later wins merit under Huo Qubing against Xiongnu.
- After Li Guang dies by suicide following a missed movement and inquiry, Li Gan blames and assaults Wei Qing; Wei Qing conceals the attack rather than pursuing punishment.
- Huo Qubing later shoots Li Gan from behind during a hunt at Ganquan Palace, which the host interprets as retaliation for the assault on Wei Qing.
- The episode says Wudi protects Huo because he is favored and publicly attributes Li Gan's death to a deer collision, making the case a source-scoped example of [[KnownMisconductNonPunishment|known-misconduct non-punishment]].
- The opening's modern intimate-partner violence statistics and Lee Boo-jin marriage story frame the episode around familiar-person risk, but they are not necessary evidence for the Han event.
