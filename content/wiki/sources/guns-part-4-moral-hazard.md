# Guns Part 4: Moral Hazard

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode argues that homicide counts combine the incidence and severity of shootings with the ability of modern emergency and trauma medicine to keep wounded people alive. Through [[RobertFKennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]]'s 1968 assassination, city-level trends, and the work of [[EdwardCornwell]], [[JordanKomisarow]], and [[NatalieHipple]], it develops [[HomicideAsSurvivalSystemOutcome]] and proposes [[NonfatalShootingMeasurement|nonfatal shooting exposure]] as a necessary complement to death counts.

The episode then advances a more interpretive claim: medical success can create [[TraumaCareMoralHazard]] by lowering the visible death toll and weakening pressure to prevent the shootings themselves. The supplied evidence supports statistical masking more directly than it demonstrates changes in public or political behavior.

## Key Claims

- Modern transport, imaging, vascular intervention, neurosurgery, bleeding control, and swelling management can convert some formerly fatal gunshot injuries into survivable ones.
- [[HomicideAsSurvivalSystemOutcome]] distinguishes potentially lethal violence from the probability that emergency and trauma systems prevent death.
- [[EdwardCornwell]] presents American gunshot care as a high-exposure medical specialty whose gains are substantial but approach limits once patients reach treatment.
- [[JordanKomisarow]] argues that a patient with Kennedy's injury would receive much faster and more coordinated treatment today, although trajectory and damage prevent a confident survival claim.
- [[NatalieHipple]] proposes counting bullet-to-skin contact because homicide and broad aggravated-assault categories can conceal changes in nonfatal shootings.
- The episode's Memphis, Washington, and national estimates suggest that falling mortality can coexist with more or more-severe gunshot injury, but the underlying studies and datasets are not supplied.
- [[TraumaCareMoralHazard]] is a plausible political interpretation of statistical masking, not a demonstrated behavioral effect in the episode evidence.

## Connections

- [[RobertFKennedy]] - assassination case used to compare 1968 and contemporary trauma response.
- [[EdwardCornwell]] - trauma surgeon describing clinical learning, survival gains, and the limits of hospital-only solutions.
- [[JordanKomisarow]] - neurosurgeon reconstructing Kennedy's treatment and a modern alternative.
- [[NatalieHipple]] - criminologist developing a nonfatal-shooting measure from police and medical records.
- [[HomicideAsSurvivalSystemOutcome]] - separates shooting incidence from medically mediated fatality.
- [[NonfatalShootingMeasurement]] - reporting layer intended to reveal violence hidden by survival and broad offense categories.
- [[TraumaCareMoralHazard]] - hypothesis that medical rescue can reduce the visible urgency of prevention.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode strengthens the distinction between violent injury and homicide while remaining compatible with evidence that violence incidence itself can also change.
- Its moral-hazard analogy is less directly supported than its medical and measurement claims: no legislative, media, advocacy, or public-opinion analysis isolates the effect of trauma survival on prevention behavior.
- Kennedy's counterfactual survival, Cornwell's survival estimate, the annual trauma-improvement rate, the 45,000-to-70,000 counterfactual homicide range, Memphis trends, and Indianapolis bullet-to-skin findings remain episode-scoped because the supplied file is a structured summary rather than a transcript, clinical record, study, or underlying dataset.
