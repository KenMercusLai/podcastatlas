# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》332-1｜古代最高危职业之一，居然有TA

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode introduces [[LiCaiWesternHan|李蔡]] as a three-reign veteran whose military service, marquisate, and eventual appointment as [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s chancellor contrast with the fame but frustrated advancement of his kinsman [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]]. It uses Li Cai's rise and later suicide to open a wider question about why the chancellorship was unusually dangerous under a forceful emperor.

The episode's durable institutional claim is a [[StrongRulerChancellorshipDoubleBind|double bind in the chancellorship]]. The officeholder must avoid excessive independence and understand the ruler's preferences, yet must also manage war, territorial expansion, finance, strategic assessment, and court coordination. The numerical account of thirteen chancellors and the character judgments about Li Cai remain episode-attributed rather than a complete institutional study.

## Key Claims

- The episode says Han Wudi used thirteen chancellors during a fifty-four-year reign; apart from one regency survivor, it classifies seven as dismissed and five as dying by suicide after accusation, with two of the dismissed later dying violently.
- Li Cai and Li Guang are presented as entering service together as attendants under Emperor Wen, after which Li Cai reaches a two-thousand-shi post under Emperor Jing and accumulates military merit under Han Wudi.
- The source says Li Cai serves as chancellor of Dai, campaigns against the Xiongnu, follows [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] against the Right Wise King as Light Chariot General, and receives the Lean marquisate in 124 BCE.
- Li Cai's successful advancement is contrasted with Li Guang's fame, battlefield reputation, failure to obtain a marquisate, and lower terminal office.
- The episode reports the transmitted judgment “为人在下中” and interprets it as a low personal ranking, but this is a reputational assessment rather than a demonstrated measure of governing ability.
- After [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] dies in office, Li Cai becomes the seventh chancellor in the episode's sequence; [[ZhuangQingdiWesternHan|庄青翟]] and [[ZhaoZhouWesternHan|赵周]] are named with him as three successive chancellors who do not end well.
- The source says Li Cai dies by cutting his own throat in spring 118 BCE. This conflicts with Hanji 332-2's account that he dies by striking the prison's iron gate.

## Key Quotes

> “为人在下中” - the transmitted reputational judgment the host uses to contrast Li Cai with Li Guang.

> “既要性格柔顺，又要懂帝王之心” - the episode's compact statement of one side of the chancellor's role conflict.

## Connections

- [[LiCaiWesternHan|李蔡]] - central case whose long career, military advancement, marquisate, chancellorship, and suicide introduce the episode's high-risk-office question.
- [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]] - famous kinsman used as a contrast between reputation and official advancement.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - forceful ruler whose personal dominance and expansion agenda jointly shape the chancellor's demands.
- [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] - long-lived predecessor presented as the rare chancellor who dies naturally while still in office.
- [[ZhuangQingdiWesternHan|庄青翟]] - successor named in the episode's sequence of chancellors who do not end well.
- [[ZhaoZhouWesternHan|赵周]] - later successor named in the same adverse-outcome sequence.
- [[StrongRulerChancellorshipDoubleBind|强势君主下的丞相双重约束]] - synthesis of the simultaneous demands for political compliance and substantial governing competence.

## Contradictions

- Kinship conflict: this episode calls Li Cai Li Guang's 堂弟, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-332-2-dahan-chengxiang-licai-zhisi-jiujing-youmeiyou-heimu-loyre4ghvm3zcp-jxez9k26du8oj|Hanji 332-2]] calls him a younger brother. The precise relationship is left source-contested.
- Death-mechanism conflict: this episode says Li Cai cuts his own throat, while Hanji 332-2 says he dies by striking the prison's iron gate. Both agree on suicide after his fall, but the mechanism is unresolved.
- Scope qualification: the supplied text does not independently establish the thirteen-chancellor count, each outcome classification, the exact sequence of offices and campaigns, or that the office's double bind by itself caused Li Cai's death.
