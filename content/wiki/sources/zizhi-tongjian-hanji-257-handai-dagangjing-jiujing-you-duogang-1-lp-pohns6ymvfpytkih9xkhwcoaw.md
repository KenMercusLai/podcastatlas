# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》257｜汉代大杠精，究竟有多杠？（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the early career and governing method behind [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]]'s later reputation as a fearless critic of [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. Ji refuses to treat remonstrance as optional politeness: he publicly challenges the emperor's claim to emulate Yao and Shun, accepts liability after using an unauthorized imperial command to relieve disaster victims, and is credited with governing Donghai through Huang-Lao restraint, capable subordinates, delegated authority, and attention to direction rather than detail.

The closing branches place that profile inside early-Wudi policy formation. [[WangHuiWesternHan|王恢]] argues that Xiongnu peace will be short-lived and favors attack, while [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] answers that pursuit across a mobile pastoral frontier will exhaust a distant army; the court accepts peace in this episode before the later [[MayiAmbushWesternHan|马邑之谋]] reopens the dispute. The final notice attributes the first commandery-and-kingdom recommendation of one filial and incorrupt candidate to advice from [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]], but the supplied text ends before developing the institution.

## Key Claims

- Ji An treats candid speech as part of high office: preserving his own life does not excuse silence when imperial conduct threatens public affairs.
- During a Henan disaster, Ji reportedly uses an unauthorized imperial command to open relief, then returns his tally and offers to accept punishment rather than hide the procedural breach.
- As Donghai governor, Ji is said to select capable subordinates, grant them authority, hold the overall direction, and avoid micromanaging minor matters; the episode credits this light-touch method with restoring order despite his illness.
- Ji publicly tells Wudi that private desire beneath a benevolent exterior is incompatible with emulating Yao and Shun, causing the emperor to end the audience in anger.
- [[YanZhuWesternHan|严助]] distinguishes Ji's ordinary administrative performance from his rare value beside a ruler: once committed, he cannot easily be induced to retreat.
- Wang Hui predicts that Xiongnu peace will be temporary, while Han Anguo emphasizes mobility, distance, fatigue, and supply; most ministers support Han and Wudi accepts renewed peace in this account.
- The episode says that in 134 BCE, on Dong Zhongshu's advice, each commandery and kingdom was ordered to recommend one person for filial conduct and incorruptibility.

## Key Quotes

> “天子置公卿辅弼之臣，宁令从谀承意，陷主于不义乎？” - Ji An's attributed defense of candid ministerial duty.

> “责大指而已，不苛小。” - the episode's compact description of Ji An's Donghai governing method.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. Ji An's willingness to bypass procedure in an emergency qualifies, rather than reverses, his later public-purpose legal criticism: the joined profile distinguishes accountable discretion for immediate relief from ruler-aligned technical punishment that obscures public purpose.
- Policy-sequence boundary: this episode ends with Wudi accepting peace, while Hanji 260 later has Wang Hui answer Han Anguo's deep-expedition objection with a border ambush. The sources can describe successive stages rather than incompatible judgments.
- Name normalization: the supplied transcription repeatedly renders 汲黯 as “几案/起岸.” The office, Donghai, relief, illness, Yan Zhu evaluation, and Wudi-remonstrance chain identify Ji An; the wiki uses the standard historical form while preserving the transcript problem here.
- The Minyue mission, disaster location and severity, unauthorized-edict mechanics, tally surrender, Donghai administration, exact offices, illness leave, dialogue, Wudi's emotions, Xiongnu settlement terms, vote, recommendation order, chronology, and institutional significance remain episode-attributed pending primary-text comparison.
