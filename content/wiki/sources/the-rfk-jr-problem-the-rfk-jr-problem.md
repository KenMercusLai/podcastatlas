# The RFK Jr. Problem

## Summary

In this [[RevisionistHistory]] episode, [[MalcolmGladwell]] tests [[RobertFKennedyJr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]'s attack on [[RotaTeq]] against the vaccine's package insert, large placebo-controlled trial, and testimony from [[PaulOffit]] and other health experts. The episode argues that Kennedy cites adverse events without using the placebo group needed to assess causation, while the trial showed dramatic protection against severe rotavirus disease and no meaningful serious-adverse-event difference between groups.

The historical branch follows overwhelmed childhood-diarrhea wards, oral rehydration, sanitation, and the long development of rotavirus vaccines. Gladwell then interprets Kennedy's resistance through [[GermTheoryTerrainTheoryConflict]], while preserving an important limit: Kennedy did not provide a recorded response, so the episode documents an evidentiary failure more strongly than it establishes his motive.

## Key Claims

- [[RotaTeq]]'s roughly 68,000-infant trial compared about 34,000 vaccine recipients with about 34,000 placebo recipients and reportedly found no meaningful difference in serious adverse events.
- The package insert result highlighted by [[SafiBahcall]] showed one diarrhea or gastroenteritis case in the vaccine group versus 51 in the placebo group.
- [[ControlledTrialAdverseEventInterpretation]] requires comparing outcome rates across assigned groups; counting illnesses after vaccination without the control rate cannot establish vaccine causation.
- The episode says Kennedy's cited annual range of 953 to 1,689 adverse reactions could not be located in the package insert he cited.
- VAERS reports record events after vaccination but do not by themselves show that vaccination caused them.
- [[PaulOffit]] says his team spent 26 years developing the vaccine and estimates that rotavirus vaccination saves about 165,000 lives globally each year.
- The decline in childhood diarrheal death is presented as a combined result of vaccination, sanitation, oral rehydration, and wider child-health improvement rather than one intervention alone.
- Gladwell argues that Kennedy favors terrain theory over germ theory, but the direction and psychological force of that commitment remain the host's interpretation.
