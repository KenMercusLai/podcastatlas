# Colleges are training the next generation of content creators

## Summary

This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode uses [[CharlieRandall]]'s double major at [[ArkansasTechUniversity]] to examine how colleges are turning platform-native production into [[DigitalCreatorEducation|formal creator education]]. The curriculum combines publishing practice, editing, thumbnails, opening hooks, audience engagement, and performance data, while Randall resists treating maximum views or AI-assisted creative choices as universal goals.

Randall's planned use of [[YouTube]] as an acting portfolio broadens the degree's employment case beyond influencer fame. The episode presents creator skills as potentially transferable to voice and motion-capture acting, production collaboration, and organizational social-media work, but provides no tuition, employment-outcome, or employer-demand evidence.

## Key Claims

- Colleges are beginning to offer classes, minors, and majors in digital content creation; [[ArkansasTechUniversity]] launched the degree discussed in the episode roughly three years before the interview.
- [[CharlieRandall]] says coursework uses performance data to examine thumbnails, greetings, opening hooks, click-through rates, editing choices, and audience engagement.
- Required weekly publishing gives students repeated production practice and a structured starting point rather than only abstract instruction.
- Students collaborate across content creation, creative writing, acting, scripting, filming, camera work, and performance.
- Randall accepts analytics as useful feedback but rejects the assumption that every creator must maximize views or imitate prominent YouTubers.
- He prefers making personal creative elements himself rather than using AI for work such as thumbnails, while acknowledging that other creators use AI successfully.
- Randall plans to turn videos into a digital acting portfolio for voice and motion-capture opportunities; he also argues that the degree can support organizational social-media roles without requiring a large personal following.
