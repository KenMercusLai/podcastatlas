## Summary
This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode treats "江湖规矩" as a social survival structure rather than only a wuxia or gangland image. It argues that weak individuals form circles through shared interest, renqing, face, symbols, and dependency, then explains Jianghu morality as a virtual-kinship order that borrows Confucian family hierarchy and "义" while narrowing it into brotherhood, patronage, violence, and wealth-seeking anxiety. The episode closes by connecting Jianghu logic to rulers such as [[LiuBang|刘邦]] and Zhu Yuanzhang, whose insecurity after gaining power is used as a brief example of why old brotherhood ties can later become threats.

## Key Claims
- Jianghu culture is not mainly fighting; it is a web of [[RenqingOrder|人情秩序]], face, relationship, and rule-like symbols for people seeking group protection.
- Jianghu circles often lack shared ideals or independent人格, but they do contain private interest, opportunistic attachment, brotherhood language, and exchange relationships.
- The episode distinguishes Confucian "大义" from Jianghu "小义": public righteousness is narrowed into loyalty to one's own brothers, teacher, adoptive father, or leader.
- [[JianghuVirtualKinship|江湖虚拟血缘]] lets strangers simulate family by becoming brothers, disciples, godchildren, or in-laws, giving belonging while also creating hierarchy.
- [[JianghuSurvivalAnxiety|江湖生存焦虑]] helps explain the emphasis on violence and money: security is imagined as stronger force, more wealth, and control over former companions.
- The episode treats Jianghu thought as syncretic, with Confucian "义" and Mohist-style侠义 especially important, while also invoking secularized Confucian, Buddhist, Daoist, and folk traditions.

## Key Quotes
> "江湖不是打打杀杀，江湖是人情世故。" — the episode's central reframing of Jianghu.

> "义是儒家文化的核心理念之一，也是江湖文化的灵魂。" — used to connect Jianghu brotherhood to older moral vocabulary.
