# Inside the Iran War and the Pentagon's Feud with Anthropic with Under Secretary of War Emil Michael

## Summary
This emergency [[AllIn|All-In]] episode interviews [[EmilMichael|Emil Michael]] about the U.S.-Israel operation against [[Iran]], AI-coordinated drone warfare, missile defense, the [[Anthropic]] dispute, and defense-industrial modernization. Its strongest contribution is a first-person Pentagon-side explanation of why acceptable-use restrictions and vendor control over a deployed model were treated as operational risks, while the episode's war analysis remains openly administration-aligned and does not include Anthropic's response.

The source connects [[DefenseAIProcurement]] to [[DefenseAIControlPlaneRisk]]: Michael says a model embedded through [[AmazonWebServices]] GovCloud and [[Palantir]] remained refreshable by its vendor, so model policy, technical control, and continuity could not be separated. It also qualifies [[HumanAuthorizedAutonomousWeapons]] by arguing that acceptable AI risk depends on mission, civilian exposure, and decision time rather than one universal autonomy rule.

## Key Claims
- Michael says the Iran operation sought to degrade nuclear, missile, and proxy-support capacity without becoming an Iraq- or Afghanistan-style ground occupation.
- [[ChamathPalihapitiya|Chamath Palihapitiya]] and [[DavidFriedberg|David Friedberg]] interpret pressure on Iranian and Venezuelan oil flows as possible leverage in a wider U.S.-[[China]] negotiation; Michael calls any China benefit second-order.
- Michael says drones caused about 70% of Russia-Ukraine casualties and argues that AI-coordinated, heterogeneous swarms will exceed the scale of human-only control.
- He distinguishes lower-risk defensive decisions from autonomy around civilians and says very short missile-defense timelines may require AI-assisted or automated action.
- Michael says Anthropic's contract terms restricted kinetic-strike planning and satellite movement, while the department wanted one standard covering all lawful uses rather than scenario-by-scenario exceptions.
- He says Anthropic retained the model control plane for a model served through AWS GovCloud and Palantir, creating concern that the vendor could refresh or change a capability embedded in sensitive workflows.
- Michael characterizes the supply-chain-risk designation as continuity and policy-dependence management rather than punishment; this is the department-side account and is not independently established by the supplied document.
- The episode says alternative providers including [[XAI|xAI]], [[Google]], and [[OpenAI]] were being pursued to create redundancy and price competition.
- Michael describes a modernization agenda built around drones, attritable systems, procurement reform, new defense primes, critical-mineral and battery supply chains, and strategic lending.
- He presents [[DARPA]] as a long-range research engine for cyber defense and biological routes to critical-mineral extraction or synthesis.

## Key Quotes
> "all lawful use" - Michael's preferred procurement standard as recorded in the supplied episode summary.

> "drone-on-drone and robot-on-robot warfare" - Michael's description of the direction of battlefield autonomy.

> "If a company does not want its tools used for Department of War work, it should not sell to the Department of War." - Michael's vendor-policy position as recorded in the supplied summary.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode is the Pentagon-side account of a disputed negotiation and does not include Anthropic or the underlying contract, designation notice, classified-use documentation, or technical control records.
- Earlier wiki sources summarize Anthropic's red lines as mass surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. Michael instead describes bulk public-information collection, kinetic-strike planning, satellite movement, scenario exceptions, and vendor control of model refreshes. These accounts may describe different contract provisions or incompatible framing; the supplied evidence does not resolve which scope is complete.
- The source speaks as though the $200 million contract was canceled and the supply-chain-risk designation was formal, while a same-day Marketplace source said Anthropic reportedly had not yet received the designation in writing and a later source described a broader federal ban. Timing, legal scope, and implementation remain unresolved.
