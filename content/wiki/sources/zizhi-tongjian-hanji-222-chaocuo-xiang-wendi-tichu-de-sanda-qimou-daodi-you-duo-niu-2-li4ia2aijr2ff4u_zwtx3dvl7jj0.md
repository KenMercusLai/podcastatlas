# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》222｜晁错向文帝提出的三大奇谋，到底有多牛？（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]]'s answer to recurrent [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] raids under [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. Because mobile raiders can observe weak points and withdraw before distant levies assemble, Chao proposes state-built fortified settlements populated by volunteers who receive housing, tools, tax relief, clothing, grain, pardons, titles, medical and ritual support, and protection until they can support themselves.

The plan goes beyond moving people. It binds defense to households, kin, neighbors, property, local knowledge, rewards, elected local leaders, military grouping, and riding-and-archery training, turning settlement into a permanent production-and-defense community. The episode then notes Liu Heng's adoption of the recruitment proposal and Chao's later appointment as crown-prince household steward, while leaving the announced agricultural-policy discussion to the next installment.

## Key Claims

- Distant annual frontier rotations react slowly, cost heavily, and do not let soldiers accumulate local terrain, enemy, or community knowledge.
- Chao proposes fortified towns at strategic passes and crossings, with houses and tools prepared before recruiting at least one thousand households per town.
- Pardons, ranks, tax exemptions, clothing, food, and household supplies compensate settlers for danger and relocation costs until they can become self-supporting.
- Rewards for recovering captured people and livestock align kinship, neighborhood protection, and material interest instead of relying on abstract virtue alone.
- Medical care, ritual provision, marriage, birth support, burial support, and well-built housing are treated as military infrastructure because they make long-term residence credible.
- Five-household and larger nested units, locally chosen leaders, riding and archery practice, and shared life history turn residents into mutually recognizable local defenders.
- Chao warns that fit settlers and capable officials are both necessary, and that the system must be ready before a likely new Xiongnu attack.
- The episode closes by identifying Chao as a trusted adviser to crown prince Liu Qi and previewing his response to an agricultural crisis.

## Key Quotes

> “这不是因为品德高尚，而是因为要保护亲族、乡邻并获得财货。” - the source summary's explanation of why local mutual defense can be sustained.

> “平时生产、战时作战。” - a concise synthesis of the fortified settler community described in the episode.

## Connections

- [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]] - adviser who turns the mobile-raiding problem into a detailed settlement, welfare, incentive, and training program.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - ruler who accepts the proposal to recruit households for permanent frontier settlement.
- [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - mobile frontier opponent whose observation, raiding speed, and withdrawal options create the defensive problem.
- [[FrontierSettlerDefense|移民实边式边防]] - the episode's core institutional design for joining settlement, production, welfare, and local defense.
- [[NomadicCavalryRaidingAsymmetry|游牧骑兵劫掠不对称]] - upstream protection and response-time problem the proposal tries to reduce.
- [[FreeRiderProblem|搭便车问题]] - adjacent collective-action problem that recovered-property rewards and shared local stakes try to reduce.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode adds an earlier constructive-policy layer to Chao Cuo's current arc before his later territorial-reduction program and political exposure under Jingdi.
- Adoption is not implementation proof: Liu Heng's recruitment order does not establish how many households moved, how completely the promised support was delivered, or how effective the settlements became in combat.
- The episode begins inside a multipart argument and ends before the agricultural-policy section, so it does not support a complete reconstruction of the advertised “three plans.”
- Source-scope caution: Xiongnu habits and intentions, town size, ditch and wall specifications, pardons and ranks, supply duration, recovery rewards, medical and ritual arrangements, unit structure, leader selection, training, attack forecast, offices, dialogue, and policy results require comparison with transmitted records.
