## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode separates "焚书" from "坑儒" and focuses on the 213 BCE book-burning order in [[QinState|秦国]] after unification. It follows [[ChunyuYue|淳于越]]'s banquet argument for reviving enfeoffment, [[LiSi|李斯]]'s rejection of classical复古 criticism, and [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s approval of a policy that burned private classics while keeping official and practical exceptions. The host reads the policy as a political technique for uniform rule and official learning, not simply as a sectarian Legalist plot against one school, while still recording the cultural loss and the policy's self-defeating incentive for private hiding.

## Key Claims
- "焚书" and "坑儒" should be treated as two incidents; this source covers the book-burning policy and leaves the坑儒 branch unresolved.
- The episode sets the 213 BCE decision after eight years of formal unification, when Qin is still burdened by war mobilization, population movement, frontier expansion, and judicial cleanup.
- [[ChunyuYue|淳于越]] challenges Qin's [[CommanderyCountyAntiEnfeoffment|郡县制反分封定局]] by arguing from Xia-Shang-Zhou precedent that the empire should restore hereditary enfeoffment and copy ancient models.
- [[LiSi|李斯]] answers that the unified empire now needs centrally issued law, productive subjects, and official legal instruction rather than private scholars using classics to criticize the present.
- The [[QinBookBurningPolicy|秦代焚书令]] targets private collections of non-Qin histories, [[Shijing|《诗经》]], [[Shangshu|《尚书》]], and other scholarly writings, while sparing official copies, medical books, divination books, and agriculture/tree-planting works.
- Punishments are presented as severe state coercion: discussion of Shijing or Shangshu, using antiquity to attack the present, official non-reporting, and post-deadline book hiding all become legal offenses.
- The host frames the policy through [[OfficialLearningMonopoly|学在王官式知识垄断]] and [[ImperialHomogenization|帝国整齐划一]]: Qin wants knowledge, law, and political imagination to return to official channels.
- The episode does not deny damage. It says the destruction of six-state histories in particular caused serious cultural loss, even if copies remained inside Qin's palace archive.
- The host calls the policy inefficient because high-pressure burning can make private book owners hide texts more carefully; later exam incentives are used as a counterfactual way to marginalize texts without open destruction.
- [[KongFu|孔鲋]] embodies that counter-effect: he hides books, waits for a future turn, later accepts [[ChenSheng|陈胜]]'s appointment through [[ChenYu|陈馀]], and dies with the new rebel government.
