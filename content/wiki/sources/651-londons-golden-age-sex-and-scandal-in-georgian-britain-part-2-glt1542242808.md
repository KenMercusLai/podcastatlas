# 651. London’s Golden Age: Sex and Scandal in Georgian Britain (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the young [[JamesBoswell]] from fashionable, sexually impulsive London through legal study and a continental tour, using his diary to hold ambition, melancholy, religious aspiration, pleasure, guilt, and self-justification in the same portrait. His growing friendship with [[SamuelJohnson]] supplies the emotional center: Johnson becomes a brusque but affectionate moral authority whose conversation, practical consolation, and willingness to travel with him answer Boswell’s search for guidance.

Boswell’s pursuit of [[JeanJacquesRousseau|Rousseau]], [[Voltaire]], and [[PasqualePaoli]] then joins [[GrandTourCulture|Grand Tour culture]] to celebrity access, political romance, and public self-fashioning. His advocacy for Paoli and Corsican independence was sincere but also enlarged “Corsica Boswell” as a persona; the episode ends with Johnson agreeing to visit Scotland in 1773, leading directly into the Hebridean journey covered by Part 3.

## Key Claims

- Boswell’s diary is valuable because it records moral aspiration, vanity, sexual compulsion, melancholy, and self-serving rationalization without resolving those tensions into a clean character arc.
- London gave Boswell social access and a stage for self-invention, but failed military ambition, illness, debt, eviction, and sexual risk strengthened his desire for discipline and paternal guidance.
- Johnson’s friendship combined moral counsel, argument, practical comfort, affectionate loyalty, and sharp rebuke rather than simple approval or idealized mentorship.
- Boswell’s continental travel turned legal education into a search for courts, famous thinkers, danger, sexual experience, and association with celebrated men.
- Paoli and the Corsican cause joined a real politics of independence to Boswell’s classical imagination and desire for a heroic public role.
- Newspaper advocacy, the successful Corsica book, costume, and the Shakespeare Jubilee converted travel and political commitment into the “Corsica Boswell” persona.
- The 1773 Scottish plan emerged from an established friendship and prepares both the later travel books and Boswell’s biographical method.

## Key Quotes

> “its own tormentor” - Johnson’s reported lesson from a moth burning itself on a candle during the journey to Harwich.

> “Corsica Boswell” - the public nickname Boswell welcomed after the success of his Corsica book.

> “empty his head of Corsica” - Johnson’s reported rebuke when Boswell’s enthusiasm had outlasted Corsican independence.
