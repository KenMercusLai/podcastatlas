# Schrödinger’s chat: can AI and scientists co-exist?

## Summary
This [[EconomistPodcasts]] episode joins three stories about abundance and interpretation. Its main segment uses [[OpenAI]]'s reported batch of mathematical results to argue that [[AIForMath]] and [[DigitallyVerifiableDiscoveryLoop|digitally verifiable discovery loops]] may make results abundant before people can understand or prioritize them, changing [[ScientificDiscoveryAutomation]], academic credit, and safety questions. The later segments show food companies using [[FoodTextureAesthetics|texture]] as a product and media differentiator, then remember [[AnnieDillard]] as a writer whose [[AttentiveWitnessing|attentive witnessing]] held natural beauty, suffering, and mystery together.

## Key Claims
- The episode reports that combining large language models with reinforcement learning is improving work in mathematics and computer science, with possible spillovers into genetics, protein design, drug development, fusion control, weather forecasting, and AI research.
- [[AIForMath]] may advance earlier than experimental science because formal proofs can be mechanically checked, while biology and other physical sciences still depend on laboratories, specimens, measurements, and eventually robotic experimentation.
- The reported OpenAI batch unsettled mathematicians through speed and scale as well as difficulty; cumbersome counterexamples or proofs may be valid without providing the elegance, explanation, or conceptual compression human mathematicians value.
- If machines generate results faster than people can interpret them, academic status and institutional design may shift toward problem selection, verification, explanation, application, and responsibility rather than result production alone.
- End-to-end research automation could accelerate socially valuable discoveries, but more autonomous scientific systems also increase safety concerns and the possibility that useful outputs exceed human understanding.
- Snack makers are treating crunch, sound, layered mouthfeel, and moisture control as product-design variables, while packaging language and amplified social-media audio turn texture into marketing as well as material engineering.
- The episode presents [[AnnieDillard]]'s close observation of a damaged moth, predation, light, and fire as a lifelong practice of witnessing creation's beauty, abundance, cruelty, and resistance to simple explanation.
