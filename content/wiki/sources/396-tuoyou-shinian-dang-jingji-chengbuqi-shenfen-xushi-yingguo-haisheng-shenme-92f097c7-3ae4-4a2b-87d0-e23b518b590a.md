## Summary
This [[ShengdongJixi|声东击西]] episode uses Brexit ten years on to examine the [[UnitedKingdom]] as a case where weak growth, imperial memory, rituals of class and belonging, and post-globalization nationalism reinforce each other. The discussion extends [[Brexit]], [[BrexitEconomicFriction]], and [[PostBrexitStrategicIdentity]] by arguing that Britain's difficulty is not only the measurable cost of leaving the [[EuropeanUnion]], but also the loss of a credible identity story after empire, EU membership, and inherited institutions no longer supply enough authority. It adds [[PostImperialIdentityGap]] and [[RitualizedSocialBoundary]] as concepts for reading British formality, boundary-making, and nostalgia as political as well as cultural signals.

## Key Claims
- British everyday formality, from Oxford dining rules to exam dress, is presented as [[RitualizedSocialBoundary]]: etiquette, clothes, invitations, accent, and setting help people mark who belongs and who does not.
- The episode treats Britain's post-Brexit malaise as a mix of [[BrexitEconomicFriction]], regional stagnation after 2008, political churn, and an unresolved [[PostImperialIdentityGap]].
- The 1975 European Community referendum is framed as economically oriented, while the 2016 Brexit referendum is framed as more identity-heavy, emphasizing borders, law, security, finance, and national protection.
- "Take back control" worked because it translated uneven globalization, deindustrialization, and lost dignity into a sovereignty story easier to mobilize than long-term economic reform.
- The hosts compare Brexit with Trumpism and other nationalist movements, treating post-globalization politics as a repeated search for sharper collective boundaries.
- The episode presents [[PostBrexitStrategicIdentity]] through "Global Britain," AUKUS, AI regulation, finance, education, life sciences, and creative industries, but argues that visibility and rule-setting do not automatically become domestic growth.
- Government turnover, the Queen's death, royal scandal, and weakening religious authority are treated as identity-support failures that leave social media and fragmented cultural belonging to carry more weight.
- The closing prescription is psychological and practical: Britain may need to accept a more ordinary middle-developed-country status, cooperate more calmly with Europe, and solve material problems instead of relying on imperial distinction.
