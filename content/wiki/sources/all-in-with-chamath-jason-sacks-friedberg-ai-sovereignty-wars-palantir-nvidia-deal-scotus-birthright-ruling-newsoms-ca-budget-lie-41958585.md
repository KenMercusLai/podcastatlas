# AI Sovereignty Wars, Palantir-Nvidia Deal, SCOTUS Birthright Ruling, Newsom's CA Budget Lie

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] episode turns "AI sovereignty" into an enterprise and government control problem spanning [[Palantir]], [[Nvidia]], [[Anthropic]], [[OpenAI]], open models, on-prem inference, proprietary data, and vendor lock-in. The hosts argue that organizations may want to own compute, data, model weights, and implementation knowledge when frontier model providers can learn from customers and later compete with them.

The second half moves from AI strategy to institutions: AI labor displacement, [[AIExportControls]], Chinese [[OpenSourceAIModels|open-source models]], [[BirthrightCitizenshipDebate|birthright citizenship]], immigration, [[DemocraticSocialistsOfAmerica|DSA]] momentum, and [[CaliforniaFiscalFragility|California fiscal fragility]]. The California budget discussion is forceful but source-scoped: [[DavidFriedberg|David Friedberg]] presents [[GavinNewsom|Gavin Newsom]]'s balanced-budget claim as debt-backed accounting rather than durable fiscal repair.

## Key Claims
- [[JasonCalacanis|Jason Calacanis]] opens with a [[Palantir]]-[[Nvidia]] sovereign AI partnership for U.S. government customers, framing agency ownership of hardware, data, and model weights as a security and control issue.
- [[DavidSacks|David Sacks]] says [[AlexKarp|Alex Karp]]'s enterprise AI safety point is that organizations must control compute, models, data, and proprietary "alpha" rather than hand strategic knowledge to model providers that may later compete.
- [[ChamathPalihapitiya|Chamath Palihapitiya]] argues companies need an independent control plane and cites an 8090 benchmark where a harness made Claude cheaper/faster and an open-source model plus harness much cheaper but slower.
- [[DavidFriedberg|Friedberg]] says [[Anthropic]] approached life-sciences companies for proprietary datasets and predicts movement from centralized AI hubs toward [[EnterpriseOwnedModels|enterprise-owned models]], medium training hubs, and distributed on-prem inference.
- The hosts treat [[ForwardDeployedEngineer|forward-deployed engineering]] as both useful implementation help and a possible route for vendors such as [[Microsoft]], [[Amazon]], [[XAI|xAI]], and [[OpenAI]] to study customer workflows.
- The jobs section is contested: Friedberg and Sacks cite current adopter headcount growth, while Jason expects future displacement in [[CustomerSupportAutomation|customer support]], data entry, driving, warehouse, and package work.
- The export-control segment presents [[Fable5|Fable]] and Mythos restrictions as a warning that model-company danger rhetoric can trigger government action, while Sacks says Chinese open models can be forked and run in U.S. data centers if safety and backdoor risks are reviewed.
- The birthright section frames the [[UnitedStatesConstitution|14th Amendment]] dispute as a tension between textual citizenship language, original-purpose arguments, long-term undocumented families, temporary visas, birth tourism, and congressional authority.
- The immigration section distinguishes legal residence, long-term undocumented non-criminal residents, work-oriented immigration, assimilation expectations, and public polling on deportation.
- The California section argues that high spending, revenue concentration in the top 1%, company exits, new taxes, pension obligations, and possible billionaire-tax politics make Newsom's zero-deficit claim fragile.

## Key Quotes
> "own the hardware, data, and model weights" - Jason's sovereign AI framing.

> "intelligence and judgment" - Chamath's distinction between renting AI and renting distribution.

> "balanced, zero-deficit" - the California budget claim Friedberg challenges.
