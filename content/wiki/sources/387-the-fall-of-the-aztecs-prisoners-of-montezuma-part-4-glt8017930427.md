# 387. The Fall of the Aztecs: Prisoners of Montezuma (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode follows [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]] and his company inside Tenochtitlan, where the city's scale, markets, state order, ritual life, and courtly hospitality complicate any simple story of immediate Spanish control. It treats the relationship between Cortes and [[Moctezuma]] as a [[ConquestCaptivityNarrativeUncertainty|captivity problem]]: the Spaniards may initially have been watched, contained, studied, or incorporated before they took Moctezuma hostage, and the surviving Spanish narratives do not securely establish the chronology. The arrival of [[PanfiloDeNarvaez|Panfilo de Narvaez]]'s expedition then forces Cortes to divide his small force and leave [[PedroDeAlvarado|Pedro de Alvarado]] in the capital.

## Key Claims

- The Spaniards encountered Tenochtitlan as a large, clean, regulated, and politically organized metropolis whose markets and administration contradicted any picture of an unsophisticated society.
- Mexica human sacrifice was real and intelligible within a cosmology in which blood sustained the universe, although Bernal Diaz's temple details and remembered dialogue still require source criticism.
- Moctezuma's hospitality, tours, hunting, games, and continued mobility make the older claim that he immediately surrendered his empire and remained an ordinary prisoner implausibly simple.
- Matthew Restall's revision proposes that Moctezuma may initially have treated the Spaniards as contained curiosities, armed dependents, or captives rather than acknowledging them as masters.
- Cortes and his followers behaved as wealth-seeking armed entrepreneurs, pressing for gold and increasing the strain on any arrangement Moctezuma thought he controlled.
- Narvaez's arrival on the coast forced Cortes to take roughly eighty men and [[Malinche]] toward Veracruz while leaving the rest under Alvarado in Tenochtitlan.
- One recent chronology places Moctezuma's hostage-taking at this departure point rather than soon after first contact, but the episode does not claim that the disputed sequence is settled.

## Key Quotes

> "armed entrepreneurs" - the episode's description of conquistadors seeking gold, status, and advancement.

> "treacherous" - the hosts' characterization of the evidence for deciding who controlled whom in Tenochtitlan.

## Connections

- [[HernanCortes]] - expedition leader navigating religious shock, greed, strategic vulnerability, and a rival Spanish force.
- [[Moctezuma]] - Mexica ruler whose hospitality, surveillance, agency, and later captivity resist a simple surrender narrative.
- [[Malinche]] - indispensable interpreter who mediated exchanges and advised Moctezuma during the reported hostage scene.
- [[MexicaEmpire]] - urban and political order represented through Tenochtitlan's administration, market, court, and ritual life.
- [[PanfiloDeNarvaez]] - rival commander whose coastal arrival forces Cortes to divide his expedition.
- [[PedroDeAlvarado]] - subordinate left with the larger Spanish group and Moctezuma in Tenochtitlan.
- [[SpanishConquestOfMexico]] - wider process presented here as uncertain encounter, coercive improvisation, and intra-Spanish rivalry rather than inevitable advance.
- [[ConquestCaptivityNarrativeUncertainty]] - framework for separating hospitality, containment, dependency, and hostage-taking from later claims of immediate Spanish mastery.
- [[ColonialSourceMediation]] - adjacent source-critical problem concerning translation, retrospective testimony, and culturally loaded categories.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with [[388-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-festival-of-blood-part-5-glt8784071073|Part 5]] was found; this episode supplies the unstable accommodation and disputed hostage chronology immediately before Narvaez's defeat and the Toxcatl massacre.
- The traditional Spanish account of an early voluntary submission conflicts with the episode's evidence of Moctezuma's continuing freedom of movement and with recent interpretations that the Spaniards were initially contained or captive.
- The date and mechanics of Moctezuma's seizure remain contested; the episode finds the later-hostage chronology plausible without treating it as established fact.
- Temple descriptions, dialogue, metropolitan population, sacrifice plans, Moctezuma's motives, Restall's zoo interpretation, and the precise balance of coercion remain source-scoped or interpretive.
