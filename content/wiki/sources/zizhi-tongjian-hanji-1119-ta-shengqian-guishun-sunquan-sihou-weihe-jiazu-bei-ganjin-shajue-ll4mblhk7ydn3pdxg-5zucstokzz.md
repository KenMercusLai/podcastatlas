# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1119丨他生前归顺孙权，死后为何家族被赶尽杀绝？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[ShiXie|士燮]] from a locally rooted and classically educated Jiaozhou elite into a long-serving governor of [[JiaozhiCommandery|交趾郡]]. It argues that he preserves practical authority by cooperating with successive inspectors, avoiding the province's highest formal office, and later accepting [[SunQuan|孙权]]'s authority through a family hostage and repeated tribute.

After Shi Xie's death in 226 CE, [[ShiHui|士徽]] rejects Sun Quan's replacement appointment and claims the Jiaozhi governorship. [[LyuDai|吕岱]] secures the surrender of Shi Hui and five brothers through a safety promise and then executes them, turning [[Jiaozhou|交州]] from a negotiated local order into more direct Wu control. The episode frames the contrast as personal political wisdom, while the durable synthesis also identifies a structural [[PersonalizedLocalAutonomySuccession|个人化地方自治继承断裂]]: accommodation tied to one broker did not create a hereditary right to his offices or territorial power.

## Key Claims

- Shi Xie's family had lived in Jiaozhou for six generations, his father served as Rinan administrator, and study of the *Zuo Commentary* in Luoyang joined local roots to classical reputation.
- After inspector Zhu Fu's extraction provoked unrest and death, Shi Xie secured commandery offices for his brothers in Hepu, Jiuzhen, and Nanhai, giving the family control across four commanderies.
- The episode credits Shi Xie's relatively lenient government, reception of northern scholars, and support for classical learning with stability and cultural growth in the far south.
- Shi Xie held local power without becoming Jiaozhou inspector; successive outside inspectors still needed his cooperation, allowing formal submission and practical autonomy to coexist.
- When Sun Quan sent Bu Zhi south, Shi Xie submitted, sent a son as hostage, offered repeated tribute, and received rank and rewards in return.
- Shi Xie died in 226 CE after more than forty years in Jiaozhi; Sun Quan then divided the province and appointed his own personnel, including Chen Shi as the new Jiaozhi administrator.
- Shi Hui rejected the appointment, claimed the office, and mobilized his kin, exposing the difference between his father's recognized tenure and an asserted hereditary succession.
- Lyu Dai used Shi Kuang to promise preservation of the household and property, accepted the surrender of six brothers, then executed them and sent their heads to Wuchang.
- The episode treats the killings as Sun Quan's removal of a deeply rooted local threat and assertion of central authority, while its own sequence also shows that Shi Hui's open resistance supplied the immediate pretext.

## Key Quotes

> “拥有地方实权，却不直接占据交州刺史之位。” - the episode's compressed explanation of Shi Xie's political positioning.

> “只要认罪投降……可以保全财产和家族。” - the surrender assurance attributed to Lyu Dai's approach through Shi Kuang.

- [[SunQuan|孙权]], [[LyuDai|吕岱]], and [[LayeredLocalEliteDemobilization|分层式地方武力拆解]] - administrative division, replacement appointments, induced surrender, and execution dismantle the Shi network.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is recorded. The episode extends Jiaozhi's existing Eastern Han profile from revolt and pacification into late-Han local dynasty formation and Sun-Wu consolidation.
- Interpretive qualification: the host emphasizes Shi Xie's restraint and Shi Hui's failure to inherit it, but the sequence also supports a structural reading in which Sun Quan intended to reclaim appointment and territorial authority after the broker who sustained the prior compromise died.
- Source-scope caution: the exact four-commandery appointment process, claims about social stability and scholarly importance, the full tribute inventory, motives behind the Guangzhou/Jiaozhou division, dialogue, assurances, and the scope of later Shi-family deaths require broader textual comparison.
- Moral qualification: describing the outcome as centralization does not neutralize Lyu Dai's alleged false assurance or the killing of surrendered brothers.
