# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》393-1｜汉武帝驾崩后，俩儿子是怎么争皇权的？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens the early [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]] reign in 86 BCE with a brief Yizhou rebellion notice, amnesty, and floods before turning to [[LiuDanYanKingWesternHan|燕王刘旦]]'s first post-[[HanWudi|汉武帝]] succession challenge. Liu Dan receives the official death edict but notices an apparently abnormal seal package, suspects a Chang'an palace crisis, and begins treating the child emperor's accession under [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] as possibly illegitimate.

The durable synthesis is that Liu Dan's later rebellion begins as a succession-legitimacy grievance before it becomes a coordinated anti-Huo plot. The episode backfills his political psychology: once Liu Hong dies and [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]] kills himself after the [[WuguIncidentWesternHan|巫蛊之祸]], Liu Dan sees himself as the senior surviving son with a plausible claim, but [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] punishes his request to enter Chang'an as a thinly veiled bid for proximity to power. After Wudi's death, Liu Dan sends trusted men to Chang'an, learns that an eight-year-old has been installed by Huo Guang and other regents, and turns suspicion about seals, age, and regent control into a broader claim that the throne should have been his.

## Key Claims

- In 86 BCE, Yizhou-region groups rebel and are defeated by a Han expedition under Shuiheng duwei Lu Pihu, while the year also includes a general amnesty and major rain damage.
- Liu Dan receives Han Wudi's death edict without mourning and focuses instead on the seal package, treating a physical sign as evidence that Chang'an may be unstable.
- Liu Dan's ambition is rooted in changed succession order: Liu Hong dies young, Liu Ju dies after the Wugu affair, and Liu Dan becomes the eldest surviving son in his own political imagination.
- Han Wudi had already read Liu Dan's request to serve near him in Chang'an as succession ambition, executing the messenger and cutting three counties from Liu Dan's fief.
- After Wudi's death, Liu Dan sends Shou Xichang, Sun Zongzhi, Wang Ru, and others to Chang'an to gather intelligence under the cover of mourning.
- The envoys report that the new emperor is eight and has been installed by Huo Guang and other regents; Liu Dan interprets this as possible forged-edict politics.
- Han Zhao's later gifts and household increase do not pacify Liu Dan because he treats the throne, not compensation, as the disputed object.

## Key Quotes

> "印封那么小" - the suspicious detail Liu Dan uses to question the imperial death edict.

> "天下本来就应该是自己的" - the episode's summary of Liu Dan's reaction to Han Zhao's conciliatory gifts.
