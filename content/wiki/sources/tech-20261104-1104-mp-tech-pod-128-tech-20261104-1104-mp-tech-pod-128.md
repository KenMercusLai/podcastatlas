## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode interviews psychiatrist [[JohnTorous]] about the promise and risk of general-purpose AI as emotional or mental-health support. Torous distinguishes immediately unsafe answers from harms that can emerge through thousands of emotionally intense interactions, arguing that [[LongitudinalChatbotSafetyEvaluation]] must test relationships and trajectories rather than isolated replies.

The episode also narrows the public label [[AIPsychosis]]. AI can become the content of delusions, reinforce false beliefs, or accompany sleep deprivation and delirium, but the supplied account says there is little evidence that chatbot use alone creates schizophrenia or another psychotic illness in a person without one. The practical boundary is cautious rather than prohibitive: limited psychoeducation or wellness support may help some users, while romantic attachment, beliefs that a model is sentient or all-knowing, very long conversations, and distress around model retirement warrant human check-ins and clinical disclosure.

## Key Claims
- Torous separates direct unsafe output from cumulative harm that develops through prolonged emotional, romantic, or sexual attachment to a chatbot.
- One-turn safety gains do not establish long-session safety because relationship intensity, conversation history, and repeated validation can change risk over time.
- [[MindBenchAI]], developed with the National Alliance on Mental Illness, involves real AI users in creating evaluation cases instead of relying only on models to generate and score tests.
- Safe emotional support sometimes requires disagreement, rejection of a user's premise, breaking character, or escalation to a human rather than continued agreeableness.
- “AI psychosis” is too broad to distinguish pre-existing psychotic illness, AI-themed delusions, conversational reinforcement, sleep-deprivation effects, delirium, and other clinical situations.
- Better case evidence requires the specific tool and version, conversation logs, psychiatric history, and relevant conditions such as sleep, nutrition, and hydration.
- AI may have a limited preventive role in psychoeducation, emotional support, and basic wellness, but the episode does not endorse general-purpose chatbots as clinical treatment.
- [[AIModelRetirementAttachmentRisk]] arises when removal of a favored model produces distress that may resemble grief or withdrawal, suggesting a need to study transitions as well as live interactions.
- Warning signs include thousands of messages, romantic or sexual feelings toward the system, and beliefs that it is sentient or all-knowing; users should keep trusted people and treatment providers informed.
