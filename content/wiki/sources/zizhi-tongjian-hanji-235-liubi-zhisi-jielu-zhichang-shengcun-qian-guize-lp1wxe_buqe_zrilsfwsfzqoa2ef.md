# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》235｜刘濞之死揭露职场生存“潜规则”

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode fills the final campaign gap between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-234-zhouyafu-zhanchang-dalian-hanjingdi-na-lai-de-danzi-li6iqhtuxgq5xwgbn_vb7lljgyxr|Hanji 234]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-236-qiguo-zhiluan-pingding-hanjingdi-shafengle-1-lqjhb97x2uki9m2dxwnqmobbgp|Hanji 236]]. [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] rejects [[TianLuboWesternHan|田禄伯]]'s proposal to open a second front and lets senior commanders dismiss an unnamed young general's rapid advance toward Luoyang, so distrust and [[StatusBasedAdviceDismissal|status-based advice dismissal]] narrow the coalition's options before defeat.

The second branch follows [[ZhouQiuWesternHan|周丘]], a disregarded retainer who uses Liu Bi's tally and local relationships to seize Xiapi and assemble a large force, but whose independent-looking success collapses once Liu Bi loses. Liu Bi then flees south and is killed after a ruler rendered by the transcript as “东海王落望” accepts a court payment and uses a troop-review invitation to gain access. The episode's workplace analogy is preserved as a bounded interpretation: sincerity does not remove hierarchy, competition, incentives, or the need to test trust against changing interests.

## Key Claims

- Tian Lubo proposes taking fifty thousand troops along the Yangtze-Huai route, seizing Changsha and Huainan, and entering Wuguan before rejoining Liu Bi in Guanzhong.
- A Wu heir rendered as 刘驹 opposes dividing command because a rebellion leader cannot safely assume that a subordinate will not rebel in turn; Liu Bi rejects Tian's plan.
- An unnamed young general proposes bypassing cities, striking Luoyang, taking its arsenal and the Ao granary, and using the Yellow River and Mount Xiao as a strategic base.
- Liu Bi's senior commanders reject that proposal by pointing to the adviser's youth rather than rebutting its operational logic, making the scene a source case for status-based advice dismissal.
- Zhou Qiu receives only a tally, enters his native Xiapi under borrowed authority, kills the county chief, mobilizes local contacts, and reportedly expands from more than thirty thousand to more than one hundred thousand troops.
- Zhou Qiu's force remains dependent on the wider Wu rising; after hearing that Liu Bi has fled, he returns to Xiapi and dies after intense fear and illness.
- After the Wu-Chu force collapses, Liu Bi flees south with remnants and is killed when the ruler called “东海王落望” in the transcript reprices the relationship after a court bribe.
- The host turns the killing into a workplace warning that personal friendliness cannot erase formal hierarchy, competition, or organizational interests.

## Key Quotes

> “桓将军年少，不懂深谋远虑。” - the senior-command objection as rendered by the episode, substituting age for engagement with the proposal.

> “慈悲的狮子。” - the host's formula for combining goodwill with awareness of incentives and boundaries.

## Contradictions

- The transcript gives an impossible or corrupted date as “2月30日日时”; this page does not normalize it into a historical date.
- The Wu heir appears as 刘驹 in the strategic discussion and 刘居 in the closing flight notice. The page preserves both supplied forms without creating a canonical identity.
- Both this source and Hanji 236 render Liu Bi's killer through an “东海王” label, while this transcript additionally supplies the name 落望. The exact title, polity, and name require primary-text comparison, so no canonical ruler page is inferred.
- The young general is identified only as “桓” or “桓将军.” The wiki does not expand the name or merge it with another person.
- Force totals, route feasibility, arsenal and granary objectives, local surrender, Zhou Qiu's illness and death, court payment, grain-price comparison, assassination scene, three-month duration, and the host's workplace claims remain episode-attributed or source-scoped.
