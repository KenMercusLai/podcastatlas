## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory|Revisionist History]] episode has [[MalcolmGladwell]] use [[YunaWong]] and [[DavidShlapak]]'s [[RAND]] war games to argue that simulation is most useful as an assumption test rather than a prediction machine. Baltic, collapsed-North-Korea, and AI-enabled East Asian scenarios make [[OperationalFrictionInStrategicPlanning]] concrete and show how independent actors, automated defenses, and commercial systems can create [[AutomatedDeterrenceInteractionRisk]].

The central synthesis is [[StrategicWarGaming]]: repeated, deliberately messy play can expose plausible events, interactions, and implementation constraints that expert analysis did not contain. The episode is strongest as an argument for intellectual humility and weakest as evidence that any particular scenario would occur, because it does not systematically examine participant bias, scenario design, sensitivity, or external validity.

## Key Claims

- War games prepare participants for surprise by surfacing possibilities they had not considered; they do not predict exact futures.
- [[ThomasSchelling]]'s observation that people cannot enumerate what would never occur to them supplies the episode's rationale for interactive simulation.
- [[DavidShlapak]] uses dice to interrupt outcomes driven only by participants' prior logic and experience.
- Repeated Baltic games in the episode let Russian forces prevail despite NATO's greater aggregate strength, showing that an uncomfortable possibility deserved examination rather than proving a real-world result.
- A North Korean state-collapse game turns nuclear-site security into a problem of border crossing, manpower, refugees, congestion, chemical protection, casualties, records, scientists, and years of work.
- [[YunaWong]]'s AI-enabled East Asia game shows an automated defense responding to an unanticipated North Korean missile, a commercial shipping system continuing into a blockade, and ambiguous anti-submarine activity escalating into human casualties.
- The episode argues that advanced technology may connect independent goals and compress response time without eliminating ambiguity, emotion, or strategic disorder.
- Repeated simulation is presented as an institutional reality check that can weaken wishful thinking before an actual crisis.

## Key Quotes

> "reality check" - Gladwell's description of what a war game can provide to an institution.

> "events they had never considered" - the episode's concise statement of simulation's learning target.

> "messy" - Wong's characterization of war games and the disorder from which participants must extract insight.
