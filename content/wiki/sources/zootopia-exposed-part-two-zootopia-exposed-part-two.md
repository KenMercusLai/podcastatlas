# Zootopia Exposed! (Part Two)

## Summary
This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode argues that [[Zootopia2Film|Zootopia 2]] may use Gary De'Snake's stolen family invention, Lynxley Manor's Disney-castle imagery, corporate jokes, and the line “it matters to him” to acknowledge [[GaryGoldman]]'s unsuccessful claim that Disney appropriated his Zootopia pitch. [[MalcolmGladwell]] then connects screenwriter [[JaredBush]]'s restoration of excluded reptiles to the Bush family's history of challenging racial, religious, and sexual exclusion, especially [[LesterBushJr]]'s archival challenge to the Mormon priesthood ban. The interpretation is coherent but circumstantial: the supplied note contains no confirmation from Bush or [[TheWaltDisneyCompany|Disney]] of the proposed intent.

## Key Claims
- Gary De'Snake's claim that the Lynxleys stole his family's weather-wall patents closely resembles Goldman's allegation that Disney rejected and later appropriated his Zootopia project.
- Lynxley Manor's castle-and-fireworks composition, a Ratatouille gag, bootleg Disney films, and weather reporter Bob Tiger—voiced by [[BobIger]]—are treated as coordinated corporate self-reference rather than isolated jokes.
- Judy Hopps's answer that the dispute “matters to” Gary is read as [[SymbolicCreativeRestitution]]: acknowledgment without a changed verdict, payment, or legal victory.
- The guests call the hypothesized maneuver a “perfect literary crime” because Disney resources and approvals would be used to criticize Disney's treatment of a creator.
- Lester Bush Jr.'s research located Black priesthood holders under Joseph Smith and argued that the exclusion arose under Brigham Young rather than from an original revelation.
- The episode associates the church's later reversal with Lester's historical reframing while also recording that he was shunned before receiving delayed private and ceremonial recognition.
- Gladwell interprets Jared Bush's story of returning reptiles to Zootopia alongside his grandfather's credit-union work and his uncle Larry's gay activism as a family pattern of defending excluded groups.
- Goldman says learning this family history gave him peace; he sent Bush a handwritten letter and received no response.

## Key Quotes
> “It matters to him.” — Judy Hopps's reply, interpreted by the speakers as recognition directed toward Gary Goldman.

> “Perfect literary crime.” — the guests' phrase for their theory of an authorized Disney film carrying an internal critique of Disney.

- The earlier film source renders one lynx character as “Pawbert,” while this source uses “Paubert”; the spelling difference remains source-scoped.
- The Goldman parallel, coordinated anti-Disney message, legal-review history, Bush family influence, church causation, dialogue, and visual details remain episode-reported because the supplied document is a structured summary rather than the film, screenplay, court record, archival article, church record, or direct testimony from Jared Bush or Disney.
