# The Sony Hack | The Mistakes Series

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode has former [[SonyPictures]] CEO [[MichaelLynton]] trace the [[SonyPicturesHack2014|2014 Sony Pictures hack]] back to his exceptional approval of [[TheInterviewFilm|The Interview]] without the studio's normal greenlight review. The episode presents the decision as an interaction among [[DecisionGuardrailBypass]], [[SchemaContextMismatch]], and [[ThreatChannelBlindness]]: enthusiasm and a desire to belong displaced a reliable process, missing review prevented geopolitical objections from surfacing, and later advice treated North Korean retaliation mainly as a physical threat while overlooking cyber capability.

The case distinguishes retrospective accountability from simple self-condemnation. Lynton accepts responsibility for approving the film and bypassing process, but the episode also identifies distributed institutional causes, incomplete expert advice, and uneven ownership among executives and creatives. Because the supplied document is a structured summary rather than a transcript or forensic record, attribution, causation, losses, dialogue, motives, and counterfactual claims remain episode-scoped.

## Key Claims

- In November 2014, attackers disabled Sony Pictures systems and exposed corporate email, employee information, scripts, and other confidential material; the source reports loss estimates above $100 million.
- Sony normally reviewed films through a cross-functional greenlight meeting involving finance, legal, marketing, public policy, and other departments.
- After an unusually successful table read, Lynton approved *The Interview* immediately and did not use that normal review process, an exception he says he had not made during more than a decade in the role.
- Lynton believes legal or public-policy review likely would have raised the North Korea–Japan relationship and that Sony's Japanese parent leadership would have rejected the project.
- Lynton and collaborators interpret his decision through a childhood-formed schema: early isolation in the Netherlands left him wanting acceptance from an admired group, and that need resurfaced in the enthusiastic creative room.
- RAND Corporation and U.S. State Department experts reportedly discounted North Korea's threats, while Lynton framed retaliation chiefly as physical harm and missed the cyber channel.
- After the breach, major theater chains declined to show the film; [[EricSchmidt]], [[PatrickCollison]], and [[Stripe]] helped enable online release and ticketing, while independent cinemas screened it.
