# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》396-2｜汉武帝之子，刘据“巫蛊之案”后复活了？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]]'s early reign before the later anti-[[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] coalition sequence. It begins with [[ShangguanTaihouWesternHan|上官氏]]'s installation as a very young empress, southwestern revolt, court appointments, and rewards to Han Zhao's maternal relatives, then centers on a man at the north gate of [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]] claiming to be [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]] after the [[WuguIncidentWesternHan|巫蛊之祸]].

The durable synthesis is that [[JunBuyiWesternHan|隽不疑]] resolves the crisis by refusing to let identity verification paralyze the court. He orders the claimant arrested and argues that even if Liu Ju had survived, a fugitive who had offended the former emperor could not simply reenter political order as a usable claimant. The later confession identifies the man as [[ChengFangsuiWesternHan|程方遂]], a diviner from Xiagui, but the episode's political weight lies in how a claimant-impostor can turn rumor, crowd attention, and uncertain succession memory into an immediate legitimacy problem.

## Key Claims

- Han Zhao's early reign remains politically delicate because he is young, was not long groomed as successor, and depends on Huo Guang's stabilizing regency.
- Shangguan Shi's installation as empress, southwestern military disorder, Li Zhong's judicial failure, Shangguan An's promotion, and rewards to Han Zhao's maternal relatives show a court already balancing household, frontier, and office questions.
- The man claiming to be Liu Ju creates danger because Liu Ju's death after the Wugu affair remains emotionally and politically charged, while a visible crowd at the palace gate turns the identity claim into public disorder risk.
- Han Zhao initially orders senior officials to inspect the claimant, but they hesitate because the man appears similar enough to the dead crown prince to make denial risky.
- Jun Buyi's decisive move reframes the issue: whether the claimant is real is less urgent than preserving the authority of the former emperor, the current emperor, and the existing state order.
- The claimant confesses before torture that he is Cheng Fangsui, a diviner who was told by a former Liu Ju attendant that he resembled the crown prince and then attempted to exchange resemblance for wealth and rank.
- Cheng Fangsui is executed by waist-cutting, and the episode uses the case as a warning about both fraud and unchecked desire.

## Key Quotes

> "即使真太子也仍是国家罪人" - the episode's summary of Jun Buyi's crisis logic.

> "这是一桩诈骗案" - the host's framing after the claimant confesses as Cheng Fangsui.
