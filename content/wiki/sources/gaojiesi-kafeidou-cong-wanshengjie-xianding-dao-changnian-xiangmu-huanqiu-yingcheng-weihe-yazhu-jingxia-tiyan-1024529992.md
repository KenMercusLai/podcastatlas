## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode combines short updates on Apple AI, Chinese airline capacity, [[Netflix]], and [[CharacterAI|Character AI]] with a deeper explanation of [[UniversalStudios|Universal Studios]]' horror business. Its central synthesis is that Halloween programs can smooth an autumn attendance gap, extend operating hours and guest spending, reuse horror-production resources, and test local or licensed IP before larger commitments; the same format faces a structural [[ImmersiveExperienceThroughputTradeoff|immersion-throughput tradeoff]] and [[LicensedIPControlRisk|licensed-IP control risk]].

## Key Claims
- The episode reports that Apple planned a December mainland-China launch of basic [[AppleIntelligence|Apple Intelligence]] functions without the newer Siri assistant, while domestic phone makers already offered their own AI services. It also says more than 150 apps resisted allowing a Doubao–Nubia phone agent to operate them, making the timing and app count source-scoped current claims.
- China's civil-aviation authority is described as limiting new carriers or added flights on routes that have reached carrier-count targets or missed load-factor thresholds, against a backdrop of excess domestic capacity, slow international recovery, high-speed-rail competition, lower fares, and wider airline losses.
- [[Netflix]] is reported to have cut roughly 1,000 jobs as subscription growth and viewing-time growth slowed, then to be adding sports, video podcasts, and a future theatrical release in response to content and distribution pressure. The figures, timing, and causal interpretation remain episode-reported.
- The episode says [[CharacterAI|Character AI]] entered bankruptcy protection after losing roughly US$8–9 million per month, while 40 state attorneys general sought stronger protection for minors and private conversation data. Reported engagement comparisons do not establish safety or commercial viability.
- [[UniversalStudios|Universal Studios]] uses Halloween haunted houses, street interactions, and night shows across Beijing, Orlando, Hollywood, and Japan, while Universal Horror Unleashed in Las Vegas turns the seasonal format into a year-round, stand-alone attraction planned for replication in Chicago.
- [[SeasonalEventDemandSmoothing]] works here by placing a distinctive event between the summer peak and colder weather, extending some park days into the night, supporting separate admission in Orlando, and increasing time available for food, retail, and nearby spending.
- Universal can reuse horror expertise, performers, props, sets, and production processes while rotating the specific IP and localizing the program for each market.
- [[SeasonalIPMarketTesting]] lets the park try external and local properties such as 《盗墓笔记》, 《第五人格》, 《王者荣耀》, and 《原神》 in a temporary format before deciding whether traffic, spending, or repeat visits justify longer-lived investment.
- Haunted houses increasingly operate like detailed small theaters, but actor interaction, opening scenes, close-range scares, and time to inspect scenery reduce capacity. Continuous guest streams raise throughput while allowing later visitors to see scares early and weakening individualized immersion.
- [[LicensedIPControlRisk]] grows when scenery, characters, and merchandise require rights-holder approval and when renewal price, term, or availability can change. The source therefore favors balancing immediate demand from external franchises with the long-term control of owned horror properties.
