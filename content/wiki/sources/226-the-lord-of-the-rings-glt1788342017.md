# 226. The Lord of the Rings

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reads [[TheHobbit|The Hobbit]] and [[TheLordOfTheRings|The Lord of the Rings]] as works in which [[JRRTolkien|J.R.R. Tolkien]] placed modern, domesticated hobbits inside an archaic mythic world. It argues that the later novel is not simple escape: the ring, Sauron's surveillance, aerial terror, the Fellowship's refusal of mastery, and the damaged homecoming all register twentieth-century war, totalitarian power, moral corruption, Catholic sacrifice, and postwar disappointment. The episode also holds Tolkien's reactionary politics beside his hatred of industrial destruction and treats contemporary race criticism as a necessary qualification rather than a reason to flatten either the books or their historical setting.

## Key Claims

- Bilbo's modern habits—comfort, tea, clocks, gardens, respectability, and reluctance toward foreign trouble—create the productive contrast between interwar English domesticity and Tolkien's ancient-seeming mythic world.
- [[TheHobbit]] remains a powerful children's quest story assembled from northern myth, Anglo-Saxon poetry, dragons, dwarves, maps, keys, lost kingdoms, and imperial adventure conventions.
- [[TheLordOfTheRings]] transforms a requested sequel into a darker account of war and power in which the ring must be destroyed because using an enemy's instruments of domination reproduces the enemy's moral logic.
- [[CorruptingPowerRenunciation]] joins mastery, addiction, hubris, sacrifice, and the voluntary surrender of strength; the hosts connect it to Tolkien's wartime suspicion that defeating evil with overwhelming destructive power could mean “conquering Sauron with the ring.”
- Gollum and Frodo complicate a simple good-versus-evil reading: their convergence shows corruption as a possibility within persons, while the story retains a non-relativistic moral order.
- Tolkien's Catholicism appears structurally rather than through churches or direct allegory, especially in sacrifice, pity, hope beyond apparent defeat, the renunciation of power, and the March 25 destruction of the ring.
- [[NorthernCourage]] and Christian hope coexist with loss: victory ends Sauron's threat but cannot prevent the elves' departure, the fading of beauty, or the long defeat of history.
- [[AntiIndustrialPastoralism]] is clearest in the Scouring of the Shire, where returning hobbits encounter felled trees, pollution, surveillance, closed inns, petty officials, and collaboration, preventing homecoming from becoming an uncomplicated victory.
- The episode presents Tolkien as politically reactionary and skeptical of democracy, yet also as environmentally prescient and unexpectedly compatible with later countercultural and multicultural readings.
- Its discussion of race distinguishes medieval categories and biblical or northern light-dark symbolism from modern racial theory while acknowledging that orcs and eastern or southern allies of Sauron can read awkwardly and harmfully now.

## Key Quotes

> “a long defeat” — Tolkien's formulation for history, used by the hosts to connect loss with glimpses of final victory.

> “conquer Sauron with the ring” — the episode's summary of Tolkien's warning about adopting destructive power to defeat destructive power.

> “goodies and baddies” — the simplified reading rejected by the hosts through Gollum and Frodo's moral convergence.

## Connections

- [[JRRTolkien|J.R.R. Tolkien]] - author whose philology, Catholicism, war experience, politics, and environmental anxieties organize the episode.
- [[TheHobbit]] and [[TheLordOfTheRings]] - children's quest and later epic whose different tones reveal the growth of Tolkien's project.
- [[CSLewis|C. S. Lewis]] - Christian literary counterpart used to distinguish Tolkien's embedded theology from direct allegory.
- [[CorruptingPowerRenunciation]] - central moral logic of refusing the ring rather than mastering it.
- [[AntiIndustrialPastoralism]] - Shire-centered critique of machinery, pollution, bureaucracy, and damaged homecoming.
- [[NorthernCourage]] - pagan-inflected courage before known loss joined to Catholic hope.
- [[NorseMythology]] and [[MythicSourceLayering]] - northern and medieval materials transformed into modern fantasy rather than copied as one-to-one sources.
- [[FirstWorldWar]] - biographical and imaginative background for changed return, loss, and mechanized destruction.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing Tolkien branch was found. The episode deepens its war, religious, political, and environmental dimensions rather than overturning the existing philological and mythic profile.
- The source's readings of the ring, Sauron's eye, the Nazgul, the Scouring, race, and multicultural adaptation are interpretive and source-scoped; they should not be treated as exhaustive authorial intent.
- Tolkien's reactionary politics and medieval categories do not erase environmental or cross-cultural possibilities in the work, but later resonance likewise does not remove troubling racialized imagery.
