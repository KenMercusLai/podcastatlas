# EP113-充满偏见的互联网时代，我们更接近真相了嘛？

## Summary
This [[WushichaYanjiusuo|无时差研究所]] episode uses the 2020 [[XiaoZhan|肖战]] fan-reporting controversy and the loss of access to [[ArchiveOfOurOwn|AO3]] in China to discuss fandom, group identity, recommendation systems, reporting, rumor, and public speech. Its central synthesis is that [[FandomCollectiveAction|饭圈集体行动]] can turn affection into data, consumption, reputation defense, and coordinated pressure, while [[WeaponizedReporting|举报武器化]] and [[VerificationCostAsymmetry|信息核查成本不对称]] help factional claims outrun careful judgment. The hosts recommend cross-group exposure and continued expression, but present these as difficult personal practices rather than cures for harassment or polarization.

## Key Claims
- Fandom is heterogeneous: professional fans, casual followers, younger participants, and adult organizers have different motives and capacities, so the most visible behavior should not be treated as a complete demographic profile.
- [[FandomCollectiveAction]] links symbolic identity to data production, buying, coordinated promotion, anti-fan work, and brand value; a traffic idol can benefit from this system without controlling every participant.
- The hosts treat the Xiao Zhan/AO3 dispute as reciprocal escalation: reporting attributed to fans is followed by counter-mobilization against the idol, fandom, and endorsed brands, so criticism can itself become a group spectacle.
- Interest communities predate recommendation feeds, but sustained same-side interaction and personalized ranking can jointly narrow the usable evidence environment. The source therefore extends [[InformationCocoon]] without proving that every feed is sealed.
- [[WeaponizedReporting]] separates legitimate reporting of unlawful conduct from mass reporting, archival digging, labeling, and rule language used to exclude disliked speech or communities.
- Fandom terms such as “黑粉,” “带节奏,” and “拉踩” compress disagreement into attributed camp membership; this extends [[LanguageCompressionLabeling]] from description into active boundary production.
- Continued public disagreement may counter a spiral of silence by leaving a visible alternative for bystanders, although exposure to abuse and reporting can rationally push speakers toward silence or protective withdrawal.
- [[VerificationCostAsymmetry]] helps rumors spread: forwarding a vivid, identity-confirming story is cheap, while checking primary material, foreign-language sources, or later corrections requires time and skill.
- Deliberately seeking different viewpoints can preserve some openness, but opposing exposure does not automatically produce understanding and may intensify [[AffectivePolarization]] in hostile formats.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode's strong recommendation-system account is qualified by later wiki evidence that information environments can be leaky through incidental exposure and multi-platform use; disagreement exposure can also worsen hostility rather than correct belief.
- The source is a structured summary of a 2020 conversational episode, not a platform audit, demographic survey, legal analysis, or verified reconstruction of the reporting chain. Claims about fan ages, who caused AO3 to become inaccessible, platform mechanics, corporate coordination, particular rumors, and long-run generational change remain attributed, anecdotal, or source-scoped.
