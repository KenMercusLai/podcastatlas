# GUEST SERIES | Dr. Andy Galpin: Optimal Nutrition & Supplementation for Fitness

## Summary
This sixth and final [[HubermanLab]] guest-series conversation has [[AndrewHuberman]] and [[AndyGalpin]] place performance supplements beneath sleep, hydration, whole-food nutrition, appropriate training, and recovery. [[PerformanceSupplementHierarchy]] separates foundational practices from fuel, stimulants, fatigue blockers, and recovery aids while favoring targeted single-ingredient products and third-party testing. The episode also extends [[CreatineMonohydrateEvidence]], [[ExerciseHydrationPerformanceBoundary]], [[DailyHydrationTiming]], [[TrainingFuelTimingContext]], [[ExerciseRecoveryReadiness]], and [[SleepAnxietyLoop]] with context-dependent dosing, timing, and safety boundaries.

## Key Claims
- [[PerformanceSupplementHierarchy]] treats supplements as optional tools layered onto sleep, nutrition, hydration, training, and recovery rather than substitutes for those foundations.
- Creatine monohydrate is Galpin's highest-return general recommendation, usually described here as three to five grams daily and requiring consistent use over weeks rather than an acute dose; claims about cognition, bone density, concussion, and neurodegenerative disease remain explicitly qualified.
- Performance aids are grouped as fuel, stimulants, or fatigue blockers, with creatine, caffeine, beta-alanine, sodium bicarbonate, beetroot, rhodiola, omega-3s, glutamine, and tart cherry differing in purpose, evidence, timing, and need for repeated use.
- [[ExerciseHydrationPerformanceBoundary]] is strengthened by warnings on both dehydration and overhydration, pre/post exercise weighing, estimated replacement of about 125% of exercise fluid loss, slow intake during activity, and sodium context.
- [[DailyHydrationTiming]] favors steady earlier-day intake and limiting large fluid loads near sleep; frequent nocturia can reflect excess intake but can also warrant assessment for sleep or medical causes.
- [[TrainingFuelTimingContext]] places total daily macronutrient intake above exact timing for many once-daily sessions, while carbohydrate availability matters more for long, intense, daily, or repeated same-day training.
- [[ExerciseRecoveryReadiness]] distinguishes useful post-training inflammation from later recovery support and says tapering training volume should precede adding supplements when hard training stops producing improvement.
- [[SleepAnxietyLoop]] gains a practical orthosomnia rule: if tracker feedback becomes stressful or compulsive, delay or reduce score checking rather than letting one estimate define the day.
- Single-ingredient products and independent testing make dose, effects, adverse reactions, and banned-substance risk easier to evaluate; caffeine and symptom-masking products can also create dependence without resolving the underlying problem.

## Contradictions
- The episode's usual three-to-five-gram creatine recommendation is lower than the 10-15 gram range for heavier people in [[science-supported-tools-to-accelerate-your-fitness-goals-scim7037527631]]. The summaries do not provide comparative evidence sufficient to harmonize the doses, so both remain source-scoped.
- Galpin says fasted training can work when a session is not limited by fuel availability, while [[female-specific-exercise-nutrition-for-health-performance-longevity-dr-stacy-sims-scim3511503121]] warns that routine fasted training in active women can compound low energy availability and endocrine or recovery problems. These are context-dependent boundaries rather than a settled universal conflict.
