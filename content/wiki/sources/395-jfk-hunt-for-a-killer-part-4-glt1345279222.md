# 395. JFK: Hunt for a Killer (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode reconstructs the first hours after the [[KennedyAssassination|assassination of John F. Kennedy]], moving among Parkland Hospital, the Texas School Book Depository, Oak Cliff, Dallas police headquarters, and Air Force One. It follows the evidence that led the [[DallasPoliceDepartment|Dallas Police Department]] from the Dealey Plaza shooting through [[JDTippit|J. D. Tippit]]'s murder to the arrest and charging of [[LeeHarveyOswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]], while showing how fear of a wider plot shaped [[LyndonBJohnson|Lyndon B. Johnson]]'s succession decisions. The episode also introduces [[JackRuby|Jack Ruby]] as an emotional, erratic police hanger-on whose easy access foreshadowed the custody failure covered in Part 5.

## Key Claims

- Police found a sixth-floor sniper's nest, a long paper bag, three spent cartridge cases, and a rifle in the Texas School Book Depository while broadcasting an eyewitness-based suspect description.
- Multiple witnesses saw a man shoot Dallas officer J. D. Tippit in Oak Cliff; the subsequent theater arrest, revolver struggle, and suspect behavior strengthened police suspicion of Oswald.
- The A. Hidell identification found in Oswald's wallet matched the name used to order the Mannlicher-Carcano rifle, joining documentary and physical evidence.
- Officials initially considered communist, Cuban, Ku Klux Klan, and right-wing plots, showing that conspiracy hypotheses began inside government during an unresolved security crisis.
- Johnson delayed the public death announcement, took the oath aboard Air Force One, and prioritized a rapid return to Washington because the scope of the threat was unknown.
- Removing Kennedy's body over the Dallas medical examiner's objection broke the expected local autopsy process and later looked suspicious, although urgency, security, and concern for Jacqueline Kennedy provide a non-conspiratorial explanation.
- Ruby moved easily through a press-crowded police headquarters, exposing an access-control weakness before he killed Oswald during the next day's transfer.
- By late evening, police and prosecutors believed the Depository evidence, Tippit case, rifle trace, Hidell alias, Oswald's departure, and interrogation conduct justified charging him with Kennedy's murder.

## Key Quotes
