# Pushkin Goes to the Olympics

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] compilation has [[MalcolmGladwell]] connect three Pushkin Olympic previews: the institutional exclusion challenged by [[KathrineSwitzer]] and [[BobbiGibb]], [[SylviaBlemker]] and [[SpringbokAnalytics]]'s MRI/AI muscle analysis, and the celebrated [[JesseOwens]]–[[LutzLong]] friendship story. Together they show how Olympic knowledge is shaped by eligibility rules, journalism, measurement technology, and emotionally attractive historical narrative.

The women-running and muscle-imaging excerpts add new evidence to the wiki. The Owens–Long excerpt is non-independent preview provenance for material covered more fully in later episodes: it presents the inspirational account and ends before the archival correction already synthesized in [[SportsReconciliationMythmaking]].

## Key Claims

- Sensational accounts of the 1928 Olympic women's 800 meters portrayed competitors as medically unfit even though all nine finished and the first six reportedly beat the prior world record; the event was then removed for three decades.
- [[KathrineSwitzer]] entered the 1967 Boston Marathon after a 31-mile training run and an eligibility check found no explicit ban on women; a race official physically tried to remove her number, but she continued and finished.
- [[BobbiGibb]] had already run Boston unofficially and was ahead of Switzer in 1967, so Switzer's importance lies in a numbered entry and visible confrontation rather than being the first woman to run a marathon or Boston.
- [[SpringbokAnalytics]] uses MRI and artificial intelligence to build three-dimensional muscle maps that can identify side-to-side and muscle-specific imbalances hidden by aggregate strength tests.
- Repeated imaging and electromyography feedback can support targeted rehabilitation, but the episode supplies no comparative outcome study, model-error data, or independent validation.
- The final preview recounts Owens's later claim that Long advised him during Berlin long-jump qualification and that their friendship continued through wartime letters, then closes by warning that the story may be too good to be true.
