# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》184｜历史恐怖片：刘邦的“彭氏肉酱”（1）

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode first closes a loose end from [[HanXin|韩信]]'s death: [[KuaiChe|蒯彻 / 蒯通]] admits that he once urged Han Xin to seek an independent position, yet escapes [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s threatened execution by arguing that people in the anti-Qin struggle served different masters. It then reconstructs [[PengYue|彭越]]'s fall. Peng Yue's illness and failure to appear personally for the [[ChenXi|陈豨]] campaign lead to rebuke, an officer's rejected rebellion proposal, and an accusation by a fugitive subordinate; Liu Bang initially strips and exiles him, but [[LuZhi|吕雉]] brings him back, urges that he is too dangerous to leave alive, and obtains a renewed accusation. Peng Yue is then executed with his clan, his body made into paste and distributed to the vassal kings as a warning.

## Key Claims
- Kuai Che's defense separates service to a former patron from treason against a later victor: he says that during Qin's collapse he knew Han Xin, not Liu Bang, and Liu Bang releases him.
- The source says Peng Yue did not initiate rebellion. He declined to accompany Liu Bang personally because of illness, sent a commander instead, planned to apologize after being rebuked, and rejected a subordinate's proposal to rise in arms.
- A Liang official who had committed an offense fled to the center and accused Peng Yue and the pro-rebellion commander; the episode treats this accusation as the decisive evidentiary turn while doubting that it established an actual plot.
- Liu Bang's first disposition was demotion, pardon, and exile to Qingyi rather than execution, showing that death was not presented as inevitable at the first judgment.
- Peng Yue's chance encounter with Lu Zhi reverses that outcome: after appearing to accept his request for exile nearer Changyi, she returns him to Luoyang and argues that leaving a proven hero in Shu would create a future threat.
- The episode attributes the renewed case to a Peng Yue attendant induced to accuse him again; the tingwei's request for clan extermination is approved, and the ban on collecting his remains extends punishment beyond death.
- Turning Peng Yue's body into paste and sending it to the vassal kings functions as political terror, not merely execution: it publicly demonstrates what can happen to a powerful non-Liu king under suspicion.
- The host compares Peng Yue with Han Xin as two cases in which rebellion charges, limited confrontation, and elimination of founding merit-holders form a repeated pattern, while acknowledging that the episode offers little competing source criticism.

## Key Quotes
> "各为其主" — the logic behind Kuai Che's successful defense before Liu Bang.

> "彭越从始至终没有实质性的谋反举动" — the host's central, source-scoped judgment on the case.

> "剁成肉酱，分送给各个诸侯王家" — the episode's description of the post-execution warning.

## Contradictions
- The source's claim that Peng Yue never materially rebelled conflicts with the in-story court finding that the accusation was established. The page preserves the difference between the reported verdict and the host's interpretation instead of treating either as independently verified.
- The episode attributes the decisive renewed prosecution to Lu Zhi, but Liu Bang approves the final extermination order; responsibility is therefore shared in the narrated sequence even though the host emphasizes Lu Zhi's initiative.
- The comparison with Han Xin is interpretive. Earlier wiki sources preserve uncertainty around Han Xin's alleged later conspiracy, so the parallel supports a recurring suspicion pattern rather than proving that the two cases were evidentially identical.
- No contradiction was found with Hanji 184 part 2 or Hanji 186: those episodes supply the ensuing public defense and the fear produced when Peng Yue's corpse-paste reaches Ying Bu.
