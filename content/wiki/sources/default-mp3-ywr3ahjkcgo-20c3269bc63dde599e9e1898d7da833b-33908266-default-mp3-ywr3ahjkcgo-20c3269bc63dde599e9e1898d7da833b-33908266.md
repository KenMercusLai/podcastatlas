## Summary

This [[PlanetMoney|Planet Money]] episode follows [[JohnSloss|John Sloss]] and [[CineticMedia|Cinetic Media]] at the 2026 [[SundanceFilmFestival|Sundance Film Festival]] to explain how independent films become theatrical or streaming releases. Historical cases and festival reporting show a concentrated [[IndependentFilmSalesMarket]] in which audience response, expectation management, test screenings, publicity, relationships, scarcity, negotiation, corporate approval, and luck help buyers assess uncertain demand. The episode also describes a softer post-pandemic market after the streaming wars, with tighter budgets, more approvals, and greater reliance on documentary sales.

## Key Claims

- Sundance selection is only an entry point: an independent film still needs a distributor or streaming buyer to reach a broad audience.
- Sales agents reduce uncertainty by coordinating screenings, publicity, market intelligence, negotiations, and release-plan comparisons.
- [[FestivalDemandSignaling]] can emerge through audience reactions, test screenings, awards, celebrity advocacy, reviews, exclusive events, and visible buyer interest, but each signal can also be staged or misread.
- Restrained publicity can let discovery amplify enthusiasm, while excessive hype can make a good screening feel disappointing.
- Once multiple buyers want a film, competition shifts from price alone toward theatrical commitments, awards support, outreach, and filmmaker fit.
- Post-pandemic theatrical weakness, mergers, tighter budgets, and talent-agency competition have reduced sellers' leverage relative to the streaming-war period.
- Successful sales create [[DeliberateLuckSurface|deliberate luck surfaces]]: teams cannot manufacture every breakthrough, but can create more chances to notice and use favorable surprises.

## Key Quotes

> “lean into” their luck — John Sloss's source-described lesson from the unexpected support that helped *Precious* secure distribution.

> “Sundance theater” — Eric Sloss's term for promotional showmanship that turns a film into a scarce, memorable buyer experience.
