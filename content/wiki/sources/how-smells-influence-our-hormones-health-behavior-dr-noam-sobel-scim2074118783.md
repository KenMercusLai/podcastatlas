# How Smells Influence Our Hormones, Health & Behavior | Dr. Noam Sobel

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] conversation has [[AndrewHuberman]] and [[NoamSobel]] examine human smell as a sensory, respiratory, social, and possible health-signal system. It extends [[Chemosensation]], [[OlfactoryTraining]], [[InhalationArousalLearning]], and [[HumanChemicalSignaling]] through scent tracking, the nasal cycle, smell loss, friendship-related body-odor similarity, fear odor, emotional tears, and sex-differentiated hexadecanal findings, while adding [[OlfactoryHealthSignal]] and [[DigitalOlfaction]]. The strongest synthesis is that human odor sampling can affect perception, physiology, and behavior without establishing a universal human pheromone system or making smell tests independently diagnostic.

## Key Claims
- Human olfaction includes orthonasal and retronasal routes, millions of receptors, distributed brain targets, rapid memory access, and trainable scent-tracking performance.
- [[InhalationArousalLearning]] gains nasal-cycle and respiratory-phase evidence: airflow alternates between nostrils, two-nostril separation can aid trail tracking, and some non-olfactory performance varies with inhalation.
- [[OlfactoryTraining]] has better support than alpha-lipoic acid in the episode's smell-loss discussion, but recovery depends on cause and time course.
- [[OlfactoryHealthSignal]] treats smell loss as clinically informative but non-specific; trauma, infection, congenital anosmia, and neurodegenerative disease require different interpretation.
- [[HumanChemicalSignaling]] includes post-handshake sampling, similar body odor among some close friends, fear-related autonomic effects, emotional-tear effects, and sex-differentiated responses to hexadecanal.
- Human reproductive-odor claims remain bounded: MHC-related preference, menstrual synchrony, and a human Bruce-like effect are suggestive, disputed, or explicitly hypothetical.
- [[DigitalOlfaction]] seeks mappings between molecular structure and perceived odor that could enable odor reproduction and, eventually, qualified diagnostic applications.
