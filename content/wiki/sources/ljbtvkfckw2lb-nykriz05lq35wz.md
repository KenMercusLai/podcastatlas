# 053 科幻外衣下的社会寓言｜大话卫斯理之《头发》

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode retells [[HairNiKuangNovel|《头发》]], in which [[WesleyNiKuang|卫斯理]] discovers that humans are discarded artificial bodies, hair once served as a knowledge-and-machine interface, and religious founders were emissaries offering a technological route back to their makers. The hosts praise [[NiKuang|倪匡]]'s suspense, conceptual audacity, and ability to provoke young readers while criticizing the novel's shallow religious synthesis, weak scientific grounding, inconsistent moral selection, and retreat from its strongest accusation: creators driven by immortality had manufactured and then condemned the beings they called sinful.

## Key Claims

- 《头发》 marks the hosts' account of the Wesley series' 1978 return after a six-year interruption and shifts the series toward a more explicit science-fiction mode.
- Shared dreams, displaced consciousness, burial, and a hidden Nepalese installation gradually turn death, reincarnation, and salvation into mechanisms for separating, screening, transferring, and receiving thought.
- The novel identifies Allah, Buddha, Christ, and Dao-coded figures as emissaries from an advanced civilization, but the hosts argue that this translation reduces distinct traditions to extraterrestrial rescue and immortality.
- Hair is imagined as a massively parallel interface for storing knowledge, exchanging information, and controlling machines; the episode treats this as an inventive fictional premise rather than biology.
- The expelled beings were originally bodies manufactured so their creators could transfer consciousness and prolong life; once those bodies developed autonomy and rebelled, the creators exiled them to Earth and labeled them sinners.
- [[CreatorResponsibilityEthics|Creator-responsibility ethics]] therefore reaches beyond abandonment: a maker who produces autonomous life for instrumental use cannot erase its own causal responsibility by judging the creation's rebellion and desire.
- The hosts see an unresolved contradiction when Wesley exposes the creators' hypocrisy but then accepts their authority to discuss eliminating earthly evil, even at the possible cost of many human lives.
- Despite scientific, theological, and ethical gaps, the novel's rapid reversals and large questions can function as an invitation to inquiry rather than a reliable answer about religion, consciousness, or human origins.

## Key Quotes

> “所谓罪人，根本就是你们制造出来的。” - Wesley's accusation after learning why the artificial bodies were created and exiled.

> “无人可以续写金庸小说。” - Ni Kuang's reported reason for declining a proposed continuation of another Jin Yong novel.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode extends the immortality branch from dependent medicine in 《不死药》 to consciousness transfer and manufactured replacement bodies.
- The creators condemn earthly desire and violence although their own wish for endless life produced the bodies, rebellion, exile, and later moral hierarchy; the novel exposes this inconsistency but does not resolve it.
- Wesley is accepted as a qualified returnee despite the hosts' account of his indirect responsibility for deaths and his later willingness to contemplate mass sacrifice in the name of removing evil.
- Identifications of the four emissaries, claims about Buddhism, Islam, Christianity, Daoism, hair biology, time dilation, publication history, authorial intention, and the novel's exact sequence remain episode-reported literary interpretation rather than independent theological, scientific, or bibliographic verification.
