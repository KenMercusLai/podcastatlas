# 279: Cameroon: The Slave General of Peter the Great

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs [[AvramGannibal|Avram Petrovich Gannibal]] from childhood enslavement and transfer through the Ottoman court into [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]]'s household, technical education, French military service, Russian engineering command, nobility, and descent to [[AlexanderPushkin|Alexander Pushkin]]. It presents a Cameroonian origin as more likely than the traditional Ethiopian account but repeatedly marks birthplace, elite parentage, and the meaning of “Fummo” as disputed. Its larger synthesis is [[RacializedAncestralClaimmaking]]: Russians, Cameroonians, Ethiopians, Soviet culture, and Black American writers made different claims on Gannibal and Pushkin as ancestry became literary, national, and racial memory.

## Key Claims

- [[AvramGannibal|Gannibal]] was probably born south of Lake Chad in what is now [[Cameroon]], kidnapped around 1703, taken to Constantinople, and acquired for [[PeterTheGreat|Peter]], but the exact birthplace and claim that he was a chief's son remain uncertain.
- Peter's treatment of Gannibal joined objectifying curiosity about a Black child to baptism, mathematics and engineering education, travel, and unusually high access to imperial service.
- Study in France, artillery training, combat experience, and technical ability made Gannibal a military engineer rather than merely a court curiosity; after returning to Russia, he taught mathematics and later rose to chief military engineer and general.
- Gannibal's fortunes depended on patronage and succession: Peter's death exposed him to Menshikov's hostility and a Siberian posting, while Elizabeth later restored him to favor and granted rank, estate, and serfs.
- Gannibal's own rise from enslavement to slaveholding nobility shows that exceptional mobility inside empire did not dismantle coercive hierarchy.
- [[AlexanderPushkin|Pushkin]] made his African great-grandfather part of his literary and personal identity through the unfinished *The Moor of Peter the Great*, while later audiences repeatedly recast that ancestry for Christian, Russian, Soviet, Cameroonian, Ethiopian, or Black diasporic purposes.
- The source treats the “Fummo” inscription, the rejected Gannibal portrait, Soviet blackface casting, and Harlem Renaissance interest as evidence that biography can become a contested racial-memory object.

## Key Quotes

> "Fummo" - disputed word on Gannibal's requested coat of arms, interpreted either through a Kotoko-language “homeland” claim or a Latin expansion about fortune changing his life.

> "The Moor of Peter the Great" - title of Pushkin's unfinished novel based on his great-grandfather.
