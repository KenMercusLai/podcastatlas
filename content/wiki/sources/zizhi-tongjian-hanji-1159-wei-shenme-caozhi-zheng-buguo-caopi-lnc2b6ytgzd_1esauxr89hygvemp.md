## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode reconstructs the [[CaoPi|曹丕]]–[[CaoZhi|曹植]] succession contest through advisers, coalition effects, and conduct under pressure. [[CuiYanLateHan|崔琰]] and [[MaoJie|毛玠]] argue directly for seniority, while [[JiaXu|贾诩]] invokes the failed heir choices of [[YuanShao|袁绍]] and [[LiuBiao|刘表]] and advises Cao Pi to keep performing ordinary duties rather than force the decision.

The episode contrasts Cao Zhi's literary display with Cao Pi's emotionally resonant farewell to [[CaoCao|曹操]], then treats the latter's steadier presentation and broader official support as more compatible with political delegation. [[XinXianying|辛宪英]] supplies the closing qualification: Cao Pi's exuberance after designation suggests that restraint during competition did not make his self-command complete or permanent.

## Key Claims

- Cao Pi's objection to a proposed marriage between a Cao daughter and [[DingYiLateHan|丁仪]] is presented as the origin of Ding Yi's grievance and later alignment with Cao Zhi.
- Cui Yan supports Cao Pi through a primogeniture argument despite kinship with Cao Zhi, while Mao Jie also favors Cao Pi; the episode says their convergent advocacy makes Cao Cao suspicious of coordinated lobbying.
- Jia Xu answers the succession question indirectly by recalling Yuan Shao and Liu Biao's harmful departures from seniority, allowing Cao Cao to draw the conclusion without a blunt endorsement.
- After Cui Yan's death and Mao Jie's punishment, Jia Xu reportedly keeps his distance and sends Cao Pi a written instruction to perform his normal duties and let time work.
- Cao Pi is then portrayed as increasingly steady, thorough, and politically predictable, while Cao Zhi's advisers are characterized as less suitable for governing work.
- During Cao Cao's departure for a campaign, Cao Zhi wins admiration with an improvised literary tribute, while [[WuZhi|吴质]] helps Cao Pi answer through filial grief and a request for his father's safe return.
- Cao Cao ultimately names Cao Pi heir, but Xin Xianying interprets Cao Pi's celebratory embrace of [[XinPiLateHan|辛毗]] as insufficiently grave for someone about to inherit responsibility for a state.
- The host generalizes the result as a long-competition advantage for lower error rates, patience, audience awareness, and self-control rather than brilliance alone.
