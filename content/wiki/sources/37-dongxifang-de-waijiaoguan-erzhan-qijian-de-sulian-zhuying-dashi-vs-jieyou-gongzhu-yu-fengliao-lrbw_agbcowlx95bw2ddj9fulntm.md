# 37 东西方的外交官：二战期间的苏联驻英大使 vs解忧公主与冯嫽

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] episode compares [[IvanMaisky|伊万·麦伊斯基]], the Soviet ambassador in Britain from 1932 to 1943, with [[FengLiaoWesternHan|冯嫽]] and the longer Han-Wusun network built through [[LiuXijunPrincessWesternHan|刘细君]] and [[XieyouPrincessWesternHan|解忧公主]]. Maisky cultivated British political and intellectual relationships while pressing collective security, the wartime alliance, Polish reconciliation, and a second European front; Feng Liao turned literacy, local language, marriage ties, regional standing, and Han credentials into mediation during a Wusun succession crisis.

The comparison grounds [[EmbeddedDiplomaticIntermediation|embedded diplomatic intermediation]]. Both cases show that an envoy's usefulness can depend on knowledge and trust accumulated inside another political society, then backed by the resources of the sending state. The analogy is bounded: Maisky served a modern embassy and also facilitated intelligence contact, whereas Feng Liao's authority emerged from a heqin household and frontier network before receiving formal imperial commission.

## Key Claims

- [[IvanMaisky|Maisky]] used language, cultural fluency, and relationships with British politicians and intellectuals to advocate Anglo-Soviet cooperation against Nazi Germany.
- British appeasement, Soviet distrust, and slow 1939 negotiations helped defeat the collective-security project before the German-Soviet non-aggression pact.
- After Germany invaded the Soviet Union, Maisky worked on the Anglo-Soviet alliance, Polish reconciliation, and pressure for a second European front.
- The embassy's contact with Klaus Fuchs illustrates how public diplomacy, political networking, and covert intelligence could overlap, although the episode's details require specialist verification.
- Maisky's recall, arrest, forced confession, later rehabilitation, and continuing communist commitments show that diplomatic contribution did not protect him from domestic political coercion.
- Han marriage diplomacy gradually pulled [[Wusun|Wusun]] away from [[Xiongnu|Xiongnu]], but exposed Liu Xijun and Xieyou to isolation, remarriage rules, succession disputes, and violence.
- Feng Liao's documents, regional travel, local language, marriage to the Wusun right general, and reputation made her a practical envoy before formal appointment.
- With Han force in the background, Feng Liao persuaded Wujiutu to relinquish the sole royal claim and helped implement the large-kunmi/small-kunmi settlement.
- Both biographies support a bounded comparative claim: locally embedded intermediaries can make state power usable by interpreting people, incentives, and timing, but their personal security remains subordinate to larger political systems.
