## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode profiles [[ZackPolanski]] as the leader turning the [[GreenPartyOfEnglandAndWales]] from a mostly environmental party into a more visible vehicle for [[EcoPopulism]] and [[YouthPrecarityPolitics]]. [[AndrewMiller]] connects Polanski's biography, performance skills, controversies, Gaza politics, and radical policy platform to a wider drift away from centrist parties in the [[UnitedKingdom]]. The episode then shifts to [[OliverMorton]] on [[SyntheticSpudCells]] and the [[LifeDefinitionSpectrum]], and to [[CaitlinTalbot]] on [[TravelAgentResilience]] in an age of [[AITravelPlanning]].

## Key Claims
- [[AndrewMiller]] frames a Green Party rave in London Docklands as a symbol of how [[ZackPolanski]] has made the [[GreenPartyOfEnglandAndWales]] feel culturally energetic to some younger voters.
- The source says Polanski has more than tripled Green Party membership and improved its polling, while critics call him dangerous or a con artist.
- The episode presents Polanski's rise as part of a wider anti-centrist shift driven by economic stagnation, housing pressure, disillusionment with establishment parties, and populist alternatives.
- Polanski's personal background is used politically: the source describes unhappy teenage years, bullying, coming to terms with being gay, Jewish identity, and involvement in Habonim Dror.
- Gaza has become a Green Party touchstone under Polanski. The source says many British Jews see his anti-Israel positioning as a betrayal, and it highlights controversy around his comments on whether British Jews faced actual or perceived unsafety.
- Polanski's acting, nightclub, waiting, and hypnotherapy background is presented as both liability and asset: it gives critics embarrassing material, but it may also help him perform attention and connect with precarious workers.
- The episode says Polanski moved from the Liberal Democrats to the Greens, then won election to the London Assembly in 2021.
- Under Polanski, the Greens are described as environmentally interested but increasingly focused on economic grievances, redistribution, and "squeezing the rich."
- The source says the Green platform includes nationalised water, rent controls, higher top income and capital-gains taxes, drug legalisation and regulation, ending the monarchy and nuclear weapons, and seeking an alternative to [[NATO]].
- The episode identifies liberal migration policy as a major vulnerability for the Greens, especially opposition to detention camps for migrants or failed asylum seekers.
- [[OliverMorton]] says [[KateAdamalla]]'s lab at the [[UniversityOfMinnesota]] has made synthetic "spud cells" from chemicals; they have no biological ancestors and can reproduce for a limited number of generations.
- The synthetic-cell segment argues that life and death are not always binary in biology labs: spud cells sit further toward living than previous lab-made systems, but still have poor reproduction and almost no metabolism.
- [[CaitlinTalbot]] says people still use human travel agents despite AI itineraries and online reviews, especially for complex, risky, emotional, or high-end trips.
- [[ABTA]] estimates about a third of British travellers booked holidays through a human last year, while the [[AmericanSocietyOfTravelAdvisers]] is described as having more than 310,000 members, up from nearly 200,000 in 2024.
- The travel segment presents [[Fora]] as a modern agency that uses an AI-powered platform to help advisers, making AI infrastructure for human agents rather than a direct replacement.
