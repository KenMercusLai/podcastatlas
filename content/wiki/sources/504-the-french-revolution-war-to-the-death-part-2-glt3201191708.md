# 504. The French Revolution: War to the Death (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]] from the constitutional settlement of 1791 to France's declaration of war on Austria on 20 April 1792. It argues that economic disorder, clerical resistance, emigre politics, conspiracy belief, royal duplicity, revolutionary universalism, and factional self-interest converged around war even though the [[Girondins]], [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]], [[MarieAntoinette|Marie Antoinette]], the Feuillants, and [[MarquisDeLafayette|Lafayette]] expected incompatible benefits.

The central counterpoint is [[MaximilienRobespierre|Maximilien Robespierre]], who warned that an expansionary liberation war could destroy the Revolution and produce a Caesar-like military ruler. His opposition exposes the tension inside [[RevolutionaryIdeologicalWar|revolutionary ideological war]]: claims to universal liberty could make armed expansion appear sacred to its advocates while looking like aggression to outsiders.

## Key Claims

- The new Legislative Assembly had weak electoral legitimacy, no returning deputies, and faced falling assignats, food pressure, clerical resistance, rural violence, and the Saint-Domingue uprising.
- [[JacquesPierreBrissot|Jacques Pierre Brissot]] and the [[Girondins]] used laws against emigres and refractory priests to force royal vetoes that discredited the constitutional monarchy and weakened the Feuillants.
- The emigre force at Koblenz was limited, but officer flight, royalist propaganda, and the Declaration of Pillnitz made it politically potent as evidence of a wider internal-external conspiracy.
- Brissot cast war as sacred, regenerative, financially restorative, and universal, joining French national ambition to Roman and American republican examples.
- Robespierre rejected the American analogy, warned that foreign peoples would not welcome armed missionaries, and predicted that war could produce a Caesar or Cromwell.
- Nearly every major faction found a different reason to support war: Girondins expected revolutionary unity, Feuillants and Lafayette expected monarchical stabilization or prestige, and the royal couple expected either restored credit or foreign rescue.
- Louis's requests for foreign intervention and Marie Antoinette's transmission of military plans made royal betrayal real even though broader conspiracy claims still exceeded the evidence.
- [[CharlesFrancoisDumouriez|Dumouriez]] expected an easy Belgian liberation campaign, but the assumption that Belgians would rise for France turned consent into an untested premise of invasion.
- The declaration of war on 20 April 1792 opened a conflict that outlasted its sponsors' short-war expectations and accelerated monarchical collapse, revolutionary radicalization, and the eventual rise of Napoleon.

## Key Quotes

> "Nobody likes armed missionaries." - Robespierre's warning against expansionary liberation war as presented by the episode.

> "A Caesar or a Cromwell." - the episode's formulation of Robespierre's warning that war could create military dictatorship.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction found. The episode supplies the prewar causes that Part 4 treats mainly through the war's military and constitutional consequences.
- Leopold II's Pillnitz declaration is presented as a cautious deterrent distorted by emigre publicity, so it does not itself establish an Austrian plan for immediate invasion.
- Robespierre's prediction of a Caesar is historically striking, but the Napoleon parallel is a retrospective interpretation rather than proof that the later outcome was inevitable.
- Legislative faction totals, turnout, emigre and army numbers, the private motives of each war supporter, reported dialogue, and claims about the conflict's total duration and deaths remain source-scoped.
