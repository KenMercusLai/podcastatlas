# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》105｜战争花样赵国李牧最会耍

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues early [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] chronology into 244 BCE: even during famine, [[QinState|秦国]] keeps attacking east, and [[MengAo|蒙敖]] takes twelve Han cities from [[HanState|韩国]]. In [[ZhaoState|赵国]], [[ZhaoDaoxiangwang|赵道襄王]] moves from the failed recovery of [[LianPo|廉颇]] to using [[LiMu|李牧]], who attacks [[YanState|燕国]] and takes 五遂 and 方城.

The main body then reconstructs Li Mu's northern-frontier strategy against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]]. Li Mu governs the Dai and Yanmen border with unusual autonomy, stores revenue as military funds, feeds and trains soldiers, watches beacons, sends spies, and forbids unauthorized sorties, letting repeated non-battle preserve people, livestock, and morale until the enemy believes he is timid.

The episode's core claim is that Li Mu's apparent avoidance is [[FrontierDefensivePatience|frontier defensive patience]], not cowardice. Because weakened Zhao cannot afford Han Wudi-style deep steppe expeditions, Li Mu uses [[NomadicCavalryRaidingAsymmetry|nomadic cavalry raiding asymmetry]] against the raiders: he exposes pasture as bait, feigns retreat, sacrifices a small party, draws the Xiongnu main force deep, then encircles it in a decisive strike.

## Key Claims
- In 244 BCE, Qin suffers famine but still continues eastward warfare because limited campaigns can supplement state resources through plunder rather than only consume them.
- Han is depicted as nearly unable to resist by this point; Meng Ao attacks Han and captures twelve cities.
- After Lian Po leaves Zhao, Zhao Daoxiang Wang appoints Li Mu, who proves effective by attacking Yan and taking 五遂 and 方城.
- The episode treats Li Mu's northern defense as a retrospective explanation of why he becomes Zhao's next great commander.
- Northern nomadic raiders are described as mobile, horse-archery-centered, and opportunistic: they fight when advantage is available and withdraw when it is not.
- The episode explains northern walls as a defensive answer to repeated border raiding, and says Qin Shi Huang later links preexisting Qin, Zhao, and Yan wall systems into a more continuous anti-Xiongnu barrier.
- Li Mu's frontier command includes local appointment power, tax revenue directed to his camp, daily meat and drink rewards for soldiers, cavalry and archery training, beacon discipline, and intelligence work.
- His standing order is that if Xiongnu forces enter, Zhao forces should withdraw people, livestock, and stores into fortified positions and not fight without authorization.
- Zhao Xiaocheng Wang removes Li Mu after this policy is criticized as cowardice, but the replacement commander repeatedly fights and loses, disrupting border farming and herding.
- Li Mu returns only after securing permission to continue the old policy, which the source presents as the condition for making the strategy work.
- Once soldiers are rested, rewarded, and eager to fight, Li Mu lets people and livestock appear outside the fortress line, stages retreat, and lets the Xiongnu capture a small party.
- The Xiongnu chanyu takes the bait and enters with a main force; Li Mu uses multiple formations, frontal fixing, flanking, and rear-cutting movement to destroy the attacking force.
- The episode says Zhao kills more than 100,000 Xiongnu cavalry, the chanyu flees, Li Mu then attacks 襜褴, Donghu, and Linhu, and Xiongnu forces avoid the Zhao frontier for more than ten years.
- The episode contrasts this with Han Wudi's later offensive war: deep pursuit of Xiongnu cavalry requires rich state resources, while post-Changping Zhao can only fight a low-cost, terrain-close decisive battle.
- The closing previews Li Mu's later confrontation with [[WangJianQin|王翦]] in the Qin-Zhao final phase.

## Key Quotes
> "富贵仗" - the source's shorthand for the resource-intensive Han Wudi-style offensive war that Zhao cannot afford.

> "能打也要装作不能打，想打也要装作不想打" - the episode's Sunzi-style summary of Li Mu's feigned weakness.
