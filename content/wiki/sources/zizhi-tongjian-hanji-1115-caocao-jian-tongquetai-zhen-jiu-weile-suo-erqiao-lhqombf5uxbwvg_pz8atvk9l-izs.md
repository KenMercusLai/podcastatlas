# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1115丨曹操建铜雀台，真就为了锁二乔？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places [[TongquePlatform|铜雀台]] in [[YeLateHan|邺城]] after [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s northern consolidation and reads it as a public monument to achievement and authority. It distinguishes that political use from the famous “铜雀春深锁二乔” story: because the platform and the relevant fu postdate the pre-Red-Cliffs persuasion scene, the episode treats [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] provoking [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] through the Two Qiaos as [[RomanceOfTheThreeKingdoms|《三国演义》]]'s literary construction rather than a historical event.

The episode's second durable contribution is [[SelfClarificationEdict|《自明本志令》]]. Cao Cao's retrospective joins modest stated beginnings, northern conquest, public denial of usurpation, willingness to return part of his fief, and refusal to surrender military command because power had become protection for himself, his descendants, and his political order. The opening advice on trust, boundaries, Xunzi, and Machiavelli is retained as the host's modern interpretive framing rather than universal proof about human nature.

## Key Claims

- The source dates construction of Tongque Platform at Ye to the winter of Jian'an 15 and interprets the building as a display of Cao Cao's northern achievements and political authority.
- A discovered bronze-bird omen is presented as a naming tradition, not independently established architectural history.
- The “locking the Two Qiaos” persuasion scene is chronologically inconsistent: Red Cliffs precedes construction of the platform and the relevant literary composition.
- The episode therefore separates the historical platform from the later novel's dramatic linkage among Cao Cao, the Two Qiaos, Zhuge Liang, and Zhou Yu.
- In the *Self-Clarification Edict*, Cao Cao narrates his rise from limited early ambition through campaigns against Dong Zhuo, the Yellow Turbans, Yuan Shu, Yuan Shao, and Liu Biao to northern consolidation.
- Cao Cao argues that his power restrained further fragmentation while acknowledging that concentrated authority made others suspect imperial ambition.
- His offer to return part of his fief reduces symbolic benefit and criticism, but he refuses to relinquish command because disarmament would expose him and his descendants.
- The source reads the edict as simultaneous candor, self-defense, reputation management, and a claim that personal security and public order had become inseparable.
- Later praise of Cao Cao's practical judgment, prose, poetry, and contribution to eventual reunification is evaluative and source-scoped rather than a settled comparative ranking.

## Key Quotes

> “如果没有我的话，真不知道天下还有多少个人会称王，又有多少人会称帝。” - the episode's rendering of Cao Cao's claim that his power limited further fragmentation.

> “我不能够放弃兵权。” - the episode's compact statement of the security boundary in the edict.

## Contradictions

- The episode contradicts a literal historical reading of “铜雀锁二乔”: its own chronology places both Tongque Platform and the relevant fu after the Red Cliffs persuasion setting.
- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is recorded. The source extends Cao Cao from institution-building and command into explicit public self-justification and extends Ye beyond siege target into a monumental political center.
- The bronze-bird discovery, the edict's modern-language rendering, precise inner motives, quoted dialogue, causal claim about later Jin reunification, and comparative judgments about Cao Cao's literary and political stature remain source-scoped.
