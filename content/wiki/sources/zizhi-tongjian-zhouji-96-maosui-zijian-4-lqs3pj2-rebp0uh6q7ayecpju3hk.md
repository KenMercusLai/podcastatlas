# 《资治通鉴·周纪》96丨毛遂自荐（4）

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues the [[Handan|邯郸]] crisis from [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-3-lj-cr4vg4e1qdfjs6yez85-3zuiv|Zhouji 96 part 3]] by explaining why [[LuZhonglian|鲁仲连]] could move [[XinyuanYan|新垣衍]]: the anti-Qin argument speaks to shared aristocratic privilege, autonomy, and fear of losing status under Qin rule. It then shifts from diplomatic persuasion to siege mobilization, where [[LiTan|李谈 / 李同]] rebukes [[Pingyuanjun|平原君]] for preserving household wealth while commoners starve, forcing Pingyuanjun to fund the defense and send his household into the army.

The episode's distinctive contribution is [[EliteCrisisBurdenSharing|elite crisis burden sharing]]. Zhao needs time for Wei and Chu relief, but morale inside Handan depends on whether the protected noble household visibly joins the city's suffering. The closing historiographical note adds a source-critical caution: the Li Tan / Li Tong name variant is explained as [[Shiji|《史记》]] name avoidance around [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s father, not as a separate person.

## Key Claims
- Lu Zhonglian's success is grounded in class interest as well as moral rhetoric: if Zhao submits to Qin, aristocrats like Xin Yuan Yan lose privilege, freedom, and security.
- Lu Zhonglian's refusal of office and money depends on wealth and networks; under Qin rule, that kind of unofficed aristocratic autonomy would become harder to sustain.
- Qin has reason to assault Handan quickly because Chu and Wei relief remain uncertain; Zhao's immediate strategic need is to buy time.
- Li Tan says Handan's people have reached the extremity of "炊骨易子而食," while Pingyuanjun's household still has fine clothes, good food, women, servants, and metal vessels.
- Pingyuanjun's late response matters because crisis mobilization requires elite sacrifice, not only orders to commoners and soldiers.
- After Pingyuanjun distributes household wealth and puts wives, concubines, and servants into the army, Zhao gathers three thousand dare-death fighters.
- Li Tan does not merely advise sacrifice; he joins the fighting and dies, after which Zhao honors his father as Li Hou.
- The Fu Wang Zhu Changxun comparison is used as a counterexample: people often cling to wealth even when surrendering it might improve survival odds.
- The Li Tan / Li Tong variant is treated as a textual-name issue caused by Sima Qian avoiding his father Sima Tan's name, with some inconsistency in the received text.

## Key Quotes
> "炊骨易子而食" - Li Tan's summary of Handan's siege extremity.

> "刑不上大夫" - the aristocratic privilege frame used to explain Lu Zhonglian's persuasion.

> "信陵君魏无忌" - the next rescuer previewed at the episode's close.
