# Pete Hegseth’s Spiritual Adviser Wants a Christian America

## Summary
This Reveal investigation follows pastor [[DougWilson]], whose relationship with [[PeteHegseth]] gives his project visibility beyond Moscow, Idaho. Wilson describes a democratically achieved Christian nation while church-linked worship, education, publishing, business, and housing make Moscow a prototype for [[ChristianNationalistInstitutionBuilding]].

The reporting also holds together experiences that resist a single account. Current members describe order, hospitality, family, and certainty; former members and other residents describe abuse allegations, patriarchal discipline, anti-LGBTQ teaching, estrangement, exclusion, and civic division. [[CanonPress]] converts this worldview into a national media and product system, while critical coverage can simultaneously expose the movement and feed [[ControversyDrivenMovementAmplification]].

## Key Claims
- Wilson says he wants America to become an explicitly Christian nation through democratic means, with abortion outlawed, same-sex marriage reversed, no-fault divorce removed, and household rather than individual political authority emphasized.
- In Wilson's ideal order, non-Christians could remain citizens and vote but could not hold public office, making formal religious supremacy compatible with unequal political membership.
- Moscow's church-linked congregations, school, college, publisher, businesses, and housing form an institutional ecosystem intended to model and expand a Christian social order.
- The community's family model assigns men and women sharply different authority, endorses submission within marriage, and permits corporal punishment in some homes and at Logos School.
- The episode juxtaposes hospitality and reported member stability with allegations of grooming or sexual abuse, anti-transgender teaching, social exclusion, and the relational cost of leaving.
- Canon Press uses books, curricula, podcasts, studios, events, and provocative culture-war media to turn doctrine and controversy into revenue, recruitment, and reach.
- Wilson says critical media attention has benefited the movement; the reporters therefore treat public-interest scrutiny and inadvertent amplification as simultaneous possibilities.

## Key Quotes
> “the terrain” - Wilson's description of nonbelievers within a spiritual conflict rather than as the enemy themselves.

> “astoundingly beneficial” - Wilson's assessment of media attention to his movement.

> “own garden” - the marital metaphor attributed to Nancy Wilson in the episode's discussion of her denial of marital rape.

## Connections
- [[DougWilson]], [[PeteHegseth]], and [[CanonPress]] - religious leader, nationally prominent admirer, and publishing-media engine at the center of the investigation.
- [[ChristianNationalistInstitutionBuilding]] and [[ConfessionalCommonwealth]] - contemporary movement strategy and a historical comparative frame for joining doctrine, education, discipline, and civic order.
- [[ControversyDrivenMovementAmplification]], [[RightWingMediaInfrastructure]], and [[EmpathicAdversarialInterviewing]] - publicity, movement-media, and reporting dilemmas raised by the episode.
- [[JudeoChristianNationalIdentity]] and [[InheritedFaithReconstruction]] - adjacent concepts distinguishing broad national religious identity from formal Christian supremacy and preserving multiple pathways through inherited faith.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction was adopted. Wilson's promise to use democratic means does not resolve the tension between electoral procedure and an intended order that bars non-Christians from office and gives Christianity formal supremacy.
- Cy Whitling's account of freedom after leaving and Linda's account of stability after returning conflict as personal outcomes but do not cancel one another; the episode presents them as evidence that certainty, belonging, constraint, and exit costs can be experienced differently.
- Wilson says an offending Logos School teacher was fired and police were contacted, while the episode reports broader allegations without supplying investigative records, court files, or a comprehensive institutional response. Those allegations and responses remain source-scoped.
