## Summary
This [[TheSocialRadars]] episode has [[JessicaLivingston]] and [[CarolynLevy]] interview [[RonConway]] about the March 2023 [[SiliconValleyBank]] collapse and the weekend push to guarantee deposits before Asian markets opened. Conway's account turns the crisis into a case in [[DepositGuaranteeCrisisResponse]], [[MoralHazardContagionTradeoff]], [[StartupPayrollSystemicRisk]], and [[CivicRelationshipsAsCrisisInfrastructure]]. Its strongest synthesis is that startup banking risk became a national payroll and contagion problem once a modern bank run compressed political, regulatory, and private-sector response into roughly one weekend.

## Key Claims
- [[SiliconValleyBank]] was deeply embedded in the startup ecosystem through deposits, loans, lines of credit, and operating cash, but the episode stresses that local small businesses also used the bank.
- Conway says SVB's balance-sheet problem came from low-rate Treasury and mortgage-backed-security holdings that lost value when the [[FederalReserve]] raised rates and SVB did not hedge enough against rate risk.
- The March 8 disclosure of a $1.8 billion loss and capital raise triggered panic, and Conway says roughly $42 billion left the bank in about eight hours.
- The episode extends [[AcceleratedBankRuns]] because founder phone calls, investor networks, and online transfers made panic visible and actionable before slower regulatory processes could settle.
- Conway's visibility came partly from SV Angel's founder summit, where many founders were trying to withdraw money while speakers such as [[SamAltman]], Diane Greene, Laurene Powell Jobs, Jony Ive, Brian Chesky, and [[BarackObama]] were scheduled.
- The Friday lunch conclusion was that the government needed to guarantee deposits, and Conway presents the two-word phrase as the operational center of the weekend.
- [[WallyAdeyemo]] connected Conway to [[GrahamSteele]] at the [[USTreasury|U.S. Treasury]], where Conway argued for a full deposit guarantee rather than treating the issue as an ordinary bank failure.
- [[NancyPelosi]] created a private "war room" with Conway and [[BarackObama]], and the episode says Pelosi and Obama framed the core policy tradeoff as moral hazard versus contagion.
- Conway says the [[FederalDepositInsuranceCorporation|FDIC]] and [[MartyGruenberg]] were still exploring FDIC control or a buyer for SVB, while Conway's network pushed for a faster guarantee.
- [[FirstRepublicBank]] appears as an immediate contagion risk in the episode, making the SVB decision a broader banking-stability question rather than only a startup bailout question.
- [[RuthPorat]] helped gather precedent and legal-pattern arguments from 2008 crisis figures, connecting the episode to [[SystemicRiskException]] reasoning.
- Conway says [[SherrodBrown]], [[MaxineWaters]], [[JanetYellen]], [[WallyAdeyemo]], Jeff Zients, [[LaelBrainard]], [[JeromePowell]], and others were treated as key decision or influence points.
- [[GarryTan|Gary Tan]] and [[YCombinator]] organized a petition and payroll-impact data to show that affected companies were distributed nationally and could miss payroll.
- Conway says [[KamalaHarris]] asked for the situation straight, then called Yellen, Biden, Zients, and Powell with urgency.
- The guarantee was announced shortly before markets opened in Tokyo, which Conway treats as the deadline for avoiding visible global contagion.
- The episode argues that civic relationships built before the crisis mattered because long-term, non-transactional relationships with policymakers made calls get answered under time pressure.
- Conway credits a broad group, including Pelosi, Obama, Adeyemo, Brown, Porat, Harris, Tan, YC, and private investors, rather than presenting the outcome as his work alone.

## Key Quotes
> "guarantee deposits" - Conway's repeated summary of the required government action.

> "moral hazard" - one side of the policy tradeoff Pelosi and Obama explain in Conway's account.

> "contagion" - the opposing risk that bank runs would spread if deposits were not guaranteed.
