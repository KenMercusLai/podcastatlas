## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview addiction-treatment and trauma-recovery specialist [[RyanSoave]] about substance and behavioral addictions. Soave frames addiction as an attempted solution to stress, pain, loneliness, shame, trauma, or dysregulation that can become a new source of harm, and he places medical stabilization before psychological exploration in acute cases. The recovery model joins [[AddictionAsAttemptedRelief]], [[RecoveryDistressTolerance]], [[EmotionalWeatherForecast]], [[RecoveryCommunityConnection]], and [[SelfDirectedStateShifting]] with the longer task of building a connected and meaningful life.

## Key Claims
- A practical first screen is whether a person still directs the substance or behavior, whether they can stop for a meaningful period, and how it affects relationships, motivation, attention, and daily functioning.
- Acute recovery begins with medical assessment, detoxification when indicated, and physical stabilization; alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous and severe cases should not be reduced to self-help.
- Addiction can begin as relief from discomfort or trauma, then generate further stress, shame, dependency, and trauma in a self-reinforcing loop.
- Recovery requires learning to experience distress without automatically returning to the fast-acting substance or behavior that previously changed state.
- Proactive practices such as therapy, peer community, exercise, yoga nidra or NSDR, meditation, breathwork, and scheduled connection complement reactive tools used during acute distress.
- A daily emotional weather forecast combines gratitude, plans, current emotions, anticipated hazards, and chosen strivings, then becomes more concrete when shared with trusted people.
- Recovery is not only abstinence: it eventually requires purpose, relationships, pleasure, accountability, and a life that is worth protecting.
