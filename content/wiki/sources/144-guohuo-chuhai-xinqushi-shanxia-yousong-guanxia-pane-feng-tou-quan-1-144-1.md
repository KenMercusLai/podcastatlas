# 144 ✪ 国货出海新趋势：山下有松、观夏、PANE

## Summary
This [[FengTouQuan]] episode argues that a small set of Chinese consumer brands are gaining overseas attention through aesthetics, story, offline retail, social media, and [[InboundTourismBrandDiscovery]] before they have necessarily built mature overseas channels. It contrasts this route with [[DJI]], [[Xiaomi]], [[Huawei]], and EV-style functional globalization: [[ShanxiaYousong|山下有松]], [[Guanxia|观夏]], [[Sandunban|三顿半]], and [[PANE]] are treated as cases where [[AspirationalEmotionalPremium|向往感]], design, cultural legibility, and brand content can create willingness to pay. The episode's strongest wiki contribution is [[ChineseConsumerBrandSoftPower]]: Chinese brands may travel globally through either Chinese aesthetic expression or international trend fit, but the number of such brands is still small.

## Key Claims
- The episode defines the new "国货出海" pattern as consumer attention arriving before full overseas distribution: foreign visitors discover stores in China, Instagram spreads the products, and overseas buyers may use reverse daigou.
- [[ShanxiaYousong]] is presented as a Chinese light-luxury bag brand whose appeal comes from Middle Plains and Shanxi-linked textures, oldness, roughness, handwork cues, and a circle-specific taste rather than European-style polish.
- High-priced fashion consumption is treated less as performance comparison than as [[AspirationalEmotionalPremium|向往感]]: consumers pay because the product helps them feel closer to an admired self-image or cultural identity.
- The source reframes "中女" around independent taste, cultural work, economic self-support, and inner stability rather than only age or gender.
- [[BrandPodcasting]] and long-form content matter more for high-ticket emotional products than for fast-moving products because they can carry trust, values, and worldview over time.
- [[Sandunban]] is used as a contrast case: good content can help a brand, but coffee as a daily fast-moving product may need upstream supply-chain and product work more than long-form storytelling.
- Korean marketing and [[FanEconomy]] are treated as reference systems for how small domestic markets can make consumer brands global through entertainment and fandom.
- [[Guanxia]] extends [[ChineseFragrance]] by translating tea, osmanthus, poetic naming, and line incense into a contemporary fragrance brand with possible appeal to foreign visitors.
- [[PANE]] is the episode's more extreme "export then domestic attention" case: the source says it first gained traction through overseas Instagram and foreign-store interest, then drew Xiaohongshu and Douyin attention inside China.
- Chinese consumer-brand globalization does not have to mean "Chinese style." [[PANE]], [[Sandunban]], [[PopMart]], and [[Labubu]] show that international aesthetics, IP emotion, and category fit can also travel.

## Key Quotes
> "情绪价值" - the episode's shorthand for the non-functional consumer value it wants to analyze.

> "向往感" - the source's key word for why high-premium consumer products can feel worth paying for.

> "产品看日本，营销看韩国" - the Korean marketing comparison cited through Sandunban founder Wu Jun.

> "就是好看" - the PANE store explanation the host uses to frame the brand as design-led rather than function-led.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found with existing wiki content.
- The source qualifies [[InboundTourismBrandDiscovery]] by supplying richer examples and recent travel context, but it still treats store observations, follower counts, reverse-daigou pricing, and PANE's overseas seed path as source-scoped rather than independently verified market proof.
- The source qualifies [[ChineseHardwareGlobalization]] and [[GlobalProductLocalization]] by separating function-led export from brand-led consumer soft power.
- The source qualifies [[ChineseFragrance]] and [[LineIncenseDailyLife]] by adding the outbound and inbound-tourism angle: line incense and Chinese fragrance are not only domestic calming rituals, but can also become culturally legible objects for foreign visitors.
