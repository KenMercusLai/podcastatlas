# How to Prevent & Treat Colds & Flu

## Summary
This solo [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] explain colds and influenza through viral diversity, transmission routes, physical barriers, innate immunity, adaptive immunity, and recovery behavior. Its practical synthesis strengthens [[LayeredRespiratoryInfectionPrevention]], [[FoundationalImmuneHealthFramework]], [[SleepImmuneRepair]], and [[FeverHeatImmuneResponseBoundary]] while adding [[ColdFluSupplementEvidenceBoundary]]: exposure reduction, sleep, appropriately dosed exercise, rest during systemic malaise, and evidence quality matter more than any single “immune-boosting” remedy.

## Key Claims
- Rhinovirus diversity helps explain repeated colds, while influenza strains and seasonal vaccines create a different strain-matching and severity-reduction problem.
- Eyes, nose, and mouth are important entry routes, so reducing contaminated hand-to-face contact complements attention to respiratory droplets and aerosols.
- Physical barriers, rapid innate responses, and slower antibody-specific adaptive responses help explain why exposure does not always become symptomatic illness.
- Adequate sleep and manageable exercise load support immune function, whereas sleep loss, prolonged calorie deficit, chronic stress, and excessive endurance work may increase vulnerability.
- Whole-body malaise, unusual fatigue, and early illness signs are presented as reasons to rest rather than push intense training; regular moderate exercise is distinguished from training while sick.
- Nasal breathing and fermented foods are discussed as microbiome-related supports, but the morning oral-water swallowing practice is explicitly speculative and lacks randomized-trial support.
- [[ColdFluSupplementEvidenceBoundary]] separates weak or qualified vitamin C and echinacea claims from deficiency-sensitive vitamin D, better-supported but dose-sensitive zinc, and limited NAC evidence.
- Sauna and other deliberate heat exposure are framed as possible pre-illness conditioning tools, not as treatment to intensify once a person is clearly sick or depleted.
