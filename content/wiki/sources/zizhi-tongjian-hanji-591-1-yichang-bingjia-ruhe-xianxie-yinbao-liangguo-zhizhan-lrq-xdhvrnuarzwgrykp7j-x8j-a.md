# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》591-1丨一场病假如何险些引爆两国之战？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills a Jianping 4 / 3 BCE [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] court debate immediately before the later Dong Xian-centered late-Western-Han sequence. A [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] chanyu's illness delays a planned court visit, and [[FuYanWesternHan|傅晏]] and [[XiFuGongWesternHan|息夫躬]] turn the delay into a possible frontier crisis involving [[Wusun|乌孙]] hostages, secret manipulation, military inspection, and exemplary killing.

The durable synthesis is that a small diplomatic delay can become dangerous when factional ambition, security storytelling, and disaster-omen reasoning reinforce each other. [[GongsunLuWesternHan|公孙禄]] argues for trust-preserving diplomacy toward a formally submissive Xiongnu counterpart, while [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]] pulls the discussion away from [[NaturalDisasterPoliticalOmen|灾异政治解释]] as war trigger and toward honest governance, procedure, and historical caution.

## Key Claims

- Fu Yan and Xi Fu Gong seek advancement by treating the chanyu's illness and delayed visit as evidence of possible fraud and rebellion.
- Xi Fu Gong links the delay to Wusun weakness, hostage leverage, and the risk that Xiongnu might absorb Wusun and destabilize the Western Regions.
- Xi Fu Gong proposes sending surrendered western Hu people as false envoys and deliberately leaking the petition to Xiongnu envoys, using deception to split Xiongnu-Wusun ties.
- Gongsun Lu rejects speculative and deceptive diplomacy, arguing that Han should honor the chanyu's reported illness and preserve credible relations with a submissive Xiongnu counterpart.
- Xi Fu Gong escalates further by invoking disasters, frontier inspection, military preparation, and killing a commandery governor to create fear and authority.
- Wang Jia rejects using disasters, star interpretation, and border rumors as pretexts for war or arbitrary killing, insisting on concrete governance and regular procedure.
- Wang Jia uses the Qin Mu Gong and Jian Shu/Baili Xi story as a warning against sharp, flattering, or malicious counsel in state decisions.
