## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three segments: [[Houthis]] advances along Yemen's western coast, weakening parts of [[DonaldTrump]]'s 2024 voter coalition before the midterms, and the persistence of family advantage in Hollywood. [[GregCarlstrom]] frames the Houthi capture of Moqa and Perim Island as a major Yemen-war shift because it gives the group leverage around Bab al-Mandab, embarrasses [[SaudiArabia]], and adds pressure to oil routes during the wider [[UnitedStates]]-[[Iran]] conflict. The later segments use [[YouGov]] polling and Hollywood research to show how economic pain, war, and inherited networks can unsettle apparently stable political and cultural hierarchies.

## Key Claims
- The Houthis captured Moqa and Perim Island near Bab al-Mandab, while drones hit a major Saudi pipeline, turning the Yemen front into a [[HouthiMaritimeChokepointLeverage]] and energy-route risk.
- Carlstrom says the advance is the largest shift in Yemen's war for several years and is embarrassing for Saudi Arabia, which had been discussing a renewed offensive.
- The Houthi success is explained partly by [[AntiHouthiCoalitionFragmentation]]: Saudi-backed forces include religious fundamentalists, southern separatists, and tribal groups, and divisions worsened after Saudi Arabia and the [[UnitedArabEmirates]] split over Yemen.
- Saudi Arabia sought outside support, but [[Turkey]] and [[Pakistan]] did not provide help despite the Mecca agreement, and Trump had not agreed to conduct U.S. airstrikes, though American personnel were helping Saudi target selection.
- Saudi Red Sea exports reportedly fell from about four million barrels per day in June to roughly 200,000 barrels per day by August, showing how maritime and pipeline pressure can transmit into oil-market anxiety.
- The U.S. politics segment says [[YouGov]] polling and an Economist voter-profile model point to [[TrumpCoalitionFragmentation]], especially among young voters and ethnic minorities dissatisfied with tariffs, inflation, and the Iran war.
- The Hollywood segment says nepotism has grown among young actors and key creative roles, making [[HollywoodNepotismNetworkAdvantage]] an industry-wide pattern rather than only a celebrity-gossip complaint.
