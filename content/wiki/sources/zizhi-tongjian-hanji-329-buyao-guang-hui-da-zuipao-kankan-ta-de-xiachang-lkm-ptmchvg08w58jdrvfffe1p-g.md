# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》329｜不要光会打嘴炮，看看他的下场！

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode moves from post-Mobei [[TuntianSystem|屯田]] and an attempted [[HanXiongnuHeqinPolicy|Han-Xiongnu heqin]] reopening into the death of [[DiShanWesternHan|狄山]]. [[RenChangWesternHan|任常]] carries [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s demand that the Xiongnu chanyu submit and appear at the frontier, but the mission ends in detention rather than agreement; Di Shan then argues for heqin, attacks [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] in court, admits he cannot govern a commandery or county, and is sent to a northern barrier where the episode says a Xiongnu raid kills him within a month.

The durable synthesis is not that policy criticism must be backed by personal military skill. It is that a court can collapse a disputed policy, the speaker's tone, and the speaker's operational competence into one punitive test. The host reads Di Shan's death as a warning against empty talk and poor expression, while the same sequence also shows how coercive assignment can silence a legitimate argument about war cost without adjudicating that argument on its merits.

## Key Claims

- After the Mobei campaign, the episode says Han digs canals, develops farmland, appoints agricultural officials, and stations fifty to sixty thousand soldiers in a frontier屯田 zone extending westward from Shuofang toward Lingju.
- The Xiongnu chanyu follows Zhao Xin's advice and asks to restore heqin after military defeat, while Han officials divide between renewed marriage diplomacy and demanding Xiongnu submission.
- Ren Chang carries the harder line to Xiongnu, but his demand for frontier attendance angers the chanyu and leads to his detention.
- Di Shan, a court erudite presented as a Confucian scholar and strong writer, argues that heqin is preferable to continued force and invokes earlier Han losses against Xiongnu.
- After Han Wudi disparages him, Di Shan attacks Zhang Tang as a falsely loyal minister and blames his handling of the Huainan and Jiangdu royal cases for frightening the imperial clan.
- Han Wudi tests Di Shan's practical capacity by asking whether he can eliminate banditry in a commandery, then a county, and finally whether he can guard one frontier barrier; Di Shan accepts only the last task.
- The episode says Xiongnu raiders take the barrier and behead Di Shan within a month, after which court officials no longer openly advocate heqin or attack Zhang Tang.
- The opening uses [[OuterRoundInnerSquareConduct|外圆内方式处世]] to distinguish firm inner principle from needlessly abrasive expression, but this modern lesson remains the host's framing of the historical episode.

## Key Quotes

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted, but this episode says earlier substitute brides were eventually discovered and Han therefore had to send genuine princesses. Hanji 179 and Hanji 180 establish Liu Jing's warning about discovery and Liu Bang's substitute-princess implementation, not an actual discovery event; the new claim remains unresolved and source-scoped.
- The supplied document is a structured episode summary rather than a primary-text transcript. The exact post-Mobei屯田 geography and force size, Zhao Xin's advice, Ren Chang's wording and detention, Di Shan's speech, office and stutter, the barrier's location, the one-month interval, and the claim that no one again proposed heqin require primary-source comparison.
- The host's “借刀杀人” reading attributes deliberate lethal intent to Han Wudi. The narrated assignment and death do not by themselves prove that intent, so the wiki records coercive assignment and chilling effect without treating planned murder as settled fact.
- Di Shan's inability to govern a commandery or county does not settle whether his heqin argument was strategically wrong. The episode itself gives little comparative accounting of renewed war, submission demands, and heqin costs.
