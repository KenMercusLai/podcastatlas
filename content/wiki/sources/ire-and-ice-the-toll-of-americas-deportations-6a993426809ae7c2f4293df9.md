# Ire and ICE: the toll of America's deportations

## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode examines three stories: [[DonaldTrump]]'s second-term deportation campaign, [[Reddit]]'s business exposure as AI answer products reuse human discussion, and the [[BayeuxTapestry|Bayeux Tapestry]] arriving at the [[BritishMuseum|British Museum]] for its first exhibition outside France. The lead segment argues that deportation enforcement has become quieter after public backlash but more systematic through funding, legal-status stripping, data tools, and crowded immigration courts.

The Reddit segment adds [[ShiraOvide]]'s account of a platform whose volunteer-moderated human conversations are valuable to [[Google]] and [[OpenAI]] while [[GoogleAIOverviews|Google AI Overviews]] may weaken search referrals. The closing cultural segment has [[AlexandraSuichBass]] frame the Bayeux Tapestry as a medieval political narrative whose unknown makers, missing ending, survival history, and scenes of violence make it a durable object for interpreting conquest and trauma.

## Key Claims
- [[AaronBraun]] says the Trump administration has not published official removal statistics since January 2025, while a DHS spokesman claimed one million deportations and about 2.2 million voluntary departures.
- [[USImmigrationAndCustomsEnforcement|ICE]] reportedly arrested 50,000 people in July, while interior enforcement and status-stripping efforts mean long-term residents, temporary protected-status holders, and humanitarian-parole entrants can be caught up in removal risk.
- Congress appropriated $70 billion more for immigration enforcement, and court testimony described a [[Palantir]]-created ICE app that shows potential targets in an area and helps agents build target lists.
- Los Angeles immigration-court observations include "mega master hearings" with 50 to 100 cases in a three-hour setting, weaker notice, unrepresented immigrants, and in absentia removal orders.
- Public support is reported as weakening: Trump's immigration approval is underwater, Americans are unhappy with ICE force, roughly half support abolishing ICE, and Hispanic voters are described as especially unhappy.
- [[ShiraOvide]] says Reddit's share price had dropped by about a third this year because investors fear AI-generated search answers will reduce traffic to Reddit links.
- Reddit's model depends on both targeted advertising and unpaid volunteer moderation, and the 2023 API strike is used as evidence that monetizing access can create community-governance conflict.
- Reddit kept its API charging policy and later announced reported annual data deals with Google and [[OpenAI]] worth about $60 million and $70 million, while also suing AI companies it says used its data illegally.
- The [[BayeuxTapestry|Bayeux Tapestry]] is described as likely stitched in England, long kept near Bayeux in France, and narrating [[WilliamTheConqueror|William the Conqueror]]'s 1066 invasion through Edward, Harold, oath, comet, battle, and victory scenes.
- [[MichaelLewisCurator|Michael Lewis]] compares the tapestry's legible visual storytelling to cartoons or graphic novels, while [[NickCullinan]] says its missing ending and unknown makers invite interpretation.
