# 027 武侠史上最经典一战|趣话《陆小凤传奇之决战前后》

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode reads [[GuLong|古龙]]'s [[DuelBeforeAndAfterGuLong|《决战前后》]] through the duel between [[XimenChuixue|西门吹雪]] and [[YeGucheng|叶孤城]]. It treats marriage and impending fatherhood as sources of Ximen's humanity and possible technical hesitation, interprets the duel as both public spectacle and private self-testing, and places “诚于人” against “诚于剑” while praising the confrontation's compression and criticizing the mystery's loose ends, abrupt friendship, and thin female characterization.

## Key Claims

- [[XimenChuixue|西门吹雪]] becomes warmer after marrying 孙秀青 and anticipating fatherhood: he smiles, speaks more, saves people, and arranges for his family's future, but attachment also makes loss and death newly consequential.
- [[YeGucheng|叶孤城]] resembles Ximen in white-clad isolation and supreme swordsmanship while remaining closer to rank, display, worldly power, and the failed palace coup.
- The duel is not adequately described as a contest for public fame. The source also reads it as mutual recognition, self-verification, and an attempt to reach or test the limit of swordsmanship.
- “诚于人” and “诚于剑” compress the novel's central dispute: whether technical mastery can be separated from honesty, moral judgment, and care for people.
- Ye's final one- or two-inch deviation is interpreted as possibly deliberate after the assassination plot fails. If so, Ximen survives without conclusively proving superiority and loses the possibility of a fair rematch.
- [[LuXiaofeng|陆小凤]] is most persuasive when he protects dignity through empathy and humor, but the source finds several declarations or reversals of friendship insufficiently earned by shared experience.
- The source praises the book-length preparation, brief decisive exchange, refusal to exploit an unsettled opponent, and economical emperor portrait, while criticizing unresolved decoys, causal gaps, direct romantic assertion, and weak female roles.

## Key Quotes

> “诚于人。” - 西门吹雪's claim that swordsmanship cannot be separated from the person's integrity.

> “只须诚于剑，不必诚于人。” - 叶孤城's counterclaim that commitment to the sword is sufficient.
