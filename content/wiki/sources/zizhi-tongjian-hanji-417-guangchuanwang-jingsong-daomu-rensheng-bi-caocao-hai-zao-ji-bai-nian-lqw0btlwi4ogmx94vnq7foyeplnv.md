# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》417｜广川王“惊悚盗墓人生”，比曹操还早几百年!

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the Dijie 1 / 69 BCE layer under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]], between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-416-xihan-xiahousheng-ruhe-pai-hanxuandi-de-mapi-lvqbs--20kvjimwdwgfciq-n6wee|Hanji 416]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-418-huoguang-sihou-weihe-huojia-zhuding-bei-miezu-lq-zlaum6yoxja6npjldfhscnsxu|Hanji 418]]. It opens with [[LiuQuGuangchuanKingWesternHan|广川王刘去]] as a violent royal-kin figure whose grave-robbing is framed as thrill, desecration, and ominous story rather than simple poverty or military plunder.

The second half returns to chronicle politics: [[LiuYanshouChuKingWesternHan|楚王刘延寿]] tries to draw [[LiuXuGuanglingKingWesternHan|广陵王刘须]] into a succession plot, the plan is exposed by Zhao Changnian, Liu Yanshou kills himself, and Xuan chooses to reassure Liu Xu with gifts rather than punish him. The closing notices add a solar eclipse and [[YuDingguoWesternHan|于定国]] becoming tingwei, with the episode stressing his cautious, evidence-sensitive judicial style.

## Key Claims

- Liu Qu is presented as a Western Han royal prince whose tomb-robbing is recreational and destructive, not driven by material need.
- The tomb episodes around [[JinYouGong|晋幽公]], [[WeiXiangwang|魏襄王]], and Luan Shu make ancient graves into spaces of wealth, memory, fear, and retribution.
- Liu Qu's household violence and later suicide after removal from kingship make him a royal-kin atrocity case rather than merely a colorful grave-robbing anecdote.
- Liu Yanshou's rebellion plan shows a more distant royal branch trying to use a closer imperial relative as the plausible claimant.
- Xuan's handling of Liu Xu shows royal-kin treason discipline can include reassurance and containment when the implicated prince is politically dangerous but not yet openly punished.
- Yu Dingguo's tingwei notice makes evidentiary doubt and lighter judgment part of Xuan-era judicial repair after cruelty, treason plots, and royal scandal.

## Key Quotes

> "不是因为缺钱盗墓" - the host's distinction between Liu Qu's motive and ordinary plunder.

> "做孽不得活" - the host's moral summary of Liu Qu's end.
