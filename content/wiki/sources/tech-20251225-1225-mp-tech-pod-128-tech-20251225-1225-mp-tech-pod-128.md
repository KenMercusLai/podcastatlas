## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty-Carino]] interview [[WillGottsagen]] of [[TheAtlantic|The Atlantic]] about AI-enabled wearables as a 2025 trend. The source compares smart glasses, pins, pendants, bracelets, watches, rings, and earbuds as attempts to move large language models out of phone and laptop chat windows.

The episode's main wiki contribution is to sharpen [[WearableAIAssistant]] and [[AIPlusTerminals]] with a consumer reality check: wearables can add vision, hearing, voice, gesture, translation, and ambient context, but they still face cloud-dependence, public awkwardness, unclear need, and bystander privacy concerns. [[Meta]]'s newest AI glasses look more mature because of display and gesture controls, yet the source still treats the [[SmartphoneAIHub]] as hard to displace.

## Key Claims
- AI-enabled wearables became more normal in 2025, but the category remains uncertain rather than fully mainstream.
- The source groups augmented reality glasses, AI note-taking pins, pendants, bracelets, smartwatches, rings, and earbuds as attempts to create a third everyday device beyond phones and laptops.
- [[WillGottsagen]] says the central product pitch is a more intuitive, always-available, context-aware interface for large language models.
- [[AppleWatch|Apple Watch]] and [[OuraRing|Oura Ring]] are used as examples of wearables that did not begin as AI products but can gain AI-enabled features as the technology spreads.
- [[AppleAirPods|Apple AirPods]] live translation is used as an example of AI entering an already familiar wearable format.
- The source presents [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Ray-Ban smart glasses]] and newer [[Meta]] AI glasses as the most concrete product case, especially because a small display and wristband-based gesture controls make the interaction feel less like only talking to a chatbot.
- Meta's glasses are pitched as contextual assistant surfaces: users can ask about what they are seeing, who they are talking to, or how to translate nearby speech into real-world subtitles.
- Gottsagen says the experience is still limited by Wi-Fi, connectivity, and the need to send AI computation to the cloud, making [[EdgeCloudAIBoundary]] central to wearable usefulness.
- The episode treats screen-time reduction as a goal but not an achieved outcome: if the device is not fluid and intuitive, it becomes another optional gadget rather than a phone replacement.
- The source notes possible accessibility upside because new human-computer interfaces can help users who are poorly served by default screens and keyboards, but it does not provide detailed disability-specific product evidence.
- Privacy remains a major constraint because some wearables may be always on, always listening, or capable of video capture in ordinary social spaces.
- The source notes that a recording indicator light helps with video awareness on Meta's glasses, while leaving open the separate concern that the device may still be listening.

## Key Quotes
> "third" - Gottsagen's shorthand for a device category beyond phones and laptops.

> "hey Meta" - voice command Gottsagen says felt awkward even inside a Meta store.

> "shiny device" - warning that weak interaction can turn AI wearables into unnecessary hardware.
