# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》214｜铁面张释之：怼皇帝拦太子（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the two-part [[ZhangShizhiWesternHan|张释之]] portrait through two廷尉 cases. When [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] wants punishment increased after a pedestrian startles the imperial horses and after a thief takes a jade ring from Gaozu's temple, Zhang insists that judgment follow the published offense and a proportional penalty ladder rather than the ruler's fear, anger, or filial display.

The episode then turns to [[Liutao|《六韬》]]'s “六守” method for observing character under wealth, status, entrusted responsibility, foreign service, danger, and practical work. Its durable synthesis joins [[LegalPenaltyStabilityAgainstSovereignAnger|法定刑阶约束君怒]] to [[SituationalCharacterTests|情境式识人考察]]: institutions need both officials who withstand pressure and rulers willing to accept an unwelcome boundary.

## Key Claims

- After a pedestrian unexpectedly emerges near a bridge and startles the imperial horses, Zhang Shizhi reportedly applies the prescribed offense of obstructing the imperial procession and imposes a four-liang fine.
- Liu Heng wants a harsher result because he might have been injured, but Zhang argues that once the case is delegated to廷尉, changing punishment with imperial anger would make the public unable to trust the law.
- In the temple-theft case, Zhang sentences the thief to death for taking a jade ring from the Gaozu ancestral temple but rejects Liu Heng's wish to exterminate the offender's family.
- Zhang defends proportional ranking by asking what greater punishment would remain if taking one ring already receives clan extermination and someone later damages the imperial tomb itself.
- Liu Heng eventually accepts both judgments, although the second acceptance reportedly follows silence and intervention by the empress dowager.
- The episode presents 《六韬》's “六守” as six situational tests: wealth for benevolence, honor for righteousness, entrusted affairs for loyalty, missions for trustworthiness, danger for courage, and practical tasks for strategic competence.
- The source says Zhou Yafu respected Zhang's fairness and integrity and sought friendship with him; its description of both men as “社稷之臣” is retained as an evaluative tradition rather than an independently reconstructed relationship.

## Key Quotes

> “法者，天子所与天下公共也。” - the legal premise attributed to Zhang when Liu Heng asks for harsher punishment.

> “法如是足也，且罪等。” - the episode's rendering of Zhang's insistence that the temple-theft sentence follow the legal classification and rank of the offense.

- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - ruler whose fear, anger, and ancestral reverence press against law, but who ultimately accepts both judgments.
- [[LegalPenaltyStabilityAgainstSovereignAnger|法定刑阶约束君怒]] - central legal principle that keeps punishment from expanding with a ruler's immediate emotion.
- [[CriminalLawAsPowerLimitation|刑法作为权力限制]] - broader legal neighbor because the cases treat punishment law as a restraint on state power as well as an authorization to punish.
- [[StatusNeutralPalaceGateEnforcement|宫门礼制的身份中立执行]] - part 1's related case in which Zhang applies palace protocol before royal pardon.
- [[Liutao|《六韬》]] and [[SituationalCharacterTests|情境式识人考察]] - text and broader framework behind the episode's “六守” talent tests.
- [[JiangTaigong|姜太公 / 姜子牙]] - strategist to whom the episode attributes the teaching.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki is adopted. The source extends part 1's protocol case into criminal adjudication and is consistent with its narrower picture of Zhang as an official who makes rules operative against immediate royal preference.
- The first case concerns a commoner who startled the imperial horses, not a proven attempt on Liu Heng's life; the emperor's feared injury explains his reaction but does not change the offense established in the episode.
- “法律面前人人平等” and “王子犯法与庶民同罪” are modernizing summaries used by the host. The cases support resistance to status-based or emotion-based override, but they do not establish a modern constitutional equality regime.
- The bridge location, exact fine, temple object, offense labels, speeches, dowager intervention, Zhou Yafu's office and friendship, “社稷之臣” attribution, 《六韬》 wording, and the historical authorship and dating of that text require comparison with transmitted sources.
