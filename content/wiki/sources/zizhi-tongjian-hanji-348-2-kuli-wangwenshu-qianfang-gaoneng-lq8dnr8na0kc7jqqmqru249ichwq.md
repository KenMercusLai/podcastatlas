# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》348-2｜酷吏王温舒 #前方高能

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode contrasts two forms of administration under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. Reappointed as zhongwei, [[WangWenshuWesternHan|王温舒]] uses knowledge of local offenses, powerful intermediaries, selective protection, legal manipulation, and prison violence to discipline some strongmen while serving the well connected; [[NiKuanWesternHan|兒宽]], as left neishi, encourages agriculture, lightens punishment, accommodates temporary tax arrears, and reportedly earns enough trust that residents mobilize grain when his post is threatened.

The contrast separates surface compliance from durable administrative cooperation. Wang's system can create fear, deaths in custody, and subordinate enrichment, while Ni's flexibility is presented as accumulating a public willingness to help the administration meet an emergency obligation. The closing branch returns to [[Nanyue|南越]]: [[ZhaoHuNanyue|赵胡]] sends heir [[ZhaoYingqiNanyue|赵婴齐]] to Chang'an for court service after Han's intervention against Minyue, and the host interprets Wudi's cultivation of the heir as part of a long incorporation strategy.

## Key Claims

- After returning as zhongwei, Wang Wenshu draws on his familiarity with Guanzhong and on compromising knowledge about powerful local actors to recruit coercive assistants.
- Wang is described as accommodating genuinely powerful people while abusing those without protection; evidence can be ignored for connected offenders and law applied aggressively against weaker targets.
- His pursuit of cunning offenders is paired with beatings, deaths in custody, legal manipulation, and subordinate enrichment, so apparent order does not establish impartial enforcement.
- Ni Kuan encourages agriculture, reduces corvee pressure, chooses humane subordinates, handles litigation with attention to local circumstances, and lets others receive credit for river work.
- Ni reportedly varies collection with harvest conditions and tolerates arrears; when wartime revenue demands put his office at risk, residents voluntarily assemble and deliver enough grain to make the jurisdiction's collection rank first.
- The episode treats that payment story as evidence that flexible administration can create reciprocal public trust, not as proof that every lenient tax regime automatically improves revenue.
- Zhao Hu's dispatch of Zhao Yingqi for court service is presented as gratitude after Han aid and as an opening for Wudi to shape a future Nanyue ruler.

## Key Quotes

> “吏民大信爱之。” - the compact judgment used to summarize Ni Kuan's relationship with officials and residents.

> “为官不为民作主，不如回家卖红薯。” - the host's modern proverb framing Ni Kuan as a model official.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki is adopted. Wang's renewed capital enforcement extends the earlier account of his coercive rise, while the later corruption case completes his fall.
- The source calls Ni Kuan “倪宽”; the wiki routes this to the established [[NiKuanWesternHan|兒宽]] identity. The exact offices, promotion sequence, educational route, river project, tax mechanism, crop conditions, and quoted judgments remain episode-attributed.
- The account does not establish how voluntary the emergency grain delivery was, how tax liabilities were calculated, or whether collection ranked first under a comparable measure. It supports a trust-and-compliance interpretation within the episode, not a universal causal law.
- Wang's youthful crime, the number and causes of prison deaths, ignored evidence, legal maneuvers, officials' wealth, and private motives require comparison with transmitted historical records.
- Wudi's intention to turn Zhao Yingqi into a compliant future king is a strategic reading. The existing Nanyue sequence shows later symbolic accommodation and continued local autonomy, not successful personal control.
