# The Alabama Murders - Part 1: The True Church

## Summary

The opening episode of [[TheAlabamaMurders]] begins with psychologist [[KatePorterfield]]'s account of [[KennethSmith]] focusing on his family's love after surviving a failed execution, then moves backward to the religious and personal setting around [[CharlesSennett]]. It presents the Shoals-area [[ChurchOfChrist]] as a close, intellectually serious community that offered love and belonging while historically enforcing rigid rules, public discipline, and fear of exclusion.

Sennett's successful ministry concealed affairs, psychiatric crisis, debt, abuse, and another relationship. The episode does not establish that church culture caused his later conduct, but [[LeeCamp]] and [[GrantAsbell]] describe a [[ReligiousShameGraceGap]] in which overwhelming shame and a limited practical account of forgiveness could make confession, divorce, and help-seeking appear catastrophic. The series frames this concealed crisis as the beginning of a [[HumanFailureCascade]] involving character, justice, and compassion.

## Key Claims

- Porterfield says Smith initially described the loving family farewells surrounding Alabama's failed execution attempt rather than the procedure itself.
- The Shoals was a closely connected center of the Church of Christ, whose worship emphasized simplicity, a cappella singing, biblical study, rules, and restored-church certainty.
- Camp describes the church as a source of love and formation that could also produce fear, exclusion, and the threat of disfellowshipping.
- Sennett rose as a charismatic preacher, lost a prominent position after affair rumors, experienced a suicidal psychiatric crisis, and later rebuilt his ministry at a smaller Sheffield congregation.
- By 1988, he was reportedly abusive toward Elizabeth, deeply indebted, erratic, and involved with another congregant while trying to hide his private life.
- The episode suggests that fear of divorce, public disgrace, lost ministry, and damnation may have intensified concealment, but it does not establish Sennett's internal reasoning.
- The “failure cascade” frame treats the concealed crisis as an initial condition that later failures of character, justice, and compassion would amplify.

## Key Quotes

> “I won't lose another church.” - Sennett's reported response when Elizabeth sought a divorce.

## Connections

- [[CharlesSennett]] - preacher whose public success and concealed collapse anchor the episode.
- [[ChurchOfChrist]] - religious community described as both sustaining and capable of fear-based discipline.
- [[LeeCamp]] - theologian interpreting the tradition's love, certainty, exclusion, shame, and grace.
- [[GrantAsbell]] - minister recalling the denomination's fear of damnation and resistance to grace-centered preaching.
- [[ReligiousShameGraceGap]] - framework for shame and discipline without an adequate practical route to confession and repair.
- [[HumanFailureCascade]] - series-level model in which distinct failures compound rather than remain isolated.
- [[TheAlabamaMurders]] - seven-part series whose crime and punishment inquiry begins with this social and theological setting.

## Contradictions

- The episode presents the Church of Christ as both loving and harmful; these are coexisting insider accounts rather than a settled judgment about every congregation or member.
- Sennett's affairs, abuse, debt, mental illness, concealment, and institutional setting may have interacted, but the source does not establish a single causal path to the later crime.
- The religious-shame explanation is interpretive and retrospective. It does not independently establish Sennett's beliefs, motives, or conduct in Elizabeth's death.
- Medical history, congregational discipline, relationships, debt, abuse, dialogue, doctrine, and causal claims remain episode-scoped because the supplied file is a structured summary rather than a transcript, church record, medical file, financial record, police file, or independent denominational history.
