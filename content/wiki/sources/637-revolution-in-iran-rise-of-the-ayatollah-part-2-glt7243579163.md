# 637. Revolution in Iran: Rise of the Ayatollah (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode follows the collapse of [[MohammadRezaPahlavi|Mohammad Reza Pahlavi]]'s monarchy from late 1978 through [[RuhollahKhomeini|Ruhollah Khomeini]]'s return and the army's neutrality in February 1979. It argues that [[ShiiteRitualRevolutionaryMobilization|Shiite ritual, martyrdom, and Muharram timing]] gave a broad anti-Shah coalition unusual force, while Khomeini's Paris media strategy let liberals, leftists, foreign journalists, and officials underestimate his program of clerical rule.

The episode then connects revolutionary change in [[Iran]] to oil disruption, inflation, gasoline shortages, and [[JimmyCarter|Jimmy Carter]]'s crisis of confidence in the [[UnitedStates]]. Its central synthesis is that political collapse came from interacting failures of legitimacy, coercive capacity, coalition interpretation, and foreign understanding, while the emerging [[ClericalRepublicDualAuthority|clerical-republican order]] turned revolutionary unity into clerical supremacy.

## Key Claims

- Khomeini used exile in Paris, extensive press access, and deliberately broad anti-Shah language to reach audiences that did not yet understand his doctrine of clerical guardianship.
- Muharram and Ashura supplied a shared calendar, symbols of martyrdom, and public rituals that helped convert religious identity into mass political action.
- The Shah's illness, indecision, and inability either to restore legitimacy or command a decisive crackdown weakened the monarchy as strikes and protests expanded.
- The Carter administration's rivalry between cautious diplomatic engagement and hard-line support for the Shah slowed adaptation and contributed to a serious misreading of the opposition.
- Khomeini's return, creation of a Revolutionary Council, appointment of [[MehdiBazargan|Mehdi Bazargan]], and rejection of Shapour Bakhtiar's government produced rival claims to executive authority.
- The army's declaration of neutrality on 11 February 1979 was the decisive institutional collapse that let revolutionary forces seize state facilities.
- Elections, parliament, and a promised constitution survived inside an order where opposition to clerical rule could be framed as opposition to Islam.
- Oil shutdowns transmitted the Iranian crisis into U.S. inflation, gasoline lines, social unrest, and a wider loss of confidence in Carter's leadership.
- Revolutionary courts, censorship, gender restrictions, and the growing [[IslamicRevolutionaryGuardCorps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] showed that the post-Shah coalition was consolidating into a coercive Islamic state before the embassy seizure.

## Key Quotes

> "velayat-e faqih" - the doctrine of guardianship of the jurist that the episode presents as Khomeini's basis for clerical rule.

> "crisis of confidence" - the language attached to Carter's July 1979 address about energy, consumerism, and national purpose.

## Connections

- [[RuhollahKhomeini]], [[MohammadRezaPahlavi]], and [[JimmyCarter]] - revolutionary leader, collapsing monarch, and U.S. president through whom the episode organizes the crisis.
- [[MehdiBazargan]] - moderate interim prime minister whose democratic promises remained subordinate to Khomeini's revolutionary authority.
- [[ShiiteRitualRevolutionaryMobilization]] - framework for Muharram, Ashura, sacrifice, martyrdom, and street action.
- [[ClericalRepublicDualAuthority]] - framework for the emerging combination of elections and administration with superior clerical authority.
- [[PresidentialSymbolicLeadership]] - U.S. branch connecting energy austerity, the crisis-of-confidence speech, cabinet turmoil, and public images of exhaustion.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode supplies the revolutionary and energy-crisis prehistory for Parts 3 and 4 and strengthens the existing account of clerical consolidation.
- The source reports the Shah's departure as 19 January 1979; that precise date remains episode-attributed rather than normalized into the entity profile.
- Crowd sizes, interview totals, oil and approval figures, remembered dialogue, private motives, the possibility of a U.S.-Khomeini accommodation, and causal weights assigned to illness, religion, media, oil, and leadership remain source-scoped.
- The episode's elite diplomatic focus leaves ordinary Iranians, women, ethnic minorities, left-wing organizations, and regional variation incompletely represented.
