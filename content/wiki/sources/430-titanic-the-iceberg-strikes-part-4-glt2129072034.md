# 430. Titanic: The Iceberg Strikes (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[RMSTitanic|RMS Titanic]] from its third-class migrant world through the ice warnings and collision of 14 April 1912. It presents steerage as unusually comfortable by contemporary liner standards and as part of a commercial [[TransatlanticMigrantPassage|migration system]] carrying rural workers, religious minorities, and families through advertisements, remittances, interpreters, employment networks, and selective exclusion.

The collision narrative centers on [[MaritimeWarningFragmentation]]. Wireless equipment failure created a passenger-message backlog, ice reports arrived piecemeal and were not combined into one operational picture, and calm water removed the surf that normally helped lookouts see ice. The episode rejects the later story that [[BruceIsmay]] forced a publicity race and instead argues that accepted speed practice, divided communication responsibilities, environmental conditions, and split-second bridge decisions combined into catastrophe.

## Key Claims

- Titanic's third-class cabins, dining, sanitation, ventilation, and common rooms were unusually comfortable for contemporary steerage, though accommodation remained segregated by sex and household status.
- The 712 third-class passengers included substantial British, Irish, Scandinavian, Lebanese, Bulgarian, Belgian, Armenian, and Chinese groups traveling for work, safety, family connection, or a new start.
- [[WhiteStarLine]] sold American opportunity abroad and served mass migration while also discouraging some Eastern European passengers from using its Southampton route.
- Migrant journeys depended on remittances, repeat travelers, interpreters, family ties, and labor intermediaries as well as individual aspiration.
- [[MarconiCompany]] operators Jack Phillips and Harold Bride handled commercial passenger traffic and navigational warnings after an equipment failure produced a backlog.
- Ice warnings from several ships reached Titanic separately and were not consolidated into a single clear risk picture for the bridge.
- The episode rejects the claim that [[BruceIsmay]] pressured [[EdwardSmith|Captain Smith]] to race for New York; Titanic was below full power, while maintaining speed in ice conditions reflected accepted but unsafe practice.
- A clear, moonless, exceptionally calm sea made the iceberg harder to detect because no swell broke visibly against its base.
- [[WilliamMurdoch]] responded to Frederick Fleet's warning by turning and reversing, but the episode argues that the resulting side scrape opened fatal hull damage.

## Key Quotes

> "iceberg right ahead" - Frederick Fleet's reported telephone warning to the bridge at 11:40 p.m.

> "the best third-class accommodation yet provided" - the episode's characterization of Titanic's steerage relative to contemporary liners.
