## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] Advice Line episode pairs [[GuyRaz]] with [[SethBerkowitz]], founder and CEO of [[InsomniaCookies]], to advise [[Cahindo]], [[SaunaSociete|Sauna Société]], and [[Desden]]. Across fashion, wellness accessories, and licensed collegiate products, the discussion argues that founders should narrow their offer, encounter customers where the product is easiest to understand, learn from behavior, and expand only after demand and operating capacity are visible. The cases add [[CustomerEarnedExpansion]], [[ContextualProductEducation]], and [[PurchaserEndUserSplit]] as reusable growth concepts.

## Key Claims
- Insomnia Cookies directly operates roughly 390 bakeries and limits licensing to an airport and three stadiums so that it can protect quality and deploy innovation without franchisee coordination.
- Berkowitz says the company previously opened too quickly in some markets and should have tracked local brand awareness before accelerating expansion.
- Cahindo should concentrate on a small group of proven special-occasion styles, learn who buys them and why, and use pop-ups, trunk shows, artisans, event-focused influencers, and smaller boutiques before broad paid acquisition.
- Sauna Société faces an education problem: customers may understand sauna culture without knowing why they would wear a sauna hat, making spas, studios, resorts, events, sampling, and co-branded gifting stronger early learning surfaces than broad advertising.
- Desden should treat students as the primary consumer even when mothers or grandmothers pay, then use ambassadors, campus moments, collaborations, QR-code offers, and license-level performance data to learn from the actual user.
- Expansion should replicate proven customer value rather than be used to manufacture demand; adjacent products, channels, locations, and licenses should be tested narrowly before wider rollout.
- The advice is strategic rather than a complete financial plan and does not deeply evaluate cash flow, inventory exposure, advertising economics, or licensing costs.

## Key Quotes
> "customer love gives a company permission to grow" - the source summary of Berkowitz's central expansion rule.

> "become excellent at something simple" - the operating lesson drawn from Insomnia Cookies narrowing back to its core offer.

> "follow what customers reveal through their behavior and choices" - the source summary of Berkowitz's closing advice.
