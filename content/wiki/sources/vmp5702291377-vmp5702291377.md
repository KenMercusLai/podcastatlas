## Summary
This archival [[HowIBuiltThis]] interview has [[GuyRaz]] trace [[JimmyWales]] from mathematics, options trading, and [[Bomis]] through [[Nupedia]] and the launch of [[Wikipedia]]. The comparison between Nupedia's expert-led review and Wikipedia's open wiki shows how [[LowBarrierCollaborativeProduction]] can increase both participation and opportunities for correction, while [[DistributedVolunteerGovernance]] explains how volunteers developed moderation and sourcing norms as the site scaled. The later nonprofit and small-donor history connects [[WikimediaFoundation]] to [[MissionAlignedRevenueModel]]: Wales rejected advertising because its incentives could redirect editorial attention away from free global knowledge.

## Key Claims
- Nupedia's seven-stage expert process produced only 21 articles in its first year and still allowed a plagiarized article through, showing that procedural strictness neither guaranteed scale nor eliminated error.
- Wikipedia launched in January 2001 as a wiki-based companion to Nupedia and exceeded Nupedia's article count in roughly two weeks.
- A minimal accurate contribution could be valuable because later participants could expand it, lowering the threshold from writing a finished article to improving a shared artifact.
- When Bomis funding disappeared after the dot-com crash, volunteers developed technical and social responses to vandalism, blocking, editorial disputes, and acceptable content rather than relying on a paid moderation staff.
- Neutral point of view became a core editorial principle through community discussion rather than a complete rule set imposed at launch.
- The 2005 false biography of John Seigenthaler prompted stronger sourcing practices and contributed to stricter treatment of negative claims about living people.
- Wales presents Wikipedia as an orientation layer rather than a final authority: readers should inspect citations, original sources, and discussion pages when claims are disputed.
- Wales says nonprofit status and rejection of advertising protected the mission from pressure to favor commercially attractive topics, markets, and page views.
- After two of three servers failed, Wikipedia's first fundraising campaign reportedly collected nearly $30,000 in about two weeks; many small donations then became the principal funding model.
- Wales attributes Wikipedia's durability to a meaningful public mission, distributed oversight across time zones, patient persuasion, and favorable timing rather than founder control alone.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode reinforces the wiki's claim that public knowledge depends on renewable human contribution while adding a pre-AI case in which open access, volunteer purpose, and dispersed small donations were designed to support one another.
- The episode is primarily Wales's retrospective and gives limited space to co-founders, early editors, critics, staff, donors, or disputes over Wikipedia's governance, labor, bias, and institutional history.
- Article counts, contributor counts, traffic rank, donation totals, operating revenue, personal-wealth estimates, exact policy causation, and claims about misinformation resilience remain episode-scoped because the supplied document is a structured summary rather than a transcript, audited report, or independent history.
