## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Yongping 2 / 59 CE immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-735-13-sui-rugong-zhongsheng-weiyu-tade-xianming-zhen-hanjian-lj2lyie73xk9fubebzwogd-w7hrq|Hanji 735]]. It centers [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]] and his former `Shangshu` teacher [[HuanRongEasternHan|桓荣]], showing how imperial ceremonies at Mingtang, Biyong, the Taichang office, sickbed visits, and funeral rites turn teacher respect into public political order.

The durable synthesis is [[ImperialTeacherHonorRitual|imperial teacher-honor ritual]]: Mingdi does not merely thank an old teacher privately. He uses the emperor's body, seating protocol, scholar audiences, stipend and title rewards, medical care, mourning, and burial grants to make Huan Rong's classical authority visible to officials and students, while the same year also shows royal-kin favor, ancestor-minister sacrifice, Dou-family punishment, Five Suburban seasonal rites, and the Yin Feng murder case as adjacent tests of ritual, kinship, and legal order.

## Key Claims

- Mingdi's 59 CE court begins by tying dynastic authority to ritual order: Mingtang sacrifice, Lingtai observation, amnesty, Biyong archery, and the first Biyong elder-care ceremony make state hierarchy public and embodied.
- Huan Rong's elevation depends on both scholarship and imperial staging: Mingdi names him `wugeng`, listens to him as teacher, has scholars ask questions in front of him, rewards him as Guannei marquis, and makes his authority visible to a large audience.
- Mingdi's teacher respect is political as well as personal; by refusing to explain classics while the teacher is present, walking to Huan Rong's sickbed with texts in hand, and mourning him publicly, Mingdi models Confucian hierarchy for the court.
- The Huan Rong story clarifies that Eastern Han classical authority could be built through ritual performance, public honor, and royal patronage rather than through texts alone.
- Mingdi's special treatment of Zhongshan king Liu Yan shows royal affection as controlled privilege: he lets Liu Yan leave for his fief late, grants guards and riders, and permits unusual movement between capital and fief.
- The Dou Lin and Yin Feng cases show early Mingdi-era discipline of elite and outer-relative households, but the source keeps the broader claim qualified because later outer-relative power remains hard to solve.
- Five Suburban seasonal rites and sacrifices to Xiao He and Huo Guang extend the year as a ritual-order cluster, though the episode's developed contribution remains the Huan Rong teacher branch.

## Key Quotes

> "老师在场，不能由自己讲解。" - Mingdi's refusal to answer a classics question while Huan Rong is present.

> "诸侯、将军、大夫再去探望桓荣时，也不敢乘车直达桓家门口。" - the episode's signal that imperial example changes court conduct.

> "桓荣的成名既靠长期学问积累，也靠东汉皇权提供的舞台。" - episode summary of scholarship plus imperial patronage.
