## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 72-73 CE / Yongping 15-16 prelude immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-742-banchao-chushi-xiyu-37-ren-da-sha-si-fang-ltutsinxtciar0tmpxkge0mnsnp1|Hanji 742]]. It explains [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] pressure through [[SteppePastoralResourcePressure|steppe pastoral resource pressure]] and [[NomadicCavalryRaidingAsymmetry|nomadic cavalry raiding asymmetry]], then follows [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]] convening [[GengBingEasternHan|耿秉]], [[DouGuEasternHan|窦固]], Ma Liao, Liu Zhang, and other officials before the four-route campaign against [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|北匈奴]].

The durable synthesis is that Eastern Han's return to offensive frontier policy is framed as both ecological and political. The source does not excuse raids as mere morality-free survival, but it shifts the explanation from simple greed toward rainfall, pasture, water, overgrazing, winter pressure, border credibility, and Western Regions strategy; Dou Gu's capture of [[YiwuGarrisonLateHan|伊吾]] becomes the concrete campaign result, while [[ZhaiRongEasternHan|翟戎]] shows the honor and punishment pressure around failed frontier command.

## Key Claims

- Xiongnu raiding is explained through harsh steppe ecology: low rainfall, seasonal water dependence, overgrazing risk, and pastoral vulnerability make outside grain and goods structurally attractive.
- Mingdi's court treats renewed Northern Xiongnu raids as a state-credibility problem: avoiding war preserves resources, but unmanaged border attacks can make frontier people lose faith in the court.
- Mingdi pairs active frontier policy with agrarian and Confucian order by performing the ploughing rite, visiting Confucius's former residence, and having royal youths lecture on classics.
- Geng Bing argues that Han Wudi's old frontier success came from taking the Hetao and Hexi corridor, separating Xiongnu from western Qiang support, and that Eastern Han should retake the Western Regions while Northern Xiongnu is weaker.
- The 73 CE campaign sends four mixed Han, Qiang, Hu, Southern Xiongnu, Wuhuan, and Xianbei columns through different frontier gates, showing that Eastern Han offensive capacity depends on allied and submitted frontier manpower.
- Dou Gu and Geng Zhong produce the clear result by reaching Tianshan, attacking the Northern Xiongnu Huyan king, killing more than one thousand, taking Yiwu, and stationing soldiers there.
- Zhai Rong's failed route, prison dismissal, deathbed instruction, and later mourning by Wuhuan, Xianbei, Liaodong officials, and local people show that frontier command reputation can survive formal punishment.
