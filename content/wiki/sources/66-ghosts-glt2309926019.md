# 66. Ghosts

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode with [[RogerClarke]] treats ghosts less as proof of an afterlife than as historical evidence for what different societies wanted the dead to mean. Moving from classical burial stories and medieval moral warnings through Reformation anxiety, Methodism, class-biased witness assessment, [[HintonAmpner]], the [[CockLaneGhost]], and [[BorleyRectory]], it argues that apparitions change with religious conflict, social hierarchy, literary convention, and the institutions that record them.

Its strongest synthesis is [[GhostBeliefAsCulturalHistory]]: an unresolved incident can remain historically useful without becoming verified supernatural evidence. [[MediaTechnologyGhostCulture]] adds the second major thread, showing how newspapers, photography, X-rays, cinema, radio, horror films, Zoom, text messages, and autocorrect supply new forms through which haunting is imagined, circulated, commercialized, and challenged.

## Key Claims

- Ghost traditions change across historical periods; ancient improper-burial stories, medieval moral warnings, Reformation purgatory anxiety, Methodist supernatural proof, and digital-age messages do not describe one stable category.
- English Reformation conflict made ghosts theologically and politically charged because purgatory and returning spirits could signal Catholic belief, while later Gothic conventions reused anti-Catholic fear.
- [[HintonAmpner]] matters because Mary Ricketts left unusually sustained family testimony about a long domestic disturbance, although [[RogerClarke]]'s links to the Battle of Cheriton and [[TheTurnOfTheScrew]] remain interpretations.
- The [[CockLaneGhost]] demonstrates how knocks, murder accusation, drink, scandal, crowds, newspapers, elite visitors, and investigation could turn a hoax into a mass public event.
- [[BorleyRectory]] shows haunting becoming professional investigation and commercial spectacle through [[HarryPrice]], technical equipment, publicity, fraud disputes, fire, photography, ruins, and paranormal tourism.
- Ghost inquiry inherited a class problem when investigators discounted servants' testimony even though servants could be witnesses, suspected tricksters, or people acting under hidden domestic pressures.
- Many strong reports are brief, unresolved incidents rather than complete narratives with an identified dead person, motive, and ending.
- New media do not simply abolish ghosts; they alter what counts as an apparition and how quickly a story can be reproduced.
