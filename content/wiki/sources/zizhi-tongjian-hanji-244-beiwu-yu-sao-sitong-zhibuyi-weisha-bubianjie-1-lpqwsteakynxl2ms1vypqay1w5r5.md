# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》244｜被诬与嫂私通，直不疑为啥不辩解？（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode moves from [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s 143 BCE instructions on escalating doubtful cases and judging leniently to the promotion and conduct of [[ZhiBuyiWesternHan|直不疑]]. Its durable contribution is a bounded form of [[AccusationResponseRestraint|诬陷回应克制]]: Zhi reportedly compensates a colleague who wrongly accuses him of theft and later declines to answer an impossible sexual accusation, behavior the host connects to low-profile Huang-Lao conduct and [[DaoistNaturalWuwei|道家自然无为]].

The opening also says [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] had already entered Jingdi's “psychological blacklist,” lost the chancellorship after claiming illness, and would later be tested at a meal without chopsticks. The supplied episode stops before that meal and its consequences are narrated, so the source establishes only the setup rather than the promised causal story.

## Key Claims

- In Jingdi's 后元元年, officials are told to refer doubtful criminal cases upward, avoid treating a lower official's corrected judgment as a fault, and decide litigation leniently.
- The episode records a general amnesty, a five-day relaxation of restrictions on drinking and feasting, and an 上庸 earthquake whose location, duration, damage, and modern estimated magnitude are presented as source-scoped details.
- After chancellor Liu She is removed, [[WeiWanWesternHan|魏绾]] becomes chancellor and Zhi Buyi becomes 御史大夫.
- When a fellow palace guard wrongly accuses Zhi of taking money, Zhi accepts blame and reimburses the loss; the truth emerges when the colleague who mistakenly took the money returns it.
- When someone later accuses Zhi of adultery with a sister-in-law, the episode says he has no elder brother and nevertheless does not go to the emperor to defend himself.
- The host interprets these episodes through Zhi's Huang-Lao learning, low visibility, deference to existing administrative practice, reluctance to advertise merit, and reputation as a “长者.”
- The modern workplace lesson is deliberately narrow: immediate self-defense is not always necessary, but the source does not establish that silence is safe or effective under every accusation or institution.

## Key Quotes

> “执法者处理诉讼时务必从宽。” - the episode's summary of Jingdi's judicial instruction.

> “直不疑从来没有去皇帝那里辩白。” - the conduct claim that anchors the episode's accusation-response lesson.

- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]], [[WeiWanWesternHan|魏绾]], and [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] - late-reign judicial policy, personnel change, and the incomplete setup to Zhou's downfall.
- [[DaoistNaturalWuwei|道家自然无为]] - the host's philosophical explanation for Zhi's low-profile, non-contentious conduct.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The theft and sexual-accusation anecdotes are presented as character stories, not independently verified reconstructions of motive or outcome.
- The opening claims that the missing chopsticks helped move Jingdi toward killing Zhou Yafu, but the supplied episode ends before narrating the meal; that causal claim remains unresolved here.
- The exact offices, decree wording, dates, drinking-policy sequence, earthquake coordinates and magnitude, private thoughts, and the claim that Huang-Lao learning explains Zhi's silence remain source-scoped pending comparison with primary and modern historical evidence.
