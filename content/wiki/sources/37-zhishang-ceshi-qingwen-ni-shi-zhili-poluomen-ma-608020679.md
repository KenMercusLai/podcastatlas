# 37.智商测试：请问你是智力婆罗门吗？

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode uses [[ZhishangCeshiBook|《智商测试》]] to trace how [[IntelligenceTesting|intelligence testing]] grew out of nineteenth-century measurement enthusiasm, [[FrancisGalton|Francis Galton / 弗朗西斯·高尔顿]]'s hereditarian thinking, [[EugenicGovernance|eugenic governance]], and later education, immigration, military, judicial, and state-policy uses. The hosts do not deny that cognitive differences exist; their central warning is that tests become dangerous when a number is turned into [[PsychometricSocialSorting|psychometric social sorting]] for schooling, entry, reproduction, employment, or dignity. The closing argument connects the history to contemporary school anxiety, "智力婆罗门" posturing, and the need for [[RationalHumility]] when one holds intellectual or educational advantage.

## Key Claims
- [[FrancisGalton|Galton]] matters because the episode treats his statistical and measurement talent as inseparable from his class, race, heredity, and [[EugenicGovernance|eugenic]] commitments.
- [[CharlesDarwin|Charles Darwin / 查尔斯·达尔文]] influenced Galton through evolution and heredity, but the source distinguishes Darwin's caution from later [[SocialDarwinism|social Darwinist]] applications.
- The episode reads early [[IntelligenceTesting|intelligence testing]] as a mixed project: [[AlfredBinet|Alfred Binet / 阿尔弗雷德·宾内]] and [[TheodoreSimon|Theodore Simon / 西奥多·西蒙]] built a school-support tool, while later institutional users turned testing into sorting and exclusion.
- [[HenryGoddard|Henry H. Goddard / 亨利·戈达德]] and the [[KallikakFamily|Kallikak family]] case show how poverty, sexuality, neighborhood reputation, criminality, and domestic conditions could be folded into supposedly scientific diagnosis.
- Immigration, army recruitment, and school placement made [[CulturalBiasInTesting|cultural bias in testing]] politically consequential because language, class, exhaustion, education access, and test familiarity all shaped measured performance.
- The episode treats [[CarrieBuck|Carrie Buck]]'s forced sterilization as the clearest warning that test labels can become bodily state violence when courts, institutions, and gendered shame align.
- [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany / 纳粹德国]] appears as the extreme endpoint where eugenic screening, administrative labels, disability persecution, and killing policies fused.
- [[EducationalTrackingByTesting|educational tracking by testing]] can look meritocratic while reproducing class advantage, especially when families with more resources can prepare children for supposedly neutral selection.
- [[MeritocraticArrogance|meritocratic arrogance]] is the present-day moral target: test scores, school labels, and vocabulary can become tools for contempt rather than self-knowledge.
- The source's practical boundary is that IQ-style tests may help an individual notice strengths and limits, but should not be used to assign worth, citizenship, reproduction rights, or social rank.

## Key Quotes
> "智商测试笑笑就好" - the episode's closing advice against turning a score into status.

> "check your privilege" - the hosts' shorthand for converting advantage into humility rather than contempt.
