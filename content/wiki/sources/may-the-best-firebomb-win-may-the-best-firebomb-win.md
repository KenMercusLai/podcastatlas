# May the Best Firebomb Win

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode reconstructs the wartime development of [[NapalmWarfare|napalm]] by [[HoytHottel]], [[LouisFieser]], and [[EBHirschberg|E. B. Hirschberg]]. It follows a competition among academic and industrial chemists from gel formulation and improvised prototypes through standardized trials, the M69 bomb, and attacks on meticulously reconstructed Japanese and German buildings at Dugway.

The episode then places that technical success beside the Bomber Mafia's precision-bombing doctrine. Napalm's ability to ignite combustible urban housing made mass area attack more practical, turning a weapon optimized through neutral-seeming measures into a strategic and moral temptation for officers who had claimed civilian populations were not their target.

## Key Claims

- Hottel organized a National Defense Research Committee competition to improve incendiary weapons, creating a smaller chemistry-centered counterpart to the physicists' Manhattan Project.
- Fieser and Hirschberg learned that viscous gelled gasoline could scatter as burning globules, adhere to surfaces, and transfer heat longer than ordinary gasoline.
- The formulation named napalm combined gasoline with aluminum naphthenate and aluminum palmitate; its effectiveness depended on balancing adhesion with dispersal rather than maximizing thickness alone.
- Hirschberg translated the gel into a deployable device using TNT and white phosphorus, while repeated field tests converted consistency, impact, temperature, altitude, and dispersal into optimization variables.
- Napalm defeated competing Standard Oil, Kodak, DuPont, and British thermite designs in the episode's account, culminating in the M69 incendiary bomb.
- Accurate Japanese and German residential replicas at Dugway made civilian-built environments experimental test systems; the reported Japanese-house trials produced uncontrollable category-A fires in 68 percent of napalm tests.
- The resulting capability intensified the conflict between precision bombing and area bombing because a demonstrably effective weapon made urban civilian destruction operationally attractive without making it morally permissible.
