# 425. The History of Chocolate

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows cacao from uncertain Olmec ritual contexts and Maya and Aztec social use through Spanish adoption, European drinking culture, Caribbean plantation production, and British industrial manufacture. Its central historical distinction is that chocolate remained primarily a bitter, sweetened, medicinal, elite, or breakfast drink for centuries before cocoa pressing and factory production made solid eating chocolate commercially scalable.

The second half uses [[Quakers]], [[Cadbury]], and [[Bournville]] to connect temperance, industrial technology, product purity, advertising, worker welfare, model-village planning, wartime supply, and falling prices to mass consumption. The episode therefore joins [[ChocolateDrinkToSolidTransition]], [[ColonialChocolateCommodityChain]], and [[QuakerIndustrialPaternalism]], while its Cadbury sponsorship and light treatment of Indigenous and plantation labor history require explicit source-scope caution.

## Key Claims

- Cacao served ritual, diplomatic, tribute, barter, and beverage roles in Mesoamerica, though the episode repeatedly marks the precise Olmec and early-contact meanings as uncertain.
- European adoption depended on sweetening with sugar, honey, or vanilla and on debates that recast chocolate as a courtly gift, breakfast drink, medicine, aphrodisiac, and fast-compatible liquid.
- Britain's chocolate trade was tied to the 1655 capture of Jamaica, Caribbean plantation production, enslaved African labor, sugar, and wider triangular-trade systems.
- [[ChocolateDrinkToSolidTransition]] accelerated after the Van Houtens' 1820s press separated cocoa powder and cocoa butter, enabling manufacturers to recombine cocoa products with sugar as solid eating chocolate.
- Quaker exclusion from elite institutions, temperance commitments, trade networks, and industrial investment helped Fry and [[Cadbury]] become major British chocolate manufacturers.
- [[Bournville]] joined improved working conditions, housing, recreation, benefits, and pensions to [[QuakerIndustrialPaternalism|Quaker industrial paternalism]] and a deliberately cultivated company culture.
- Dairy Milk, advertising, wartime provisioning, the 1919 Cadbury-Fry combination, lower prices, and expanding household consumption turned chocolate into an everyday British mass comfort.

## Key Quotes

> "Absolutely pure, therefore best." - Cadbury slogan cited by the episode as part of its Victorian quality and advertising strategy.

> "exotic, exciting, erotic, medicinal" - the hosts' summary of chocolate's European identity before its solid-food transformation.
