## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode uses a conversation with [[KirstenKorosek]] of [[TechCrunch]] to assess why 2025 became a breakout year for robotaxis. It presents [[Waymo]] as the visible leader, while also tracking [[Zoox]], [[Tesla]], [[Uber]], [[Wayve]], [[AVRide|AV Ride]], [[WeRide]], [[PonyAI|Pony.ai]], and [[Momenta]] as part of a wider autonomous-mobility field.

The episode's main wiki contribution is to separate deployment momentum from proof of a durable business. Commercial permits, freeway service, ride volume, city expansion, and global competitors show that robotaxis are becoming ordinary urban infrastructure, but [[RobotaxiEconomics]], [[PathToProfitability]], [[AutonomousVehicleRegulatoryPatchwork]], and [[RobotaxiLocalAcceptance]] remain unresolved.

## Key Claims
- 2025 made robotaxis more visible: [[Waymo]] Jaguar EVs became common on city streets and recently reached freeways, while [[Uber]] and [[Zoox]] expanded fleets and [[Tesla]] launched a limited CyberCab service in Austin.
- Korosek frames the current moment as the result of years of development, testing, hype, consolidation, and company failures rather than a sudden technical leap.
- [[Waymo]] and [[Zoox]] are presented as survivors of that consolidation, with Zoox now owned by [[Amazon]].
- Waymo's August 2023 [[California]] commercial permits are treated as a turning point, followed by expansion momentum in 2024 and a stronger push in 2025.
- Waymo was giving about 250,000 rides per week in April 2025, and its next stated goal is one million rides.
- Zoox remains a company to watch, but its non-traditional vehicle design creates a federal regulatory issue: it has an exemption to demonstrate vehicles and wants permission for commercial service.
- Other companies to watch include UK-based [[Wayve]], Texas-based [[AVRide|AV Ride]], and activity in autonomous trucking and middle-mile transport.
- Korosek says robotaxi businesses are not profitable yet based on available evidence; for public companies in robotaxis or self-driving trucks, expenses are outpacing revenue.
- Waymo's internal numbers are not public, but Korosek says her sources indicate expenses still outpace revenue even though the service is generating money.
- The regulatory environment remains fragmented: [[California]] has more demanding state-level rules, [[Texas]] and [[Arizona]] are lighter-touch hubs, and there is no single comprehensive federal rule for all companies.
- Public response varies by city. Santa Monica officials added rules for properties where Waymo charges and maintains vehicles after noise complaints, while earlier orange-cone actions show how low-tech protest can disrupt autonomous vehicles.
- Korosek is watching Waymo's planned expansion into Dallas, Denver, Detroit, Houston, San Antonio, Nashville, and Miami in 2026.
- Chinese competitors such as [[WeRide]], [[PonyAI|Pony.ai]], and [[Momenta]] are expanding, with Pony.ai aiming for a global fleet of 3,000 robotaxis by the end of 2026.

## Key Quotes
> "boring story" - Korosek's shorthand for the years of development and testing behind the visible 2025 deployment wave.

> "patchwork" - the regulatory structure around autonomous vehicles across state and federal authority.

> "one million rides" - Waymo's stated next ride-volume goal after reaching about 250,000 weekly rides in April 2025.
