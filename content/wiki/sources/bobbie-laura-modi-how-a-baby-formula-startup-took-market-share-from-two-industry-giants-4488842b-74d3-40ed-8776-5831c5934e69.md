## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] episode follows [[LauraModi]] from [[Google]] and [[Airbnb]] into founding [[Bobbie]] after her own painful experience with breastfeeding pressure and buying [[InfantFormula|infant formula]]. The episode's main wiki contribution is a regulated CPG case where [[FormulaFeedingStigma]], [[InfantFormulaRegulatoryMoat]], [[RegulatedCPGMessaging]], [[FormulaSupplyChainResilience]], and [[WICFormulaContracting]] shape both the product and the company strategy. It also extends the wiki's [[ConsumerBrandMoat]] branch by showing that in a parent-and-baby category, trust depends on emotional permission, FDA compliance, manufacturing control, subscription reliability, and policy engagement.

## Key Claims
- The U.S. infant formula market is presented as a roughly $6 billion category dominated by [[Enfamil]] and [[Similac]], with little perceived change for decades.
- [[LauraModi]]'s founder insight came from a 2016 feeding experience: breastfeeding difficulty, infection, a late-night formula purchase, and shame around asking for access to locked formula.
- The episode frames Bobbie's position as pro-choice for feeding, not anti-breastfeeding: Modi calls breast milk irreplaceable while arguing that many families need high-quality alternatives.
- Modi saw demand signals in U.S. parents informally importing European formula, and she built Bobbie around a European-style nutritional frame made for the U.S. market.
- The first product path was risky: Bobbie launched a German-made pilot as a "companion formula" to 93 San Francisco families, but the [[FoodAndDrugAdministration|FDA]] treated it as infant formula and issued a national recall.
- The recall became a trust and compliance turning point because Bobbie apologized, stopped fighting the regulator publicly, and later won manufacturing support from [[Perrigo]].
- FDA approval and manufacturing conversion took about 18 months plus another six-month wait for a green-light letter, forcing Bobbie to build community and content before revenue.
- [[SarahHardy]] joined as co-founder and COO because Modi wanted a culture-first partner from [[Airbnb]] rather than only a scientist as co-founder.
- Bobbie launched in January 2021 as a direct-to-consumer formula and quickly exceeded first-year expectations, reaching $16 million in sales against an initial hope of about $4 million.
- Demand quickly exposed a forecasting and inventory problem: formula lead times of five to six months forced Bobbie to protect existing subscribers and turn off growth.
- The 2022 formula shortage, tied in the episode to an [[AbbottNutrition]] [[Similac]] recall and facility shutdown, made Bobbie's subscriber-protection choice strategically central.
- Bobbie later bought [[NaturesOne]] and its Ohio facility, kept the [[BabysOnly]] toddler-formula brand, and pursued owned manufacturing after realizing dependence on one contract manufacturer was a survival risk.
- The episode says Bobbie was doing hundreds of millions of dollars in sales in 2026, had just under 4% U.S. market share, and wanted to reach about 10% rather than become the largest incumbent.
- [[WIC]] is presented as a structural limit: state contracts can restrict eligible families to one winning formula brand, and Bobbie is not yet large enough to serve that program.
- [[BobbieForChange]] extends the business into advocacy, including a mentioned bill for insurance coverage of infant formula for women who have had breast cancer.

## Key Quotes
> "liquid gold" - Modi's phrase for breast milk while explaining why formula should not be framed as an attack on breastfeeding.

> "companion formula" - the label Bobbie used for its German-made pilot before the FDA recall.

> "hustle with heart" - Modi's phrase for the mix of work, luck, and mission behind Bobbie's progress.
