# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》408｜废帝界天花板，汉废帝刘贺下岗啦！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the procedural core of [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]]'s deposition of [[LiuHeHaihunHou|刘贺]] before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-409-hanxuandi-liubingyi-cong-yuzhong-zouchulai-de-chuanqi-huangdi-ljyxm9nd5rvheusna29wkxuraqux|Hanji 409]]'s aftermath. It follows Huo Guang from a high-official meeting, through [[ShangguanTaihouWesternHan|上官太后]]'s formal authorization, the isolation of Liu He's Changyi followers, palace control of Liu He himself, and the public reading of the indictment that makes deposition appear as protection of ritual, ancestral temples, and the state.

The durable synthesis is that deposing an emperor required more than coercive capacity. The episode presents Huo Guang's success as procedural legitimacy work: ministers are made to share responsibility, the dowager supplies formal command, the former ruler's support network is physically separated, and Liu He's alleged failures are organized as mourning, palace, seal, reward, sexual, ritual, and administrative offenses rather than as a bare power struggle.

## Key Claims

- Huo Guang first converts elite silence into collective endorsement by convening senior officials, accepting blame for disorder, and letting the meeting become a visible ministerial consensus.
- The deposition is routed through the fifteen-year-old Shangguan Taihou so that an empress-dowager edict, not only Huo Guang's private force, authorizes palace closure and exclusion of Changyi followers.
- Huo Guang separates Liu He from the roughly two hundred Changyi officials who entered with him, while [[ZhangAnshiWesternHan|张安世]] and the Yulin troops surround and imprison them.
- Liu He is guarded carefully because his sudden death or suicide would let Huo Guang be accused of killing his lord.
- The impeachment indictment makes ritual and moral unfitness central: Liu He is accused of violating mourning, misusing the imperial seal and tally, bringing old followers into the palace, staging entertainment before Han Zhaodi's coffin, violating palace boundaries, distributing honors and goods improperly, and sacrificing to his father before proper ancestral rites.
- The figure of twenty-seven days and 1,127 orders turns Liu He's short reign into evidence of administrative and ritual disorder.
- The indictment cites filial piety, Spring and Autumn precedent, ancestral-temple service, and the people's security to frame removal as dynastic protection rather than ordinary ministerial usurpation.

## Key Quotes

> "可" - the episode's climactic one-word dowager approval after the indictment is read.

> "二十七天" and "一千一百二十七次" - the numerical contrast used to dramatize Liu He's alleged disorder.
