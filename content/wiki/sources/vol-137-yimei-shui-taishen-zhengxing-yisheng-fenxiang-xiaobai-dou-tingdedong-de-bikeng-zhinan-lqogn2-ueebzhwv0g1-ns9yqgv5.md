# VOL.137医美水太深！整形医生分享小白都听得懂的避坑指南

## Summary
This [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode has [[Atang|阿汤]], plastic-surgery doctor [[LuluPlasticSurgeon|Lulu]], and vascular-surgery doctor [[FengXiaoliuVascularSurgeon|冯小六]] frame medical aesthetics as medicine rather than ordinary beauty consumption. It connects illegal injectable materials, venue and clinician verification, procedure grading, sales-led consultation, risk-benefit tradeoffs, recovery, irreversibility, and personal experience to a practical rule: verify the institution, clinician, procedure scope, and product before letting price, familiarity, or promised speed drive the decision.

## Key Claims
- Illegal injectable substitutes can create infection, tissue incompatibility, migration, difficult removal, and life-threatening complications, while low price and informal settings make accountability weaker.
- A venue's registered name, permitted scope, anesthesia capability, and procedure grade matter; a beauty salon, medical-aesthetic clinic, and plastic-surgery hospital should not be treated as interchangeable.
- The person assessing a patient should be a qualified clinician rather than only a sales consultant, and the treating doctor's title, specialty qualification, and registered practice location should match the intervention.
- Elective appearance decisions should balance likely benefit against complication severity, recovery time, pain, functional cost, reversibility, budget, and the person's actual goal.
- Marketing can manufacture or intensify dissatisfaction through low-price offers, friend-like referrals, disguised patient outreach, prestige titles, and sales incentives.
- First-time patients should begin cautiously, understand pain and downtime, and avoid stacking multiple procedures before learning how they personally tolerate the risks and recovery.
- Personal reports of light-based treatment, water-light injections, or fillers describe individual experience and do not establish universal effectiveness, candidacy, or safety.

## Key Quotes
> “对于你个人来说，不管发生率是多少，落到你头上就是百分之百。” - Lulu's explanation of why low probability does not make an elective complication trivial.
