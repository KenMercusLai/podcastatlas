# How to Better Regulate Your Emotions | Dr. Marc Brackett

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[MarcBrackett]] about emotional intelligence, emotion regulation, vulnerability, schools, leadership, masculinity, AI companionship, and practical regulation tools. Brackett's core synthesis is that emotions are not inherently good or bad; regulation means using them wisely in relation to goals, context, relationships, and long-term values. The episode strengthens [[EmotionRegulationToolkit]], [[EmotionalGranularity]], and [[EmotionalCoRegulationFit]] while adding [[RULERFramework]], [[MetaMoment]], [[YaleCenterForEmotionalIntelligence]], and a source-scoped school and leadership branch.

## Key Claims
- Brackett defines emotion regulation as using emotions wisely to achieve goals, not erasing feelings or expressing every feeling in every context.
- The episode separates accepting emotions from unfiltered expression: anxiety, anger, happiness, sadness, shame, and excitement can all carry information while still needing context-sensitive response.
- Brackett's PRIME frame treats regulation as strategy selection: preventing unwanted emotions, reducing difficult ones, initiating, maintaining, or enhancing useful states.
- Precise emotion vocabulary matters because anxiety, stress, pressure, fear, sadness, disappointment, shame, and excitement call for different communication and regulation strategies.
- [[MetaMoment]] names the pause between trigger and response: breathe, create space, imagine the best version of oneself in the role, and choose a deliberate response.
- [[RULERFramework]] summarizes emotional intelligence as recognizing, understanding, labeling, expressing, and regulating emotions, and Brackett argues these skills are definable, measurable, teachable, and predictive of outcomes.
- Vulnerability is framed as strategic honesty rather than emotional dumping: useful disclosure names the feeling and clarifies what the person is doing with it.
- Boys and men are described as often more inhibited around sadness, disappointment, and shame, with anger socially easier to express; the episode therefore extends male vulnerability and masculinity pages without treating those patterns as fixed biology.
- School and workplace emotion skills require shared language and system-level practice across leaders, teachers, students, parents, and teams.
- Brackett warns that adolescent AI companionship or AI-as-therapist use may offer some advice but should not replace human connection, emotional presence, or professional support.

## Key Quotes
> "using emotions wisely" - Brackett's practical definition of emotion regulation.

> "fine, okay, or upset" - examples of vague emotion labels that weaken strategy choice.

> "best version of oneself" - the values-oriented prompt inside the meta-moment.
