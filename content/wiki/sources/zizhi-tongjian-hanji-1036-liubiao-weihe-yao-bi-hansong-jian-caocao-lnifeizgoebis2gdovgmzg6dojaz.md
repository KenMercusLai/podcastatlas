# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1036丨刘表为何要逼韩嵩见曹操？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the setup immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1037-zhongyu-caocao-or-liubiao-jiemi-hansong-de-zhongyi-jueze-lomrbxffcu6euantozh2fjvrykva|Hanji 1037]]. In 199 CE, [[YuanShao|袁绍]] asks [[LiuBiao|刘表]] for support against [[CaoCao|曹操]], but Liu Biao neither aids Yuan Shao nor openly joins Cao Cao, creating the [[FenceSittingStrategicExposure|骑墙式战略暴露]] that [[HanSongLateHan|韩嵩]] and [[LiuXianLateHan|刘先]] warn against.

The core mission problem is not simple cowardice. Han Song says he can go to [[Xudu|许都]] if Liu Biao has already decided to submit to the court-Cao side; if Liu Biao is still undecided, then any appointment from [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] will alter Han Song's formal duty and make him unable to remain only Liu Biao's private subordinate. Liu Biao misreads this as fear or evasion and forces him to go, setting up the later imprisonment branch.

## Key Claims
- The opening "高级的苦" frame contrasts passive consumption with goal-directed effort, preparing the historical question of whether a person actively chooses a costly path or merely endures drift.
- In 199 CE, Yuan Shao seeks military help from Liu Biao, but Liu Biao answers without sending troops and also avoids materially helping Cao Cao.
- [[HanSongLateHan|韩嵩]] and [[LiuXianLateHan|刘先]] argue that neutrality has a cost: with roughly 100,000 troops, Liu Biao cannot indefinitely offend both sides and still remain safely outside the Yuan-Cao contest.
- Han Song, Liu Xian, and the source's account of [[KuaiYue|蒯越]] judge Cao Cao as the likelier winner because he fights well, uses strategy, and attracts capable people.
- Han Song conditionally accepts the mission only if Liu Biao first commits to respecting the emperor and submitting to Cao Cao's court-centered order.
- Han Song's "圣达节，次守节" explanation frames his concern as a role-duty problem: once he receives a court office, the recognized sovereign's command outranks Liu Biao's local patronage.
- Liu Biao's coercion turns an information-gathering mission into a loyalty trap because he wants the option value of probing Cao Cao without first accepting the obligations created by court contact.
