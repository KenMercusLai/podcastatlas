# 371. The 1973 Chilean Coup: General Pinochet Seizes Power (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[Chile]] from the failed June 1973 Tanquetazo through [[CarlosPrats|Carlos Prats]]'s resignation, [[AugustoPinochet|Augusto Pinochet]]'s elevation, the September 11 coup, and [[SalvadorAllende|Salvador Allende]]'s death. It argues that United States interference helped create the climate for the coup without reducing Chilean officers to foreign puppets, and that domestic class conflict, institutional deadlock, military choice, and Cold War pressure must be held together.

The episode rejects rehabilitation of Pinochet. Whatever fears Allende's opponents held or economic claims later admirers made, the post-coup arrests, torture, killings, disappearances, personal dictatorship, and corruption were choices rather than automatic consequences of removing the government.

## Key Claims

- Allende's land redistribution, nationalization, and welfare expansion built loyalty among poor and rural supporters while inflation, shortages, black markets, and constitutional conflict intensified middle-class and conservative fear.
- The United States had links to Chilean officers, welcomed a coup, and helped create a hostile political climate, but the episode rejects the claim that the CIA remotely designed the September 11 operation.
- The failed Tanquetazo exposed both military discontent and the importance of constitutionalist army commander [[CarlosPrats]], whose resignation allowed Pinochet to replace him on August 22.
- Pinochet joined the coup late enough to have been underestimated, then displayed operational ruthlessness during the attack on La Moneda and refused to surrender the rotating junta leadership afterward.
- Allende refused exile, delivered a final radio address, and died by suicide during the assault; the episode distinguishes the forensic account from the later story that he died fighting.
- Mass detention, stadium torture and killing, the Caravan of Death, and DINA converted a successful coup into organized state terror.
- Cold War context and Chile's internal polarization explain support for the coup but do not excuse the destruction of democratic government or Pinochet's continuing repression.

## Key Quotes

> "The CIA did not mastermind the coup from Washington" - the hosts' rejection of a single-puppet-master account while retaining U.S. responsibility for interference and encouragement.

> "Individuals in power have choices" - the episode's final standard for judging Pinochet's post-coup violence.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction found. The episode explicitly preserves a disputed causal balance: U.S. actions helped create the conditions and encouraged plotters, but Chilean actors retained agency and the CIA is not presented as the operational author of the coup.
- Allende's possible plan to announce a referendum, the precise timing of Pinochet's commitment, intercepted-command interpretation, casualty estimates, and political motives remain source-scoped. The episode treats the 2011 forensic finding of suicide as much firmer than the revolutionary martyr story of death in combat.
