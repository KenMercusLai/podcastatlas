# 327: Coronations: The Deep History (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces [[BritishCoronationRitual|British coronation]] beneath its later pageantry to [[EdgarThePeaceful|Edgar's]] 973 ceremony, earlier British and English inauguration practices, Roman and Carolingian models, and biblical royal theology. It presents the rite as a layered political technology in which place, regalia, oath, prostration, crowning, and [[RoyalAnointingTradition|anointing]] join sacred service to claims of dynastic and territorial authority.

The strongest synthesis is that continuity does not require an unchanged ceremony or an uncontested artifact. Westminster, the Liber Regalis, the anointing spoon, the coronation chair, and the [[StoneOfScone|Stone of Scone]] carry authority because institutions repeatedly reinterpret them; the ceremony can therefore preserve very old symbolic structures while adapting to conquest, confession, state-building, and modern media expectations.

## Key Claims

- Edgar's delayed 973 coronation at Bath linked a more unitary English kingdom, Roman-imperial imagery, Pentecost, royal oath, humility before God, and the authority of [[Dunstan]].
- English coronation is layered rather than invented at one moment: older martial and territorial inauguration customs were Christianized and then connected to Roman, Carolingian, biblical, Syrian, and Egyptian precedents.
- Westminster Abbey, Edward the Confessor's memory, the Cosmati pavement, the Liber Regalis, and surviving regalia make institutional continuity visible across dynastic and confessional change.
- The [[StoneOfScone|Stone of Scone]] shows that a contested object's political force can depend more on inherited belief, seizure, placement, and territorial symbolism than on a provable origin story.
- [[RoyalAnointingTradition|Royal anointing]] gave rulers sacred legitimacy beyond inheritance or conquest by adapting biblical ideas of consecrated kingship and binding the monarch to service under God.
- [[ElizabethII|Elizabeth II's]] understanding of anointing and the planned privacy around [[CharlesIII|Charles III's]] anointing preserve a sacred inner act inside a mass public event.
- Modern reception remains unresolved: historical depth may produce awe, antiquarian interest, patriotic feeling, skepticism, or absurdity rather than one shared interpretation.
