## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode follows [[AmyScott]] to Duck, North Carolina, where [[Vesta]] placed more than 8,000 metric tons of olivine on the sea floor to test [[OceanAlkalinityEnhancement]]. The segment explains [[OlivineCarbonRemoval]] as a proposed way to help seawater convert CO2 into bicarbonate and increase the ocean's chemical capacity to store carbon.

The source is cautious rather than promotional. [[JacquelineSentiner]] of [[HourglassClimate]] explains the mineral chemistry, while [[AdamSubhas]] of the [[WoodsHoleOceanographicInstitution]] frames alkaline minerals as an ocean "antacid" and stresses unresolved questions around scale, speed, tradeoffs, and effects on marine life.

## Key Claims
- The ocean already absorbs a large share of human carbon emissions, but that absorption has contributed to [[OceanAcidification]] and harm to shell-forming organisms and other marine life.
- [[Vesta]] placed more than 8,000 metric tons of olivine on the sea floor near Duck, North Carolina, as a field demonstration.
- [[JacquelineSentiner]] says olivine can dissolve in seawater and convert CO2 into bicarbonate, a carbon form already present in the ocean and used by many organisms to build shells.
- The episode frames [[OceanAlkalinityEnhancement]] as an acceleration of a natural geological process rather than as a fully proven climate tool.
- Researchers are sampling ocean chemistry at different offshore locations to see whether the olivine changes seawater conditions as expected.
- [[AdamSubhas]] says the field still needs evidence on whether ocean alkalinity enhancement can scale, work quickly enough, and avoid damaging tradeoffs.
- [[Vesta]] estimates the Duck project could remove and store about 5,000 tons of CO2 after accounting for project-related emissions.
- The company says olivine could eventually be used in coastal restoration projects if the approach works with minimal marine-life impact.
- The episode points listeners toward [[HowWeSurvive]] for broader [[MarineCarbonRemoval]] and geoengineering reporting.

## Key Quotes
> "antacid" - the episode's metaphor for how alkaline materials can neutralize CO2 in seawater.

> "more than 8,000 metric tons" - the reported amount of olivine placed on the sea floor by [[Vesta]].

> "5,000 tons" - [[Vesta]]'s estimate of net CO2 removal from the Duck project.
