# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1111丨曹操最痛的一年：儿子夭折，求联姻挨怼？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode joins a family-and-succession crisis around [[CaoCao|曹操]] to a Jiangdong pacification campaign. After the death of thirteen-year-old [[CaoChongLateHan|曹冲]], Cao Cao's remark to [[CaoPi|曹丕]] makes the political consequence explicit: the loss is his own disaster but benefits the surviving sons, suggesting that affection had made Cao Chong a serious potential successor even without a documented formal designation.

Cao Cao then asks [[BingYuanLateHan|邴原]] to join Cao Chong posthumously to Bing Yuan's dead daughter. Bing Yuan refuses on ritual grounds, and Cao Cao accepts the correction. The episode's second half follows [[HeQiLateHan|贺齐]], who spends more than a month reconnoitering a fortified mountain position, fixes the defenders' attention with noise and frontal display, and sends selected troops up an apparently impassable cliff before [[SunQuan|孙权]] turns victory into the new Xindu commandery.

## Key Claims

- Cao Chong dies from illness at thirteen after Cao Cao prays for his recovery; the episode connects Cao Cao's grief to regret over having killed Hua Tuo but does not establish a diagnosis or counterfactual cure.
- Cao Cao tells Cao Pi that Cao Chong's death is his own misfortune but the other sons' good fortune, and the episode treats this as evidence that Cao Chong had become a plausible succession contender.
- Cao Pi is later said to acknowledge that he might not have obtained the realm had Cao Chong lived.
- Cao Cao proposes a posthumous marriage between Cao Chong and Bing Yuan's deceased daughter, but Bing Yuan argues that marriage for dead children violates ritual propriety.
- Bing Yuan grounds his refusal in role consistency: Cao Cao valued him for principled conduct, so accepting an improper request would destroy the very reason for that trust.
- Cao Cao accepts Bing Yuan's criticism and abandons the posthumous-marriage plan, showing that grief-driven preference can still be checked by direct counsel.
- In Jiangdong, He Qi refuses a costly frontal attack against a steep mountain stronghold and spends more than a month examining terrain.
- After finding traces of a hidden ascent, He Qi uses drums and shouting to concentrate the defenders and their rolling-log and stone defenses at the front.
- Selected troops climb the cliff by rope ladder at night, attack from the rear, and collapse the stronghold; Sun Quan then creates Xindu commandery and appoints He Qi administrator.

## Key Quotes

> “此我之不幸，而汝曹之幸也。” — the succession consequence attributed to Cao Cao after Cao Chong's death.

> “如果此时答应，便会成为没有操守的俗人。” — the episode's rendering of Bing Yuan's consistency argument.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is recorded. The source extends He Qi's Hanji 1061 pacification profile with a terrain-led operation and Xindu appointment.
- Succession qualification: Cao Cao's and Cao Pi's reported remarks support Cao Chong as a serious potential contender, not as a formally designated heir.
- Medical qualification: Cao Cao's regret over Hua Tuo does not establish that Hua Tuo could have diagnosed or cured Cao Chong.
- Source-scope caution: the illness, prayers, exact dialogue and physical reactions, the identity and legal status of Bing Yuan's daughter, force and household counts, month-long reconnaissance, cliff traces, rope-ladder mechanics, place readings, and administrative sequence require comparison with underlying historical texts.
