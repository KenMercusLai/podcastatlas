## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode connects three institutional signals: worsening [[PISA]] results and [[CrossNationalLearningDecline]], the expansion of [[Miami]] as a business and wealth center within [[SouthFloridaTechMigration]], and the [[JeremyVineShow]] as a case of [[PhoneInRadioPublicMood]]. Across the segments, visible success or persistence—technology adoption, mobile capital, or stable radio listening—does not by itself establish durable learning, urban resilience, or representative public opinion.

## Key Claims
- The episode says average 15-year-old performance in mathematics and reading across rich countries has fallen to a level associated with 14-year-olds a decade earlier, with decline beginning before COVID-19 and worsening during and after the pandemic.
- [[PISA]] is presented as testing whether students can apply mathematics, science, and reading knowledge to practical problems, not merely recall curriculum content.
- Absence, pandemic disruption, grade inflation, classroom disorder, and haphazard education-technology deployment are offered as possible contributors, but the segment does not isolate their causal weights.
- AI is too recent to explain most of the measured decline; the latest survey evidence is said to show heavier use among weaker students, while benefit depends on whether it supports learning or substitutes for producing an answer.
- East Asian systems are presented as combining strong social expectations, paid supplementary lessons, intensive teacher preparation, and in some cases fewer better-paid teachers with larger classes; England is presented as a European case of stable or improving performance after curriculum and inspection changes.
- [[Miami]] is said to have expanded through domestic migration, low taxes, business-friendly politics, Latin American and Gulf capital, property development, corporate services, and proximity to [[DonaldTrump]]'s South Florida network.
- The Miami boom may be reversible because mobile wealth can depart, research and productivity investment may lag, property dominates the economy, and hurricane, sea-level, geology, and insurance exposure remain severe.
- The [[JeremyVineShow]] mixes serious politics with ordinary and comic concerns, while listener calls and rapid topic selection can reveal issues such as fly-tipping and online harms before formal political attention catches up.
- Britain's roughly one billion weekly radio-listening hours are presented as evidence that simple, shared broadcasting remains resilient despite fragmented online media.
