# 23 张向荣&陆大鹏：如何学古文，如何学英语

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] episode has [[LuDapeng|陆大鹏]] and, according to the title metadata, [[ZhangXiangrong|张向荣]] compare learning English and German with reading classical Chinese. Their shared method is [[ContextualLanguageLearning|contextual language learning]]: encounter words and structures inside stories, conversations, books, films, podcasts, audiobooks, and historical situations rather than treating isolated vocabulary or grammar labels as the final goal.

The episode extends [[SelfDirectedLearning|self-directed learning]] and [[ClassicalProseReading|classical-prose reading]]. Learners should choose material that can sustain interest, tolerate partial understanding, infer before looking everything up, and revisit the same work across media. For classical Chinese, the practical goal is reading and cultural understanding rather than archaizing performance; annotated editions, broad historical knowledge, repeated reading, and selective memorization matter more than a rigid chronology or complete word-by-word certainty.

## Key Claims

- Vocabulary becomes usable when learned in phrases, sentences, recurring situations, and authentic media rather than as decontextualized definitions.
- Extensive reading supplies the input needed for later output; familiar plots, learner adaptations, translations, and screen versions can scaffold entry into original-language books.
- Films, podcasts, and audiobooks diversify input and make everyday expression, pronunciation, performance, and repeated exposure available during ordinary life.
- Interest is a practical selection mechanism: detective fiction, science fiction, fantasy, popular history, classical prose, poetry, or dynastic history can all be valid starting points when they sustain continued contact.
- [[ClassicalProseReading|Classical Chinese reading]] should aim at access to historical texts and cultural worlds, not conspicuous use of obscure characters or forced imitation of ancient style.
- Annotated editions and contextual knowledge about people, institutions, customs, allusions, and material culture often help more than grammatical labeling or full modern-language translation.
- Partial understanding is not failure: readers can preserve continuity, grasp the larger meaning, mark genuine uncertainty, and return later instead of stopping at every unknown word.
- Reading and selective memorization can strengthen modern written-language sensitivity and stylistic judgment even when composing classical Chinese is not the goal.
