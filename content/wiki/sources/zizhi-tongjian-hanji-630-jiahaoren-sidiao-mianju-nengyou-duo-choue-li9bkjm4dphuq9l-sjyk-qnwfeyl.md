# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》630丨假好人撕掉面具，能有多丑恶？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills [[WangMang|王莽]]'s 7 CE regency crisis between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-629-liujiaren-shui-kangqi-fankang-wangmang-de-daqi-lmhws7-jptdh9tf45bqtlfvzdw55|Hanji 629]] and the suppression sequence in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-631-gudai-sharen-zhenhui-ba-baigu-duichengshan-lgwrpwwu51hp6-lgqmna2ab7yvab|Hanji 631]]. It starts with Wang Mang's first high-denomination currency change and gold-collection order, then follows [[ZhaiYiRebellion|翟义起义]] from Zhai Yi's anti-usurpation judgment through Liu-family coalition building, Liu Xin's figurehead enthronement, public accusations against Wang Mang, and Wang Mang's emergency defense of Chang'an and the passes.

The durable synthesis is that Zhai Yi's uprising begins as legitimacy warfare before it becomes the defeated rebellion of Hanji 631. Zhai Yi reads Wang Mang's "辅政" and child-heir arrangement as a test for usurpation, uses a Liu royal claimant and public indictment to name Wang Mang as a Han traitor, and briefly draws a large response; Wang Mang answers fear with mobilization, layered capital defense, and a renewed Zhougong analogy that tries to turn the crisis back into proof of his sacred guardianship.

## Key Claims

- Wang Mang's 7 CE currency package adds nominally high-value knife and large-coin forms whose face values far exceed their metal-cost difference from five-zhu coins.
- The ban on private gold below the listed aristocratic level creates an extractive credibility problem because the promised equal exchange is not delivered in the episode's account.
- Zhai Yi interprets Wang Mang's acting-emperor status and use of young Ruzi Ying as a Zhougong-style trial balloon for replacing the Liu house.
- Zhai Yi, Chen Feng, Liu Yu, Liu Xin, and Liu Huang turn Dong Commandery military review into a rebel launch point and install Liu Xin as emperor.
- The uprising's proclamation accuses Wang Mang of poisoning Han Pingdi and using regency as a step-by-step removal of Han rule.
- The rebel army's growth to more than 100,000 people by Shanyang shows that anti-Wang sentiment can scale once attached to a Liu-family legitimacy banner.
- Wang Mang responds with panic but also effective emergency organization: eastward armies, pass defense, Chang'an-area counterforces, and palace patrols.
- Wang Mang's Zhougong analogy tries to neutralize the indictment by recasting Zhai Yi and Liu Xin as a replay of Guan Shu, Cai Shu, and Wu Geng rather than as legitimate Han loyalists.
