# 16. Pompeii

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode with archaeologist [[SophieHay]] presents [[Pompeii]] as both a city destroyed by the [[EruptionOfVesuvius79|79 CE eruption]] and a centuries-deep urban site whose buildings, inscriptions, food remains, streets, and excavation history resist the cliché of a frozen time capsule. Its central framework, [[PompeiiArchaeologicalPalimpsest]], joins everyday life to changing interpretation: conservation work produces new finds, fragmentary evidence can reconstruct people such as [[GaiusAlleiusNigidiusMaius]], and scans or contextual evidence can overturn confident stories projected onto bodies and artifacts.

The discussion also compares [[Herculaneum]], qualifies the traditional 24 August eruption date, and treats plaster casts, erotic art, slavery, sanitation, religion, and political uses of excavation as evidence problems rather than spectacle alone. It reinforces [[MultiEvidenceDisasterReconstruction]] while keeping manuscript variants, seasonal clues, the undated year of a 17 October graffito, disputed Christian and Jewish traces, individual identities, and archaeological motives explicitly uncertain.

## Key Claims

- Pompeii was a working port town with Oscan, Greek, Samnite, and Roman layers; its preserved destruction horizon should not erase its long urban development.
- About one third of the city remained unexcavated in the episode's account, while changing access and conservation needs make the visible tourist site an incomplete and managed selection.
- The eruption unfolded over roughly a day, giving inhabitants changing reasons to shelter or flee rather than presenting one instantaneous moment of destruction.
- Medieval manuscripts of Pliny the Younger preserve competing eruption dates, while autumn fruit, grape-processing debris, and a charcoal graffito challenge 24 August without proving one exact alternative date.
- Plaster casts humanize the catastrophe but invite voyeurism and unsupported biography; scans have revised assumed sex, pregnancy, ethnicity, and relationships.
- Inscriptions and dispersed finds can reconstruct otherwise obscure lives, as the probable tomb of [[GaiusAlleiusNigidiusMaius]] extends evidence known from rental notices and games advertisements.
- Food, sewers, waste, bars, graffiti, political notices, and enslaved labor reveal ordinary urban systems that elite literary history often obscures.

## Connections

- [[Pompeii]] - layered city, excavation site, tourist destination, and principal case.
- [[Herculaneum]] - comparison town with different preservation, urban character, and modern overbuilding.
- [[MountVesuvius]] - volcano whose eruption preserved and destroyed the two towns.
- [[GaiusAlleiusNigidiusMaius]] - Pompeian reconstructed through inscriptions discovered across two centuries.
- [[PompeiiArchaeologicalPalimpsest]] - framework separating a destruction horizon from the city's longer history and later interpretation.
- [[MultiEvidenceDisasterReconstruction]] - method joining texts, seasonal remains, inscriptions, bodies, food, architecture, and volcanology.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with [[355-roman-apocalypse-pompeii-79-ad-glt6664993493]] was found. Both favor a later-autumn eruption over an unqualified 24 August date while retaining uncertainty about the exact day.
- The 17 October charcoal graffito lacks a year, and a coin once used to support a later date is too worn for its decisive inscription to be read securely.
- A supposed Herculaneum crucifix is more plausibly a shelf fitting, and the reported Christian inscription, Jewish presence, body identities, ancient cultural memory of volcanism, and individual motives remain disputed or source-scoped.
