# Graham Allison on the Global Realignment: Iran, China, Israel, Greenland

## Summary

This [[AllIn|All-In]] interview with [[GrahamAllison|Graham Allison]] separates demonstrations of military and intelligence power from the harder political tasks of defining objectives, controlling aftermaths, and ending wars. [[Iran]] is the central case: Allison praises U.S. and Israeli operational performance while warning through [[MilitarySuccessPoliticalFailure]] and [[UndefinedWarObjectives]] that regime destruction can produce civil war, fragmentation, or a longer conflict rather than a governable settlement.

The interview broadens that caution into [[GreatPowerRivalryEscalationDynamics]], [[GreenlandStrategicAccess]], and [[NuclearNonproliferationOrder]]. Allison treats a near-term Chinese attack on [[Taiwan]] as unlikely absent provocation, argues that U.S. Arctic requirements can be met through leases and allied access rather than ownership, and uses an "80-80-9" frame to describe the exceptional but fragile postwar record of avoiding great-power war, avoiding wartime nuclear use, and limiting the number of nuclear-armed states.

## Key Claims

- Allison distinguishes operational success from political success: destroying Iranian leadership, military assets, or nuclear capacity does not establish a stable postwar order.
- He says he sees no evidence in the episode for imminent Iranian attack on the United States, imminent acquisition of a nuclear weapon, or an Iranian ICBM attack capability, and he describes [[BenjaminNetanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] rather than a demonstrated grand strategy as the main driver of the war.
- War outcomes remain uncertain because oil and gas prices, other economies, Taiwan's energy supply, Ukraine's air-defense needs, civil conflict, separatism, and alliance politics can convert tactical gains into second-order strategic costs.
- Allison estimates the probability of a Chinese attack on Taiwan in 2026 or 2027 at about five percent absent major provocation, citing Beijing's peaceful-reunification theory, Taiwan's 2028 election, Chinese military purges, and possible U.S. involvement.
- China is presented as a historically rapid rising power whose proximity to Taiwan, manufacturing depth, robotics adoption, and technological advance create a dangerous rivalry without making war inevitable.
- Greenland matters for missile defense and Arctic routes, but Allison argues that long leases and allied agreements can provide what the United States needs without invasion or ownership.
- The "80-80-9" framework treats post-1945 great-power peace, nuclear non-use, and nuclear nonproliferation as achievements that require active maintenance rather than natural conditions.
- Allison argues that concentrated wealth can destabilize democracy while remaining skeptical that unconditional income support preserves motivation, leaving redistribution design open rather than endorsing one program.

## Key Quotes

> "this is Bibi's war" - Allison's attribution of the Iran intervention's central political impetus.

> "fog of war" - his warning against treating an unfolding conflict as strategically settled.

> "80-80-9" - his shorthand for great-power peace, nuclear non-use, and nine nuclear-armed states.
