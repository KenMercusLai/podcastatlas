# Master Self Control & Overcome Procrastination | Dr. Kentaro Fujita

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[KentaroFujita]] about self-control, procrastination, motivation, delayed gratification, intrinsic motivation, goal pursuit, and goal disengagement. The core synthesis is that self-control is broader than willpower: it can be supported by [[SelfControlStrategyToolkit|strategy selection]], [[PsychologicalDistanceSelfControl|psychological distance]], meaningful "whys," social modeling, role models, regulatory fit, intrinsic interest, and context-sensitive choice between [[AbstinenceModerationSelfControl|abstinence and moderation]]. The episode keeps classic findings such as the marshmallow test, ego depletion, and reward effects useful but qualified rather than treating them as simple character tests or universal protocols.

## Key Claims
- [[KentaroFujita]] distinguishes willpower as effortful suppression from self-control as a broader toolbox of attentional, behavioral, social, motivational, and meaning-making strategies.
- [[MarshmallowTestContext]] matters: delayed gratification in children depends on strategy learning, trust, experimental setup, and socioeconomic context, not only innate discipline.
- [[PsychologicalDistanceSelfControl]] can help because immediate temptations narrow attention toward concrete "how" details, while distance and higher-order "why" thinking can strengthen goal-consistent action.
- [[SelfControlStrategyToolkit]] treats failed attempts as tool-fit information: a strategy that helps one person or one conflict can fail in another context.
- [[AbstinenceModerationSelfControl]] is a fit problem rather than a moral ranking; abstinence pre-decides choices and supports streaks, while moderation can preserve flexibility when one lapse does not destroy the goal.
- [[GoalDisengagementAndReengagement]] matters because well-being may require letting go of goals that are no longer possible and redirecting effort toward viable alternatives.
- Intrinsic motivation and meaning support persistence, but [[MotivationCrowdingOut]] remains a risk when external rewards change how people interpret already-valued activity.

## Key Quotes
> "Willpower is effortful inhibition" — Fujita's distinction between suppression and broader self-control.

> "Failure means the tool did not work" — the episode's practical framing of self-control strategy fit.
