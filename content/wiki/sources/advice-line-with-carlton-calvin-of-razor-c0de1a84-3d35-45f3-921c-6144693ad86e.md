## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] Advice Line episode pairs [[GuyRaz]] with [[CarltonCalvin]] of [[RazorUSA]] to advise founders behind [[EulogyGame|Eulogy]], [[HoneyBespokeStationery]], and [[LittleWaterDistillery]]. The episode turns consumer-product growth into focus questions: prove demand before licensing, use founder visibility and wholesale relationships to escape platform dependence, and concentrate scarce cash on the products already showing pull. Its main wiki contribution is an Advice Line case where [[ConsumerTrendSpotting]], [[ToyInventorLicensingPath]], [[FounderVisibilityMarketing]], [[SKUFocusUnderCashConstraint]], [[CPGDistribution]], and [[FounderDelegationDiscipline]] interact.

## Key Claims
- [[CarltonCalvin]] says [[RazorUSA]] is trying to institutionalize its ability to spot consumer trends rather than rely only on one founder's instinct.
- Patent litigation can clean up a copycat market, but Carlton warns that lawsuits may cost millions and produce little recovery when infringers have shallow pockets.
- [[SeanBarassa]] has sold just under 1,000 copies of [[EulogyGame|Eulogy]] after a 2024 [[Kickstarter]] campaign, but the advice is to create more proof through conventions, small stores, game nights, and visible player reactions before expecting licensing.
- [[ShireenTippett]] has built [[HoneyBespokeStationery]] mostly through Etsy and licensed collegiate stationery, but Guy and Carlton push her toward school-connected stores, markets, reps, and founder-led design content.
- For Shireen, paid [[Meta]] ads are treated as hard to scale without a clearer strategy; organic trust through [[Instagram]], behind-the-scenes video, and wholesale relationships may be more useful.
- [[MarkLittleWaterDistillery|Mark]] and [[LittleWaterDistillery]] illustrate the burden of doing manufacturing, sales, distribution, tasting-room hospitality, and retail at once.
- The advice for [[48BlocksEspressoMartini]] is to focus on the products already resonating, especially the espresso and chocolate martinis, rather than keep every SKU alive for tasting-room breadth.
- Carlton's closing lesson is that avoiding sales-rep margin can become false economy: founders cannot scale every selling task themselves, and delegation can transform the company.

## Key Quotes
> "sales reps are better at selling" - Carlton's summary of why his younger self should have delegated earlier.

> "do not fight the culture" - Carlton's advice when Mark asks whether canned cocktails weaken premium positioning.

> "one or two things resonate" - the operating rule behind the SKU-focus advice to Little Water Distillery.
