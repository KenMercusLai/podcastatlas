# 11 压岁钱与圣诞礼物的起源

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] New Year episode has [[LuDapeng|陆大鹏]] and [[ZhangDabai|张大白]] compare Chinese New Year money with Christmas gifts. It places the Spring Festival inside [[CalendarReformLegitimacy|calendar reform]], year-end sacrifice, agricultural harvest, ancestor remembrance, and the modern separation of Gregorian 元旦 from lunar 春节, then traces 厌胜钱, spendable currency, film, and child-rearing debate as changing layers of 压岁钱.

The Christmas branch moves from the Magi and courtly gift exchange through Reformation-era child gifting, [[AChristmasCarol|*A Christmas Carol*]], [[SaintNicholas|Saint Nicholas]], Father Christmas, Dutch-American tradition, and [[SantaClaus|Santa Claus]]. Its durable synthesis is [[FestivalGiftCustomEvolution|festival gift-custom evolution]]: ritual objects and hierarchical offerings can become family-centered gifts, money, moral education, charity, and commerce without preserving one unchanged form or losing all older meaning.

## Key Claims

- “过年” is older than the modern holiday name “春节”; the episode presents Spring Festival as a season extending from late-year preparation through Lantern Festival rather than one day.
- The selection of a year beginning is both calendrical and political. The episode links the enduring first-month convention to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s 104 BCE Taichu reform while acknowledging earlier and later changes.
- Harvest celebration, year-end sacrifice, ancestor remembrance, protection, reunion, and enjoyment accumulated into a changing festival rather than a fixed ancient package.
- Han-era 厌胜钱 were non-circulating protective objects with auspicious words and images; the proposed linguistic and ritual path from 厌胜钱 or 压祟钱 to 压岁钱 remains conjectural.
- Ordinary currency could become a widespread New Year gift only within a sufficiently monetized economy; the episode cautiously associates broader diffusion with the Song through Ming-Qing periods.
- The 1937 film [[LuckyMoneyFilm1937|《压岁钱》]] follows a silver dollar through Shanghai society and ends with paper money, linking the custom to class observation and monetary change.
- Late-twentieth-century anxiety about children receiving cash produced proposals such as “压岁书”; the hosts instead frame supervised saving, spending, and record-keeping as opportunities for financial learning.
- Christmas gift-giving layers the Magi's gifts, ruler-centered offering, Reformation-era child gifting, regional gift dates and figures, Dickensian generosity, and the modern domestic gift-bringer.
- [[AChristmasCarol|*A Christmas Carol*]] strengthens an ethic of compassion, employer responsibility, charity, and personal moral change while also illustrating how literature can reshape festival imagination and food practice.
- [[SantaClaus|Santa Claus]] is presented as a nineteenth-century American convergence of Saint Nicholas and Dutch tradition that later merged with Father Christmas and interacted with other European gift-bringers.

- The 厌胜钱-to-压岁钱 etymology, the timing and scale of monetary diffusion, the 1914 naming date, film-credit details, gift-giving chronologies, literary influence on turkey consumption, and individual origin stories remain episode-attributed pending specialist verification.
