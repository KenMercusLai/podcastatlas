# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》222｜晁错向文帝提出的三大奇谋，到底有多牛？（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode begins [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]]'s response to recurrent [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] attacks under [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. His 《言兵事书》 treats battlefield success as a system: terrain, training, weapons, troop type, disciplined command, and the ruler's choice of commander must fit one another rather than being reduced to courage or numbers.

Chao first concedes Xiongnu advantages in mountain movement, mounted archery, and endurance, then identifies Han advantages in flat-ground chariot and cavalry action, strong bows and armor, formation discipline, concentrated missile fire, and close combat. He proposes equipping and organizing submitted frontier peoples under trusted commanders for difficult ground while Han formations operate on plains, producing mutual support through differentiated roles. The episode then turns to Qin's northern and southern expansion as a negative precedent: campaigns pursued for territorial gain, environmental mismatch, widening forced service, unrewarded death, and undiminished taxes can convert war burden into mass resentment and political revolt.

## Key Claims

- Chao identifies favorable terrain, trained soldiers, and effective weapons as the three immediate conditions of combat, then traces failure upward through soldier, commander, and ruler responsibility.
- Weapons, armor, missile range, formation discipline, and command selection have value only when matched to the battlefield and the troops using them.
- Xiongnu forces are presented as stronger in mountains, dangerous paths, mounted archery, bad weather, hunger, and hardship, not as universally superior.
- Han forces are presented as stronger in flat-ground chariot and cavalry operations, long-range missile fire, armored formation fighting, and dismounted close combat.
- Chao recommends arming submitted Hu, Yiqu, and other frontier peoples, placing them under commanders they trust, and combining their difficult-ground role with Han chariots and archers on plains.
- The episode treats this differentiated force design as a risk-control proposal, but it does not establish the unit's scale, implementation, coercion, or battlefield results.
- Chao uses Qin's northern and southern campaigns to distinguish territorial expansion from protection of land and people, emphasizing environmental attrition, widening conscription, unpaid loss, continuing taxes, and accumulating resentment.
- The source links those burdens to the wider conditions behind [[DazexiangUprising|大泽乡起义]], while stopping short of proving that military conscription alone caused Qin's collapse.

## Key Quotes

> “兵器锋利、战士优秀、将领尽责、君王善于选将。” - the episode's compact responsibility chain from material readiness to sovereign selection.

> “在险阻地带由外籍兵团出击，在平原则由汉朝原有战车部队和弓箭部队联合出击。” - the proposed division of labor by terrain and troop strength.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. Part 1's terrain-specific mixed-force proposal and part 2's fortified settler system address different layers of the same frontier problem and can coexist.
- Title boundary: the supplied episode develops the military memorial and begins the Qin warning, but does not fully establish all “three plans” advertised by the title.
- Causal boundary: the account plausibly connects expansion, environmental mismatch, coercive service, unrewarded death, and continuing taxes to resentment, but it does not establish a single-cause explanation of Qin collapse or the Dazexiang uprising.
- Category boundary: the episode's “外籍兵团” is a modern explanatory label for submitted frontier peoples in Han service; the source does not establish a modern foreign-legion institution, uniform identity, voluntariness, or autonomy.
- Source-scope caution: the 169 BCE date and place, wording and title of 《言兵事书》, force ratios, comparative military claims, names and status of submitted groups, equipment, command arrangements, imperial reply, Qin levy sequence, casualties, taxation, and causal chain require comparison with transmitted records.
