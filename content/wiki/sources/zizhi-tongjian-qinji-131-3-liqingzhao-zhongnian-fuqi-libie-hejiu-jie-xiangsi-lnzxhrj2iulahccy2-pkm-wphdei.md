## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues the alcohol thread from [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-131-2-pandian-huangdimen-jiuhou-xiaopihao-livpg3zh7lsqffgca2pomiqnlvyd|Qinji 131-2]] by asking how drinking can also support sociability, poetic inspiration, emotional expression, and self-command. It uses [[LijiText|《礼记》]], [[LiBai|李白]], [[DuFu|杜甫]], [[LiQingzhao|李清照]], [[GenghisKhan|成吉思汗]], and [[Confucius|孔子]] to split the subject into two durable frames: [[PoeticWineCulture|诗酒文化]] and [[TemperateDrinkingSelfControl|节制饮酒与自我控制]]. The source's core synthesis is that alcohol can warm feeling and reveal character, but only becomes constructive when the drinker remains clear enough not to be ruled by it.

## Key Claims
- The episode shifts from alcohol's violent or destructive side toward the claim that drinking can also help people recognize character, build social warmth, and release emotion.
- [[LijiText|《礼记》]] is used as an early norm for drinking without losing composure: enjoyment and conviviality matter, but so does the ability not to become disordered.
- [[LiBai|李白]] and [[DuFu|杜甫]] turn the alcohol discussion into [[PoeticWineCulture|诗酒文化]], where wine is a poetic scene, a sign of temperament, and a source of imaginative momentum.
- [[DuFu|杜甫]]'s 《饮中八仙歌》 is treated as a set of wine-shaped portraits of capital literati, including He Zhizhang, Su Jin, Li Bai, Zhang Xu, and others.
- [[LiQingzhao|李清照]] receives the episode's fullest literary treatment: the host cites [[WangZhongwen|王仲闻]]'s annotation statistics to argue that wine is unusually frequent in her ci corpus.
- The episode reads wine across Li Qingzhao's life phases: youthful leisure sorrow, midlife marital separation and longing, and later grief after widowhood and national crisis.
- [[VirginiaWoolf|Virginia Woolf / 弗吉尼亚·伍尔夫]] is invoked through the room-and-money argument to explain why material stability matters for women's writing, while Li Qingzhao is also praised for freedom, candor, and self-possession.
- [[GenghisKhan|成吉思汗]] is presented as a model of restrained drinking within a Mongol social world where feast drinking was common.
- [[Confucius|孔子]] is used for the closing moral vocabulary: serving public office, family, mourning ritual, and not being trapped by drink are mapped onto loyalty, filiality, reverence, and caution.
- The episode's practical warning is that drinking becomes dangerous when habit outruns self-command; the source explicitly links heavy drinking to family harm, physical danger, and failed work.

## Key Quotes
> "得酒趣，不失酒德" - the host's closing standard for enjoying drink without losing moral and practical restraint.

> "不为酒困" - the Confucian phrase used to make sobriety and caution part of self-cultivation.
