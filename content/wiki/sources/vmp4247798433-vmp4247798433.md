# Dell Computers: Michael Dell. The College Dropout Who Took on IBM (2018)

## Summary
This archival [[HowIBuiltThis]] interview has [[GuyRaz]] trace [[MichaelDell|Michael Dell]]'s path from childhood selling experiments and a University of Texas dorm-room upgrade business to [[DellTechnologies|Dell]]. Dell explains how customer targeting, technical disassembly, direct selling, rapid assembly, low inventory, on-site service, and fast correction of failed projects formed an operating system that challenged slower PC distribution. The episode also covers FCC compliance, the 1988 public offering, market leadership, later share loss, and Dell's 2007 return as CEO.

## Key Claims
- Dell's newspaper-sales work used marriage-license and housing-move signals to identify likely subscribers, establishing an early pattern of testing customer segments rather than selling indiscriminately.
- At university he bought IBM-compatible PCs, added memory or hard drives, and resold them; the operation reportedly reached roughly $50,000 to $80,000 in monthly business before he left school after his freshman year.
- [[DirectPCBuildToOrderModel]] joined direct demand signals, configurable machines, competitive pricing, and on-site service, initially serving businesses, governments, and professional customers more than individual consumers.
- [[LowInventoryHardwareCostAdvantage]] used current sales information and frequent supplier deliveries to reduce capital tied up in parts, incorporate newer components, and benefit from falling hardware costs.
- Dell says a 12 MHz machine sold for $1,995 while [[IBM]] sold a 6 MHz model for $3,995, but the interview does not independently establish a like-for-like product comparison.
- The company resolved an early [[FederalCommunicationsCommission|FCC]] Class B approval problem, then went public in 1988 to finance hiring, factories, systems, products, and international expansion while gaining customer legitimacy.
- Dell presents customer listening as a better opportunity-finding discipline than fixation on competitors, while acknowledging failed Dell Unix and Olympic projects and early inventory-control problems.
- After Dell lost PC market leadership and the market shifted toward cloud computing, Michael Dell returned as CEO in 2007 and acknowledged that the company had underinvested in some innovation areas.

## Key Quotes
> "listen and learn" - Dell's description of the customer-oriented discipline he contrasts with copying competitors.

> "fix it as soon as you find it" - Dell's operating lesson from failed products and inventory problems.
