# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》232｜吴王刘濞凭什么敢发起七国之乱？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode turns the coalition prepared in Hanji 231 into the opening mobilization of the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]]. After territorial reduction reaches Wu, [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] kills court officials, mobilizes Wu, receives support attributed to Minyue and Dongyue, joins [[LiuWuChuKingWesternHan|楚王刘戊]], and attacks Liang under the public slogan of punishing [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]]; Qi's reversal and Jibei's internal restraint simultaneously show that the royal coalition is broad but unstable.

The episode frames this military sequence with [[MuXianshengWesternHan|穆先生]]'s earlier withdrawal from Liu Wu's court. A missing sweet-wine courtesy matters not as an isolated slight but as a small sign that inherited respect and the moral basis of service are eroding. His departure, followed by the humiliation of advisers who remain, creates [[IncipientRiskExit|见几而作式风险退出]] as a source-scoped judgment pattern: infer a changed power relationship from a weak signal, then act before coercion becomes explicit.

## Key Claims

- Liu Wu's failure to continue an inherited courtesy toward Mu is treated as evidence of a deeper change in ruler-minister relations rather than as a dispute about a drink.
- Mu withdraws before overt persecution; the later binding and public humiliation of Shen and Bai function in the episode as retrospective confirmation, not as proof that every broken courtesy predicts violence.
- Territorial reduction of Kuaiji and Yuzhang is presented as the immediate trigger for Liu Bi's transition from conspiracy to open rebellion.
- Wu, Chu, Jiaoxi, Jiaodong, Zichuan, Jinan, and Zhao rise in coordination, while Qi reverses course and Jibei is neutralized internally, revealing an uneven coalition.
- Liu Bi's confidence rests on prior recruitment, attributed mass mobilization in Wu, support from Minyue and Dongyue, rapid junction with Chu, and early success against Liang.
- “诛晁错” supplies public political framing, while simultaneous attacks, foreign outreach, and movement against Liang show a wider military project.
- Jingdi answers with a multi-theater command structure: [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] against Wu-Chu, [[LiJiWesternHan|郦寄]] against Zhao, [[LuanBu|栾布]] toward Qi, and Dou Ying at Xingyang.

## Key Quotes

> “知几其神乎。” - the classical formula used to describe recognition of danger before it becomes obvious.

> “诛晁错。” - the coalition's public slogan as rendered by the episode.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode advances the existing chain from capacity and coalition construction into mobilization; it also qualifies any image of a unified seven-state bloc because Qi reverses course and Jibei is prevented from joining.
- Causal boundary: force size, allied response, and early victory help explain why Liu Bi could expect success, but they do not prove that rebellion was rational, inevitable, or militarily sustainable.
- Signal boundary: the later abuse of Shen and Bai supports Mu's judgment inside the narrative, but one omitted courtesy is not made a universal or independently validated predictor of persecution.
- Identity boundary: the transcript's Minyue/Dongyue wording, royal names, offices, troop totals, ages, territorial reductions, campaign routes, deaths, speeches, and command assignments require comparison with transmitted records.
