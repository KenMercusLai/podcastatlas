# 546. The French Revolution: The Monarchy Falls (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[FrenchRevolution|French Revolution]] from the abolition of monarchy on 21 September 1792 through [[BattleOfValmy|Valmy]], the first conflicts inside the [[NationalConvention|National Convention]], French expansion, and the opening case against [[LouisXVI|Louis XVI]]. It presents the Republic as born without a fully settled constitutional identity: military survival made the new order credible, factional accusations narrowed the space for moderation, ideological war universalized French claims, and the king's continued existence became a test of republican legitimacy.

## Key Claims

- The Convention abolished monarchy unanimously and began “Year One,” but the episode argues that the Republic still lacked one universally decisive constitutional or ceremonial founding moment.
- Low turnout, public oral voting, wartime disruption, intimidation, and the purging of moderates and royalists qualify any simple claim that the Convention represented an uncontested national mandate.
- At [[BattleOfValmy|Valmy]], French regulars, effective artillery, Prussian logistical exhaustion, and Dumouriez's threat to enemy supply lines turned a limited battlefield survival into a transformative republican victory.
- The [[Girondins]], the Plain, and the [[Montagnards]] operated as fluid camps rather than modern disciplined parties, while the Mountain's leaders remained distinct rather than forming a unified triumvirate.
- Failed Girondin attacks on Marat and Robespierre strengthened their opponents and illustrate [[RevolutionaryFactionalOutflanking|revolutionary factional outflanking]], in which yesterday's radicals could be recoded as reactionaries or traitors.
- French victories moved policy from territorial defence toward [[RevolutionaryIdeologicalWar|ideological war]], promising aid to peoples seeking liberty and exporting anti-feudal, anti-clerical, and republican reforms.
- Louis's Temple imprisonment joined domestic routine to political degradation and growing danger for the royal family.
- Mailhe's natural-law argument and [[LouisAntoineSaintJust|Saint-Just]]'s claim that kingship itself made Louis an enemy pushed debate beyond the penalties allowed by the old constitution.
- The discovery of the iron safe supplied evidence of contacts with foreign and counterrevolutionary forces, strengthening the case for trial while leaving legal procedure inseparable from symbolic regime formation.

## Key Quotes

> “Vive la Nation” - Kellermann's rallying cry at Valmy as presented by the episode.

> “Louis must die so that the country may live.” - Robespierre's argument as paraphrased by the episode.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing French Revolution branch. This episode supplies the September-December 1792 bridge between the Revolution's earlier structural crisis and the January 1793 trial and execution represented by Part 4.
- Calling Valmy a French “victory” remains qualified: the episode describes an artillery confrontation and aborted Prussian attack whose political effect exceeded its tactical decisiveness.
- The claim that France had no clear founding proclamation is the hosts' interpretive framing; abolition, “Year One,” and later regicide can each be treated as distinct founding moments.
- Turnout causes, battlefield dialogue, exact faction boundaries, private motives, the meaning of the iron-safe papers, and the symbolic necessity of Louis's death remain source-scoped reconstructions.
