# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》231｜汉景帝酒后竟造就乌龙传位事件（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode moves from [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]]'s personal grievance into the structural and coalition-building prehistory of the [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|Rebellion of the Seven States]]. It presents [[WuKingdomEarlyHan|吴国]]'s copper, salt, tax relief, service payments, patronage, and refusal to surrender fugitives as the resource-and-autonomy base behind [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]]'s judgment that Liu Bi would rebel whether or not the court reduced his territory.

After [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] accepts direct reductions against Chu, Zhao, and Jiaoxi, Liu Bi sends [[YingGaoWesternHan|应高]] to persuade [[LiuAngJiaoxiKingWesternHan|胶西王刘昂]]. Ying Gao converts separate royal punishments into a shared survival threat, offers “诛晁错” as the public slogan, outlines a campaign toward Hangu Pass, Xingyang, and Ao Granary, and promises a later division of territory. Liu Ang accepts despite ministerial warnings and contacts Qi, Zichuan, Jiaodong, Jinan, and other kingdoms, turning grievance and fear into an incipient coalition.

## Key Claims

- Liu Bi's access to copper mining and salt production lets the episode portray Wu as unusually wealthy and able to reduce taxes, subsidize service, distribute gifts, and attract loyalty.
- Wu's refusal to hand over fugitives to outside officials is treated as evidence that fiscal capacity and local protection are becoming political autonomy from the center.
- Chao Cuo repeatedly urges reduction under Emperor Wen and Jingdi, arguing that Liu Bi's grievance, nonattendance, mining, minting, salt production, and recruitment of fugitives make rebellion likely even without削藩.
- The episode attributes to Chao the timing argument that early reduction may trigger rebellion sooner but on a smaller and less prepared basis.
- Jingdi's court reduces territory from Chu, Zhao, and Jiaoxi after investigating or invoking specific offenses, while Dou Ying's dissent creates distance between him and Chao.
- Ying Gao reframes distinct royal liabilities as one shared threat and turns “诛晁错” into the coalition's legitimating slogan rather than its complete objective.
- Liu Ang accepts a plan that already includes strategic objectives and postwar territorial division, then sends secret messages to recruit additional kingdoms despite his officials' warnings.

## Key Quotes

> “今削之亦反，不削亦反。” - Chao Cuo's episode-attributed claim that the core rebellion risk precedes the immediate reduction decision.

> “方今计独斩晁错。” - the public cause Ying Gao proposes for the coalition while also discussing military objectives and division of territory.

- [[WuKingdomEarlyHan|汉初吴国]] - copper-and-salt resource base whose fiscal and protective practices support Liu Bi's independence.
- [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]] - adviser who argues that delayed reduction will allow a larger rebellion and becomes the coalition's named target.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - ruler who convenes debate and implements successive reductions against royal kingdoms.
- [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] - dissenter whose disagreement with Chao shows that court support is not unanimous.
- [[YingGaoWesternHan|应高]] - envoy who transforms shared fear into a slogan, campaign design, and bargaining offer.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends rather than replaces the existing causal chain: Liu Xian's death supplies personal grievance; Wu's resource base and autonomy supply capacity; direct削藩 supplies the immediate trigger; and Ying Gao's mission supplies coalition construction.
- Causal qualification: Chao's “削也反，不削也反” is a strategic judgment attributed by the episode, not proof that rebellion was inevitable or that the timing and scale counterfactual can be verified.
- Slogan qualification: “诛晁错” is not treated as the coalition's complete purpose because the same account includes routes, granary seizure, defense against the imperial army, and postwar division of territory.
- Name boundary: the supplied source writes 刘昂, matching the existing canonical [[LiuAngJiaoxiKingWesternHan|刘昂]] page; the personal name, speeches, offices, and transmitted written forms still require primary-text collation.
- Source-scope caution: Wu's tax regime, copper and salt control, service payments, gifts, fugitive policy, Chao's memorials, every reduction and offense, court debate, omens, coalition membership, route plan, territorial promises, Liu Bi's disguise, and ministerial warnings remain episode-attributed.
