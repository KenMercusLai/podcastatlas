# 49 毒药与投毒史：从武大郎到希特勒

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] episode compares poison in Chinese and Western history as medicine, hunting technology, execution method, suicide means, court weapon, intelligence instrument, and literary device. Its strongest synthesis is that [[PoisonAsCovertPower|poison as covert power]] joins low visible force to knowledge, access, intimacy, concealment, and deniability, while [[MedicinePoisonBoundary|the medicine–poison boundary]] depends on substance, preparation, dose, route, and use rather than a timeless binary.

The episode also adds an evidence warning. Roman court stories, the poisonous zhen bird, universal antidotes, silver-needle testing, and literary signs of arsenic death range from limited historical experience to legend or fiction; they cannot be treated as validated toxicology merely because they are vivid. The 1978 killing of [[GeorgiMarkov|Georgi Markov]] supplies the modern counterpoint: a tiny perforated pellet, compatible symptoms, and later intelligence testimony support a ricin-assassination account, yet no one was prosecuted and the exact operational chain remains partly unresolved.

## Key Claims

- Poison has served hunting, warfare, execution, coerced suicide, private murder, and political assassination; the same substance can move among medical, domestic, agricultural, and lethal uses.
- Concealed poisoning reduces the need for physical dominance but requires knowledge, access to a substance, proximity to the target, and a plausible delivery path.
- Ordered suicide or poison could preserve the body and public dignity, allowing rulers to remove political opponents while softening the official account and sometimes protecting a family’s status.
- [[MithridatesVI|Mithridates VI]] is presented as experimenting with small doses and compound antidotes after his father’s poisoning, but neither universal tolerance nor the historical formula is established.
- Silver is not a universal poison detector; the episode explains its limited reputation through sulfur impurities in historical arsenic preparations that could blacken silver.
- Markov’s 1978 death illustrates an intelligence-era delivery system designed for concealment and deniability, but attribution remains partly testimonial and no perpetrator was convicted.
- Chinese aconite and arsenic histories show how therapeutic use, household utility, production knowledge, legal control, and ordinary availability can coexist with lethal misuse.
- The *Water Margin* poisoning of Wu Dalang combines plausible access and concealment with literary symptoms and postmortem signs that do not fully match the forensic tradition attributed to Song Ci.
