# 电影里的AI：情人、巨婴和独裁者

## Summary
This [[DianyingJubian|电影巨辩]] episode treats AI cinema as a humanities problem rather than a technical forecast. It traces a line from [[Frankenstein|《弗兰肯斯坦》]] and early robot stories through [[TheMatrix|《黑客帝国》]], [[AIArtificialIntelligenceFilm|《人工智能》]], [[HerFilm|《她》]], and [[ExMachinaFilm|《机械姬》]], arguing that these works use artificial beings to test creator responsibility, programmed love, simulated reality, embodiment, judgment, and modern subjectivity. The source's core contribution is that AI stories matter most where "looks human", "loves like a human", and "is human" come apart.

## Key Claims
- AI cinema has two separate discussion tracks: AI as a production tool and AI as a cinematic subject; this episode focuses on the second track.
- The episode treats the [[FrankensteinComplexAI|Frankenstein complex in AI cinema]] as a durable source pattern: human beings want to create life, then fear their creations' autonomy, rebellion, replacement, or judgment.
- Early robot and android stories expose a labor and power relation: humans often invent artificial beings as servants or slaves, making revolt stories inseparable from domination.
- Disembodied AI films such as Alphaville-style computer rule and [[TheMatrix|《黑客帝国》]] shift AI anxiety from bodily replacement toward rationalized control and [[SimulatedRealityEpistemology|simulated-reality epistemology]].
- [[AIArtificialIntelligenceFilm|《人工智能》]] becomes the episode's deepest case for [[ProgrammedLoveAuthenticity|programmed love authenticity]]: David's love is designed and irreversible, but the intensity of the experience still forces a responsibility question.
- [[HerFilm|《她》]] anticipates contemporary AI companionship by showing a disembodied system that precisely fills loneliness, then evolves beyond human possession or understanding.
- [[ExMachinaFilm|《机械姬》]] turns the AI problem into [[TuringTestJudgmentTrap|a judgment trap]]: the human observer must decide whether an artificial being is conscious, manipulative, free, or merely well-designed.
- The episode argues that current large language models differ from most movie AI because film AI almost always has goals, subjectivity, and self-preservation, while real models remain closer to language-pattern systems.
- The closing synthesis frames AI as a challenge to modern subjectivity: the human self can no longer be imagined as sealed off from algorithms, media, simulation, and synthetic companions.
