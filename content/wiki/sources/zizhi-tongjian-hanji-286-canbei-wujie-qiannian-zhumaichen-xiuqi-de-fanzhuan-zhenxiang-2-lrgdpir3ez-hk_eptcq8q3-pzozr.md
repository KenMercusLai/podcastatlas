# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》286｜惨被误解千年，朱买臣休妻的反转真相（2）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the immediate separation aftermath from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-286-canbei-wujie-qiannian-zhumaichen-xiuqi-de-fanzhuan-zhenxiang-1-lpvzvzomh6u6zbr1ispaiycenl3f|Hanji 286 part 1]]. It says [[CuiShiZhuMaichenWife|崔氏]], now with another husband, encounters an impoverished [[ZhuMaichenWesternHan|朱买臣]] and feeds him rather than treating him as an enemy. Zhu later reaches Chang'an in hardship, gains access through fellow townsman [[YanZhuWesternHan|严助]], demonstrates learning in the [[Chunqiu|《春秋》]] and *Chuci*, and receives appointment from [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].

The episode then shifts to [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]]. Zhu defeats Gongsun Hong's objections to the Shuofang commandery in court debate; Gongsun subsequently narrows his recommendation to abandoning Canghai commandery and southwest expansion while concentrating resources on Shuofang. When [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] publicly calls Gongsun Hong's austere lifestyle hypocritical, Gongsun concedes the criticism and praises Ji's candor, converting attack into an image of humility. The durable synthesis is therefore not simply that low posture wins: policy revision can preserve a viable priority, while respectful concession can absorb criticism without proving either the accusation or the responder's sincerity.

## Key Claims

- Cui Shi reportedly encounters Zhu Maichen after remarriage, sees him cold and hungry, and provides him a meal; the episode uses the scene to complicate the later stereotype of total betrayal.
- Zhu reaches Chang'an with a local reporting party, waits without a summons until his food is exhausted, and survives through help before Yan Zhu secures an audience with Han Wudi.
- The episode attributes Zhu's appointment as zhong dafu to his presentation of learning in the *Chunqiu* and *Chuci*.
- In debate over frontier commitments, Zhu supplies ten reasons for retaining Shuofang, after which Gongsun Hong withdraws that objection and recommends ending Canghai commandery and southwest expansion instead.
- Gongsun Hong's severe personal economy is praised by Wudi but attacked by Ji An as performance inconsistent with high office.
- Gongsun answers by accepting Ji's charge, calling him a close friend, and crediting his loyalty; the episode interprets the reply as a politically effective low-posture maneuver.
- The closing preview says [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]] left for the Western Regions in 139 BCE and was detained by the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] for thirteen years, but the current source does not develop that mission.

## Key Quotes

> “汲黯说得对。” - the episode's rendering of Gongsun Hong's immediate concession before the emperor.

> “如果不是汲黯的忠心，陛下听不到这些真话。” - the praise that turns personal criticism into testimony about a candid official.

## Connections

- [[ZhuMaichenWesternHan|朱买臣]] - receives help from Cui and Yan Zhu before entering office, then argues successfully for retaining Shuofang.
- [[CuiShiZhuMaichenWife|崔氏]] - former wife whose act of feeding Zhu complicates the episode's own separation narrative.
- [[YanZhuWesternHan|严助]] - fellow townsman and access broker who helps Zhu reach Han Wudi.
- [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] - revises frontier priorities after debate and absorbs Ji An's attack through concession and praise.
- [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] - blunt critic whose accusation is not retracted even though Gongsun controls the response.
- [[RespectBasedConflictDeescalation|尊重式冲突降温]] - extended from recognizing a rival's competence to affirming a critic's candor while conceding part of the attack.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. Cui's reported meal qualifies a simple hostility narrative without reversing part 1's account of prolonged hardship and separation.
- The supplied document is a six-minute structured summary rather than a primary-text transcription. The meal scene, remarriage sequence, waiting conditions, Yan Zhu's exact role, learning displayed, appointment title, ten-point argument, and frontier-policy chronology remain episode-attributed.
- The source does not establish whether Gongsun Hong's austerity was sincere, performative, or mixed. Ji An's charge and Gongsun's response are preserved as competing public acts rather than resolved private motives.
- The host's claim that Gongsun was an unmatched court genius and the comparison with Li Si, Zhao Gao, and Huhai are retrospective interpretations, not demonstrated equivalences.
- The Zhang Qian material is only a preview and does not alter the fuller mission synthesis already supplied by Hanji 287 parts 1 and 2.
