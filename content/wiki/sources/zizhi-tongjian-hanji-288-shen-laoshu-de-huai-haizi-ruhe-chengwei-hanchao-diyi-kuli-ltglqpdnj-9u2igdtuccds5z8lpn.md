# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》288｜审老鼠的“坏孩子”，如何成为汉朝第一酷吏？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode introduces [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] through the *Shiji* anecdote in which the child Zhang captures a mouse, stages an evidentiary trial, writes a judgment, and dismembers it. The story establishes early familiarity with case procedure and punishment in the episode's portrait, but it does not prove that childhood behavior caused Zhang's adult career or cruelty.

The main institutional contribution is Zhang's promotion to tingwei under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. The episode says he combines legal vocabulary, Confucian display, patronage ties, and close attention to imperial preference so that conviction, release, or mitigation track what the emperor wants. [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] attacks this fusion of technical law and sovereign desire as fear-producing government, making the episode an early case for both [[RulerWillAsLaw|君意即法]] and [[HarshOfficialInstrumentalRule|酷吏工具化统治]].

## Key Claims

- As a child, Zhang Tang allegedly captures a mouse after it steals meat, binds it, treats the recovered scraps as evidence, stages confession and judgment, and submits a written decision to his father.
- Zhang's father is said to recognize the judgment as resembling an experienced official's work and then teach him legal documents; the episode uses the story as character foreshadowing rather than independent developmental evidence.
- In the Han Wudi-era setting, continuing Xiongnu pressure, centralization, salt-and-iron administration, and court coercion increase the value of officials able to translate imperial priorities into legal action.
- After becoming tingwei, Zhang publicly aligns himself with the emperor's interest in Confucian learning, respects figures such as [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] and [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]], and uses [[NiKuanWesternHan|倪宽]] in legal reporting.
- The episode says Zhang condemns those Wudi wants condemned and releases or mitigates punishment for those Wudi wants spared, presenting legal form as an instrument of current ruler preference.
- Zhang cultivates powerful relationships and protects friends' descendants; the episode argues that this network helps preserve a good reputation despite suspicion, harsh statutory interpretation, and unfair judging.
- Ji An condemns Zhang for altering inherited law, arguing over technical details, and making people afraid to move beyond tightly policed boundaries.

## Key Quotes

> “足智以拒谏，言足以饰非。” - Ji An's attributed diagnosis of Zhang Tang's ability to defeat correction and decorate error.

> “天下谓刀笔吏不可以为公卿，果然。” - Ji An's attributed warning against giving document-and-statute specialists governing power.

## Connections

- [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] - the episode supplies the childhood mouse-trial anecdote, tingwei promotion, court-network strategy, and direct ruler-preference account.
- [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] - principled critic who opposes Zhang's technical legal argument with a judgment about public order and fear.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler whose centralizing priorities and case preferences make Zhang's legal skill politically useful.
- [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]], [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]], and [[NiKuanWesternHan|倪宽]] - Confucian and legal-administrative figures through whom the episode presents Zhang's learned public posture.
- [[RulerWillAsLaw|君意即法]] - Zhang's reported case outcomes show legal form tracking the ruler's desired punishment or mercy.
- [[HarshOfficialInstrumentalRule|酷吏工具化统治]] - Zhang's severity becomes useful through appointment, imperial alignment, and elite relationship management.
- [[LegalComplexityDiscretionPower|法律繁密与裁量权力]] - Ji An's inability to answer Zhang's technical detail illustrates how specialist command of dense rules can overpower principle-based criticism.
- [[OuterConfucianInnerLegalistGovernance|外儒内法式治理]] - the episode juxtaposes Confucian learning and personnel with coercive, ruler-centered legal practice.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode supplies an earlier career-and-method layer before later notes cover Zhang's fiscal design, prosecutions, accumulated enemies, suicide, and posthumous reversal.
- The childhood mouse trial is a transmitted anecdote and retrospective character portrait. Its procedure, wording, Zhang's age, his father's response, and any causal connection to adult cruelty remain source-scoped.
- Ji An's condemnation is a hostile contemporary judgment within the episode, not neutral proof of every motive or every case outcome. Zhang's reported personal frugality in later episodes does not resolve whether his legal work was fair.
- The source's descriptions of Han Wudi's centralization, salt-and-iron policy, Confucian exclusivity, frontier events, offices, appointments, and quotations are compact popular-history claims that require primary-text comparison for a full institutional chronology.
