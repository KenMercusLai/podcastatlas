# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》339-2｜以死杀人，酷吏张汤是如何做到的？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the downfall sequence opened in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-338-2-yinwei-yi-chang-anmo-yinfa-de-xuean-lp8mwe-z21lhqhg4lwnj-jmeotag|Hanji 338-2]]. [[JianXuanWesternHan|减宣]]'s report over [[LiWenWesternHan|李文]] and [[LuYezhuWesternHan|鲁谒居]] moves [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] from suspicion to formal accusations, and [[ZhaoYuWesternHan|赵禹]]'s appointment as interrogator convinces [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] that continued resistance will bring the methods of a fellow cruel official upon him.

Zhang kills himself but leaves a short accusation against [[ZhuMaichenWesternHan|朱买臣]], [[WangChaoWesternHan|王朝]], and [[BianTongWesternHan|边通]]. His documented modest estate and [[ZhangTangMotherWesternHan|张汤母亲]]'s refusal of an elaborate funeral then change the emperor's reading of the case. The three chief clerks are executed and Chancellor [[ZhuangQingdiWesternHan|庄青翟]] is driven to suicide, making Zhang's death a posthumous political counterattack rather than a simple endpoint.

## Key Claims

- Han Wudi initially treats suspected leakage of policy and commercial information as grounds to warn Zhang Tang rather than immediately kill him.
- Jian Xuan's report connecting the Li Wen case to Zhang and Lu Yezhu moves the matter from suspicion to an eight-charge confrontation in the episode's account.
- Zhang continues to claim innocence, but loses hope when Han Wudi appoints Zhao Yu, his former legal collaborator and another severe official, to handle the case.
- Before suicide, Zhang names Zhu Maichen, Wang Chao, and Bian Tong as the three chief clerks who caused his death.
- The inventory of Zhang's estate finds only documented imperial rewards, while his mother rejects a costly burial and uses a simple coffin and ox cart.
- The will, estate, and funeral operate together as an evidentiary bundle that changes Han Wudi's judgment after Zhang can no longer defend himself directly.
- Han Wudi executes the three chief clerks, forces Zhuang Qingdi to suicide, releases Zhang's associate, and promotes Zhang's son in the episode's closing account.

## Key Quotes

> “没有这样的母亲，也生不出这样的儿子。” - the emperor's response to the mother's refusal of an elaborate funeral.

> “杀臣者，三长史也。” - the compact accusation attributed to Zhang Tang's final letter.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. Hanji 338-2 supplies the opening investigation; this episode supplies the accusations, suicide, evidentiary reversal, and punishments.
- Transcript normalization: “贤轩,” “赵瑜,” “鲁业居,” and “庄卿宅” are normalized to [[JianXuanWesternHan|减宣]], [[ZhaoYuWesternHan|赵禹]], [[LuYezhuWesternHan|鲁谒居]], and [[ZhuangQingdiWesternHan|庄青翟]] from the established Zhang Tang sequence.
- The episode's wording that Zhang and Lu “共同谋害” Li Wen is stronger than Hanji 338-2's described chain of covert accusation, assigned adjudication, concealment, and execution. The wiki preserves the mechanism and treats the stronger legal characterization as source-scoped.
- The eight charges, exact estate unit and value, text of the final letter, officials' coordination, funeral sequence, punishments, releases, promotions, private motives, and the causal effect of each item on Han Wudi remain episode-attributed pending direct primary-text comparison.
- The closing claims that no one except the emperor wins, that morality necessarily yields to power, and that Zhang's childhood mouse dissection explains his adult cruelty are interpretive judgments rather than settled causal findings.
