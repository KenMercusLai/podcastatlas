## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] conversation has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[TonyHawk]] about finding skateboarding, building technical skill, responding to criticism, recovering from a broken femur, and adapting practice across a long career. Hawk connects [[LifelongSkillProgression]] to intrinsic reward, systematic experimentation, community, and acceptance of changing physical limits, while his failed first rehabilitation makes [[InjuryRecoveryAdherence]] a caution against confusing athletic toughness with sound recovery. The discussion also covers [[Birdhouse]], [[TonyHawksProSkater]], family, women’s skateboarding, fame, and [[TheSkateparkProject]].

## Key Claims
- Hawk describes learning tricks by recombining known components, reasoning through body position and timing, making hundreds of attempts when necessary, and treating the feeling of progress as a reward in itself.
- His career supports [[LifelongSkillProgression]] as a cycle of curiosity, deliberate problem-solving, repeated practice, criticism tolerance, adaptation, and selective risk rather than a simple product of early talent.
- Hawk’s first post-fracture recovery failed after he ignored professional restrictions and skated through a non-union femur; after a second surgery he followed immobilization, sleep, diet, and rehabilitation guidance and returned to the 540.
- He accepts that some tricks and grabs no longer work, showing that return to a valued activity can coexist with revised limits rather than requiring restoration of every former capacity.
- [[TonyHawksProSkater]] emerged after earlier game pitches failed; Hawk contributed extensive development feedback and declined a $500,000 royalty buyout before the franchise’s unexpected success.
- Hawk founded [[Birdhouse]] during skateboarding’s early-1990s downturn, using home equity and taking on overlapping roles as skater, coach, tour manager, and business owner.
- He presents [[TheSkateparkProject]] as his proudest work because public skateparks can provide underserved young people with community, identity, and a place to belong.

## Key Quotes
> “it was my thing” — Hawk’s description of recognizing skateboarding as the activity around which he wanted to organize his effort.

> “I had to do it for myself” — Hawk on returning to the 540 after his femur injury.

> “proving ground” — Hawk’s description of repeatedly visiting Upland Pipeline to broaden his skill and answer criticism through performance.
