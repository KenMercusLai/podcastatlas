# 320: Hundred Years' War: The Black Prince (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode carries the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]] from the post-Crecy truce and Black Death through the [[BattleOfPoitiers|Battle of Poitiers]]. It contrasts [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III's]] Arthurian kingship, the [[OrderOfTheGarter]], and [[GeoffreyDeCharny|Geoffrey de Charny's]] French chivalric program with bribery, factional murder, [[Chevauchee|chevauchée]], civilian destruction, and tactical adaptation.

The narrative makes French political fragmentation part of the military explanation. [[JohnIIOfFrance|John II]] alienates nobles while [[CharlesTheBad|Charles the Bad]] changes sides, leaving the [[EdwardTheBlackPrince|Black Prince]] and [[JohnChandos|John Chandos]] able to raid from Gascony before a failed junction with Lancaster traps their exhausted army near Poitiers. English archery, French command confusion, and flanking attacks then turn a weak English position into a victory that kills Charny and captures John II.

## Key Claims

- The Black Death interrupts campaigning after Crecy and Calais but does not end Edward III's territorial and sovereignty aims in France.
- Edward uses Arthurian spectacle and the Order of the Garter to turn victory, religious legitimacy, and knightly fellowship into royal political theater.
- Charny's career and the Order of the Star show French efforts to join chivalric honor to practical battlefield discipline, but the oath against retreat can make adaptation harder.
- John II's favoritism and violence toward rivals deepen noble disaffection, while Charles the Bad's shifting alliances repeatedly open French territory to English intervention.
- The Black Prince's 1355 southern chevauchée gains loot, ransom prisoners, and strategic shock by destroying settlements and treating civilian suffering as permissible within elite war.
- The 1356 attempt to unite Lancaster's and the Black Prince's armies fails at the flooded Loire, leaving the prince exhausted, short of supplies, and blocked near Poitiers.
- Poitiers is not explained by a single weapon: archery checks mounted attack, French lines lose coordination, the Order of the Star discourages retreat, and English-Gascon flanking attacks collapse the final French line.
- John II's capture creates extraordinary English leverage while setting up the later problem of converting battlefield success into a workable political settlement.

## Key Quotes

> "a new King Arthur" - the episode's description of Edward III's chivalric self-presentation.

> "never flee in battle" - the Order of the Star's defining obligation in the hosts' account.

> "no pleasure in the slaughter" - Edward III's reported response to news of Poitiers, paired with gratitude for victory and hope for peace.
