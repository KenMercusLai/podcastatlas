# 273: Portugal: The Carnation Revolution

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[Portugal]] from the last years of [[EstadoNovoPortugal|Estado Novo]] through the April 1974 [[CarnationRevolution]] and the unstable [[PortugueseRevolutionaryProcess|revolutionary process]] that ended in a pluralist democratic settlement. It argues that dictatorship collapsed through interacting pressures: the long [[PortugueseColonialWars|colonial wars]], fiscal strain, blocked reform under [[MarceloCaetano]], and a personnel measure that turned junior-officer resentment into the [[ArmedForcesMovementPortugal|Armed Forces Movement]].

The post-coup story resists treating the bloodless takeover as the whole transition. [[AntonioDeSpinola|Antonio de Spinola]], [[VascoGoncalves]], [[OteloSaraivaDeCarvalho]], [[AlvaroCunhal]], the Group of Nine, workers, land occupiers, conservatives, and foreign governments competed over decolonization, nationalization, councils, parties, and military authority until the failed radical move of 25 November 1975 strengthened moderates and [[AntonioRamalhoEanes|Antonio Ramalho Eanes]].

## Key Claims

- [[AntonioDeOliveiraSalazar|Antonio de Oliveira Salazar]] left Portugal with an economically poor, politically repressive, culturally conservative state and unresolved African wars.
- [[MarceloCaetano]] attempted limited liberalization, but the 1973 oil crisis, regime hardliners, colonial costs, and an officer-promotion reform narrowed his room to govern.
- The promotion reform helped turn professional resentment among younger academy officers into the [[ArmedForcesMovementPortugal|Armed Forces Movement]], which joined corporate grievance to decolonization, elections, and abolition of the secret police.
- [[OteloSaraivaDeCarvalho]] coordinated the 24-25 April coup through radio-song signals, while [[AntonioDeSpinola|Spinola]] supplied senior authority for [[MarceloCaetano|Caetano]]'s surrender.
- Carnations in rifles and the low death toll gave the [[CarnationRevolution]] its peaceful public image, although the flower-origin story is partly mythologized and four people were killed by the secret police.
- The fall of dictatorship released a wider [[PortugueseRevolutionaryProcess|revolutionary process]] of occupation, nationalization, land seizure, decolonization, regional backlash, and competing military projects that Spinola could not control.
- [[VascoGoncalves]] and [[OteloSaraivaDeCarvalho|Otelo]] represented radicalizing paths, while the Group of Nine and [[AntonioRamalhoEanes|Ramalho Eanes]] helped defeat the 25 November 1975 challenge and consolidate electoral democracy.
- Western governments treated Portugal as a possible Cold War crisis, but the episode judges Portuguese military moderates and a moderate public center more decisive than external intervention.

## Key Quotes
