# 151 ✪ 从「需要」到「想要」：中国时尚品牌集体向上

## Summary

This [[FengTouQuan|疯投圈]] episode uses Double 11 platform data, company reports, and [[LengYun|冷芸]]'s industry analysis to argue that premium Chinese fashion is growing despite the broader consumption-downgrade narrative. It distinguishes clothing that satisfies need from fashion that creates desire, then connects [[ChineseFashionBrandPremiumization]] to core customer groups, aspirational storytelling, social-media discovery, improved online presentation, and stronger local cultural confidence. Its central qualification is that growth and GMV do not establish a durable brand without full-price sales, active brand search, repeat purchase, new-product contribution, healthy inventory, creative talent, and sustained international recognition.

## Key Claims

- The episode reports broad growth among domestic mid- to high-end fashion brands across apparel, bags, and footwear, treating the pattern as possible structural upgrading rather than one breakout brand.
- Fashion differs from functional clothing because much of its premium comes from desire, imagined character, aesthetic identity, and lifestyle aspiration rather than materials or technical specifications alone.
- Social-media segmentation and recommendation systems can aggregate geographically dispersed niche tastes into a commercially meaningful [[CoreTribePositioning|core customer group]].
- A premium online presentation needs models, scenes, styling, detail imagery, language, and atmosphere that let customers imagine a person and life around the product.
- [[DigitalFlagshipBrandShowroom|digital flagship stores]] can function as brand showrooms, while buyer-led commerce and livestream commerce offer different discovery and conversion strengths.
- The source reports annual repurchase rates of roughly 42% for [[ShanxiaYousong|山下有松]] and 68% for [[Icicle|之禾 / ICICLE]], but does not define the cohorts, channel coverage, return treatment, or calculation method.
- [[FashionBrandOperatingQuality]] requires more than payment GMV: the episode recommends active brand search, full-price conversion, repeat purchase, high-quality customer share, new-product contribution, gross margin, marketing expense, and inventory turnover.
- High livestream return rates can make payment GMV diverge from retained revenue and can add inventory, logistics, and resource costs.
- A unified assortment and price system across online and offline channels is presented as stronger brand infrastructure than online-only products, channel conflict, or treating ecommerce as a clearance outlet.
- The episode identifies four medium-term tests: loyalty to a clear style, operating quality, resistance to price wars, and international recognition.
- Patient capital and shortages of creative directors and styling directors remain long-horizon constraints because premium fashion requires consistent coordination across product, marketing, retail, and image.
