# How Your Thoughts Are Built & How You Can Shape Them | Dr. Jennifer Groh

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] conversation has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[JenniferGroh]] about how vision, hearing, eye position, body state, and context are combined into stable perception and thought. It develops [[CrossModalSpatialRemapping]] through sound localization, ventriloquism, and saccade-linked ear signals; proposes [[SensoryMotorThoughtSimulation]] as a qualified account of thinking; and shows through [[AcousticEnvironmentPerception]] and [[FlowEnvironmentDesign]] how rooms, music, screens, and deliberate friction shape attention. The durable judgment is mechanistic rather than prescriptive: sensory context changes what the brain can bind and prioritize, while personal focus tactics, music-evolution theories, and broad cognitive claims remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- [[CrossModalSpatialRemapping]] describes perception as an active coordinate problem: auditory location depends on binaural timing, intensity, pinna filtering, eye position, and the brain's inference about which sights and sounds share a source.
- Groh reports that eye-position and eye-movement effects appear across the auditory pathway, including tiny, direction-sensitive eardrum movements time-locked to saccades; their functional role remains a proposed early integration signal.
- [[AcousticEnvironmentPerception]] explains why reflections, absorption, reverberation, and delay alter apparent location, distance, intelligibility, and the musical forms that work in a space.
- [[SensoryMotorThoughtSimulation]] proposes that thinking partly reactivates perceptual and motor systems, which can explain associative thought and interference when speech competes with difficult visual-motor behavior.
- [[FlowEnvironmentDesign]] gains a practical but non-universal branch: changing location, removing network access, using familiar non-lyrical music, and creating project-specific cues can reduce competing contexts and help attention stabilize.
- [[MusicMemoryCueing]] extends beyond clinical reminiscence because ordered melody and rhythm can cue the next word or verse, although the source supplies observation rather than comparative memory evidence.
- Hearing safety depends on cumulative sound exposure. Earbuds used to mask the environment can encourage excessive volume, while noise cancellation may reduce the need to overpower background noise.
- Bounded phone use begins with a declared purpose and a clear stopping point; separating social media onto another device adds friction against automatic, endless use.
