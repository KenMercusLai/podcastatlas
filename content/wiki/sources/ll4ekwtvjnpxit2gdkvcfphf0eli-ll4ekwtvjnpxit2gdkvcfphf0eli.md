# 徕芬科技创始人叶洪新×罗永浩！初中毕业后，我先造飞机去了......

## Summary
This [[LuoyonghaosCrossroads|罗永浩的十字路口]] interview follows [[YeHongxin|叶洪新]] from limited formal schooling and early ecommerce through failed aircraft and imperfect scooter projects to founding [[Laifen|徕芬]]. Its central synthesis is that accumulated motor, design, production, and quality knowledge can travel across failed products, but a consumer-hardware company still has to make technical value understandable, build commercial capability, transfer trust across categories, and turn founder judgment into an organization. The episode extends [[HardwarePrototypeToProductionGap]] and [[FounderUniqueValuePreservation]] while adding [[ProductCapabilityCommercializationGap]], [[ConsumerProductValueLegibility]], and [[CrossCategoryBrandTrust]].

## Key Claims
- Ye describes computer training and early web work as the first settings in which interest, practice, and peer recognition replaced a history of weak academic feedback.
- His aircraft project exhausted several million yuan without overcoming endurance, component, reliability, funding, and supply-chain constraints, but built motor, carbon-fiber, structure, and hardware knowledge later reused in scooters and hair dryers.
- The first electric scooter attracted design interest yet exposed weak manufacturing, quality, and delivery capability; a later overseas partnership added testing and quality-system knowledge while also revealing the cost of weak sales, financing, and brand ownership.
- Laifen reportedly developed its own roughly 110,000-rpm motor after available suppliers could not jointly meet speed, power, noise, patent, and reliability requirements.
- The first hair dryer initially sold only a few units per day; detailed third-party explanation, a low-budget technical launch video, and later widely clipped founder content made the product's value more legible and coincided with rapid sales growth.
- The interview distinguishes affordable value from cheapness: a lower price can open a category, but lasting loyalty requires design, experience, service, communication, and reasons to choose the brand beyond comparison with a premium incumbent.
- Laifen's toothbrush case shows that engineering sophistication and optional configuration do not automatically become user value when fewer than one in ten users reportedly open the app and most rely on defaults.
- Ye argues that small appliances rarely retain an uncopyable technical moat; the proposed long-run moat is repeated delivery across products, while the unresolved organizational task is teaching product judgment so multiple teams can operate without founder micromanagement.
