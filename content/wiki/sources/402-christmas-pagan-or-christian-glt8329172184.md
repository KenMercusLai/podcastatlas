# 402. Christmas: Pagan or Christian?

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode tests the claim that Christmas was copied from pagan festivals by separating resemblance from documented transmission. It rejects unsupported parallels involving [[Mithras]] and other gods, treats the December 25 feast of [[SolInvictus]] as real but evidentially ambiguous, and argues that [[Saturnalia]] overlapped with Christmas neither in date nor in a demonstrated line of inheritance.

The episode presents [[December25ChristmasDating]] as a contest between borrowing and internal Christian calculation. Its preferred explanation derives December 25 from March 25 as the date of Christ's conception or incarnation plus nine months, while acknowledging that Jesus' actual birth date is unknown and early Christmas practice is poorly documented.

## Key Claims

- No cited ancient source places the births of Osiris, Adonis, or Dionysus on December 25, gives the infant Krishna gold, frankincense, and myrrh, or describes Mithras dying and rising after three days.
- Persian Mithra and Roman [[Mithras]] should not be collapsed into one unchanged cult; the later Roman mystery religion is presented as a first-century AD development with constructed exotic elements.
- Mithras and [[SolInvictus]] were distinct deities even when inscriptions or imagery linked them through the title "unconquered sun."
- The Calendar of Philocalus records both Christmas and a December 25 celebration of "the unconquered one," but the entry does not establish which festival came first or that Christians copied the date.
- [[Saturnalia]] centered on December 17 and expanded only through about December 23; its feasting, inversion, gambling, candles, and gifts do not by themselves demonstrate descent into Christmas customs.
- A commonly cited explanation of Christian borrowing comes from a twelfth-century Syrian writer, not a fourth-century witness, and reflects a later Eastern-Western calendar dispute.
- The episode favors an internal Christian calculation: March 25 as incarnation or conception, followed by nine months to December 25.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content found.
- The episode contradicts the popular claim that Mithras was born on December 25, died, was buried in rock, and rose after three days; it reports no ancient evidence for that sequence.
- It qualifies rather than denies a December 25 observance associated with the unconquered one: the evidence does not establish priority, identity, or direct Christian borrowing.
- Its March 25 calculation is presented as the preferred explanation, not proof of Jesus' historical birth date.
