# You 2.0: In the Heat of the Moment

## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode has psychologist [[GeorgeLoewenstein|George Loewenstein]] explain the [[HotColdEmpathyGap]]: people in a calm state systematically mispredict how excitement, sexual arousal, fear, shame, pain, hunger, anger, or craving will alter attention and choice, then have difficulty reconstructing those motives after the state passes. Personal stories and [[JulieWoodzicka|Julie Woodzicka]]'s harassment experiment show that imagined assertiveness can give way to fear, freezing, compliance, or rationalization under social pressure without making those responses consent or proof of character.

The practical response is [[StressRehearsalAndPreparedResponse]] rather than confidence alone. Repeated practice under relevant pressure, small preselected actions, accessible protective resources, and carefully designed delay can make cold-state priorities easier to enact in a hot state. The account supports humility and compassion across emotional states while preserving responsibility, consent, and institutional safeguards.

## Key Claims
- [[HotColdEmpathyGap]] operates prospectively when people mispredict future conduct and retrospectively when a later calm self cannot reconstruct the force of an earlier drive or emotion.
- Excitement and sexual arousal can narrow risk assessment and support rationalization, while fear and shame can produce freezing or compliance rather than the anger and confrontation imagined in advance.
- In Woodzicka's reported study, most women imagining an inappropriate interview predicted assertive action or departure, but no participant in the live condition left; experienced participants reported more fear than imagined participants expected.
- Prior exposure alone does not close the gap because the motivational force of pain, arousal, or craving becomes difficult to access after the state ends.
- [[StressRehearsalAndPreparedResponse]] uses repeated realistic practice, small action sequences, and simple prepared language to improve access to a response under pressure.
- Environmental design can support cold-state priorities by making condoms or other safeguards available and by delaying high-consequence decisions made during acute anger or craving.
- Empathy-gap explanations should reduce overconfident self-prediction and victim-blaming without treating emotion as automatic exoneration or weakening consent and accountability.
