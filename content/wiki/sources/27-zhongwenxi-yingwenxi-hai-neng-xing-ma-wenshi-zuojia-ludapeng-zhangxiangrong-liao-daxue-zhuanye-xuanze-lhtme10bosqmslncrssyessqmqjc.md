# 27 中文系、英文系，还能行吗？——文史作家陆大鹏 & 张向荣聊大学专业选择

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] episode has [[LuDapeng|陆大鹏]] and [[ZhangXiangrong|张向荣]] use their Chinese- and English-department experience to discuss major choice under employment pressure and AI uncertainty. Their historical account places modern language-and-literature departments between specialized research, national-cultural preservation, language mastery, and broader humanistic formation, making [[ModernDisciplinaryFragmentation|modern disciplinary fragmentation]] a central tension rather than treating either degree as writer or translator training.

The practical synthesis extends [[AIEraMajorChoice|AI-era major choice]], [[SelfDirectedLearning|self-directed learning]], [[AITranslation|AI translation]], and [[NonInstrumentalUnderstanding|non-instrumental understanding]]. Because technology and labor markets are difficult to forecast, the speakers favor a revisable choice based on interest or at least tolerable fit, joined to language competence, cross-disciplinary reading, practice, and lifelong learning. They reject both a guaranteed “hot major” and the claim that machine translation makes language learning valueless.

## Key Claims

- Modern Chinese and English departments are historically formed institutions rather than timeless containers: each combines language, literature, theory, research, and cultural work in different proportions.
- [[ModernDisciplinaryFragmentation]] makes scholarship more specialized but can split classical materials such as the Five Classics or the *Book of Songs* across literature, history, philosophy, politics, ritual, philology, and linguistics.
- Chinese departments do not primarily train novelists, and English departments are not only translation schools; prospective students may underestimate theory, criticism, linguistics, and research requirements.
- Language-literature education can build reading, writing, speaking, cultural interpretation, and self-study, but enrollment alone does not guarantee those capabilities.
- [[AITranslation]] is sufficient for some routine travel communication, yet direct language ability can still matter for trust, difficult texts, cultural understanding, and access to current technical material.
- A major cannot reliably determine a lifetime career under technological change; interest, realistic constraints, real exposure, and the ability to learn again are more durable than forecasting one permanently safe field.
- Non-instrumental reading, hobbies, and exploration can sustain attention and agency, while constant comparison and premature optimization can intensify anxiety.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode reinforces existing warnings against safe-major lists and AI shortcuts while adding a stronger defense of interest, non-instrumental formation, and revisability.
- The historical narrative, curriculum descriptions, student-skill estimates, employment implications, and future-university scenarios come from two retrospective personal accounts rather than current comparative curriculum or labor-market data.
- Claims about the origins and institutional missions of Chinese and English departments, the 1862 Tongwen Guan lineage, national-language anxiety, overseas Chinese studies, and particular university arrangements remain source-scoped pending specialist verification.
- The speakers explicitly qualify their own university experience as belonging to a less competitive and less socially networked period, so it should not be copied as a current admissions formula.
