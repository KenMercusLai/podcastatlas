# 现场！现场！ | 2025年终特辑：三联女记者揭秘新闻背后的苦与光

## Summary
This [[TalkSanlian|Talk三联]] year-end special for [[XianchangXianchang|现场！现场！]] has [[WuLiwei|吴立伟]] speaking with [[QinSi|秦思]], [[ChengJing|程静]], and [[WeiZhaoyang|魏昭阳]] about the work of women reporters inside [[SanlianLifeWeekly|三联生活周刊]]. The episode moves from gendered expectations around investigation and field reporting to concrete methods: entering difficult scenes, building trust, reporting international stories remotely, carrying emotional pressure, and finding meaning through reader feedback. Its main contribution is to connect [[FemaleJournalistFieldwork|female journalist fieldwork]], [[RemoteReportingPresence|remote reporting presence]], [[ReporterSourceTrustNetwork|reporter-source trust networks]], and [[DeepReportingEmotionalLabor|deep reporting emotional labor]] to the wiki's existing journalism branch.

## Key Claims
- The "all women reporters" label is useful only if it opens a method discussion: the speakers reject the idea that women reporters are limited to empathy or soft topics, and describe advantages and constraints in field entry, covert investigation, and trust-building.
- [[ChengJing|程静]] treats international news as social investigation, using kidnappings, earthquakes, war letters, mountaineering deaths, overseas crime, and migration enforcement to connect individual cases with political, economic, and industry structures.
- [[QinSi|秦思]] argues that investigative reporting does not require a stereotypically aggressive "江湖气"; it can be built through persistence, social-science reasoning, field contact, and careful evidence chains.
- [[WeiZhaoyang|魏昭阳]] shows the vulnerability of a first-year reporter: proximity to young victims, long trips, heavy source relationships, and publication pressure can make public-interest reporting feel personally destabilizing.
- The episode extends [[InterviewAsEmbodiedReporting|interviewing as embodied reporting]] by showing that even remote interviews need field-like discipline: pre-reading, timeline reconstruction, translation planning, and targeted questioning.
- Reporting often depends on help from strangers, including translators, local volunteers, family members, and listeners who suggest leads; the reporter's work is therefore partly a temporary trust network rather than a solitary act.
- The speakers frame suffering in journalism as a consequence of caring about whether a story can be adequately restored to readers, not merely as generic workplace hardship.
- The episode links sustainability to body, sleep, food, rest, and life outside work, extending the wiki's [[MediaWorkAsLifestyle|media work as lifestyle]] branch beyond autonomy into physical and emotional maintenance.
