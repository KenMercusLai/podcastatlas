# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》108-3｜皇太后和情夫嫪毐情色背后的启示

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]], [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]], and [[LaoAi|嫪毐]] branch developed in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-2-laoai-de-hougong-mishi-lof4xuuxfkehm9igpdvihv7exqdy|Qinji 108-2]] and already summarized politically in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-1-lishi-gan-dangmian-dingzhuang-qinshihuang-diyi-ren-lg0dssyicp387foiraohkv1x5aij|Qinji 108-1]]. It follows Zhao Ji's move to [[YongchengQin|雍城]], Lao Ai's expansion from secret favorite into armed faction, [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s suppression and purge, Lu Buwei's removal, and the later restoration of mother and son.

The episode's distinct contribution is interpretive. It treats the scandal as a case of [[PalaceScandalPowerConsolidation|宫廷丑闻式收权]] while also extending [[GenderedHistoriographicalDesireFraming|性别化史书欲望叙事]] and [[FemaleSubjectivityInClassicReading|经典阅读中的女性主体性]]: Zhao Ji is read not only as a disgraced queen mother but as a lonely, politically constrained woman whose body, affection, motherhood, and status are repeatedly made useful to powerful men.

## Key Claims
- The host opens by using Buddhist language about desire, attachment, discipline, and release to frame the Lao Ai affair as more than erotic gossip.
- Zhao Ji is presented as emotionally isolated after widowhood and personal-rule transition pressure; the host speculates that her attachment to Lao Ai may have involved loneliness, dependence, and unresolved feeling around Lu Buwei, not only lust.
- After becoming pregnant, Zhao Ji uses a divination/illness pretext to leave [[Xianyang|咸阳]] for [[YongchengQin|雍城]], where she lives with Lao Ai and secretly raises their two sons.
- The episode contrasts Zhao Ji with [[XuanTaihou|芈八子 / 宣太后]], whose relationship with the [[YiquState|义渠]] king and later male favorite are treated as less hidden because she had stronger political capital and operated in a different Qin court environment.
- Lao Ai's favor becomes political: he receives status, a [[TaiyuanWarringStates|太原]] fief, servants, thousands of retainers, and enough confidence to contend symbolically with [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]].
- Lao Ai's drunken claim to be Ying Zheng's "假父" is treated as the moment where household humiliation, sexual scandal, and sovereign authority collide.
- A hostile informant tells Ying Zheng that Lao Ai is not a real eunuch, has two sons with Zhao Ji, and has allegedly talked about making one of those sons successor after the Qin king's death.
- The episode presents that accusation as a direct threat to Ying Zheng's legitimacy and personal rule, not merely a family embarrassment.
- Lao Ai's rebellion and defeat let Ying Zheng kill Lao Ai, destroy his kin and party network, kill the two sons, and confine Zhao Ji at Yongcheng.
- The host reads the purge as politically useful to Ying Zheng: it removes Lao Ai, weakens Zhao Ji's palace power, and creates the occasion to pressure Lu Buwei.
- Ying Zheng wants to kill Lu Buwei, but the episode says Lu Buwei's old merit, broad support, and Ying Zheng's still-incomplete power make immediate execution premature; the result is dismissal from the chancellorship and removal from the capital.
- [[MaoJiao|茅焦]]'s advice leads Ying Zheng to bury the killed remonstrants, go to Yongcheng, and bring Zhao Ji back, restoring the mother-son relationship in the source's "复为母子如初" frame.
- The host gives Zhao Ji a tragic reading: Lu Buwei treats her as a gift, [[QinZhuangxiangwang|Yiren]] and Ying Zheng make her useful to dynastic order, and Lao Ai uses her as a source of power.
- The episode also credits Zhao Ji with protecting and raising young Ying Zheng through war and danger, arguing that this part of her role cannot be erased by later scandal.
- The closing reflection places the court crisis inside "human suffering" and attachment language: desire, resentment, separation, and inability to let go trap the actors before any political judgment is made.

## Key Quotes
> "假父" - Lao Ai's reported boast in the episode's account.

> "复为母子如初" - the source phrase used for Zhao Ji and Ying Zheng's restoration.

> "一念执着，就受困" - the host's closing moral frame.
