# A Dollop Crossover | Ben Naddaff-Hafrey on Staten Island and the Revolutionary War

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] crossover with [[TheDollop]] uses [[ChristopherBillopp|Christopher Billopp]] and [[StatenIsland|Staten Island]] to recover a strongly Loyalist local experience of the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]]. The island's profitable Royal Navy trade, rejection of the Continental boycott, and substantial Loyalist mobilization make allegiance materially intelligible rather than simply ideological.

The seven-year [[BritishOccupationOfStatenIsland|British occupation of Staten Island]] then reversed much of that support. Theft, inflation, destroyed farms and churches, failed civil government, Patriot raids, and enslavement or re-enslavement by both sides show civilians trapped inside a civil and imperial war, while militia refusal, intelligence sharing, and attacks on British patrols illustrate [[LoyalistSupportErosionUnderOccupation|occupation-driven erosion of allied support]].

## Key Claims

- Staten Island's prewar commerce with Royal Navy ships helped sustain opposition to the colonial boycott and attachment to the Crown.
- Approximately 500 island men entered royal service in July 1776 from a total population reported as 2,847, making Loyalist mobilization locally substantial.
- Billopp's wealth, militia command, repeated abductions, imprisonment, confiscation, and exile make the revolution's civil-war dimension visible at household scale.
- [[NathanielFitzRandolph|Nathaniel Fitz Randolph]]'s raids and Billopp's retaliatory capture show kidnapping and prisoner exchange operating across the narrow New Jersey–Staten Island frontier.
- British occupation progressively damaged the property, prices, institutions, worship spaces, and security of the population it depended on.
- American forces and accompanying civilians also looted the island; the episode reports the abduction and sale of Black residents and the seizure of an enslaved man from Billopp's estate.
- By 1780 Billopp could not mobilize his own militia, and by 1783 some islanders were spying on, ambushing, or openly celebrating the departure of British forces.

## Key Quotes

> "inveterate enemies" - George Washington's reported description of Staten Islanders supplying British ships.

> "without compensation" - Billopp's gravestone claim, qualified by the episode's account of later British payments.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode deepens the wiki's Patriot–Loyalist civil-war frame and qualifies clean patriotic narratives by documenting predation and enslavement associated with both sides.
- Billopp's first release, the precise location and details of the 1776 peace conference, some population and force totals, individual motives, and causal claims about changing allegiance remain episode-reported or explicitly uncertain.
- Billopp's gravestone claim that he left without compensation conflicts with the episode's account of a £400 payment and £80 annual allowance; the source treats the inscription as self-presentation rather than a reliable financial inventory.
