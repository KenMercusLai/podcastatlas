# VOL.141这学非上不可吗？这班非上不可吗？Don’t push the river！｜世界儿童日

## Summary
This [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] World Children's Day episode has [[Atang|阿汤]], child mental-health guest [[ZhangDaming|张大明（本期转写）]], and a second guest transcribed as [[CuiJiabinSchoolRefusalGuest|崔家斌]] discuss school refusal through emotion, agency, family interaction, peers, teachers, education pressure, and bodily distress. It extends [[AdolescentSchoolRefusalMentalHealth]], [[ChildMentalHealthFamilySystemsSupport]], and [[SomatizationAsBodyAlarm]]: listen and help the child name the experience before arguing about attendance, restore bounded choice without abandoning structure, and change the surrounding conditions as well as the child's behavior.

## Key Claims
- [[AdolescentSchoolRefusalMentalHealth]] should ask what school-linked event or feeling the child is avoiding rather than treating “I hate school” as a complete explanation or moral failure.
- Morning delay, inability to get up, repeated promises followed by inability to attend, and school-triggered headache, dizziness, nausea, abdominal pain, or vomiting can be early distress signals rather than simple disobedience.
- [[SomatizationAsBodyAlarm]] gains a child communication branch: after appropriate medical assessment, bodily symptoms may express distress the child cannot yet organize into words.
- When arousal is high, lectures, parental sacrifice narratives, and escalating pressure can intensify resistance; emotion identification, quiet presence, and asking what help is wanted may reopen communication.
- Returning agency does not mean withdrawal of care. Children need informed participation and meaningful choices alongside predictable routines, achievable work expectations, and clear boundaries.
- [[ChildMentalHealthFamilySystemsSupport]] treats school refusal as a feedback loop among child distress, caregiver anxiety, family support, school fit, teacher style, peer relationships, and education pressure rather than a defect located only in the child.
- Play, peer contact, rest, exploration, and other “third-space” activity can help children discover competence and meaning outside school performance.
- Changing class or school may sometimes help, but environmental change alone may not resolve the problem; support may also need to build the child's adaptive capacity and coordinate family, school, and qualified care.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode adds a less coercive response to school refusal without making attendance optional in every case or opposing the existing need for qualified assessment, safety planning, routine, and coordinated support.
- “Giving the choice back,” “Don't push the river,” family-therapy examples, third-space claims, and the fish-and-water analogy are public-education frames, not universal instructions for attendance, treatment, school transfer, or family responsibility.
- Headache, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, back pain, and other bodily symptoms should not be presumed psychological; new, severe, persistent, or worsening symptoms require appropriate medical judgment.
- The source title omits “就” from the body title, and the supplied note transcribes the guests as 张大明 and 崔家斌. The stable [[ZhangDaming]] page already records 张大明/张达明 variation. 崔家斌 remains separate from [[CuiJiabin|崔嘉宾]] and [[CuiJiabinShanxi|崔嘉斌]] because this source does not establish identity.
- The episode is public mental-health education, not individualized pediatric, psychiatric, psychological, family-therapy, educational, or crisis guidance. Self-harm, suicidal speech, acute danger, severe functional decline, or inability to maintain safety requires urgent qualified help.
