# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》331-4｜吃花椒能自杀？别傻了！

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode examines historical reports of killing or suicide with pepper. It moves from stories involving [[WangMang|王莽]] and [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]] to classical descriptions of “closed-mouth” pepper in [[SiminYueling|《四民月令》]] and *Zhouhou Beiji Fang*, then asks why ordinary culinary use does not produce comparable mass poisoning.

The episode's durable contribution is a cautious [[ForensicToxicology]] frame: a substance name alone does not establish a lethal exposure. Species or variety, plant condition, preparation, route, amount, and evidentiary quality all matter. The host proposes neuromuscular or respiratory impairment as a possible explanation for old symptom descriptions, but the supplied document does not provide the compound, dose, study, or case evidence needed to validate that mechanism.

## Key Claims

- Historical texts and narratives contain multiple reports of pepper or pepper wine being used to kill, including the disputed account of Wang Mang poisoning Han Pingdi.
- The episode says old writings distinguish ordinary pepper from especially dangerous “closed-mouth” pepper and cite symptoms such as throat obstruction, inability to breathe, foaming, and bodily coldness.
- Everyday restaurant use is offered as a reality check against the categorical claim that any closed peppercorn is lethal.
- The host argues that risk depends on variety, condition, preparation, route, and dose rather than the ingredient name alone.
- Small culinary quantities are distinguished from concentrated powder or decoction, although the source does not establish a lethal threshold for any preparation.
- A proposed mechanism involving sensory and muscle-nerve paralysis is presented as a hypothesis, not as demonstrated toxicology.
- [[DaizheHuajiaoQuShangchao|《带着花椒去上朝：古杀十九式》]] is identified as an important modern reference for the episode's “椒杀” discussion.

## Key Quotes

> “不谈剂量而单说植物毒性” - the episode's compact warning against categorical danger claims detached from exposure conditions.

> “不得出气，口吐白沫” - symptoms attributed to an old emergency-remedy text in the episode's discussion of closed-mouth pepper.
