# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》825丨历史告诉你：当你没用时，人性最自私

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the first 145 CE succession crisis immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-826-luanshi-de-zui-yu-e-beihou-shi-tanlan-de-renxing-ljk0q4zq4zy6l9rvoo-pipdcsijv|Hanji 826]] and the later [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-827-yi-ju-hua-yinfa-de-diwang-zhisi-lnf6rz1i2vmgsclgiejbh0qevzc2|Hanji 827]] death of [[EmperorZhiOfHan|汉质帝]]. After [[EmperorChongOfHan|汉冲帝]] dies, [[LiGuLateHan|李固]] blocks [[EmpressDowagerLiangNaLateHan|梁妠 / 梁太后]]'s proposed delay in announcing the death, urges [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] to choose the older and respected [[LiuSuanLateHan|刘蒜]], and then watches Liang Ji and Liang Na install the eight-year-old Liu Zuan instead.

The durable contribution is that Liang-family succession control appears before the later poisoning crisis. Li Gu converts death announcement, successor choice, burial scale, and court cleanup into public-order problems, while Liang Ji's preference for a controllable child and the dismissed officials' anonymous accusations show how threatened interests recode institutional repair as factional danger.

## Key Claims

- In 145 CE, Emperor Chong dies at Yutang Front Hall while Xu-Yang disorder and summoned royal candidates make the succession environment unstable.
- Liang Na considers delaying the public announcement until the royal candidates arrive, but Li Gu warns that hiding a ruler's death violates the governing relationship and recalls Qin Shi Huang's concealed-death crisis and the Beixiang Hou precedent.
- Liu Suan arrives with a reputation for gravity and ritual propriety, and many senior officials see him as a credible successor.
- Li Gu privately urges Liang Ji to choose an older, wise, and worthy ruler who can handle government personally, citing Zhou Bo's choice of Emperor Wen and Huo Guang's choice of Emperor Xuan as positive precedents.
- Liang Ji and Liang Na reject that advice and install Liu Zuan, the eight-year-old Bohai king's son who becomes Emperor Zhi.
- Li Gu then argues for a modest burial inside Emperor Shun's Xianling precinct because war and banditry have already strained taxes, labor, and military finance.
- Liang Na temporarily entrusts government to the Three Excellencies and senior ministers, allowing Li Gu's recommendations to remove some harmful eunuchs and irregular officials.
- More than one hundred dismissed officials, sensing Liang Ji's dissatisfaction, forge anonymous letters accusing Li Gu of public-spirited hypocrisy, faction-building, and disrespect at Emperor Chong's bier.
- Zhang Ying rises again at Guangling after Zhang Gang's death, showing that earlier pacification can decay when the specific trusted official disappears.

## Key Quotes

> "秘不发丧" - the proposed concealment of Emperor Chong's death that Li Gu rejects.

> "皇权太诱人" - the host's explanation for Liang Ji rejecting Li Gu's older-ruler advice.

> "表面上公正，实际上借公事谋私" - the accusation frame used against Li Gu in the anonymous letters.
