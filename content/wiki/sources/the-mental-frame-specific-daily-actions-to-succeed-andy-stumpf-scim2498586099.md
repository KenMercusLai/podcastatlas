# The Mental Frame & Specific Daily Actions to Succeed | Andy Stumpf

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[AndyStumpf]] about tools from [[Drownproof]], especially separating what occupies attention from what a person can actually influence. The conversation links [[ConcernInfluenceSorting]], [[SlightlyHarderChoice]], high-risk wingsuit and base-jump judgment, divorce, parenting, pain disclosure, suicide, military transition, and the need to define enough before success consumes relationships and ordinary life.

## Key Claims
- [[ConcernInfluenceSorting]] turns worry into a sorting exercise: concerns are usually numerous, while direct influence often returns to one's own thoughts, words, planning, time use, and responses.
- Social media expands the concern field by making distant people, global problems, and comparison cues constantly available; Stumpf's reduced-phone experiment suggests the pull can persist even after the user notices mental-health improvement.
- [[SlightlyHarderChoice]] is the episode's practical discipline frame: small unobserved choices such as making the bed, replacing toilet paper, drinking water before coffee, or doing the task correctly now can compound into agency.
- [[AndyStumpf]] treats wingsuiting and base jumping as narrow-margin risk systems, not just thrill seeking: preparation, early-flight mechanics, one-parachute constraints, and the question of whether one succeeded or merely got away with it all matter.
- The wingsuiting sections extend [[OutdoorFlowState]] by presenting extreme focus on the next few seconds as a mental reset that can leave Stumpf calmer and more patient afterward.
- The divorce, parenting, surgery, and suicide sections support [[CompetenceStatusMentalHealthBlindSpot]]: elite background, public competence, and status do not make a person immune to shame, isolation, pain, alcohol misuse, or distorted self-view.
- The closing frame extends [[KnowingEnough]]: major goals can have hidden prices, so success should be judged against family, relationships, fulfillment, and the point at which more stops serving life.

## Key Quotes
> "Did you nail it or did you get away with it?" - Stumpf's risk-calibration question.

> "it always takes longer to do it wrong" - the household-discipline lesson.

> "slightly harder" - the choice frame for small daily discipline.
