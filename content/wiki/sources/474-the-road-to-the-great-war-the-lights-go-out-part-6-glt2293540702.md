# 474. The Road to The Great War: The Lights Go Out (Part 6)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode closes its road-to-war sequence by following the final days of the [[JulyCrisis|July Crisis]] from Russian general mobilisation through Germany's declarations of war and invasion of [[Belgium]] to Britain's entry into the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]]. It argues that the principal decision-makers were not simply oblivious sleepwalkers: many understood that a European catastrophe was approaching, but perceived obligations, military timetables, strategic fear, national honour, and fatalism narrowed their room for manoeuvre.

The British decision is the episode's central case. [[EdwardGrey|Edward Grey]] and [[HHAsquith|H. H. Asquith]] faced a divided cabinet, an undeclared moral commitment to protect the French Channel coast, fear of German power in the Low Countries, and Germany's ultimatum to Belgium. Grey's Commons speech then turned those overlapping strategic and diplomatic motives into a durable public narrative centred on Belgian neutrality, British honour, and defence of France.

## Key Claims

- Russian general mobilisation triggered German emergency measures and ultimatums to Russia and France, while Germany's operational planning made escalation difficult to reverse.
- British leaders had no formal alliance requiring war for France, but naval conversations had encouraged France to concentrate its fleet in the Mediterranean on the expectation that Britain would protect the Channel coast.
- The British cabinet remained divided through 1-2 August; Grey, senior Foreign Office figures, Conservative support for intervention, and Churchill's fleet measures increased pressure on the anti-war majority.
- A misunderstood conversation between Grey's secretary and German ambassador Prince Lichnowsky produced a false German belief that Britain might remain neutral and guarantee French neutrality.
- Wilhelm II's relief at that apparent offer, Moltke's resistance to changing mobilisation, and both men's later distress support the episode's argument that desire, political choice, and executable military planning had diverged.
- Germany's ultimatum to Belgium and invasion on 4 August gave British Liberals an international-law and treaty frame that could unite strategic interest with a moral case for intervention.
- Grey's 3 August Commons speech combined the French naval commitment, Channel security, Belgian independence, honour, and reputation into Britain's wartime justification.
- Public cheering did occur, but testimony from Britain, France, Germany, Austria, and Russia also records fear, grief, tears, and foreknowledge of disaster.
- German leaders presented themselves as fighting defensively even while admitting that violating Belgian and Luxembourg neutrality was wrong, a self-image important to later grievance narratives.

## Key Quotes

> "The lamps are going out all over Europe" - Grey's reported line at dusk on 3 August, used as the episode's summary of the July Crisis.

> "the suicide of Europe" - German ambassador Wilhelm von Schoen's description when delivering the declaration of war on France.

> "a scrap of paper" - Bethmann Hollweg's phrase for the Belgian-neutrality guarantee during his final meeting with the British ambassador.

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found; the episode backfills the July 1914 decision chain before the wiki's existing 1915 war branches.
