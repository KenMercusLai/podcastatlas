# Using Stem Cells to Cure Autism, Epilepsy & Schizophrenia | Dr. Sergiu Pașca

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] interview with [[SergiuPasca|Sergiu Pașca]] treats autism and schizophrenia as behaviorally defined, biologically heterogeneous categories whose mechanisms are difficult to isolate in living human brains. Its translational arc moves from patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cells through [[HumanNeuralOrganoidsAndAssembloids|neural organoids and assembloids]] to transplantation models and a proposed [[TimothySyndromeTherapyTranslation|Timothy syndrome therapy]], while keeping unregulated stem-cell injections, animal-to-human inference, and early clinical promise inside explicit evidence boundaries.

## Key Claims
- [[AutismBiologicalHeterogeneity]] is central: shared behavioral labels can contain polygenic and rare high-penetrance conditions with different developmental pathways, co-occurring epilepsy, intellectual disability, sleep problems, and treatment implications.
- [[HumanNeuralOrganoidsAndAssembloids]] models selected human neural cell types, regions, migration, and circuit formation; these systems are not whole brains or evidence of consciousness.
- Patient-derived models can reveal cellular phenotypes, developmental timing, and treatment response that flat cultures or animal models may miss, but each model captures only part of whole-organism biology.
- [[TimothySyndromeTherapyTranslation]] is the episode's leading case: a calcium-channel mutation is followed across neurons, organoids, assembloids, and transplanted human cells toward a nucleic-acid therapeutic preparing for clinical testing.
- Unregulated stem-cell injections lack a clear replacement-cell rationale for autism and can expose families to unknown products, weak trials, placebo-by-proxy, and physical harm.
- Brain gene therapy remains constrained by delivery, cell-type diversity, viral cargo, immune response, reversibility, and the difference between correcting a causal variant and treating a broad diagnosis.
- Ethical governance should address donor consent, animal welfare, pain, transplantation, nomenclature, and possible emergent properties before experiments rather than after public controversy.

- The source's prevalence, heritability, genetic-diagnosis, penetrance, sleep, transplantation, and therapeutic-rescue figures are episode-attributed and not independently validated here.
- The proposed Timothy syndrome treatment is preclinical or trial-preparatory in this source. Rescue in cells or transplanted human tissue does not establish safety or efficacy in patients.
