# 667. The Mystery of the Mona Lisa

## Summary
This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode explains how [[MonaLisa|the Mona Lisa]] became the world's most famous painting through a layered history of [[LeonardoDaVinci|Leonardo da Vinci]]'s technique, sitter identification, Romantic writing, reproduction, theft, parody, museum display, and tourism. It treats the sitter as [[LisaGherardini|Lisa Gherardini]], wife of [[FrancescoDelGiocondo]], and presents the 2005 Heidelberg marginal-note discovery as the decisive support for a 1503 start date and the traditional identification. The episode's larger synthesis is that artistic fame is cumulative: the painting's ambiguity matters, but its modern power also depends on media systems and public institutions.

## Key Claims
- [[MonaLisa|The Mona Lisa]] became a global icon through the interaction of pictorial ambiguity, [[LeonardoDaVinci|Leonardo]]'s reputation, [[ArtReproductionAndCirculation|reproduction technology]], theft publicity, parody, and Louvre tourism.
- [[GiorgioVasari]]'s identification of the sitter as [[LisaGherardini|Lisa Gherardini]] is strongly reinforced by Agostino Vespucci's October 1503 marginal note, discovered by Armin Schlechter in Heidelberg in 2005.
- The painting's visual force depends on [[RenaissancePortraitAmbiguity|ambiguous portraiture]]: sfumato around the eyes and mouth, the sitter's turn toward the viewer, plain dress, and an uncanny invented landscape.
- [[LeonardoDaVinci|Leonardo]]'s decision to keep the painting remains unresolved, with possible explanations including unfinished work, self-presentation, attachment to a masterpiece, or a failed/withheld commission.
- The 1911 Louvre theft by [[VincenzoPerugia]] transformed the painting's public status by making the absent object a global news event.
- Later parody and reproduction, including Marcel Duchamp's and Andy Warhol's uses of the image, confirmed that the painting had become both high-art symbol and mass-cultural material.
