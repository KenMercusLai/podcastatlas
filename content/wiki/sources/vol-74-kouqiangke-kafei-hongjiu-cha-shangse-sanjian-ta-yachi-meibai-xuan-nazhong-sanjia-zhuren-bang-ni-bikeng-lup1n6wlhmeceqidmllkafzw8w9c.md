## Summary
This [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode places tooth whitening after diagnosis and basic oral health rather than treating every yellow or stained tooth as the same cosmetic problem. It strengthens [[DentalAestheticTreatmentBoundary]] by separating natural tooth color, removable surface stain, intrinsic discoloration, chemical bleaching, and restorative procedures, while [[OralCareProductTreatmentBoundary]], [[OralMicrobiomePreventiveCare]], and [[LifecycleOralHealthPrevention]] connect whitening choices to effective cleaning, symptom assessment, and qualified care.

## Key Claims
- [[DentalAestheticTreatmentBoundary]] begins with cause: healthy enamel can reveal naturally yellow dentin, while fluorosis, tetracycline exposure, trauma, loss of pulp vitality, and external staining from tobacco, coffee, tea, or red wine call for different responses.
- Surface stain may improve through professional cleaning, polishing, or appropriately used stain-removal toothpaste; this restores the underlying appearance rather than producing the same effect as chemical bleaching.
- Whitening strips and in-office light-assisted whitening generally use peroxide-based oxidizing agents. Effect, duration, irritation, and sensitivity vary, and pre-existing sensitivity, cracks, gum recession, periodontal problems, or other disease should be assessed first.
- Veneers and crowns may address selected severe intrinsic color changes but are restorative procedures with greater structural burden, not interchangeable upgrades from toothpaste or bleaching.
- [[OralCareProductTreatmentBoundary]] keeps toothpaste, whitening strips, water flossers, and rinses in supporting roles: ingredients and devices do not replace diagnosis, effective technique, professional removal of calculus, or treatment of inflammation.
- [[OralMicrobiomePreventiveCare]] prioritizes complete but non-injurious cleaning with a suitable manual or electric brush, interdental care, and cause-finding when water-flosser use repeatedly produces bleeding.
- [[LifecycleOralHealthPrevention]] includes lower repeated sugar exposure, childhood cleaning and checks, timely cavity and missing-tooth care, professional cleaning, and individualized review of impacted wisdom teeth.
