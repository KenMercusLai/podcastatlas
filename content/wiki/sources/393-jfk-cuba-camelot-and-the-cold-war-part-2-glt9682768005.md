# 393. JFK: Cuba, Camelot and the Cold War (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode examines [[JohnFKennedy|John F. Kennedy]] before Dallas in order to test which political motives for his assassination are plausible. It portrays him as a charismatic but flawed establishment Democrat: cautious on civil rights until events forced stronger action, conventionally anti-communist, and more interested in foreign affairs than radical domestic change. The [[BayOfPigsInvasion|Bay of Pigs]], Berlin, the [[CubanMissileCrisis|Cuban Missile Crisis]], and Vietnam created real conflict and resentment, but the hosts distinguish those tensions from proof that Cuban exiles, the military, intelligence agencies, or other institutions killed him.

## Key Claims

- Kennedy won the 1960 Democratic nomination and presidency as a coalition-minded centrist whose Catholicism, regional identity, youth, and narrow victory mattered more than a radical domestic program.
- His advisers and tax policy placed the administration inside the political establishment, weakening theories that business or conservative elites saw him as a socialist threat.
- Kennedy moved toward stronger civil-rights action under pressure from desegregation crises and national events, but the episode does not treat his early record or legislative prospects as unambiguously radical.
- The failed Bay of Pigs invasion gave anti-Castro Cuban exiles a concrete grievance because Kennedy refused to escalate a collapsing operation with greater U.S. air power.
- During the Cuban Missile Crisis, Kennedy resisted immediate military strikes, used a naval blockade, and accepted a quiet missile trade involving Turkey, making restraint central to the episode's positive assessment of his presidency.
- Kennedy increased the number of U.S. military advisers in Vietnam while also fearing a quagmire; the hosts therefore reject certainty that he had already decided on withdrawal.
- Strong approval, a likely contest with Barry Goldwater, and the coalition purpose of the Texas trip make Kennedy's 1964 prospects important context for Dallas without proving any assassination motive.

## Key Quotes

> "New Frontier" - Kennedy's campaign language for youth, national ambition, and Cold War competition.

> "missile gap" - campaign claim used to attack the Eisenhower-Nixon foreign-policy record.
