# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》615丨瞠目结舌！古代彩礼这么高？？？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills 3 CE under [[HanPingdi|汉平帝]], before the 4 CE Wang Mang petition, inner-court, and ritual-project sequence in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-620-shishang-zui-chuanqi-de-quanmin-daxuan-llijvsut0ldv-ugfa281sllst4gu|Hanji 620]] through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-623-ma-pi-hai-shi-zhenxin-zhichangren-ruhe-zai-kuajiang-yu-chanmei-jian-zhao-pingheng-lrpnacm1jbtd9gry-psse-q-uvnb|Hanji 623]]. It follows [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] sending senior officials to propose marriage for Pingdi to [[WangMang|王莽]]'s daughter, the court's divination and ancestral notification, the enormous imperial bride-price precedent, Wang Mang's partial acceptance and kin distribution, and his later social-rank regulation and court-power consolidation.

The durable synthesis is that Wang Mang's pre-usurpation ascent begins inside imperial marriage and ritualized public virtue, not only petitions and omens. The empress selection makes Wang-family power part of Han sacrificial continuity; the bride-price refusal-and-distribution performance turns private marriage wealth into public humility and kinship capital; and the summer regulations on ranks, ceremonies, property transfers, slaves, and local teaching posts show Wang Mang extending ritual order into everyday social administration before the later 4 CE prestige sequence.

## Key Claims

- The court treats Pingdi's marriage to Wang Mang's daughter as a dynastic and sacrificial matter: officials inspect her suitability, divination is performed, and the match is announced to Han ancestors.
- The episode records a precedent in which the emperor's bride-price for an empress is golden value of 20,000 jin, converted in the episode to 200 million cash.
- Wang Mang does not accept the full amount; he takes 63 million cash and distributes 43 million to eleven accompanying families and poorer Wang-clan relatives.
- The host reads that acceptance pattern as both humility performance and reputation management, because refusal, partial acceptance, and redistribution make him look modest and generous.
- The discussion of "九族" frames Wang Mang's kin distribution as unusual in a tradition more often remembered through collective punishment.
- Wang Mang's later rank regulations cover carriages, clothing, receptions, marriage and mourning, slave transactions and treatment, land and house transfers, and utensils.
- The same regulatory package includes a grain-sacrifice temple and local teaching officials, showing early social-standardization ambition before the later Ming Tang, Biyong, Taixue, and praise-politics episodes.
- The closing "四府三公制" frame says Wang Mang already holds both inner and outer authority as Taifu and Da Sima while Wang Zhengjun withdraws from ordinary political decisions.
