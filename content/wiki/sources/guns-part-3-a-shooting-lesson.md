# Guns Part 3: A Shooting Lesson

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode uses [[GregWallace]]'s shooting lesson to distinguish civilian semi-automatic AR-15 rifles from selective-fire military weapons and to examine how feature-based assault-weapon bans regulate grips, stocks, magazines, and other configurations while leaving functionally similar firearms available. [[MalcolmGladwell]] disputes claims from [[JoshSugarman]] and the [[ViolencePolicyCenter]] that blur semi-automatic fire, automatic fire, and “spray firing,” while continuing to support gun control in broader terms.

[[BabakSarani]] then describes a review of 232 autopsies from 23 mass shootings in which handgun victims had a higher likelihood of death than rifle victims, reportedly because handgun attackers were closer, struck the head or torso more often, and fired repeated shots. The episode uses that result to strengthen [[WeaponCategoryPolicyFraming]] and to argue within [[MultiInterventionGunViolencePrevention]] for common rules across firearm categories rather than relying on a narrow, feature-defined rifle ban.

## Key Claims

- Civilian semi-automatic rifles fire once per trigger pull, whereas selective-fire military rifles can continue firing while the trigger remains depressed.
- The AR-15 is a modular platform whose grips, stocks, optics, triggers, muzzle devices, and internal components often change handling or configuration without defining the underlying firing mechanism.
- The episode argues that the 1994–2004 federal ban and later proposals targeted combinations of listed features while exempting many functionally comparable firearms.
- Magazine limits may create a pause, but Wallace's demonstration suggests that trained reloading or switching weapons can make that pause brief; the episode does not establish that limits have no population-level effect.
- Gladwell argues that advocacy language about killing power and “spray firing” obscures material differences between semi-automatic and automatic operation.
- Sarani's episode-reported autopsy review found handgun victims more likely to die within its selected mass-shooting sample, making distance, shot placement, repeated hits, and concealability relevant alongside firearm category.
- The closing policy claim favors common rules for rifles and handguns and treats accurate classification as a starting point rather than a complete response to firearm harm.
