# CA Governor Candidate Steve Hilton on Why California is Destroying Itself & How a Republican Can Win

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] interview profiles [[SteveHilton|Steve Hilton]]'s 2026 Republican campaign for governor of [[California]]. Hilton presents a broad reset argument: California is too expensive, too captured by unions and special interests, too hostile to building and business, and too ineffective at basic outcomes.

The conversation turns Hilton's biography and Thatcher-era political formation into a policy platform around [[CaliforniaAffordabilityGovernanceReset]], [[CaliforniaBuildingCostCapture]], [[CaliforniaEnergyPermittingPolitics]], school accountability, crime enforcement, and [[CaliforniaHomelessnessEnforcementTreatment]]. The hosts repeatedly press implementation constraints, especially the Democratic legislature, making the episode useful as both campaign messaging and a test of what a Republican governor could actually change in a heavily Democratic state.

## Key Claims
- Hilton frames California as a modern version of 1970s Britain: union-dominated, high-tax, overregulated, hard to build in, and ready for a change-election challenge.
- His tax plan would exempt the first $100,000 of income from California state income tax and apply a 7.5% rate above that threshold, which he describes as both pro-worker and pro-growth.
- Hilton says the plan would reduce revenue by about $60 billion, or 18.5%, and argues that spending could return toward pre-pandemic levels by cutting waste, fraud, and ineffective programs.
- [[CalDOGE]] is presented as Hilton's audit-and-efficiency project, with [[HerbMorgan]] running for controller as the enforcement partner who could audit recipients of state funds and stop suspect payments.
- Hilton says housing costs are driven by layered building mandates, litigation risk, project labor agreements, prevailing wage rules, fees, and union influence rather than by scarcity alone.
- Hilton argues California's energy costs are partly self-inflicted because the state restricts in-state oil production while importing much of the oil it uses.
- He presents school underperformance, crime, prison capacity, homelessness, addiction, and mental-health treatment as enforcement-and-accountability problems rather than only funding problems.
- Hilton's electoral theory depends on reaching California's top-two general election and making 2026 a "change versus more of the same" race against one Democrat.

## Key Quotes
> "the unions run this place" - Hilton's paraphrase of a legislator explaining why housing reforms could not be publicly supported.

> "as long as we use oil and gas, we should use our own" - Hilton's rule of thumb for California energy production.

> "change versus more of the same" - Hilton's proposed general-election frame.
