# 363. Sixties Fashion: The Teenage Revolution (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode explains how wartime rationing, utility clothing, postwar recovery, youth earnings, boutiques, magazines, television, and fashion photography prepared London's brief rise as a world fashion center. It follows the path from [[ChristianDior|Christian Dior]]'s New Look and Teddy Boy, mod, and rocker styles to [[MaryQuant|Mary Quant]]'s youth-oriented retail and [[JeanShrimpton|Jean Shrimpton]] and [[DavidBailey|David Bailey]]'s image-making, presenting fashion as evidence of a wider [[PostwarFashionConsumerRevolution|postwar consumer revolution]].

## Key Claims

- Wartime clothing rationing and utility rules made durability, restraint, and “make do and mend” the baseline against which later abundance and novelty appeared revolutionary.
- [[ChristianDior]]'s 1947 New Look restored Parisian glamour through fabric-heavy skirts, narrow waists, and curves, rejecting wartime utility rather than yet establishing London's youth-led model.
- Teddy Boys, mods, and rockers used clothing to build distinct youth identities from Edwardian, Italian, and American references, showing that subcultural novelty often came through recombination.
- Full employment, pocket money, weekend work, and rising living standards gave young people more control over appearance while provoking adult anxiety about consumption and independence.
- [[MaryQuant]]'s Chelsea boutique joined childlike and retro references, shorter hemlines, informality, and later mass distribution, although its early “classless” image overstated affordability.
- Teenage-girl magazines, television music shows, boutiques, and colour supplements linked aspiration, advertising, retail, and celebrity into a scalable youth-fashion system.
- [[DavidBailey]]'s informal photography and [[JeanShrimpton]]'s willowy public image made the model-photographer partnership itself a form of international celebrity.

## Key Quotes

> “make do and mend” - wartime thrift slogan used to frame the austerity baseline.

> “young” - adjective the episode says Vogue was already using as a persuasive lifestyle and fashion value by 1959.
