# 404. The Nazis in Power: The Night of the Long Knives (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the [[NightOfTheLongKnives|Night of the Long Knives]] as the violent settlement of an unstable coalition inside [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]]. [[AdolfHitler]] used false coup claims, SS and police capacity, army pressure, and the personal arrest of [[ErnstRohm|Ernst Röhm]] to destroy the leadership of the [[Sturmabteilung|SA]] while also killing conservative and personal rivals.

The purge subordinated the mass stormtrooper movement, strengthened [[HeinrichHimmler]] and the SS, reassured the army, and made murder an instrument of publicly defended state authority. Hindenburg's death, the merger of the presidency with Hitler's office, the army's personal oath, and a plebiscite then completed the episode's account of [[FuhrerStateConsolidation|Führer-state consolidation]].

## Key Claims

- Conservative elites helped make Hitler chancellor because they believed they could contain and use him; emergency powers, intimidation, and the Enabling Act rapidly dismantled constitutional limits.
- Röhm's mass SA embodied demands for a “second revolution” and challenged both Hitler's authority and the professional army by proposing that the stormtroopers become Germany's principal defense force.
- Hindenburg, Blomberg, the army, and conservative criticism increased pressure on Hitler to end party disorder, while Himmler, Heydrich, Göring, the SS, and police supplied the organizations needed for a purge.
- The alleged Röhm coup was false: the episode says the SA was not seriously preparing the treason used to justify the killings.
- Hitler personally arrested Röhm and other SA leaders, while Göring's Berlin operation widened the violence to Schleicher, Papen associates, Gregor Strasser, Gustav Ritter von Kahr, and accidental victims.
- The cabinet's 3 July law retroactively declared the killings legal, and Hitler later claimed the role of “supreme judge” of the German people.
- Public resentment of SA bullying, army approval, SS advancement, the leadership plebiscite, and the army oath helped convert the purge into stronger personal rule.

## Key Quotes

> “supreme judge” - Hitler's description of his authority when defending the purge before the Reichstag, as reported by the episode.

> “second revolution” - Röhm's demand for renewed Nazi radicalism after the seizure of power.
