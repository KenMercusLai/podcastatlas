## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] Advice Line episode pairs [[GuyRaz]] with [[DaymondJohn]] of [[FUBU]] to advise [[SaborAMexicoSalsas]], [[RaisedNutrition]], and [[CooksWhoFeed]]. The episode treats founder story as useful only when backed by customer value, numbers, operational discipline, and staged proof. Its main wiki contribution is a consumer-products and B2B-sales case where [[FounderPitchEvidenceFit]], [[SmallRetailerChannelTest]], [[NicheOriginStoryExpansion]], and [[MissionAlignedVendorSwitching]] interact.

## Key Claims
- [[DaymondJohn]] looks for founders who know their market, audience, and numbers, and who can explain why they are the right founder at the right moment.
- Founder stories are strongest when they come from personal pain, joy, or lived customer understanding, but story has to translate into value rather than remain autobiography.
- [[SaborAMexicoSalsas]] should treat farmers markets as a high-feedback, high-margin base and test small Chicago retailers before pursuing larger grocery accounts.
- [[SmallRetailerChannelTest]] lets a local food brand learn whether its story, sampling, pricing, and replenishment work before it accepts big-retail complexity.
- [[RaisedNutrition]] should keep its jiu-jitsu origin story because the first tribe explains why the product exists, even as health-and-wellness buyers broaden demand.
- [[CooksWhoFeed]] should sell B2B prospects a low-risk trial that combines quality, price, customization, and the meal give-back story rather than asking buyers to switch vendors only out of virtue.
- For [[CooksWhoFeed]], the best pitch makes the client the hero: the linen purchase helps restaurants, cruise lines, culinary schools, or hospitality groups tell their own sustainability and impact story.
- Direct customer feedback remains valuable even for B2B-heavy companies; Daymond recommends live selling on [[Whatnot]] or [[TikTok]]-style platforms because it exposes real consumer objections and language.

## Key Quotes
> "why me, why now, and why this" - Daymond's pitch test for founder-market fit.

> "high tolerance for pain" - Daymond's shorthand for the rejection and doubt founders must survive.

> "undeniable package" - Daymond's phrase for a low-risk B2B test offer.
