# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》323-1｜为什么善良的他，会被皇帝误解？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode gives the early biography behind [[BuShiWesternHan|卜式]]'s later public rise. After raising his younger brother, leaving him most of the family property, and rebuilding wealth through sheep herding, Bu Shi offers half his property for the Xiongnu war; [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] treats the apparently disinterested act as contrary to ordinary motives, and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] initially lets the proposal lapse.

Repeated conduct changes the court's judgment. Bu Shi later gives 200,000 cash to local disaster relief, returns an imperial labor-service reward to the public treasury, accepts work tending sheep in Shanglin, and turns successful herd management into a claim about governing people. The episode then traces his trial as Goushi magistrate, promotion to Chenggao, and appointment as tutor to the king of Qi. Its durable contribution is a conduct-based correction path: an unusual act can trigger reasonable questions, but consistent action across family, war, relief, reward, work, and office supplies stronger evidence than either initial suspicion or retrospective moral idealization.

## Key Claims

- Orphaned young, Bu Shi raises his younger brother, leaves him most inherited property at division, and later repeatedly helps him after rebuilding wealth through more than a decade of sheep herding.
- During war against the Xiongnu, Bu Shi offers half his property for military expenditure and denies seeking office or redress for a grievance.
- Gongsun Hong calls the proposal contrary to ordinary human motives and advises against making such conduct a public model; Wudi initially follows that advice.
- During a later famine, Bu Shi gives 200,000 cash to the Henan governor for local relief, bringing his earlier offer back to Wudi's attention.
- Wudi grants him the labor-service payments of four hundred people, but Bu Shi turns the reward over to the public treasury; the episode contrasts this with wealthy households hiding assets and avoiding levies.
- After accepting a court post and tending sheep in Shanglin, Bu Shi tells Wudi that regular routines and separating disruptive animals also describe governance; the analogy reveals practical management ability but carries a coercive edge.
- Wudi tests him as Goushi magistrate, then promotes him through Chenggao to tutor to the king of Qi after the episode reports successful local administration.

## Key Quotes

> “此非人情，不轨之臣，不可以为化而乱法。” - the episode's formulation of Gongsun Hong's objection to treating the donation as an exemplar.

> “治民亦犹是也。” - Bu Shi's compressed move from sheep husbandry to governing people.

## Connections

- [[BuShiWesternHan|卜式]] - shepherd, donor, relief contributor, and local administrator whose repeated conduct revises the court's first judgment.
- [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] - gatekeeper who treats the initial half-property offer as abnormal and potentially disruptive as a public example.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler who first accepts the skeptical recommendation, later recognizes a pattern of contribution, and tests Bu Shi through familiar work and local office.
- [[StatusFilteredTalentMisrecognition|门第筛选式人才误判]] - framework for how lack of office and an unconventional motive can block credibility before conduct is tested.
- [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - war context for Bu Shi's initial offer of wealth to the state.

## Contradictions

- No settled event-level contradiction is adopted. Hanji 323-1 supplies the early-life, donation, relief, Shanglin, and local-office sequence that Hanji 323-2 only summarizes before Bu Shi's later Nanyue offer.
- The episode's claim that late-Wudi society was uniformly selfish, its broad account of commercial decline, the precise chronology and amounts, and the reported success of each office remain source-scoped pending primary-text comparison.
- Gongsun Hong's objection may reflect status filtering, fraud or motive risk, fear of disruptive precedent, or several concerns at once. The episode supports initial institutional suspicion more securely than a single causal explanation.
- Bu Shi's herd analogy demonstrates transfer of practical management reasoning in the episode, but removing “unruly” members is not adopted as a general or normatively sufficient theory of governing people.
