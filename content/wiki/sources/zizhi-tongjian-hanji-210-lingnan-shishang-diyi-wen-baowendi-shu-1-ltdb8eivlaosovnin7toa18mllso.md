# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》210｜岭南史上第一文《报文帝书》（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies [[ZhaoTuo|赵佗]]'s reply to the reconciliation letter delivered by [[LuJia|陆贾]] for [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. Zhao Tuo attributes the rupture to Empress Lü-era trade restrictions, three detained envoys, reports that his ancestral graves and relatives had been harmed, the cancellation of his kingship, and suspicion that the Changsha ruler encouraged the conflict. He then accepts restored exchange and royal recognition, removes his outward imperial title and ceremony, resumes submission and tribute, and presents the result as a return to [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s earlier settlement.

The reply is conciliatory without being unconditional. Zhao Tuo says his imperial title had been for internal use, invokes his Central Plains ancestry and Liu Bang's precedent, and stresses Nanyue's land, soldiers, and surrounding royal field. The episode therefore completes the post-Lü repair as [[RecognitionBackedFrontierDiplomacy|recognition-backed frontier diplomacy]] while sharpening [[NominalSubmissionLocalAutonomy|nominal submission with local autonomy]]: Han regains an exclusive outward imperial hierarchy, Zhao Tuo retains his local base and bargaining dignity, and both sides avoid war. The final transition notes the death of [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|齐王刘襄]] but does not develop his later political consequences.

## Key Claims

- Zhao Tuo's reply says Empress Lü-era restrictions on metal tools, livestock, and exchange broke the earlier Han-Nanyue relationship.
- He reports that three missions to Chang'an did not return and that rumors said his ancestral graves had been destroyed and his relatives killed.
- Zhao Tuo explains his imperial title as a response to the rupture and says it was used within Nanyue rather than as a direct attack on Han.
- Liu Heng's restoration of Zhao Tuo's title, kin relations, and exchange allows him to remove the imperial title and ceremony, resume submission, and promise tribute.
- Zhao Tuo nevertheless emphasizes Nanyue's territory, soldiers, and relations with surrounding southern rulers, turning the reply into both reassurance and a display of bargaining power.
- His reference to “先人” joins his own Central Plains ancestors to Liu Bang's earlier recognition settlement and asks Liu Heng to honor both lines of continuity.
- The host interprets the exchange as a mutually acceptable settlement in which status concessions and local autonomy prevent a costly war.
- The closing notice calls Liu Xiang a major initiator of the anti-Lü mobilization and records his death, but does not establish a cause.

## Key Quotes

The supplied episode document is a structured summary rather than a verbatim transcript, so no sentence is promoted as an exact quotation. Its central attributed formulations are Zhao Tuo's promise to remove the imperial title and his appeal to the prior settlement made by the two sides' “先人.”

- No settled contradiction is adopted. This source completes [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-209-zhoubo-wendi-duoquan-de-xishengpin-2-llj5lvhytwo7ush06yks6qc0whqc|Hanji 209 part 2]], which stops after Liu Heng's letter is delivered.
- Zhao Tuo's account is diplomatic self-justification. Claims about detained envoys, destroyed graves, killed relatives, Changsha's instigation, purely internal use of the imperial title, territorial scale, troop strength, and his reasons for settlement remain attributed to the reply or the host's reading rather than independently verified facts.
- The episode says Zhao Tuo removes the imperial title and ceremony; later [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-349-2-ti-shang-kuzi-bu-ren-ren-ta-ruhe-rang-hanwudi-de-mouhua-paotang-lgmw_edznbj_oypxpnln93bzu7yr|Hanji 349-2]] says imperial-style privilege and a “Nanyue Wudi” seal persist internally. The wiki treats these as compatible layers of outward rank retreat and internal symbolic autonomy, pending primary-text comparison.
- The identities and status of the southern groups and rulers listed in the strength display, the exact wording of the omitted passage, dates, gifts, and tribute terms require comparison with transmitted texts.
