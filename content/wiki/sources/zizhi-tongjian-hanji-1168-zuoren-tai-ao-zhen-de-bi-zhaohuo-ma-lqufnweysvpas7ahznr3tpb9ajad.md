## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[GuanYu|关羽]] from the high point of his 219 CE northern campaign into a strategically exposed position. [[SimaYi|司马懿]] and [[JiangJiLateHan|蒋济]] advise [[CaoCao|曹操]] to exploit tension between [[SunQuan|孙权]] and [[LiuBei|刘备]], while [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]] argues that controlling [[Jingzhou|荆州]] and the Yangtze is more defensible for Eastern Wu than taking cavalry-friendly Xuzhou.

The operational sequence joins alliance repricing to [[AdversaryTraitExploitation|对手性格弱点利用]]. Lyu Meng's public illness and withdrawal invite Guan Yu to reduce his rear defense; the little-known [[LuXunDongWu|陆逊]] then uses deferential praise to reinforce Guan Yu's confidence. The episode treats Guan Yu's pride as an exploitable vulnerability, while preserving material interests, geography, force allocation, and multi-actor strategy as independent causes.

## Key Claims

- A Luhun labor levy reportedly triggers Sun Lang's revolt; Guan Yu gives him rank, seals, and troops, contributing to wider response south of Xudu and to Cao Cao's relocation concern.
- Sima Yi and Jiang Ji argue that Yu Jin's defeat followed flooding rather than collapse of Cao's state and that Guan Yu's rise will alarm Sun Quan, making a Cao–Sun approach possible.
- Lu Su's earlier accommodation of Guan Yu is presented as threat ordering: while Cao Cao remains strongest, Sun Quan and Liu Bei benefit from preserving their coalition despite bilateral conflict.
- Lyu Meng rejects a first move against Xuzhou because open terrain would make conquest hard to hold against Cao Cao's cavalry; he prefers removing Guan Yu and controlling the Yangtze corridor.
- Guan Yu's refusal of a marriage proposal for his daughter, including the episode's reported insult to Sun Quan's envoy, further damages a relationship already divided by Jingzhou interests.
- Lyu Meng publicly claims serious illness and leaves Lukou so Guan Yu will infer lower rear risk and shift troops toward Fancheng.
- Lu Xun recognizes that Guan Yu's recent success and contempt for lesser-known officers create an opening, but Lyu Meng keeps the existing plan compartmentalized until recommending him to Sun Quan.
- Lu Xun's deferential letter praises Guan Yu's victories and asks for guidance; the episode says Guan Yu responds by moving more rear-defense troops north.
- The deception succeeds through a chain of plausible signals rather than one lie: alliance incentives, Lyu Meng's apparent incapacity, an obscure successor, flattering correspondence, and observable redeployment reinforce one another.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends Hanji 1166's Fancheng high point and Hanji 1105's compressed death endpoint by supplying the intervening alliance and deception sequence.
- Causal qualification: pride and status sensitivity may help explain Guan Yu's response, but they do not replace territorial claims, Sun Quan's interests, Cao-side inducement, geography, force allocation, or the agency of Lyu Meng and Lu Xun.
- Strategic qualification: Lyu Meng's plan offers immediate territorial and defensive gains while weakening the longer anti-Cao coalition; the source does not establish the full counterfactual cost of either choice.
- Source-scope caution: the Luhun sequence, Cao Cao's relocation deliberation, land promise, marriage exchange, Lu Su and Lyu Meng dialogue, illness mechanics, secrecy, Lu Xun's letter, motives, and exact troop movements require comparison with primary texts and wider scholarship.
- The opening claims that self-restraint necessarily brings fortune and indulgence necessarily brings disaster are moral framing, not a complete causal model of political or military outcomes.
