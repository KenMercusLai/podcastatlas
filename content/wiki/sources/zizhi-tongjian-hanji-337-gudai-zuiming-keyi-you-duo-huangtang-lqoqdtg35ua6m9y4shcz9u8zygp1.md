# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》337｜古代罪名可以有多荒唐？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses [[YanYiWesternHan|颜异]]'s death to show how opposition to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s fiscal policy could be converted into the speech crime later called 腹诽. After Yan objects that a forty-wan white-deer-skin currency is disproportionate to the several-thousand-cash tribute it supports, hostile investigator [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] later treats Yan's silent “微反唇” response to criticism of an edict as concealed defamation deserving death.

The durable synthesis is institutional rather than psychological: once even silence and expression can be interpreted as disloyal thought, officials learn that neither direct advice nor noncommittal restraint is safe. The episode connects that chilling effect to [[JiaYi|贾谊]]'s [[GuoQinLun|《过秦论》]] warning about rulers who reject advice and to [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s wider concern with political feedback, while leaving the exact legal status and documentary development of “腹诽罪” for fuller source comparison.

## Key Claims

- The episode presents Yan Yi as a clean and upright official who rises to one of the Nine Ministers before opposing a fiscal proposal associated with Zhang Tang.
- Yan argues that using a white-deer-skin currency valued at forty wan to pad tribute worth only several thousand cash reverses the proper relation between principal and accessory.
- Zhang Tang and Yan already have a hostile relationship when another accusation gives Zhang authority to investigate him.
- When a guest criticizes a newly issued edict, Yan does not answer but makes a slight lip movement described as “微反唇.”
- Zhang recodes the silence and expression as a Nine Minister recognizing a bad edict, failing to remonstrate, and instead defaming it inwardly; Yan is then condemned to death.
- The episode argues that Yan's case teaches later ministers to preserve themselves through flattery and leaves popular grievances unable to reach the ruler.
- It contrasts the case with Emperor Wen's reported repeal of slander-related offenses and uses Jia Yi and Sima Qian to frame rejected advice as a cause of feedback collapse and dynastic danger.

## Key Quotes

> “异不应，微反唇。” - the small, nonverbal response that the episode says becomes evidence against Yan Yi.

> “见令不便，不入言而腹诽。” - Zhang Tang's attributed reasoning for converting failed remonstrance and inferred inner criticism into a capital offense.
