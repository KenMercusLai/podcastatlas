## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty-Corino]] interview [[JulieOsk|Julie Osk]] of [[OskAdvisory|Osk Advisory]] about why AI infrastructure spending is pushing large technology companies into the corporate bond market. The episode frames [[Amazon]], [[Alphabet]], [[Meta]], [[Oracle]], [[Nvidia]], and [[SpaceX]] as part of a broader shift in which data-center and compute demand can outrun even very large companies' prior budgets and free cash flow.

The source's main contribution is to expand [[AIInfrastructureDebtFinancing]] from one-off company cases into a sector-wide bond-market question. Osk argues that long-term debt can match the useful life of data-center assets, but the financing story still depends on interest rates, capex ROI, bond supply, AI token demand, regulation, public backlash, power and water access, and whether companies become more disciplined about when AI is actually worth using.

## Key Claims
- The episode says major technology companies driving the AI boom are expected to spend more than $700 billion on data-center infrastructure this year.
- [[Amazon]], [[Alphabet]], [[Meta]], and [[Oracle]] are described as issuing corporate bonds at a scale the technology industry has not seen before.
- Osk says demand for AI tokens and compute feels insatiable, while available infrastructure supply is still not enough.
- The episode says [[Alphabet]] capital expenditures rose from about $32 billion in fiscal 2023 to about $91 billion in fiscal 2025, with spending expected to almost double this year.
- Osk says capex used to be a fraction of free cash flow for major technology companies, but now can become a multiple of free cash flow.
- The episode cites the [[WallStreetJournal|Wall Street Journal]] for a figure of $244 billion in large-tech bond issuance this year, with [[Amazon]], [[Nvidia]], and [[SpaceX]] accounting for $75 billion in June.
- Osk explains that bondholders generally sit ahead of equity holders in the capital structure, so some investors may see bonds as a different way to take AI exposure.
- The episode says data centers are a different asset-heavy model from the advertising-led businesses of [[Alphabet]] and [[Meta]].
- Osk argues that long-term bonds can fit data centers because the physical assets are expected to last for many years.
- Interest-rate risk matters because inflation, volatility, and uncertainty around AI returns can raise borrowing costs even for large technology issuers.
- Osk identifies market risk, uncertain token demand, competition with China, regulation, public backlash, and water or resource constraints as risks for bond investors.
- The episode says [[Meta]] is not yet earning back its AI spending, making ROI scrutiny more visible.
- Osk says repeated bond issuance can itself create supply-and-demand risk if investors are asked to absorb too much AI-related debt too quickly.
- The episode frames 2026 as a year when companies are asking harder questions about ROI, quality, cost, efficiency, and whether some tasks are still cheaper or better for humans.
- Osk says financing alternatives include more equity issuance, more bonds while debt remains affordable, and ecosystem deals among companies that buy from one another.

## Key Quotes
> "AI bond binge" - the episode's shorthand for the surge in technology-company debt issuance.

> "insatiable" - Osk's description of demand for AI tokens and compute.

> "cheaper or better for humans" - Osk's description of the ROI question companies are beginning to revisit.
