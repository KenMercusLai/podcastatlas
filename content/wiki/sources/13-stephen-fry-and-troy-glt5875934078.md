# 13. Stephen Fry and Troy

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] interview has [[StephenFry]] discuss his retelling of the Trojan War with [[TomHolland]] and [[DominicSandbrook]]. Fry treats Troy as both a culmination of Greek myth and a borderland between myth and history, where [[Homer]], archaeology, variant traditions, and modern narrative judgment cannot be collapsed into one account.

Its strongest synthesis is [[MythicDoubleDetermination]]: an event can remain simultaneously a human action and a divine intervention, allowing mythic, psychological, and moral explanations to coexist. The conversation also connects the enduring force of [[TheIliad]], [[Achilles]], [[Hector]], [[Priam]], [[HelenOfTroy]], [[Odysseus]], and [[Athena]] to ambiguity, grief, honor, cunning, retelling, and children's willingness to encounter difficult old stories.

## Key Claims

- Fry presents the Trojan War as a closing movement in the Greek mythic cycle, leading from divine origins toward human war and the difficult homecomings that follow it.
- The [[TheIliad|Iliad]] remains powerful because hand-to-hand violence is interrupted by restrained human moments, especially Achilles grieving [[Patroclus]] and Priam seeking Hector's body.
- [[HelenOfTroy]] resists a single judgment: willing departure, abduction, divine influence, seduction, shame, and divided loyalty remain live possibilities across the tradition.
- Troy “leaks into historicity,” but [[HeinrichSchliemann]]'s discoveries, self-promotion, destructive excavation, and premature identifications demonstrate why material remains do not authenticate the epic plot.
- [[MythicDoubleDetermination]] allows Paris's arrow to be both his shot and Apollo's guidance, or Athena's intervention to be both divine action and a representation of Achilles mastering rage.
- The war's decisive force is not only martial excellence but [[Odysseus]]' deception, storytelling, and adaptive cunning, making victory morally ambiguous as well as distinctly human.
- Greek myth remains available to children and modern readers because extreme violence, grief, wonder, and symbolic explanation do not require forced contemporary slang to remain intelligible.
- Influential literary tellers such as Homer and Hesiod give oral traditions authoritative shapes, but repeated retelling keeps those shapes open to later judgment and reinvention.

## Key Quotes

> "double determination" - Fry's phrase for an event that can be understood at once through human agency and divine intervention.

> "rewilding history" - Holland's phrase for restoring divine actors to accounts of how ancient people understood events.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. Fry's developmental account of Greek myth, contrast with Egyptian stability, claims about Greek belief in progress, reconstruction of early mythmaking, and judgments of individual characters are broad interpretive proposals rather than established historical conclusions.
- The episode complements [[HomericMortalityReading]] by emphasizing small scenes of grief and family contact, while adding [[MythicDoubleDetermination]] as a separate account of how divine and human causation coexist.
