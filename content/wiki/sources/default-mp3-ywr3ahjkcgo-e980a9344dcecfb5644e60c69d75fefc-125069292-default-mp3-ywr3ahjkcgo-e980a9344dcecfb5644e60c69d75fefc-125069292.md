## Summary
This [[TomorrowToday]] interview has [[ShekharNatarajan]] question [[JonathanBrill]] about invention, organizational resilience, AI, logistics, and five-year forecasting. Brill's unifying method is to test ambition against physical capacity, economics, timing, channels, incentives, and operating knowledge. He expects AI to distribute analytical capability through flatter organizations, but argues that people retain responsibility for framing, exceptions, relationships, trust, and accountability.

## Key Claims
- [[PhysicalConstraintForecasting]] estimates what can scale by tracing energy, compute, network, infrastructure, procurement, cost, and deployment constraints rather than predicting the exact next invention.
- [[WholeSystemInnovationViability]] requires desirability to align with economics, manufacturability, timing, lifespan, infrastructure, distribution, and an intelligible customer story.
- Customer observation and design thinking can improve features, but they do not by themselves establish that a new product or platform can survive manufacturing, channel, and business-model requirements.
- [[ShockReadyStrategicResilience]] treats external disruption as recurring and asks firms to build capabilities that can turn shocks into options rather than timing transformation for calm conditions.
- Internal incentives and shareholder expectations can make transformation structurally irrational even when technology and leadership imagination are available.
- [[AIDistributedOrganizationalIntelligence]] may give frontline workers more context, let individuals supervise many agents, translate operational signals across levels, and flatten some managerial layers.
- AI does not remove corporate accountability: companies still need explainability, auditability, dependable performance, and people who handle edge cases and protect accumulated brand trust.
- Brill expects demographic pressure, labor shortages, geopolitics, energy, and physical supply chains to slow or redirect technology adoption through 2030.

## Key Quotes
> "follow the physics" - Brill's shorthand for grounding forecasts in measurable physical and economic constraints.

> "people, not computers" - his proposed durable source of differentiation after AI becomes broadly available.
