# 338: Ireland: Home Rule, Mutiny - and Civil War? (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode with [[DanJackson]] presents the 1912-1914 [[IrishHomeRule|Home Rule]] crisis as a United Kingdom-wide constitutional emergency rather than only an Irish prelude to 1916. The Parliament Act made a third bill achievable, but [[EdwardCarson]], [[BonarLaw]], Conservatives, and mass Ulster unionism answered parliamentary change with a covenant, volunteer forces, gun-running, electoral pressure, and support for possible military disobedience.

Its central synthesis is that statute and consent had come apart. [[JohnRedmond]] and [[HHAsquith|H. H. Asquith]] could move Home Rule through Parliament, yet rival volunteer forces, the Curragh incident, contested exclusion of Ulster, and the inability to compel or reconcile unionists left the settlement near violent breakdown until the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]] suspended implementation and changed the political sequence.

## Key Claims

- Liberal dependence on the Irish Parliamentary Party after the 1910 elections and the Parliament Act of 1911 made a third Home Rule bill legislatively viable.
- Ulster Protestant resistance joined fear of Catholic-majority rule to British union, empire, industrial identity, anti-Catholicism, and Scottish covenanting memory.
- [[EdwardCarson]] turned opposition into mass politics through the Ulster Covenant, public performance, and the original [[UlsterVolunteerForce|Ulster Volunteer Force]].
- [[BonarLaw]] and [[FESmith|F. E. Smith]] made resistance a Conservative cause even when it challenged parliamentary authority and encouraged possible army disobedience.
- Larne gun-running and the Curragh incident exposed the government's difficulty in coercing Ulster and the uncertain political neutrality of the army officer corps.
- [[IrishVolunteers|Irish Volunteer]] organization and Howth gun-running showed that unionist militarization had generated a nationalist armed answer, while unequal state responses deepened grievance.
- By July 1914, partition or temporary exclusion remained unresolved; the European war suspended Home Rule and prevented the crisis from reaching its unknown domestic outcome.
