# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》341-2｜到底多优秀，让张骞成为古代带货第一人

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-1-yongchui-qingshi-zhangqian-ruhe-zaokong-xiyu-lrjtsfu1auxhbgbawt-e5dm_fbgo|Hanji 341-1]] by shifting from [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]]'s mission design to its claimed afterlife. It describes Han-Western Regions contact becoming recurrent, places southern and northern routes inside a field previously dominated by [[Xiongnu|匈奴]], and joins envoy travel to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s Jiuquan-Wuwei frontier consolidation.

Its durable contribution is a corridor-system synthesis: the [[AncientSilkRoad|ancient Silk Road]] is presented not as one trip or one commodity but as repeated missions, accumulated route knowledge, frontier administration, long travel times, gifts, military demand, and movement of goods and techniques. The title's “古代带货第一人” is a modern metaphor for Zhang Qian's catalytic role, not evidence that he personally carried every listed product or single-handedly created later Eurasian exchange.

## Key Claims

- After Zhang Qian's missions, Han envoys reportedly travel west five or six to more than ten times per year in parties ranging from tens to hundreds, while delegations from western polities also reach Chang'an.
- The episode divides travel beyond Yumen and Yangguan into a southern route through Shanshan toward Yuezhi and Parthia and a northern route through Cheshi toward Dayuan, Kangju, and Yancai.
- Xiongnu's earlier position is described through attack, taxation, plunder, and resident supervision around Yanqi, Weixu, and Yuli, making Han entry into the region part of strategic competition as well as exchange.
- After Wusun declines eastward relocation, Han establishes Jiuquan in former Hunye territory, moves settlers there, and later separates Wuwei to obstruct Xiongnu-Qiang contact.
- The source calls Zhang Qian's two missions “凿空之举” and credits the second with establishing a practical Han-Central Asian route that later supports political, commercial, technical, and material exchange.
- Silk, metal tools, and ironworking are said to move west, while glass, precious metals, pomegranates, horses, and coriander move toward Han; Wudi's demand for Dayuan “heavenly horses” shows the military-political side of exchange.
- Distant round trips reportedly take seven or eight years and nearer ones several years; as route knowledge grows, missions reduce the gifts they carry, suggesting learning and cost adjustment rather than frictionless trade.

## Key Quotes

> “凿空之举” - the episode's name for Zhang Qian's route-opening missions.

> “古代带货第一人” - the title's modern shorthand for a catalytic role in cross-regional movement of goods and techniques.

## Connections

- [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]] - envoy whose route knowledge and delegated contacts are presented as the transition from exploratory missions to recurring exchange.
- [[AncientSilkRoad|丝绸之路]] - corridor system built from geography, diplomacy, frontier capacity, repeated travel, and material circulation.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - competing powers whose military and administrative strategies shape the route environment.
- [[Wusun|乌孙]] - polity whose refusal to relocate east is followed by Han territorial consolidation in the Hexi corridor.
- [[DayuanWesternHan|大宛]], [[Kangju|康居]], [[Yuezhi|月氏]], [[CheshiKingdomWesternHan|车师]], and [[ParthianEmpire|安息]] - route destinations and intermediaries named by the source.
- [[EnvoyGovernanceCredibilityCollapse|使团治理失控与外交信用崩塌]] - later episode evidence qualifies the present account of growing mission frequency by showing how expansion can outrun screening, supply, and trust.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. This episode extends Hanji 341-1's mission-opening account into recurring contact and material exchange, while Hanji 357-1 later qualifies that expansion with envoy-governance failure.
- The episode's “彻底打通” and “古代带货第一人” formulations are rhetorical. The wiki treats Zhang Qian as a catalyst within a corridor system, not the sole creator or merchant carrier of the ancient Silk Road.
- The claims that tea, porcelain, and hairpins reached Europe through this opening collapse products and periods into one short list and are not promoted as Han-era facts without chronological evidence.
- “西域三十六国,” route dimensions, annual mission frequency, party sizes, travel durations, gift reductions, commandery chronology and purposes, Xiongnu administrative details, and the direction and timing of every listed commodity or technology remain episode-attributed pending primary-source and archaeological comparison.
- The episode closes with Hangu Pass's eastward relocation and a teaser about a dangerous enfeoffment title; because it does not identify or develop the title, no new canonical page or conclusion is created from that teaser.
