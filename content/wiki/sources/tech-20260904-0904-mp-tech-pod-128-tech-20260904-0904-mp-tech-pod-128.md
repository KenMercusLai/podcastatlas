## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] weekend review has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty Carino]] interview [[JoannaStern]] about [[Apple]]'s CEO transition, [[Google]] antitrust remedies, [[NewYorkCityPublicSchools|New York City public schools]] restricting AI through middle school, and real-world tests of consumer shopping agents. The episode's through line is that AI pressure now reaches product launches, antitrust analysis, classroom rules, child-safety policy, and ordinary shopping workflows.

The source extends the wiki's Apple succession branch by moving [[JohnTernus|John Ternus]] from named successor into a source-described CEO taking over before a major iPhone launch. It also adds focused concepts around [[AICompetitionAntitrustRemedy]], [[SchoolAIBoundaries]], and [[ConsumerAIShoppingAgentBenchmark]], while sharpening [[AIGuidedLearningGuardrails]] with a school-system policy case.

## Key Claims
- [[JoannaStern]] contrasts [[SteveJobs]] as a product-focused founder with [[TimCook]] as an operations-focused leader whose legacy was building Apple into a global technology superpower.
- The episode says [[JohnTernus|John Ternus]] takes over [[Apple]] before a major [[IPhone|iPhone]] launch, with expected foldable-phone entry, AI-linked component cost pressure, and higher prices.
- Stern says [[Samsung]], [[Huawei]], and [[Google]] already have foldable phones, so Apple's late entry will have to justify timing, price, and product value.
- Stern frames Apple's AI moment as improving because a delayed new [[Siri]] is expected with iOS 27, connected device integration, and a stronger privacy-centered assistant story.
- A federal judge is described as finding [[Google]] had a digital-advertising monopoly while still not requiring a sale of its ad tech business.
- The episode compares the ad-tech outcome with the Google search case, where a possible Chrome spin-off also did not happen.
- Stern argues that antitrust attempts to break up large technology companies have had limited success, and that fines or smaller remedies may not meaningfully alter trillion-dollar platform power.
- The search-case discussion says a judge cited AI competition as one reason for a more conservative remedy, while Stern notes that [[ChatGPT]] rose quickly without yet matching Google's advertising scale.
- [[NewYorkCityPublicSchools|New York City public schools]] announced restrictions limiting AI and some other technologies through middle school.
- The policy is described as prohibiting AI at certain levels, restricting individual screens for younger students, barring teachers from using AI to grade assignments, and banning companionship chatbots.
- Stern sees the companion-chatbot ban as especially important because emotional AI companions raise different risks than ordinary homework help.
- The discussion treats school policy as a boundary against untested AI education technology, including AI embedded into classroom tools and devices.
- The episode preserves criticism of school AI bans: students still need AI literacy, and the policy's method for teaching responsible AI use remains unclear.
- Stern repeats a back-to-school shopping-agent benchmark using a fourth-grade supply-list PDF, a sub-$100 budget, a shipping deadline, and the same task across [[Gemini|Google Gemini Spark]], [[Claude|Claude Cowork]], and [[ChatGPT]].
- Stern says [[Claude|Claude Cowork]] performed best because it asked clarifying questions and completed the shopping task; [[ChatGPT]] was close but overconfident and mistake-prone, while [[Gemini|Gemini Spark]] missed items and required repeated confirmations.
- The shopping-agent benchmark improved from about 30 minutes in the previous year to about 15 minutes, but reliability and follow-through still varied by product.
