# 005 串台|《进击的巨人》又双叕完结了？到底烂尾没？

## Summary

This crossover between [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] and 千梦群岛 evaluates the ending of [[AttackOnTitan|《进击的巨人》]] by separating pacing, rule coherence, character motivation, and thematic closure. It argues that the late story is compressed and that Ymir's love-based explanation cannot carry the earlier inquiry into freedom, slavery, war, and humanity, while maintaining that [[ErenYeager|艾伦·耶格尔]]'s catastrophic choice grows from traits visible from the beginning.

The discussion then uses the Rumbling, the Eldian sterilization proposal, and the Scouts' refusal of global destruction to examine [[ExistentialSelfDefenseEthics|existential self-defense]], [[InheritedHistoricalGuilt|inherited historical guilt]], deterrence, and irresolvable political choice. [[ReinerBraun|莱纳·布朗]] provides the most developed counterpoint: indoctrination and national duty explain his participation in violence, but recognizing the enemy as human begins accountability rather than erasing guilt.

## Key Claims

- The ending's principal weakness is compressed execution: characters, powers, explanations, and reversals accumulate faster than the narrative can develop them.
- Eren's Rumbling is morally indefensible but psychologically continuous with his early impulse to destroy whoever he identifies as an enemy.
- The anime's added acknowledgment that Eren wanted destruction makes him more than a passive executor of ethnic self-defense and preserves personal responsibility.
- Sterilization, global destruction, and morally constrained resistance expose different answers to an existential conflict without producing a clean permanent solution.
- Ending Titan power does not end human war; fear, power, memory, and enemy construction can reproduce conflict through new instruments.
- Descendants should not inherit personal guilt for ancestral violence, but refusing inherited guilt does not justify denial or forgetting.
- Reiner's divided identity shows how indoctrination can collapse when sustained contact makes the designated enemy recognizably human.
