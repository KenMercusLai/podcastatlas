# Senators John Fetterman and Dave McCormick: Bipartisanship, Money in DC, Datacenters, Graham Platner

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] episode uses [[JohnFetterman|John Fetterman]] and [[DaveMcCormick|Dave McCormick]], [[Pennsylvania]]'s Democratic and Republican senators, as a case study in [[BipartisanSwingStateRepresentation]] during intense polarization. The discussion moves from shutdown votes, the filibuster, party primaries, Israel, and campaign spending into [[PennsylvaniaAIInfrastructureBoom]], where AI data centers, energy, manufacturing, and skilled trades become both a local economic-development claim and a U.S.-China competition claim.

The source's strongest synthesis is that AI infrastructure politics is no longer only a utility-rate or siting debate. In this episode, [[DataCenterBacklash]], [[HyperscaleDataCenterMoratorium]], [[DataCenterCommunityConsent]], [[DataCenterPowerBottleneck]], [[CraftLaborBottleneck]], and [[GoodJobsForNonCollegeWorkers]] are tied to Senate coalition management, blue-collar work, China competition, and whether voters trust government to solve hard problems without reflexive tribalism.

## Key Claims
- [[JohnFetterman|Fetterman]] and [[DaveMcCormick|McCormick]] both say they voted to keep the government open and frame shutdown avoidance as a basic duty to [[Pennsylvania]] and the [[UnitedStates|United States]].
- The senators present their relationship as a practical response to a divided swing state: they can vote differently while still maintaining trust, personal friendship, and issue-specific cooperation.
- Fetterman says he was wrong to support eliminating the filibuster in 2022 and now defends it as a minority-rights and cross-party-work mechanism, adding a concrete reversal to [[FilibusterMinorityRights]].
- McCormick argues that Pennsylvania's AI, energy, manufacturing, and life-sciences position can create blue-collar upside if policy supports power, permitting, and workforce needs.
- Both senators reject a data-center moratorium as strategically harmful in the AI race with [[China]], while still acknowledging that communities need clearer commitments on energy, water, tax base, schools, infrastructure, and local benefits.
- The data-center discussion links [[DataCenterBacklash]] to both legitimate community concern and alleged misinformation or political mobilization; those external-influence claims remain source-scoped.
- The episode frames [[PennsylvaniaAIInfrastructureBoom]] as a skilled-trades and manufacturing story: McCormick cites a $92 billion investment-commitment figure and says electricians and welders can earn high wages at energy, data-center, and industrial sites.
- Fetterman criticizes parts of the [[DemocraticParty|Democratic Party]] for reflexive anti-Trump positioning, hostility to Israel, flirtation with socialism or communism, and willingness to support candidates he sees as extremist.
- [[GrahamPlatner|Graham Platner]] becomes the episode's candidate-quality case: the senators and hosts treat alleged Nazi-tattoo, anti-Army, and antisemitism-related controversies as evidence that backlash politics can keep disqualifying candidates viable.
- The campaign-finance segment treats Senate races as reputation-destruction markets: McCormick says his race cost roughly $500 million total, while Fetterman says his 2022 race cost $330 million and expects future spending to rise.

## Key Quotes
> "country over party" - the episode's bipartisan shorthand.

> "China first" - Fetterman's label for a data-center moratorium.

> "$500 million" - McCormick's approximate total-spending figure for his Senate race.
