## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode combines short business updates on [[Pinduoduo]], [[Walmart]], [[SHEIN]], and European football-club ownership with a main explainer on why global beauty and personal-care companies are turning back to hair care. Its durable wiki contribution is the claim that hair care is no longer only a stable shampoo-and-conditioner category: growth is moving toward anti-hair-loss products, scalp care, hair oils, ingredient-led routines, fragrance and emotional benefits, and professional salon trust. The episode connects [[LOreal|L'Oreal / 欧莱雅]], [[Kerastase|Kérastase / 卡诗]], [[Vichy|薇姿]], [[CeraVe|适乐肤]], [[Moroccanoil]], and [[Unilever]] to [[HaircareSkincareization]], [[PremiumHaircareMarket]], [[SalonChannelTrust]], and [[BeautyProblemNaming]].

## Key Claims
- [[Pinduoduo]] launched a "fastest tomorrow" entrance on its app home page, backed by shared warehouses and more than 150 node locations, making the side item a concrete addition to [[EcommerceFulfillmentComplexity]] and [[InstantRetail]].
- [[Walmart]]'s Shenzhen community-store expansion shows a small-format route for getting closer to high-frequency daily demand, but the source frames that route as dependent on localized supply-chain and store-operating efficiency.
- [[SHEIN]] reportedly reduced leased warehouse area near Ho Chi Minh City by roughly 60% after trying to build Vietnam into an export base, showing that tariff or de minimis pressure does not automatically make supply-chain relocation efficient.
- The episode says U.S. investors remain interested in European football-club equity because top American sports teams are expensive and rarely sold, while European clubs may accept partial capital injections without full control transfer.
- The source frames [[LOreal|L'Oreal / 欧莱雅]]'s first-half hair-care growth as a category signal: its L'Oreal, [[Kerastase|Kérastase / 卡诗]], [[Vichy|薇姿]], and [[CeraVe|适乐肤]] hair-related businesses all grew by double digits in the episode's account.
- The source cites Euromonitor's forecast that the global shampoo and hair-care market will be 24% larger by 2030, outpacing categories such as color cosmetics and fragrance in the episode's comparison.
- Mass shampoo remains a high-volume staple, but the episode argues that the real growth space is in high-end and specific-needs hair care, where [[Kerastase|Kérastase / 卡诗]] and other professional foreign brands led the cited 618 rankings while [[BeeAndFlower|蜂花]] and [[HeadAndShoulders|海飞丝]] faced pressure.
- Anti-hair-loss shampoo is described as the only shampoo category that can hold price because users fear stopping treatment; the source treats that fear as pricing power, not proof of efficacy.
- Hair oil is described as the fastest-growing online hair-care subcategory in the first four months of the cited data, with selling points that combine anti-loss, nourishment, scent, and decompression.
- The episode links hair-care demand to stress-related shedding after the pandemic and to [[GLP1Agonists|GLP-1]] weight-loss-drug popularity, while keeping those claims as demand explanations rather than clinical advice.
- [[HaircareSkincareization]] is the episode's main category frame: ingredients such as niacinamide, probiotics, and amino acids, plus scalp microbiome, UV damage, hard-water deposits, and multi-step routines move hair care closer to skincare.
- [[Xiaohongshu]] and other social-media surfaces amplify demand by naming ordinary appearance problems, such as "high skull crown makes the face look smaller," and turning them into product-search terms for oil-control and volumizing products.
- [[SalonChannelTrust]] matters because professional stylists can make a high-end hair-care brand feel credible, while salon channels are fragmented and operationally harder than ordinary ecommerce or mass retail.
- The episode argues that hair care may polarize: high-end professional and mass-value products can both perform, while mid-priced brands without technical trust, salon endorsement, or channel efficiency face pressure.
