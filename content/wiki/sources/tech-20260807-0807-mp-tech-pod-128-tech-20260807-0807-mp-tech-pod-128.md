## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] Bytes episode has [[JoannaStern]] discuss three stories: [[SpaceX]]'s first public earnings report, gig workers filming chores for humanoid-robot training data, and a possible [[MySpace]] relaunch. The strongest contribution is the robotics segment, which shows that household robots need physical-world data and that some of that data is being gathered through low-status task work by people who may be helping train future automation.

The episode also extends the wiki's space and social-platform branches. SpaceX's new financial transparency links [[Starlink]] strength, AI infrastructure spending, lunar manufacturing talk, and [[OrbitalDebrisGovernance]], while MySpace nostalgia points to frustration with algorithmic feeds, bots, influencers, and centralized platform control.

## Key Claims
- SpaceX reported more than $18 billion in capital expenditures in the second quarter, with most of the increase described as AI infrastructure spending.
- [[Starlink]] is presented as a strong business line, especially through partnerships including airlines.
- SpaceX's discussion of manufacturing on the [[Moon]] sits beside concerns about human-made objects accumulating in space and a discarded rocket collision with the Moon.
- Robotics companies need large amounts of physical-task footage before humanoid or household robots can reliably perform ordinary chores.
- Some startups are paying gig workers to wear cameras and film tasks such as laundry, dishwashing, cleaning, mechanical work, plumbing, and other embodied labor.
- Useful footage is defined by model-training requirements: visible hands and action traces matter more than whether the chore was performed well by human standards.
- The training pipeline is described as anonymizing videos, blurring personally identifying details, analyzing hand movements, and converting those movements into robot-training code.
- The segment frames a labor tension: workers can be paid for training data while also helping create systems that might eventually compete with their work.
- Stern is skeptical that useful household robots are near-term because data, safety, and dexterous robotic hands remain hard problems.
- MySpace relaunch buzz is treated less as proof of a viable comeback than as a signal that users miss chronological, friend-centered, less algorithmic social media.
- [[Bluesky]] is used as a comparison point for more user-controlled or decentralized social-network design, while [[Facebook]] is contrasted as the platform that built a stronger early real-world social graph and kept adapting.

## Key Quotes
> "dystopian" - Stern's description of workers filming labor that could help train replacement-capable robots.

> "long way off" - Stern's near-term assessment of capable household chore robots.

> "chronological feeds" - the social-media experience MySpace nostalgia is said to evoke.
