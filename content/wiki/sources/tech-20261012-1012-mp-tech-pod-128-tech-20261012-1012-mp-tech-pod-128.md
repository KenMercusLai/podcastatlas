## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode interviews researcher [[JackManning]] about “generative ghosts,” AI systems intended to recreate interaction with someone who has died. His study compared a third-person representation that acted like a family archivist with a first-person reincarnation that spoke as the deceased, finding that most participants described the supervised experience as positive, informative, or cathartic.

The episode's central contribution is that experiential authenticity did not track factual accuracy alone. Participants often tolerated invented biographical details to sustain a desired conversation, while mismatched message length, emoji use, and communication style more readily broke the illusion. Manning therefore argues that [[GenerativeGhostInteractionDesign]] should follow what users actually value, while substantially stronger safeguards are needed before commercial systems expose grieving or otherwise vulnerable users directly to uncontrolled model output.

## Key Claims
- The study generated conversations from a lightweight profile containing biographical details, hobbies, preferred activities, and one shared memory, so unanswered questions required the model to infer or invent details.
- The representation condition answered questions about the deceased in the third person, while the reincarnation condition spoke as though it were the deceased person.
- Most participants described the supervised conversations as positive, informative, or cathartic, and some wanted to continue them.
- Participants raised dependence concerns but often projected the risk onto relatives while describing their own use as beneficial.
- Minor factual errors rarely ended the experience because many participants willingly suspended disbelief to continue the conversation they wanted.
- Relational style mattered more visibly: message length, emoji use, reactions, and the difference between texting and email habits affected perceived authenticity.
- Potential uses extend beyond recent bereavement to family traditions, intergenerational storytelling, recipes, and other family knowledge.
- Participants communicated through a researcher during a video call rather than directly with the language model, allowing inappropriate output to be screened.
- Commercial deployment adds manipulation, dependency, and profit-incentive risks that the supervised study did not test.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction found. The findings qualify a factuality-first account of AI safety: hallucinations remain a risk, but participants' sense of authenticity was often more sensitive to relational style than to minor invented details.
- The positive participant reports do not establish long-term safety because the study was brief, used limited personal data, and placed a researcher between each participant and the model.
