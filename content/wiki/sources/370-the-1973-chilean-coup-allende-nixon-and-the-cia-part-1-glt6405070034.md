# 370. The 1973 Chilean Coup: Allende, Nixon and the CIA (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces [[SalvadorAllende|Salvador Allende]] from his medical and parliamentary formation through his 1970 election, socialist reform program, and the worsening crisis before Chile's September 1973 coup. It presents documented United States interference—campaign funding, propaganda, economic pressure, opposition support, and encouragement of military action—without reducing [[Chile]]'s political breakdown to foreign control.

The episode also treats Allende's project as internally consequential. Nationalization, redistribution, health, education, food, wage, and cultural programs produced material benefit and dignity for supporters, while inflation, falling copper prices, shortages, institutional conflict, and fear of radicalization deepened opposition and helped close off constitutional compromise.

## Key Claims

- Allende was a long-serving parliamentary Marxist who pursued power through elections and presented socialist transformation as compatible with constitutional government.
- The United States intervened before both the 1964 and 1970 elections through funding, propaganda, diplomatic pressure, and contacts intended to prevent an Allende presidency.
- After Allende narrowly led the 1970 vote, the Nixon administration explored both congressional obstruction and military intervention; the failed kidnapping of constitutionalist commander [[ReneSchneider|René Schneider]] ended in his death and strengthened ratification of Allende.
- Allende's early government joined copper and bank nationalization to land redistribution, health, education, food, wage, social-security, and cultural programs.
- Falling copper prices, expansionary spending, inflation, shortages, and pressure on trade and finance combined rather than supplying a single sufficient explanation for the economic crisis.
- U.S. loan restrictions, investor pressure, supply disruption, and CIA support for opposition media and organizations deliberately worsened a crisis that also had domestic and global causes.
- The March 1973 congressional election denied the opposition the supermajority needed to remove Allende and made military overthrow appear to some opponents to be the remaining route.

## Key Quotes

> "Make the economy scream" - Richard Helms's note of the Nixon administration's instruction as presented by the episode.

> "Here lies the next president of Chile" - Allende's joke about his repeated electoral defeats before 1970.

## Connections

- [[SalvadorAllende]], [[RichardNixon]], and [[CIA]] - elected socialist president and the U.S. political-intelligence effort to prevent or reverse his accession.
- [[ReneSchneider]] and [[ConstitutionalistMilitaryNorm]] - commander and non-intervention principle that blocked an early military route against the election result.
- [[Chile]] and [[ChileanCoup1973]] - national setting and democratic rupture whose preconditions the episode reconstructs.
- [[ColdWarRegimeChangePressure]] - combined propaganda, opposition funding, economic coercion, and coup encouragement directed against Allende.
- [[FidelCastro]] - visitor whose extended 1971 tour intensified opposition fears that Chile could follow Cuba's path.
