# Rodents and Red Wine with Maria Konnikova

## Summary

[[MariaKonnikova]] begins her ombudsman role on [[RevisionistHistory]] by challenging [[MalcolmGladwell]] on iodine and fluoride policy, nutrition evidence, and the [[MinnesotaStarvationExperiment]]. Their central dispute becomes [[UnknownRiskInformedConsent]]: Gladwell argues that altruistic volunteers may rationally accept serious harm for large public benefit, while Konnikova emphasizes uncertainty, patriotic pressure, nonconsenting fetuses, and the abuses that explain modern safeguards.

The [[DutchHungerWinter]] supplies a contrasting natural experiment with lifelong and possibly intergenerational findings, while [[EvidencePopulationRepresentativeness]] limits how far results from white adult men can travel to women or other populations. The episode ends with [[OptInLongitudinalHealthObservation]], a proposal to gather continuous sensor data from millions of volunteers without deliberately imposing harm, but privacy, governance, security, and misuse remain unresolved.

## Key Claims

- Public-health fortification can produce large benefits, but autonomy, evidence, authority, and public trust remain part of the intervention rather than afterthoughts.
- Observational associations between moderate wine drinking and longevity cannot isolate alcohol from lifestyle, while rodent resveratrol doses do not translate directly into a practical human benefit.
- The [[MinnesotaStarvationExperiment]] had an urgent humanitarian purpose, yet unknown long-term harm and wartime pressure complicate the meaning of voluntary consent.
- [[UnknownRiskInformedConsent]] distinguishes disclosure of known risks from the harder problem of authorizing exposure when researchers cannot specify all consequences.
- The [[DutchHungerWinter]] produced valuable longitudinal evidence without deliberate famine exposure, but fetal famine and adult experimental restriction are not interchangeable.
- [[EvidencePopulationRepresentativeness]] requires researchers to ask whether sex, age, race, pregnancy, and other biological or social differences limit generalization from a narrow sample.
- [[OptInLongitudinalHealthObservation]] could widen causal discovery through scale and duration, but voluntariness does not by itself resolve privacy, security, access, or downstream-use risk.
