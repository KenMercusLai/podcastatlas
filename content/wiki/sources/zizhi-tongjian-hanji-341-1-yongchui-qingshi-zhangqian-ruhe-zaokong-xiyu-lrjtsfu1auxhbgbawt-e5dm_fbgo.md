# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》341-1｜永垂青史，张骞如何“凿空”西域？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]]'s later mission to [[Wusun|乌孙]] as the operational bridge between [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s anti-[[Xiongnu|匈奴]] strategy and wider Western Regions contact. Zhang proposes drawing Wusun eastward with gifts, alliance, and a marriage offer; he then leads a large mission whose credentialed deputy envoys branch toward [[DayuanWesternHan|大宛]], [[Kangju|康居]], [[Yuezhi|月氏]], and [[DaxiaWesternHan|大夏]].

The durable synthesis is that “凿空” is not treated as trade appearing by itself. Military pressure opens a corridor, gifts and ritual stage Han status, Wusun independently tests Han capacity before committing, and delegated envoys turn one bilateral approach into a wider diplomatic network that later supports the [[AncientSilkRoad|ancient Silk Road]].

## Key Claims

- Zhang Qian links contact with Wusun to the larger goal of weakening Xiongnu's western alliance position, described as “断匈奴右臂.”
- He proposes moving Wusun toward the former territory of the surrendered Hunye king, binding it to Han through gifts, brother-state language, and a possible imperial marriage.
- Han Wudi appoints Zhang Qian zhonglangjiang and sends a roughly three-hundred-person mission with horses, large livestock herds, gold, silk, and multiple credentialed deputy envoys.
- The deputies make the mission divisible: at route branches they can carry gifts and imperial credentials toward Dayuan, Kangju, Yuezhi, Daxia, and other polities.
- Zhang Qian refuses to let the Wusun ruler receive Han gifts under the same protocol used for Xiongnu envoys; the ruler accepts the gifts after performing the demanded rite.
- The Wusun ruler does not immediately accept relocation, marriage, or an anti-Xiongnu break, but sends observers back with Zhang Qian to inspect Han's actual strength.
- Zhang Qian returns to Chang'an and becomes 大行; more than a year later, deputy envoys return with representatives from several western polities.

## Key Quotes

> “断匈奴右臂” - the episode's strategic shorthand for separating Xiongnu from western partners.

> “凿空之举” - the historical frame through which the episode connects Zhang Qian's missions to later Silk Road exchange.

## Connections

- [[ZhangQianWesternHan|张骞]] - envoy who combines alliance design, protocol bargaining, mission logistics, and delegated outreach.
- [[Wusun|乌孙]] - strategically valuable polity that accepts contact while delaying alignment until it can assess Han strength.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - ruler and rival that give the mission its anti-Xiongnu strategic purpose.
- [[DayuanWesternHan|大宛]], [[Kangju|康居]], [[Yuezhi|月氏]], and [[DaxiaWesternHan|大夏]] - destinations in the deputy-envoy network.
- [[AncientSilkRoad|丝绸之路]] - later route system whose diplomatic and information foundations the episode associates with Zhang Qian's western missions.
- [[CapacityBackedDiplomaticHierarchy|实力支撑的外交名分]] - the Wusun audience links ritual demands and rich gifts to an independently tested judgment of Han capacity.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode supplies an earlier mission-design and cautious-contact layer before Hanji 370-1 and 370-2's completed Wusun marriage alliance and Hanji 357-1 and 357-2's later envoy-governance breakdown.
- “昆莫” is treated here as the Wusun ruler title used by the source, not promoted as a separate personal name. The episode does not independently settle the ruler's personal identity or the exact relationship between this audience and the later Liejiaomi marriage sequence.
- The troop, animal, gift, and monetary totals; exact route; dialogue; ritual sequence; deputy destinations; return timing; office equivalence; and private motives remain episode-attributed.
- The source identifies 大夏 as the Greco-Bactrian kingdom. Because the episode supplies no chronological or specialist evidence for that equation, [[DaxiaWesternHan|大夏]] preserves it as a source-scoped identification rather than a settled identity.
- The modern equality critique of requiring a foreign ruler to kneel is the host's evaluative frame. The wiki retains the event as evidence about contested hierarchy without treating either the demand or compliance as proof of equal sovereignty or durable submission.
