# 7-11 败走印度市场，为何牵手本土巨头仍难站稳脚跟？

## Summary

This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode opens with short updates on [[Starbucks]], [[YerenXiansheng|野人先生]], [[AppleIntelligence|Apple Intelligence]], and [[Binance]], then explains why [[SevenEleven|7-Eleven]] closed its Indian stores after five years. Its durable contribution is that a proven convenience-store format cannot import its economics through brand and a large partner alone: [[KiranaRetail|kirana stores]] already satisfy neighborhood convenience, [[InstantRetail]] satisfies delivery convenience, regional diets and price levels fragment assortment design, and too few stores prevent fresh-food, logistics, rent, and management costs from reaching [[ConvenienceStoreDensityEconomics|density economics]].

## Key Claims

- 7-Eleven entered [[India]] in 2021 through a franchise agreement with a large local retailer that already had more than 10,000 stores and more than 200 warehouses and distribution centers, but the chain reportedly peaked at only a little over 60 Indian stores before closing them all.
- India had an estimated 12-13 million kirana stores in 2021; their low overhead, flexible hours, neighborhood relationships, and growing use of mobile ordering and delivery make them a resilient local format rather than a purely pre-digital incumbent.
- The episode says more than 90% of Indian fast-moving consumer-goods sales still passed through traditional stores, while quick commerce had reached a reported fiscal-2025 market size of roughly $7-8 billion.
- 7-Eleven's fresh food, private label, and localized snacks did not establish enough differentiation between cheap neighborhood stores and delivery platforms.
- Regional differences in diet, religion, festivals, income, portion size, and food-separation rules increase procurement, production, forecasting, inventory, and merchandising complexity.
- A convenience chain needs frequent visits and fast stock turns, while dense stores let warehouses, vehicles, and management serve more units; roughly 60 stores did not create that network in India.
- High urban rent plus labor, inventory, logistics, and electricity costs created a loop in which insufficient density kept costs high and continuing losses constrained the expansion needed to lower them.
- [[LawsonRetail|Lawson]] reportedly plans to enter India in 2027 with five Mumbai stores, reach 100 by 2030, and target 10,000 by 2050; these are plans rather than evidence that the same constraints have been solved.
- Side items report Starbucks U.S. comparable-sales recovery and store closures, Yeren Xiansheng's first external financing, a limited China release path for Apple Intelligence, and a very large Binance theft; all remain episode-scoped current claims.

## Key Quotes

> "门店不足导致成本过高、持续亏损又限制进一步扩张" - the supplied summary's formulation of 7-Eleven's density trap.
