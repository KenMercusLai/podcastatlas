# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》272｜平津侯公孙弘，如何从猪倌逆袭成丞相（1）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode begins [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]]'s career before the rivalry and survival material in Hanji 273. It presents a poor former local clerk who loses office, tends pigs, begins sustained study in his forties, and enters [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s court through a 140 BCE call for worthy candidates. His ascent is therefore a concrete [[LateBloomingTalent|大器晚成式人才显现]] case in which preparation, an open recruitment channel, and ruler recognition all matter.

Gongsun Hong's policy response argues that heavy rewards and severe punishments cannot substitute for trustworthy conduct by those above. It joins ruler self-correction, ability-based appointment, restraint on useless speech and production, protection of food and labor, differentiated merit and punishment, and the Confucian virtues of benevolence, righteousness, ritual, and wisdom. The examining office reportedly ranks the response as ordinary, but Wudi selects it first and appoints Gongsun Hong as a boshi. The source thereby adds the positive entry point to a later morally ambivalent profile: ruler-fit and classical language first open access, while later episodes show how institutional skill can serve compromise, exclusion, or punishment.

## Key Claims

- The episode frames the Wudi-era chancellorship as unusually dangerous and uses Gongsun Hong's later natural death in office to pose a political-survival question that this first part does not yet answer.
- It presents Gongsun Hong as poor, dismissed from a minor local post, forced into pig tending, and only beginning sustained study of the *Chunqiu* and *Gongyang Zhuan* in his forties.
- A 140 BCE imperial call for learned people who understood current affairs gives the already elderly Gongsun Hong a channel back into public service.
- His response argues that political trust begins with upright conduct above; generous rewards and harsh punishments cannot reliably produce good conduct when rulers and officials lack credibility.
- The response joins moral rule to administrative principles: assign office by ability, reject useless proposals and objects, protect food and labor, advance virtue and merit, and apply reward and punishment according to conduct.
- The episode describes political, moral, and natural order as mutually responsive, culminating in a harmony-and-auspicious-sign vision that remains a classical cosmological claim rather than an empirically established mechanism.
- Although the reviewing office reportedly finds the response unremarkable, Wudi ranks it first, appoints Gongsun Hong as boshi, and continues to value memorials that combine classical language with practical bureaucratic knowledge.

## Key Quotes

> “早成者未必有成，晚成者未必不达。” - the episode's life-course frame for Gongsun Hong's delayed study and ascent.

> “上正者下信。” - the response's compressed claim that trust below depends on upright conduct above.

> “因能任官。” - the episode's formulation of ability-matched appointment.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source backfills the entry stage before Hanji 273 and is compatible with later episodes that make Gongsun Hong politically adaptive and morally ambivalent.
- Biographical details including the exact birth year and place, office title, dismissal cause, age at study, teacher relationship, twenty-one-year interval, and first-recruitment chronology remain source-scoped pending primary-text comparison.
- The episode does not establish that pig tending was continuous, that late study alone caused advancement, or that merit consistently overrode status in Wudi's court.
- The eight-part reconstruction, verbatim wording, examination process, appearance-based reaction, office details, and claim that repeated memorials made Gongsun Hong's career smooth remain episode-attributed.
- Harmony among ruler virtue, popular conduct, weather, harvests, and auspicious signs is preserved as the source's classical cosmological language, not adopted as a causal account of nature.
