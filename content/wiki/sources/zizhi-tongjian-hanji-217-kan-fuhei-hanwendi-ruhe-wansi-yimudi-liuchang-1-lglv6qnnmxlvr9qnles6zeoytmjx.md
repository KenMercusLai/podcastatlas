# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》217｜看腹黑汉文帝，如何玩死异母弟刘长（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode reconstructs [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]]'s final crisis under [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]. Liu Chang replaces centrally appointed senior officials, makes his own law, kills subjects, grants titles, reacts angrily to a warning letter sent through [[BoZhaoWesternHan|薄昭]], and joins a rebellion plan involving domestic associates, [[Xiongnu|匈奴]], and the Minyue king. After ministers seek execution, Liu Heng commutes the sentence to loss of title and exile; Liu Chang then refuses food and dies in a sealed prison cart.

The episode's distinctive judgment is the host's [[IndulgenceAsPoliticalElimination|纵容式政治清除]] interpretation: repeated imperial leniency allegedly lets Liu Chang cross ever more serious boundaries until he can be removed as a succession threat. The narrated sequence supports foreseeable risk and failed restraint, especially because [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] warns that exile may kill Liu Chang, but it does not independently prove that Liu Heng designed the entire escalation or intended the death.

## Key Claims

- Liu Chang reportedly makes law in Huainan, expels court-appointed officials, asks to appoint senior officers himself, kills subjects, and grants titles as high as 关内侯.
- Liu Heng grants the requested appointment power despite the stated rule that officials of at least two thousand shi should be centrally assigned.
- After Liu Chang answers criticism with an angry offer to surrender his title, Liu Heng has Bo Zhao send a letter recalling prior favors and warning him through the example of another punished prince.
- Liu Chang then joins more than seventy associates and Chai Qi in a rebellion plan, seeks Xiongnu and Minyue support, and prepares a forty-cart attack at Gukou before disclosure leads to arrest.
- Zhang Cang and other senior officials seek execution, but Liu Heng abolishes Liu Chang's title and exiles him toward Yandao in Shu rather than approving death.
- Yuan Ang says the proud prince may die after sudden humiliation and that the emperor will bear the name of killing his brother; Liu Heng publicly answers that the punishment is temporary and return will follow reform.
- Liu Chang is transported in a sealed prison cart, refuses food, and is discovered dead at Yong; Liu Heng then punishes local officials who did not open the cart, buries Liu Chang with marquis-level rites, and grants rank to his four sons.
- The host interprets the full sequence as deliberate “宠杀 / 捧杀,” but the source supplies no direct order or documentary proof that Liu Heng intended rebellion or death.

## Key Quotes

> “欲使其灭亡，必先使其疯狂。” - the host's summary of the proposed indulgence-to-elimination mechanism.

> “陛下素骄淮南王，弗为置严傅相。” - the remonstrance attributed to Yuan Ang, locating part of the crisis in earlier imperial indulgence and weak supervision.

## Contradictions

- Kinship error: the episode correctly calls Bo Zhao Liu Heng's maternal uncle but also calls him “窦太后的弟弟.” The existing bounded evidence identifies him as [[BaoJi|薄姬 / 薄太后]]'s brother, not Empress Dowager Dou's brother.
- Motive boundary: the host's “宠杀 / 捧杀” theory is an interpretation of leniency, escalation, and foreseeable transport risk. The source does not provide direct evidence that Liu Heng intended Liu Chang to rebel, starve, or die.
- Death boundary: this episode presents self-starvation in the sealed cart, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-220-minjian-geyao-fengci-wendi-jia-renyi-liuheng-fanying-guguai-1-liuajl1w1byd6m_hjy0obirtmlu8|Hanji 220]] also preserves Jia Yi's illness-after-commuted-exile defense. The wiki retains both political narratives rather than resolving medical causation.
- The laws, offices, killings, title grants, warning letter, conspiracy members, foreign contacts, cart count, route, sealed-cart procedure, officials' sentence, burial rank, exact speeches, and private intentions require comparison with transmitted histories.
- This source is the substantive Liu Chang installment. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-217-kan-fuhei-hanwendi-ruhe-wansi-yimudi-liuchang-2-ljmhwnxkjcegqfevo8hmvoblrcye|part 2]] retains the same headline but its supplied body turns to Han-Xiongnu diplomacy and adds no Liu Chang evidence.
