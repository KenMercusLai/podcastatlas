# 628. Jack The Ripper: The Killer Unmasked (Part 5)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode closes its [[JackTheRipper|Jack the Ripper]] series by testing royal, Masonic, artistic, police-backed, forged-document, and victim-connected suspects against alibi, geography, witness description, physical capacity, known behavior, method, and provenance. It rejects a definitive identification and favors a bounded profile of an unidentified local man familiar with Whitechapel, night work, knives, and possibly slaughterhouse or butchery practice.

Its central contribution is [[ColdCaseSuspectEvaluation]]: a dramatic crime does not require a famous or proportionately dramatic offender, cumulative suspicion is not proof, and even official suspect lists must be separated from evidence capable of attaching guilt.

## Key Claims

- The likely killer was probably a locally knowledgeable man able to move through Whitechapel at night, approach victims without immediate alarm, use a knife, and disappear quickly; the weekend and bank-holiday pattern may indicate regular employment.
- Famous candidates including Gladstone, Lewis Carroll, W. G. Grace, Lord Randolph Churchill, Joseph Merrick, Boston Corbett, and Prince Albert Victor fail basic tests of recognizability, location, alibi, physical plausibility, or evidence.
- The royal-Masonic theory associated with Stephen Knight and Sir William Gull became culturally influential through works such as *From Hell*, but its carriage logistics, Masonic claims, and Gull's age, obesity, and post-stroke condition make it evidentially weak.
- [[WalterSickert]]'s art, interests, and biography do not establish guilt; Ripper letters were probably largely hoaxes, the DNA chain is weak, and his possible presence in France during murders is a major qualification.
- The 1894 [[MacnaghtenMemorandum]] is important because it records Aaron Kosminski, Montague John Druitt, and Michael Ostrog as police suspects while explicitly conceding that proof could not be attached to any of them.
- George Chapman had local access, surgical training, violence toward women, and a later murder record, but his slow poisoning method differs sharply from the Whitechapel mutilations; Kosminski, Druitt, Ostrog, Francis Tumblety, and James Maybrick each have comparable evidentiary or provenance failures.
- Charles Lechmere and a slaughterhouse-worker profile better fit the ordinary local opportunity pattern than celebrity suspects, but the evidence remains circumstantial and does not identify the killer.

## Contradictions

- The episode narrows the earlier wiki presentation of [[WalterSickert]] as suspicious but unproven: it gives greater weight to unreliable letters and DNA, possible French alibis, and the danger of reading criminal confession into art. This is a changed plausibility judgment, not a settled factual contradiction.
- The slaughterhouse or butcher profile explains several opportunity and skill features more economically than celebrity theories, but it remains circumstantial and does not justify naming a killer.
- Witness descriptions, murder count, anatomical skill, employment pattern, exact suspect locations, asylum behavior, and reasons the killings stopped remain source-scoped or uncertain.
