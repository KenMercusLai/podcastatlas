# 043 最被低估的金庸小说？| 闲话《碧血剑》之复仇、历史与传奇

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode reads [[BixueJian|《碧血剑》]] as an early [[JinYong|金庸]] experiment whose revenge, martial inheritance, treasure, and dynastic-history lines exceed the dramatic force of its unusually well-equipped protagonist. Its central political reading is that family vengeance gives way to [[PeopleCenteredHistoricalJudgment|people-centered historical judgment]]: rulers and rebels are tested by whether ordinary people escape violence, not only by ethnic identity, legitimacy, or revolutionary origin. [[LiZicheng|李自成]]'s corruption, the paired deaths of 袁崇焕 and 李岩, and [[YuanChengzhi|袁承志]]'s withdrawal then support [[MartialHeroHistoricalAgencyLimits|the limits of martial-hero historical agency]].

## Key Claims

- Yuan Chengzhi's revenge cannot be completed as a private duel: Chongzhen and Hong Taiji are rulers protected by armies and constrained by recorded history, so the plot converts filial vengeance into judgment about governance and popular suffering.
- The hosts call this an “爱民史观”: anti-Qing heroism can coexist with recognition of beneficial Qing governance because care for ordinary people outranks a simple Han-Manchu binary.
- Yuan reaches the Jianghu already trained by reliable teachers and equipped with orthodox, agile, and unorthodox martial arts, weapons, armor, antidotes, and later treasure; this reduces danger and makes his moral perfection feel less dramatic than his capabilities.
- [[XiaXueyi|夏雪宜]] provides a sharper revenge figure: his family trauma is sympathetic, but a plan to kill fifty members of the Wen family makes retaliation excessive, while his dead-person legacy organizes martial learning, treasure, and later conflict.
- Li Zicheng initially embodies revolt against predatory rule but loses moral authority after entering Beijing, failing to discipline plunder, taking 陈圆圆, and believing that his common origins guarantee continuing representation of common people.
- Li Zicheng's destruction of 李岩 mirrors Chongzhen's destruction of 袁崇焕, turning “碧血” into a recurring pattern in which suspicious power consumes loyal or idealistic servants.
- The repeated robbery of 张朝唐—first by Ming troops and later by rebel troops—makes regime change morally empty when ordinary people continue to suffer the same violence.
- Individual martial excellence cannot substitute for military discipline, command, institutions, or legitimate government; Yuan can rescue people and win encounters but cannot reverse war, corruption, or the historical outcome.
- Overseas withdrawal follows political disillusionment, yet the hosts doubt that Yuan's ethical character and martial ability provide the statecraft needed to build a durable ideal society.
