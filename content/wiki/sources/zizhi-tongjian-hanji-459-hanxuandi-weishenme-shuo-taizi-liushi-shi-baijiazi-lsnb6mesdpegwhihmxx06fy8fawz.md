# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》459｜汉宣帝为什么说太子刘奭是败家子？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Ganlu 1 / 53 BCE under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]], between the [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-458-xihan-chongqi-daren-zhangchang-fan-sizui-weihe-neng-huo-lgla09-xws3c2sctxcojzmwbfvkb|Hanji 458]] Zhang Chang case and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-460-60sui-cisha-qinfu-ta-shi-shishang-zui-biaohan-de-heqin-gongzhu-lntrasdevraz8u4k8tlckm3on1jv|Hanji 460]]'s Xiongnu-Wusun frontier branch. It centers Xuan's anger at crown prince Liu Shi, later [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]], after Liu Shi criticizes Xuan's reliance on legal specialists and urges greater use of Confucian scholars.

The durable synthesis is that the later Yuan-era weakness has a pre-accession diagnosis in Xuan's court. The episode presents Liu Shi as humane and Confucian but politically underpowered: he lacks timing, practical control, and the ability to combine moral learning with administrative force. Xuan considers replacing him with [[LiuQinHuaiyangKingWesternHan|淮阳王刘钦]], but abandons the move because Liu Shi is the established嫡长 crown prince, because [[XuPingjunWesternHan|许平君]]'s death leaves Xuan emotionally indebted to that branch, and because a late heir change could reopen violent succession conflict.

## Key Claims

- Xuan's phrase "乱我家者，太子也" is framed as an angry political forecast, not a casual complaint.
- Liu Shi is described as Confucian, gentle, and humane, but too weak and bookish to master ministers, favorites, outer relatives, and eunuchs.
- The father-son conflict turns on whether Han rule should rely heavily on刑法 and practical control or move toward a more purely Confucian mode.
- Xuan's "汉家自有制度，本以霸王道杂之" position is presented as a governing balance: moral legitimacy and coercive-administrative machinery both matter.
- [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s comment qualifies Xuan's harsher anti-Confucian wording by distinguishing shallow "俗儒" from true Confucian statecraft.
- Liu Shi's failure is not only ideological; the episode stresses timing and expression because he challenges Xuan directly during a private meal.
- Xuan considers [[LiuQinHuaiyangKingWesternHan|刘钦]] because Liu Qin is clever, legally minded, capable, and more like Xuan.
- Xuan ultimately keeps Liu Shi because Xu Pingjun's family had protected him in hardship, Xu was killed by the Huo family, Liu Shi had been targeted, and replacing an established嫡长 crown prince carried large political risk.
- Xuan's appointment of [[WeiXuanchengWesternHan|韦玄成]] as Huaiyang zhongwei is read as a signal to educate Liu Qin toward restraint rather than succession struggle.

## Key Quotes

> "乱我家者，太子也" - Xuan's judgment on Liu Shi as presented by the episode.

> "汉家自有制度，本以霸王道杂之" - the source's core formula for Xuan's mixed-governance position.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode strengthens Hanji 499's Yuan diagnosis by giving it an earlier Xuan-era warning scene.
- Scope caution: the claim that Liu Shi leads toward Western Han decline remains the episode's interpretive frame; this source alone does not prove the complete causal path from Liu Shi's character to Wang Mang's usurpation.
- Identity caution: this source's 淮阳王刘钦 is distinct from the later [[LiuQingHuaiyangKingWesternHan|淮阳王刘清]] in the Jing Fang case.
