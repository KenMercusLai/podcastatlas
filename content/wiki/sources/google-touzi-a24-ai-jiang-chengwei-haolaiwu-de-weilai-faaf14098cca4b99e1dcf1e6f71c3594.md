# Google投資A24，AI將成為好萊塢的未來？

## Summary
This [[DuanwenNewsPodcast|端聞 / 端傳媒新聞播客]] episode uses [[GoogleDeepMind]]'s reported roughly USD75 million investment in [[A24]] to examine why generative AI is entering film despite technical limits and creator resistance. With guest [[ChenZiFilmCultureCommentator|陳子]], it argues that the strategically valuable object may be less A24's finished catalogue than its selection, development, and production decisions, while treating both that motive and the deal's eventual products as unconfirmed.

The episode places the partnership inside [[GenerativeAIHollywoodProduction]]: AI already supports advertising, short drama, concept art, storyboards, and other workflows, but feature films still demand sustained character, performance, narrative, visual, and departmental continuity. It connects resistance to [[AICreativeProcessDatafication]] and explains apparently inevitable adoption through [[FearDrivenAIAdoption]]—companies and creators may collectively build the future they fear because none wants to be left behind.

## Key Claims
- The announced relationship is described as a research collaboration plus an investment of roughly USD75 million, not a sale of A24's film catalogue or an agreement to train on it.
- Public information does not establish what product, tool, or workflow the collaboration will ultimately produce.
- [[A24]]'s author-driven identity intensifies backlash because audiences read AI partnership as a threat to the creative independence and hand-made cultural distinction the brand sells.
- AI is already used in short-form video, advertising, previsualization, concept art, storyboarding, and business or production support, but mature feature films still require long-horizon continuity and coordination that short clips do not prove.
- The episode interprets creators' objections as a defense of process as well as employment: problem solving, selection, judgment, and lived experience are part of the artistic value, not merely costs on the route to an output.
- [[AICreativeProcessDatafication]] captures the episode's central hypothesis that project selection, discarded alternatives, aesthetic judgment, and production decisions could become a new data asset even without access to finished-film rights.
- [[FearDrivenAIAdoption]] explains how competitive anxiety can make a technically and commercially uncertain transition self-reinforcing when studios, technology companies, and creators all fear being late.
- Hollywood's public caution coexists with AI hiring, investment, internal platforms, licensing exploration, and partnerships, so resistance and preparation can occur at the same time.

## Key Quotes
> "創作流程本身視為潛在的數據資產" - the episode summary's formulation of its central inference.

> "不可避免" - the future whose apparent inevitability may be produced by collective fear of falling behind.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction found. The episode extends the existing Hollywood-AI branch by distinguishing short-form adoption from feature-film readiness and finished-film data from process-level decision data.
- The reported investment amount, partnership boundaries, hiring ratio, individual filmmaker reactions, and examples of studio use are source-scoped and not independently audited here.
- The claim that Google primarily wants A24's aesthetic judgment or creative process is explicitly an inference; neither company has disclosed a concrete product or confirmed that strategic motive.
