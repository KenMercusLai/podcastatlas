## Summary
This [[ShuoDeHaoTing]] episode interviews [[YeLuMureka|叶律]] from [[Mureka]] and [[TonyACRCloud|Tony]] from [[ACRCloud|ACR Cloud]] about what matters after [[GenerativeAIMusic|AI music]] can already produce listenable songs. The discussion shifts the wiki's AI-music branch from demo capability toward [[AIMusicWorkflowIntegration]], [[AIMusicDetectionAndLabeling]], [[AIMusicSettlementParity]], copyright checks, user-feedback loops, and platform cleanup of low-value bulk uploads. Its core synthesis is that AI music is becoming both a creator workflow layer and a governance problem: generation quality, detection infrastructure, labeling rules, rights compensation, discovery scarcity, and long-term musical value have to evolve together.

## Key Claims
- [[Mureka]] is presented as a Chinese music-model product that helps musicians and creators use AI across music creation steps.
- [[ACRCloud|ACR Cloud]] is presented as a business-facing audio-recognition and fingerprinting API provider, with AI-music uses around training-data checks, upload checks, copyright detection, and AI-generated-song recognition.
- [[YeLuMureka|叶律]] says ordinary listeners already struggle to tell whether a song is AI-made, but professional release standards still expose gaps in mixing logic, arrangement, and human detail.
- The episode frames [[GenerativeAIMusic|AI music]] as moving from technical spectacle into product value, especially as a helper inside existing musician workflows rather than only a one-prompt generator.
- The speakers argue that future AI music may move from generator toward agent or skill-like workflows, where personal habits, voice, style, tools, and planning can become reusable creative assets.
- [[AIMusicDetectionAndLabeling]] remains unsettled because detection may rely on model patterns and spectrum features even though "AI participation percentage" lacks a clear institutional authority.
- The episode treats [[AIMusicSettlementParity]] as contested: Tony argues equal user value should imply equal DSP payout, while [[YeLuMureka|叶律]] argues AI outputs draw on industry labor that may not be compensated by single-song revenue.
- Bulk generation is not assumed to create value; the episode stresses that music supply was already abundant and that platforms may remove long-zero-play tracks that add catalog noise without listener demand.
