# 566. The Great Northern War: Slaughter on the Steppes (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode presents the 1709 [[BattleOfPoltava|Battle of Poltava]] as the military and geopolitical hinge of the [[GreatNorthernWar|Great Northern War]]. [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]] commits an exhausted, outnumbered, poorly coordinated Swedish force against [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]]'s fortified and reformed army; defeat destroys Sweden's field army and confirms the [[RussianEmpire|Russian Empire]] as a European great power. The aftermath follows Charles into Ottoman exile, Sweden's continued resistance, his death in Norway, and the 1721 Treaty of Nystad that ends the [[SwedishEmpire|Swedish Empire]]'s Baltic primacy.

## Key Claims

- Poltava was not an isolated reversal: Charles reached it after the loss of his supply column, diversion into Ukraine, a catastrophic winter, and a severe wound had already narrowed Swedish options.
- The Swedish assault depended on speed, surprise, discipline, and close combat compensating for inferior numbers, little artillery, fortified Russian redoubts, and command friction between Rehnskiöld and Lewenhaupt.
- Attacks on the redoubts, Roos's separation, Lewenhaupt's displacement, and confused command broke Swedish coordination before the main lines met.
- Peter's reformed army absorbed the Swedish charge, used artillery, reserves, and numerical depth, and converted tactical disruption into the destruction of the Swedish infantry.
- Charles's escape preserved him as a symbol of continued resistance but abandoned most of the surviving army and could not reverse the coalition's return to war against Sweden.
- Poltava reordered northern and eastern Europe by ending Sweden's dominant position, increasing Russian influence over Poland and the Baltic, and securing Saint Petersburg's strategic future.
- Charles's refusal to concede prolonged devastation in Sweden and Finland until his death in 1718; the Treaty of Nystad in 1721 formalized Russia's Baltic gains and Peter's imperial title.

## Key Quotes

> "teachers in the art of war" - Peter's reported toast to the captured Swedish officers after Poltava.

> "final stone" - Peter's description of Poltava's place in the foundation of Saint Petersburg.
