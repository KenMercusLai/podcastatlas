## Summary
This [[Banlatte|半拿铁]] episode reconstructs China's 2014-2018 [[O2OPlatformEconomics|O2O platform]] and [[ChineseSharingEconomyBubble|sharing-economy]] boom through shared bikes, power banks, shared cars, unmanned shelves, home massage, home manicure, cooking, housekeeping, laundry, car washing, tutoring, and community services. It uses [[Mobike]], [[Ofo|OFO]], [[Bluegogo|小蓝单车]], [[HelloBike|哈啰]], [[JiedianPowerBank|街电]], [[Helijia|河狸家]], [[DuduMeijia|嘟嘟美甲]], and [[DingdongXiaoqu|叮咚小区]] to argue that many offline needs were real, but not all could support venture-speed expansion, subsidies, deposits, and asset-heavy platform narratives.

## Key Claims
- [[O2OPlatformEconomics]] became attractive when mobile internet, payment, smartphone adoption, policy language, and service-sector growth made offline services look like the next internetable layer after traffic-heavy online sectors had consolidated.
- [[SharedBikeCapitalWar]] turned [[Mobike]] and [[Ofo|OFO]] into a proxy fight among founders, operators, and backers such as [[ZhuXiaohu|朱啸虎]], [[GSRVentures|金沙江创投]], [[Didi]], [[Tencent]], and [[Alibaba]].
- The episode distinguishes real demand from weak platform economics: users may need bikes, power banks, cleaning, repair, or manicure, while the company still fails because fulfillment, quality control, travel time, utilization, subsidy cost, deposits, and regulation do not scale cleanly.
- [[Mobike]] and [[Ofo|OFO]] show two different shared-bike failure modes: Mobike emphasized expensive custom hardware and was sold to [[Meituan]], while OFO chased cheap expansion, weak lock control, high subsidies, investor conflict, and finally a deposit-refund crisis.
- Shared power banks survived better than many other "shared" categories because small, standardized, high-urgency assets in dense commercial venues matched the rental formula more closely than cars, umbrellas, gyms, wardrobes, or unmanned shelves.
- [[LocalServicesO2O]] was harder than the pitch decks implied because skilled human services such as massage, manicure, cooking, housekeeping, laundry, car washing, and tutoring were hard to standardize and often lost economics to travel time and local trust.
- The episode's final synthesis is that O2O did not disappear: ordinary service providers can now use [[Xiaohongshu]], [[WeChat]], payments, short video, and local communities to run smaller online-to-offline loops without a universal intermediary platform.
