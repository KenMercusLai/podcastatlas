# Hitler’s Olympics, Part 5: The Amateur’s Hour

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode examines how [[AveryBrundage]] helped secure United States participation in the 1936 Berlin Olympics after a Nazi-managed inspection of Jewish athletes' conditions. His public commitment to equal opportunity and [[OlympicAmateurism]] is tested against antisemitic correspondence, institutional ambition, dismissal of reported violence and exclusion, and his acceptance of assurances that qualified Jewish athletes could compete.

[[MargaretLambert|Margaret Lambert]], born Gretel Bergmann, supplies the athlete-level counterexample. Nazi authorities pressured her to train as evidence of supposed inclusion, denied her equal facilities, and excluded her after she equaled the German high-jump record; her treatment shows how formal eligibility promises can become propaganda when political power controls training, selection, and testimony.

## Key Claims

- After Lambert's sports club expelled her for being Jewish, Nazi authorities later pressured her to pursue Olympic selection so that her candidacy could be cited internationally as evidence of fair treatment.
- Brundage traveled to Germany in 1934 without German, relied on Nazi officials and interpreters, and questioned Jewish sports representatives in the presence of regime officials.
- Jewish representatives reported closed facilities, disrupted events, assaults, and inadequate training opportunities, but Brundage dismissed or failed to pursue their complaints and accepted Nazi assurances of equal Olympic eligibility.
- Brundage publicly defended equality and political separation while privately describing the boycott campaign in antisemitic terms and seeking entry into the [[InternationalOlympicCommittee]].
- The episode finds no explicit bargain tying an IOC seat to delivery of the American team, but interprets Brundage's ambition, correspondence, conduct, and later appointment as evidence against simple deception.
- Lambert equaled the German high-jump record despite inferior preparation and was then excluded on a false performance rationale, confirming her candidacy's propaganda function.
- Brundage entered the IOC shortly before the Games and watched Hitler open them while Lambert sought a visa to leave Germany.

## Contradictions

- The episode challenges a charitable reading of Brundage as merely deceived by Nazi assurances and creates interpretive tension with [[hitler-s-olympics-part-9-a-plague-on-both-your-houses-hitler-s-olympics-part-9-a-plague-on-both-your-houses|Part 9]]'s use of him as a “soft idealist.” Part 5's correspondence and inspection conduct support knowing accommodation, while the exact motive and any explicit appointment bargain remain unproven.
- Nazi assurances that qualified Jewish athletes could compete are contradicted within the episode by unequal access to training and Lambert's exclusion after record-level performance.
- The source supports deliberate propaganda and institutional complicity more strongly than it proves every private motive, conversation, or causal link. Its archival descriptions, translated meeting account, psychological comparison, and claims about IOC ambition remain episode-scoped rather than independently verified here.
