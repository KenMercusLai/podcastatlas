# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》294-1｜武帝不怕权臣不怕匈奴，为何独独怕他？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] as a court figure whose principled refusal to flatter formal power earns respect from both [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. Its central contrast is ritual: other officials bow to the favored general and the emperor receives Wei Qing or [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] informally, whereas Ji keeps equal guest etiquette with Wei and reportedly makes Wudi unwilling to receive him without proper dress and posture.

The episode interprets Wudi's apparent “fear” as respect mixed with caution before a critic whose conduct had accumulated [[MoralAuthorityConstrainsFormalPower|道德威望约束权力]]. It uses a short passage from the *Dizigui* as a modern moral frame, but that pedagogical frame is not evidence for the historical scenes.

## Key Claims

- After Wei Qing becomes da jiangjun and attracts widespread court deference, Ji An refuses the expected bow and argues that a great general benefits from retaining a guest who greets him with an equal-standing salute.
- Wei Qing reportedly accepts the refusal without anger, calls Ji worthy, and repeatedly asks him about state affairs.
- Liu An and Zhuang Zhu are cited as evaluators of Ji's directness, integrity, and resistance to being moved by improper advantage.
- Wudi is portrayed as highly informal with Wei Qing and inconsistently formal with Gongsun Hong, but unwilling to receive Ji while bareheaded or sitting improperly.
- In the culminating anecdote, Wudi sees Ji approach while the emperor is inside the military tent without his cap, withdraws behind the tent, and has an attendant approve Ji's request without an audience.
- The host reads these scenes as evidence that moral reputation can constrain the conduct of people with much greater formal power.
- The opening *Dizigui* material connects persuasion to reason, upright example, and humane treatment; this is the host's interpretive frame rather than a demonstrated source for Ji's behavior.

## Key Quotes

> “大将军有揖客，反而更能显得他虚怀大度。” - the episode's paraphrase of Ji An's defense of equal guest etiquette with Wei Qing.

> “他说啥我都批准，让他赶紧走吧。” - the host's colloquial rendering of Wudi avoiding an improperly dressed audience with Ji An.

## Connections

- [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] - central official whose stable etiquette and candid reputation make formal superiors regulate their own behavior around him.
- [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] - favored general who accepts Ji as an equal-standing guest and reportedly seeks his advice.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler whose unusual concern for correct dress and posture supplies the episode's “fear” claim.
- [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] - comparison minister before whom Wudi is said to remain relatively informal.
- [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]] - one of the figures whose evaluation is used to establish Ji's reputation for integrity.
- [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - external enemy used by the title and Wei Qing's war record to heighten the contrast with Wudi's caution before Ji.
- [[MoralAuthorityConstrainsFormalPower|道德威望约束权力]] - synthesis distinguishing coercive command from reputation-based behavioral constraint.
- [[PowerEtiquetteReading|权力礼仪细读]] - adjacent method for reading bows, salutes, headwear, posture, and audience procedure as political evidence.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode adds a ritual and interpersonal mechanism to the existing profile of Ji An as a critic whose corrective value Wudi finds uncomfortable but cannot dismiss.
- The transcript repeatedly renders 汲黯 as “己案” and 公孙弘 as “公孙洪”; both are normalized from the joined Western Han identity and episode context. “庄祝” is likely 庄助, but the source note leaves that secondary evaluator unlinked pending direct textual collation.
- The exact wording, chronology, offices, number of consultations, Wudi's dress and posture, location and meaning of “武帐,” and whether the avoided audience indicates fear, respect, embarrassment, or retrospective moralizing remain episode-attributed pending comparison with primary editions.
- The *Dizigui* passage is a much later pedagogical frame and should not be treated as Ji An's own intellectual source or as independent confirmation of the Han anecdotes.
