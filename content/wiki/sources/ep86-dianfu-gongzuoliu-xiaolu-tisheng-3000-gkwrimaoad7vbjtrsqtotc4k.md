# EP86 颠覆工作流，效率提升3000%

## Summary
This [[ZongHengSiHai]] Mini MBA episode turns [[OperationsManagementWorkflow|operations management]] into a personal and organizational workflow toolkit. The host moves from process view into [[TheoryOfConstraintsPersonalWorkflow]], [[WIPLimitPersonalProductivity]], [[QueueingTheorySlack]], [[CriticalPathMethodPersonalPlanning]], [[BullwhipEffectInformationAmplification]], [[ToyotaProductionSystemPersonalWaste]], [[AgileSprintPersonalWorkflow]], [[FlowMasterPersonalWorkflow]], and [[LeanStartupFeedbackLoop]]. The central synthesis is that goals do not produce results by themselves; bottlenecks, WIP, slack, critical paths, information distortion, waste removal, sprint review, and low-cost validation determine whether effort becomes finished work.

## Key Claims
- [[OperationsManagementWorkflow]] reframes companies and personal work as processes that transform inputs into outputs under cost, speed, quality, and variety tradeoffs.
- [[TheoryOfConstraintsPersonalWorkflow]] argues that system output is governed by the bottleneck, so teams and individuals should find the Herbie-like constraint before optimizing everything locally.
- [[WIPLimitPersonalProductivity]] uses Little's Law to explain why too many open tasks increase waiting, context switching, psychological load, and delivery time.
- [[QueueingTheorySlack]] treats calendar buffer, batched interruptions, and capacity headroom as necessary design choices when random demand hits a fixed throughput system.
- [[CriticalPathMethodPersonalPlanning]] separates activities that determine total duration from tasks that can run in parallel or consume slack without delaying the project.
- [[BullwhipEffectInformationAmplification]] connects supply-chain demand amplification to organizational message distortion and personal overcorrection after schedule slippage.
- [[ToyotaProductionSystemPersonalWaste]], [[AgileSprintPersonalWorkflow]], and [[LeanStartupFeedbackLoop]] translate TPS, Scrum, and MVP/validated learning into personal systems for waste removal, short feedback loops, and small-cost reality checks.
