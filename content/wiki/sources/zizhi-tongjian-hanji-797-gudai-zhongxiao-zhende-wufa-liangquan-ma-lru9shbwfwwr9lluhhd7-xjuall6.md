## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 121-122 CE [[EmperorAnOfHan|汉安帝]] sequence immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-798-donghan-shiqi-de-baixing-you-duo-ku-loolveumdtv4eo-omubfi5z2p2r5|Hanji 798]]. It first treats three-year mourning for senior officials as a live governance dispute: [[ChenZhongLateHan|陈忠]] defends filial mourning as ritual order, but eunuch obstruction precedes Emperor An's cancellation of the policy.

The second half turns mourning into frontier statecraft. After [[GoguryeoLateHan|高句丽]] attacks Xuantu and its king dies, [[YaoGuangLateHan|姚光]] wants to attack during royal mourning, while Chen Zhong persuades An to send condolences, rebuke earlier aggression, and pardon instead; the later forged-edict case against Yao and [[FengHuanLateHan|冯焕]] shows how document authority can turn private revenge into lethal public action.

## Key Claims

- The episode's title question about loyalty and filiality is narrower than the source's actual political problem: official mourning tests whether state service may suspend ritual feeling toward parents.
- Chen Zhong argues that three-year mourning for officials is not a private luxury but a way to guide ordinary people toward filial order through elite example.
- Eunuch obstruction matters because the anti-mourning memorial is blocked before it reaches the emperor, making court access part of the policy outcome.
- Emperor An's 121 CE decision cancels three-year mourning for officials of 2,000 shi and above, even after Chen Zhong invokes Han institutional precedent and Mencian moral extension.
- Chen Zhong applies mourning logic outward in the Goguryeo debate: attacking an enemy during royal mourning is framed as unrighteous and strategically inferior to condolence-backed rebuke.
- The forged-edict case around Yao Guang and Feng Huan shows a different institutional weakness from bad advice: private enemies can exploit command-document obedience to cause official execution and arrest.
- The Qiang, Xianbei, disaster, locust, earthquake, hail, and rain notices keep the An-era state under continuous environmental and frontier pressure rather than treating the mourning debate as an isolated moral anecdote.

## Key Quotes

> "不让子女为父母尽哀，是毁灭天性" - Yuan Hong's judgment, as cited by the episode, frames the anti-mourning order as damage to human feeling rather than administrative simplification.

> "趁丧进攻是不义" - Chen Zhong's objection to attacking Goguryeo during royal mourning turns ritual timing into a standard for frontier policy.

> "诏书文字不对" - Feng Huan's son Feng Gun notices the forged command's wording problem, enabling the court to expose the plot.
