## Summary
This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode explains [[Italy]]'s May 1915 entry into the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]] as a voluntary, openly acquisitive intervention driven by territorial ambition, [[ItalianIrredentism|Italian irredentism]], elite bargaining, and nationalist street pressure. It follows [[GabrieleDAnnunzio|Gabriele D'Annunzio]]'s ecstatic pro-war rhetoric, Antonio Salandra's "sacred egoism," parliamentary crisis, and the role of crowds in making anti-war politics appear illegitimate.

The battlefield half turns the intervention into a warning about rhetoric meeting terrain. [[LuigiCadorna|Luigi Cadorna]]'s doctrine of relentless frontal attack collided with the [[IsonzoFront|Isonzo front]]'s mountains, limestone, machine guns, barbed wire, and Austro-Hungarian defense, producing enormous casualties and limited gains. The episode then links the campaign's trauma and the postwar "mutilated victory" mood to the emotional preconditions of [[ItalianFascism|Italian fascism]] and [[BenitoMussolini|Benito Mussolini]]'s later politics.

## Key Claims
- Italy stayed neutral in 1914 because the Triple Alliance was defensive and [[AustriaHungary|Austria-Hungary]] had acted against Serbia without proper consultation.
- Salandra and Italian leaders bargained with the Allies for territorial rewards, demanding South Tyrol, Trentino, Trieste, Gorizia, Istria, Dalmatia, parts of Albania, the Dodecanese, and money.
- The episode presents the intervention as unusually explicit conquest politics: Italy was not defending its homeland but trying to make a greater Italy around the Adriatic.
- Public opposition was broad, especially in poorer southern regions, while pro-war pressure came disproportionately from nationalist intellectuals, poets, students, journalists, and street crowds.
- [[GabrieleDAnnunzio|D'Annunzio]]'s speeches sacralized blood, conquest, betrayal, and national rebirth, prefiguring methods later associated with fascist politics.
- [[LuigiCadorna|Cadorna]] planned a sweeping offensive through the Alps toward Trieste, Ljubljana, Zagreb, and the Habsburg heartland despite weak mobilization, poor equipment, literacy and dialect barriers, and difficult terrain.
- The early Isonzo battles showed the familiar First World War offensive problem in a mountain theater: repeated frontal attacks against fortified high ground produced huge casualties and minimal strategic change.
- Austro-Hungarian defenders under Svetozar Boroevic had strong defensive advantages, and many Slovenes, Croats, and Bosnians were defending land they regarded as their own.
- By the end of 1915 Italy had suffered vast losses without decisive success; later Caporetto, the Piave, Vittorio Veneto, and the peace settlement left both victory and resentment available for postwar politics.

## Key Quotes
> "sacred egoism" - Salandra's phrase for pursuing Italian advantage during the war.

> "unredeemed Italy" - the irredentist premise behind demands for Italian-speaking or Adriatic territories under foreign rule.

> "Duce" - the title D'Annunzio later used at Fiume before Mussolini made it central to fascist leadership.
