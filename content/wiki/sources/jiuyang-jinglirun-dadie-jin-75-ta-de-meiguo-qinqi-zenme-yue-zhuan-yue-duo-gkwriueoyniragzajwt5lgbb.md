# 九阳净利润大跌近 75%，它的美国「亲戚」怎么越赚越多？

## Summary

This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode contrasts profit pressure among Chinese small-appliance companies with [[SharkNinja]]'s source-reported growth in the United States. Its durable explanation is a [[CrossBorderConsumerBrandCapabilityStack]]: local brand recognition and retail access combine with [[Joyoung|九阳]]-linked product engineering, Chinese supply-chain speed, demonstrable products, social-media marketing, and diversified sales channels.

The opening news scan also covers [[WandaGroup|万达集团]], weak Chinese cinema attendance, [[Skydance]] cost reduction and streaming integration, and [[Google]]'s faster [[TPU]] release cadence. Those current figures remain episode-reported; the deeper small-appliance segment supplies the source's strongest reusable synthesis.

## Key Claims

- The episode reports that third-quarter sales rose in China's small-appliance market while profits fell at Supor, Bear Electric, and Joyoung, with Joyoung's decline close to 75%.
- SharkNinja is presented as the leading U.S. small-appliance company by sales, with recent double-digit revenue growth and strong positions in categories including blenders and air fryers.
- U.S. offline retail gives established local brands an access advantage because retailers allocate limited prominent shelf space selectively; less intense multi-category competition then creates room for adjacent-category expansion.
- After the 2017 investment led by Joyoung's parent and CDH, SharkNinja reportedly expanded engineering in China, the United States, and Europe and used Joyoung-linked technical capability to develop products such as a multifunction cooker.
- The episode treats Chinese engineering and supply-chain speed as an iteration advantage, but not as a complete route to U.S. demand without local brand recognition, retail distribution, and product demonstration.
- Small appliances fit short video and livestreaming because they are relatively affordable, visually demonstrable, and quick to evaluate; SharkNinja combines social media with its own site, television shopping, and physical retail.
- Tariffs, the cost of moving production beyond China, and fast imitation of hit products may weaken the growth system unless product development, brand investment, and channel access continue to compound.
