# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》220｜民间歌谣讽刺文帝“假仁义”，刘恒反应古怪（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses [[BoZhaoWesternHan|薄昭]]'s death to test [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s law-and-kinship judgment. It says Bo Zhao killed an imperial envoy, refused an initial request to take his own life, and finally committed suicide after Liu Heng sent officials in mourning dress to stage funeral lamentation; the emperor then personally mourned him and preserved succession to his marquisate.

The episode presents three competing judgments. [[LiDeyu|李德裕]] argues that decisive punishment neglected the living [[BaoJi|薄太后]]'s bond with her only brother; [[SimaGuang|司马光]] defends law as a public instrument that must bind close kin and blames the earlier decision to give Bo Zhao military power without adequate guidance; [[CaoPi|曹丕]] pushes prevention further, arguing that imperial uncles should receive support rather than authority. The durable synthesis is therefore not simply whether Bo Zhao should die, but how granting power to close relatives can create an avoidable conflict between equal enforcement and family obligation.

## Key Claims

- In the episode's chronology, a drought occurs in 171 BCE and Liu Heng visits Ganquan Palace in the following winter.
- The transmitted record is described as sparse: 《史记》 omits Bo Zhao's death, 《汉书·文帝纪》 records it briefly, and 《汉书·外戚恩泽侯表》 links his suicide to killing an imperial envoy.
- A commentator's story says Bo Zhao formed a lethal grudge after a drinking incident and had an attendant killed; the episode does not independently establish the anecdote.
- Liu Heng reportedly first sends ministers to urge Bo Zhao to commit suicide, then sends officials in mourning clothes to lament at his gate until he yields.
- Liu Heng personally mourns Bo Zhao and permits Bo Zhao's son Bo Rong to inherit a reconstituted marquisate, combining punishment with posthumous family accommodation.
- Li Deyu criticizes the decision from ritual and filial obligation because Bo Zhao was the living dowager's only brother.
- Sima Guang argues that law is a public instrument and must apply without distinction between close and distant people, while also criticizing Liu Heng for giving Bo Zhao military power before preventing arrogance and abuse.
- Cao Pi treats the upstream error as empowering an imperial uncle: kin can be supported materially without receiving authority that later makes punishment necessary.

## Key Quotes

> “法者，天下之公器。” - the legal principle attributed by the episode to Sima Guang.

> “养之以恩，而不假之权。” - the preventive outer-relative principle attributed to Cao Pi.

## Connections

- [[BoZhaoWesternHan|薄昭]] - imperial uncle whose alleged killing of an envoy produces the episode's punishment, ritual, and prevention dispute.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - ruler who makes punishment unavoidable after previously entrusting Bo Zhao with military authority, then preserves mourning and succession accommodations.
- [[BaoJi|薄姬 / 薄太后]] - emperor's mother and Bo Zhao's sister, whose grief anchors Li Deyu's ritual objection.
- [[LiDeyu|李德裕]] - commentator who weighs decisive punishment against filial and ritual obligation to the dowager.
- [[SimaGuang|司马光]] - historian who defends kin-neutral law while locating a prior failure in appointment and instruction.
- [[CaoPi|曹丕]] - ruler-commentator who distinguishes material support for imperial kin from delegation of public power.
- [[OuterRelativeCommandAppointmentRisk|外戚任重职风险]] - the case adds a completed failure sequence from kin appointment through serious abuse to an accountability dilemma.
- [[RuleOverKinshipPower|法度压过亲缘特权]] - the punishment branch supports equal-law credibility while its coercive form and family costs remain qualified.

## Contradictions

- The episode preserves a source-layer gap rather than resolving it: terse dynastic records identify killing an envoy, while the chess-and-drinking motive comes from later annotation.
- The exact identity and office of the victim, the drinking incident, the sequence of suicide demands, the mourning-clothes performance, dialogue, dates, and Bo Rong's title require comparison with transmitted sources.
- Li Deyu, Sima Guang, and Cao Pi supply different normative evaluations, not mutually exclusive factual accounts: filial obligation, equal enforcement, and preventive appointment design operate at different stages of the case.
- Calling the method “古怪” is the host's interpretation. The supplied account supports indirect but forceful compulsion, not a settled judgment about whether it was legally required or ritually proper.
