## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] conversation has [[AndrewHuberman]] and [[TonyWyssCoray]] examine how blood, cerebrospinal fluid, exercise-induced molecules, and proteomic clocks may reveal or influence aging. Mouse parabiosis and transfer experiments support [[CirculatingFactorAgingBiology]] and [[ExerciseConditionedBloodSignaling]] as causal research programs, while early human plasma studies and [[OrganSpecificAgingClock|organ-specific clocks]] remain promising but incomplete translations. The practical frame favors healthspan, exercise, ordinary risk reduction, and controlled trials over young-plasma services, unproven stem-cell treatments, supplements, or a single rejuvenation molecule.

## Key Claims
- Young circulation or young plasma can reactivate neural stem cells, reduce inflammation, increase neural activity, and improve memory in old mice, but these animal results do not establish a human anti-aging treatment.
- Blood proteins can act as signals as well as biomarkers; age-related inflammatory factors may be detrimental, while some young or exercise-induced factors may support stem cells, mitochondria, or brain function.
- Blood proteomics can estimate age-like patterns for individual organs, and an organ's age gap is reported to predict later disease risk in that organ.
- [[VeroBiosciences]] is presented as combining organ-age signatures with clinical and wearable data so interventions can be selected and then reassessed.
- Exercise-conditioned plasma transferred some brain benefits to sedentary mice; clusterin and GPLDH are named as candidate mediators rather than complete explanations.
- Young cerebrospinal fluid improved regeneration and cognition in old mice, while human cerebrospinal-fluid synaptic-protein ratios were associated with cognitive resilience independently of pathology markers.
- No human intervention has been validated to extend lifespan; the source prioritizes healthspan and treats exercise and diet as better supported than NAD precursors, fasting protocols, exosomes, or unapproved cell therapies.

## Key Quotes
> "No human intervention has been tested and validated to extend lifespan." — the episode summary's evidence boundary.

> "The field should focus on healthspan: preserving organ function and cognition until death, not merely extending years." — the episode summary's outcome priority.
