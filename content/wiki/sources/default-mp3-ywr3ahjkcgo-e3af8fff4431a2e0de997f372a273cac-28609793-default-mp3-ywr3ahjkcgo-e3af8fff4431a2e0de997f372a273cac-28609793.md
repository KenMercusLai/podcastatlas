# You 2.0: Regrets, I Have a Few...

## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode has psychologist [[AmySomerville|Amy Somerville]] explain regret as a comparison between reality and an imagined alternative that can reveal values, mistakes, and possible future action. [[RegretIntegration]] and [[RuminationVsReflection]] separate learning from intrusive replay, while [[PsychologicalCounterfactualThinking]] distinguishes upward alternatives that can guide improvement from downward alternatives that can provide relief.

Listener stories about estrangement, suicide, childhood bullying, and missed opportunities also expose the danger of exaggerated self-blame. [[RegretResponsibilityAttribution]] keeps personal agency in view while restoring situational and multi-actor causes, and [[SatisficingDecisionStrategy]] offers a prospective response: choose an option that meets meaningful standards instead of extending comparison indefinitely in search of the unknowable best.

## Key Claims
- Regret is common and can be constructive when comparison with an alternative produces learning, value clarification, repair, or a future behavioral change.
- [[RuminationVsReflection]] distinguishes useful review from repetitive, intrusive thought that yields no new understanding and is associated in the episode with depression and anxiety symptoms.
- Regret after action can be stronger immediately, while regret over inaction may persist because an unfinished goal remains mentally open.
- [[RegretResponsibilityAttribution]] explains why people may overstate their own causal role in tragedy, especially when a simple “what if” restores a feeling of control over an unpredictable loss.
- [[PsychologicalCounterfactualThinking]] separates upward counterfactuals that imagine improvement from downward counterfactuals that imagine a worse result; the accessible comparison can change satisfaction without changing the outcome itself.
- Regret and guilt overlap but are not identical: guilt additionally compares conduct with personal standards, duties, or goals.
- [[SatisficingDecisionStrategy]] reduces open-ended comparison by stopping when an option meets defined standards rather than attempting to prove it is the absolute best.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends [[RegretIntegration]] by showing that preserving a painful event in one's life story is useful only when review produces understanding, repair, value clarification, or changed action.
- Situational explanation does not excuse bullying or remove duties to repair harm; it prevents responsibility from being reduced either to total personal blame or to total exoneration.
- Downward comparison may provide relief without teaching what to change, while upward comparison may guide learning but intensify distress when the event is psychologically close or irreversible.
