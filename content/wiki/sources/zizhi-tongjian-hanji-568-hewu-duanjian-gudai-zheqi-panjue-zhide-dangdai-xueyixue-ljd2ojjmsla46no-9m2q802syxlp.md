# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》568丨何武断剑！古代这起判决值得当代学一学

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Suihe 2 / 7 BCE under [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]], immediately before the Chengdi death and Aidi early-reign sequence developed in later Hanji 570s notes. It moves from the court debate over whether [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s temple should be removed, to [[HeWuWesternHan|何武]]'s famous "断剑" inheritance judgment, then to He Wu's removal and [[ShiDanWesternHan|施丹]]'s repeated remonstrance against Aidi's rushed early personnel and kinship changes.

The durable synthesis is that the episode treats legal, ritual, and political interpretation as related forms of judgment. He Wu's inheritance ruling reads a father's sword bequest as a symbolic instruction to restore the son as household successor, illustrating [[SpringAutumnIntentAdjudication|春秋决狱式意图裁判]] while also creating modern fairness concerns. The later court sequence shows Aidi preserving Han Wudi's temple through merit-based exception, removing He Wu on a filial-duty pretext, and receiving Shi Dan's warnings that a new ruler should not reorder kin, office, and policy too quickly before mourning and succession legitimacy have stabilized.

## Key Claims

- Kong Guang and Wu Shang ask Aidi to discuss removing ancestral temples whose kinship relation has become distant, and Peng Xuan's group argues that even Han Wudi's temple can be removed once亲尽.
- Wang Shun and Liu Xin oppose removing Han Wudi's temple by citing the Book of Rites rule that temples honored as "zong" do not count against the ordinary seven-temple limit.
- He Wu's "断剑" case centers on a wealthy father whose literal will leaves wealth to daughters and only a sword to the young son, while delaying the sword transfer until the son turns fifteen.
- He Wu reads the sword as a symbol of decisional authority and judges that the adult son should recover the household estate after the daughters and sons-in-law have enjoyed it for fifteen years.
- The host treats the judgment as humane and insight-sensitive, but also notes that from a modern legal perspective He Wu may look insufficiently impartial or text-bound.
- Aidi removes He Wu as Da Sikong after critics attack He Wu's handling of his stepmother's travel from Shu during Chengdi's death.
- Shi Dan, newly appointed Da Sikong, repeatedly warns Aidi that early reign changes, Ding/Fu kin favoritism, and hasty policy shifts violate mourning restraint and risk making the emperor's errors larger.
