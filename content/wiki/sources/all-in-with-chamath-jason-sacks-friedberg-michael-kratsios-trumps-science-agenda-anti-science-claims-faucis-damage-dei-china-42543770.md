# Michael Kratsios: Trump's Science Agenda, Science Funding, Fauci, DEI, and China

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] interview has [[MichaelKratsios|Michael Kratsios]], Director of the [[OfficeOfScienceAndTechnologyPolicy|White House Office of Science and Technology Policy]], defending the Trump administration's [[NewGoldenAgeScienceAgenda|"Science, A New Golden Age" agenda]] against anti-science criticism. The episode reframes support for science around funding outcomes, politicization, public trust, and competition with [[China]] rather than only annual research-and-development totals. Kratsios argues for [[ScienceFundingPortfolioLogic|meta-science units, reviewer golden tickets, flexible grants, and portable fellowships]], while also tying [[GenesisMission|Genesis Mission]], energy abundance, and [[USChinaScienceCompetition|U.S.-China science competition]] to national scientific capacity.

## Key Claims
- [[OfficeOfScienceAndTechnologyPolicy|OSTP]] is described as a White House coordinator across decentralized U.S. science and technology agencies rather than a consolidated science ministry.
- Kratsios rejects the anti-science label and presents [[NewGoldenAgeScienceAgenda|Science, A New Golden Age]] as an attempt to renew American science through goals, funding experiments, and outcome measurement.
- The discussion treats politicization as a central trust problem: DEI requirements, COVID-era authority, and climate-emergency language are framed as cases where institutions may substitute dogma for inquiry.
- Kratsios says the administration disrupted some active grant funding because it wanted to redirect money away from politicized work, while distinguishing proposed budgets from congressional appropriations.
- The source cites a Ted Cruz-linked Senate analysis claiming roughly 25% of Biden-era [[NationalScienceFoundation|NSF]] grants were DEI-related; the wiki keeps the figure source-scoped.
- Climate discussion accepts that humans contributed CO2 through fossil-fuel use but disputes the emergency framing, with RCP 8.5 used as the main example of an extreme scenario shaping policy and media attention.
- [[EnergyAbundanceClimateStrategy|Energy abundance]] is presented as the preferred climate and development route: nuclear, fusion, and affordable power are favored over restriction or economic contraction.
- The [[NationalInstitutesOfHealth|NIH]] section uses funding growth, Eroom's law, and perceived scientific stagnation to argue that output per dollar matters as much as budget totals.
- The clearest funding reform proposal is [[ScienceFundingPortfolioLogic|portfolio-style science funding]]: meta-science units, reviewer golden tickets, short fast-track grants, longer multi-year grants, and portable fellowships.
- Kratsios says government-funded research should emphasize basic, early-stage, pre-competitive discovery where private markets are less likely to fund the work naturally.
- The agenda uses discrete national missions in space, quantum computing, fusion, and [[GenesisMission|AI-enabled science]] as a way to restore a more ambitious public science posture.
- The [[USChinaScienceCompetition|China competition]] section treats scientific leadership as economic and national-security infrastructure, while emphasizing the U.S. venture ecosystem, universities, private industry, public research funding, and global talent attraction.
- The STEM talent section prioritizes a stronger domestic pipeline and gifted education while leaving high-skill immigration reform comparatively undefined.

## Key Quotes
