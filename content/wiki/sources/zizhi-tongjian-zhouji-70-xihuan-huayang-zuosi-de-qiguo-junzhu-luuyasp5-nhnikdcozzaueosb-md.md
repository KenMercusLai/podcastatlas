# 《资治通鉴·周纪》70丨喜欢花样作死的齐国君主

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the reversal preview in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-69-bikan-gudai-diwang-qipa-sifa-da-pandian-lukvjsktg-pgfjhizvypwmdmw-13|Zhouji 69]]: in 284 BCE, [[YanZhaowang|燕昭王]] appoints [[LeYi|乐毅]] to command the anti-Qi coalition, [[QiState|齐国]] collapses after defeat west of the Ji River, and [[Linzi|临淄]] falls. It frames [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]]'s later flight and death under [[ZhuoChi|卓齿]] as the cumulative result of post-victory arrogance, killed remonstrance, lost people-support, and international isolation. The ending turns to [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s use of [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s Wangba chapter, presenting the episode as political instruction rather than only a violent ruler-death story.

## Key Claims
- [[LeYi|乐毅]]'s five-state command is the operational payoff to [[YanZhaowang|燕昭王]]'s earlier [[TalentAttractionThroughRespect|礼贤下士式人才吸附]]: recruited talent becomes strategic coordination and battlefield execution.
- The coalition against [[QiState|齐国]] is a mature [[CommonEnemyAlliance|共同敌人联盟]] case, but its members still pursue different gains after victory.
- [[JuXin|剧辛]] warns that small [[YanState|燕国]] should take border gains rather than drive deep into large Qi, showing a strategic alternative to Le Yi's collapse-exploitation plan.
- Le Yi's counterargument is political as much as military: because [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] alienated ministers and people, a battlefield defeat can trigger internal collapse if pursued quickly.
- The fall of [[Linzi|临淄]] converts Qi's earlier urban strength into occupation prize; the episode says Yan seizes treasure and ritual valuables and sends them home.
- Qi Min Wang's failed refuge sequence in [[WeyState|卫国]], [[ZouState|邹国]], and [[LuState|鲁国]] makes arrogance in defeat a practical security problem, not only a character flaw.
- [[ZhuoChi|卓齿]] is presented with mixed motives: possible righteous anger, personal irritation, material interest, and possible tacit alignment with Yan all remain in play.
- Zhuo Chi's heaven-earth-human warnings turn omens and public grief into an indictment of tyrannical rule.
- Qi Min Wang's execution fulfills the prior [[RulerNonNaturalDeathTypology|帝王非命死法类型]] preview, but with a more graphic and punitive death than the earlier categories alone suggested.
- The closing [[Xunzi|荀子]] quotation makes the episode's "资治" lesson explicit: the story is selected to teach rulers what kind of rule creates abandonment, coalition, and violent vulnerability.
