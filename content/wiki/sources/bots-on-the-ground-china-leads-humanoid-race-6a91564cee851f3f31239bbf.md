# Bots on the ground: China leads humanoid race

## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode connects three short briefings: [[DonWeinland]] on [[China]]'s humanoid robot push, [[AliceFullwood]] on [[Meta]]'s teen social-media settlement, and [[AlexandraSuichBass]] on [[ShakespeareAndCompany]] in Paris. The robotics segment is the strongest wiki update: spectacle at the World Humanoid Robot Games points to momentum, but [[HumanoidRobotCommercialization]] still depends on massive physical-world training data, cost control, and useful repeatable tasks.

The settlement segment extends [[SocialMediaProductLiability]] by treating Meta's deal as a template for other platforms, while the bookshop segment extends [[BookstoreSerendipity]] into a high-tourism cultural-retail case where myth, location, scarcity, buying ritual, merchandise, events, and offline community reinforce each other.

## Key Claims
- [[China]] is described as being at the forefront of the humanoid robot industry, with Chinese companies expected to sell around 50,000 humanoid robots this year, roughly triple last year's figure.
- The World Humanoid Robot Games in [[Beijing]] displayed progress and fragility at once: robots ran, boxed, lifted, played football, crashed, fell, and in one case caught fire.
- Useful humanoid robots require more than hardware; the episode says they may need around 100 million hours of real-world movement data to learn safe physical interaction.
- [[RobotTrainingCenters]] are presented as China's main real-machine data strategy, with 53 existing centers, 34 planned centers, the [[HubeiHumanoidInnovationCenter]] in [[Wuhan]], and a larger Shanghai center planned for 1,000 humanoids.
- [[JDCom|JD.com / 京东]] is described as recruiting 100,000 staff and 500,000 outside participants to wear sensor gear, potentially collecting 10 million hours of movement data over two years.
- Today's humanoid robot demand is partly self-referential: many robots are bought by training centers, so current sales can reflect investment in future capability rather than present utility.
- [[Meta]] agreed to pay at least $12 billion and as much as $17 billion over 10 years, with teen restrictions on [[Facebook]] and [[Instagram]] including age assessment, two-hour daily caps, a midnight-to-6am blackout, notification limits, hidden likes, and chronological-feed options.
- The settlement is framed as a template for [[TikTok]], [[YouTube]], [[Snap]], and other platforms, with Pennsylvania's suit against [[Snapchat]] cited as an immediate follow-on signal.
- [[ShakespeareAndCompany]] draws 2,000 to 3,000 visitors per day because its Paris location, literary inheritance, photo ban, book stamp, merchandise, events, and mythology make the shop more than an inventory point.

## Key Quotes
> "real-time machine data" - Weinland's label for movement traces collected from actual humanoid robots.

> "egocentric data" - the human first-person sensor data route used for robot training.

> "big tobacco" - the analogy used for social-media litigation, with financial and business-model limits preserved.

> "2,000 to 3,000 visitors per day" - the bookshop's reported visitor volume.
