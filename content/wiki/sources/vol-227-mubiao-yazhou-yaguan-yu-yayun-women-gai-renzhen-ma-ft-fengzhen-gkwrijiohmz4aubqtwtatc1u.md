## Summary
This [[Budongqiu|不懂球]] episode with football commentator [[FengZhenFootballCommentator|丰臻]] argues that Chinese football should treat the Asian Games and [[AFCChampionsLeague|AFC Champions League]] as practical competitive steps rather than dismissing them while fixating on the World Cup. Its central synthesis is that [[ChineseFootballAsianCompetitionPriority|Asian competition priority]] must join ambition to institutional support: clubs owe effort first to themselves and their supporters, while league scheduling, match supply, foreign-player depth, and continental incentives determine whether that effort is sustainable. The discussion also extends [[NaturalizedPlayerPolicyInChineseFootball|naturalization]] as a possible short-term national-team tool without presenting it as a substitute for league and player-development reform.

## Key Claims
- China currently lacks the competitive margin to treat Asian-level national-team events as disposable development fixtures; results can build experience and confidence, although using over-age players also raises expectations.
- Supporting national teams should not mean repeatedly suspending professional leagues for long camps or multi-sport events, because irregular scheduling weakens professionalization and market continuity.
- [[AFCChampionsLeague|AFC Champions League]] participation is primarily a club-and-supporter responsibility; league coefficient and reputation gains follow from clubs pursuing their own competitive goals.
- Continental performance depends on incentives and structure as well as attitude: squad depth, draws, domestic title pressure, travel cost, prize money, and schedule coordination all affect club choices.
- [[ChineseSuperLeague|Chinese Super League]] expansion and a steadier supply of meaningful matches are presented as more urgent than immediate cross-year-calendar reform.
- [[ChineseFootballForeignPlayerPolicy|Foreign-player rules]] could allow more registered players while retaining a lower matchday limit, improving injury resilience and competitive quality without necessarily returning to uncontrolled spending.
- The source treats [[NaturalizedPlayerPolicyInChineseFootball|naturalized players]] as a realistic short-term route for raising the national team's ceiling, while acknowledging administrative caution after earlier disappointing outcomes.
- The broader claim is that World Cup qualification should emerge from accumulated league, club, and Asian-competition strength rather than being pursued apart from those foundations.
