# 621. The Nazis at War: Blitzkrieg (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[NorwegianCampaign1940|German invasions of Denmark and Norway]], the Norway Debate that brought [[WinstonChurchill]] to the premiership, and the opening German breakthrough in the West. It presents Norway as a contest over Swedish iron ore, Narvik, and naval position in which Norwegian resistance imposed serious losses but poorly prepared Allied intervention failed.

The western campaign then qualifies the image of effortless German technological supremacy. Allied and German material strength was closer than later myth suggests; the decisive difference lay in surprise, operational concentration through the Ardennes, air-ground coordination, movement speed, and an Allied command system unable to recognize and answer the breakthrough before German forces reached the Channel.

## Key Claims

- Narvik's ice-free route for Swedish iron ore and the fear that the other side would move first made neutral Norway strategically important to both Germany and the Allies.
- The sinking of the Blücher delayed the German seizure of Oslo long enough for [[HaakonVII|Haakon VII]], the government, and gold reserves to escape; the king then rejected [[VidkunQuisling|Quisling's]] proposed puppet government.
- Denmark capitulated within hours because geography and military imbalance made sustained resistance impracticable, whereas Norway fought for roughly two months and forced substantial German naval and air losses.
- Allied intervention in Norway suffered from late decisions, poor intelligence, inadequate equipment, and weak coordination; the resulting parliamentary crisis removed Chamberlain and elevated Churchill despite Churchill's own responsibility for the operation.
- France and Germany were more evenly matched in tanks, artillery, and general force than the later mechanized-war legend implies; much of the German army still depended on horses.
- [[ErichVonManstein|Manstein's]] and [[HeinzGuderian|Guderian's]] Ardennes plan used the attack into the Low Countries to draw Allied armies north while armored forces crossed the Meuse and turned toward the Channel.
- The Allied collapse grew from planning assumptions, missed opportunities against congested German columns, slow command response, weak air-ground coordination, civilian flight, and loss of strategic control rather than material inferiority alone.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode reinforces [[43-1940-glt2702093158]] by explaining German victory through concentrated combined action and faster decisions rather than overwhelming equipment superiority.
- The episode's account of the Norway campaign distinguishes Norwegian military resistance from Allied operational failure and does not treat Denmark's rapid capitulation as evidence of cowardice.
- The label [[Blitzkrieg]] is retained as a familiar description, while the source itself shows a risky, congested, only partly mechanized campaign rather than a uniform doctrine of irresistible machines.
- Casualty, force, equipment, aircraft, ship-loss, and refugee figures; remembered dialogue; commander motives; amphetamine distribution; counterfactual Allied air attacks; and claims about campaign duration remain source-scoped.
