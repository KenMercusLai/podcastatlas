# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》310-1｜为什么说康王坟能养活一个省？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode moves from [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘建]]'s death to imperial burial wealth and the saying “打开康王坟，山东不受贫.” It identifies the legend's likely Kang king as [[LiuJiJiaodongKingWesternHan|胶东康王刘寄]], while using [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s Changling and elite grave goods to explain why royal tombs attract treasure stories.

The episode then connects the saying to an attempted robbery in the 六曲山 tomb area near Pingdu, Qingdao around 2000. Police arrest the group before digging, and the suspects reportedly say the proverb drew them there; the durable point is therefore that an unverified wealth legend can still guide real search and criminal behavior, not that the tomb actually contained enough treasure to support Shandong.

## Key Claims

- Royal and imperial tombs could contain precious goods and collected objects, making burial wealth a recurring target of grave robbers.
- The saying “打开康王坟，山东不受贫” imagines the Kang king's tomb as wealthy enough to support an entire province, but the episode itself calls the formulation exaggerated.
- Because history contains several rulers titled Kang, the saying does not identify its subject by title alone.
- When the saying is tied to Shandong, the episode points to [[LiuJiJiaodongKingWesternHan|胶东康王刘寄]]; the supplied summary writes his name as 刘继.
- Around 2000, a group reportedly marked and approached a large tomb in the 六曲山 area at night before Qingdao police arrested them.
- The episode says the area contained more than 360 ancient tombs, many reputed to belong to nobles, but supplies no archaeological report for that number or for the target tomb's identity.
- The suspects reportedly said the Kang-tomb saying attracted them, making the proverb an example of [[TombWealthLegendRobberyIncentive|陵墓财富传说的盗掘激励]].

## Key Quotes

> “打开康王坟，山东不受贫” - the wealth proverb whose exaggeration and behavioral afterlife organize the episode.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The identification of the Kang king is compatible with the existing Liu Ji page after normalizing the summary's 刘继 to 刘寄, but this episode does not establish the tomb's location or archaeological identity.
- The proverb is evidence of a wealth legend, not evidence that opening one tomb could support Shandong or that the claimed treasure survived intact.
- The dating and operational details of the arrest, the number and status of the Liuchu Mountain tombs, the suspects' quoted motive, and Guo Moruo's interest are episode-attributed pending police, archaeological, or documentary sources.
- The place name is rendered “六曲山” in the supplied summary. No independent location normalization is attempted here.
- The source is a compact opening installment. It raises the identity, location, and wealth questions but does not yet answer them through excavation evidence or primary historical records.
