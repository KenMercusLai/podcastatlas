# How to Learn Skills Faster

## Summary
This early [[HubermanLab]] solo episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] organize motor-skill learning around safe repetition density, informative errors, stage-matched attention, quiet post-practice consolidation, sleep, and supplemental mental rehearsal. It supplies the full-length provenance for the later Essentials edit and reinforces [[MotorSkillRepetitionDensity]], [[StageMatchedMotorSkillPractice]], [[PostPracticeMotorConsolidation]], and [[MentalPracticeAndVisualization]]. The practical synthesis is bounded: physical practice remains primary, later-stage tools depend on task and proficiency, and the episode's thresholds, mechanisms, supplement guidance, and study percentages remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- Motor learning is framed around repetitions per unit time rather than elapsed hours alone, provided attempts remain safe and attentive enough to produce useful feedback.
- Open-loop skills use discrete attempts and delayed outcome feedback, while closed-loop skills require continuous adjustment during ongoing movement; practice design should reflect that difference.
- Errors are presented as attention and correction signals, while feedback that feels punitive can reduce persistence and therefore reduce the number of learning attempts.
- [[StageMatchedMotorSkillPractice]] begins with ordinary-speed attempts and later adds feature-specific attention, slow-motion work, or metronome pressure after basic control emerges.
- [[PostPracticeMotorConsolidation]] treats brief quiet wakefulness and later sleep as part of practice by reducing interference and supporting replay, although the source's directional replay mechanism is not established here.
- [[MentalPracticeAndVisualization]] can recruit movement-command pathways and improve some outcomes, but it omits proprioceptive error, muscle activation, spinal patterning, and real-world consequences.
- Caffeine and Alpha GPC are presented only as possible pre-practice performance supports; neither replaces repetitions, and any stimulant benefit must be weighed against sleep disruption and individual risk.
