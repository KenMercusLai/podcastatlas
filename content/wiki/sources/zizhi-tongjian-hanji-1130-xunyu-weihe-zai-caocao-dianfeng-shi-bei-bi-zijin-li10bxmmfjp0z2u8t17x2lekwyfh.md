# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1130丨荀彧为何在曹操巅峰时被逼自尽？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[XunYu|荀彧]]'s opposition to [[CaoCao|曹操]] becoming Duke of Wei and receiving the [[JiuXiRitualPromotion|九锡礼遇]] as the final rupture between two former partners. Xun Yu continues to defend the Han restoration language that once helped Cao Cao build legitimacy, while Cao Cao's accumulated military and political power has moved beyond ordinary ministerial status.

The episode then treats the reported empty food box as [[ObjectMediatedPoliticalUltimatum|器物式政治最后通牒]] and reads Xun Yu's death as both political defeat and an attempted defense of principle. It closes with a historiographical caution: the box's meaning, Cao Cao's intent, Xun Yu's motive, and the moral value of loyalty to a failing dynasty cannot be settled by one dramatic narrative.

## Key Claims

- In 212 CE, Dong Zhao's proposal that Cao Cao become Duke of Wei and receive the Nine Bestowments makes the gap between exceptional ministerial power and possible dynastic replacement publicly visible.
- Xun Yu opposes the proposal by invoking Cao Cao's original commitment to stabilize and restore the Han order and by insisting on the ruler-minister distinction.
- The episode interprets their break as a conflict between a legitimacy program Xun Yu still treats as binding and a power structure Cao Cao now treats as obsolete.
- Cao Cao has Xun Yu moved away from the central court during the eastern campaign; Xun Yu later remains ill at Shouchun.
- In the source's dramatic reading, an empty food box says without words that Xun Yu's food, office, political relationship, or life has reached its end.
- Xun Yu's subsequent death is framed as political defeat joined to an attempt to preserve loyalty and moral consistency, not as proof that his whole career was free of contradiction.
- The episode preserves two unresolved ethical questions: whether Xun Yu's final stand repairs his earlier role in using the emperor for Cao Cao's advantage, and whether loyalty to the Han protected the public or elevated dynastic fidelity above public welfare.
- The closing “Rashomon” comparison warns that later accounts of the box, suicide, motive, and coercion are viewpoint-shaped and require source criticism.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source changes the judgment of Xun Yu from a consistently effective Cao-side adviser to a partner whose original Han-legitimacy program eventually conflicts with Cao Cao's elevation.
- Evidentiary caution: the empty box, its exact meaning, whether Cao Cao issued a direct death command, whether Xun Yu died by poison or illness, private motives, exact dialogue, and the sequence at Qiao, Ruxu, Shouchun, and court require direct comparison among historical records.
- Ethical caution: Xun Yu's final opposition may show consistency, but it does not by itself resolve whether his earlier service strengthened the very power that displaced the Han court or whether dynastic loyalty served the population better than continued participation in Cao Cao's order.
