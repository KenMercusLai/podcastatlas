# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》309-2｜荒淫之极的他，竟害闺女远嫁80岁老头

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode profiles [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘建]] through allegations of sexual misconduct, deliberate cruelty, wrongful killing, curse ritual, weapons accumulation, forged imperial and military instruments, and threatened rebellion. Once the evidence is discovered, Liu Jian kills himself, his queen is executed, and Jiangdu is abolished and incorporated as Guangling Commandery.

The second half turns from the offender to his daughter [[LiuXijunPrincessWesternHan|刘细君]]. The episode treats her politically weakened status and marriage to the ruler of [[Wusun|乌孙]], her attributed 《悲愁歌》, homesickness, illness, and early death as collateral consequences of a father's conduct, then extends that history into a modern family-ethics warning. That causal interpretation is emotionally central to the episode but stronger than the compact evidence alone can establish.

## Key Claims

- Liu Jian is identified as Liu Fei's son, Han Jingdi's grandson, and king of Jiangdu.
- The episode alleges that Liu Jian had sexual relations with a consort favored by his father and with his sister Liu Zhengchen.
- It narrates a lake incident in which Liu Jian laughs at two drowning guards, forbids rescue, and lets them die; it also attributes nearly thirty-five wrongful killings to him.
- Fear of punishment leads Liu Jian and his queen toward curse ritual and preparations for rebellion, including weapons, a private jade seal, forged official seals and tallies, ribbons, and reward documents.
- After investigators find the material, Liu Jian kills himself, the queen is executed and exposed in the marketplace, Jiangdu is abolished, and its territory becomes Guangling Commandery.
- The episode corrects a tomb-identification rumor by saying the Dayunshan tomb belongs to Liu Jian's father Liu Fei rather than Liu Jian.
- Liu Xijun is sent to Wusun after becoming a politically exposed offender's daughter; the episode presents her as a genuine Han royal princess and an important successful heqin case.
- Her attributed 《悲愁歌》 expresses displacement and longing for home; the episode says she remains unhappy and dies from illness not long afterward.
- The closing interpretation makes Xijun's fate an example of parental misconduct harming children across a generation.

## Key Quotes

> “许多儿女的不幸来自家庭中不靠谱的父母” - the host's modern family-ethics conclusion as summarized by the source.

## Contradictions

- No direct contradiction is adopted. The rebellion preparation, suicide, and Xijun lineage align with the existing Hanji 382-2 and Hanji 370-1 source layers while adding detail.
- The title's “80岁老头” is not substantiated with an age in the episode body and remains headline rhetoric rather than settled biography.
- The sexual allegations, lake episode, nearly thirty-five wrongful killings, exact seized objects, queen's role and punishment, administrative conversion, and tomb correction are bounded to this compact retelling pending comparison with the cited 《汉书》 passage and archaeological reporting.
- The claims that Xijun was the first genuine Han heqin princess and the first successful heqin figure remain episode-attributed historical-priority claims.
- 《悲愁歌》 is attributed to Xijun by the episode, but the source supplies only a thematic description rather than the poem's text or transmission history.
- Xijun's cause and timing of death and the claim that Liu Jian's wrongdoing caused her selection, unhappiness, illness, or death are not established as a single factual chain here. The final parent-child lesson is commentary, not an independent historical finding.
