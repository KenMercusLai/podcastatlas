# No.224 用 AI「偷」明星声音和形象侵权卖货，直播间该怎么管？

## Summary
This [[SanWuHuan|三五环]] episode has [[LiuFei|刘飞]] interviewing [[ZhuWeiLegalScholar|朱威]] and [[JiaxuDouyinEcommerce|嘉旭]] about infringement governance in [[DouyinEcommerce|抖音电商]]. It turns AI-enabled celebrity likeness, voice, name, account, shop-decoration, product-detail, and live-room imitation into a concrete [[AIEcommerceInfringementGovernance]] problem rather than only a generic deepfake concern.

The durable contribution is a platform-operations account of rights enforcement under low-cost generation. The episode argues that AI changes the cost and ambiguity of infringement, while the legal and product questions remain grounded in consent, authorization scope, consumer confusion, proof, false positives, and whether the platform can maintain reliable [[PlatformRightsPreReview]] without becoming the sole judge of every offline contract.

## Key Claims
- [[JiaxuDouyinEcommerce|嘉旭]] says infringement in Douyin ecommerce can involve portrait, voice, name, reputation, trademark, product-detail, account, and live-room signals that mislead consumers into believing a celebrity, creator, or brand is endorsing a product.
- The episode describes tactics moving from direct reuse of celebrity livestream clips toward AI face changes, masks, cropped faces, voice substitution, nickname hints, emoji signals, and live-room style imitation.
- [[ZhuWeiLegalScholar|朱威]] separates explicit rights such as personality rights and trademarks from broader protected interests such as consumers' fair-transaction interest in truthful identity and endorsement signals.
- [[PlatformRightsPreReview]] appears in Douyin ecommerce through rights-owner submissions of names, nicknames, aliases, portrait information, voice information, and authorization relationships that can support active blocking before publication or sale.
- AI lowers imitation cost and makes consumer confusion easier in immersive live-commerce settings, especially when sellers choose product categories already associated with a copied celebrity or creator.
- Authorization is not binary: [[PlatformAuthorizationBoundary]] depends on contract parties, platform, time period, product category, account, MCN or agency transfer, deep-synthesis scope, and public-order limits.
- False positives remain a governance cost: similar-looking ordinary users, model misidentification, and legitimate but unsubmitted authorization can all trigger mistaken enforcement.
- The source treats AI as a neutral tool for commerce, review, and user verification; the relevant boundary is whether use is infringing, deceptive, illegal, or platform-rule violating.
