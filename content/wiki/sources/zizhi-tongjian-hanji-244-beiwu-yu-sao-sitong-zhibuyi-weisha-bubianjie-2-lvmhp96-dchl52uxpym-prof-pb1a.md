# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》244｜被诬与嫂私通，直不疑为啥不辩解？（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]] downfall preview left unresolved by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-244-beiwu-yu-sao-sitong-zhibuyi-weisha-bubianjie-1-lpqwsteakynxl2ms1vypqay1w5r5|Hanji 244 part 1]]. It moves from [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s meal without chopsticks, through the purchase of discarded armor and shields as burial goods and a laborer's report, to interrogation, a rebellion accusation, and Zhou's death after five days without food.

The host explains the ending through accumulated political enmity and Zhou's inflexible manner: he had opposed succession change, refused [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]] immediate military relief, and resisted enfeoffing Empress Wang's brother. The durable synthesis is narrower than that personality verdict. Court ritual, prior trust loss, a family member's misconduct, ambiguous military objects, and a hostile prosecution can compound into a lethal case, while the emperor's intention, the legal sufficiency of the accusation, and physiognomic prediction remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- Jingdi serves Zhou meat without chopsticks; Zhou asks an attendant for chopsticks, reacts with displeasure, and leaves quickly after a terse exchange that the host interprets as an imperial test.
- Zhou's son purchases five hundred discarded suits of armor and shields for burial, then allegedly withholds transport wages and beats workers; a worker's named report brings the goods to official attention.
- Zhou initially refuses to answer an investigating official, is arrested, and says the objects were intended for burial rather than rebellion.
- The chief judicial official reportedly recasts even underground use of the burial goods as rebellion, subjects Zhou to harsh interrogation, and closes off the distinction Zhou offers.
- After an earlier suicide attempt is stopped by his wife, Zhou refuses food for five days and dies after vomiting blood in prison.
- The host places the case after accumulated disputes involving the former crown prince Liu Rong, Liu Wu, Dowager Dou, and Empress Wang's brother Wang Xin, but does not independently establish that each grievance caused the prosecution.
- The episode contrasts battlefield directness with court survival and treats Zhou's unchanged rigidity as politically costly; this is an interpretive lesson rather than proof that personality alone caused his death.
- The closing [[XuFuPhysiognomist|许负]] story presents prison self-starvation as fulfillment of a prediction that Zhou would eventually die of hunger.

## Key Quotes

> “此不足君所乎？” - Jingdi's rebuke after Zhou asks an attendant to fetch chopsticks.

> “君侯纵不反地上，即欲反地下耳。” - the judicial official's reported move from burial explanation to an unfalsifiable rebellion accusation.

## Contradictions

- This episode resolves part 1's transcript gap by narrating the banquet and later death, but it does not verify the host's stronger claim that the meal proves Jingdi had already decided to kill Zhou.
- Jingdi's leniency instructions in part 1 sit uneasily beside the hostile and expansive prosecution described here. The two sources support a policy-versus-personalized-enforcement tension, not a settled contradiction in chronology or a proven single motive.
- The episode marks many details as fact while separately labeling the emperor's test, the son's role in making the case irretrievable, accumulated resentment, and personality causation as inference; this note preserves that distinction.
- Armor count and legal status, interrogation wording, use of torture, duration and physiology of the fast, exact succession and enfeoffment disputes, “刻薄寡恩,” and the Xu Fu prediction require comparison with primary and modern historical evidence.
