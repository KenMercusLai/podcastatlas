# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1141丨精于算计的韩遂，为何在曹操面前不堪一击？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode retells [[HanSui|韩遂]]'s career from coerced entry into the Liangzhou rebellion through violent consolidation, unstable partnership with [[MaTeng|马腾]], defeat beside [[MaChao|马超]] in the [[WeinanBattleLateHan|渭南之战]], and final collapse under [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]]'s western pressure. Its durable synthesis is that Han Sui's skill at bargaining, switching, and exploiting fragmented local coalitions did not create a stable command or political base able to withstand an opponent coordinating legitimacy, logistics, alliance fracture, and force.

The episode also compares Han Sui with the Tang commander Li Huaiguang to frame a recurring tension between central governments and regional military strongmen: each side may need the other against immediate threats while continuing to fear the other's independent coercive capacity. The analogy is useful as a bounded structural comparison, not proof that the two careers had the same causes or sequence.

## Key Claims

- The episode presents Han Sui, originally Han Yue, as a local official coerced by [[BeigongBoyuLateHan|北宫伯玉]]'s rebels before he accepts the new military arena and changes his name.
- Han Sui reportedly grows by navigating Liangzhou groups and eliminating or absorbing [[BianZhangLateHan|边章]], Beigong Boyu, and [[LiWenhouLateHan|李文侯]], turning coercive entry into independent command.
- Han Sui and [[MaTeng|马腾]] cooperate because Ma Teng contributes local standing while Han Sui contributes a larger force, but disputes over interests repeatedly turn the partnership into armed conflict.
- After Ma Teng enters court and Ma Chao inherits field command, Han Sui joins Ma Chao against Cao Cao; public familiarity and an altered letter then deepen suspicion inside the coalition.
- The episode treats the coalition's defeat as a failure of trust and command integration, not evidence that Han Sui lacked all tactical or political skill.
- After the Weinan defeat, Han Sui retreats west while Ma Chao goes to [[Hanzhong|汉中]] under [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]].
- Xiahou Yuan reportedly attacks the Changli Qiang rather than Han Sui directly, forcing Han Sui to choose between losing Qiang support and accepting an unfavorable field battle.
- The source says Han Sui's subordinates Qu Yan and Jiang Shi eventually kill him and send his head to Cao Cao, while his descendants at Ye are also killed; the exact death mechanism requires comparison with primary records.
- The Li Huaiguang comparison argues that a central government may recruit a regional military strongman for immediate security while still treating his independent following as a long-run threat.
- The episode's title contrast extends [[TacticalStrategicCapabilityGap|战术—战略能力落差]]: local calculation and coalition maneuver do not equal the institutional capacity to preserve trust, supplies, legitimacy, and a renewable base.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode compresses multiple decades into a single rise-and-fall arc and often presents one causal interpretation where the existing wiki preserves coercion, institutional collapse, resource asymmetry, and shifting frontier incentives.
- Death caution: the claim that Qu Yan and Jiang Shi personally kill Han Sui and send his head to Cao Cao is retained as source-scoped pending comparison with primary records and alternative death accounts.
- Attribution caution: Han Sui's name change, private motives, force totals, elimination of peer leaders, killing of Ma Teng's family, dialogue, Qiang dependency, and the intentions of his subordinates require broader verification.
- Analogy caution: Li Huaiguang is not made a canonical page from this single comparison. Different dynastic institutions, offices, military systems, and immediate causes limit the analogy.
- Source-form caution: the supplied file is a structured popular-history summary rather than a transcript, critical edition, or primary-source study; dramatized dialogue and psychological claims are not treated as settled fact.
