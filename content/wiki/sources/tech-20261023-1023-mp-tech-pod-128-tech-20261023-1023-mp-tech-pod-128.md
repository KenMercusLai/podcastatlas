# iPhone Duo's crease is put to the test

## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] week-in-review episode has [[JoannaStern]] connect three technology-business stories: a 32,000-fold test of [[IPhoneDuo|iPhone Duo]], a possible [[SpaceX]]–[[Tesla]] merger within [[ElonMusk]]'s expanding company network, and unsealed materials describing internal [[TikTok]] child-safety warnings. The episode's strongest shared theme is accountability: novel hardware must prove useful beyond spectacle, corporate integration must be weighed against concentrated private power, and platform safety claims must be tested against internal knowledge and product decisions.

## Key Claims
- Stern's purpose-built test opened and closed [[IPhoneDuo|iPhone Duo]] more than 32,000 times without a significant change in hinge operation, although a groove remained visible under some angles and backgrounds.
- At just under $2,000, with two rear cameras and no Face ID, the first-generation Duo is framed as an enthusiast product whose larger screen is already useful for reading and video but does not yet replace an iPad for work.
- [[FoldablePhoneProductivity]] depends on developer support: low-adaptation reading and video already benefit from the display, while serious work requires applications designed for the folding canvas.
- The episode reads Duo, Home View, and a possible touchscreen laptop as signs that [[Apple]] is more willing to enter established rival categories and accept visible first-generation compromises.
- A possible SpaceX–Tesla merger could simplify management and connect AI, autonomy, vehicles, satellite infrastructure, and space ambitions, but would also intensify shareholder-conflict and private-power concerns.
- Reporting that Musk ordered slower military [[Starlink]] service in [[Ukraine]] remains explicitly unverified and disputed; the White House reportedly denied directing such action.
- Unsealed depositions and internal documents reportedly show TikTok employees warning leaders about sexual exploitation, harmful content, age-verification weaknesses, and parental-control loopholes, while TikTok says plaintiffs presented the materials selectively.
- Mounting litigation may make preventive youth-safety changes cheaper than repeated lawsuits and reputational damage, even if near-term financial effects remain manageable for large platforms.

## Key Quotes
> "Big Tobacco" - the episode's analogy for a possible shift toward treating foreseeable platform harms as corporate responsibility.

> "one SpaceX share to every child in America" - Musk's source-reported proposal, for which the episode says few details were available.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode's 32,000-fold result is an early controlled test, not long-term ownership evidence, and it does not remove the visible groove.
- The reported Starlink slowdown is unverified and disputed, so it cannot establish that Musk or SpaceX deliberately constrained Ukrainian military service.
- TikTok says the unsealed materials were selectively presented and did not show its complete response; the episode does not independently resolve that evidentiary dispute.
