# 669. Greece vs. Persia: The Battle of Marathon (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the [[BattleOfMarathon|Battle of Marathon]] as a risky Athenian decision to leave a defensible blocking position and attack the [[AchaemenidEmpire|Persian]] army while its cavalry was absent. It follows [[Miltiades]]' containment strategy, the Plataean alliance, the reported intelligence from Ionian conscripts, the hoplite charge, the fighting at the ships, and the forced march back to [[Athens]] before the Persian fleet could exploit possible betrayal.

The episode's strongest wiki contribution is a qualified account of Marathon's significance. The hosts preserve the battle's importance for [[AthenianDemocracy]] and later [[GrecoPersianWarMemory]], but they also resist a simple freedom-versus-despotism story by stressing Persian imperial perspective, Athenian later empire, source dependence on [[Herodotus]], and the mythic afterlife around Pheidippides and Pan.

## Key Claims

- [[BattleOfMarathon]] was not an immediate frontal clash; the Athenians first blocked the road from Marathon to Athens and avoided open ground while Persian cavalry was active.
- [[Miltiades]]' decisive move was to attack during a narrow window when the Persian cavalry had left the plain and the Persian force appeared divided.
- The Athenian line used strong wings and a thinned center, allowing the wings to break the Persian flanks and then turn inward on the center.
- The battle was not fully won until the exhausted Athenians marched back to [[Athens]] and deterred the Persian fleet from landing at Phalerum.
- Marathon mattered because an Athenian defeat might have destroyed, enslaved, or exiled the city before its later democratic and cultural influence matured.
- [[GrecoPersianWarMemory]] later turned Marathon into a civilizational freedom story, but the source treats that framing as powerful and reductive.
- The reconstruction remains source-scoped because [[Herodotus]] dominates the surviving Greek evidence and no comparable Persian narrative survives.

## Key Quotes

> "freedom over despotism" - the later ideological frame the episode explicitly complicates.
