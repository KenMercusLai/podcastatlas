# Curing All Human Diseases & the Future of Health & Technology | Mark Zuckerberg & Dr. Priscilla Chan

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab|Huberman Lab]] conversation with [[PriscillaChan|Priscilla Chan]] and [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] presents the [[ChanZuckerbergInitiative|Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]] as a long-horizon biomedical infrastructure effort: fund basic science, support open dissemination, build reusable software and hardware, and organize interdisciplinary [[ChanZuckerbergBiohubNetwork|Biohubs]] around problems too large for one laboratory. Single-cell atlases, [[Cellxgene]], imaging tools, and nonprofit AI compute support a proposed [[VirtualCellWorldModel|virtual-cell]] path, but the guests repeatedly retain experimental and human-translation boundaries.

The second half treats social media, [[MixedReality|mixed reality]], smart glasses, embodied computing, and creator-controlled AI assistants as design problems rather than inherently beneficial or harmful technologies. Zuckerberg argues that connection quality, user control, safety defaults, contextual controls, physical engagement, privacy signals, affordability, and creator authorization determine whether new interfaces improve experience; the interview remains largely optimistic and does not independently test Meta's product, safety, or health claims.

## Key Claims
- CZI's stated century-scale goal is to help scientists cure, prevent, or manage all diseases by funding research, building shared tools, and operating interdisciplinary Biohubs rather than producing every therapy itself.
- [[BiomedicalResearchToolInfrastructure]] treats measurement, imaging, data access, and experimental instrumentation as prerequisites for faster discovery; examples include Napari, [[Cellxgene]], electron microscopy, and phase-plate development.
- [[SingleCellRNASequencing|Single-cell methods]] and atlases can reveal cell types and gene-expression patterns hidden by bulk measurements, while large datasets create a need for computational interpretation.
- CZI's proposed virtual-cell work uses transformer-like architectures and biological data to generate hypotheses about cell states, disease, drugs, and interactions, but computation is framed as an accelerator rather than a replacement for experiments or human validation.
- The Biohub model joins independent, focused teams with university collaborators in San Francisco, Chicago, and New York to pursue cell mapping, tissue sensing, and engineered immune-cell reporting.
- Zuckerberg says social media is more beneficial when it supports meaningful connection than when it produces passive, negative consumption, and describes safety defaults, parental oversight, nudges, blocking, and restricting as complementary controls.
- Mixed reality is framed as whole-body computing for exercise, training, education, creativity, and spatial work, while smart glasses are positioned as a lower-friction interface for audio, capture, and context-aware AI.
- AI versions of creators should remain controlled by the represented person and should not enable impersonation.

## Key Quotes
> "give scientists the tools" — Zuckerberg's description of CZI's role rather than a claim that it will directly produce every cure.

> "computing for your whole body" — Zuckerberg's contrast between mixed reality and earlier mind-centered computing devices.

> "not gospel" — the discussion's boundary for treating generative biological model outputs as hypotheses rather than established facts.
