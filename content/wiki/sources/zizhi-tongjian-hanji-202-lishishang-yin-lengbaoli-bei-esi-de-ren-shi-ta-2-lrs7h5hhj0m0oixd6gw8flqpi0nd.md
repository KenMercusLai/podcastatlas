# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》202｜历史上因冷暴力被饿死的人是他（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents two linked forms of Lü-regency power. [[LiuHuiZhaoKingWesternHan|赵王刘恢]] is moved from Liang to Zhao, married to [[LuChanWesternHan|吕产]]'s daughter, watched by her Lü-family entourage, and later dies by suicide after his favored concubine is poisoned; [[LiuZeLangyeKingWesternHan|刘泽]] uses kinship, military merit, marriage, office, money, and [[TianZichunWesternHan|田子春]]'s indirect palace access in an unfinished attempt to rise from marquis to king.

The durable synthesis is that royal or marquis rank does not itself secure autonomy. A marriage can become a household surveillance and control structure, while advancement can depend on a broker who first builds access through the chief eunuch's trust network. The episode stops before Tian's plan succeeds, so Liu Ze's eventual kingship is not attributed to this setup alone.

## Key Claims

- [[LuZhi|吕雉]] moves Liu Hui from Liang to Zhao, appoints Lü Chan as king of Liang while keeping him at court as grand tutor, and later renames Liang as Lü in the episode's account.
- Liu Hui's marriage to Lü Chan's daughter is presented as [[CoercedImperialMarriage|强制赐婚]] joined to household surveillance: the queen's Lü-aligned attendants exercise power, investigate him, and report his conduct.
- After the queen has Liu Hui's favored concubine poisoned, Liu Hui dies by suicide; Lu Zhi then reportedly blocks his descendants from inheriting because he ended the ancestral line over a woman.
- Liu Ze is presented as a distant Liu relative, a veteran who fought under [[FanKuai|樊哙]], captured [[WangHuang|王黄]], and received the Yingling marquisate.
- Liu Ze's marriage to the daughter of [[LuXuWesternHan|吕媭]] and Fan Kuai places him across Liu-family, Lü-family, and founding-merit networks, while his later offices include palace-guard and general commands in the episode's compressed biography.
- Seeking promotion from marquis to king, Liu Ze gives Tian Zichun two hundred jin of gold; Tian returns to Qi and delays for two years until Liu Ze's threatening letter pushes him to act.
- Tian does not petition directly. He rents a conspicuous Chang'an residence, cultivates elite visibility, and places his son under a trusted chief eunuch, creating an [[InnerCourtAccessBrokerageRisk|inner-court access]] route.
- The episode ends before the actual kingship maneuver, so the efficacy, legality, and causal weight of Tian's access strategy remain unresolved.

## Key Quotes

The supplied episode document is a structured summary rather than a verbatim transcript, so no sentence is promoted as an exact quotation. Its central reported formulations concern Liu Hui's loss of the will to live, Lu Zhi's condemnation of him for ending ancestral sacrifice over a woman, and Liu Ze's warning that Tian Zichun could not simply abandon their relationship.

## Contradictions

- The title says the episode concerns a person starved to death through "cold violence," but the supplied body says the previously discussed Zhao king Liu You was starved and says Liu Hui dies by suicide after his favored concubine is poisoned. The title is therefore not treated as a reliable description of Liu Hui's death.
- No settled contradiction with the existing Liu Ze page was found. This source backfills his pre-kingship career, marriage, and lobbying attempt before Hanji 206 presents him as king of Langye.
- Liu Hui's emotions, the queen's motive, the attendants' reports, Lu Zhi's reasoning, Liu Ze's genealogy and office history, the exact gold amount, Tian's motive for delaying, the threatening force of Liu Ze's letter, the eunuch's identity, and all dialogue remain episode-attributed.
- The episode ends before Tian Zichun executes the decisive petition or Liu Ze receives a royal title; later status does not by itself prove this particular access chain caused the grant.
