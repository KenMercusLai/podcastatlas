# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》266｜“死灰复燃”与一泡尿的故事（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the prison anecdote begun in part 1: [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] is restored to high office, summons the jailer [[TianJiaJailerWesternHan|田甲]] who had humiliated him, answers the earlier urination taunt, and then declines to punish him. It uses that reversal to argue against despair in temporary defeat and against abusing people whose fortunes may change.

The episode then gives Han a mixed profile—strategically capable, fair-minded, tolerant, practical, and money-seeking—before a riding injury reportedly blocks his expected elevation from acting chancellor and opens the office to [[XueZeWesternHan|薛泽]]. Han's opposition to war with the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]], despite continued military trust from [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], adds an earlier layer to [[PoliticalMilitaryEraFit|政军能力的时代适配]] without proving that he lacked ability.

## Key Claims

- Empress Dowager Dou reportedly prompts Liu Wu to appoint the imprisoned Han Anguo as Liang's civil-administration official, restoring him to high office.
- Tian Jia flees because he fears retaliation, returns after Han threatens his family, and is ultimately spared after Han repeats the “rekindled ash” and urination exchange.
- The host treats the anecdote as a warning not to abandon hope and not to humiliate someone merely because that person is temporarily powerless.
- Han is characterized as strategically capable and broad-minded but also money-seeking; the episode separates his personal flaw from his ability to recommend upright talent.
- After Tian Fen's death, Han serves as acting chancellor but reportedly loses the permanent appointment after falling from a horse and breaking a leg.
- Xue Ze is appointed chancellor instead and is portrayed as surviving roughly seven years in office through low visibility, few achievements, and no major fault under an autocratic ruler.
- Han opposes offensive war against the Xiongnu, yet Wudi still gives him command, which the host reads as evidence that policy disagreement did not erase imperial confidence in his military ability.

## Key Quotes

> “死灰复燃了，你可以撒尿了。” - Han Anguo's taunt to Tian Jia after returning to office, as rendered by the episode.

> “塞翁失马，焉知非福。” - the host's frame for Han's lost chancellorship and later mismatch with Wudi's Xiongnu policy.

## Connections

- [[HanAnguoWesternHan|韩安国]] - restored Liang official whose restraint after recovery, mixed character, injury, and strategic disagreement form the episode's main arc.
- [[TianJiaJailerWesternHan|田甲]] - jailer who humiliates Han in prison, fears revenge after Han's restoration, and is ultimately spared.
- [[XueZeWesternHan|薛泽]] - official who becomes chancellor after Han's injury and is presented as surviving through caution and lack of major fault.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler whose chancellorial danger and aggressive Xiongnu policy define the episode's political environment.
- [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - opponent at the center of the policy disagreement between Han Anguo and Wudi.
- [[PoliticalMilitaryEraFit|政军能力的时代适配]] - framework clarified by the coexistence of strategic disagreement, continuing military trust, accidental injury, and changed advancement prospects.
- [[StrategicForbearanceGovernance|退让留余地式治理]] - adjacent restraint pattern; Han's personal decision not to retaliate preserves proportion after the power reversal.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the current wiki was found. Part 2 completes part 1's explicitly unfinished rehabilitation sequence, while Hanji 275's description of Han as money-seeking and ill-fitted to Wudi's frontier order is compatible with the mixed profile here.
- Dialogue caution: the exact threats, urination wording, movements, motives, and decision not to punish Tian Jia remain episode-attributed and require comparison with the transmitted records.
- Office and disability caution: the claim that Han's broken leg made him unsuitable to represent the court as chancellor is the host's explanation, not an independently established appointment rule or imperial motive.
- Xue Ze caution: the tenure length, absence of a biography, lack of achievement, lack of major fault, knowledge of Wudi's autocracy, and survival-by-caution interpretation remain source-scoped.
- Frontier-policy caution: the source establishes the host's account of Han's opposition to war and Wudi's continued command assignment, but it does not detail the disputed strategy, campaign, appointment, or chronology.
- Chronology caution: the source compresses Han's restoration, Tian Fen's death, the acting chancellorship, injury, Xue Ze's tenure, Han's recovery, and later command; exact dates and causal order should not be reconstructed from this short episode alone.
