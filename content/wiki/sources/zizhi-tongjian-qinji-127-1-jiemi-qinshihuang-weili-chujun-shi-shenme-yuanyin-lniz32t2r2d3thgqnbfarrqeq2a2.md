## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s response to late omens through his fifth and final tour, worsening illness, and death at [[Shaqiu|沙丘]]. Its central argument is that the emperor's aversion to discussing death helped leave the succession unsettled: only when he believed he was dying did he order [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] to write to [[FuSu|扶苏]], and the unsent letter remained in Zhao Gao's hands. The episode ends as Zhao Gao begins using [[LiSi|李斯]]'s fear of losing status to [[MengTian|蒙恬]] to prepare the transition crisis developed in the next episode.

## Key Claims
- The episode carries the [[QinLateOmenCrisis|秦始皇晚年异象危机]] into action: the "游徙吉" response leads into population movement and Qin Shi Huang's fifth imperial tour in 210 BCE.
- The tour combines political display and ritual practice. Qin Shi Huang visits and sacrifices at places associated with Shun and Yu, engraves achievements, and asserts imperial possession of the realm.
- A dream of fighting a human-shaped sea deity and an interpretation involving great fish make the emperor more receptive to [[XuFu|徐福]]'s claim that sea monsters block access to Penglai; the episode treats the claim as a likely excuse for failed results.
- Qin Shi Huang becomes gravely ill near Pingyuan Ford and sends [[MengYiQin|蒙毅]] away to conduct a distant sacrifice to Qin's mountains and rivers, removing a trusted Meng-family official from the immediate deathbed circle.
- The source distinguishes public ritual from personal reliance: sacrifices across conquered territory can perform sovereignty, while the final appeal to Qin homeland deities is interpreted as closer to the emperor's own trust.
- Qin Shi Huang has more than twenty sons but no formally settled heir. The episode argues that his refusal to hear death-related discussion makes ministers unable to raise succession without appearing to predict or welcome his death.
- Once he believes he may not recover, Qin Shi Huang orders Zhao Gao to write to his eldest son Fusu, summoning him to [[Xianyang|咸阳]] for the funeral and, in the episode's reading, succession.
- The letter is not dispatched immediately because Qin Shi Huang still hopes to recover; it remains with Zhao Gao, whose office as 中车府令 and responsibility for seals place him at the crucial document-and-access bottleneck.
- Qin Shi Huang dies at Shaqiu in the seventh month at age fifty, the same place associated with [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]]'s earlier fatal succession crisis.
- The closing exchange begins [[StatusAnxietyPersuasion|位阶焦虑式说服]]: Zhao Gao asks Li Si to compare himself with Meng Tian in talent, strategy, merit, popularity, and Fusu's trust, and Li Si admits inferiority on all five measures.

## Key Quotes
> "立储君就等于是谈论皇帝的身后事" - the episode's explanation of why succession discussion becomes politically dangerous.

> "与丧会咸阳而葬" - the instruction used to summon Fusu to the capital for the funeral.

## Contradictions
- Source-level tension with [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw|Qinji 127-3]] remains unresolved. This episode and Qinji 127-2 follow the received account in which the Fusu letter creates an opening for Zhao Gao's manipulation, while the [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]] branch allows a deathbed decision for nearby Hu Hai.
- The claim that death aversion explains the absence of a designated heir is the episode's psychological interpretation, not direct evidence of Qin Shi Huang's private reasoning.
- The source presents sacrifices in conquered territory as political performance and Qin homeland worship as sincere belief, but that distinction is interpretive rather than independently demonstrated.
