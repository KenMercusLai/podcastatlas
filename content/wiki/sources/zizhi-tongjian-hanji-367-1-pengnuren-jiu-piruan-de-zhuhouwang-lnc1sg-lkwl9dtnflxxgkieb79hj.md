# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》367-1｜碰女人就疲软的诸侯王

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode profiles [[LiuDuanWesternHan|胶西王刘端]], a son of [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] whose illness, sexual relationships, violence, and repeated attacks on officials made royal privilege a local-governance danger. The durable historical thread is not the title's sexual curiosity but the mismatch between a vassal king's legal insulation and his obligations: court officials repeatedly seek punishment, yet [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] does not execute his elder brother, while Liu Duan obstructs taxation, closes his palace, abandons royal routines, and kills or frames senior administrators.

The closing branch connects [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] and [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]]. The episode presents Gongsun Hong's recommendation that Dong serve as Jiaoxi chancellor as political exclusion; Liu Duan respects Dong's scholarly reputation, but Dong still judges the post unsafe and withdraws through illness. His exit becomes a bounded [[PoliticalSurvivalFirst|政治生存优先]] lesson: recognizing that temporary respect does not remove structural danger can preserve later study and public value.

## Key Claims

- Liu Duan was a son of Han Jingdi, became king of Jiaoxi after the Seven States crisis, and died in 108 BCE after forty-seven years as king.
- The source describes him as impotent and becoming ill for months after contact with women; it then links favored young male attendants, sexual disorder inside the palace, and Liu Duan's killing of attendants and some relatives.
- Liu Duan repeatedly violated court rules, obstructed or waived tax collection, closed the main palace gate, traveled under changed names, and sought charges against officials sent to govern according to Han law.
- Han officials repeatedly requested severe punishment, but the episode says Han Wudi spared Liu Duan because of sibling ties and because he did not appear to present a usurpation threat.
- The episode treats this tolerance as a form of royal privilege that let private violence and erratic conduct become administrative harm.
- Gongsun Hong recommends Dong Zhongshu as Jiaoxi chancellor despite knowing the post is dangerous; the episode interprets this as rivalry and attempted political removal.
- Liu Duan treats Dong Zhongshu respectfully because of his reputation, but Dong still fears eventual punishment, leaves by claiming illness, and returns to scholarship.

## Key Quotes

> “为人贼戾，又阴痿” - the historical characterization used by the episode to introduce Liu Duan.

> “一近妇人，病数月” - the bodily condition around which the episode builds its opening narrative.

> “独董仲舒可使相胶西王” - Gongsun Hong's recommendation that places Dong Zhongshu in the dangerous Jiaoxi office.

## Connections

- [[LiuDuanWesternHan|胶西王刘端]] - central vassal king whose protected status, violence, and obstruction of officials define the episode's governance problem.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - emperor who repeatedly declines to execute his elder brother, placing kinship restraint beside the cost imposed on local officials.
- [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] - scholar-official who receives respectful treatment from Liu Duan but still exits before that protection proves unreliable.
- [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] - court rival whose recommendation sends Dong Zhongshu into the high-risk Jiaoxi chancellorship.
- [[PoliticalSurvivalFirst|安全第一政治生存]] - frame for Dong Zhongshu's choice to leave a structurally dangerous office while he still can.

## Contradictions

- The episode's claim that impotence produced Liu Duan's cruel or distorted personality is an interpretive causal leap. The bodily condition and violent conduct can be recorded together, but this source does not establish a medical or psychological causal mechanism.
- The episode describes Liu Duan's favored young attendants as male lovers. The transmitted account supports favored young men appointed as palace attendants and subsequent sexual disorder, but the exact modern identity label should remain source-scoped.
- The episode calls Liu Duan's posthumous treatment a “美谥.” The transmitted `Hanshu` passage calls him 胶西于王 but does not, in the cited account, support the episode's positive-value characterization of that title; the wiki therefore does not adopt “美谥” as settled fact.
- No contradiction with existing wiki synthesis was found. The source adds a new Western Han vassal-governance case and a more concrete political-exit branch for Dong Zhongshu.
