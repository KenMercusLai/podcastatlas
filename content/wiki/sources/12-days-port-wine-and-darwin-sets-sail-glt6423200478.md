# 12 Days: Port wine and Darwin sets sail

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] anniversary episode pairs the 1703 [[MethuenTreaty]] with [[CharlesDarwin|Charles Darwin]]'s departure aboard [[HMSBeagle]] on 27 December 1831. The port story connects war, tariff preference, the [[AngloPortugueseAlliance]], British merchants, and drinking culture; the Darwin story reconstructs the uncertain career, social arrangement, difficult preparations, failed departures, and geological reading behind a voyage later remembered mainly through its scientific consequences.

The two halves share a contingency argument. [[PortWine]] became culturally British through geopolitics and cross-border trade rather than taste alone, while Darwin's intellectual transformation depended on an invitation to serve as [[RobertFitzRoy|Robert FitzRoy]]'s gentleman companion, family persuasion, [[CharlesLyell|Charles Lyell]]'s geology, a cramped ship, and a voyage that grew from an expected two years to five.

## Key Claims

- The War of the Spanish Succession reduced England's access to or appetite for French wine, making Portuguese wine strategically and commercially attractive.
- [[JohnMethuen|John Methuen]] negotiated the 1703 treaty under which Portugal changed alignment, English cloth entered Portugal, and Portuguese wine received a duty advantage over French wine.
- Earlier English merchant interest, wine fortification for travel, treaty preferences, and merchant houses together helped make port a major eighteenth-century British drink and social ritual.
- Darwin was considering a clerical career because it could leave room for natural history when few direct scientific careers were open to him.
- FitzRoy wanted a socially equal companion aboard the Beagle partly because isolation and the previous captain's suicide made command psychologically risky.
- Darwin's participation remained contingent on his father's consent, his uncle's intervention, and FitzRoy's first-choice companion declining the place.
- Lyell's account of slow geological change, field collection, and the five-year voyage supplied experiences that later contributed to Darwin's changing account of natural history.
- Storms, a fatal man-overboard event, failed departures, cramped quarters, and severe seasickness complicate the retrospective image of an inevitable heroic expedition.

## Key Quotes

> "the most important event in his life" - the episode's rendering of Darwin's later assessment of the Beagle voyage.

> "claret is for boys, port for men, and brandy for heroes" - the hosts' checked version of the saying attributed to Dr Johnson.
