# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》269｜一瓶酱引发的“南丝路”开疆传奇（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places the “夜郎自大” story inside [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s early Southwestern Yi policy. After [[ZhaoHuNanyue|赵胡]] asks Han for help against [[DongyueWesternHan|东越]], [[TangMengWesternHan|唐蒙]] reaches [[Nanyue|南越]], notices Shu sauce there, and asks merchants how it traveled. Their answer links Shu, [[YelangStateWesternHan|夜郎]], and a navigable river corridor, turning an ordinary commodity into [[CommodityTraceRouteInference|商品踪迹式路线推断]] and a possible route for attacking Nanyue from the northwest.

Tang Meng then proposes using Han prestige and Ba-Shu resources to open the route, recruit Ye Lang forces, descend the river, and establish officials. The episode says [[YelangKingDuotongWesternHan|夜郎王多同]] and local chiefs accept gifts and a provisional arrangement, but also expect distance and terrain to prevent durable Han occupation. The durable synthesis is therefore not a simple peaceful annexation: trade reveals connectivity, diplomacy converts that information into conditional access, and local submission remains calculated and incomplete.

## Key Claims

- Nearly seventy years of early-Han recovery are presented as the material base for Wudi's simultaneous northern, western, and southwestern expansion.
- Dongyue attacks Nanyue, Zhao Hu seeks Han aid, and a Han campaign ends after Dongyue people kill their own ruler and submit.
- While on a follow-up mission to Nanyue, Tang Meng encounters Shu-produced sauce and traces it through merchant testimony to Ye Lang and the Zangke river corridor.
- The sauce establishes indirect circulation and a route hypothesis, not by itself a direct state-controlled road.
- Tang Meng argues that the usual Changsha-Yuzhang approaches to Nanyue are difficult and that a navigable northwestern river route could create operational surprise.
- His proposal combines an attack route with administrative expansion: use Han prestige and Ba-Shu resources to open Ye Lang, appoint officials, and incorporate the area.
- Duo Tong and the chiefs accept gifts and agree provisionally, while privately assuming that difficult access will prevent complete Han control.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was adopted. The episode adds a pre-conquest Ye Lang phase before Hanji 355's post-Nanyue royal recognition and Hanji 512-513's much later crisis under King Xing.
- The supplied document writes “张柯江/藏科江.” This note treats the intended river as the Zangke/牂柯 or 牂牁 corridor but does not create a canonical river identity without textual or geographic apparatus.
- The document's “皇巫左道” is likely a transcription of the imperial insignia phrase “黄屋左纛”; the exact memorial wording and its interpretation remain source-scoped.
- The episode's force totals, river width, territory size, dialogue, gift inventory, chiefs' inner reasoning, exact offices, and description of the result as a peaceful territorial expansion require primary-text and specialist comparison.
- The source uses the later moral idiom “夜郎自大” to frame geographic information asymmetry. The question supports limited knowledge in this retelling but does not by itself establish Duo Tong's full character, political intelligence, or view of Han.
