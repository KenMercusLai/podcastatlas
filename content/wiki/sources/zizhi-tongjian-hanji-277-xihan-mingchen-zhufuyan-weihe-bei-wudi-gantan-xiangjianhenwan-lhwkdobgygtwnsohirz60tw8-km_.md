# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》277｜西汉名臣主父偃，为何被武帝感叹相见恨晚？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the early-career prehistory of [[ZhuFuYan|主父偃]]. It moves from poverty, repeated exclusion, and a shift from纵横术 toward the learning favored at [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s court to the direct memorial that wins a same-day audience after [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]]'s recommendation fails to secure one.

The episode explains the “相见恨晚” response through 《谏伐匈奴》. Zhufu Yan uses the reported costs of [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]] and [[MengTian|蒙恬]]'s northern expansion, then [[LiuBang|汉高祖]]'s [[BaidengEncirclement|白登之围]], to argue that anger, prestige, and nominal territorial gain do not justify campaigns whose logistics, deaths, fiscal burden, and civilian exhaustion damage the state. The closing “穷人乍富” analogy proposes a psychological bridge from deprivation to later money and power fixation, but the episode stops before demonstrating that later fall.

## Key Claims

- Zhufu Yan is presented as a poor but ambitious student who first studies纵横术, then adapts toward the Zhouyi and broader learning after recognizing the court's preference for Confucian scholars.
- Rejection and poverty continue through travel in Yan, Zhao, and Qi and into his arrival at Chang'an; Wei Qing recommends him, but the emperor does not initially summon him.
- Zhufu Yan then submits a memorial directly and receives an audience the same day, turning written political argument into the decisive access channel.
- 《谏伐匈奴》 begins from the maxim “国虽大，好战必亡” and treats anger-driven war as a poor strategy when it consumes people, money, and state capacity.
- The Qin precedent emphasizes the difficulty of defeating a mobile enemy without fixed cities or stores, the cost of desert supply, the low agricultural value of conquered land, and the long burden of northern defense.
- The early-Han precedent uses Liu Bang's near-disaster at Baideng and the subsequent [[HanXiongnuHeqinPolicy|汉匈和亲政策]] to argue that failed coercion should prompt policy reassessment.
- The memorial arrives after the failed Mayi scheme while policy toward the [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] remains unsettled; the episode says its historical reasoning makes Wudi feel that he has met Zhufu Yan too late.
- The host links long deprivation to later fixation on wealth and power through a comparison with Zhao Dehan from 《人民的名义》, while deferring evidence for Zhufu Yan's later conduct to the next installment.

## Key Quotes

> “国虽大，好战必亡。” - the maxim used to frame war as a state-capacity problem rather than a test of anger or courage.

> “相见恨晚。” - the episode's description of Han Wudi's reaction after reading the memorial.

- [[ZhuFuYan|主父偃]] - central figure whose poverty, intellectual adaptation, direct memorial, and first imperial breakthrough are narrated.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler persuaded by the anti-overextension argument during an unsettled phase of Xiongnu policy.
- [[HistoricalCostPrecedentRemonstrance|历史成本先例式劝谏]] - persuasion mechanism that converts earlier campaigns into a ledger of logistics, casualties, fiscal burden, and policy correction.

- [[LiuBang|汉高祖]] and [[BaidengEncirclement|白登之围]] - early-Han ruler and failed coercive precedent in the second example.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing later Zhufu Yan arc was found. This episode supplies the rise that Hanji 281, 284, and 285 presuppose, while its poverty-to-fixation thesis remains an explanation proposed before the later conduct is narrated.
- Chronology caution: the supplied summary says Zhufu Yan mastered纵横术 in his thirties, also paraphrases him as having wandered or studied for more than forty years, and still places him in his forties when he entered Chang'an. Those statements cannot all be literal measures of elapsed adult study and require comparison with the underlying classical text.
- Text caution: the title, exact boundaries, wording, and authorship context of 《谏伐匈奴》, together with the “国虽大” quotation, need primary-text collation rather than reliance on the episode summary alone.
- Historical-detail caution: Li Si's warning, the extent and value of Qin's northern gains, reported deaths and transport losses, Cheng Jin's warning, and the causal account of Baideng remain episode-attributed here.
- Policy caution: the memorial's warning does not by itself establish pacifism, prove that heqin was optimal, or settle the later strategic value and human cost of Han Wudi's anti-Xiongnu wars.
- Psychological caution: poverty and exclusion can make urgency intelligible but do not prove that deprivation caused Zhufu Yan's later greed, revenge, or abuse of power.
