# Essentials: How to Defeat Jet Lag, Shift Work & Sleeplessness

## Summary
This condensed [[HubermanLab]] solo episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] explain jet lag and night work through the timing of light, activity, meals, temperature, and the estimated daily temperature minimum. It reinforces [[TemperatureMinimumClockShifting]], [[CircadianTravelAdaptation]], and [[ShiftWorkCircadianHealth]], while treating [[NonSleepDeepRestRecovery]] as a possible downshifting aid and keeping melatonin inside [[SleepSupplementBoundary]]. Because it condenses the already-ingested full episode [[scim4224560223-scim4224560223]], the overlap adds accessible provenance rather than independent confirmation.

## Key Claims
- [[TemperatureMinimumClockShifting]] uses the estimated low point roughly 90 minutes to two hours before usual waking to reason about whether light, activity, meals, or temperature cues may advance or delay sleep-wake timing.
- [[CircadianTravelAdaptation]] distinguishes transit fatigue from time-zone mismatch, treats eastward travel as often harder than westward travel, and coordinates pre-travel shifting with local light, movement, meals, and darkness.
- Very short trips may not justify fully changing biological time; the episode's roughly 48-hour to three-day home-schedule suggestion remains a source-scoped heuristic rather than a universal rule.
- [[ShiftWorkCircadianHealth]] favors stable shift blocks, light during intended wakefulness, and darkness during intended sleep when occupational and family constraints permit.
- [[NonSleepDeepRestRecovery]] is offered during fragmented sleep or nighttime caregiving as a way to downshift arousal, not as a replacement for sufficient sleep or clinical care.
- [[SleepSupplementBoundary]] treats melatonin mainly as a sleepiness and timing signal, questions routine reliance, and favors behavioral timing tools first; its endocrine claims remain source-scoped.
