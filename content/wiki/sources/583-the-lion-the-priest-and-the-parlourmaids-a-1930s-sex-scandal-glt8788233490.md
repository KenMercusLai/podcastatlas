# 583. The Lion, the Priest and the Parlourmaids: A 1930s Sex Scandal

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode follows [[HaroldDavidson|Harold Davidson]], rector of Stiffkey, from theatrical ambition and irregular ministry among vulnerable young women to a sensational 1932 church trial, defrocking, fairground performances, and death after a lion attack. It presents him as charitable, theatrical, financially reckless, intrusive, and persistently drawn to teenage girls while refusing to resolve contested testimony into either complete innocence or a simple predatory profile.

The episode also treats his downfall as an interwar media and leisure story. Newspaper access made the consistory case a national scandal; crowds turned Stiffkey into a destination; and [[Blackpool]] and Skegness converted clerical degradation, bodily risk, and celebrity ruin into paid [[CommercializedScandalSpectacle|commercial spectacle]].

## Key Claims

- Davidson's self-described rescue ministry combined real material help with parish neglect, boundary violations, theatrical self-conception, and conduct repeatedly experienced as pestering.
- The 1932 case arose from local conflict and an ecclesiastical investigation that used a private detective, vulnerable witnesses, disputed testimony, and a highly damaging photograph.
- The evidence supports neither uncomplicated exoneration nor confidence in every allegation: most reported conduct stopped short of attempted intercourse, while Barbara Harris's evidence and the photograph are presented as potentially coached or staged.
- London proceedings and intensive newspaper interest turned a disciplinary hearing into a formative British tabloid scandal.
- [[Blackpool]] monetized Davidson's fall through barrel, coffin, and oven performances in which audiences paid to see a former rector and Oxford man humiliated.
- Davidson retained agency within his ruin: he repeatedly chose theatrical self-display and in 1936 again approached two 16-year-old girls with promises of stage work.
- The lion act and its aftermath show scandal continuing after death, as Davidson, the lion, and the teenage trainer were rapidly repackaged as attractions.
