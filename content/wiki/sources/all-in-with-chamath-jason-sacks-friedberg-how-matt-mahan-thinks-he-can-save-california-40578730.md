# How Matt Mahan Thinks He Can Save California

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] interview presents [[MattMahan|Matt Mahan]]'s campaign for California governor as a test of whether local performance management can scale from [[SanJoseCalifornia|San Jose]] to the state. Mahan argues that California's central problem is not a shortage of revenue or policy ambition but a system that rewards spending, legislation, process, and organized interests without consistently measuring public outcomes.

Across housing, homelessness, energy, insurance, pensions, healthcare, taxation, and immigration, Mahan applies the same framework: publish goals, measure costs and results, reduce regulatory friction, expand practical capacity, and stop financing programs that fail. The interview supplies a coherent candidate platform, but most statistics and causal claims are speaker-provided and not independently verified in the source.

## Key Claims
- Mahan says state spending rose sharply while housing, homelessness, public safety, and infrastructure outcomes failed to improve, making [[OutcomeBasedGovernmentAccountability]] the campaign's organizing idea.
- He uses [[SanJoseCalifornia|San Jose]] as his proof point, claiming the city improved safety, reduced unsheltered homelessness, added lower-cost shelter, accelerated permits, and enabled new housing without raising taxes.
- He treats housing scarcity as a supply and cost problem driven by zoning, fees, building codes, litigation, approval time, and construction-defect liability, extending [[HousingAffordabilitySupplyMechanics]] and [[CaliforniaBuildingCostCapture]].
- His homelessness approach combines cheaper interim shelter with treatment capacity, public-order enforcement, and involuntary intervention for people he says cannot make rational self-care decisions, qualifying [[MandatoryTreatmentHomelessnessPolicy]].
- He links budget growth, headcount, pensions, retiree healthcare, and program accumulation to [[CaliforniaFiscalFragility]], and proposes zero-based budgeting tied to measurable outcomes.
- He rejects a state wealth tax and single-payer healthcare proposal as fiscally or behaviorally impractical while supporting narrower federal tax reform, healthcare competition, prevention, and expanded clinical roles.
- He positions himself between progressive and Trump-style populism: secure the border, remove violent offenders, create legal status for long-settled nonviolent residents, cooperate with federal authorities when useful, and focus state politics on basic services.

## Key Quotes
> "back to basics" - Mahan's closing frame for narrowing government to affordability, schools, safety, homelessness, and measurable service delivery.

> "safetyism" - Mahan's term for responding to every failure by adding rules, checks, fees, and process regardless of downstream cost.

> "revenue, revenue, revenue" - the answer Mahan attributes to Eric Swalwell and uses to contrast his own performance-first campaign.

## Contradictions
- No settled factual contradiction is adopted. Mahan's spending, fraud, mortality, housing-cost, shelter, crime, gasoline, insurance, pension, staffing, healthcare-savings, and disaster-aid figures remain source-scoped campaign claims.
- His claim that San Jose became the country's safest big city and reduced unsheltered homelessness by roughly one-third is not independently benchmarked in the source.
- The interview adds a second pro-compulsion homelessness account to [[MandatoryTreatmentHomelessnessPolicy]], but does not resolve its tension with housing-supply, civil-liberties, disability-rights, or treatment-effectiveness perspectives.
- His opposition to a California wealth tax converges with prior All-In capital-flight arguments, while differing from them by acknowledging inequality and supporting debate over capital-gains treatment, borrowing against appreciated assets, and step-up in basis.
