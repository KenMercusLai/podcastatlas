# 306: Columbus: The Adventure Begins (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode reconstructs [[ChristopherColumbus|Christopher Columbus]] before 1492 as the product of Genoese commerce, Portuguese Atlantic navigation, Mediterranean and Atlantic slavery, speculative geography, chivalric reading, Catholic providentialism, and Iberian rivalry. Its central synthesis is [[ColumbusPreVoyageFormation]]: Columbus was an unusually persistent and experienced promoter, but his westward project depended on inherited networks and knowledge while resting on a serious underestimate of the distance to Asia.

The episode follows rejection by [[JoaoIIOfPortugal|João II of Portugal]] and Castilian experts through the fall of Granada and the final intervention of [[LuisDeSantangel|Luis de Santángel]]. [[IsabellaIOfCastile|Isabella I]] and [[FerdinandIIOfAragon|Ferdinand II]] ultimately accepted a comparatively inexpensive gamble shaped by Portuguese competition, postwar financial need, crusading ambition, and Columbus's demand for hereditary status.

## Key Claims

- Columbus came from a modest Genoese background and pursued titles, hereditary office, and social recognition as strongly as wealth.
- Genoese merchant networks and Portuguese voyages gave him practical Atlantic experience; European expansion, plantation slavery, and African captive trading predated his project.
- Educated fifteenth-century Europeans knew the Earth was spherical; the dispute concerned ocean width and the distance to Asia, which Columbus calculated badly.
- Marco Polo, Pierre d'Ailly, Toscanelli, western-island rumors, chivalric romances, and religious symbolism combined in Columbus's geographic imagination.
- João II's experts rejected the plan on sound distance grounds, while Portugal's route around Africa reduced the need for a western gamble.
- The Canaries and Granada supplied precedents for privately financed conquest, plantation settlement, conversion, enslavement debate, and crusading mission.
- Santángel's low-cost and competitive-risk argument helped reverse another rejection after Granada's surrender.
- The 1492 agreement rewarded Columbus with ambitious hereditary titles and a share of returns while leaving settlement, trading-post, and discovery aims unclear.

## Key Quotes

> "Christ-bearer" - the religious meaning Columbus attached to his name and providential role.

> "Admiral of the Ocean Sea" - the hereditary status Columbus demanded before proving that his route worked.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with Parts 2–4 is found; this installment supplies the formation, sponsorship, and geographic-error prehistory for their voyage and colonial narratives.
- The source rejects both the flat-Earth myth and the claim that Columbus invented Atlantic slavery, while preserving his later responsibility for geographical denial, captive-taking, and colonial coercion.
- Columbus's exact origins, reported northern voyages, western castaway stories, inner motives, Isabella's reasons for reversal, and the relative weight of religion, commerce, rivalry, and status remain source-scoped.
