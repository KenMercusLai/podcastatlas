# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1120丨潼关之战背后的博弈：马超开局就中套？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the political opening of the 211 CE [[WeinanBattleLateHan|渭南之战]]. It argues that [[CaoCao|曹操]] announced an expedition against [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]] but used the required passage through [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] to pressure [[HanSui|韩遂]], [[MaChao|马超]], and the other western commanders into moving first.

The episode reads that sequence as [[ProvokedRebellionLegitimacyTransfer|诱发反叛式名分转移]]: once the coalition rose, Cao Cao could present himself as suppressing rebellion rather than initiating an attack on nominally submissive actors. It also sharpens the family-collateral problem created when [[MaTeng|马腾]] and his household entered the court orbit while Ma Chao retained field command.

## Key Claims

- The opening modern-management segment distinguishes a durable team organized around shared purpose, transparent distribution, member development, and problem-solving from a clique organized around a leader's private interest, favoritism, control, and blame shifting.
- In Jian'an 16 / 211 CE, Cao Cao orders forces west under the stated objective of attacking Zhang Lu in Hanzhong.
- [[GaoRouLateHan|高柔]] warns that the movement will make Han Sui and Ma Chao fear an attack and join in resistance; Cao Cao does not accept the advice.
- The episode argues that the Guanzhong commanders are the nearer priority but cannot easily be attacked directly because they nominally submit to the Han court and have supplied family or household guarantees.
- Passage toward Hanzhong puts military pressure on the western commanders without requiring Cao Cao to declare them enemies first.
- Ma Chao, Han Sui, and more than ten Guanzhong forces then form a coalition and attack Cao-side troops; the reported force total remains source-scoped.
- Cao Cao sends [[CaoRen|曹仁]] to Tong Pass and orders that the coalition not be attacked first, reinforcing the episode's interpretation that sequencing matters politically as well as militarily.
- Once Ma Chao rises, Cao Cao can frame the campaign as court-authorized suppression, while Ma Chao bears the public cost of breaking the prior arrangement and exposing Ma Teng's household at Ye.
- The episode stops at the campaign's political opening and defers the battlefield resolution to later installments.

## Key Quotes

> “假道伐虢” - the episode's analogy for using passage toward Hanzhong to pressure Guanzhong.

> “不做准备的人，就是准备失败的人。” - the host's closing lesson about strategic preparation.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the wiki is recorded. The episode fills the missing pre-battle layer before Hanji 1105's retrospective account of coalition division and defeat.
- Interpretive caution: Cao Cao's alleged hidden priority, certainty that Ma Chao would rise, deliberate effort to make the coalition move first, and manipulation of public legitimacy are the host's strategic reconstruction rather than independently established motives.
- Source-scope caution: ages, the coalition's exact size and membership, the number and legal status of Ma Teng's relatives, detailed command appointments, dialogue, and the “假道伐虢” analogy require broader textual comparison.
- The opening team-versus-clique framework is modern organizational advice and is not treated as direct evidence for the late-Han campaign.
