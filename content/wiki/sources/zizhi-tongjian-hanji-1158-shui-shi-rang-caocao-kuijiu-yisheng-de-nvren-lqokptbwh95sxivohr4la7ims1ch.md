## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses [[LadyDingCaoCao|丁夫人]]'s break with [[CaoCao|曹操]] after [[CaoAng|曹昂]]'s death to connect grief, refusal, and relationship agency. It extends the household branch through Cao Cao's failed reconciliation attempt and [[LadyBianCaoCao|卞夫人]]'s reported respect for Lady Ding, then turns to the [[CaoPi|曹丕]]–[[CaoZhi|曹植]] succession contest and the advisers forming around each candidate.

The supplied document is a structured episode summary rather than a transcript or cited historical study. Its private dialogue, motives, and allegation that Lady Ding caused Lady Liu's death therefore remain episode-scoped; the more durable synthesis is the already-supported sequence from Wancheng command failure to Cao Ang's death, Lady Ding's lasting refusal, and Cao Cao's unresolved guilt.

## Key Claims

- The opening argues that self-knowledge requires identifying the life one wants, judging one's capacities honestly, and accepting transitional hardship for a chosen direction rather than entering marriage or parenthood mainly to escape an earlier family.
- The episode presents Lady Ding as Cao Cao's original wife and Cao Ang's foster mother, but its claim that she caused the pregnant Lady Liu's death is not supported with a cited historical text and remains an allegation within this source.
- At Wancheng, Cao Cao's relationship with [[LadyZouWancheng|邹氏]] helps provoke the already-submitted [[ZhangXiu|张绣]]; Cao Ang gives Cao Cao his horse and dies alongside [[DianWei|典韦]] and [[CaoAnmin|曹安民]].
- Lady Ding blames Cao Cao for the avoidable loss, leaves for her natal home, and refuses his later attempt at reconciliation; the episode interprets that refusal as an exercise of agency after irreversible harm.
- Lady Bian becomes chief wife after Lady Ding's departure and is said to preserve Lady Ding's senior dignity at later meals and arrange her funeral, although the dates, seating details, and private exchange require verification against underlying texts.
- Cao Cao's reported final worry about how he could answer Cao Ang concerning Lady Ding makes the Wancheng loss a continuing household and moral memory rather than only a military casualty.
- After Cao Ang's death, Cao Cao evaluates Cao Pi and Cao Zhi as possible successors; the episode contrasts Cao Zhi's literary brilliance and weak self-command with Cao Pi's political control and battlefield experience.
- [[CuiYanLateHan|崔琰]], [[JiaXu|贾诩]], [[ChenQun|陈群]], and [[SimaYi|司马懿]] are placed around Cao Pi, while [[DingYiLateHan|丁仪]] and [[YangXiu|杨修]] are placed around Cao Zhi; these alignments support a factional field but do not prove identical motives or coordinated conduct by every named adviser.
