## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens the [[JingFang|京房]] sequence before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-487-xiang-bubugao-sheng-bixu-dongdian-zhichang-boyilun-llyn3gebws-gkmy8bavltcugrbet|Hanji 487]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-488-yixue-dashi-jingfang-ruhe-shenxian-bisi-ju-llf_4yvk8urtkgidnj2qhvca3vah|Hanji 488]]. It explains Jing Fang's Yi-learning lineage, [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]'s early trust in his disaster-omen interpretations, the first court debate over the [[KaogongfaReformBacklash|kaogong]] proposal, and the private conversation in which Jing Fang pushes Yuan to ask whether the trusted inner power around him is corrupt. The episode ends with the target becoming clear as [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]], setting up the access-stripping and death-case episodes that follow.

## Key Claims
- Jing Fang's danger begins inside his strength: he turns Yi-learning and disaster interpretation into political diagnosis, making him useful to Yuan but exposed once the diagnosis points at court power.
- The kaogong proposal starts as a disaster-response reform: Jing Fang argues that appointment by praise and blame rather than actual performance produces disorder and heavenly warnings.
- Yuan initially authorizes Jing Fang to discuss a performance-assessment law, but court ministers and provincial inspectors criticize it as too detailed and difficult to implement.
- Shi Xian and [[WuluChongzongWesternHan|五鹿充宗]] appear as the existing power background against which kaogong becomes threatening, while [[ZhengHongWesternHan|郑弘]] and Zhou Kan move from opposition toward support.
- Jing Fang's historical questioning of Yuan uses Zhou, Qi, and Qin precedents to make the ruler admit that past kings usually thought they were using worthy people while actually empowering flatterers.
- By listing Yuan-era disasters and asking whether the age is ordered or disordered, Jing Fang converts omen interpretation into a direct personnel warning.
- Jing Fang does not immediately name Shi Xian, but he identifies the culprit as Yuan's most trusted palace insider who participates in state discussion and controls appointments.

## Key Quotes
> "得我道，以亡身者，京生也" - Jing Fang's teacher's warning that the student who receives the method will die through it.

> "后代看今天，就像今天看古代" - Jing Fang's warning that Yuan may be repeating the same misjudgment later readers see in earlier rulers.
