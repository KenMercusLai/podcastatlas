## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode follows three international stories: [[AlternativeForGermany|AfD]]'s 44% win in [[SaxonyAnhalt|Saxony-Anhalt]], [[OperationBlueSkies]] as a test of [[ContrailClimateMitigation|contrail climate mitigation]], and [[SecularBuddhismAppeal|young South Koreans' renewed interest in Buddhism]]. The Germany segment updates the prior pre-election source by shifting the Saxony-Anhalt problem from possible outright AfD majority to CDU collapse, [[BundnisSahraWagenknecht|BSW]] leverage, and possible [[GermanCaretakerStalemate|caretaker stalemate]]. The climate segment treats contrails as a high-impact aviation externality that might be reduced by small altitude changes and better satellite/ML monitoring. The South Korea segment frames Buddhist expos, raves, temple stays, and AI monk products as a practical but contested route into religious practice.

## Key Claims
- [[AlternativeForGermany|AfD]] won 44% in Saxony-Anhalt according to the episode's exit-poll/election-night account, much higher than expected and far ahead of the [[ChristianDemocraticUnionGermany|CDU]] at 17%.
- The result weakens [[FriedrichMerz]] and the CDU, while making [[SvenSchulze]] vulnerable to calls for resignation despite the legal possibility that he could remain acting premier if parliament deadlocks.
- [[BundnisSahraWagenknecht|BSW]] entered the state parliament with five of 83 seats and had not ruled out cooperation with AfD, making it a possible kingmaker around Russia sanctions, energy policy, and school curricula.
- [[UlrichSiegmund]] says he does not want to lead a minority government, so the post-election path may run through BSW cooperation, minority arithmetic, a caretaker government, or fresh elections rather than immediate stable AfD rule.
- The AfD result tests the [[BrandmauerCordonSanitaire|Brandmauer]] because Germany's post-war identity and party system were built around excluding extremists, while state intelligence services describe the party as right-wing extremist.
- [[OperationBlueSkies]] tests whether small air-traffic-control altitude changes over the North Atlantic can reduce persistent warming contrails without large safety or cost tradeoffs.
- The contrails segment argues that persistent contrails may account for about one-third, and possibly up to half, of aviation's climate impact, while targeted changes to perhaps 2-5% of flights could deliver much of the available benefit.
- [[JogyeOrder|The Jogye Order]] and South Korean Buddhist groups are using expos, raves, meditation festivals, temple stays, and modern products to reach younger secular Koreans, while monks such as [[VenerableSeowon]] worry about balancing accessibility with dilution.
