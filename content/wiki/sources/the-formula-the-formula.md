# The Formula

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode follows producer [[BenNaddaffHafrey]] and baker [[RachelWyman]] as they reverse-engineer [[ThomasEnglishMuffins|Thomas' English muffins]]. High hydration, deliberate overproofing, ingredient substitution, fermentation control, and staling reproduce the signature nooks and crannies, but 61 percent of Culinary Institute students still identify the odd sample in a triangle test. The result makes [[ProductReverseEngineering]] a process-learning exercise rather than proof that the commercial formula has been duplicated.

The episode then contrasts [[BimboBakeries]]' lawsuit against former executive [[ChrisBotticella]] with the [[CarthusianOrder]]'s trust-based custody of the [[Chartreuse]] recipe. It uses that comparison to distinguish [[TradeSecretAsLaborControl]] from [[SecrecyAsCommunityBond]]: secrecy can operate as a legal and managerial claim over worker mobility, or as a shared obligation sustained by purpose and trust. Nearly 80 percent of testers preferred Wyman's sourdough muffin, shifting the practical goal from exact imitation toward understanding and improvement.

## Key Claims

- A commercial ingredient list does not determine a finished food because ratios, fermentation, hydration, handling, proofing, refrigeration, griddling, and storage also shape the result.
- [[RachelWyman]] and her students reproduced the visual interior structure of [[ThomasEnglishMuffins|Thomas' English muffins]] through high hydration, overproofing, closer ingredient substitution, and controlled refrigeration.
- About 61 percent of participants correctly identified the different sample in a triangle test, so the experiment did not produce a perceptually indistinguishable copy.
- Nearly 80 percent preferred Wyman's sourdough version to Thomas', suggesting that reverse engineering can reveal how to improve a product even when exact duplication fails.
- [[BimboBakeries]]' legal action against [[ChrisBotticella]] is presented as an example of a trade-secret claim extending corporate control into an employee's planned job change.
- Former Carthusian Father Michael Halloran says the [[CarthusianOrder]] relied on trust rather than threat after he left with knowledge of the [[Chartreuse]] formula.
- The episode argues that a secret's social meaning depends on the relationships and purposes that sustain it, not only on the information withheld.
