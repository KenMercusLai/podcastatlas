# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》215｜千古之冤，文帝贬谪贾谊竟是为了他好？（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[ZhouBo|周勃]] from fearful retirement in his fief through a treason accusation, imprisonment, official abuse, family bribery, and release after intervention associated with [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]], [[BoZhaoWesternHan|薄昭]], and [[BaoJi|薄太后]]. It presents the case as evidence that the last conspicuous veteran of the coalition that installed [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] remained politically dangerous even after leaving high office.

The episode also says [[JiaYi|贾谊]] pleaded for respectful treatment of Zhou Bo despite Zhou's earlier opposition to him. Its strongest additional claim—that [[JiBu|季布]], then Hedong commandery governor, may have been secretly authorized to kill Zhou Bo—is the host's inference rather than directly evidenced history. The closing notices of the Gucheng temple and [[ZhangCang|张苍]] becoming chancellor mainly prepare the next Jia Yi policy sequence.

## Key Claims

- Zhou Bo reportedly met visiting Hedong officials while armored and surrounded by armed household members, conduct later used in a treason accusation.
- After the accusation reached Liu Heng, the廷尉 arrested Zhou Bo; the episode says prison officers humiliated him until his family paid a large bribe for advice about using the emperor's daughter, Zhou's daughter-in-law, as a witness.
- Bao Ji argues that Zhou Bo had possessed the imperial seals and northern army at the moment of greatest opportunity after the Lü crisis, making rebellion from a small fief implausible; Liu Heng then pardons him.
- Yuan Ang is presented as the court official who openly defended Zhou Bo while other ministers stayed silent.
- Jia Yi reportedly asked that Zhou Bo be treated with dignity despite Zhou's earlier role in pushing him out, making the scene a case of policy principle and personal restraint rather than retaliation.
- The episode compares Zhou Bo's prosecution with the cases of [[HanXin|韩信]] and [[PengYue|彭越]], arguing that a “treason” charge could convert residual merit and prestige into a ruler-security problem.
- The host infers that Liu Heng may have summoned and returned Ji Bu in connection with a covert plan against Zhou Bo, but the supplied material offers no direct order, testimony, or corroborating evidence for an assassination mission.
- The episode closes with Liu Heng's Gucheng temple, Zhang Cang's succession as chancellor, and a preview of Jia Yi's reform advice.

## Key Quotes

> “吾尝将百万军，然安知狱吏之贵乎。” - the episode's rendering of Zhou Bo's reflection after experiencing prison power.

> “以公主为证。” - the reported prison officer's paid hint about the defense route.

## Connections

- [[ZhouBo|周勃]] - founding veteran whose fear, arrest, prison treatment, defense, and release form the episode's central case.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - ruler whose actual belief and political purpose remain disputed by the host.
- [[BaoJi|薄姬 / 薄太后]], [[BoZhaoWesternHan|薄昭]], and [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] - family and court channels through which the accusation is challenged.
- [[JiaYi|贾谊]] - earlier political opponent who nevertheless argues for respectful treatment of Zhou Bo.
- [[JiBu|季布]] - Hedong governor whom the host places in a speculative covert-action theory.
- [[ZhangCang|张苍]] - new chancellor and teacher associated by the episode with the coming Jia Yi reform sequence.
- [[TreasonAccusationTrap|谋反指控陷阱]] - pattern through which weak evidence can make residual prestige operationally dangerous.
- [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]] - Zhou Bo remains vulnerable after leaving the chancellorship and returning to his fief.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode itself distinguishes recorded arrest-and-release events from its stronger interpretation of Liu Heng's intent.
- Ji Bu's supposed assassination assignment is speculative: the episode infers it from his temperament, office, and an earlier summons, but supplies no direct evidence that Liu Heng issued such an order or that Ji Bu attempted to carry it out.
- The comparison with Han Xin and Peng Yue is interpretive and does not establish that all three accusations had the same evidentiary basis or decision process.
- Exact dates, the accuser's identity, the amount and mechanics of the bribe, the princess's role, Yuan Ang's intervention, Bao Ji's words, Gucheng temple details, and Zhang Cang's teacher relationship require comparison with transmitted histories.
