# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》564丨古代学术大佬，比想象得更牛！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Suihe 2 / 7 BCE under [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]], just before the nearby [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-567-ta-jiujing-shi-muqinhe-haishi-yichang-beiai-lsqtdjxamrpm2zit0t3ffgqpbittk|Hanji 567]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-568-hewu-duanjian-gudai-zheqi-panjue-zhide-dangdai-xueyixue-ljd2ojjmsla46no-9m2q802syxlp|Hanji 568]] backfill notes. It begins with [[LiuXinWesternHan|刘歆]] as a scholar-official who completes [[LiuXiang|刘向]]'s imperial-text collation work and compiles [[Qilue|《七略》]], then turns to [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]]'s attempt to limit land and enslaved dependents amid late-Western-Han inequality.

The durable synthesis is that the episode links knowledge ordering and statecraft. Liu Xin's classification of texts into the seven summaries and the nine schools turns scholarship into a map of usable political knowledge, while the later limit-land-and-slave proposal shows the opposite pressure: when reform threatens royal, aristocratic, and favorite-group property, even an approved policy can be suspended before implementation.

## Key Claims

- Wang Mang recommends Liu Xin for court service because of his talent and conduct, and Liu Xin later receives imperial trust as shizhong and Guanglu Dafu.
- Liu Xin changes his name to Liu Xiu because Han Aidi's personal name, Liu Xin, creates a name-taboo problem.
- Han Aidi orders Liu Xin to audit and collate texts brought to the capital; Liu Xin completes Liu Xiang's unfinished work and submits Qilue.
- Qilue divides the library into Jilue, Liuyi, Zhuzi, Shifu, Bingshu, Shushu, and Fangji, while its Zhuzi account groups thought into Confucian, Daoist, Yin-Yang, Legalist, School-of-Names, Mohist, Diplomatist, Syncretist, and Agricultural streams.
- The episode reads Liu Xin's account of the nine schools as a synthesis model: schools arise after royal order declines, each holds one side of the problem, and their claims can be corrected and used by a wise ruler.
- Han Aidi's court considers land and enslaved-dependent caps after Shi Dan revives Dong Zhongshu's older anti-concentration argument.
- Kong Guang and He Wu propose specific caps and a three-year transition, but royal kin, nobles, powerful families, and imperial favorites press Aidi until the policy is suspended.
