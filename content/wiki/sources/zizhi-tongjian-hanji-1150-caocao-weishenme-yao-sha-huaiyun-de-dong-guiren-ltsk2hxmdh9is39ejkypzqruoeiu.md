# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1150丨曹操为什么要杀怀孕的董贵人？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝 / 刘协]] after [[CaoCao|曹操]] has captured effective court power. The killing of [[ZhaoYanYilangLateHan|议郎赵彦]], the execution of the pregnant [[DongGuirenLateHan|董贵人]] after [[DongChengLateHan|董承]]'s failed plot, and the removal of [[FuShouEmpress|伏皇后]] show the emperor losing the ability to protect advisers, spouse, children, or household.

The episode also stresses that effective control does not eliminate danger for the controller. Cao Cao reportedly fears palace guards and historical precedents in which a nominal ruler suddenly destroys an overmighty minister, producing [[CapturedSovereignResidualThreat|受制君主的残余威胁]] rather than reciprocal power. The final branch contrasts this violence with Liu Xie's post-abdication life as Shanyang Gong under [[CaoPi|曹丕]]: the predecessor retains title, household, and succession, while the podcast's medical-service anecdotes remain local or source-scoped tradition rather than independently verified biography.

## Key Claims

- In Jian'an 19 / 214 CE, Cao Cao kills yilang [[ZhaoYanYilangLateHan|赵彦]] after treating his policy advice to Emperor Xian as preparation for resistance.
- After the Jian'an 5 / 200 CE clothes-belt edict plot fails, [[DongChengLateHan|董承]] and his allies are killed; Dong Cheng's pregnant daughter [[DongGuirenLateHan|董贵人]] is executed despite the emperor's plea.
- The host interprets the unborn child as a possible future focus for anti-Cao forces, but that motive is an inference rather than independently established intent.
- [[FuShouEmpress|伏皇后]] secretly asks [[FuWanLateHan|伏完]] to remove Cao Cao after Dong Guiren's death; when the letter is exposed, [[HuaXin|华歆]] participates in taking her from the palace, her position is abolished, and the source reports the deaths of her sons and large-scale punishment of the Fu clan.
- The episode presents the emperor's reported plea to Cao Cao and Cao Cao's fear of armed palace attendants as unequal mutual fear: the ruler has residual symbolic and close-access danger, while the minister controls organized force and punishment.
- Cao Cao places three daughters in Liu Xie's household, and [[CaoJieEmpressLateHan|曹节皇后]] later becomes empress, tightening Cao-family control through marriage as well as office and force.
- After Cao Pi receives the abdication, Liu Xie becomes Shanyang Gong and reportedly lives another fourteen years; his posthumous imperial honor and the continuation of the fief make the settlement unusual among the podcast's examples of dynastic replacement.

## Key Quotes

> “君若能相辅，则厚；不尔，幸垂恩相舍。” - the episode's rendering of Emperor Xian asking Cao Cao either to assist him properly or release him.

> “我亦不知命在何时。” - the emperor's reported answer when Fu Shou asks him to save her.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends the wiki's earlier Emperor Xian sequence from military captivity and Xu-court relocation into household purge, abdication, and post-Han settlement.
- Chronology qualification: the Dong Cheng/Dong Guiren crisis belongs to 200 CE, while Zhao Yan and Fu Shou belong to the later 214 CE court crisis; the episode links them thematically rather than as one continuous incident.
- Source-scope caution: motives, exact dialogue, palace staging, the unborn child's political role, the number and method of family deaths, Cao Cao's inner fear, and comparisons with Liang Ji and Chunshen Jun require checking against the underlying historical texts.
- Afterlife caution: Liu Xie's herb gathering, free treatment of poor patients, and specific Yuntai Mountain activity are retained as the podcast's local-memory account, not as independently verified court-history evidence.
- Comparative caution: the episode's claims about how other defeated dynasties treated predecessor houses are broad contrasts rather than a systematic comparison of legal status, chronology, or survival outcomes.
