# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》956丨庸人和智者，究竟差距在哪里？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the immediate pre-[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-957-ta-ceng-xiongba-yifang-weihe-ruonuo-dao-zai-cesuo-zijin-lnkhah144822mc4aa9bnezklgvoo|Hanji 957]] setup for [[HanFuLateHan|韩馥]]'s loss of [[JizhouLateHan|冀州]] and the earlier [[DongZhuo|董卓]] court-terror field. It contrasts [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]] and [[CaiYong|蔡邕]] using careful defensive speech under Dong Zhuo with [[YuanShao|袁绍]] using threat orchestration and persuasion to make Han Fu surrender Jizhou. The core claim is that the difference between ordinary and wise actors lies in condition-sensitive judgment: Han Fu has troops, grain, and corrective advisers, but fear makes him choose the option that destroys his real safety.

## Key Claims

- The episode frames "庸者谋利，能者谋局，智者谋势" as a judgment hierarchy: wise actors read changing conditions rather than only visible advantage or immediate comfort.
- [[HuangfuSongLateHan|皇甫嵩]] survives [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s intimidation by turning the question of fear into a public legitimacy test: virtuous assistance creates no fear, abusive killing makes all under heaven afraid.
- [[CaiYong|蔡邕]] restrains Dong Zhuo's title ambition by praising the premise but delaying the honorific until the Guandong rebellion is pacified, making him another case of [[DefensiveSpeechUnderTyranny|暴政下的防御性言说]].
- Dong Zhuo's registration and killing of alleged unfilial, disloyal, corrupt, or disrespectful people turns moral categories into terror administration and lets private revenge hide under public discipline.
- [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]] and [[YufuluoChanyu|于扶罗]] attach to [[YuanShao|袁绍]] near the Zhang River, while Han Fu's jealousy appears through a plan to reduce Yuan Shao's grain.
- [[QuYiLateHan|麹义]]'s rebellion against Han Fu gives Yuan Shao a useful opening: after Han Fu fails to suppress Qu Yi, Yuan Shao pulls Qu Yi toward his own camp.
- Yuan Shao's advisers propose taking Jizhou by pressure rather than frontal attack: invite [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] south, then send insiders to frighten Han Fu into yielding.
- [[GaoGanLateHan|高干]], [[XinPingLateHan|辛评]], [[XunChenLateHan|荀谌]], and [[GuoTu|郭图]] form the persuasion mission that presents Yuan Shao, Gongsun Zan, and regional response as one closing danger field.
- Xun Chen's questioning makes Han Fu repeatedly admit that he is inferior to Yuan Shao in benevolence, courage/judgment, and generosity, then converts that self-abasement into the argument for surrender.
- [[GengWuLateHan|耿武]], [[MinChunLateHan|闵纯]], [[LiLiHanFuRetainer|李历]], [[ZhaoFuLateHan|赵浮]], and [[ChengHuanLateHan|程奂]] give the counter-reading: Jizhou has troops and grain, while Yuan Shao is hungry, scattered, and not yet securely supported.
- Han Fu ignores those warnings and reframes fear as yielding to a worthier man, making the episode's Jizhou story a case of [[PsychologicalSurrenderTrap|心理迫降陷阱]] and [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]].
- Yuan Shao receives Jizhou and enters [[YeLateHan|邺城]] without direct battle; Geng Wu and Min Chun die trying to resist the transfer, while Han Fu receives only an empty general title.

## Key Quotes

> "庸者谋利，能者谋局，智者谋势" - the episode's opening judgment hierarchy.

> "让贤是古人称赞的美德" - Han Fu's self-justifying language for yielding Jizhou.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is recorded. This source supplies the precondition for Hanji 957's post-Jizhou collapse rather than reversing that aftermath.
- Source-scope caution: the host's "庸者/能者/智者" hierarchy and harsh diagnosis of Han Fu are interpretive framing, not independent proof of inner psychology.
- Source-scope caution: the source treats Jizhou's troops and grain as enough to resist Yuan Shao, but this remains the counter-advice of Han Fu's subordinates within the episode.
