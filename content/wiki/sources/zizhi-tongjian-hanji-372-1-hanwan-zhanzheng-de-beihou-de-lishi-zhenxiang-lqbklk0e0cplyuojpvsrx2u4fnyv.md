# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》372-1｜汉宛战争的背后的历史真相

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode reconstructs the immediate diplomatic prelude to the Han-Dayuan war. After Han gains access through the Hexi corridor and learns that [[DayuanWesternHan|大宛]]'s Ershi city has horses superior to those sent by [[Wusun|乌孙]], [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] sends gold and a golden horse to seek an exchange; Dayuan refuses, the Han envoy insults the court and damages the golden horse, and Dayuan forces under the Yucheng king kill the departing mission and seize its property.

The durable synthesis is multi-causal. Horses explain the attempted exchange, but the escalation also depends on prior military friction, poor envoy conduct, Dayuan's belief that distance, terrain, Xiongnu pressure, and supply scarcity make Han retaliation impossible, and Han concern for imperial authority after an envoy party is killed. The source supplies the trigger and reciprocal miscalculation, not the later campaign, its outcome, or proof that every reported motive was shared by both courts.

## Key Claims

- Han control of the Hexi corridor and Zhang Qian's missions make more regular contact with Western Regions polities possible before the Dayuan crisis.
- Wusun horses first demonstrate the value of Western Regions stock, and later envoy reports say Ershi city holds still better horses that Dayuan will not provide.
- Wudi sends substantial gold and a golden horse in an attempted exchange, making the mission a formal diplomatic transaction rather than a simple seizure at the outset.
- Dayuan's king [[WuguaDayuan|毋寡]] and his ministers judge a Han expedition unlikely because the route is distant, the northern path faces Xiongnu interference, the southern path lacks water and pasture, and even small missions suffer severe attrition.
- Dayuan refuses the horses after earlier Han military pressure and the new mission's arrogant conduct have already damaged relations.
- The Han envoy responds to refusal with abuse and by striking and abandoning the golden horse, converting a failed exchange into a public insult.
- Dayuan expels the mission, then has the Yucheng king intercept it; the envoys are killed and their property is seized.
- The episode argues that envoy killing and robbery transform the dispute from Wudi's desire for horses into a crisis of Han authority and Western Regions order.

## Key Quotes

> “不尽然” - the episode's rejection of the claim that the war can be explained only by Han Wudi's love of horses.

> “数百人为辈来，而常乏食，死者过半” - the source's rendering of Dayuan's distance-and-supply calculation.

## Connections

- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler whose horse demand becomes entangled with anger, authority, and Western Regions strategy after the mission is killed.
- [[Wusun|乌孙]] - earlier horse-giving partner that provides the comparison leading Han toward Dayuan's Ershi horses.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The source expands the failed-exchange and envoy-killing shorthand in Hanji 372-2 into a sequence of offer, refusal, insult, expulsion, interception, killing, and robbery.
- Analytical tension: Dayuan correctly identifies the route's severe logistical barriers, which later help defeat the first expedition, but incorrectly concludes that those barriers will prevent Han from sustaining retaliation altogether.
- Responsibility remains divided rather than flattened: the Han envoy's abuse and destruction of the golden horse intensify the crisis, while Dayuan's killing and robbery cross a more consequential diplomatic boundary.
- The Wusun and Ershi horse rankings, gold quantity, golden-horse details, envoy social origins and conduct, earlier encirclement of Dayuan during Han action against Loulan and Gushi, Wugua's deliberation, route alternatives, mission mortality, and Yucheng interception remain episode-attributed pending primary-source comparison.
