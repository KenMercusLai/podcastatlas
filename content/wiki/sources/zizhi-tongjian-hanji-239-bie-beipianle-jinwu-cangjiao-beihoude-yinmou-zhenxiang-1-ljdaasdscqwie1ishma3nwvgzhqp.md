# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》239｜别被骗了！金屋藏娇背后的阴谋真相（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode reframes “金屋藏娇” as the memory of a succession coalition rather than evidence of a self-sufficient childhood romance. After [[ConsortLiJingdiWesternHan|丽姬]] rejects [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]]'s proposed marriage between [[LiuRongWesternHan|刘荣]] and [[EmpressChenAjiaoWesternHan|陈阿娇]], Liu Piao turns to [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王娡]] and the child [[HanWudi|刘彻]]; the marriage proposal, repeated access to [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]], and praise of Liu Che then become parts of a new court alliance.

The episode attributes Liu Rong's deposition to a longer sequence rather than the refusal alone. Liu Piao reportedly attacks Li's reputation, Jingdi tests whether Li would protect other consorts and sons after his death, and Wang covertly prompts a minister to request Li's elevation as empress so that Jingdi reads the memorial as harem interference. The broad sequence is useful for [[PalaceMarriageSuccessionCoalition|宫廷婚姻—储位联盟]], but the “金屋” dialogue, dream and childhood-name stories, private motives, covert instruction, exact speeches, and one-plan causation remain transmitted story or host interpretation rather than settled institutional fact.

## Key Claims

- Liu Piao first proposes marrying Chen Ajiao to Crown Prince Liu Rong, presenting marriage as protection for her daughter and continued access for herself.
- Li rejects the proposal because she resents Liu Piao's role in introducing women to Jingdi; the episode treats that refusal as the point at which Liu Piao changes sides.
- Liu Piao and Wang then agree on a Chen Ajiao-Liu Che match, while the “金屋藏娇” line attributed to the child Liu Che gives the political arrangement a romantic afterlife.
- Liu Piao repeatedly criticizes Li and praises Liu Che to Jingdi, using sibling access and omen language as succession advocacy.
- Jingdi reportedly asks Li to care for other consorts and their sons after his death; her angry refusal weakens confidence that her son's accession would preserve the wider imperial household.
- Wang is said to prompt a minister to request Li's elevation because “母以子贵”; Jingdi interprets the request as unauthorized harem intervention, executes the minister, and later removes Liu Rong.
- The episode presents Li's fall as accumulated political misjudgment, but its contrast between “high-level” Wang and an emotional, unintelligent Li is a moralized character reading rather than independently established psychology.

## Key Quotes

> “若得阿娇作妇，当作金屋贮之。” - the childhood promise attributed to Liu Che through the 《汉武故事》 tradition.

> “子以母贵，母以子贵。” - the succession-and-status formula used in the episode's account of the fatal memorial.

## Connections

- [[ConsortLiJingdiWesternHan|丽姬]] - favored consort whose refusal, failed household-security test, and alleged link to a ministerial request precede her son's deposition.
- [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]] - marriage broker and imperial sister who shifts support after Li rejects her proposal.
- [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王娡]] - rival consort who accepts the Chen-Liu alliance and is credited with the memorial trap.
- [[LiuRongWesternHan|刘荣]] - crown prince whose alliance loss and deposition form the immediate succession outcome.
- [[EmpressChenAjiaoWesternHan|陈阿娇]] and [[HanWudi|刘彻]] - children whose proposed marriage binds Liu Piao's access to Wang's succession interest.
- [[PalaceMarriageSuccessionCoalition|宫廷婚姻—储位联盟]] - mechanism by which a marriage proposal redistributes advocacy, access, and perceived household security around an heir.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode backfills the demotion sequence before Hanji 239 part 2's Wang-empress/Liu-Che-crown-prince pairing and Hanji 240's account of Liu Rong's later prosecution and death.
- Name and text caution: the supplied document writes 刘飘 and 王志 and calls Liu Che “小智”; canonical pages normalize 刘嫖 and 王娡, while the childhood name, spider gloss, Gaozu dream, and “金屋” dialogue require textual verification.
- Causal caution: the refusal can explain a coalition shift without proving that it alone caused Liu Rong's deposition. Jingdi's preferences, Li's conduct, Liu Piao's advocacy, Wang's maneuver, the minister's agency, and wider succession conditions cannot be reduced to one demonstrated plot from this summary.
- Chronology, ages, dates, the minister's identity and execution, Jingdi's test, Li's words and death, Wang's covert request, and all private motives remain source-scoped.
