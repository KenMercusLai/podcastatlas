# How to Enhance Performance & Learning by Applying a Growth Mindset

## Summary
This solo [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] connect [[GrowthMindset]], [[ProcessFocusedPerformanceFeedback]], and [[ThreatChallengeStressReappraisal]] into a practical learning loop: separate identity from current performance, describe controllable actions, examine errors for corrective information, and interpret manageable stress arousal as resource mobilization. It draws chiefly on work by [[CarolDweck]], [[ClaudiaMueller]], [[AliCrum]], and [[DavidYeager]], while keeping intervention effects, physiology, and generalization source-scoped and preserving the need for real instruction, opportunity, support, and recovery.

## Key Claims
- [[ProcessFocusedPerformanceFeedback]] should describe effort, persistence, attention, strategy, and returning after error rather than label someone smart, talented, gifted, or bad at a domain.
- The episode reports that intelligence praise led children toward easier image-protecting tasks and more favorable score misreporting, whereas effort praise increased hard-task choice, persistence, and later performance.
- [[GrowthMindset]] becomes operational when an error is acknowledged and attention turns toward the behavior, strategy, knowledge gap, or support that could change the next attempt.
- [[ThreatChallengeStressReappraisal]] treats elevated heart rate, narrowed focus, sweating, and other arousal as potentially usable preparation rather than automatic evidence that performance will fail.
- The episode presents growth mindset and stress-is-enhancing framing as complementary: one changes the interpretation of ability and error, while the other changes the interpretation of arousal under demand.
- A brief combined intervention is reported to improve self-regard, difficult-course passing, and later challenge selection, but the numerical effects and their boundary conditions remain source-scoped.
- Sleep, recovery, hydration, exercise, instruction, feedback, and help-seeking remain practical supports; cognitive framing does not make resources or environmental conditions irrelevant.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki was found. The later direct [[DavidYeager]] interview sharpens this episode's affirmative intervention account: a brief message may start a recursive process, but durable change still depends on credible instruction, strategy, opportunity, support, and environmental response.
- Effort praise is not treated as praise for effort alone regardless of outcome. Useful process feedback must remain truthful, strategy-aware, and connected to a viable next action.
- The child-praise experiments, error-related brain signals, cortisol duration, stroke volume, peripheral blood flow, self-regard effect, course-passing effect, and challenge-choice results are summarized without complete methods here and remain source-scoped.
- Stress reappraisal does not make chronic stress, trauma, unsafe conditions, sleep disruption, illness, or demands that exceed real resources beneficial.
