## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three pressure points: [[HalHodson]] on the collapse of [[UnitedStates]]-[[Canada]] trade talks between [[DonaldTrump]] and [[MarkCarney]], [[MoekaIida]] on [[Japan]]'s [[EmploymentIceAgeGeneration|employment ice age generation]], and a military-history segment using [[ChristopherNolan]]'s [[TheOdyssey]] as a prompt for [[SiegeTypology]]. The trade segment makes [[TradeDiversificationStabilityParadox]] the core policy trap: Canada wants less dependence on the United States, but tariff war makes the investment needed for diversification harder to secure. The Japan segment adds [[EntryCohortLaborMarketScarring]], while the siege segment distinguishes symbolic sieges from materially decisive ones.

## Key Claims
- [[MarkCarney]] called off Canada-US trade talks when a deal appeared close, and said [[Canada]] would match [[DonaldTrump]]'s tariffs dollar for dollar if no agreement emerged.
- [[HalHodson]] frames Carney's dilemma as needing enough stability with the [[UnitedStates]] to attract investment before Canada can reduce its dependence on the American market.
- The episode says three American demands helped derail talks: excluding medium and heavy-duty trucks from tariff reductions, limiting Canada's ability to make other trade deals, and weakening French-language and culture protections affecting sales into [[Quebec]].
- Canada's internal response is constrained because [[Quebec]] faces high tariff exposure while [[Alberta]] has much lower tariff exposure and supplies more than half of America's imported oil.
- Energy retaliation against the [[UnitedStates]] is politically difficult for Carney because it could anger Alberta at a moment when provincial separatist pressure is already salient in the source.
- New tariffs came into force on about $20 billion of Canadian exports, on top of existing tariffs on aluminium, wood, cars, and vehicles.
- The episode says the tariffs could push Canada's fragile economy back toward stagnation or contraction, raise inflation, and threaten about 90,000 Canadian jobs.
- Carney's announced waiting period until September 8 makes domestic American price pressure and the midterm-election calendar part of Canada's bargaining strategy.
- [[MoekaIida]] reports that Japan's employment ice age generation entered the workforce roughly between 1993 and 2004 after the early-1990s bubble collapse.
- [[TorigoeAtsushi]] is used as a concrete case: he found only low-paid work after graduating in bad conditions, later saw younger recruits offered higher base salaries, and still carries weak earnings growth.
- The source says many ice age workers eventually caught up in regular employment, but early irregular work still damaged long-term earnings, pensions, and housing security.
- Japan's recent wage growth is described as concentrated among younger workers, leaving the employment ice age cohort exposed to a second round of relative disadvantage.
- The Japanese government's policy discussion has moved beyond regular-job placement toward asset building and housing, but the episode says more serious planning will be needed as the cohort ages.
- [[JeremyBlack]] distinguishes totemic sieges, whose later power is symbolic or civilisational, from utilitarian sieges, whose importance lies in strategic and material consequences.
- The siege segment names Vienna in 1683 and the Alamo in 1836 as totemic examples, while Tyre, Leningrad, and Malta are used as utilitarian examples.
