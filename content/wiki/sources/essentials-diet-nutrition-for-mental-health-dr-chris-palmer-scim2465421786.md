## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] Essentials episode features [[AndrewHuberman]] discussing nutrition, ketogenic diets, and mental health with [[ChrisPalmer]]. Palmer uses his own metabolic-syndrome response to the Atkins diet, treatment-resistant psychiatric cases, epilepsy history, and mitochondrial science to argue that [[NutritionMentalHealth]] and [[MetabolicPsychiatry]] deserve serious clinical attention. The episode is careful to keep severe disorders, psychiatric medication changes, and high-ketosis interventions inside [[PsychiatricMedicationSupervisionBoundary]] rather than ordinary self-experimentation.

## Key Claims
- [[ChrisPalmer]] says his own metabolic syndrome resolved after trying the Atkins diet, alongside improvements in mood, energy, concentration, sleep, and later clinical interest in dietary interventions.
- In treatment-resistant psychiatric cases, Palmer reports that some patients improved only after entering ketosis, including a schizoaffective-disorder case with weight loss, improved engagement, fewer voices, and reduced paranoia.
- The episode distinguishes low-intensity nutrition changes from ketogenic treatment: some mood-disorder patients may benefit from removing junk food or lowering glucose and insulin, while chronic schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, or bipolar disorder are framed as higher-supervision cases.
- [[KetogenicDietEpilepsyEvidence]] supplies the historical neuroscience anchor: ketogenic diet was developed in 1921 to mimic fasting for epilepsy, with major seizure reductions in some treatment-resistant patients.
- Palmer proposes [[MitochondrialMentalHealthModel]] as a unifying mechanism linking metabolism, neurotransmitters, stress hormones, inflammation, mitophagy, mitochondrial biogenesis, and psychiatric symptoms.
- The psychiatric claims remain qualified because Palmer says large randomized controlled trials for mental disorders are not yet available, although trials are underway.

## Key Quotes
> "stopping psychiatric medications can be difficult and dangerous" - Palmer's medication caution as summarized in the source.

> "large randomized controlled trials for mental disorders are not yet available" - the evidence boundary for psychiatric ketogenic claims.

> "diet-related tools may be among the most powerful ways currently known to stimulate mitophagy" - Palmer's source-scoped mechanism claim.
