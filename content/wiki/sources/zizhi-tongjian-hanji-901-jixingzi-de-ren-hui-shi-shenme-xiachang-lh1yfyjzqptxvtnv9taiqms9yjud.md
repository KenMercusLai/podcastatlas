# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》901 丨急性子的人会是什么下场？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 172 CE late-Han court-ritual layer immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-902-kongzhi-qingxu-shi-rensheng-bixiuke-ljynylvdlsdtjf4es9jmilav863b|Hanji 902]]. It opens with impatience, self-importance, and delayed reaction through [[TheMetamorphosisKafka|Kafka's The Metamorphosis]], then follows the court debate over whether [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]] should be buried with [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]. The core synthesis is that personal impatience and court politics both turn on timing and role judgment: [[LiXianTaiweiLateHan|李贤]] and [[ChenQiuLateHan|陈球 / 陈求]] break a dangerous silence, while [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]], [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]], and [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]] try to keep Dou's posthumous status from becoming an anti-eunuch rehabilitation signal.

## Key Claims

- The host frames "急性子" as poor emotional control, usually mixed with anxiety, compulsion, rigid thinking, result fixation, and overestimation of one's own indispensability.
- The opening advice is practical and narrow: count "一二三" or otherwise delay action until the immediate emotional surge has passed, making the episode a small branch of [[EmotionRegulationToolkit|情绪调节工具箱]] and [[ReactionChoiceFrame|反应选择框架]].
- [[TheMetamorphosisKafka|Kafka's The Metamorphosis]] is used as a self-importance example: Gregor Samsa still worries about catching a train and supporting his family after waking as an insect, yet his family later finds ways to live without him.
- In 172 CE, [[LiXianTaiweiLateHan|李贤]] attends the court meeting while ill, carries poison, and treats [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦皇太后]]'s burial status as a matter worth dying over.
- [[ChenQiuLateHan|陈球 / 陈求]] is the first senior official to state that Dou should share the former emperor's sacrificial and burial status, breaking the meeting's silence under [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]]'s pressure.
- Chen Qiu's argument says Dou helped [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] inherit the throne, should not bear full responsibility for the Dou family's political failure, and has a stronger ritual claim than [[FengGuirenLateHan|冯贵人]].
- [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] and [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]] oppose the proposal by invoking prior imperial-consort precedents and family-guilt logic, making the dispute a case of [[CollateralPunishmentAndGuiltByAssociation|collateral punishment]] applied to posthumous status.
- Li Xian answers through [[LiAsPoliticalOrder|礼制政治秩序]] and [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]]: later officials should not casually undo ruler-minister and mother-son status once a legitimate empress dowager has occupied that role.
- [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] accepts Li Xian's memorial and Dou is buried at Xuanling with the posthumous title Huan Si Empress, creating a successful but narrow [[ImperialBurialLegitimacy|imperial burial legitimacy]] correction.
- The host reads eunuch opposition as defensive politics: recognizing Dou would indirectly revive the injustice memory around [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]] and [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]], which threatened the moral standing of the eunuch faction that had defeated them.
