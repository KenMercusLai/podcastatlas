## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three institutional-stress cases: [[Ukraine]]'s wartime command reshuffle, rising police killings in the [[UnitedStates]], and [[Japan]]'s market for [[JikobukkenIncidentProperty|jiko bukken]] or death-associated homes. The Ukraine segment presents [[VolodymyrZelensky]]'s dismissal of [[MykhailoFedorov]] and later removal of [[OleksandrSyrskyi|Oleksandr Sersky]] as a fight over [[UkraineMilitaryCommandReform]], drone-enabled modernization, and wartime public confidence. The U.S. segment uses [[Albuquerque]], [[NewMexico]], [[MappingPoliceViolence]], and the [[AlbuquerquePoliceDepartment]] to show how [[PoliceKillingRateDivergence]] and [[PoliceConsentDecreeCultureGap]] can persist even as murders fall. The Japan segment turns [[JikobukkenIncidentProperty|incident properties]] into a case where [[HousingStigmaDiscount]], death ritual, inflation, and disclosure markets meet.

## Key Claims
- The episode says [[Ukraine]] and [[Russia]] had recently intensified attacks, with ballistic strikes on [[Kyiv]] and Ukrainian strikes in [[Crimea]] and Russian cities.
- It presents [[VolodymyrZelensky]]'s firing of [[MykhailoFedorov]] as the trigger for public protests, then says Zelensky removed [[OleksandrSyrskyi|Oleksandr Sersky]] after pressure built.
- [[ShashankJoshi]] interprets the Fedorov-Syrskyi conflict as a dispute about the ethos of Ukraine's war effort, not only a personnel fight.
- In the source, [[MykhailoFedorov]] represents a reformist technology model: drone scale-up, procurement incentives, and more resources for effective brigades and innovative units.
- [[OleksandrSyrskyi|Syrskyi]] is described as a divisive, Soviet-style disciplinarian whose tactics and command culture clashed with Fedorov's modernization push.
- [[MykhailoDrapatyi|Mykhailo Drapati]] is presented as a new-generation successor who had overseen northeastern defenses and whose public comments are read as a coded rejection of higher-casualty command practice.
- The source argues that leadership reform may improve army governance but cannot by itself solve [[AirDefenseSaturation]], [[PatriotMissileSystem|Patriot]] interceptor scarcity, or pressure on Black Sea exports.
- [[DanielKnowles]]'s U.S. report says police killings are rising nationally even as murder rates are falling, making [[PoliceKillingRateDivergence]] the core analytical tension.
- [[Albuquerque]] is used as a case study: the episode cites [[JoseArmas]]'s May 26 death, a history of fatal police shootings, and New Mexico's recurring high police-killing rates.
- The police segment links fatal encounters to [[MentalHealthCrisisPoliceResponse]], drug and overdose pressure, weak social-service capacity, and a policing culture that may survive formal reform.
- The episode says the [[AlbuquerquePoliceDepartment]] spent 11 years under a federal consent decree, gained policies such as restrictions on drawing guns, tasers, and body cameras, but still did not reduce shootings enough.
- [[MoekaIida]]'s Japan segment defines [[JikobukkenIncidentProperty|jiko bukken]] as homes associated with tragic or brutal deaths and says they can rent or sell at steep discounts.
- The Japan report links these discounts to ancestral worship, Buddhist mourning rituals, abnormal-death stigma, psychological discomfort, and a business ecosystem of maps, YouTube visits, cleansing rituals, and [[Kachimodo]]'s no-paranormal-activity certificates.
- Rising housing and living costs are presented as weakening some taboos around [[JikobukkenIncidentProperty|incident properties]], turning stigma into a negotiable market price.
