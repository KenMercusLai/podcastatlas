# 524. Charlemagne: Pagan Killer (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[Charlemagne]] from sole Frankish kingship through the conquest of Lombard Italy, campaigns in Spain and against the Avars, and the decades-long Saxon war. It treats Frankish expansion and [[CoerciveChristianization]] as one program at its harshest: shrine destruction, compulsory baptism, capital laws, mass execution, crop burning, hostage-taking, and deportation joined territorial rule to enforced religious conformity.

The episode then turns to [[Alcuin]], whose argument that faith must be voluntary helped qualify the coercive policy, and to [[CarolingianCorrectio]] and [[CarolingianLearningReform]]. Court scholarship, standardized biblical texts, more readable writing, priestly instruction, and parish teaching extended reform beyond conquest, while preserving the central moral tension between Charlemagne's violence and his effort to build a learned Christian order.

## Key Claims

- Charlemagne's resource base enabled campaigning on several frontiers at a scale the episode compares with Roman power, culminating in Lombard kingship, Spanish intervention, Avar treasure, and sustained expansion eastward.
- The decentralized Saxons could not be defeated by capturing one capital, so the war became repetitive and punitive; the episode records devastation, hostages, deportations, and Charlemagne's execution of 4,500 prisoners in 782.
- Forced baptism and death penalties for refusing baptism or maintaining selected pagan practices made the Saxon settlement an unusually severe program of Christian coercion.
- Alcuin argued that baptism without voluntary faith did not produce genuine belief and that new converts required gradual instruction; the episode connects his intervention to a less punitive charter in 797.
- Carolingian correctio joined political order, clerical competence, textual accuracy, and salvation rather than treating education as an autonomous secular project.
- Tours manuscript production, consolidated biblical volumes, clearer spacing and punctuation, instructional books for priests, and required teaching of the Creed and Lord's Prayer made learning a tool of rural Christianization.
- The “father of Europe” label is most defensible here as a claim about durable Christian and educational infrastructure, not the longevity or unity of Charlemagne's empire.

## Key Quotes

> “faith is a voluntary matter” - the episode's rendering of Alcuin's objection to coercive baptism.

> “father of Europe” - a contemporary label the hosts qualify through the realm's limited political durability.

## Connections

- [[Alcuin]] and [[CarolingianCorrectio]] - learned counsel that challenged forced conversion while retaining a Christian reform mission.
- [[CarolingianLearningReform]] - manuscript standardization, readable script, priestly training, and parish-level instruction.
- [[Einhard]] - court biographer whose praise helps reveal the ruler's cultivated image as well as his power.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is adopted. The episode broadens the imperial profile by making conquest, coercive conversion, corrective counsel, and mass instruction central rather than treating coronation as the whole reign.
- Charlemagne's apparent easing of forced baptism after Alcuin's criticism did not end military pressure, deportation, or the use of monasteries inside the Saxon conversion project.
- The hosts' Roman-scale comparison, Old Testament influence, descriptions of the 782 execution, chronology and scale of individual campaigns, fate of Carloman's sons, role of plague at Pavia, first-Bible formulation, script innovations, and “mass education” label remain episode-attributed or source-scoped.
