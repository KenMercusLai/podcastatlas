# 《资治通鉴·周纪》46丨骂你是个“二百五”的历史来源

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues the 317 BCE [[WarringStatesPeriod|战国时期]] sequence by treating [[XiuyuBattle|修鱼之战]] as the immediate battlefield sequel to the failed five-state attack on [[QinState|秦国]] at [[HanguPass|函谷关]]. Under [[Chulizi|樗里子]], Qin pursues [[HanState|韩国]] and [[WeiState|魏国]], wins heavily at Xiuyu, and then continues fighting at [[ZhuozeBattle|浊泽]]. The second half closes the [[SuQin|苏秦]] branch through his assassination in [[QiState|齐国]], his deathbed trap for the killer, and the host's rejection of the popular "二百五" origin story through [[IdiomOriginSkepticism|典故来源辨伪]].

## Key Claims
- In 317 BCE, after the failed 318 BCE anti-Qin campaign, Qin attacks back rather than returning to defensive quiet.
- [[Chulizi|樗里子]] commands the Qin pursuit; the episode identifies him as [[YingSi|秦惠文王]]'s half-brother and as a Qin figure nicknamed "智囊."
- [[XiuyuBattle|修鱼之战]] happens around today's Yuanyang County in Henan, in a central-plains position near Zhengzhou and Kaifeng and close enough to Wei's capital zone that Han and Wei cannot simply keep retreating east.
- The source says Qin wins brutally at Xiuyu, cutting down eighty thousand, then continues to [[ZhuozeBattle|浊泽]] and captures Han generals rendered in the source as Wei Sou and Shen Chai.
- The Xiuyu-Zhuoze sequence turns [[CoalitionSelfPreservationFailure|联盟自保失灵]] into a military consequence: the anti-Qin coalition not only fails at [[HanguPass|函谷关]], but leaves Han and Wei exposed to Qin's retaliatory pursuit.
- [[SuQin|苏秦]] is killed in Qi by an assassin sent by a Qi grandee; the source leaves the motive open between court competition and discovery of Su Qin's Yan-aligned covert role.
- Following [[Shiji|《史记》]], the episode says Su Qin tells the Qi ruler to mutilate his corpse publicly and accuse him of serving [[YanState|燕国]], so that the killer will surface to claim credit.
- The host rejects the folk explanation that "二百五" comes from four people splitting a thousand-gold reward for killing Su Qin, arguing that the story fits poorly with pre-Qin economic conditions and aristocratic incentives.
