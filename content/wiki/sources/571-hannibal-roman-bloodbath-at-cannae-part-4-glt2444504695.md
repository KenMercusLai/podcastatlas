# 571. Hannibal: Roman Bloodbath at Cannae (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the [[BattleOfCannae]] as the tactical climax of [[HannibalBarca]]'s Italian campaign and as an episode of extreme physical suffering. Hannibal seizes a Roman supply depot, chooses cavalry-friendly ground, and uses a yielding Spanish-Gallic center, disciplined Libyan infantry, and mobile cavalry to turn Rome's exceptional concentration of manpower into confinement and encirclement.

The episode keeps the battle subordinate to the [[SecondPunicWar]]'s political problem. Hannibal seeks to break the [[RomanRepublic]]'s Italian alliance system rather than erase the city itself; Cannae destroys an army on an extraordinary scale, but the closing reminder that Rome has not surrendered separates tactical perfection from final victory.

## Key Claims

- Hannibal's seizure of Cannae threatened Roman supply and offered bait on terrain where Carthaginian cavalry superiority could matter.
- Rome abandoned the [[FabianStrategy]] and assembled an exceptionally large army, but its component legions had not trained together and its compressed depth reduced normal manipular flexibility.
- Hannibal's thin convex center yielded under pressure without breaking, drawing Roman infantry forward until Libyan infantry could attack the flanks.
- Hasdrubal's heavy cavalry defeated the Roman horse, crossed behind the infantry, helped drive off the allied cavalry, and returned against the Roman rear to complete the encirclement.
- Roman confidence, depth, and forward momentum became liabilities once crowding disrupted formation, visibility, movement, and command.
- The killing phase lasted for hours and should not be reduced to a clean diagram of double envelopment.
- Even an annihilating battlefield victory did not itself compel Roman peace or complete the political destruction of Rome's alliance system.

## Key Quotes

> "bait" and "hook" - the episode's framing of Hannibal's army and the captured depot at Cannae.

> "one of the most remarkable victories ever won by a general" - the episode's assessment of Hannibal's tactical achievement.
