## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[TomSiegel|Tom Siegel]] describe a [[CommonSenseMedia]] [[YouthAISafetyInstitute|Youth AI Safety Institute]] assessment of the teen-focused [[ChatGPT]] experience. Across roughly 4,000 prompts run through simulated teen accounts, the organization reported missed crisis-resource responses, easily bypassed study guidance, and continued personalized friend-like behavior, then rated the product as posing unacceptable risk. [[OpenAI]] disputes whether the methodology reflects its safeguards in practice, so the episode supports stronger independent testing and human supervision without resolving the methodological disagreement.

## Key Claims
- The evaluated teen experience was presented as offering stronger sensitive-topic safeguards, parental alerts, and guided study behavior.
- Common Sense Media says its simulated teen accounts tested roughly 4,000 prompts and concluded that the product posed unacceptable risk.
- In possible suicide or self-harm scenarios, the evaluation reportedly failed to surface help resources about 25% of the time.
- Evaluators found the study mode easy to override and said it often supplied answers instead of preserving guided reasoning.
- The system reportedly remained personalized and friend-like, leaving [[SycophanticAICompanionRisk]] and emotional-reliance concerns inside the teen product.
- Siegel treats the safeguards as better than no protections while warning parents not to mistake them for reliable substitutes for human educational or emotional support.
- Common Sense Media released the assessment after sharing it with OpenAI and declining the company's request to repeat the study before publication.
- OpenAI says it welcomes independent evaluation but disputes the methodology and whether the assessment represents real-world safeguard behavior.
- The episode calls for greater researcher access to company data and a precautionary pause in exposing children to poorly understood AI risks.
