# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》190｜萧何入狱

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[XiaoHe|萧何]]'s proposal to let commoners cultivate unused land in the imperial park at [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]]. [[LiuBang|刘邦]] interprets the request as evidence that Xiao He has accepted merchant money and is using public benefit to win independent popular credit, then has the chancellor shackled and jailed. [[WangWeiweiWesternHan|王卫尉]] answers that benefiting the people falls within a true chancellor's duty and that Xiao He had far better opportunities to betray the ruler while governing [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] during the wars. Liu Bang releases Xiao He, but the minister's barefoot apology and later caution show that correction does not restore an equal or secure relationship.

## Key Claims
- Xiao He's proposal joins public benefit and administrative economy: opening unused imperial-park land would let households farm it, reduce official maintenance work, and return grain to the state.
- Liu Bang treats the proposal less as a land-policy question than as a threat over motive and reputation, alleging merchant bribery and independent acquisition of popular support.
- The imprisonment turns prior merit into a defense rather than protection. Wang argues that Xiao He controlled Guanzhong during Liu Bang's campaigns against Xiang Yu, Chen Xi, and Ying Bu, so a late pursuit of small commercial gain is implausible.
- Wang defines remonstrance for public benefit as a chancellor's proper duty and rejects Liu Bang's use of Li Si as a model for assigning good outcomes to the ruler and blame to the minister.
- Liu Bang accepts the intervention enough to free Xiao He but does not plainly confess error; his claim that the jailing publicized himself as a tyrant is presented by the host as face-saving rhetoric.
- Xiao He's barefoot apology after release shows the asymmetry of correction: the ruler reverses the punishment, while the wronged minister still performs guilt and becomes more cautious.
- The episode links Xiao He's case to a broader late-reign trust crisis among Liu Bang and his former commoner companions, then previews [[LuWan|卢绾]] as the most intimate relationship still vulnerable to that structure.

## Key Quotes
> "真宰相" - the episode's label for Wang's standard that a chancellor should request what benefits the people.

> "夏桀、商纣" - Liu Bang's self-comparison while explaining the imprisonment after Xiao He's release.

> "如履薄冰" - the episode's description of Xiao He's increased caution after imprisonment.

## Connections
- [[XiaoHe|萧何]], [[LiuBang|刘邦]], and [[PoliticalCreditAppropriation|善行君恩化]] - beneficial policy becomes dangerous when the ruler reads its popular credit as belonging to the minister.
- [[WangWeiweiWesternHan|王卫尉]] - official who converts Xiao He's war record and chancellor duty into a successful remonstrance.
- [[ShanglinParkWesternHan|上林苑]], [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], and [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] - unused imperial land, capital setting, and the rear region Xiao He had long administered.
- [[PowerExitTrap|权力退场困境]] and [[VictimBlamingAsInstitutionalDefense|受害者责难作为制度防卫]] - high merit fails to create safety, while later caution is a survival response rather than proof that the original policy was improper.
- [[HanXin|韩信]], [[ChenXi|陈豨]], [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]], and [[LuWan|卢绾]] - surrounding merit-holder and intimacy crises that make Liu Bang's suspicion structural rather than isolated.
- [[LiuYing|刘盈]] - successor whom Xiao He continues to assist after Liu Bang's death, according to the episode's closing biography.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode reinforces existing sources that treat Xiao He's merit as both indispensable service and later political exposure.
- The bribery allegation is Liu Bang's accusation, not an established fact; Wang's argument and the episode's narration reject it as implausible.
- The claim that Liu Bang jailed Xiao He deliberately to advertise his own tyranny is preserved as the ruler's face-saving explanation, not accepted as the actual original motive.
- The episode calls the land a Chang'an imperial garden; the wiki links it to [[ShanglinParkWesternHan|上林苑]] while retaining the exact park identity and boundaries as source-scoped.
