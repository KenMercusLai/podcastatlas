## Summary
This [[PlanetMoney|Planet Money]] conversation has executive producer [[AlexGoldmark]] interview [[LedaMeritz]], NPR's senior director of subscriptions growth, about why separate show subscriptions were consolidated into [[NPRPlus|NPR+]]. The episode presents the bundle as a way to lower administrative duplication, widen listener benefits, and direct support across [[NPR]] and participating member stations rather than reserve each payment for the show that generated it. It also uses contribution rates, platform fees, churn, advertising, and supporter data to explain the economics and public-service argument behind [[ListenerSupportedMedia]].

## Key Claims
- Existing Apple subscribers to individual show products such as Planet Money+ were moved to [[NPRPlus|NPR+]] at their original subscription price, while new individual-show enrollments stopped.
- The episode says fewer than 2% of NPR podcast listeners support NPR+, compared with fewer than 10% of broadcast listeners who donate to local public-radio stations.
- NPR says its subscription products have returned about $17 million to the public-radio system over their lifetime.
- At the time described, Apple enrollment cost $5.99 monthly or $59.99 annually, with [[Apple]] taking 30% of first-year revenue; direct enrollment cost $8 monthly and included a donation directed to a participating station or shared network fund.
- [[DirectSubscriptionRelationship|Direct enrollment]] gives NPR more net revenue plus a supporter's name and email, enabling event invitations, an NPR Shop discount, and a relationship beyond the listening app.
- [[PodcastSubscriptionBundling|Bundling]] reduces the hidden operating cost of maintaining separate products and can improve value for listeners who follow multiple NPR podcasts.
- NPR distinguishes voluntary cancellation from avoidable involuntary churn caused by expired cards, bad payment details, or fraud flags.
- Goldmark argues that precise advertising can underfund broad-audience service by assigning low commercial value to listeners outside desirable target groups; audience support is presented as a way to keep journalism freely available.
- NPR does not pay interview subjects because payment could distort truth-telling, although it may commission and pay journalists for reporting work.

## Key Quotes
> "all-you-can-eat bundle" - Meritz's comparison for moving from separate show subscriptions to network-wide NPR+ access.

> "baked in" - the production explanation for why a subscriber can occasionally hear a permanently edited NPR+ promotion.
