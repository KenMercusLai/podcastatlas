## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has youth online-safety researcher [[PamelaWisniewski|Pamela Wisniewski]] argue that bans and parental controls are incomplete without gradual access, practical risk education, and opportunities for young people to develop self-regulation. She presents AI as dual use: it may detect harm, moderate group conversations, coach judgment, or support realistic safety practice, while also helping scammers and online predators produce more credible, expert-seeming, emotionally manipulative interactions. The strongest synthesis is that youth safety should preserve agency while testing safeguards against real failure and abuse modes.

## Key Claims
- Children need staged access to connected technology because unrestricted access is difficult to retract and total restriction can prevent practice in self-regulation.
- Parents should model honest reflection about their own screen habits and act as guides who set developmental boundaries rather than relying on continuous surveillance.
- Platform safety features such as time limits, curfews, and parental controls need evidence that they reduce harm without eliminating beneficial participation or adolescent agency.
- AI can support real-time risk detection, content moderation, coaching, or mediation, including in group conversations where the source says teens encounter more body shaming than in direct messages.
- AI safety agents can themselves be abused or attacked, so testing and red-teaming are part of the intervention rather than optional follow-up.
- Large language models can lower the expertise barrier for scams and grooming by supplying realistic dialogue, local detail, specialist language, and relationship-building tactics.
- Simulated predator conversations and high-risk social scenarios may help adolescents recognize grooming stages and practice decisions when feedback is informative rather than parentalizing or coercive.
- Practical guidance should explain why teens should protect details such as full names and addresses instead of offering unrealistic rules that ignore ordinary online social life.
- The episode closes by citing [[CommonSenseMedia]] testing that found gaps in teen-focused [[ChatGPT]] safeguards, including parental notification, reinforcing the need for independent evaluation.
