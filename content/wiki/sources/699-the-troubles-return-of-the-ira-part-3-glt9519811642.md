# 699. The Troubles: Return of the IRA (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[NorthernIreland]] from the arrival of British troops into the 1970-1971 phase when [[TheTroubles]] became a deepening military and political quagmire. It centers on [[EdwardHeath]]'s government, the rise of the [[ProvisionalIRA]], the [[FallsCurfew]], loyalist mobilization, and the decision to introduce [[InternmentWithoutTrial]] through [[OperationDemetrius]].

The episode's core interpretation is that several measures intended to restore order instead damaged legitimacy and strengthened armed narratives. [[CounterinsurgencyLegitimacyTrap]] is the central pattern: house searches, curfew, poor intelligence, abuse claims, and sectarian-looking arrests helped convert Catholic fear and anger into recruitment opportunities while loyalist retaliation escalated the [[AtrocityRetaliationCycle]].

## Key Claims

- [[TedKennedy]]'s October 1971 "Britain's Vietnam" frame is treated as imprecise but politically revealing: [[UnitedKingdom|Britain]] had troops stuck in an apparently worsening conflict with no clear political exit.
- [[EdwardHeath]] inherited a crisis his own officials already described as near disaster, but the source portrays him and [[ReginaldMaudling]] as lacking a coherent Northern Ireland strategy.
- The [[ProvisionalIRA]] emerged from the split with the [[OfficialIRA]] and was not only a defensive neighborhood body; the episode stresses that many founders were long-standing armed-struggle republicans.
- The Provisionals built capacity through arms and money channels involving the Republic of Ireland, figures such as [[CharlesHaughey]] and [[NeilBlaney]], and Irish-American fundraising through [[NORAID]].
- [[ShortStrand]] fighting in June 1970 gave the Provisionals Catholic defensive credibility, while the July 1970 [[FallsCurfew]] badly damaged the [[BritishArmy]]'s reputation even though weapons were found.
- [[JamesChichesterClark]] and later [[BrianFaulkner]] faced hardline unionist pressure for a tougher response, but police and army intelligence were too poor to make [[InternmentWithoutTrial]] precise.
- [[OperationDemetrius]] arrested 342 people, all Catholics and no Protestants, turning [[IntelligenceFailureMassArrest]] into a legitimacy disaster rather than a clean security success.
- Interrogation and detention abuses, especially the [[FiveTechniques]], made British policy look colonial and repressive to critics while further alienating Catholic communities.
- The killings of [[RobertCurtis]] and the three [[RoyalHighlandFusiliers]] show the British Army moving from buffer force toward combatant inside a revenge-prone security environment.
- Loyalist mobilization accelerated through the [[UlsterDefenceAssociation]], while IRA and UVF bombings, including [[McGurksBarBombing]], made daily fear and retaliation a recruitment engine.
- The source treats British public indifference as part of [[PublicIndifferencePolicyDrift]]: the crisis consumed government attention but rarely forced a clear democratic settlement from mainland voters.

## Key Quotes

> "Britain's Vietnam" - Ted Kennedy's frame for the deteriorating Northern Ireland crisis.

> "trembling on the edge of disaster" - Burke Trend's warning to Heath about the best-case trajectory.

> "Five Techniques" - the source's shorthand for the coercive interrogation methods used on some internees.
