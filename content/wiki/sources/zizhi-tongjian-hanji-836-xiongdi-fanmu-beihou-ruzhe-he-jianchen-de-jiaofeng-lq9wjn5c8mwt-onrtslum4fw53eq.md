# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》836丨兄弟反目背后：儒者和奸臣的交锋

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 151 CE [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] field immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-837-weiren-chushi-weihe-buneng-xinci-shouruan-luznyzqldwdrbkfm7ce-1huuxm7b|Hanji 837]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-838-tanlan-de-youhuo-rang-ta-kuangbiao-zai-zuosi-de-lushang-lrydilyni6o6yzhk1-e74m6aigwr|Hanji 838]]. It follows [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]] turning conflict with his scholar-leaning brother [[LiangBuyiLateHan|梁不疑]] into surveillance and retaliation against visiting officials.

The source's durable contribution is the pairing of outer-relative coercion with scholar-official humiliation. [[MaRongLateHan|马融]] and [[TianMingLateHan|田明]] are punished after visiting Liang Buyi, making [[PunitiveHairRemovalHumiliation|髡刑式身份羞辱]] a visible tool for attacking士人 dignity; the second half then contrasts Liang Ji's coercion with [[YangBingLateHan|杨秉]]'s omen-coded remonstrance after Huan privately visits Liang Ji's son Liang Yin.

## Key Claims

- In 151 CE, Liang Buyi and Liang Ji are brothers but are politically and temperamentally opposed: Liang Buyi is aligned with learning and士人, while Liang Ji is portrayed as arrogant, indulgent, and corrupt.
- Liang Ji uses office power to move Liang Buyi to the Guanglu xun post and place his sixteen-year-old son Liang Yin as Henan Yin.
- Liang Buyi resigns and closes his doors, but Liang Ji still posts disguised watchers outside the household to monitor who visits.
- Ma Rong and Tian Ming pass Liang Buyi's house on the way to new commandery posts, visit him, and are then framed by Liang Ji's side.
- Both visiting officials receive hair-removal and flogging punishment and are exiled to Shuofang; Ma Rong is later recalled, while Tian Ming dies in exile.
- The episode treats hair-removal punishment as less about bodily pain than public dignity, family honor, and士大夫 status loss.
- Ma Rong's background as a learned classical scholar makes the retaliation politically meaningful: Liang Ji is attacking recognizable scholar-official authority, not only anonymous subordinates.
- Emperor Huan's private visit to Liang Yin's house is paired with wind, darkness, and uprooted trees, letting Yang Bing convert disaster language into a ritual and palace-security warning.
- Yang Bing argues that imperial movement should be reserved for major ritual duties and constrained by palace defense, hierarchy, and seal security; Huan ignores the warning.
- Yang Bing's clean biography, late office entry, gift refusal, and "three refusals" toward wine, sex, and money supply an early integrity layer before his later anti-eunuch enforcement cases.

## Key Quotes

> "身体发肤，受之父母" - the episode's explanation for why shaving was a status injury.

> "天虽然不说话" - Yang Bing's premise that disasters can warn the ruler.

> "三不惑" - the compact label for Yang Bing's refusal of wine, sexual entanglement, and money.
