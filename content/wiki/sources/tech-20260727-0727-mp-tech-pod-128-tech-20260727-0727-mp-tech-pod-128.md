## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode centers on [[SinaLaubenstein]] of the [[InstituteForStrategicDialogue]] and her team's research into AI tools that generate sexualized images or videos from real people's photos. The episode says the team found 181 nudify websites and argues that mainstream platforms, app stores, payment networks, crypto intermediaries, and law all shape whether those services remain easy to find and profitable.

The source extends [[AINonConsensualIntimateImageAbuse]] from a platform-integrated model-output problem into a broader [[NudifyAppEcosystem]]. Its strongest contribution is the enforcement map: [[YouTube]] and [[Twitter|X]] drove traffic, [[Facebook]] and [[Instagram]] hosted ads, app stores changed some search and removal behavior, and payment options such as [[PayPal]], [[Mastercard]], and [[Visa]] appeared alongside crypto routing that can weaken banking oversight.

## Key Claims
- The Institute for Strategic Dialogue found 181 websites offering tools that let users upload images of real people and generate nudified images or videos.
- Major social platforms acted as gateways to these sites, with YouTube and X described as especially strong traffic drivers.
- Meta platforms hosted ads for nudify tools, including one set of identical ads posted more than 600 times and some ads that ran for months.
- The ads often used direct wording, stock images, or AI-generated images rather than subtle coded language, suggesting enforcement failure was not only a keyword-evasion problem.
- Some platforms and app stores contacted researchers, removed some apps, or adjusted search settings after the report, but the apps and services did not disappear.
- The host connects the issue to platform terms of service, app-store rules, the federal [[TakeItDownAct|Take It Down Act]], and state laws against non-consensual intimate imagery.
- Laubenstein argues that adjacent products such as AI companions, AI girlfriends, wearable devices, and smart devices may evolve faster than existing legislation.
- The report found trusted payment options displayed on nudify websites, while transactions could be routed through crypto intermediaries in ways that bypass banking oversight.
- The source treats [[AbusePaymentInfrastructure]] as a practical intervention point: policymakers can pressure payment, banking, app-store, and distribution layers instead of focusing only on social-media posts.
- The episode says San Francisco's city attorney sent cease-and-desist letters to [[Apple]] and [[Google]] under [[California]] law, arguing that app stores should not profit from nudify apps.
