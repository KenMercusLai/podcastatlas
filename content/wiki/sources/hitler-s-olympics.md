# Hitler’s Olympics

## Summary

This short [[RevisionistHistory]] trailer introduces a nine-episode investigation of the 1936 Berlin Olympics and asks why the United States participated despite [[AdolfHitler|Adolf Hitler's]] use of the Games to promote racial supremacy, legitimize the Nazi regime, rally Germans, and display national power. It frames the season as an inquiry into the political maneuvering and moral compromises behind the competition rather than a conventional account of athletic results.

The preview argues that [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany]] helped establish the large-scale spectacle associated with the [[ModernOlympicGames|modern Olympic Games]]. It announces profiles of [[CharlesHitchcockSherrill]], [[AveryBrundage]], [[DorothyThompson]], and [[JesseOwens]], along with later material connecting the Olympics to Black disenfranchisement and *Giles v. Harris* in Alabama.

## Key Claims

- Berlin 1936 was a decisive demonstration of how a state could turn the Olympics into a mass political spectacle.
- Hitler intended the Games to promote “Aryan supremacy,” legitimize his regime, mobilize domestic support, and present Germany as a world power.
- United States participation is presented as the season's central moral and institutional problem.
- The series examines the ambitions, compromises, and misjudgments of Olympic officials alongside the clearer warnings of contemporary critics.
- Sherrill and Brundage represent different routes through which personal ambition and institutional loyalty became entangled with the participation decision.
- Thompson is introduced as a clear-eyed reporter of Hitler's danger, while Owens's familiar four-gold-medal story is promised a less familiar historical context.
- The season will connect Olympic history to American racial disenfranchisement, showing that the moral comparison is not confined to Nazi Germany.

## Key Quotes

> “the Games behind the Games” - the trailer's description of its focus on political maneuvering before Berlin 1936.
