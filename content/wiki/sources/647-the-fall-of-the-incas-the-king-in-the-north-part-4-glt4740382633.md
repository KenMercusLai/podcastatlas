# 647. The Fall of the Incas: The King in the North (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[SpanishConquestOfPeru|Spanish conquest of Peru]] from [[Atahualpa|Atahualpa's]] execution through the occupation and looting of Cusco and the defeat of northern resistance around Quito. [[FranciscoPizarro]] uses the Inca civil war to install Túpac Hualpa and then [[MancoInca]] as Spanish-backed rulers, while Indigenous allies and divisions within the empire remain essential to the advance.

The episode presents conquest as political fragmentation, coalition warfare, sacred destruction, and colonial extraction rather than a Spaniards-alone victory. [[Ruminawi|Rumiñawi]] and [[Quisquis]] continue resistance in the north, but Cañari allies, Spanish cavalry, collapsing morale, and [[PedroDeAlvarado|Pedro de Alvarado's]] competing expedition help turn the seizure of Cusco and Quito into settlement, encomienda, and the founding of Lima.

## Key Claims

- Pizarro exploited the Huascar-Atahualpa civil-war divide by presenting Spanish rule as liberation and installing claimants from the Cusco faction.
- Spanish movement from Cajamarca to Cusco depended on Indigenous allies and local support as well as horses, steel, and small Spanish forces.
- Cusco's occupation joined political theater to large-scale sacred and material plunder, including the melting of exceptional gold and silver objects from Coricancha and other royal and religious sites.
- Northern resistance remained formidable under Quisquis and Rumiñawi, but factional violence, morale collapse, Cañari support for the Spaniards, and Spanish mobility prevented a unified recovery.
- Alvarado's Ecuador expedition brought thousands of Guatemalan auxiliaries and extreme violence, but environmental disaster and rivalry with other conquistadors ended with his withdrawal after selling his ships and equipment to Almagro.
- The rapid shift from campaigning to Lima's foundation, land distribution, and compelled labor shows colonial formation beginning before military resistance had ended.
- Manco's initial accommodation became untenable as Spanish abuse, extraction, and disposal of Inca authority revealed the limits of puppet rule.

## Key Quotes

> "sniff of gold" - the hosts' phrase for the news that drew more Spanish adventurers toward Peru.

> "more perfect than anything they possessed" - the episode's rendering of Cristóbal de Molina's criticism of the destruction of Inca metalwork.

## Connections

- [[Atahualpa]], [[MancoInca]], and [[FranciscoPizarro]] - succession crisis and puppet rule after Cajamarca.
- [[Ruminawi]] and [[Quisquis]] - northern commanders whose resistance outlasted Atahualpa.
- [[PedroDeAlvarado]] and [[DiegoDeAlmagro]] - competing conquistador expeditions whose Quito rivalry ended in a negotiated buyout.
- [[SpanishConquestOfPeru]] and [[IndigenousAllianceInSpanishConquest]] - conquest as factional and coalition warfare.
- [[SacredPrestigeUnderConquest]] - seizure and destruction of Cusco's royal, ritual, and temple wealth.
- [[EncomiendaColonialLaborSystem]] - transition from military occupation to land and labor allocation.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with [[648-the-fall-of-the-incas-battle-for-the-sacred-city-part-5-glt6392035011|Part 5]] or [[649-the-fall-of-the-incas-the-last-emperor-part-6-glt4505534064|Part 6]] is adopted. This episode explains the accommodation and early extraction that the later installments follow into revolt, civil war, and Vilcabamba resistance.
- Spanish chroniclers supply much of the surviving narrative; speeches, force totals, casualty estimates, intimate motives, atrocities, and detailed battle sequences therefore remain source-scoped.
- The counterfactual possibility of a surviving Andean highland state is presented as plausible but increasingly unlikely, not as a settled historical conclusion.
- Calling southern elites or Cañari forces Spanish allies does not establish equal, stable, or freely chosen partnership; choices were made amid imperial rivalry, coercion, revenge, and rapidly changing danger.
