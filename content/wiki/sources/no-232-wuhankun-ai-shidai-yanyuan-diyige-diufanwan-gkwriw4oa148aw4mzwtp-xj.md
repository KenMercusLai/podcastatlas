# No.232 吴汉坤：AI 时代，演员第一个丢饭碗？

## Summary
This [[SanWuHuan|三五环]] episode has [[LiuFei|刘飞]] interviewing actor and AI creator [[WuHankun|吴瀚坤]] about his route from business school and the [[CreationOfTheGodsI|《封神》]] training camp into coding, [[FrederickAI]], and AI short-film making. It uses [[PinkParadox|《粉色悖论》]] and [[PopulationAnomaly|《人口异常》]] to show how [[AIVideoProductionWorkflow]] changes scripting, storyboarding, image generation, editing, music, and performance control.

The durable contribution is an actor-side boundary for generative video. Wu argues that AI can replace simple, homogeneous, technical, or postproduction-like work, but timing, facial micro-rhythm, embodied emotion, life experience, and artistic expression still need a human subject. The episode also frames AI anxiety as something reduced by direct use, workflow knowledge, and a return to what the creator actually wants to express.

## Key Claims
- [[WuHankun|吴瀚坤]]'s path combines acting, coding, and product work: after pandemic-era career interruption, he learned programming, took CS50, worked in consulting, and built [[FrederickAI]] as an early agent product for startup tasks.
- [[PinkParadox|《粉色悖论》]] began as a short role-farewell demo after [[WuHankun|吴瀚坤]] played Zhu Yangqi in 《陷入我们的热恋》, then expanded into a fuller AI short-film exercise.
- AI short-film production lowered the need for a traditional crew but still required hard work across script, storyboard, images, video generation, editing, music, visual detail, and repeated tool learning.
- [[ActorAIPerformanceBoundary]] is clearest in rhythm: Wu says a half-second difference in glance, nod, pause, or comic timing can change a performance, and that text prompts struggle to control this reliably.
- The near-term film path in the episode is not pure replacement: Wu sees more value in recording real actor performance and using AI for background, scene, and postproduction transformations, similar to a stronger green-screen pipeline.
- [[ActorLikenessAuthorization]] separates commercial use of a face from artistic authorship; a third party controlling an authorized face with prompts or motion may create value without making the result the actor's own performance.
- AI-generated performers and AI artists are limited by "human flavor": intentional errors, broken voice, accent, pauses, and awkwardness can be expressive in human performance but read as model failure when generated blindly.
- [[HandsOnAIAnxietyReduction]] appears as the episode's practical advice for film workers: do not chase every new model or anxiety-selling slogan, but use AI directly enough to learn its limits and decide what remains worth doing.
- The episode's broader value claim is that AI shifts people from standard workflows toward self-defined purpose; once tools remove some routine execution, knowing what one wants to make becomes more important.

## Key Quotes
> "不如做吧" - Wu's shorthand for starting before certainty.

> "电影是遗憾的艺术" - Liu's phrase for why finished films still carry regret.

> "AI像Word" - the episode's tool analogy: the same software does not equal the same expressive ability.

> "使用AI是消解焦虑的方法之一" - Wu's practical answer to AI anxiety.
