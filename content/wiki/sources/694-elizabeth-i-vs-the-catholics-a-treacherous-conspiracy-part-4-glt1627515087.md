# 694. Elizabeth I vs The Catholics: A Treacherous Conspiracy (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[FrancisDrake]]'s return from circumnavigation into [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]]'s larger Catholic-Protestant crisis. Drake's treasure, the [[GoldenHind]] ceremony, [[PhilipII|Philip II]]'s enlarged Iberian power, and the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]] push [[England]] toward a harder anti-[[Spain]] posture while Elizabeth still tries to keep options open through [[PrivateeringPlausibleDeniability]] and [[MarriageDiplomacyAsStrategicOption]].

The episode then follows the French and Dutch setting behind the [[ThrockmortonPlot]]: [[FrancoisDukeOfAnjou|Francois, Duke of Anjou]]'s Dutch bid fails in the [[FrenchFury]], [[HenryDukeOfGuise|Henry, Duke of Guise]] and Catholic allies prepare an invasion plan, [[BernardinoDeMendoza]] and [[MichelDeCastelnau]] become diplomatic channels, and [[FrancisWalsingham]]'s intelligence network exposes [[FrancisThrockmorton]]. The result is Throckmorton's execution, Mendoza's expulsion, tighter controls on [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary, Queen of Scots]], the [[BondOfAssociation]], and a clearer road toward [[ConfessionalWarEscalation]] and the [[SpanishArmada]].

## Key Claims

- [[FrancisDrake]] returned in September 1580 with treasure worth about 400,000 pounds, making him both a Protestant hero and a diplomatic liability for [[ElizabethI|Elizabeth I]].
- Elizabeth handled Drake's treasure through [[PrivateeringPlausibleDeniability]]: she registered it formally, quietly kept a large share, returned only some money to claimants, and denied Spain's broader accusation.
- [[PhilipII|Philip II]]'s acquisition of [[Portugal]] and its empire, Spanish pressure in the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]], and Spanish activity around [[Ireland]] made England's strategic environment more dangerous.
- The Privy Council split over Drake, with [[WilliamCecil|William Cecil / Lord Burleigh]] cautious and [[FrancisWalsingham]], [[ChristopherHatton]], and [[RobertDudley|Robert Dudley / Earl of Leicester]] more supportive.
- Elizabeth's 1 April 1581 [[GoldenHind]] ceremony made Drake's anti-Spanish prestige public while using a French envoy to keep diplomatic signalling ambiguous.
- [[FrancoisDukeOfAnjou|Anjou]] offered Elizabeth a potential French counterweight to Spain, but the proposed marriage drew Protestant and Catholic criticism, including [[JohnStubbs]]' pamphlet and [[WilliamAllen]]'s opposition.
- The [[LowCountries|Low Countries]] mattered because Spanish victory there could endanger English trade, Protestant worship, and England's security across the Channel.
- The Dutch [[ActOfAbjuration]] and Anjou's proclamation as Lord of the Netherlands tried to replace Philip II's rule, but Anjou's failed Antwerp coup in the [[FrenchFury]] destroyed the preferred French solution.
- [[HenryDukeOfGuise|Guise]], [[WilliamAllen]], the papal nuncio, [[JamesBeaton]], Mendoza, and others developed a Catholic invasion plan centered on northwest England and [[MaryQueenOfScots|Mary]]'s replacement claim.
- [[FrancisThrockmorton]] linked Mary, London conspirators, Paris conspirators, and foreign diplomats, making the [[ThrockmortonPlot]] the operational bridge between continental invasion hopes and English Catholic networks.
- [[FrancisWalsingham]] used moles inside the French embassy and surveillance of cipher traffic to expose the plot; the disputed [[HenryFagot]] code name shows both the reach and uncertainty of the intelligence record.
- Throckmorton's torture, confession, retraction, and execution left ministers convinced Mary remained dangerous even though the legal path to executing her was not yet clear.
- Mendoza's expulsion on 19 January 1584 severed normal diplomacy with Spain, while the [[BondOfAssociation]] created a political framework for excluding or killing anyone implicated in Elizabeth's death.
- Anjou's death on 10 June 1584 pushed [[France]] into the [[WarOfTheThreeHenrys]], removing France as an immediate invasion platform and making Anglo-Spanish conflict more direct.

## Key Quotes

> "the enemy sleeps not" - the episode's framing of Elizabethan ministers' belief that Catholic plotting was continuous.
