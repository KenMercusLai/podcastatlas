# How to Make Better Decisions | Dr. Michael Platt

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] interview with [[MichaelPlatt]] uses primate neuroscience to explain how attention, social information, hierarchy, hormones, arousal, fatigue, imitation, and evolved constraints shape human choice. The episode's practical synthesis is that decisions are not isolated acts of conscious calculation: environmental options alter switching, social cues acquire economic value, physiology changes the gain on motives, and accuracy often improves when people reduce arousal or offload choices under fatigue. Monkey and laboratory findings provide mechanisms rather than automatic proof about complex human relationships, politics, markets, or brands.

## Key Claims
- [[AttentionForagingModel]] treats attention as prioritization under opportunity cost: richer environments and nearby devices make switching more likely even when the alternative is not consciously selected.
- [[SocialValueAccounting]] describes social information and reciprocity as value-bearing inputs, including status cues and unequal exchanges such as grooming traded for protection.
- [[ContextDependentSocialHormoneEffects]] rejects one-hormone slogans: testosterone can amplify status and risk motives, while oxytocin can reduce vigilance or increase affiliation, but effects depend on sex, hierarchy, threat, and context.
- [[InterpersonalSynchronyAndRapport]] links coordinated gaze, movement, physiology, and brain activity with communication, trust, and group performance, while treating synchrony as a marker rather than proof of causation.
- [[DecisionSpeedAccuracyControl]] frames high arousal and fatigue as conditions that can favor fast, noisy choices; slowing down or shifting decisions to a prepared external structure may protect accuracy.
- [[LossAversion]] is partly attention-sensitive: longer gaze at possible losses predicts stronger aversion, while visual salience can shift attention and choice.
- [[SocialImitationMarketBubbles]] explains how sensitivity to other actors can produce copying and bubble-like markets even when isolated choices are more accurate.
- [[BiologicalRationalityMismatch]] distinguishes computational limits from evolutionary fit: brains tuned for smaller, slower, face-to-face environments can struggle with mass media, global markets, rapid change, and processed abundance.
- [[BrandBelief]] can become social identity when celebrity, status, community, and empathy attach product choice to relationship-like valuation.

## Key Quotes
> "Attention as prioritization" - the episode's compact definition of selective processing.

> "30-million-year-old Swiss army knife" - Platt's alternative to treating the brain as a general-purpose computer.

> "Biological rationality" - the episode's term for evaluating choice relative to the environment a brain evolved to navigate.
