# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》400-2｜一代穷世代穷，难道穷人就该代代穷？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] short episode uses modern time-travel fantasy to explain how difficult class mobility could be in premodern China. It contrasts [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s talent-search measures with [[ZhuYuanzhang|朱元璋]]'s Ming system of household, occupation, dress, housing, utensils, and hairstyle regulation.

The durable synthesis is that social order was managed through both appointment channels and daily-life classification. Han Wudi's use of talent recommendation partly loosened birth constraints at the official-selection level, while Zhu Yuanzhang's [[MingDynasty|明朝]] rules show [[HouseholdOccupationControl|户籍与职业管控]], [[HeavyAgricultureLightCommercePolicy|重农轻商政策]], and [[StatusCodedEverydayRegulation|身份编码式日常管制]] turning occupation and status into visible, enforceable routines.

## Key Claims

- The episode argues that ordinary people, merchants, and poor households in ancient settings faced severe limits on upward mobility, making romanticized time-travel fantasies misleading.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] is presented as a ruler who needed new talent because of outer-relative pressure and therefore used ability-based selection and local recommendation to bring non-hereditary talent into office.
- The Western Han examples include low- or non-elite figures such as 主父偃, [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]], [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]], [[HuoQubing|霍去病]], and 金日磾, plus later recommended figures such as [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] and 公孙弘.
- [[ZhuYuanzhang|朱元璋]] is presented as the stronger class-fixation case: Ming household registration recorded people from birth, updated information every ten years, restricted migration, and tied adults to inherited occupation.
- The episode says Ming occupational categories were backed by punishment; people who fled after disasters could be returned to their origin registration and punished under Ming law.
- Clothing and material rules made status visible: ranks were separated by cut, color, pattern, fabric, sleeve length, and forbidden motifs, while farmers and merchants were assigned different permitted fabrics.
- Merchants are treated as explicitly degraded in the episode's Ming branch: merchant households were barred from some fabrics even when only one family member engaged in trade.
- Housing size, building decoration, drinking vessels, boots, hats, and women's hairstyles are used to show that Zhu Yuanzhang's status order entered routine life rather than remaining at the level of offices and tax registers.
- The host reads these rules through imperial stability: Zhu Yuanzhang wanted visible boundaries between compliant subjects and disorderly people because he believed clear ritual hierarchy made the realm governable.

## Key Quotes

> "比登天都难" - the episode's phrase for changing class as a poor person or merchant in an ancient setting.

> "赤裸裸地歧视商人" - the host's judgment on Ming restrictions that lowered merchant-household dress status.

> "这可不是小事，这是关乎帝国兴亡的大事" - the host's reconstruction of Zhu Yuanzhang's logic for daily-life regulation.

## Connections

- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - comparative case for opening official selection beyond hereditary background through ability and recommendation.
- [[ZhuYuanzhang|朱元璋]] and [[MingDynasty|明朝]] - main case for status order enforced through household, occupation, dress, residence, and objects.
- [[HouseholdOccupationControl|户籍与职业管控]] - the episode extends the concept from administrative legibility into Ming hereditary occupation and migration restriction.
- [[HeavyAgricultureLightCommercePolicy|重农轻商政策]] - the episode adds Ming fabric and household restrictions as a sharper merchant-status degradation case.
- [[StatusCodedEverydayRegulation|身份编码式日常管制]] - new concept for making hierarchy visible through clothing, utensils, housing, and other ordinary practices.
- [[RitualizedSocialBoundary|仪式化社会边界]] and [[LiAsPoliticalOrder|礼制政治秩序]] - broader ritual and boundary frames that explain why daily form could be treated as political order.
