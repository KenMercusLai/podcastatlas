# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》318｜汲黯：汉武帝，我是你的破壁人

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] challenging [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] after the emperor receives a “divine horse,” asks [[SimaXiangru|司马相如]] and other writers for rhapsodies, and has [[LiYannianWesternHan|李延年]] set the work to music. Ji argues that music performed in the ancestral temple should honor predecessors and educate the people rather than monumentalize one ruler's delight in a horse.

The second branch turns from ritual purpose to personnel policy. Although Wudi seeks talent energetically, the episode says that trusted officials can be executed after errors and deception are discovered. Ji warns that capable people are finite and cannot be consumed indefinitely, connecting fearless remonstrance to [[TalentSummonsRulerSelfExamination|求贤中的君主自省]] and the public cost of a ruler's punitive habits.

## Key Claims

- The episode places the horse inside a war economy: repeated campaigns, service burdens, purchasable exemptions, and a later horse payment make equine supply both a state resource and an imperial passion.
- After a horse reportedly emerges from the Wowa waters in the northwest, Wudi commissions literary celebration and has Li Yannian coordinate music for the new work.
- Ji An argues that royal music has public and ritual purposes—honoring ancestors above and educating the people below—that a song celebrating one horse does not satisfy.
- The episode therefore treats the proposed ancestral-temple performance as a case where private excitement attempts to acquire public-sacred authority.
- Ji's “破壁” role is to puncture the emperor's self-confirming atmosphere and restore the distinction between personal pleasure and rulership duty.
- A second remonstrance identifies a personnel contradiction: Wudi seeks worthy people but, in the episode's account, executes valued officials when error and concealment appear.
- Ji warns that talent is limited and cannot support state governance if the ruler continually destroys it.

## Key Quotes

> “上以承祖宗，下以化兆民。” - the episode's compact statement of Ji An's standard for royal music.

> “天下人才有限，不能供皇帝无限诛杀。” - the episode's paraphrase of Ji An's personnel warning.

## Connections

- [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] - direct-speaking official who challenges both the ritual elevation of a private pleasure and the destruction of scarce talent.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler whose horse enthusiasm, cultural commissioning, recruitment effort, and punitive severity create the episode's two governance disputes.
- [[SimaXiangru|司马相如]] and [[LiYannianWesternHan|李延年]] - writer and music official through whom imperial delight is converted into court literature and song.
- [[ImperialPrivateDesireGovernanceRisk|帝王私欲治理风险]] - the horse song adds a symbolic pathway in which personal delight seeks public and ancestral legitimacy rather than only money, office, or material reward.
- [[TalentSummonsRulerSelfExamination|求贤中的君主自省]] - Ji's warning supplies an event-level statement that recruitment cannot compensate indefinitely for ruler-created talent destruction.
