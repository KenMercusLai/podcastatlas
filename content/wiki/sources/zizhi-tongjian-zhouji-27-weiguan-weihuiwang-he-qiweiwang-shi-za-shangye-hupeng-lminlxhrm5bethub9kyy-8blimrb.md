# 《资治通鉴·周纪》27丨围观魏惠王和齐威王是咋商业互捧

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode moves from [[QinState|秦国]]'s 335 BCE seizure of [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]] to the 334 BCE [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]] between [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]] and [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王 / 梁惠王]]. It reads the event as post-[[MalingBattle|马陵之战]] diplomatic reordering: [[TianYing|田婴]] wants [[QiState|齐国]] to cash in victory prestige, while [[HuiShi|惠施]] persuades [[WeiState|魏国]] to use a low posture toward Qi for strategic breathing room. The episode then contrasts Hui Shi's tactical yielding with [[ShenBuhai|申不害]]'s broader [[LowPostureStatecraft|低姿态权宜]] logic, warning that voluntary weakness can become real weakness if it turns into a permanent survival strategy.

## Key Claims
- In 335 BCE, [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] records Qin attacking [[HanState|韩国]] and taking [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]], a place the episode compares to Wei's Hexi as a long-coveted strategic target.
- In 334 BCE, the chronicle gives three notices: Qi and Wei meet at Xuzhou and mutually recognize kingly titles; [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]] builds a high gate and receives criticism from Qu Yijiu; and Yue Wang Wujiang's attack on Qi becomes the setup for Yue's destruction.
- The episode develops the first notice, "齐王、魏王会于徐州以相王," as a staged diplomatic result rather than a simple ceremony.
- Tian Ying is presented as Qi's key promoter. The episode follows [[Shiji|《史记》]] in identifying him as Qi Wei Wang's younger son, while noting Yang Kuan's suspicion that Sima Qian may have confused the genealogy and that Tian Ying may instead have been a younger brother.
- After Maling, Qi's international reputation rises sharply. Tian Ying's policy is to use that victory prestige to draw Wei and Han into a Qi-centered order.
- Wei Hui Wang initially hates Qi and wants revenge, but Hui Shi argues that an all-out attack would leave Wei exposed after previous conflicts with Zhao and Qi.
- Hui Shi's plan is to have Wei appear deferential to Qi, spread the news, and then exploit Chu's likely anger by stirring conflict between [[ChuState|楚国]] and Qi.
- The two strategies fit together: Wei proposes honoring Qi's ruler as king, Qi accepts the recognition, then Qi returns the favor by recognizing Wei Hui Wang's royal title.
- The resulting mutual praise gives Qi new status while letting Wei avoid looking like a fully defeated second-rate state.
- Han's absence from the Xuzhou scene is explained through an earlier low-posture move: Shen Buhai had already advised Han Zhaohou to visit Wei Hui Wang in a deferential, minister-like posture.
- The episode distinguishes Hui Shi's low posture from Shen Buhai's. Hui Shi is choosing the lesser harm for a wounded Wei; Shen Buhai treats showing insufficiency as a more general political method.
- Shen Buhai's "示人有余者人夺之，示人不足者人与之" is treated as a form of "softness overcoming hardness," but the host argues that in Warring States survival competition, permanent self-weakening can become dangerous.

## Key Quotes
> "齐王、魏王会于徐州以相王。" - the Zizhi Tongjian notice at the center of the episode.

> "示人有余者人夺之，示人不足者人与之。" - the Shen Buhai maxim used to explain low-posture strategy.

> "主动示弱只能作为权宜之计" - the host's warning about turning weakness into a basic strategy.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The episode extends the wiki's post-Maling arc by moving from battlefield victory and Qi court aftershock into interstate recognition.
- The Tian Ying kinship note is a source-critical qualification rather than a resolved contradiction: earlier pages follow the episode tradition that he is Qi Wei Wang's younger son, while this source records Yang Kuan's doubt about that genealogy.
- The episode qualifies the existing Shen Buhai governance branch by separating tactical low posture from a long-term identity of weakness; it does not reject the earlier [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment|因功赏罚]] or [[LegalistRulerTechnique|法家君术]] material.
