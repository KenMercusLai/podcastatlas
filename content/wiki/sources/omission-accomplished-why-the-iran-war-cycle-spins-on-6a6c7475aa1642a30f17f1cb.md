## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three segments: the still-spinning [[UnitedStates]]-[[Iran]] war cycle after the June memorandum of understanding, Chinese driverless-taxi technology moving abroad, and [[WallyFunk]]'s late vindication in space. [[GregCarlstrom]] argues that vague diplomatic text can postpone the hard parts of settlement rather than ending conflict, making the Iran segment a source for [[ThinDiplomaticFrameworks]]. [[DonWeinland]] uses robotaxis to show how Chinese AI services can earn more abroad and shape technical standards, while [[AnnWroe]] frames Funk's career as a case of [[GenderBarrierSpaceflight]].

## Key Claims
- [[Oman]] proposed a roughly ten-day truce and a joint regional consortium to manage the [[StraitOfHormuz]] without mandatory tolls, but [[Iran]] rejected the proposal and demanded complete control over inbound Gulf traffic.
- [[GregCarlstrom]] says the June MOU claimed that America and Iran were permanently ending their war, yet fighting resumed after about three weeks.
- The two sides still disagree over frozen Iranian funds: Iran expected immediate release, while America wants phased access after compliance.
- The MOU's 60-day period ends on August 16, 2026, but the episode says serious face-to-face talks on the nuclear file, sanctions relief, and other issues have barely begun.
- Further American strikes could degrade ballistic missiles but would not end drones, short-range missiles, or Iranian political will; blockade pressure would work only slowly, and Iranian retaliation may not force American concessions.
- Carlstrom treats the MOU as thin text, about 14 points over a page and a half, and compares it with unresolved Trump-era files involving [[Houthis]] and [[SaudiArabia]], [[Gaza]] and [[Hamas]], plus [[Lebanon]] and [[Hezbollah]].
- [[DonWeinland]] says a driverless cab outside [[China]] or the [[UnitedStates]] is increasingly likely to use Chinese technology, with [[Baidu]] tied to a Switzerland project and other possible deployments in Singapore, Slovakia, and Saudi Arabia.
- The robotaxi business case can be stronger overseas: the episode contrasts a 10km [[Wuhan]] taxi ride at about 23 yuan with a similar Switzerland ride at about $54.
- The China segment argues that AI-service exports can let China gain export value while pushing some labor-market disruption abroad.
- Weinland warns that first movers can shape local technical standards before countries have mature rules for technologies such as driverless taxis, potentially forcing later [[Waymo]] or other American entrants to conform.
- [[WallyFunk]] joined [[WilliamRandolphLovelace]]'s early women-in-spaceflight testing program at 21 and took the same tests as male candidates.
- Funk later worked for the [[FederalAviationAdministration]] and [[NationalTransportationSafetyBoard]], set up a flight school in Taos, and finally reached space at 82 on a [[BlueOrigin]] flight as [[JeffBezos]]'s guest.

## Key Quotes
> "permanently ending their war" - MOU wording as summarized in the episode.

> "cold feet about girls" - [[AnnWroe]]'s explanation for why Funk and the tested women were excluded from the astronaut corps.

> "first movers" - the China segment's standard-setting warning.
