# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1090丨他曾冒死藏兄、为母哭血，真是不孝之人吗？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the death branch of [[KongRong|孔融]] in 208 CE. It presents Kong Rong's proposal that no fief should lie within the capital region as the political trigger, [[LuCuiLateHan|路粹]]'s bundled accusations as the prosecution channel, and the killing of [[KongRongChildren|Kong Rong's two young children]] as the household cost behind “覆巢之下，安有完卵.”

The episode's strongest synthesis is that political elimination can be made publicly defensible by combining security accusations with private-morality stigma. The charge of “unfilial” speech clashes with the same source's account of Kong Rong's pear story, youthful sheltering of [[ZhangJianLateHan|张俭]], willingness to die for his brother, and extreme mourning for his mother. [[ZhiXiLateHan|脂习]]'s decision to mourn Kong Rong's exposed body then extends [[FuneraryDefianceUnderPoliticalTerror|funerary defiance under political terror]], while Cao Cao's decision to spare him supports the episode's inference that symbolic dissent was tolerated when it did not become an organized power threat.

## Key Claims

- In 208 CE, Kong Rong proposes that the capital's thousand-li region should contain no enfeoffed domains; the host interprets this as a direct threat to [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s political base around [[Xudu|许都]].
- [[LuCuiLateHan|路粹]] submits accusations that combine Kong Rong's Beihai military activity, alleged disloyal contact with [[SunQuan|孙权]]'s envoy, mutual praise with [[MiHengLateHan|祢衡]], and disrespect toward court hierarchy.
- The source reads the resulting case as political prosecution: old conduct, speech, association, and private character are bundled into a death-worthy public image after Kong Rong challenges Cao Cao's power.
- When Kong Rong is arrested, his children answer his plea for their lives with “覆巢之下，安有完卵”; both children are later killed with him.
- [[ZhiXiLateHan|脂习]], an old friend who had repeatedly warned Kong Rong to restrain his speech, publicly mourns his exposed body when others are afraid to approach.
- Cao Cao spares Zhi Xi; the host infers that loyalty and grief could be tolerated when they carried no comparable political capacity.
- The episode treats Kong Rong's allegedly unfilial sayings about parent-child attachment as reputation-destroying material in a Han political culture that publicly privileged filial conduct.
- The same episode counters that label with Kong Rong's childhood pear story, his sixteen-year-old sheltering of Zhang Jian and attempt to bear guilt for [[KongBaoLateHan|孔褒]], and his reported blood-weeping grief after his mother's death.
- The closing pivot contrasts [[LiuBiao|刘表]]'s earlier violent consolidation of Jingzhou with later judgments that he was indecisive, setting up the following succession sequence without resolving it here.

## Key Quotes

> “覆巢之下，安有完卵。” — the children's answer to the hope that the household might escape Kong Rong's sentence.

> “孔文举，你就这么弃我而去，我活着还有啥意思。” — Zhi Xi's mourning speech as presented by the episode.

## Contradictions

- The source itself presents a deliberate contradiction between the “unfilial” accusation and Kong Rong's remembered filial or self-sacrificing conduct. The wiki records this as a conflict between a political label and biographical evidence, not as proof that every attributed saying is false.
- No settled contradiction with existing wiki pages was found. Hanji 895 independently supplies the Zhang Jian shelter case, while Hanji 1007 supplies a genuine capacity and advice-tolerance critique; neither makes the 208 CE prosecution neutral or the “unfilial” label sufficient by itself.
- Transcript normalization: “希律” is normalized to the received late-Han accuser [[LuCuiLateHan|路粹]]. The episode's “京城王畿千里不设封国” wording, exact charge text, children's ages and dialogue, alleged filial sayings, and Cao Cao's motive remain source-scoped pending primary-source verification.
- The closing Liu Biao section is a preview rather than a developed argument; its quotations, the count of fifty-five killed local leaders, and the claim of late-life personality change remain source-scoped.
