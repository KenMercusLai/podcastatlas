# 085 《我和僵尸有个约会》：集灵异、僵尸、悬疑、爱情、奇幻于一体的童年经典

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode reconstructs the opening of [[MyDateWithAVampire|《我和僵尸有个约会》]], from the 1938 红溪村 encounter that turns 况国华, [[FushengMyDateWithAVampire|复生]], and [[YamamotoKazuoMyDateWithAVampire|山本一夫]] into vampires through the 1998 Japan ghost case and the principal characters' convergence around 佳佳大厦. The hosts praise the series for joining supernatural folklore, police investigation, wartime history, urban life, comedy, and romance, while testing its power rules and character logic. Their central ethical question is whether endless life is a gift or an irreversible burden: [[KuangTianyou|况天佑]] and Fusheng experience it as estrangement, yet Kuang's refusal to transform the dying 阿秀 also overrides her stated choice.

## Key Claims

- The series modernizes the jiangshi tradition by placing a stylish professional exorcist, vampires, ghosts, police work, commerce, friendship, and romantic melodrama inside the same contemporary world.
- [[MaXiaoling|马小玲]]'s short skirts, boots, fee-based “cleaning company,” portable ritual tools, and family mission make her a modern urban professional rather than a conventional robed Daoist master.
- Immortality is represented through everyday costs rather than abstract power alone: Kuang must repeatedly change identities and watch others age, while Fusheng remains trapped in a child's body and repeatedly returns to primary school.
- [[ImmortalityTransformationConsent|Immortality-transformation consent]] remains morally unresolved. Kuang refuses 阿秀's request because he regards vampirism as a curse, but protection from an irreversible burden does not automatically justify substituting his judgment for hers.
- Yamamoto's live-blood diet and concealed elderly appearance partly explain unequal abilities, but the hosts treat the power hierarchy, his personal feeding, and several later explanations as possible narrative patches rather than fully settled rules.
- The nine-character formula “临、兵、斗、者、皆、阵、列、在、前” illustrates how a ritual phrase can move from Chinese textual tradition into Japanese esoteric and popular culture, then return through anime and Hong Kong screen media with altered performance and meaning.
- The discussion distinguishes plot fact from interpretation: casting motives, Jiangchen's intentions, sequel explanations, and authorial design are plausible readings rather than verified production history.
