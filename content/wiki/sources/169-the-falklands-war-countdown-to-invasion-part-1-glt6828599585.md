# 169. The Falklands War: Countdown to Invasion (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces the [[FalklandsWarOrigins|origins of the Falklands War]] from competing sovereignty claims and the [[FalklandIslands|Islanders]]' British identity to Argentina's domestic crisis, Britain's attempted leaseback settlement, and the signals that encouraged the junta to expect no military response. It presents the invasion as a contingent miscalculation: [[LeopoldoGaltieri|Leopoldo Galtieri]] and [[JorgeAnaya|Jorge Anaya]] sought nationalist legitimacy, while British retrenchment and the planned withdrawal of [[HMSEndurance|HMS Endurance]] appeared to make seizure cheap.

The South Georgia scrap-metal incident then disrupted Argentina's later timetable and produced a rushed operation. The episode ends with Governor [[RexHunt|Rex Hunt]] receiving warning of the approaching task force, evacuating civilians, destroying classified papers, and preparing 69 Royal Marines to defend Government House.

## Key Claims

- Britain established permanent control of the Falkland Islands in 1833 after earlier British, French, Spanish, and Argentine claims and settlements.
- By 1980 roughly 1,800 Islanders identified strongly as British, had no Indigenous population displaced by the current community, and opposed transfer to Argentina.
- The Malvinas claim became a nationalist symbol through which Argentine leaders expressed resentment of British influence and disappointment with national decline.
- Galtieri's junta faced inflation, strikes, unrest, and the legacy of domestic repression; Anaya promoted recovering the islands before the 150th anniversary of British control.
- Argentina expected the United States to remain supportive and read British economic weakness, political division, defense cuts, and sovereignty negotiations as evidence that London would not fight.
- Britain's planned withdrawal of HMS Endurance did not cause the invasion alone, but reinforced the impression of declining commitment despite warnings from Foreign Secretary Peter Carrington.
- The leaseback proposal showed that London wanted a negotiated transfer, while Islander rejection made an imposed settlement politically difficult.
- The South Georgia landing by nominal scrap-metal workers exposed Argentine activity and brought the invasion forward, reducing preparation time.
- Hunt's warning on 1 April converted a diplomatic crisis into immediate local defense preparations before Argentine forces landed early on 2 April.
