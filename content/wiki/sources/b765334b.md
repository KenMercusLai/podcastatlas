## Summary
This [[TheSaaSPodcast]] episode features [[OmerKhan]] interviewing [[RajivKanhaja]] about turning [[AdButler]] from a teenage side project into an eight-figure ad-serving SaaS company with roughly 20–25 employees. The practical case extends [[FounderLedSales]] and [[CustomerValueBasedPricing]]: accumulated product depth, live demonstrations, rapid customization, accessible engineers, relationship-oriented pricing, and optional month-to-month terms can support enterprise growth without a conventional sales organization. Kanhaja also offers a source-scoped software thesis in which [[AtomicCapabilityServices]] remain stable while agents generate customer-specific interfaces and workflows as [[DisposableSoftware]].

## Key Claims
- Kanhaja first built Pollit.com, then created AdButler after DoubleClick was too expensive and initially unwilling to serve him.
- The dot-com crash collapsed advertising rates and erased receivables owed by a failed ad network, pushing the business from advertising-supported free tools toward paid software.
- AdButler accumulated features through customer requests for roughly a decade before becoming the company's main focus and later moving into enterprise accounts.
- Kanhaja says AdButler generates eight-figure revenue with about 20–25 employees and serves customers including [[Costco]], [[HEB|H-E-B]], and [[HomeDepot|Home Depot]].
- Costco's inbound RFP and live demonstration opened the enterprise market; the company then added an enterprise tier and received further inbound demand.
- AdButler sells through product specialists and demonstrations rather than a conventional enterprise sales organization, while its engineering culture supports fast customer-specific delivery.
- The company prices for healthy delivery and durable relationships rather than maximum extraction, permits month-to-month enterprise arrangements, and reports zero enterprise churn; contract values and retention are founder-reported.
- Some enterprise customers communicate directly with engineers through shared Slack or Microsoft Teams channels, making AdButler operate partly like an embedded ad-tech team.
- Kanhaja attributes delivery speed partly to small, independently deployable components that engineers can build in parallel.
- His AI thesis predicts that cheap code will weaken fixed configurable SaaS: stable APIs or MCP-like capabilities will remain underneath agent-generated customer interfaces, workflows, and business logic.

## Contradictions

- The claim of zero enterprise churn sits in productive tension with month-to-month contracts: it supports a customer-value interpretation but remains founder-reported without cohort definitions or audited retention data.
- Revenue, headcount, customer, contract, pricing, retention, delivery-speed, and historical financial figures are source-scoped. The supplied document is a structured episode summary rather than a transcript, customer study, contract record, or audited financial statement.
