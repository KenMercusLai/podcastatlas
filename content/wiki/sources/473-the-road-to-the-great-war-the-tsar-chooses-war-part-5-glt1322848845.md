# 473. The Road to The Great War: The Tsar Chooses War (Part 5)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[JulyCrisis|July Crisis]] from the expiry of Austria-Hungary's ultimatum to Serbia through the shelling of Belgrade and [[NicholasII|Tsar Nicholas II]]'s approval of Russian general mobilisation. It presents several plausible off-ramps - Grey's conference proposal, Wilhelm's "stop in Belgrade" plan, royal telegrams, and delayed military measures - but shows ministers, generals, alliances, public commitments, and mobilisation systems progressively narrowing their usefulness.

The central contrast is between personal hesitation and state machinery. [[KaiserWilhelmII|Wilhelm II]] and Nicholas corresponded warmly and each resisted some escalatory advice, yet Austria-Hungary sought visible retribution, Russia treated inaction as unacceptable, France protected its alliance while avoiding the appearance of aggression, and Britain kept its freedom of action over [[Belgium]]. Russian general mobilisation did not mechanically make wider war inevitable, but it sharply strengthened military timetables and threat perceptions explored in Part 6.

## Key Claims

- Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on 28 July despite poor coordination between political aims, operational readiness, and contingency planning for Russian intervention.
- Wilhelm judged Serbia's reply a moral victory and proposed occupying Belgrade as security before stopping, but German transmission was weak and Austria-Hungary rejected symbolic limitation as inadequate punishment.
- [[HelmuthVonMoltkeTheYounger|Helmuth von Moltke the Younger]] believed war with Russia was inevitable and delay strategically harmful, even while anticipating civilizational catastrophe.
- Britain debated Belgian neutrality as a political choice rather than an automatic trigger and refused to promise neutrality because ambiguity preserved leverage.
- Nicholas and Wilhelm used personal telegrams to seek restraint, but royal preference could not reliably control ministerial, military, alliance, or logistical systems.
- Russia moved from partial to general mobilisation after Sazonov persuaded Nicholas that delay would leave the empire strategically exposed.
- France prepared quietly and kept troops back from the frontier so that France and Russia would not appear to be the aggressors before British opinion.
- The first Austro-Hungarian shots at Belgrade converted diplomatic crisis into open war while leaders still pursued or discussed mediation.

## Key Quotes

> "stop in Belgrade" - Wilhelm's proposed limited occupation, presented as one of the crisis's last plausible off-ramps.

> "monstrous slaughter" - Nicholas's description of the war for which he did not want to be responsible.

- Wilhelm and Nicholas sometimes sought restraint, but personal reluctance does not erase their authority, earlier commitments, or responsibility for decisions made by their states.
