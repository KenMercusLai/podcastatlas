# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》238｜ 七国之乱，淮南王刘安如何转危为安？（1）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows the immediate aftermath of the Rebellion of the Seven States. Its main political case is [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]]: he reportedly intends to answer Wu's appeal, but the unnamed Huainan chancellor asks for command, closes the gates after receiving it, refuses the king's orders, and keeps the kingdom aligned with the Han court until central support arrives. Liu An's survival therefore reflects a subordinate's institutional intervention rather than a demonstrated loyal choice by the prince.

The episode contrasts that blocked defection with the conduct of [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|庐江王刘赐]] and [[LiuBoJibeiKingWesternHan|衡山王刘勃]], who reject the rising and are later reassigned. It also supplies the 153-151 BCE succession setting: passage controls return after the rebellion, [[LiuRongWesternHan|刘荣]] becomes crown prince, [[HanWudi|刘彻]] becomes king of Jiaodong, and [[EmpressBoJingdiWesternHan|薄皇后]] is deposed after losing the protection associated with Empress Dowager Bo.

## Key Claims

- In 153 BCE, the court restores checkpoint travel credentials that Emperor Wen had abolished, with the episode attributing the reversal to fear of post-rebellion coordination.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] makes Liu Rong crown prince and Liu Che king of Jiaodong in the same month; the host reads Liu Che's simultaneous enfeoffment as a sign of unusual favor.
- Liu An initially prepares to respond to Wu, but the Huainan chancellor gains command, closes the gates, and refuses further royal orders, preventing the kingdom from joining the rebellion.
- A central force led by the marquis of Qucheng later supports Huainan; the episode treats the kingdom's nonparticipation as the reason it avoids destruction.
- Liu Ci, then king of Lujiang, rejects rebellion and seeks Nanyue support; Liu Bo, then king of Hengshan, holds his defenses and is praised after appearing at court.
- Jingdi moves Liu Bo to Jibei and Liu Ci to Hengshan, combining reward for wartime loyalty with a reorganization of southern royal territory.
- The episode places construction and encouraged settlement at Yangling, another Xiongnu marriage alliance, and several royal deaths in the post-rebellion chronology.
- After Empress Dowager Bo's death removes the deposed empress's senior protector, Jingdi strips Empress Bo of rank and confines her in the palace.

## Key Quotes

> “不再听淮南王刘安的命令，继续效忠朝廷。” - the episode's account of the Huainan chancellor's conduct after taking military command.

> “淮南国因此没有受到七国叛乱的牵连。” - the episode's summary of the institutional intervention's effect.

## Connections

- [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]] - prince whose intended participation is blocked by his own chancellor after military authority is delegated.
- [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|衡山王刘赐]] - then Lujiang king, presented here as rejecting the rebellion before his much later anti-court turn.
- [[LiuBoJibeiKingWesternHan|济北王刘勃]] - loyal Hengshan king moved to Jibei after the rebellion.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - ruler restoring travel controls, setting the succession field, and reorganizing royal territories.
- [[LiuRongWesternHan|刘荣]] and [[HanWudi|刘彻]] - crown prince and Jiaodong king in the episode's 153 BCE succession arrangement.
- [[EmpressBoJingdiWesternHan|薄皇后]] - empress whose deposition follows the death of her senior Bo-family protector.

## Contradictions

- Liu An's later profile as a sophisticated planner does not erase this earlier failure of command. The episode specifically makes his survival depend on a chancellor who takes control and disobeys him.
- Liu Ci's rejection of the Seven States rising qualifies any simple lifelong-rebel profile. Later sources describe a separate escalation under Han Wudi after official review and loss of appointment power.
- Identity note: this Liu Bo is the son of [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]], first king of Hengshan and later king of Jibei. He is distinct from [[LiuBoChangshanKingWesternHan|常山王刘勃]] and [[LiuBoChangyiKingWesternHan|昌邑王刘髆]].
- The episode's motives for restoring checkpoint controls, favoring Liu Che, reassigning royal territories, and deposing Empress Bo remain source interpretations; it does not establish the complete legal or court process.
