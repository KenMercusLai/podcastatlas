# The Future of Everything: What CEOs of Circle, CrowdStrike & More See Coming in 2026

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] Davos episode strings together CEO interviews on technologies the guests expect to shape 2026: regulated stablecoins, AI-native cybersecurity, electric air taxis, defense aviation, AI cloud infrastructure, and energy. The durable pattern is that frontier technology becomes a business only after it clears trust, regulation, certification, security, financing, labor, and power constraints. [[Circle]] and [[CrowdStrike]] supply the software-and-trust half of the episode, while [[ArcherAviation|Archer Aviation]] and [[Crusoe]] move the discussion into aircraft certification, defense systems, project finance, gas turbines, batteries, and new energy sources.

## Key Claims
- [[Circle]] frames [[USDC]] and [[Stablecoins|stablecoins]] as internet-native, programmable dollars rather than mainly speculative crypto assets.
- Circle's regulated path is presented as a credibility advantage: open public networks are paired with reserves, auditors, regulators, and bank relationships.
- The source says the [[GENIUSAct|GENIUS Act]] prevents a payment stablecoin issuer from paying interest directly to stablecoin holders, while leaving room for platforms and distributors to use rewards or loyalty incentives.
- Stablecoins are positioned as infrastructure for remittances, cross-border workers, small businesses, prediction markets, on-chain credit, and possible AI/smart-contract underwriting.
- The AI labor discussion warns that capital owners may capture much of the gain if AI performs more labor, making broad ownership and management of AI agents part of the social-contract question.
- [[GeorgeKurtz|George Kurtz]] says AI compresses cyberattack timelines and lets less sophisticated attackers operate with greater sophistication.
- The CrowdStrike section names [[PromptOnlyAutonomousMalware|prompt-only autonomous malware]], fake remote employees, session-token theft, help-desk weakness, browser-layer security, and agent permission escalation as concrete AI-era risks.
- [[AIDetectionAndResponse|AI detection and response]] is presented as a new security category for a workplace where each employee may control many AI agents.
- [[ArcherAviation|Archer Aviation]] says eVTOL commercialization depends first on certification, safety proof, limited early city launches, and public comfort.
- Archer's [[ProjectNix|Project Nix]] partnership with [[Anduril]] reframes eVTOL technology as a defense-aviation cost and pilot-risk problem, not only urban mobility.
- [[Crusoe]] presents [[EnergyFirstNeocloud|energy-first neocloud]] strategy as a response to the physical bottlenecks of AI: power, data centers, labor, gas turbines, batteries, cooling, financing, and customer leases.
- Crusoe's Abilene project combines a large substation, onsite gas generation, thousands of workers, and a long-term [[Oracle]] lease, making [[AIInfrastructureDebtFinancing|AI infrastructure debt financing]] inseparable from power and customer-credit underwriting.
- Crusoe argues that rising rack density, Blackwell/Vera Rubin systems, second-life batteries, SMRs, hydro, geothermal, and behind-the-meter generation all become part of AI infrastructure strategy.
- The episode is mostly CEO-led and optimistic; risks are often framed through the operating needs of the companies being interviewed.

## Key Quotes
> "HTTP for dollars" - Circle's stablecoin infrastructure analogy.

> "prompt-only autonomous malware" - CrowdStrike's AI-era offensive-malware example.

> "AI detection and response" - CrowdStrike's proposed category for defending agent-heavy workplaces.

> "Stargate" - Crusoe's source term for very large OpenAI-related compute efforts.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found.
- The stablecoin section extends rather than resolves the existing [[Stablecoins]] tension: Circle's compliance path supports trust and banking adoption, while prior pages still preserve issuer, AML, sanctions, political-brand, and irreversible-transfer risks.
- The Crusoe section qualifies [[DataCenterDebtRisk]] and [[AICircularInfrastructureFinancing]] without eliminating them. The source argues that long-term leases and transferable AI demand can support project debt, but the wiki still treats utilization, refinancing, chip depreciation, and customer concentration as live tests.
- The eVTOL section is in tension with ordinary [[AviationSafetyRules]] caution: the source expects fast public rollout and autonomy over time, while certification, pilot safety, air-traffic infrastructure, and public acceptance remain the real gate.
