## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s early branch from [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-6-dingji-tianshilun-touziren-lvgong-lhqqja27zxdiypgf6wuv1abgkw3j|Qinji 128-6]] by showing how a corvee escort failure becomes exile, follower recruitment, mythic reputation, and finally [[PeiCountyUprisingMobilization|沛县起义动员]]. It then introduces [[XiangYu|项羽]] and [[XiangLiang|项梁]] as a sharply different anti-Qin line: Chu noble memory, martial ambition, and [[KuaijiCommanderyCoup|会稽郡守府夺权]] convert [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]] resources into an eight-thousand-person force. The core contrast is that Liu Bang rises through local trust, county-office ties, and opportunistic legitimacy, while Xiang Liang and Xiang Yu begin from aristocratic restoration identity and direct violent seizure of commandery power.

## Key Claims
- [[LiuBang|刘邦]] is ordered as Sishui亭长 to escort Pei County corvee laborers toward [[Lishan|骊山]], shortly after he himself has returned from labor service connected to Qin imperial projects.
- Laborers keep fleeing during the journey; because Qin punishment for missing personnel threatens Liu Bang as escort, he releases the remaining men and turns fugitive himself.
- [[FanKuai|樊哙]] and several others follow Liu Bang out of gratitude, showing that his first armed base grows from personal reciprocity before formal political authority.
- Liu Bang hides around [[MangdangMountain|芒砀山]], where the white-snake/red-emperor story begins to circulate and makes local youth think he has unusual fate.
- After the [[DazexiangUprising|大泽乡起义]] spreads, the Pei County magistrate first tries to summon Liu Bang's outlaw group back through Fan Kuai on the advice of [[XiaoHe|萧何]] and [[CaoShen|曹参]].
- When Liu Bang returns with hundreds of followers, the magistrate fears he cannot control them, closes the gates, and tries to kill Xiao He and Cao Shen.
- Xiao He and Cao Shen escape to Liu Bang, who shoots a letter into the city urging local elders to kill the magistrate before outside rebels arrive and punish Pei County.
- Pei County residents kill the magistrate, open the city, and name Liu Bang [[PeiCounty|沛公]]; Xiao He and Cao Shen then help gather more than three thousand men.
- The episode stresses that Liu Bang still begins from a weak social and military position compared with the Chu noble line.
- [[XiangYu|项羽]] is introduced as a Chu aristocratic descendant and [[XiangYan|项燕]]'s grandson, with a boyhood claim that he wants to study the "万人敌" rather than mere writing or swordsmanship.
- Xiang Yu's gifts and flaws are presented together: unusual strength, talent, and ambition, but also impatience and unwillingness to study military texts deeply.
- [[XiangLiang|项梁]], Xiang Yan's son and Xiang Yu's uncle, uses [[YinTong|殷通]]'s anti-Qin invitation as an opportunity to seize rather than serve commandery power.
- Xiang Liang brings armed Xiang Yu into the meeting under the pretext that only Xiang Yu knows [[HuanChu|桓楚]]'s hiding place.
- Xiang Yu kills Yin Tong, Xiang Liang takes the commandery seal, and Xiang Yu's violence against resisting guards shocks the commandery office into submission.
- Xiang Liang then presents the killing as a righteous anti-Qin rising, even though the source says Yin Tong was already planning to respond to [[ChenSheng|陈胜]].
- Xiang Liang becomes Kuaiji commandery governor, Xiang Yu becomes deputy general, and the two collect nearly eight thousand troops in 209 BCE, when Xiang Yu is twenty-four.
- The transcript ends by moving toward a Zhao-camp servant-soldier story, so this source page keeps that final thread as incomplete setup rather than a settled claim.

## Key Quotes
> "大丈夫有什么好害怕的" - Liu Bang before the snake-slaying episode.

> "学万人敌" - Xiang Yu's stated ambition beyond writing or one-on-one swordsmanship.

> "殷通想黑白通吃" - the source's judgment on Yin Tong's failed attempt to manage both Qin office and rebel opportunity.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction found. This source deepens the Liu Bang and Xiang Yu contrast already foreshadowed in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-6-dingji-tianshilun-touziren-lvgong-lhqqja27zxdiypgf6wuv1abgkw3j|Qinji 128-6]] rather than overturning it.
- The white-snake story is recorded as a legitimacy narrative around Liu Bang, not as evidence that sacred genealogy is historically verifiable.
- The source creates a moral tension around Xiang Liang's justification: it calls the killing of Yin Tong an anti-Qin uprising, while also noting that Yin Tong himself intended to rise against Qin and was preempted because Xiang Liang refused a subordinate position.
