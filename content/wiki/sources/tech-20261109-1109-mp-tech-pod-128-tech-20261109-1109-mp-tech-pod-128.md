## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] installment of [[ClockedOut|Clocked Out]] follows [[DrewHaven]] and [[LindsayHaven]], former technology workers whose initially temporary departures became a household life organized around caregiving, health, community, and control of time. Their case extends [[EarlyRetirementWorkAutonomy]] by showing that leaving paid employment can depend not only on accumulated resources but also on [[EnoughBasedWorkExit|defining enough]], lowering consumption, and repeatedly choosing time over additional purchases.

The episode also keeps the transition's costs visible. Lindsay describes gendered promotion barriers and profit pressure in video games; Drew describes a Silicon Valley culture increasingly dominated by profit and work that damaged his sleep, stress, and health. Outside employment, both have had to rebuild identity and social connection while managing financial limits, including one car shared by three adults.

## Key Claims
- Lindsay left the video game industry, returned to school, and later became a full-time caregiver after family circumstances and the pandemic changed her expectation of returning to paid work.
- Lindsay says she enjoyed game work but objected to profit maximization and experienced subtle gender discrimination in promotion decisions.
- Drew left paid technology work after becoming disillusioned with company culture and concluding that job stress was harming his sleep and health.
- Drew describes Silicon Valley as shifting away from building toward profit as technology companies and venture capital consolidated, while treating many corporate social-purpose claims as marketing.
- Neither departure began as a firm promise of permanent retirement; both became durable only as family structure, caregiving, health, and priorities changed.
- Paid employment continued to shape identity after exit: Drew found the word "retired" strange, while Lindsay felt pressure to justify caregiving and volunteering or explain when she would return to work.
- The couple rebuilt purpose and relationships through local permaculture, hobby groups, volunteering, family life, and neighborhood community rather than workplace ties.
- Their job-free arrangement depends on deliberate limits around housing, vehicles, and purchases, not on freedom from financial concern.

## Key Quotes
> "rat race" - Lindsay's description of Bay Area congestion and the time and spending demands surrounding technology work.

> "enough" - Drew's framing of making existing resources support the life they wanted rather than waiting for a predetermined retirement target.
