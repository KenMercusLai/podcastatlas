# Not in Kansas any more: Republicans get a thrashing

## Summary

This [[EconomistPodcasts]] episode interprets the reported 2026 U.S. midterms as a broad [[DemocraticParty|Democratic]] victory: control of both congressional chambers, several governorships, gains in normally Republican states, and especially large movement in Texas. It links the result to cost-of-living frustration, young and Hispanic voter movement, opposition to aggressive immigration enforcement, weak Trump-backed nominees, and [[RedistrictingBackfireRisk|redistricting that expanded Republican exposure instead of protecting the party]].

The discussion also separates electoral rejection from governing capacity. [[DonaldTrump]] accepts the result and offers cooperation, but presidential vetoes, possible resistance to oversight, and pressure for impeachment may limit Democratic delivery. The result resolves earlier source-scoped tests around [[TexasElectoralRealignment]], [[AbdulElSayed]], and [[MidtermBacklashForecasting]] while opening a [[RepublicanParty]] identity struggle over Trumpism and the 2028 succession.

## Key Claims

- The source reports that Democrats won the House, Senate, and governorships including Texas, Ohio, Georgia, and Iowa, with gains extending into states such as North Carolina, South Carolina, Kansas, and Texas.
- The election proceeded without serious reported fraud allegations, intimidation, or immediate court challenges, and Trump publicly accepted the outcome; the episode attributes this partly to the scale of the result.
- Young and Hispanic voters moved toward Democrats, with the episode tying the Hispanic reversal to unmet price and opportunity promises and to a distinction between border security and widespread community arrests by ICE.
- [[JamesTalarico]] won a Texas Senate seat and [[GinaHinojosa]] won the governorship, which the episode treats as evidence of a deeper [[TexasElectoralRealignment|Texas shift]] rather than a single isolated upset.
- Trump-backed candidates such as Ken Paxton are presented as loyalty-selected but weak with broader electorates, while harsh campaigning and mid-cycle district redesign are said to have repelled or exposed voters rather than securing Republican control.
- [[AbdulElSayed]] won Michigan's Senate race, but by a smaller margin than less-partisan Democrats in more Republican states, preserving a distinction between left-populist viability and the broader reach of less overtly partisan candidates.
- Democrats inherit a cost-of-living and practical-governance mandate but face veto power, limited leverage over subpoenas, pressure for impeachment, and difficulty reversing healthcare cuts or ending the war in Iran.
- The defeat intensifies Republican uncertainty over whether the party remains Trumpist and populist, returns toward conventional conservatism, or reorganizes around a new 2028 field.
