# 2026秋季篇番外｜进入大厂两个月，她选择了自杀

## Summary

This [[NittanWuyu|日谈物语]] episode reconstructs [[MoriMina|森美菜]]'s death after 72 days at [[WatamiGroup|和民集团]] and her parents' 2,736-day campaign to establish responsibility. It argues that understaffing, minimal practical training, late-night transport failure, performance-scored study and volunteering, and a corporate ideology of dreams and gratitude combined to turn nominally voluntary activity into [[CoercedVoluntaryLabor|controlled labor]] and acute sleep deprivation. The later workers' compensation decision and settlement make the case a bridge between [[InternalizedOverwork]], [[CorporateValuesLaborControl]], and [[WorkRelatedSuicideRecognition]].

## Key Claims

- [[MoriMina|森美菜]], a 26-year-old Kyoto art graduate attracted by Watami's agricultural and social-change vision, was assigned to a late-closing izakaya rather than the rural or art-related work she hoped to pursue.
- [[WatamiGroup|和民集团]] reportedly recruited large numbers of young management candidates into a narrow promotion funnel while three-year employee retention remained below five percent.
- New hires received extensive ideology and founder-centered study but only about four hours of kitchen training before frontline placement.
- Mina's nominal 4 p.m.–1 a.m. schedule routinely extended to 4 a.m. or later; the last train had already stopped, walking to the dormitory was unsafe and slow, and waiting for the first train further compressed sleep.
- Weekly reading, reports, training, and volunteering were described as voluntary but affected assessment and advancement, illustrating [[CoercedVoluntaryLabor]].
- Mina recorded pain, slowed functioning, low mood, and a plea for rescue; store management reported her excessive hours and fatigue, but headquarters treated the problem as ordinary newcomer maladjustment.
- She died on June 12, 2008 after 72 days of employment; the episode reports 227 hours outside prescribed work time and only three to four hours of sleep per day near the end.
- Her parents used schedules, wage records, training plans, notebooks, computer files, union support, media attention, workers' compensation review, and litigation to challenge Watami's personal-psychology explanation.
- Her death was recognized as work-related in 2012, and a 2015 settlement required legal responsibility, compensation, public apology, governance changes, and recurrence-prevention measures.

## Key Quotes

> "有没有人能够救救我" — the plea the episode attributes to Mina's May 15 notebook.

> "可以不参加、但会失去考核分数" — the episode's summary of nominally voluntary activities tied to advancement.

> "没有任何一件工作是必须完成的" — the hosts' closing boundary for workers whose health or safety is failing.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode's evidence strengthens the wiki's distinction between nominal choice and actual organizational control.
- Watami initially attributed the death primarily to an individual psychological problem and disputed whether study, volunteering, waiting, and early arrival were work; the later compensation decision and settlement rejected a complete separation between those burdens and employment.
- The episode is a secondary narrative. Exact hours, employee counts, retention, promotion ratios, agricultural procurement, Japanese legal thresholds, business outcomes, dialogue, motives, and the case's nationwide doctrinal effect remain source-scoped pending primary judgments, regulations, and company records.
