# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》551丨揭秘汉成帝的皇位传承之谜

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Yuanyan 3-4 / 10-9 BCE under [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]], immediately before the succession decision later discussed in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-552-tongtou-renxing-de-ren-zhichang-pingbuqingyun-lnnhr8e-67qdqwhzi1h0qav-valw|Hanji 552]]. It moves from Chengdi's Changyang Palace hunting spectacle and [[YangXiong|扬雄]]'s resulting fu context to the court comparison between the Dingtao king [[HanAidi|刘欣 / 后来的汉哀帝]] and [[LiuXingZhongshanKingWesternHan|中山王刘兴]].

The durable synthesis is that Chengdi's heir choice emerges before the formal ministerial debate as a mixture of performance, kinship form, consort-house networking, and survival strategy. Liu Xin looks legally prepared and ritually alert, while Liu Xing looks clumsy in learning and etiquette; yet the episode keeps open a source-scoped alternative that Liu Xing's visible incompetence may have been a protective withdrawal from a succession contest he was unlikely to win.

## Key Claims

- Chengdi's Changyang Palace animal spectacle shows late-reign luxury and coercive mobilization behind public display.
- [[YangXiong|扬雄]]'s 《长杨赋》 is tied to this Chengdi-era hunting occasion, extending his wiki role beyond later biography and remonstrance.
- The Dingtao king distinguishes himself in front of Chengdi by bringing proper officials, citing legal procedure, and interpreting the [[Shijing|《诗经》]].
- [[LiuXingZhongshanKingWesternHan|中山王刘兴]] loses standing through weak answers, halting [[Shangshu|《尚书》]] recitation, poor dining etiquette, and visible inattention to dress.
- [[FuTaihouWesternHan|傅太后]] advances the Dingtao line by gifting [[ZhaoFeiyanWesternHan|赵飞燕]], [[ZhaoHedeWesternHan|赵合德]], and [[WangGenWesternHan|王根]], who then praise Liu Xin to Chengdi.
- Chengdi's preference for Liu Xin is framed as personal affection for [[LiuKangDingtaoKingWesternHan|定陶恭王刘康]]'s son, ritual convenience of uncle-nephew adoption, and avoidance of the awkward brother-to-son problem.
- The host proposes that Liu Xing's poor performance may also be read as self-protection, since failed succession contention could be lethal.
