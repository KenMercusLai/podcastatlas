# 470. The Road to The Great War: The Kaiser's Blank Cheque (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[JulyCrisis|July Crisis]] from [[FranzJosephI|Franz Joseph I]]'s appeal for German support through [[AlexanderHoyos|Alexander Hoyos]]'s Berlin mission, [[KaiserWilhelmII|Kaiser Wilhelm II]]'s endorsement, and [[TheobaldVonBethmannHollweg|Theobald von Bethmann Hollweg]]'s confirmation of the German "blank cheque" to [[AustriaHungary]]. It argues that Berlin and Vienna were not executing a settled plan for general European war: they wanted Serbia punished, expected German backing to deter Russia, and continued to describe their posture as defensive even while enabling an aggressive local war.

The episode makes miscalculation consequential without turning it into innocence. German leaders gave Austria-Hungary broad discretion, accepted preventive-war logic if Russia intervened, and underestimated how their support would alter Vienna's risk tolerance. Vienna then lost the advantage of speed through [[IstvanTisza|Istvan Tisza]]'s resistance, dual-monarchy bargaining, railway and harvest-leave constraints, and a desire to wait out the French visit to St Petersburg; by the time Franz Joseph approved the harsh ultimatum on 21 July, he understood that Russia might not tolerate it.

## Key Claims

- Franz Joseph framed Sarajevo as part of a Serbian and pan-Slav threat to his dynasty, empire, and European monarchy rather than treating the assassination as a merely convenient pretext.
- Hoyos's mission found senior German leaders dispersed for the summer, weakening the case for a fully staged German timetable while not reducing the importance of the assurance they gave.
- Wilhelm and Bethmann Hollweg promised full support and urged speed because they expected Russia to protest but probably retreat in the face of German backing.
- The blank cheque was intended to localize a punitive war but was dangerously open-ended because Germany did not define limits on Austria-Hungary's action.
- German fear centered on Russia's army, railways, rearmament, French alliance, and future strength; some leaders therefore treated an earlier war as preferable if deterrence failed.
- Austria-Hungary delayed while Tisza resisted immediate war, mobilization collided with railway and harvest constraints, and officials sought to separate the ultimatum from the Franco-Russian summit.
- Vienna developed no clear Russian contingency or Serbian end state, yet Franz Joseph approved a deliberately harsh ultimatum despite recognizing its likely international danger.

## Key Quotes

> "full support" - the substance of Wilhelm's assurance that Germany would stand behind Austria-Hungary.

> "blank cheque" - the later name for Bethmann Hollweg's open-ended confirmation that Berlin would not dictate Vienna's course.
