## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] episode follows [[NicoleBernardDawes]] from her childhood around [[CapeCodChips]] through the founding, crisis, pivot, and eventual loss of control of [[LateJulySnacks]]. The central business lesson is that organic mission and national retail access did not become a durable company until the brand found tortilla chips, a category with stronger [[ProductCategoryVelocity]], better allergy fit, and broader [[CPGDistribution]] potential. The episode also introduces [[Nixie]] as Nicole's next taste-first, organic beverage company after [[SnydersLance]] and [[CampbellSoupCompany]] changed Late July's ownership path.

## Key Claims
- [[CapeCodChips]] shaped Nicole's belief that natural or better-for-you snacks still had to win on taste.
- Late July's 2003 trade-show orders and national retail access did not translate into repeat consumer demand.
- Organic crackers moved too slowly, and premium sandwich cookies were loved but difficult to make profitable on promotion.
- The 2009 crisis combined [[SteveBernard]]'s death, a called equipment loan, discontinued cookie revenue, and allergy-driven product risk.
- [[GaryHirschberg]], [[MegHirschberg]], [[Stonyfield]], and [[RSFFinance]] illustrate [[MissionAlignedRescueCapital]] during a financing shock.
- Tortilla chips worked because they matched the brand promise while improving category frequency, margin prospects, gluten-free fit, nut-free fit, and channel legibility.
- [[StopAndShop]] turned the tortilla chip launch from a natural-retail test into a conventional grocery expansion path.
- The [[SnydersLance]] and [[CampbellSoupCompany]] ownership sequence made [[AcquisitionContractLanguage]] and [[PostAcquisitionFounderIdentity]] concrete rather than abstract.
- [[Nixie]] applies Nicole's [[TasteFirstCPGPositioning]] to organic sparkling water and soda after Late July.

## Key Quotes
> "overnight success seven years in the making" - Nicole's description of the [[StopAndShop]] win.

> "taste is everything" - Nicole's closing lesson about repeat purchase.

> "key person insurance" - Nicole's retrospective financing lesson after the death-of-a-member loan default.
