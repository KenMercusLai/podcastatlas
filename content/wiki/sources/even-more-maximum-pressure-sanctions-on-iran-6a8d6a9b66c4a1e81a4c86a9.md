# Even-more-maximum pressure: sanctions on Iran

## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three cases where headline-level change depends on implementation details. [[FraserMcIlwraith]] examines [[ScottBessent|Scott Bessent / 贝森特]]'s "Operation Economic Outcast" and argues that renewed [[IranSanctions]] can hurt through oil revenue, banking pressure, [[China]], and the [[UnitedArabEmirates]], but that the concrete announcement was smaller than the rhetoric. [[JoshSpencer]] then uses Philippine offshoring to show AI augmenting workers while compressing basic tasks, and [[VishnuPadmanabhan]] closes with [[GauravKarna]]'s [[LinkedIn]]-based evidence that city wage gaps contain large place premiums.

## Key Claims
- Bessent presents Operation Economic Outcast as a severe U.S. sanctions campaign against Iran, but the source says the actual announcement was narrower than the "Economic D-Day" rhetoric suggested.
- Iran has lived under heavy sanctions since 1979, so new threats do not automatically create political shock.
- The source says Iran's economy is under severe strain: the rial has lost major value, fighting has damaged industry, and U.S. blockade enforcement has reportedly collapsed oil income.
- The main sanctions targets are not only Iran itself but third-country enablers, especially Chinese oil buyers and UAE-based trade, exchange-house, and shadow-bank channels.
- The UAE's August 18 halt to Iran-linked trade and financial transactions makes it an important compliance node, while Chinese-bank sanctions are harder because of U.S.-China escalation risk.
- Iran may read the sanctions pivot as evidence that America is less willing to continue military pressure, and it may retaliate against cooperating states, bases, energy facilities, or the [[StraitOfHormuz]].
- The Philippine offshoring segment says the industry had about 1.9 million workers at the end of 2025 and was still growing despite AI exposure.
- AI helps some offshore workers resolve more complaints, handle email, and move into more complex or outcome-priced work.
- Basic tier-one tasks such as data entry and simple order-status checks are already more vulnerable to AI automation.
- AI monitoring can intensify call-center discipline when quality-scoring systems influence termination decisions.
- The wage segment separates city wage gaps into worker composition and city place effects.
- Karna's LinkedIn-based research is cited for the claim that place effects explain a large share of within-country wage gaps and most cross-border wage gaps.
