## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty-Corino]] interview [[SuyashPasi]] of the [[HumanRightsResearchCenter|Human Rights Research Center]] about the 2025 relationship between major technology leaders and the [[DonaldTrump]] [[WhiteHouse]]. The episode frames the relationship as strategic accommodation: AI companies benefited from deregulation and federal pressure against state AI rules, while immigration policy and protectionist [[MAGA]] factions created friction with technology employers.

The episode's main wiki contribution is [[TechGovernmentAccommodation]]: even leaders who are not ideological "true believers" may become publicly or privately agreeable to the federal government when access, regulation, immigration, crypto, and defense contracts are at stake. It also adds [[FederalAIPreemption]], [[H1BVisaCoalitionFaultLine]], and [[DualUseDefenseTechnology]] as connected policy surfaces.

## Key Claims
- The episode presents 2025 as a year when prominent technology leaders visibly aligned themselves with the Trump administration, beginning with their presence at Trump's inauguration.
- [[SuyashPasi]] describes a mix of incentives and disincentives pushing companies and leaders to comply with, placate, or work around the administration.
- AI companies are framed as immediate beneficiaries of deregulation and federal efforts to limit state-level AI regulation.
- The source treats crypto's renewed prominence and more libertarian, anti-DEI policy language as part of the same political environment.
- Pasi argues that some technology leaders now appear to need greater agreeability toward the federal government than they did in earlier periods.
- The episode uses Bill Gates' reported dinner with Trump and possible interest in softening USAID cuts as an example of access-seeking behavior, while keeping the example source-scoped.
- Immigration is the clearest fault line: technology leaders want more skilled-worker visas, while protectionist MAGA figures, including the [[SteveBannon]] faction, resist H-1B-style migration.
- Pasi expects neither elimination of H-1B visas nor major immigration expansion in the near term, because Trump has signaled that the visas have value even while restrictionist factions remain influential.
- [[Palantir]] and other defense or government technology firms are framed as gaining prominence as military tech becomes more acceptable and AI's dual-use character links civilian and military capability.
- The [[Ukraine]]-[[Russia]] war and [[China]]'s drone-production advantages are used to argue that U.S. military technology emphasis may grow, especially around dual-use systems with civilian or economic benefits.
- Pasi says the 2026 midterms will test how the "tech right" and less ideological technology leaders continue aligning with the administration.

## Key Quotes
> "web of incentives and disincentives" - Pasi's frame for how companies respond to the administration.

> "tech right" - faction label for ideologically aligned technology actors.

> "endless wars" - the political boundary Pasi says some protectionist factions may use around military technology.
