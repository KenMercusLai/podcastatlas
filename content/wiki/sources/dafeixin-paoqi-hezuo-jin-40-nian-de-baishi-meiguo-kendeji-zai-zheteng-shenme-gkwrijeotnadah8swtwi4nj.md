# 达霏欣｜抛弃合作近 40 年的百事，美国肯德基在折腾什么？

## Summary

This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode opens with short updates on [[Anthropic]], [[Wendys|Wendy's]], [[Audi]], and [[Instagram]], then explains why U.S. [[KFC]] plans to replace [[PepsiCo|Pepsi]] with [[CocaCola|Coca-Cola]] after a relationship dating to 1986. Its durable contribution is [[FastFoodBeverageOccasionStrategy]]: customized refreshers, dirty sodas, and limited drinks can create off-peak visits and higher tickets, but their value depends on [[BeverageDevelopmentFeasibility]] and whether added ingredients, equipment, training, and supply complexity remain operable at chain scale.

## Key Claims

- The episode says U.S. KFC will begin changing fountain-drink supply from Pepsi to Coca-Cola in 2027 while international markets continue serving Pepsi products; it links the old relationship to Pepsi's 1986 purchase of KFC and the 1997 restaurant-business separation.
- KFC is presented as a pressured U.S. business despite global growth: the source reports first-half U.S. sales down 2% and says newer chicken chains gained share with sandwiches and boneless products that are easier to eat in cars.
- KFC management is described as using Coca-Cola's beverage-development capability to create more reasons to visit, including outside normal meal periods; the episode says Coca-Cola won a competitive proposal process partly through a future-sales-growth commitment.
- Refreshers and dirty sodas turn drinks from a standard meal accompaniment into colorful, customizable products aimed at younger consumers, afternoon demand, social sharing, and incremental spending.
- The source says limited-product customers spend about US$3 more per visit on average and reports [[TacoBell|Taco Bell]] targeting US$5 billion in annual beverage sales by 2030; both figures remain episode-attributed.
- New drinks require more ingredients and steps than fountain soda. The episode says only about one fifth of roughly 80 annual [[Starbucks]] concepts pass internal tests and that [[McDonalds|McDonald's]] plans dedicated beverage staff and work areas across about 14,000 U.S. stores.
- The growing need for customized drinks can loosen traditional single-supplier relationships: McDonald's is reportedly developing drinks beyond Coca-Cola's portfolio and using additional suppliers, including energy-drink bases.
- [[PepsiCo|PepsiCo]] is presented as having divided management attention between beverages and snacks. Inflation, price increases, GLP-1 use, and protein-oriented consumption are described as pressures on the snack portfolio that had previously offset beverage weakness.
- The source cautions that traffic gains after restaurant beverage-supplier changes often fade after the first few months, so Coca-Cola's long-run effect on KFC demand is unresolved.
- Short items report that Anthropic had published an IPO filing with rapid revenue growth and large long-term compute commitments; Wendy's China expansion and reported Haidilao talks had stalled; Audi split its China brand systems between FAW and SAIC; and Instagram was testing longer and episodic video. These claims remain source-scoped current-event summaries.
