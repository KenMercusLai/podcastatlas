# 660. Dawn of the Samurai: Japan's Greatest Warrior (Part 3)

## Summary
This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[MinamotoNoYoshitsune]] from childhood legend into the [[GenpeiWar]] politics that bring him to Kyoto. The hosts use the Benkei bridge story, Yoshitsune's hidden origins, and his 1180 meeting with [[MinamotoNoYoritomo]] to distinguish legendary hero-making from the more certain record of a fast, aggressive commander.

The episode's main political arc is [[KisoYoshinaka]]'s sudden rise and collapse. Kiso defeats the [[TairaClan]] at [[KurikaraBattle|Kurikara]], drives them from [[Kyoto]], mishandles the capital, attacks [[GoShirakawa]], and thereby gives Yoritomo the legitimacy pretext to send Yoshitsune and Noriyori against him.

## Key Claims
- Yoshitsune is presented as both a real commander and a mythic hero whose youth is preserved through stories of Benkei, Mount Kurama, Tengu training, and revenge against the Taira.
- After Kiyomori's death, famine, plague, and food politics weaken Taira prestige while Yoritomo builds reputation from the east.
- Kiso's victory at [[KurikaraBattle|Kurikara]] is framed as one of the decisive samurai battlefield successes, combining deception, terrain, timing, and panic.
- Go-Shirakawa uses military protectors opportunistically: he abandons the Taira, legitimizes Kiso, then secretly turns to Yoritomo when Kiso becomes dangerous.
- Kiso's attack on the cloistered emperor transforms him from anti-Taira victor into a legitimacy problem that Yoritomo can destroy.
- Yoshitsune's crossing at Uji and relief of Go-Shirakawa show the first historically clear version of his speed, decisiveness, and political usefulness to Yoritomo.

## Key Quotes
> "wild monkey of Kiso" - Go-Shirakawa's reported description of Kiso after the occupation of Kyoto.
