## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode continues the show's AI-infrastructure series by visiting [[RedwoodMaterials]], an EV battery recycling startup expanding into grid-scale storage for data centers. [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty-Corino]] uses [[ColinCampbell]]'s San Francisco lab tour and Redwood's [[Nevada]] deployment to show why fast power access has become part of the AI buildout. The source adds [[SecondLifeEVBatteryStorage]] as a concrete response to [[AIEnergyBottleneck]] and [[DataCenterPowerBottleneck]].

## Key Claims
- [[RedwoodMaterials]] is described as an electric vehicle battery recycling startup that has recently expanded into grid energy storage for data centers.
- [[ColinCampbell]], Redwood's chief technology officer, explains that AI companies are differentiated by how quickly they want power and infrastructure to move.
- The episode says battery storage paired with renewables can often be deployed faster than waiting for grid interconnection or building natural gas turbines.
- Redwood activated a Nevada system using 60 megawatt-hours and 12 megawatts of reused electric vehicle batteries to power a local data center.
- Campbell says the Nevada data center is completely disconnected from the grid and that the system took four months to build.
- The episode notes that [[Nvidia]] recently invested in Redwood to accelerate this work.
- Redwood is doing production engineering in San Francisco for what Campbell describes as the beginning of a possible 10 gigawatt-per-year manufacturing line, likely at its Nevada facility.
- Campbell says the business model can also sell into existing grid-scale energy storage markets, even if those customers move more slowly than AI data-center customers.
- The closing [[HowWeSurvive]] promo with [[AmyScott]] mentions climate solutions and geoengineering ideas, but the main source contribution is Redwood's battery-storage role in AI infrastructure.

## Key Quotes
> "move quickly" - Campbell's framing of what distinguishes AI infrastructure customers.

> "completely disconnected from the grid" - Campbell's description of the Nevada data-center setup.

> "10 gigawatt per year" - Campbell's description of the possible manufacturing-line scale.
