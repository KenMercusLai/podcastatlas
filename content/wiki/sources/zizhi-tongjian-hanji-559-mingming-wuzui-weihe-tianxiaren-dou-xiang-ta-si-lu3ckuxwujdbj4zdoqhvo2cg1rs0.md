# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》559丨明明无罪，为何天下人都想他死？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Suihe 2 / 7 BCE under [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]], immediately after [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-558-ai-zhuang-caihuiying-wangmang-you-duo-hui-yinren-llvrlct7laohoo54s3lhzgwvsfc9|Hanji 558]]. It centers on [[ZhaiFangjinWesternHan|翟方进]]'s death after an [[ChineseAstrologicalPolitics|荧惑守心]] omen makes imperial responsibility politically dangerous and Chengdi turns the crisis toward his chancellor.

The durable synthesis is that omen politics can turn a responsibility problem into a scapegoat death when ruler self-correction, ministerial resentment, and administrative office liability align. Zhai Fangjin's harsh enforcement style and many enemies made him an easy target, while [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s judgment turns the episode from court technique into political ethics: killing an undeserving minister to block disaster deceives Heaven, and killing a deserving one while concealing the punishment deceives the people.

## Key Claims

- In 7 BCE, Mars lingering in the Heart mansion is read as a dangerous warning aimed directly at the emperor and imperial house.
- Instead of bearing the omen pressure through self-criticism, Chengdi lets responsibility move downward toward the chancellor.
- [[LiXunWesternHan|李寻]] warns Zhai Fangjin that the disaster may fall on him and suggests choosing another person from the chancellor's staff to absorb the fatal danger.
- Zhai Fangjin hesitates and does not create a substitute, leaving himself exposed when court discussion turns toward ministerial sacrifice.
- The astrologically knowledgeable lang official Ben Li argues that a great minister should stand forward to protect the Son of Heaven, making the chancellor the obvious target.
- Chengdi sends Zhai a condemning sealed document along with wine and beef, a courtesy-coded order to die; Zhai kills himself that day.
- The court then gives Zhai posthumous honors and public mourning, preserving imperial decorum while hiding the coercive character of his death.
- Zhai's strict, rule-bound enforcement and many impeachments had built resentment, so disaster accusations become a channel through which earlier enemies can aggregate blame.
- Sima Guang rejects the moral logic of transferring disaster onto a minister, arguing that Chengdi's handling either deceives Heaven or deceives the people.

## Key Quotes

> "好自为之" - the episode's rendering of Chengdi's fatal courtesy language to Zhai Fangjin.

> "天命不慆，不贰其命" - Sima Guang's quoted warning, via Yanying, against imagining that disaster can simply be shifted to another person.
