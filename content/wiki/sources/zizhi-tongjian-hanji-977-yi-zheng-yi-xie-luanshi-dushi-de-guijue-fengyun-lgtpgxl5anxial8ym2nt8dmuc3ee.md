# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》977丨亦正亦邪：乱世毒士的诡谲风云

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode is a short interpretive overview of Three Kingdoms advisers as morally ambiguous "poison strategists." It uses [[JiaXu|贾诩]], [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]], [[FaZheng|法正]], and [[ChengYu|程昱]] to build [[DarkAdviserCraft|毒士灰度谋略]]: advisers may use false intelligence, moral pressure, selective briefing, law-like retaliation, and stigma absorption while presenting themselves through loyalty, ritual, and public order. The wiki keeps the episode's strongest claims source-scoped because the transcript is a thematic commentary rather than a full evidentiary reconstruction of each case.

## Key Claims
- The episode defines disorder-era advisers less as pure loyalists than as actors balancing ruler service, self-protection, and morally uncomfortable technique.
- [[JiaXu|贾诩]] is presented as a false-intelligence operator who can induce enemy-side distrust, then later as a survival-minded adviser who reduces his visible toxicity after joining [[CaoCao|曹操]].
- The source uses "toxicity regulation" to argue that an adviser changes posture according to patron, risk, and political environment rather than staying permanently conspiratorial or upright.
- [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] is framed as using moral language, inherited mission, and selective information to pressure rulers or shape what a ruler can see.
- The Zhuge Liang branch treats morality as a political instrument as well as a value claim; ritual and loyalty language can become decision control.
- [[FaZheng|法正]] is introduced as a dangerous case where legal or administrative tools can be used to settle private resentment while preserving a formal public face.
- [[ChengYu|程昱]] is presented as an adviser who can absorb moral stigma for [[CaoCao|曹操]], solving dirty problems while helping protect the ruler's benevolent image.
- The episode also treats selective silence or "precise blindness" as a strategic act: advisers may choose not to intervene when intervention would damage the broader design.
- The closing formula says these advisers package hard power techniques in refined classical language, making moral vocabulary part of the shield around coercive action.
- The source's strong metaphors are useful as synthesis language, but details such as the Li Yan, Fa Zheng, and Cheng Yu examples need independent verification before being treated as settled chronology.
