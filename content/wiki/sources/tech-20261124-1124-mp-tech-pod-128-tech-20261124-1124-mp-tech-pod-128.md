# What happens if AI becomes its own successor?

## Summary

This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[RachelSteratore|Rachel Steratore]] of [[RAND]] separate three stages of AI-assisted development: automating known research work, generating research ideas or methods that people carry forward, and an AI system directly building its successor. It treats the first two as emerging inside model labs while stating that no Type 3 system has been publicly disclosed.

The episode's main policy contribution is [[AIResearchAutomationOversight]]. If advanced research systems remain internal rather than becoming planned public releases, release-linked testing alone may not give government visibility into the capability most relevant to [[RecursiveSelfImprovement]]. Steratore therefore argues for research-capability benchmarks, confidential reporting on automation and compute allocation, and comparison of automated capabilities work with automated safety work.

## Key Claims

- A [[METR]] report is described as estimating that increased AI use in research and development may explain about 80% of a reported acceleration in selected [[Anthropic]] model capabilities; the episode does not provide the underlying methods.
- [[RecursiveSelfImprovement]] means that AI helps improve later AI, with each generation potentially shortening the work needed to create its successor.
- Type 1 automates an existing research pipeline, including coding, experiment execution, and other predefined work.
- Type 2 has AI propose algorithms, designs, or training methods that humans incorporate into later systems.
- Type 3 has AI directly build its successor; the episode says no such system has been publicly disclosed.
- Reduced human participation can make failure harder to understand because developers already lack complete explanations for some model behavior.
- Estimates for major acceleration range from a few years to a decade, while chips, energy, physical infrastructure, and harder research problems can slow the loop.
- Policymakers depend heavily on private labs for information about internal development capability.
- The episode says a June 2026 executive order directed U.S. national-security agencies, including the [[NationalSecurityAgency]], to create a classified process for evaluating advanced models' cyber capabilities.
- Steratore proposes adding meaningful AI-research capability to government tests because research automation and cyber capability may overlap.
- Testing planned releases may miss internal research systems, so oversight would require visibility into large-scale internal use rather than only public model launches.
- Confidential reporting could cover automated research, compute allocation, and whether capability automation is advancing faster than safety automation.
