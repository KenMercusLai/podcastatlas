# Alamo Drafthouse Cinema: Tim and Karrie League (2023)

## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] episode follows [[TimLeague]] and [[KarrieLeague]] from a failed Bakersfield repertory theater to founding [[AlamoDrafthouseCinema]], a cinema-and-restaurant chain built around first-run films, food and alcohol, strict audience rules, and locally rooted programming. The durable business account links site selection, scarce startup cash, hands-on operating learning, community events, franchise conflict, workplace-culture failures, pandemic bankruptcy, and eventual acquisition by [[SonyPictures]].

Its strongest synthesis is that Alamo did not compete with home viewing or conventional multiplexes on screen size alone. It made concentrated, collective moviegoing into a designed service: the venue choreographed food delivery, rejected pre-show advertising, enforced silence and phone rules, and invested in events whose brand and community value could exceed their immediate profit. The supplied document is a structured Chinese summary of a founder interview, not an audited company history or full transcript.

## Key Claims
- Tim and Karrie's first theater failed because enthusiasm and low-cost improvisation could not overcome a weak location, safety problems, denied alcohol licensing, and unstable demand.
- The Austin launch survived a severe early cash shortage after advertising, positive coverage, and a second-run hit generated traffic for the food-and-film model.
- [[CinemaAudienceBehavioralContract]] made attention part of Alamo's product through no-talking and no-phone enforcement, limited service interruption, and refusal of ordinary pre-show commercials.
- Theme meals, outdoor roadshows, unusual screenings, and creator interaction turned programming into community and long-horizon brand investment rather than only near-term ticket yield.
- [[LocalCinemaCommunityScaling]] required repeatable accounting and operations without erasing local programming, a balance the founders say was damaged by early franchise structure and partially restored after the company recombined.
- A strong customer-facing culture did not ensure a safe workplace; Tim identifies his decision to rehire an accused harasser and the company's response to later misconduct and discrimination allegations as leadership failures.
- COVID-19 exposed debt, lease, and expansion fragility; Chapter 11 preserved the operating company but imposed losses before Sony's later acquisition bought out the founders' stakes.
