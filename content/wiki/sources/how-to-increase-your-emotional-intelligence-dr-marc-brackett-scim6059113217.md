# How to Increase Your Emotional Intelligence | Dr. Marc Brackett

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[MarcBrackett]] about emotional intelligence as a trainable set of intrapersonal and interpersonal skills. [[RULERFramework]], [[EmotionalGranularity]], and [[EmotionRegulationToolkit]] organize the practical account: recognize the state, understand its cause, label it precisely, decide how to express it, and select a context-sensitive response. The episode adds [[MoodMeterEmotionalCheckIn]], [[EmotionMentorship]], and [[BullyingPreventionThroughEmotionalSkills]] while grounding those ideas in Brackett's childhood bullying, his relationship with Uncle Marvin, and applications across families, schools, workplaces, digital communication, and adult relationships.

## Key Claims
- Emotional intelligence is not one global personality trait; it is a learnable group of skills for reasoning with and about emotion, both within oneself and between people.
- Precise labels matter because anxiety, stress, fear, anger, disappointment, jealousy, envy, happiness, and contentment point to different causes and therefore different regulation strategies.
- Behavior is an unreliable direct readout of emotion: stomping, withdrawal, or hostility can conceal sadness, shame, fear, bullying, or unmet expectations.
- [[MoodMeterEmotionalCheckIn]] maps emotion by pleasantness and energy without treating unpleasant states as morally bad or automatically in need of repair.
- Suppression is presented as generally costly in the episode's Western-cultural frame, while reappraisal, breathing, walking away, direct conversation, and psychological distance are possible tools whose fit depends on emotion and context.
- Useful empathy combines understanding and felt concern with regulation, questions, compassion, and movement toward strategy; repeated retelling without direction can intensify distress.
- Texting and short digital reactions are useful for logistics but can weaken nuance when they replace face-to-face intimacy, emotional support, or difficult conversation.
- [[BullyingPreventionThroughEmotionalSkills]] requires more than rules: repeated harm, intent, and power imbalance must be recognized, while adults and students practice empathy, perspective-taking, emotional perception, role play, and intervention.
- [[EmotionMentorship]] gives a person permission to feel through nonjudgmental attention, active listening, compassion, and practical support rather than immediate correction.

## Key Quotes
> "For whom were you an Uncle Marvin?" - the question that turns received emotional support into a responsibility to support others.

> "permission to feel" - Brackett's phrase for emotional acceptance without judgment.

> "you’re so emotional" - an example of a label that may dismiss strong feeling rather than understand it.
