# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》237｜娶母淫妹，汉景帝儿孙有多荒唐？（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode compares three sons of [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] and the next Jiangdu generation. [[LiuYuLuKingWesternHan|鲁共王刘余]] supplies the relatively restrained opening case through palace construction and the discovery of old-script texts in the walls of Confucius's former residence; [[LiuFeiJiangduKingWesternHan|江都易王刘非]] supplies the martial but extravagant father; and [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘建]] and [[LiuDuanWesternHan|胶西王刘端]] show how territorial royal power could turn sexual coercion, lethal amusement, household violence, and attacks on officials into a wider accountability problem.

The durable synthesis is not that every descendant of Jingdi behaved alike. Liu Yu is explicitly used as a relative contrast, Liu Jian eventually faces investigation and dies when the rebellion case closes around him, and Liu Duan is repeatedly spared while remaining a low apparent threat to the throne. The episode therefore adds two different limits to royal privilege: delayed but terminal punishment after evidence of rebellion, and prolonged [[RoyalKinshipPenaltyMitigation|宗室亲缘减刑]] that leaves local victims and administrators bearing the cost of imperial kinship.

## Key Claims

- Han Jingdi is said to have had fourteen sons; the episode groups Liu Yu, Liu Fei, and Liu Duan as sons of Consort Cheng and uses their households to introduce the conduct of Jingdi's descendants.
- Liu Yu is described as fond of palace and park construction, animals, and music; demolition at Confucius's former residence reportedly exposes old-script classical texts in a wall.
- Liu Fei is described as physically strong, extravagant, active in palace construction, and willing to gather haoxia figures around him.
- Liu Jian, Liu Fei's son and successor, is accused of seizing a woman intended for his father, sexual abuse within the royal family and household, humiliating palace dependants, and deliberately preventing rescue while people drown.
- Fear of punishment leads Liu Jian and his queen into a curse ritual and preparations marked by weapons and forged royal or imperial instruments; investigation ends in his suicide, the queen's execution, and abolition of Jiangdu.
- Liu Duan is accused of killing a favored young attendant and relatives after palace sexual misconduct, fabricating charges against centrally appointed officials, and poisoning some of them.
- Officials repeatedly seek Liu Duan's execution, but Han Wudi is said to spare his elder brother; Liu Duan dies after forty-seven years as king, and Jiaoxi is abolished because he has no heir.

## Key Quotes

> “人性的恶、丑、毒和残忍” - the host's moral frame for the royal-household violence narrated in the episode.

> “不能只是等死，要做人所不能做的事” - the episode's paraphrase of Liu Jian's turn from fear of punishment toward forbidden action.

## Connections

- [[LiuYuLuKingWesternHan|鲁共王刘余]] - relative contrast whose construction project is linked to the discovery of wall-hidden classical texts.
- [[LiuFeiJiangduKingWesternHan|江都易王刘非]] - Jingdi's son and Jiangdu ruler whose strength, extravagance, and household form the setting inherited by Liu Jian.
- [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘建]] - successor whose coercion, killings, forged instruments, rebellion case, and suicide dominate the episode.
- [[LiuDuanWesternHan|胶西王刘端]] - protected royal brother whose household violence and attacks on officials damage local government.
- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - father whose large princely line supplies the episode's dynastic frame.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler whose court investigates Liu Jian but repeatedly declines to execute Liu Duan.
- [[RoyalKinshipPenaltyMitigation|宗室亲缘减刑]] - pattern illustrated by Liu Duan's continued protection despite repeated punishment requests.

## Contradictions

- Identity normalization: the supplied summary alternates between “刘渐” and “刘建” for the Jiangdu successor. The father, kingdom, rebellion instruments, Yuan-shou 2 suicide, and Guangling outcome identify [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|刘建]], so “刘渐” is treated as a transcription error.
- Identity normalization: “鲁共王刘渝” is normalized to [[LiuYuLuKingWesternHan|鲁共王刘余]], and “交西王” to [[LiuDuanWesternHan|胶西王刘端]]. The received *Hanshu* names Consort Cheng's sons as 刘余、刘非、刘端 and gives the same Lu wall-text, Jiangdu, and Jiaoxi narratives.
- The episode says Liu Yu dies after twenty-seven years as king, while the received *Hanshu* table gives twenty-eight years; chronology and inclusive counting therefore remain unsettled in this source note.
- The compact summary attributes precise counts of *Liji*, *Shangshu*, *Chunqiu*, and *Lunyu* texts to Ban Gu. The received accounts differ in titles, counts, and transmission framing, so the discovery is retained while the exact inventory remains source-scoped.
- The episode's “half of later royal kin” claim, ordinal numbers among Jingdi's sons, exact victim counts, sexual allegations, dialogue, curse procedure, seized objects, offices, poisoning details, tax policy, and administrative conversions require direct textual comparison and are not generalized beyond this source.
- Liu Duan's illness and his violence are recorded together without adopting the episode's implied medical or psychological causal link. No settled contradiction is adopted with Hanji 309-2 or Hanji 367-1; this earlier episode overlaps them while adding Liu Yu and a comparative dynastic frame.
