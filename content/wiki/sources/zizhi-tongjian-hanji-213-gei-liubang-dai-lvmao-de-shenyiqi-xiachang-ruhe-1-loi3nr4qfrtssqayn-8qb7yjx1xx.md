# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》213｜给刘邦戴绿帽的审食其，下场如何（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places three early-reign events inside [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]]'s consolidation problem: the return of marquises to their fiefs and removal of [[ZhouBo|周勃]] from the chancellorship, the early death of [[LiuZhangChengyangKingWesternHan|城阳王刘章]], and [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]]'s killing of [[ShenYiji|审食其]]. It argues that founding merit, royal prestige, and knowledge about the accession settlement could all become liabilities after the new ruler's position strengthened.

The episode also compares [[Shiji|《史记》]] with [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]], arguing that the chronicle omits Liu Bang's reported knowledge of Liu Chang's mother's pregnancy and thereby shifts responsibility toward [[LuZhi|吕雉]] and Shen Yiji. Its stronger claim—that Liu Heng tacitly allowed the killing because Shen Yiji could challenge the accession narrative and Liu Chang was a potential rival—is a political inference without direct evidence of instruction or agreement.

## Key Claims

- In 177 BCE Liu Heng reportedly orders marquises back to their fiefs, invokes Zhou Bo as the expected example, removes him as chancellor, appoints [[GuanYing|灌婴]], abolishes the separate grand-commandant office, and places its military responsibility under the chancellor.
- The host reads Zhou Bo's removal as a response to the prestige and power of a founding minister who had helped install Liu Heng, but the source supplies no direct statement of that motive.
- Liu Zhang, credited with killing Lü Chan during the anti-Lü action, dies at twenty-three; the episode links his early death to the pressure surrounding royal contributors while acknowledging that the transmitted account does not establish a cause.
- Liu Chang is described as the son of an imprisoned Zhao woman who died after giving birth; he blames Shen Yiji for failing to obtain Lu Zhi's intervention even though the episode assigns primary responsibility to Liu Bang and Lu Zhi.
- Liu Heng repeatedly excuses Liu Chang's arrogant or unlawful conduct, including informal address and extraordinary ceremonial access, before Liu Chang visits Shen Yiji with a concealed iron hammer, strikes him, and has an attendant decapitate him.
- Liu Chang bares his back and surrenders at the palace, but Liu Heng pardons him on the stated ground of filial revenge; the episode stresses that the killing is not referred to the廷尉 despite Shen Yiji's status and the legal norm against homicide.
- The host proposes a two-target political interpretation: Shen Yiji's death removes a possible witness against Liu Heng's accession narrative, while the public killing damages Liu Chang as a future contender. No direct order, conspiracy evidence, or contemporary admission is supplied.
- After the pardon, Liu Chang allegedly becomes still more presumptuous in Huainan, and [[YuanAngWesternHan|袁盎]] warns Liu Heng that unchecked royal arrogance will eventually produce disaster.

## Key Quotes

> “杀人者死。” - the legal baseline the host invokes when asking why Shen Yiji's killing did not reach ordinary judicial review.

> “一石二鸟。” - the host's summary of the unproven theory that the killing simultaneously removed Shen Yiji and politically discredited Liu Chang.

## Connections

- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] - new ruler whose treatment of founding ministers, royal kin, and the homicide anchors the consolidation interpretation.
- [[ZhouBo|周勃]] - founding minister removed from the chancellorship and sent back to his fief.
- [[ShenYiji|审食其]] - former chancellor and court intimate killed by Liu Chang.
- [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]] - royal killer whose pardon extends the pattern of selective indulgence.
- [[LiuZhangChengyangKingWesternHan|城阳王刘章]] - anti-Lü contributor whose unexplained early death is placed in the same political field.
- [[LuZhi|吕雉]] and [[LiuBang|刘邦]] - rulers whose decisions shape the episode's competing responsibility narratives for Liu Chang's mother's death.
- [[IndulgenceAsPoliticalElimination|纵容式政治清除]] - later intent hypothesis for interpreting Liu Heng's repeated tolerance of Liu Chang.
- [[TextualOmissionAttributionShift|史书删节与责任归属转移]] - source-critical pattern applied to the Shiji–Zizhi Tongjian difference.

## Contradictions

- No settled factual contradiction is adopted. The source adds an earlier stage to Hanji 217's Liu Chang sequence and strengthens the evidence for repeated indulgence, but it does not prove that the indulgence was designed to cause rebellion or death.
- Responsibility boundary: the episode argues that Liu Bang and Lu Zhi knew enough to bear primary responsibility for the imprisoned woman's death, while Liu Chang directs revenge at Shen Yiji. Exact knowledge, petition channels, and culpability require comparison with the transmitted texts.
- Motive boundary: Liu Heng's alleged tacit permission, fear of an accession reversal, and “one stone, two birds” design are inferences from benefit, pardon, and missing judicial process, not direct evidence.
- Death boundary: Liu Zhang's age and anti-Lü role are reported, but neither the source nor the absence of a detailed record establishes political causation.
- Offices, dates, the military-command transfer, homicide procedure, ceremonial privileges, exact speeches, weapon, attendants, and accession-witness theory remain episode-attributed pending textual comparison.
