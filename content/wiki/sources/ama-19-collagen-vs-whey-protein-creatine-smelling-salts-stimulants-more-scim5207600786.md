# AMA #19: Collagen vs. Whey Protein, Creatine, Smelling Salts, Stimulants & More

## Summary
This short [[HubermanLab]] AMA excerpt has [[AndrewHuberman]] compare whey, collagen, and bone broth by intended outcome rather than rank them as universally better or worse. It extends [[ProteinBodyCompositionLever]] and introduces [[ProteinSourceGoalMatching]]: whey and other leucine-rich complete proteins are framed as stronger choices for muscle protein synthesis and recovery, while collagen or bone broth may have a narrower skin-elasticity and appearance role. The practical claims remain bounded by total diet quality, individual tolerance, acne response, and the limited study detail supplied in the excerpt.

## Key Claims
- Protein sources differ in amino-acid composition and bioavailability, so equal gram amounts need not be equivalent for every goal.
- [[ProteinSourceGoalMatching]] treats whey as the stronger option for muscle growth, strength, repair, and training recovery because of its leucine-rich amino-acid profile.
- Collagen and bone broth count toward total protein intake but are not presented as nutritionally interchangeable with whey for muscle protein synthesis.
- The episode reports modest skin-elasticity and appearance findings from roughly 15 grams of collagen per day for two weeks or longer, but does not review study methods or effect sizes.
- Most daily protein should come from suitable whole foods when practical, with powders and bars functioning as convenient supplements rather than mandatory foundations.
- Whey or other high-leucine foods may worsen acne for some people; the suggested response is an individual removal-and-observation test, not a claim that whey universally causes acne.
- Digestive tolerance, menstrual-cycle context, dietary pattern, training goal, and medical context can change which protein source is useful.

## Key Quotes
> "scientists of themselves" - Huberman's phrase for testing a food or supplement against one's own response rather than assuming a universal effect.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction found. The excerpt complements the existing protein synthesis by distinguishing total protein intake from amino-acid quality and intended outcome.
- Its loose suggestion of about one gram of protein per pound of body or desired body weight is higher than the population-requirement frame in the Gardner source and the approximate muscle-building plateau described in the Norton source; these are different source-scoped optimization claims, not one universal requirement.
- The collagen dose and timeline, whey-acne mechanism, menstrual-cycle sensitivity, leucine and mTOR explanation, and bone-broth leucine content are not independently established here. Huberman explicitly expresses uncertainty about the last point.
