## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode follows writer [[PaulaMejia]] from a roughly $9,000 stay at [[GoldenDoor]] to a lower-cost experiment in changing her own phone routines. It extends [[DigitalDetoxEconomy]] beyond apps and blocking devices into luxury retreat services and "aspirationally offline" status performance.

The episode also sharpens [[ScreenTimeFriction]] by contrasting environmental rules with routine substitution. Phone-free common areas, an out-of-sight sleep bag, morning reading, time with a dog, and accepting social invitations all interrupted automatic checking, but Mejia's screen time rebounded within days. Her conclusion shifts the burden from willpower alone toward cultural and [[WorkRestBoundary|workplace boundaries]] that make constant availability less normal.

## Key Claims
- The [[DigitalDetoxEconomy|digital-detox market]] includes retreats, apps, devices, and coaching, with sustained disconnection increasingly sold as a premium experience.
- [[GoldenDoor]] allows phones and Wi-Fi in guest rooms but prohibits phones in common areas and gives guests a bag that keeps the device out of sight.
- The episode says Golden Door's least expensive stay is approximately $9,000 and that its clientele is becoming younger.
- Because complete disconnection was impractical for her freelance work, [[PaulaMejia]] aimed to reduce daily phone use by about one hour.
- Replacing immediate morning checking with reading or time with her dog, and accepting social invitations, reduced Mejia's screen time during the experiment.
- Screen time rose again within days, showing that a short intervention did not create a durable new baseline.
- "Aspirationally offline" analog activities can become online status performance when participants post them to social platforms.
- The episode argues that individual consumers should not carry the full burden of countering technologies designed to command attention; work-life norms and institutional boundaries also matter.

## Key Quotes
> "aspirationally offline" - the episode's label for analog-looking disconnection that may still be performed for an online audience.

> "sleep bag" - [[GoldenDoor]]'s name for the pouch guests use to keep phones out of sight.
