## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty-Corino]] interview [[BenjaminAlarie]] of the [[UniversityOfToronto|University of Toronto]] about whether AI can make legal help more accessible. Alarie's core frame is [[SuperJustice|Super Justice]]: AI could relieve legal scarcity by making legal reasoning and legal assistance more abundant, but only if humans remain accountable for design, deployment, verification, and use.

The episode extends the wiki's legal-AI branch beyond [[VibeLawyering]] and [[LegalAIHallucination]] into [[AIAccessToJustice]], [[PersonalizedLegalGuidance]], and [[LegalAIVerificationAuditability]]. Its strongest caution is that cheaper legal help is not automatically fair legal help: legal AI has to support same-rights treatment, human-centered decision-making, auditability, privacy limits, and professional responsibility rather than letting attorneys, accountants, or institutions blame machines.

## Key Claims
- AI is already changing legal work such as research and contract drafting, while also producing high-profile failures.
- [[BenjaminAlarie]] defines [[SuperJustice|Super Justice]] as relieving legal scarcity so legal reasoning and legal help become abundant rather than scarce.
- [[PersonalizedLegalGuidance]] should change the explanation and route through the law, not the rights or rules people receive.
- Humans should remain at the core of legal systems; AI should support and facilitate rather than decide what counts as a fair system.
- Cheap legal help is not the same as fair legal help, because society still has to decide who legal AI is built for and who is accountable when it is wrong.
- [[LegalAIVerificationAuditability]] is necessary because legal and tax professionals need to verify AI answers, find mistakes, and use tools to make stronger judgments and advocacy.
- The episode identifies uncritical adoption as a major risk even when AI systems become more accurate.
- Mining case law for patterns, experience matching, and consequences could improve legal insight, but it also intensifies [[CivilLibertiesSurveillanceRisk]] when personal and case data are gathered or reused.
- Alarie says humans will remain in the loop for a long time, especially after examples of lawyers filing AI-invented cases and being sanctioned.
- The same professional accountability principle should apply across law and accounting: AI assistance does not excuse the responsible professional.

## Key Quotes
> "a new era of justice for all" - the episode's opening question.

> "super justice" - Alarie's term for relieving legal scarcity.

> "the machine did it" - the excuse Alarie says professionals should not be able to use.
