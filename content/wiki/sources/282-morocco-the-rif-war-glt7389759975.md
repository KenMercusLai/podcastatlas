# 282: Morocco: The Rif War

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode presents the [[RifWar]] as both a late colonial conflict and a proving ground for methods, institutions, and officers that later shaped the [[SpanishCivilWar]]. It follows [[MohammedAbdelKrim]] from Spanish-linked judge and journalist to anti-colonial leader, then traces how the disaster at Annual, the [[RepublicOfTheRif]], Franco-Spanish intervention, chemical weapons, aerial bombing, and the Alhucemas landing transformed the war.

The episode's strongest synthesis is [[ColonialWarMilitaryIncubation]]: Spain's imperial humiliation and weak conscript army encouraged an Africanist officer culture, while the rebuilt [[ArmyOfAfrica]] gave [[FranciscoFranco]] and other officers combat authority, coercive methods, and political mythology later carried into Spain. The same conflict also shows anti-colonial state-building under severe external pressure, though the Moroccan perspective is mediated primarily through the hosts' narrative.

## Key Claims

- Spanish expansion into northern [[Morocco]] combined post-1898 imperial humiliation, royal and mining interests, Catholic crusading language, and the 1912 protectorate settlement.
- [[MohammedAbdelKrim]] used education, Islamic legal authority, Spanish administrative experience, and anti-colonial legitimacy to unite parts of the Rif after breaking with Spain.
- Spain's 1921 defeat at Annual exposed corrupt command, thin supply lines, dispersed blockhouses, poor equipment, and contempt for Rif forces; the episode gives a death toll of roughly 13,000.
- Abdel Krim created the unrecognized [[RepublicOfTheRif]], with roads, a small bureaucracy, and an Islamic legal code, but its cohesion and survival depended partly on resistance to outside enemies.
- Spain rebuilt around Moroccan regulares and a Foreign Legion, creating the [[ArmyOfAfrica]] milieu in which [[FranciscoFranco]] gained command experience and a providential public image.
- Spain used chemical weapons despite signing the 1925 Geneva Protocol, while French entry and foreign volunteer air raids widened the conflict's colonial and civilian violence.
- The 1925 Alhucemas landing combined amphibious assault, armor, artillery, naval fire, and extensive air support; Abdel Krim surrendered to France in 1926 and later lived in exile.

## Key Quotes

> “the last Victorian-style colonial war” — the episode's framing of one side of the conflict's historical ambiguity.

> “a precursor to the Spanish Civil War” — the episode's central claim about the war's institutional and political afterlife.

> “white man's civilisation” — the justification attributed to American volunteer pilot Charles Sweeney.
