# Memorial Day, 2020 | Part 1

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode reconstructs [[GeorgeFloyd]]'s fatal restraint as a failure to revise an initial threat interpretation. [[GaryKlein]]'s [[DecisionFixation]] framework explains how disconfirming evidence can be absorbed into a failing script, while [[KennethDodge]]'s [[HostileIntentAttribution]] research describes the learned tendency to read ambiguous behavior as hostile.

The episode then compares the 2020 killing with [[DerekChauvin]]'s reported 2017 restraint of a 14-year-old boy. That earlier recorded incident turns an individual decision failure into an institutional question: why did evidence of the same threat-perception and restraint pattern not remove Chauvin from police work before Floyd's death?

## Key Claims

- Dispatcher Jenna Scurry recognized that the restraint had remained unchanged for an alarming length of time and made an unprecedented call to a police sergeant.
- Floyd was unarmed and repeatedly deferential, while his efforts to breathe, weakening voice, loss of consciousness, seizure, and absent pulse supplied escalating evidence of medical crisis.
- Chauvin rejected another officer's recovery-position suggestion and continued the restraint after bystanders, a firefighter, another officer, and Floyd's body supplied reasons to reassess.
- Decision fixation occurs when a person remains committed to an initial interpretation and incorporates contrary evidence into the same failing script instead of restarting the analysis.
- Hostile-intent attribution can make ambiguous, fearful, or defensive behavior appear threatening; it may be adaptive in dangerous environments but destructive when generalized.
- A reported 2017 encounter showed Chauvin striking and restraining a non-attacking 14-year-old, including continued prone restraint after the child reported blacking out and paramedics arrived.
- The episode argues that the earlier recorded pattern made Floyd's death a preventable institutional failure as well as an individual act.
