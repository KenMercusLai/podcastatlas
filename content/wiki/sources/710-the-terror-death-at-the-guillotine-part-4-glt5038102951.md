# 710. The Terror: Death at the Guillotine (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[GeorgesDanton|Georges Danton]] from charismatic revolutionary organizer and early [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety|Committee of Public Safety]] leader to advocate of clemency and victim of the [[RevolutionaryTribunal]]. His compromised record, suspected corruption, and association with [[PhilippeFabreDEglantine|Fabre d'Églantine]] made him vulnerable, but the case against the Dantonists ultimately depended on a politically assembled conspiracy and a trial whose rules were changed when Danton's defence threatened the desired verdict.

The episode also traces the destruction of the radical [[Hebertists]] and moderate [[Indulgents]]. Military recovery and stronger central authority did not end the Terror because [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]] and [[LouisAntoineSaintJust|Saint-Just]] increasingly treated coercion as an instrument for constructing a virtuous republic; eliminating both factional wings then intensified fear within the governing coalition and created a shared incentive to strike before becoming the next target.

## Key Claims

- Danton combined extraordinary charisma, revolutionary mobilization, and pragmatic leadership with credible suspicions of corruption and an earlier willingness to justify emergency violence.
- By late 1793, Danton and [[CamilleDesmoulins|Camille Desmoulins]] were associated with the Indulgents, who sought clemency, peace, and an end to the Terror.
- Desmoulins's *[[LeVieuxCordelier|Le Vieux Cordelier]]* moved from attacking Hébert with Robespierre's approval to criticizing terror itself and demanding a Committee of Clemency.
- The [[LawOfSuspects|Law of Suspects]] made arrest depend on conduct, association, speech, or writing; contradictory levels of revolutionary zeal could both become incriminating.
- Military victories, restored central control, price intervention, and suppression of revolt weakened the emergency case for terror without ending it.
- [[JacquesHebert|Jacques Hébert]] joined economic radicalism and de-Christianization to an attempted March 1794 insurrection, but the Commune and National Guard withheld support and the Hébertists were executed.
- Fabre and [[FrancoisChabot|François Chabot]] used foreign-plot allegations to conceal real corruption; Robespierre combined their stories into a wider conspiracy that implicated political factions and Danton's circle.
- After the Hébertists fell, Robespierre and Saint-Just moved against Danton and Desmoulins when reconciliation failed and opposition to terror continued.
- Danton's trial joined unrelated defendants to simulate one conspiracy; when his courtroom performance endangered conviction, the Convention authorized removal of defendants accused of insulting justice.
- The executions of Danton and Desmoulins on 5 April 1794, followed by Lucile Desmoulins, destroyed a moderate alternative but also made deputies and committee members fear that no revolutionary standing guaranteed safety.

## Key Quotes

> “Burning is not an answer.” - Desmoulins's Rousseau-derived reply when Robespierre proposed burning his newspaper, as presented by the episode.

> “Show my head to the people; it is worth seeing.” - Danton's reported final instruction to the executioner.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with Parts 1–3. This installment extends the sequence from institutional formation and explicit state terror into the elimination of Hébertist and Dantonist opposition.
- Danton's move toward clemency does not erase his earlier support for exceptional justice, his responsibility around the September Massacres, or credible corruption suspicions.
- The episode rejects a sole-dictator model of Robespierre: he worked through committees whose members had distinct military, administrative, police, and political roles.
- Claims that terror stabilized the economy, defeated rebellion, or helped reverse the war do not isolate terror's effect from price controls, mass mobilization, administration, military operations, or other causes.
- Fabre's and Chabot's corruption was real, but the wider foreign conspiracy built from their allegations was fabricated or radically overextended.
- Danton's reported final words, private reconciliation attempts, motives, dialogue, arrest warnings, crowd reactions, prison totals, death totals, and causal claims about later Thermidorian fear remain source-scoped reconstructions.
