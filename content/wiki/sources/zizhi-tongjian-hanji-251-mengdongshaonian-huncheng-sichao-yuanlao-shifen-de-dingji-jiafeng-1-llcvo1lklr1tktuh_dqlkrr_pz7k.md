# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》251｜懵懂少年混成四朝元老，石奋的顶级家风（1）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode joins two early-Wudi problems. In the first, [[ZhaoWanWesternHan|赵绾]] and [[WangZangWesternHan|王臧]] try to reduce the Eastern Palace's role in major decisions, but [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] forces an investigation and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] abandons the reformers rather than confront a stronger senior court power.

The second presents [[ShiFenWesternHan|石奋]] as a minimally educated but intensely deferential household patriarch whose dress, silence, reception of rewards, and treatment of descendants turn ritual conduct into daily family discipline. His sons [[ShiJianWesternHan|石建]] and [[ShiQingWesternHan|石庆]] carry that caution into court service, where private counsel and deliberately verified answers become sources of imperial trust.

## Key Claims

- The episode frames Wudi's early support for Confucian statecraft and Dowager Dou's Huang-Lao preference as both an ideological dispute and a conflict over who controls major decisions.
- Zhao Wan reportedly proposes that major affairs no longer be reported to the Eastern Palace; Dowager Dou responds by ordering investigation of Zhao and Wang Zang.
- Wudi stops the Mingtang project, turns Zhao and Wang over for judgment, and accepts the dismissal of Dou Ying and Tian Fen; the episode interprets this as a tactical retreat while his power is still insecure.
- Shi Fen and four sons each hold offices rated at two thousand shi, producing the household total behind Emperor Jing's “Wan Shi Jun” nickname.
- Shi Fen makes deference habitual through formal dress, dismounting at the palace, bowing before imperial gifts, silent correction at meals, and requiring acknowledgment of wrongdoing before reconciliation.
- Shi Jian gives candid advice only in private and appears inarticulate before the assembled court, a division of settings that the episode says wins Emperor Jing's trust.
- Shi Qing counts the horses before answering a simple imperial question, making verification itself an act of caution and respect.

## Key Quotes

> “不是光嘴上说，而是以身作则。” - the episode's contrast between Shi household practice and moral talk.

> “赵绾、王臧之死可以理解为一次政治试探失败后的代价。” - the supplied summary's boundary on Wudi's early reform retreat.

## Contradictions

- The title calls Shi Fen a “four-reign senior statesman,” while the body calls him a “three-reign senior statesman” and does not establish a complete reign-by-reign service chronology.
- The supplied text writes 赵婉; this note normalizes the figure to 赵绾 where the referent is clear. Offices, dates, dialogue, motives, and the precise legal process still require comparison with transmitted records.
- The episode treats Zhao Wan and Wang Zang as acting with Wudi's authorization and calls their abandonment “弃卒保帅,” but it does not independently establish the private instruction or Wudi's complete motive.
- Tian Fen's claim that Liu An might succeed because Wudi had no son is preserved as political flattery, not a finding that Liu An held a lawful or viable succession claim.
- The total-rank nickname, Shi household scenes, appointments, private advice, horse count, and judgments about Confucian hypocrisy remain bounded to the supplied episode.
