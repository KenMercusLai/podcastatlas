# Why Silicon Valley can’t let go of its superstar CEOs

## Summary

This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode uses [[JensenHuang|Jensen Huang]]'s reported removal and rapid return at [[Nvidia]], alongside [[SamAltman]]'s 2023 removal and return at [[OpenAI]], to examine why Silicon Valley treats some founders as inseparable from company value. [[DavidKarp|David Karp]] argues that venture-capital selection, financing, advice, customer access, and repeated success can create a self-reinforcing [[FounderIndispensabilityMyth]] that attributes network and institutional advantage to exceptional individual genius.

The episode does not deny that founders can supply vision, technical context, relationships, or early fundraising power. Its durable governance claim is stage-specific: a mature company should turn founder-specific strengths into succession capacity, operational systems, and accountable authority rather than make one executive untouchable. The market response to Huang's return may therefore show perceived key-person value while also exposing [[FounderLedPlatformSuccessionRisk]], weak succession planning, and limits on board independence.

## Key Claims

- The episode reports that market instability and presidential intervention led to Huang's return after Nvidia initially said he had resigned and would not be reinstated; those events remain episode-attributed rather than established here through company filings or board records.
- Karp traces the founder ideal to 1990s techno-optimism and a growing view among successful technology leaders that government, journalism, democracy, and corporate boards obstruct builders.
- [[YCombinator]] illustrates the institutional mechanism: selecting founders, investing early, supplying advice and financing, and connecting customers and alumni can help produce success that is later narrated as proof of unique founder talent.
- Founder centrality can be valuable during startup formation, when technical conviction, fundraising, recruiting, and customer persuasion are concentrated, without implying that mature firms should remain permanently person-dependent.
- Nvidia's investments in companies that later buy its chips are presented as one reason Huang's departure threatened confidence across AI financing as well as inside Nvidia.
- A market capable of losing enormous value after one executive departs may be revealing governance and financial fragility rather than only measuring that executive's contribution.
- [[Apple]] and [[Microsoft]] qualify a simple founder-return story because successor leadership, operational execution, and logistics can create value at scales that founder vision alone does not deliver.
- Super-voting structures can protect long-horizon strategy, but the episode argues that public ownership loses accountability when boards and shareholders cannot meaningfully constrain the chief executive.
