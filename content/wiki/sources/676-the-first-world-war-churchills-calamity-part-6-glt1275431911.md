## Summary
This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[GallipoliCampaign|the Gallipoli campaign]] after the failed April 1915 landings, showing how [[WinstonChurchill|Winston Churchill]]'s Dardanelles shortcut became an attritional trench disaster. It connects battlefield stalemate, disease, Westminster crisis, failed reinforcement, evacuation, and later memory into one account of military and political failure.

The episode's strongest wiki contribution is to link [[FailedStrategicShortcutAttritionTrap]], [[TrenchSanitationDiseaseBurden]], [[WartimeCoalitionAccountability]], and [[AnzacSpirit]] inside one campaign. Gallipoli becomes a reputational catastrophe for Churchill, a foundation moment in [[Australia]] and [[NewZealand]] public memory, and a career-making battlefield for [[MustafaKemalAtaturk|Mustafa Kemal / Ataturk]].

## Key Claims
- Churchill's Dardanelles plan aimed to force the straits, reach the Black Sea, threaten Constantinople, and knock the [[OttomanEmpire|Ottoman Empire]] out of the war.
- After the naval campaign failed, Allied landings at Anzac Cove and Cape Helles on 25 April 1915 left troops clinging to difficult beachheads.
- Repeated attacks around Krithia, Anzac Cove, Suvla Bay, Lone Pine, and the Nek produced heavy casualties for little or no strategic gain.
- The episode presents Gallipoli as a local version of the wider [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]] offensive problem: trenches, machine guns, barbed wire, and defensive firepower defeated assumptions about speed and dash.
- Poor terrain, shallow trenches, heat, unburied bodies, flies, inadequate latrines, and dysentery made daily conditions especially destructive.
- Churchill remained committed to the campaign as Jackie Fisher, Lloyd George, the press, and naval officers turned against him, helping force his removal from the Admiralty.
- The Asquith coalition crisis delayed a clear answer to Ian Hamilton's urgent reinforcement request, showing Westminster instability prolonging battlefield suffering.
- The August offensive and Suvla Bay landing failed to break the stalemate, while Mustafa Kemal's Ottoman counterattack strengthened his military reputation.
- By late 1915 Keith Murdoch's report, Hamilton's removal, and Charles Monro's inspection helped shift London from reinforcement toward evacuation.
- The December 1915-January 1916 evacuation was operationally successful, removing large numbers of troops with minimal loss, but it did not redeem the strategic failure.
- The hosts argue that even tactical success on the peninsula would not necessarily have guaranteed passage through the straits, Constantinople's occupation, or Ottoman surrender.
- Gallipoli damaged Churchill's reputation for years, helped make Mustafa Kemal a trusted defender of Turkey, and became central to Australian and New Zealand national identity through Anzac memory.

## Key Quotes
