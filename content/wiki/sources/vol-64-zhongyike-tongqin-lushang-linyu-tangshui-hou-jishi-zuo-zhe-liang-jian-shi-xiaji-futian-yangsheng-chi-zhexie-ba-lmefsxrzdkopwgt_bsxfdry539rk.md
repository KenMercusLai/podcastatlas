# VOL.64中医科｜通勤路上淋雨、蹚水后及时做这两件事｜夏季伏天养生吃这些吧

## Summary
This [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode has host [[Atang|阿汤]] ask traditional-Chinese-medicine guest [[MaoZheTCMDoctor|毛哲]] about commuting through rain or floodwater, wet footwear, foot drying, soaking, fans, summer sweating, air conditioning, sleep, porridge, lamb, ginger, and 藿香正气. Its most durable practical message is [[RainExposureFootCareBoundary|promptly to wash contaminated feet, dry them, change out of wet conditions, and avoid prolonged damp exposure]], while advice should vary by weather, health status, symptoms, and personal tolerance. The episode's cold-damp, damp-heat, yang, spleen, induced-sweating, food-therapy, and patent-medicine mechanisms remain source-scoped TCM claims rather than established diagnosis or treatment guidance.

## Key Claims
- After contact with rain or street water, the lowest-complexity action is to rinse visible contamination with clean water, dry the skin and footwear promptly, and avoid remaining in a warm, wet shoe environment.
- The guest frames rainwater as a source of “damp” or “cold-damp” in northern climates and sometimes “damp-heat” in southern climates. This is a TCM regional-pattern interpretation, not a validated medical diagnosis from exposure alone.
- Rubber or foam clogs may drain more easily than absorbent shoes, but prolonged moisture, heat, dirt, and occlusion can still irritate skin and favor fungal problems; shoe type does not remove the need to clean and dry the feet.
- The episode recommends warm foot soaking and rejects direct fan cooling of the soles, but it also says hot soaking is unsuitable for some people with impaired venous return. The source provides no examination, temperature limit, duration, vascular-risk protocol, or evidence review.
- [[TCMSummerPatternDifferentiation]] favors moderate seasonal sweating and comfortable air conditioning over either staying cold all day or forcing heavy sweating; the source itself says amount and tolerance depend on the person.
- Porridge, lamb, ginger, bitter foods, sleep, and warmth are presented through seasonal heart, spleen, qi, and yang ideas. Preparation and digestive tolerance are acknowledged, but therapeutic benefit is not established by the episode.
- The guest promotes 藿香正气 for selected northern “cold-damp” or “summer-damp” experiences and discusses alcohol-extracted, water-extracted, capsule, soft-capsule, and pill formulations. These product, extraction, absorption, and efficacy claims lack dose, interaction, contraindication, and clinical-evidence detail.
- Alcohol-containing formulations and significant dizziness or illness create a driving-safety boundary: the episode's conservative conclusion is not to drive after relevant use or while impaired, rather than relying on a claimed short breath-test interval.
- Menstrual status is used to justify stronger avoidance of wading and post-exposure sweating with ginger and brown sugar. Those recommendations remain source-scoped and should not replace evaluation of significant pain, bleeding, infection, skin injury, or systemic illness.
