# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》257｜汉代大杠精，究竟有多杠？（2）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode contrasts [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]] and [[ChengBushiWesternHan|程不识]] after their separate frontier commands. Li Guang camps loosely, minimizes paperwork and night signals, relies on close observation, and wins strong soldier affection; Cheng Bushi keeps formations, camps, watches, and military administration tightly ordered at much higher day-to-day cost. Neither force is attacked in the reported episode.

The durable synthesis comes from [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s evaluation rather than a simple winner declaration. Li Guang's method may work in the hands of an exceptional commander, but it is unsafe as a general model because successors and peer commanders cannot reliably reproduce his judgment or control the expectations his leniency creates. Cheng Bushi's heavier routine is presented as a lower-variance institutional baseline: it may not guarantee distinction, but it makes catastrophic surprise and command breakdown less likely.

## Key Claims

- Li Guang's force follows water and grass without tightly regulated formations or fortified camps, minimizes night-watch signals and paperwork, and depends on his close observation of the surroundings.
- Soldiers find Li Guang's command comfortable and are described as willing to die for him; Xiongnu is said to fear his strategy.
- Cheng Bushi keeps marching order, encampment, night watches, and military documents tightly regulated, leaving officers and soldiers with much less rest.
- Both forces return without suffering an enemy attack, so the episode does not provide a direct battlefield test proving that either system always succeeds.
- Cheng Bushi acknowledges Li Guang's soldier loyalty but argues that his loose organization would be difficult to recover from under sudden attack.
- Sima Guang distinguishes exceptional personal performance from a teachable rule: Li Guang can use the method because of unusual talent, but others should not imitate it.
- The institutional warning extends beyond Li Guang's own camp because troops who compare commanders may resent ordinary discipline and become harder for successors or peers to control.
- The episode closes with an amnesty, a [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] edict asking commanderies and kingdoms to recommend worthy people and Five Classics scholars for an imperial examination, and a solar eclipse.

## Key Quotes

> "以李广之才如此焉可也，然不可以为法。" - the episode's core rendering of Sima Guang's distinction between exceptional ability and a general rule.

> "效程不识，虽无功，犹不败。" - the episode's concise case for disciplined procedure as a defensive baseline.

- [[ChengBushiWesternHan|程不识]] - procedural commander whose formations, watches, camps, and paperwork trade comfort for routine readiness.
- [[ExceptionDependentMilitaryCommand|例外才能依赖式治军]] - distinction between outcomes secured by unusual personal capacity and methods safe enough to teach or inherit.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the current wiki was found. The episode adds an earlier command-style layer to Li Guang's existing evidence for shared hardship, tactical composure, severe losses, route failure, and accepted responsibility.
- Outcome boundary: neither force is attacked in this account. Their safe return supports two viable reported cases, not proof that Li Guang's camp would fail under surprise or that Cheng Bushi's system would prevent defeat in every battle.
- Evaluation boundary: the strong claim that imitating Li Guang leads toward total destruction is Sima Guang's normative and counterfactual judgment as presented by the episode, not an observed result of these two deployments.
- Scope boundary: unit sizes, exact routes, offices, dates, camp details, enemy perceptions, soldier preferences, quoted wording, and the causal effect of each command style remain episode-attributed pending primary-text comparison.
- The title's “大杠精” is promotional framing and does not identify Li Guang, Cheng Bushi, or Sima Guang as a stable historical category.
