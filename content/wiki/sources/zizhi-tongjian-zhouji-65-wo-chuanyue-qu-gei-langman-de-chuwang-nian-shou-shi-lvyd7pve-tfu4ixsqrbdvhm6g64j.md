# 《资治通鉴·周纪》65丨我穿越去给浪漫的楚王念首诗

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens the 周纪四 frame of [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]], nominally covering 297-273 BCE, but concentrates on 297-296 BCE and the end of [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]]. It narrates his failed escape from [[Xianyang|咸阳]], the refusal of [[ZhaoState|赵国]] officials to receive him while [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]] is away, his recapture by [[QinState|秦国]], and his death in Qin custody. The episode then balances condemnation of Qin with a retrospective reassessment of Chu Huaiwang as an early capable, expansionary, and literary-minded ruler whose later fate was shaped by [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]], [[ZhengXiu|郑袖]], weak court feedback, and his own vanity and credulity.

## Key Claims
- The episode begins 周纪四 after [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-64-gudai-gangjing-gongsunlong-yangchengji-lkrchesllfi9ipgfapft7av2mnh5|Zhouji 64]], framing a 297-273 BCE span while mostly explaining the opening two years.
- In 297 BCE, [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] escapes [[Xianyang|咸阳]], but Qin blocks the direct southeastern route through [[Wuguan|武关]] back toward Chu.
- Chu Huaiwang turns toward Zhao, but Zhao officials refuse to decide while [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]] is away in Dai; the episode reads this as a decision failure produced by Zhao's divided post-abdication power structure.
- Chu Huaiwang then tries to reach [[WeiState|魏国]] but is caught by Qin and taken back to Xianyang.
- In 296 BCE, Chu Huaiwang dies in Qin custody. Qin returns his coffin to Chu, and the episode treats Chu public grief and wider criticism of Qin as evidence that Qin's coercive diplomacy carried moral-reputation costs.
- Qi, Han, Wei, Zhao, and Song form a five-state anti-Qin coalition and attack as far as [[EzhiWarringStates|阏氏]], but Qin breaks the pressure by returning [[WusuiWarringStates|武遂]] to Han and [[FenglingWarringStates|封陵]] to Wei.
- The episode presents [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] as the eastern states' necessary anti-Qin tool, while showing its fragility once Qin can distribute selective concessions.
- [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]] inspects Zhao's northern frontier, reaches Xihe, meets a [[Loufan|楼烦]] chieftain, and recruits more frontier fighters, extending the [[HufuQishe|胡服骑射]] and [[WarringStatesMilitaryTransformation|战国军事形态转变]] branch.
- The annalistic succession notices add [[LuPinggong|鲁平公]] dying and [[LuMingong|鲁湣公]] succeeding, [[WeiXiangwang|魏襄王]] dying and [[WeiZhaowang|魏昭王]] succeeding, and [[HanXiangwang|韩襄王]] dying and [[HanXiwang|韩釐王]] succeeding.
- The episode argues against reducing Chu Huaiwang to a fool: early in his reign, he is credited with talent recruitment, territorial expansion, anti-Qin coalition work, and military pressure on Wei and Qin.
- Chu Huaiwang's later vulnerability is explained through [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s praise-and-promise rhetoric, the king's vanity and greed, [[ZhengXiu|郑袖]]'s palace manipulation, and the weakening of reliable court correction.
- The closing recitation of a [[WangShipeng|王十朋]] poem turns the episode from annalistic narration into posthumous sympathy for a ruler remembered by Chu people despite catastrophic judgment failures.

## Key Quotes
> "楚人皆怜之，如悲亲戚" - the grief formula used for Chu Huaiwang's return after dying in Qin.

> "唯一的救命仙丹" - the episode's description of hezong as the eastern states' anti-Qin remedy.

> "嫉妒就是杀心" - the host's moral label for Zheng Xiu's treatment of the Wei beauty.
