# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》341-4｜被低估的他，堪称中国最硬核的诗人

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the preceding [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-341-3-chuangzuo-331-ge-chengyu-de-dalao-you-duo-lihai-lpxxovwptomp2mmt64o5armp8xrk|Hanji 341-3]] portrait of [[HanYu|韩愈]] by moving from remonstrance and demotion to his dangerous mission among mutinous Chengde troops. It presents Han as a writer-official who accepts risk as part of [[ScholarOfficialPublicDuty|士大夫公共责任]], then uses the remembered fate of earlier rebels and their descendants to make submission materially intelligible to [[WangTingcouTang|王廷凑]]'s soldiers.

The close returns to poetry, death, and later reputation. *Early Spring Presented to Zhang Shiba of the Ministry of Waterworks*, Su Shi's retrospective praise, and a Zhang Zai maxim are used to join literary delicacy, political courage, hardship, and Confucian service into one admiring life judgment.

## Key Claims

- After the 821 Chengde mutiny and the killing of military governor Tian Hongzheng, the court's failed punitive response leaves Wang Tingcou in control and makes a later pacification mission dangerous.
- Han Yu accepts the mission despite official concern and an imperial warning not to enter too deeply, arguing in the episode's telling that receiving the ruler's command creates a ministerial obligation that cannot be abandoned for personal safety.
- Wang Tingcou attempts to intimidate Han with assembled troops, but Han answers by recounting the destructive outcomes suffered by rebels and their descendants since the An Lushan rebellion.
- The episode says Han's speech wins over the troops, stabilizes the situation, and helps free surrounded soldiers and civilians; it attributes the result to nerve, moral position, and persuasive speech rather than military force.
- Han's 823 appointment as vice minister of personnel and his spring poem to Zhang Ji add a quiet, observant literary register to the episode's otherwise martial portrait.
- Han dies in 824, and the episode uses Su Shi's later praise to combine literary renewal, transmission of the Way, public rescue, and willingness to anger a ruler.
- The final moral reading treats orphanhood, examination failure, demotion, and repeated hardship as conditions through which Han preserves purpose, not as proof that suffering automatically produces greatness.

## Key Quotes

> “天街小雨润如酥，草色遥看近却无。最是一年春好处，绝胜烟柳满皇都。” — Han Yu's spring poem, quoted to show the gentler observational side of the episode's “hardcore” figure.

> “为天地立心，为生民立命，为往圣继绝学，为万世开太平。” — Zhang Zai's later maxim, applied by the host as a summary of Han Yu's scholar-official ideal.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is adopted. The source extends Han Yu's profile from dangerous remonstrance and local duty to dangerous commissioned persuasion.
- The supplied summary says “郑州兵变”; the role of Wang Tingcou, the killing of Tian Hongzheng, and the Chengde setting point to the Zhenzhou/镇州 mutiny, so the wiki preserves the transcript wording as an error rather than creating a Zhengzhou event.
- “袁稹” is normalized in prose to the Tang writer-official 元稹, but the brief mention does not warrant a standalone page here.
- The source is an admiring five-minute public-history summary rather than a critical event reconstruction. The exact imperial message, dialogue, troop response, captives released, causal effect of Han's speech, and the claim that the mission single-handedly stabilized the crisis remain source-scoped.
- Su Shi's and Zhang Zai's language is later retrospective framing, not contemporary evidence for the details of the Chengde mission; the episode's “most hardcore poet” label is evaluative rhetoric.
