# 724. The Charge of the Light Brigade: Into The Valley of Death (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode reconstructs the [[BattleOfBalaclava|Battle of Balaclava]] on 25 October 1854, from the fall of the Ottoman-held redoubts through [[ColinCampbellBritishArmy|Colin Campbell]]'s Thin Red Line and [[JamesYorkeScarlett|James Scarlett]]'s Heavy Brigade victory to the [[ChargeOfTheLightBrigade|Charge of the Light Brigade]]. Its central explanation joins terrain and unequal visibility to ambiguous orders, urgency, hierarchy, and the hostile relationships among [[FitzRoySomerset1stBaronRaglan|Raglan]], [[GeorgeBingham3rdEarlOfLucan|Lucan]], [[JamesBrudenell7thEarlOfCardigan|Cardigan]], and [[LouisEdwardNolan|Nolan]].

The Light Brigade reached and temporarily silenced the battery at the end of the North Valley, but the attack consumed a cavalry force preserved through the earlier campaign and did not recover the Causeway Heights. Loss of the Vorontsov Road worsened the British siege supply problem, while contemporary reporting and Tennyson's poetry rapidly turned the soldiers' discipline and sacrifice into [[HeroicFailureMemory|heroic-failure memory]] without resolving institutional responsibility.

## Key Claims

- Balaclava's port and the Vorontsov Road were essential to the British siege of Sevastopol, making the battle a contest over campaign supply as well as a sequence of famous tactical actions.
- Campbell's rifle-armed 93rd Highlanders stopped Russian cavalry in a two-deep line, while Scarlett's outnumbered Heavy Brigade won an uphill cavalry melee.
- Raglan could see Russian troops removing captured British guns from the Sapuni Heights, but Lucan below could not see those guns or share Raglan's battlefield picture.
- Raglan's urgent written order did not identify the target clearly enough for Lucan's position, and Nolan's gesture or explanation failed to establish a shared intent before the attack began.
- Lucan transmitted the order to Cardigan despite recognizing the visible North Valley battery as an exceptionally dangerous target; Cardigan obeyed and kept the brigade in disciplined formation under fire.
- The Light Brigade broke through the Russian battery and dispersed cavalry beyond it, but this limited tactical achievement did not justify the losses or repair the wider campaign position.
- Failure to retake the Causeway Heights and Vorontsov Road forced supplies onto worse routes and deepened the winter siege's logistical vulnerability.
- William Howard Russell's reporting and Tennyson's poem helped preserve the charge as both a story of extraordinary courage and an indictment of the system that spent it.
