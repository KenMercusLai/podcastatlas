# 164.古文的力量：请收下这份千年未变的深情、洞见和勇气

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode has [[QinZong|秦总]] and [[Beimin|北明 / 北民]] discuss [[DuRuo|杜若]]'s [[GuwenDeLiliang|《古文的力量》]], a book shaped by QinZong's relationship to her former Chinese teacher and by her editorial interest in reopening classical prose. The episode argues that [[ClassicalProseReading|古文阅读]] should not remain trapped in exam memory: works such as [[XiangJiXuanZhi|《项脊轩志》]], [[YouBaochanShanJi|《游褒禅山记》]], [[ShangShumiHanTaiweiShu|《上枢密韩太尉书》]], and [[HongmenYan|《鸿门宴》]] preserve emotional restraint, ambition, failure, youthful courage, and power judgment. Its strongest synthesis is that old prose can still train attention to real human situations: private grief, institutional ambition, self-directed growth, and moral pressure inside political competition.

## Key Claims
- [[GuwenDeLiliang|《古文的力量》]] is presented as an editorial and teacher-student project as well as a reading guide: [[DuRuo|杜若]] taught QinZong to love Chinese and classical prose before QinZong later edited the book.
- The episode distinguishes classical prose from classical poetry: prose can be longer, freer, more narrative, and more argumentative, which makes it especially good at preserving scenes, reasoning, and social situations.
- The book's method is not a grammar drill in function words and usage points; it asks readers to recover reality, feeling, and human value from familiar school texts.
- [[XiangJiXuanZhi|《项脊轩志》]] is read against both boredom and internet moral simplification. The small room, family partition, grandmother's hopes, deleted allusions, wife memories, and later repair all belong to one emotional structure.
- The episode treats "庭有枇杷树" as a case of [[RestrainedEmotionInClassicalWriting|restrained emotional writing]]: deep feeling often appears through objects, space, and silence rather than direct confession.
- The deleted allusions in [[XiangJiXuanZhi|《项脊轩志》]] become a case of [[TextbookExcerptFlattening|textbook excerpt flattening]], because removing a young man's ambition can leave readers with only sadness.
- [[WangAnshi|王安石]] and [[YouBaochanShanJi|《游褒禅山记》]] are used to discuss [[SubjectivityThroughFailure|subjectivity through failure]]: a failed public project may still matter if a person truly "尽吾志" rather than living only by external evaluation.
- [[SuZhe|苏辙]]'s [[ShangShumiHanTaiweiShu|《上枢密韩太尉书》]] is read as youthful expansion rather than pure flattery: "养气" requires seeing the world, meeting people, and enlarging experience beyond books alone.
- [[SongRenzong|宋仁宗]]'s later handling of Su Zhe's harsh policy criticism becomes a political-truth-telling example: if the ruler asks for direct speech, punishing direct speech destroys the invitation itself.
- [[HongmenYan|《鸿门宴》]] is read through [[PowerEtiquetteReading|power etiquette reading]]: seat order, address, honorifics, ritual face, and gift handling expose the actual balance among [[XiangYu|项羽]], [[LiuBang|刘邦]], [[ZhangLiang|张良]], [[FanZeng|范增]], and [[FanKuai|樊哙]].
- [[FanZeng|范增]] sees Liu Bang's danger but fails at influence because he misreads Xiang Yu's need for recognition, showing that correct judgment still needs political handling and human psychology.
- The episode's closing moral claim is that "要脸容易输" can push society toward thicker cynicism; [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s placement of Xiang Yu in imperial annals preserves respect for historical force and residual moral face even under brutal power struggle.

## Key Quotes
> "庭有枇杷树，今已亭亭如盖矣" - the episode's central example of object-based grief.

> "文者气之所形" - Su Zhe's formula for prose as the visible form of cultivated inner force.

> "竖子不足与谋" - Fan Zeng's failed contemptuous outburst after Liu Bang escapes.
