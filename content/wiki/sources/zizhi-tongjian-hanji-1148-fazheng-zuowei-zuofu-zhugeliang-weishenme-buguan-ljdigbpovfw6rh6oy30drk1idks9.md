# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1148丨法正作威作福，诸葛亮为什么不管？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode asks why [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] does not stop [[FaZheng|法正]] from using his new power against former enemies after [[LiuBei|刘备]] takes [[YizhouLateHan|益州]]. It interprets the restraint through political consolidation and [[AdvisorRoleBoundary|adviser role boundary]]: Fa Zheng is valuable to Liu Bei and connected to the newly incorporated Yizhou interest, while final authority over a core appointee belongs to Liu Bei rather than Zhuge Liang.

The episode then turns to Zhuge Liang's strict administration after the lax rule attributed to [[LiuZhang|刘璋]]. Its durable contribution is to [[SituationalStrictLenientGovernance|宽严相济的因时治理]]: severity is presented as a temporary correction for weak rules and authority, joined to clear standards, punishment of real offenses, and positive recognition. The same account also exposes a qualification—the claimed tolerance of Fa Zheng's private retaliation sits uneasily with impartial law and the costs borne by his victims.

## Key Claims

- The episode portrays Fa Zheng as retaliating against people who had offended him after Liu Bei's conquest of Yizhou, including using imprisonment and administrative pressure.
- Zhuge Liang reportedly declines requests to restrain Fa Zheng because Fa Zheng made an important contribution to Liu Bei's access to Yizhou and remains politically valuable to the new regime.
- The host argues that intervention could challenge Liu Bei's personnel authority and unsettle Yizhou-linked support during a fragile consolidation period.
- This is framed as role-aware restraint and strategic noncompetition, but the episode does not show that Zhuge Liang approved every retaliatory act or that nonintervention was legally harmless.
- The episode rejects the claim that Liu Bei and Zhuge Liang became simply estranged after entering Shu; it instead presents Fa Zheng as a forward political and military instrument and Zhuge Liang as the administrator stabilizing the rear.
- Zhuge Liang's strict laws are explained as a corrective to Liu Zhang's lax order, unclear discipline, and weak authority rather than as a universal preference for severity.
- “宽猛相济” is presented as alternating remedy: long laxity may require strict correction, while long oppression may require lenient repair.
- Effective strict governance is said to require both punishment and positive recognition, with explicit rules making permitted and prohibited conduct legible.
- The closing preview introduces [[JiangWan|蒋琬]]'s drunken neglect at Guangdu and Liu Bei's impulse to execute him, but leaves the outcome to Hanji 1149.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends existing portraits of Fa Zheng's strategic value and retaliatory danger and adds a governance-stage layer to Zhuge Liang's profile.
- Normative tension: the episode praises Zhuge Liang's impartial rulemaking while also defending nonintervention in Fa Zheng's alleged private retaliation; political explanation does not remove the victims' harm or establish equal enforcement.
- Motive caution: faction stability, respect for Liu Bei's authority, noncompetition with Fa Zheng, and long-term mission are the host's explanations, not direct proof of Zhuge Liang's private reasoning.
- Governance caution: Liu Zhang's laxity, public acceptance of Liu Bei's rule, the speed and causal effect of strict law, and the balance of punishment and reward require comparison with underlying historical texts.
