# 图拉斯｜把校徽印上卫衣 T 恤，美国大学如何把授权校名做成一门生意？

## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode opens with short business and technology updates on [[Apple]], [[AntaSports|安踏]], [[Adobe]], [[Anthropic]], [[OpenAI]], and [[GoogleDeepMind]], then uses U.S. collegiate logo apparel to explain how trademark rights, licensing agents, college sports, fandom identity, and standardized apparel supply chains create a retail market. Its core synthesis is that school-name and mascot merchandise scales when brands can reuse product bases across many institutions while licensing infrastructure and [[CollegeSportsFandomDemand|college sports fandom demand]] concentrate demand around visible teams.

## Key Claims
- U.S. university logo merchandise has moved beyond campus bookstores into mainstream retail and apparel brands, with [[Nike]], [[Adidas]], [[Lululemon]], [[Fabletics]], [[HomeFieldApparel]], and [[HypenVice]] named as examples.
- [[CollegiateLicensingCompany|Collegiate Licensing Company / CLC]] and other agents reduce transaction costs by screening licensees, product plans, supply chains, marketing plans, and channels before universities approve use of marks.
- [[NationalCollegiateAthleticAssociation|NCAA]] sports, local community identity, alumni networks, and family fandom create demand that is wider than current students.
- T-shirts, hoodies, and hats make [[LogoSwapMerchandiseStandardization|logo-swap merchandise standardization]] possible because brands can reuse fabric, fit, sizing, manufacturing, and warehousing while changing school marks, colors, and names.
- Sales are uneven: sports powers can generate large retail volume, while lesser-known schools need sharper demand forecasting, production discipline, and inventory control.
- China's university cultural-creative market is presented as a contrast: some schools operate stores and IP partnerships, but the source says the overall market is still scattered and less systematized.
