# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》912丨秘密到底该不该说出口？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 178 CE secret-memorial prelude immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-913-kan-dong-saiweng-shima-wu-tou-rensheng-fuhuo-lkfbrwtgsjma85bzugqonzk3hy4g|Hanji 913]]. It opens with [[HanFei|韩非]]'s secrecy warning and advice-risk frame, then follows [[YangCiLateHan|杨赐]] and [[CaiYong|蔡邕]] using palace omens to criticize harem, eunuch, Hongdu, and appointment disorder under [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. The core case is [[CaiYong|Cai Yong]]'s request that his memorial remain confidential, [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]'s leak of it, and the retaliation path that turns loyal correction into [[ConfidentialRemonstranceLeakage|密奏泄露风险]].

## Key Claims
- The opening quotes Han Fei's secrecy principle and treats premature disclosure as a way to give opponents time to obstruct, retaliate, or reinterpret an intended action.
- The host argues that advice is risky because many requesters want confirmation more than correction; if contrary advice later proves right, the adviser can still become a resentment target.
- In 178 CE, black qi, a blue-green rainbow in the palace, and hens turning male are read by the court as omens of state disorder rather than as isolated curiosities.
- Yang Ci links the omens to harem/eunuch influence, Hongdu-favored shallow talent, blocked worthy officials, forged-command channels, and imperial extravagance.
- Cai Yong expands the critique by tying omens to harem interference, eunuch or favored-person power, specific court figures, and appointment standards that reward decorative writing and private request.
- Cai Yong asks Emperor Ling to keep the memorial secret so loyal speech will not become material for jealous retaliation.
- Cao Jie reads the emperor's reaction, peeks at the confidential memorial while Ling is away, and leaks the contents to the criticized network.
- The leak gives Cai Yong's targets motive and information to retaliate, setting up Cheng Huang's anonymous accusation against Cai Yong and Cai Zhi that Hanji 913 continues.
- Cai Yong's later self-defense frames his mistake as overestimating imperial protection: he assumed loyal frankness would be sheltered, but the emperor still became suspicious once slander arrived.
- The episode's modern analogy remains source-scoped: giving advice requires risk awareness, audience judgment, and aftermath capacity, not only sincere intent.
