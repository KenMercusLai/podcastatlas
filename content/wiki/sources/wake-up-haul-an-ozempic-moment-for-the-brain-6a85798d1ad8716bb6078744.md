## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three evidence-led briefings: [[ShaileshChitnis]] on [[OrexinAgonists|orexin agonists]] as a possible post-[[GLP1Agonists|GLP-1]] drug excitement, [[EthanWu]] on [[Japan]]'s weak startup record, and [[BenjaminSutherland]] on procrastination as [[ProcrastinationSelfRegulationFailure|self-regulatory failure]]. The drug segment is promising but explicitly early: [[Ovoperextone]] and [[NarcolepsyWakeSleepBoundary|narcolepsy]] supply the first use case, while depression, ADHD, and addiction remain future hopes. The Japan and procrastination segments treat social context as practical constraint: capital markets and status norms shape startup outcomes, while sleep, distraction, exercise, planning, and executive function shape delayed action.

## Key Claims
- [[OrexinAgonists]] mimic orexin, a brain neuropeptide the episode describes as central to wakefulness and the boundary between sleep and waking.
- [[Ovoperextone]], made by [[TakedaPharmaceutical]], is source-described as an [[FoodAndDrugAdministration|FDA]]-approved narcolepsy drug, with [[Alkermes]] and [[EliLilly]] also working on orexin agonists.
- [[EliLilly]]'s purchase of [[SentessaPharmaceuticals]] is presented as evidence that large pharma sees orexin as commercially important.
- [[EmmanuelMignot]]'s [[StanfordUniversity|Stanford]] dog research is used as the discovery story linking narcolepsy to failure to respond to orexin.
- The episode compares the category to [[GLP1Agonists]] and [[Ozempic]], but [[ShaileshChitnis]] cautions that many plausible drug ideas fail before broad market use.
- [[MorganStanley]] is cited for an estimate that orexin medicines could generate almost $16 billion a year by 2035, compared with about $3 billion for current narcolepsy drugs.
- [[EthanWu]] says [[Japan]] has produced only six of roughly 1,400 global unicorns, despite postwar startup successes such as [[Sony]], [[Honda]], and [[SoftBank]].
- The Japan segment makes [[JapaneseStartupExitConstraint]] a three-part problem: too little late-stage venture capital, low social status for entrepreneurship, and pressure to list too early.
- [[GlobalEntrepreneurshipMonitor]] survey evidence is used to show low desirability of entrepreneurship in Japan compared with the global average.
- [[TokyoStockExchange]] reform is presented as a possible correction: from 2030, growth-market firms unable to maintain a 10 billion yen market cap after five years may be delisted.
- [[SakanaAI]] is named as Japan's most valuable unicorn and an AI tailwind, while the episode notes that Japan has not yet produced a decacorn.
- [[BenjaminSutherland]] frames procrastination as [[ProcrastinationSelfRegulationFailure]] linked to lower income, delayed medical treatment, pain, lower self-esteem, and future depression.
- The episode treats [[MentalContrastingImplementationIntentions]] and [[ExerciseSelfMasteryForProcrastination]] as stronger intervention candidates than diet associations, while medication evidence remains indirect and ADHD-specific.

## Key Quotes
> "Ozempic moment" - the episode's framing for possible orexin expansion beyond narcolepsy.

> "self-regulatory failure" - the research label used for procrastination.

> "starting point rather than the end goal" - [[YamajiHiromi]]'s frame for healthier IPOs.
