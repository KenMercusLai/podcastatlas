## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the setup immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-488-yixue-dashi-jingfang-ruhe-shenxian-bisi-ju-llf_4yvk8urtkgidnj2qhvca3vah|Hanji 488]]. It uses workplace promotion language to explain how [[JingFang|京房]]'s [[KaogongfaReformBacklash|考功法]] proposal threatened [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] and [[WuluChongzongWesternHan|五鹿充宗]], leading Shi Xian to remove Jing Fang from the capital by making him Wei Commandery taishou. The core synthesis is that reformers can understand danger yet still lose protection when [[PowerCenterProximityAdvantage|power-center proximity]], direct reporting, and information channels are cut off.

## Key Claims
- Promotion and survival inside large organizations depend not only on effort or competence, but also on who can lift someone upward and whether the person remains near the effective power center.
- Jing Fang's plan to send students such as Ren Liang and Yao Ping to test kaogong in the provinces threatened personnel interests by shifting appointment logic from personal influence toward measurable assessment.
- Shi Xian's decisive move is not immediate accusation but spatial separation: he persuades [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] to send Jing Fang out as Wei Commandery taishou, reducing Jing Fang's access to direct imperial protection.
- Jing Fang recognizes the danger and tries to preserve a reporting route by requesting exemption from ordinary supervision and annual return-to-capital reporting, but Yuan later cancels the return channel.
- Jing Fang's memorials show both political clarity and method weakness: he correctly identifies the effort to block his meetings with Yuan, yet expresses the crisis through omen language that the host treats as politically immature.
- The episode sets up Hanji 488 by noting that Jing Fang's repeated secret memorials are not yet the lethal trigger; the more dangerous vulnerability will come through his trust in [[ZhangBoWesternHan|张伯]].
