## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] interview with psychiatrist and neuroscientist [[RobertMalenka]] explains dopamine as a context-sensitive signal for salience, reinforcement, arousal, memory, and behavior selection rather than a synonym for pleasure. It connects [[RewardCircuitContext]], [[AddictionRelatedRewardPlasticity]], and [[SocialRewardNeuromodulation]] through the VTA, nucleus accumbens, cortical and limbic inputs, and interacting dopamine, serotonin, and oxytocin systems. The discussion then uses [[EmpathyLikeBehavioralAssays]], autism models, and [[MDMA]] as cautious translational examples while preserving animal-to-human, diagnostic, legal, and clinical boundaries.

## Key Claims
- [[RewardCircuitContext]] frames reward as a changing circuit interpretation shaped by body state, memory, expectation, emotion, sensory input, and behavioral rules rather than a fixed property of a stimulus.
- Addictive liability is associated in the episode with both the amount and speed of dopamine increase, while [[AddictionRelatedRewardPlasticity]] explains how drugs and learned cues can persistently reorganize reward-related synapses and wanting.
- Wanting and reinforcement can diverge from liking: a stimulus may increase the behavior that obtains it even when the experience is no longer pleasurable.
- [[SocialRewardNeuromodulation]] treats positive social interaction as evolutionarily adaptive reinforcement produced by interacting dopamine, serotonin, and oxytocin systems rather than one “social molecule.”
- [[EmpathyLikeBehavioralAssays]] operationalizes pain transfer, social analgesia, generosity, and harm prevention in mice without treating those tasks as complete human empathy.
- [[AutismBiologicalHeterogeneity]] rules out blanket claims about social motivation or empathy and supports subgroup- and dimension-specific interpretation of animal and human findings.
- [[MDMA]] is presented as a serotonin- and dopamine-releasing research probe with possible prosocial and therapeutic relevance, not as a miracle cure or unsupervised protocol.

## Key Quotes
> “I hate this and I can’t wait to do it again.” — a recovered cocaine user’s description, quoted in the episode to distinguish reinforcement from pleasure.

> “Reinforcing” — Malenka’s preferred term for a stimulus that increases the behavior leading to it, whether or not it feels good.
