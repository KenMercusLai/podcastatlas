# 218. Theodora: Empress of Byzantium (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode introduces [[Theodora]] and [[JustinianI]] by setting their rise within [[Constantinople]], the wealthy Christian capital of the continuing eastern [[RomanEmpire|Roman Empire]]. It rejects “Byzantine” as the inhabitants' own identity, emphasizes the city's strategic geography and Roman institutions, and presents the sixth-century state as administratively and financially strong rather than a decadent remnant.

Theodora's movement from the stigmatized entertainment world of the Hippodrome through Alexandria, [[MiaphysiteChristianity|Miaphysite Christianity]], marriage, and elevation as Augusta supplies the biographical arc. Because much of her early life comes through [[Procopius]]'s hostile sexual invective, the episode distinguishes recoverable context from sensational allegation while showing how faction, theology, law, status, and imperial partnership converged in her ascent.

## Key Claims

- Constantinople's defensible position between Europe and Asia, access to the Danube and Persian frontiers, aqueducts, walls, wealth, and Roman institutions made it a durable imperial capital rather than an eastern afterthought.
- The people later called Byzantine understood themselves as Romans; the modern label can obscure continuity in state identity, institutions, and imperial ambition.
- The Hippodrome joined entertainment to politics: its Blues and Greens could operate as sporting factions, social networks, militias, and participants in religious conflict.
- Theodora grew up in this low-status but highly visible entertainment economy, yet the sexually explicit details preserved by Procopius remain hostile testimony rather than settled biography.
- Her Alexandrian religious experience and Miaphysite commitment became a durable part of her political and confessional identity.
- Justinian's rise joined education, court access, Blue faction support, Christian Roman ambition, and the succession of his uncle Justin I.
- A legal change allowing a repentant actress to marry across status barriers enabled Justinian's marriage to Theodora; her elevation as Augusta in 527 made their governing partnership explicit.

## Key Quotes

> “Romans” - the identity the episode says the empire's inhabitants claimed for themselves rather than “Byzantines.”

> “new Rome” - the political and Christian role assigned to Constantinople after Constantine's foundation.
