# 521. Warlords of the West: Killer Queens (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[Fredegund]] and [[Brunhilde]] through marriage, assassination, succession crisis, regency, war, and dynastic revenge in the sixth-century [[MerovingianDynasty|Merovingian]] kingdoms. Their authority was extraordinary but not free-standing: husbands, sons, royal maternity, aristocratic support, territorial rivalry, and survival supplied the political channels through which both women governed.

The narrative is also an exercise in source caution. [[GregoryOfTours|Gregory of Tours]] is openly hostile to Fredegund and more sympathetic to Brunhilde, so accusations of assassination, witchcraft, and cruelty must be separated from better-grounded political outcomes. Fredegund died in 597 after securing Neustria for her son Clothar; Brunhilde's much later defeat and execution let Clothar's victory recast the feud around her alleged crimes.

## Key Claims

- Partition among Clothar's sons made rivalry between Chilperic's Neustria and Sigebert's Austrasia both familial and territorial.
- Fredegund rose from likely servile status through Chilperic's household, became queen after Galswintha's murder, and repeatedly used intelligence, alliance, intimidation, and violence to survive dynastic crises.
- Brunhilde, a Visigothic princess and Galswintha's sister, converted marriage, royal maternity, diplomacy, and regency into durable authority in Austrasia and Burgundy.
- Sigebert's assassination reversed Chilperic's near-defeat, but Fredegund's responsibility remains an inference based on motive, reputation, and benefit rather than demonstrated proof.
- Both queens governed as regents at the same time; Fredegund also led a smaller Neustrian army to victory at Doisy, showing that their authority could extend beyond court intercession.
- Queenship remained structurally precarious because it depended on male dynastic ties, minor heirs, noble support, and successful control of succession.
- Gregory's asymmetrical treatment and Clothar's final victory shaped the moral memory of both women as much as their recoverable actions did.

## Key Quotes

> “never met” - the episode's striking summary of two rulers whose decades-long feud was conducted through kin, courts, agents, and armies.

> “her humble servant” - Fredegund's self-presentation to Guntram while seeking protection for herself and her infant son after Chilperic's murder.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is adopted. The episode extends the Merovingian profile backward from Carolingian-era palace-mayoral dominance to a period when queens, minor kings, bishops, and regional aristocracies still fought through the royal dynasty itself.
- Gregory's accusations against Fredegund, including responsibility for particular assassinations and reliance on witchcraft, are not treated as neutral proof; the episode sometimes reports suspicion more securely than causation.
- The episode argues that Fredegund had little strategic reason to arrange Chilperic's death while her heir was an infant, qualifying rather than resolving the accusation.
- Brunhilde's final prosecution for murdering ten kings is treated as victor's political narrative, while the exact responsibility of either queen for many killings, private motives, speeches, and court anecdotes remains uncertain or source-scoped.
