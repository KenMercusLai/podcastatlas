# Anthropic's Fable Backlash, Nationalizing AI, Inflation Heats Up & California's Broken Elections

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] episode links [[Anthropic]]'s [[Fable5|Fable 5]] launch controversy to larger arguments about [[FrontierModelAccessRestrictions]], [[AIRegulatoryCaptureRisk]], [[OpenSourceAIModels]], and where frontier-model safety controls should operate. The hosts treat prompt retention, silent downgrades, enterprise trust, and biology/cyber guardrails as product-governance issues, while proposing narrower alternatives such as [[FrontierModelVerifiedAccess]] and [[SyntheticBiologyScreeningSafeguards]].

The second half turns AI control into political economy: [[BernieSanders]]' proposal for public AI-company ownership becomes a debate over [[AIPublicOwnershipProposal]], [[AIAutomationRedistribution]], [[SocialSecurity]], and whether AI CEOs' own job-loss rhetoric invites redistribution politics. The closing macro and election segments connect hot CPI/PPI claims, [[Iran]]-linked energy risk, and the Los Angeles mayoral primary to [[ElectionAdministrationTrust]] and [[BallotHarvestingTrustRisk]], while keeping fraud claims source-scoped and unresolved.

## Key Claims
- The hosts describe [[Fable5|Fable 5]] as a frontier model whose capability and price matter less, for enterprise trust, than questions about prompt retention, data use, access stability, and undisclosed downgrades.
- [[DavidFriedberg|Friedberg]] says biology and genomics users may be pushed toward local [[OpenSourceAIModels|open-source models]] when frontier API guardrails block legitimate research prompts.
- The episode uses genome language models and plant-breeding workflows to show that [[GenomeLanguageModels]] can be practical industrial infrastructure, not only AI-safety abstraction.
- [[DavidSacks|Sacks]] argues that broad release restrictions plus calls for an AI approval agency can look like [[AIRegulatoryCaptureRisk]], especially when narrower tools such as user verification are not tried first.
- The hosts present downstream nucleic-acid synthesis screening and record keeping as a more targeted biosecurity safeguard than broad refusal behavior inside general models.
- [[ChamathPalihapitiya|Chamath]] argues that gigawatt-scale AI compute costs make infrastructure, power, capital, and cloud relationships part of the model-control problem.
- [[BernieSanders]]' public-ownership proposal is framed as a predictable political response to AI executives warning about job loss after training on public human knowledge.
- Friedberg rejects confiscatory ownership but proposes a [[SocialSecurity]] equity-ownership alternative in which citizens hold market exposure through retirement accounts.
- The hosts argue that AI's business value may expand revenue and engineering capacity rather than simply eliminate jobs, but they acknowledge that danger and job-loss messaging can make redistribution politics more plausible.
- The macro segment treats May CPI/PPI and [[Iran]]-related energy risk as source-attributed warnings that geopolitical shocks and government spending could keep inflation pressure alive.
- The Los Angeles election segment argues that mail-ballot timing, ballot collection, voter-roll quality, weak ID rules, and chain-of-custody concerns can damage legitimacy even when fraud is not proven.
- [[JasonCalacanis|Jason]] supports voter ID and tighter ballot controls while repeatedly distinguishing suspicious statistics from current evidence that a major election was fraudulently swung.
