# 613. Nelson: Glory at Trafalgar (Part 6)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the [[BattleOfTrafalgar|Battle of Trafalgar]] from the fleets' dawn sighting on 21 October 1805 through [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]]'s death, the storm-damaged aftermath, state funeral, and monumental afterlife. It presents the victory as the conjunction of [[NavalBreakthroughTactics|risk-heavy breakthrough tactics]], trained crews, delegated command, and a [[FiscalMilitaryNavalState|long-built national system]], while treating Nelson's sacrifice, flaws, empire, and patriotic symbolism as inseparable parts of his endurance in British memory.

## Key Claims

- Nelson and [[CuthbertCollingwood]] attacked the larger French-Spanish fleet in two columns, accepting fire during the slow approach so they could split the line and exploit British close-range gunnery, crew preparation, and local initiative.
- The “England expects” signal, Nelson's conspicuous exposure, prayer, farewell codicil, and refusal to move to a safer frigate made duty, fatalism, religious submission, and public self-fashioning part of battlefield leadership.
- Victory's stern-raking broadside against Bucentaure and the close struggle with Redoutable show both the destructive payoff and personal danger of line penetration; a marksman aboard Redoutable mortally wounded Nelson.
- [[PierreCharlesVilleneuve]] and [[FedericoGravina]] are not presented as cowards: both remained conspicuous under fire, while the combined fleet's uneven formation and weaker preparation limited its response.
- The episode credits Trafalgar to the [[RoyalNavy]] and a wider mobilization of sailors, officers, dockyards, workshops, finance, government, and industrial labor rather than to Nelson alone.
- Nelson's death during victory, fleet-wide grief, state funeral, monuments, and Nelson's Column turned an admiral into a national icon whose memory still carries disputes over empire, abolition, and patriotic honor.

## Key Quotes

> "England expects that every man will do his duty." - the signal sent from Victory before contact, after signal lieutenant John Pascoe proposed “expects” as a practical substitute for Nelson's requested “confides.”

> "God and my country." - the episode's account of Nelson's final whispered words; reported dialogue and last words remain source-scoped.
