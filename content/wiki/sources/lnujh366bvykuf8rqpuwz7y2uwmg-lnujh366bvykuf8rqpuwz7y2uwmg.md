# 032 教师节特辑 | 韦小宝和他的师父们

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode reads [[LuDingJi|《鹿鼎记》]] through two contrasting teachers of [[WeiXiaobao|韦小宝]]. [[HaiDafu|海大富]] never formally accepts him as a disciple, yet teaches immediately usable techniques and the unequal reality of imperial power. [[ChenJinnan|陈近南]] becomes his formal master, moral exemplar, and surrogate father, although the relationship begins in mutual utility and never erases their disagreement over loyalty.

The episode admires Chen's courage, organization, and willingness to pursue an almost hopeless cause while criticizing his dynastic orthodoxy and unconditional service to the Zheng family. Wei answers abstract loyalty with obligation to particular people who have treated him well. Chen's death inside the faction he serves therefore joins personal bereavement to a wider diagnosis: shared political purpose does not prevent succession rivalry, private interest, or organizational self-destruction.

## Key Claims

- [[HaiDafu|海大富]] is an effective teacher without formal status because his instruction transfers directly into Wei's immediate survival, while his warning about the emperor teaches the hierarchy behind apparent friendship.
- [[ChenJinnan|陈近南]] combines heroic integrity, organizational ability, instrumental recruitment, adaptive judgment of talent, and a dynastic loyalty that the episode treats as his fatal political limit.
- Chen's relationship with [[WeiXiaobao|韦小宝]] changes from using a palace insider to recognizing a capable disciple and entrusting him with emotionally and politically significant responsibilities.
- [[ConcreteReciprocityOverAbstractLoyalty|Concrete reciprocity over abstract loyalty]] explains Wei's attempt to help both Kangxi and Tiandihui: he ranks obligations to people through treatment, friendship, and non-betrayal rather than through one exclusive doctrine.
- [[SharedCauseFactionalErosion|Shared-cause factional erosion]] explains why anti-Qing groups and the Zheng regime continue to fight over claimants, succession, kinship, and interest despite professing the same restoration goal.
- Chen's death by the Zheng faction he protects turns the novel's heroic ideal into tragedy and intensifies Wei's disillusionment without making Chen's courage or moral influence unreal.
