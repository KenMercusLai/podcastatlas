## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty-Corino]] interview [[ElizabethLaird]] of the [[CenterForDemocracyAndTechnology|Center for Democracy and Technology]] about national polling on Americans' concern over federal personal-data practices. Laird says the concern is broad across political affiliation, race, ethnicity, gender, and geography, and that many respondents feel the government has extensive data about them with little practical recourse.

The episode's core synthesis is that privacy mistrust is not only an abstract civil-liberties issue; it can change access to public services. Uncertainty about how data will be used may discourage people from applying for education, housing, health, or nutrition benefits, while concern about data sharing with law enforcement or the [[USDepartmentOfHomelandSecurity|Department of Homeland Security]] makes [[EnforcementAgencyDataSharing]] and [[GovernmentDataAccountability]] central to public trust.

## Key Claims
- [[ElizabethLaird]] says the [[CenterForDemocracyAndTechnology|Center for Democracy and Technology]] conducted nationally representative polling on federal government data practices.
- The episode says three in four respondents were concerned about what the federal government knows about them.
- Laird says high concern appeared across political affiliation, race and ethnicity, gender, and location.
- A majority agreed with the idea that the government has a lot of data about them and there is little they can do about it.
- Laird argues that high concern plus helplessness creates conditions for public backlash around [[FederalDataPracticeTrust]].
- The episode highlights access to public benefits and services, including education, housing, health, and nutrition.
- Nearly half of respondents said they would not sign up for benefits if they were unsure what would happen with their data.
- Laird says many Americans are concerned about personal data being shared with law enforcement or the [[USDepartmentOfHomelandSecurity|Department of Homeland Security]].
- Laird frames the survey as evidence that Americans want oversight and accountability when agencies may be misusing personal data.
- The closing promo mentions How We Survive, Marketplace's climate-solutions podcast, but the main source contribution is federal data trust.
