# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》199｜中国历史上第一位女皇帝 不是武后（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[LuZhi|吕雉]]'s early regency as a reallocation of formal office, practical access, and dynastic reward. [[WangLingHanMinister|王陵]] openly opposes making Lü relatives kings and is moved to the prestigious but practically weaker office of grand tutor before removal; [[ChenPing|陈平]] and [[ZhouBo|周勃]] instead accept the immediate balance while preserving room for later action. The resulting court places Chen Ping over external administration and [[ShenYiji|审食其]] near the palace center, producing a clear [[NominalOfficePracticalPowerMismatch|名位—实权错位]] between chancellorial title and actual work.

The second half joins that institutional restructuring to personal memory and family expansion. The episode interprets Zhao Yao's dismissal, Ren Ao's promotion, posthumous honors for Lü Gong and [[LuZe|吕泽]], grants to children attributed to [[LiuYing|刘盈]], and Lü Tai's elevation as a mixture of fear, gratitude, revenge, and procedural preparation for Lü-family kingship. Those motives, the children's parentage, and the closing disaster symbolism remain episode-attributed rather than settled fact.

## Key Claims

- Wang Ling rejects Lu Zhi's proposal to honor her father and brother as kings, while Chen Ping and Zhou Bo argue that direct confrontation is less useful than preserving the Liu dynasty over the longer term.
- Lu Zhi moves Wang Ling from the chancellorship to grand tutor for the young emperor; the episode reads the prestigious appointment as a practical demotion, after which Wang Ling claims illness, is dismissed, and withdraws from court.
- Chen Ping becomes right chancellor, while Shen Yiji becomes left chancellor but reportedly performs palace-facing work resembling that of the chamberlain rather than ordinary chancellorial administration.
- The host explains Shen Yiji's access through his care for Lu Zhi during the Chu-Han hostage years and through Lu Zhi's fear that founding ministers might exploit the weak authority of a child ruler.
- The episode reports that Lu Zhi and Shen Yiji briefly considered withholding news of Liu Bang's death and killing senior merit ministers until Li Shang's warning, delivered through Shen Yiji, stopped the plan.
- Lu Zhi removes [[ZhaoYao|赵尧]] and promotes Ren Ao; the host interprets the changes as revenge for Zhao Yao's protection of Liu Ruyi and repayment for Ren Ao's reported protection of Lu Zhi during an earlier imprisonment.
- Lu Zhi posthumously honors her father as Xuan Wang and Lu Ze as Dao Wu Wang, abolishes collective-family execution and the “妖言” prohibition, and grants titles to several boys attributed to Liu Ying.
- The source says Lu Zhi first grants Liu-family titles and then has Zhang Shi mobilize a ministerial request to make Lu Tai king of Lü, using territory separated from Qi to establish the new kingdom.
- A Zhao palace fire and autumn flowering of peach and plum trees close the episode, but no causal relationship between the anomalies and the political changes is established.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source extends the existing account of Wang Ling's opposition and Chen Ping and Zhou Bo's accommodation by adding the grand-tutor removal and the Chen Ping–Shen Yiji division of work.
- The supplied text repeatedly writes “沈一基”; the wiki normalizes this to [[ShenYiji|审食其]] from the Biyang Hou title, hostage biography, and surrounding early-Han context.
- The title's “first female emperor” is an interpretive description of Lu Zhi's governing position, not evidence that she formally assumed the 皇帝 title.
- The alleged plan to conceal Liu Bang's death and kill merit ministers, Li Shang's intervention, Shen Yiji's exact palace powers, and Lu Zhi's insecurity are episode-attributed and require comparison with the transmitted texts.
- The motives assigned to Zhao Yao's dismissal and Ren Ao's promotion, Ren Ao's prison intervention, title dates and territories, the parentage of Liu Ying's alleged sons, Zhang Shi's orchestration, and the later killings foreshadowed by the host remain source-scoped.
- The palace fire and out-of-season blossoms are reported anomalies; treating them as omens of political disorder would be interpretation rather than demonstrated causation.
