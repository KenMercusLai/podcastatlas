# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1080丨曹操凯旋后重赏反对者？背后暴露顶级领导力

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode centers on [[CaoCao|曹操]] rewarding the people who had opposed his dangerous [[Wuhuan|乌桓]] campaign after he returned victorious. By calling the success fortunate rather than repeatable, Cao Cao protects future criticism; the episode contrasts this response with [[YuanShao|袁绍]] killing [[TianFengLateHan|田丰]] after rejected advice proved prescient.

The closing movement links the hard return journey and *《观沧海》* to [[GuoJia|郭嘉]]'s death. Its claim that this death cleanly divides Cao Cao's undefeated and post-[[RedCliffsBattle|赤壁之战]] careers is a dramatic retrospective interpretation, not established single-cause history.

## Key Claims

- Cao Cao's army returns from the Wuhuan campaign after severe winter, drought, water, food, and transport pressure; the episode uses those costs to explain why victory did not make the original decision safely repeatable.
- Cao Cao identifies the officers who had advised against the campaign, rewards them rather than punishing them, and asks them to keep criticizing him.
- The episode interprets Cao Cao's public attribution of success to fortune as a feedback-protection act: outcome success does not by itself prove that a high-risk decision was sound.
- Yuan Shao's killing of Tian Feng supplies the negative comparison, because advice vindicated by defeat becomes a threat to ruler face instead of an input to correction.
- Cao Cao's return by Jieshi Mountain is connected with 《观沧海》 and an expansive image of political ambition; the literary interpretation remains the host's reading.
- Guo Jia dies during the return journey, setting up Hanji 1081's fuller treatment of the capability loss inside Cao Cao's adviser system.

## Key Quotes

> "虽得侥幸，非万全之计。" - the episode's rendering of Cao Cao's refusal to treat the victory as a normal model.

> "诸君之谏，万安之计，是以相赏，后勿难言之。" - the episode's core statement protecting future remonstrance.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is recorded. The episode's leadership lesson is compatible with the wiki's morally mixed Cao Cao profile: protecting dissent in this case does not erase coercive or violent conduct elsewhere.
- The transcript contains corrupt names, dates, and quotations. The unidentified loyalist rendered as “千钊,” exact march figures, dialogue wording, and literary mind-reading are not promoted as settled facts.
- The claim that Guo Jia's death divides an undefeated early career from later failure is retained as dramatic framing; it neither proves that Guo Jia would have prevented Red Cliffs nor makes his death the sole cause of Cao Cao's later limits.
