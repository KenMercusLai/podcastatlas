# Bombs Away LeMay

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode contrasts [[HaywoodHansell]]'s high-altitude precision-bombing doctrine with [[CurtisLeMay]]'s low-altitude incendiary campaign against Japan. It presents the jet stream, navigation limits, weather, organizational pressure, and LeMay's preference for action as the chain that turned failed attacks on the Nakajima aircraft plant into [[NapalmWarfare|area firebombing]] of Tokyo and 66 other cities.

The account treats LeMay as a highly capable practical commander whose decisiveness solved an operational problem while crossing an immense moral boundary. Operation Meetinghouse is presented as militarily consequential and catastrophically destructive, with the episode postponing rather than resolving judgment over mass civilian death and the claim that air power helped avoid a ground invasion.

## Key Claims

- LeMay's practical competence, willingness to share aircrew risk, and preference for action made him an effective crisis commander while also weakening hesitation when a mission's human consequences changed.
- Hansell's attempt to destroy Japanese aircraft production through high-altitude precision bombing failed because cloud, navigation limits, targeting accuracy, and the newly encountered jet stream defeated the doctrine's operating assumptions.
- The shift below the jet stream created a cascade: low altitude increased daylight vulnerability, night operations removed precision, and the resulting doctrine targeted urban areas with incendiaries.
- Institutional investment in the B-29 and Mariana bases, pressure for measurable results, and fear of command replacement reinforced LeMay's willingness to adopt a radically destructive alternative.
- Operation Meetinghouse sent 334 B-29s against a densely populated Tokyo district, producing a firestorm that the episode says burned about 16 square miles and killed more than 100,000 people.
- Subsequent raids struck another 66 Japanese cities; the episode places their claimed military success beside unresolved questions of civilian protection, command responsibility, and counterfactual invasion costs.
- The episode's contrast between Hansell and LeMay illustrates both an [[InnovationVisionExecutionGap]] and a [[ConstraintDrivenDoctrineShift]]: failure of one humane-seeming technical theory did not make the chosen alternative morally inevitable.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode deepens the existing Bomber Mafia account by specifying how the jet stream and related constraints defeated Hansell's campaign and how LeMay converted those constraints into area bombing.
- The claim that the air campaign succeeded by helping avoid a ground invasion is a counterfactual strategic judgment, not proof that incendiary attacks were necessary, proportionate, or the only effective alternative.
- The idealist-versus-problem-solver contrast clarifies the narrative but may understate responsibility elsewhere in the chain of command and the extent to which Hansell's supposedly precise campaign also caused civilian harm.
