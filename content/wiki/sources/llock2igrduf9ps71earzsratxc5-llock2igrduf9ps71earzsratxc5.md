# 024 奇人 奇遇 奇功：闲话金庸封笔作《鹿鼎记》

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode reads [[WeiXiaobao|韦小宝]] as the antiheroic center of [[LuDingJi|《鹿鼎记》]]. Although luck and protective equipment repeatedly matter, his durable advantage is a combination of [[InformalSelectiveLearning|street-formed learning]], rapid diagnosis of desire and fear, willingness to take calculated risks, selective disclosure, generosity, self-knowledge, and the ability to recruit people with skills he lacks.

The discussion connects that character design to [[MatureGenreDeconstruction|wuxia deconstruction]]. The novel retains masters, combat, secret societies, court politics, and historical events while asking why martial victory is treated as noble but ash, poison, deception, or improvised traps are dismissed as vulgar. Its favorable literary treatment of [[KangxiEmperor|康熙]] is likewise tied to [[PeopleCenteredHistoricalJudgment|popular welfare]] rather than ethnic identity alone, although the episode's historical claims and judgments about authorial intention remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- Wei never follows the conventional rise from unknown youth to martial master; equipment, escape technique, companions, improvisation, and social judgment compensate for weak combat ability.
- Brothel life, storytelling, theater, gambling, and street conflict function as an informal education in language, information, risk, deception, status, and human motives.
- His encounters are often accidental, but opportunity becomes consequential only because he notices power shifts, identifies desires and fears, and is willing to act under danger.
- Capturing Oboi, protecting Shunzhi and Kangxi, exposing the false empress dowager, disrupting Wu Sangui's alliances, assisting Sophia, and coordinating frontier war make Wei an implausibly concentrated agent of early Kangxi-era history.
- The novel questions whether respectable martial procedure is morally superior to a weaker person's ash, poison, deception, or ambush when the outcome is survival against stronger violence.
- Wei's most transferable competence is not a weapon or specialized discipline but self-knowledge, selective speech, recruitment, generosity, and integration of other people's abilities.
- The hosts interpret Shunzhi's “never increase taxes” standard and Kangxi's favorable portrayal as evidence that rulers should be judged by treatment of ordinary people rather than Han-Manchu identity alone.
- Palace, brothel, Jianghu, religion, and foreign court are presented as different surfaces over recurring structures of exchange, performance, hierarchy, fear, and opportunism.
