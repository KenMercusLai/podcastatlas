# 564. The Great Northern War: The Battle of the Baltic (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode introduces the [[GreatNorthernWar|Great Northern War]] through the rivalry between [[CharlesXII|Charles XII]] and [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]]. Sweden begins as the Baltic's disciplined military hegemon, defeats Russia at the [[BattleOfNarva|Battle of Narva]], and then spends years subduing [[AugustusTheStrong|Augustus the Strong]] while Peter turns humiliation into [[DefeatDrivenMilitaryReform|military adaptation]], territorial advance, and the founding of [[SaintPetersburg|Saint Petersburg]]. Charles's pursuit of total victory leaves the [[SwedishEmpire|Swedish Empire]] exposed to a rebuilt [[RussianEmpire|Russia]] and culminates in the 1707 decision to march on Moscow.

## Key Claims

- Russia enters the war to secure Ingria and Karelia and obtain reliable Baltic access, while Sweden begins with a wealthy, administratively sophisticated empire and one of Europe's most effective armies.
- Charles's upbringing, ascetic discipline, personal courage, religious certainty, and refusal to end a supposedly just war without total victory combine military charisma with strategic rigidity.
- At Narva, a smaller Swedish force destroys Peter's inexperienced army; the victory strengthens Charles's contempt for Russia even as defeat gives Peter both the urgency and time to rebuild.
- Peter responds through conscription, drilling, flintlocks, bayonets, artillery production, taxation, coercive labor, and the capture of Ingria, joining military learning to [[ModernizingAutocracy|modernizing autocracy]].
- The capture of Nöteborg and Nyenskans enables Peter to found Saint Petersburg as a port, fortress, and west-facing state project, but construction rests on forced movement, harsh labor, disease, and mass death.
- [[CatherineI|Catherine I]] enters Peter's household through the war's violence and social dislocation, moving from captive servant to partner and secret wife.
- Charles's prolonged pursuit of Augustus through Poland and Saxony achieves deposition and surrender but gives Peter time to recover, take territory, and secure Saint Petersburg.
- By rejecting a limited Baltic settlement and choosing a march on Moscow, Charles turns a territorial war into an attempt to break the Russian state.

## Key Quotes

> "I shall never begin an unjust war, nor ever end a just war without defeating my enemy." - Charles's reported declaration to his council.

> "window on the West" - the episode's description of Saint Petersburg's intended strategic and cultural role.
