# Gilets jeunes? France’s school protests

## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode examines three institutions people turn to under uncertainty. French secondary-school complaints about timetables, absent teachers, and deteriorating buildings widen into national anger over public spending, living costs, policing, and the next presidential election; the Cornell Seven case exposes unstable, burdensome, and weakly supervised campus sexual-misconduct procedure under [[TitleIX]]; and Indian fund managers and companies use astrology despite little evidence that it predicts financial outcomes. Together the segments extend [[ProtestEscalationLoop]], establish [[CampusSexualMisconductAdministrativeJustice]], and sharpen the evidence boundary around [[FinancialAstrologyDecisionMaking]].

## Key Claims
- The episode says more than 250,000 people joined French school protests, over 1,000 secondary schools were blockaded or affected by violence, and 5,000 people had been arrested by Monday, 85% of them minors.
- Student grievances began with demanding baccalaureate-era timetables, teacher absences, and poorly maintained buildings, then widened into anger about spending cuts, living costs, police tactics, and the prospect of a [[MarineLePen]]-led government.
- Prime Minister [[SebastienLecornu]] offered a half-day suspension of ordinary classes for school-council and headteacher discussions, while political factions blamed either far-left incitement or excessive government policing.
- The French government feared that school unrest could become a broader national movement; the source expects half-term to interrupt school mobilization without resolving pressure over petrol prices and household costs.
- In the Cornell Seven case, an anonymous student accused seven men of drugging and raping her at a fraternity party; the men denied wrongdoing, no criminal charges were brought, and a university [[TitleIX]] investigation and later civil suit followed.
- The complainant's suit says she participated in twelve hearings, illustrating how extensive procedural protections can also burden and discourage a person pursuing a campus complaint.
- The episode says only 14% of New York incidents reported to Title IX coordinators in 2024 proceeded to investigation, while completed federal civil-rights investigations into campus sexual violence fell sharply after the 2020 rule.
- [[CornellUniversity]] says students found responsible for sexual misconduct were expelled or suspended, but the source questions deterrence because sanctions and outcomes vary and a 2025 Cornell survey reported sexual assault among more than one-third of female undergraduates.
- Governor [[KathyHochul]] appointed Attorney General [[LetitiaJames]] to investigate the Cornell case, adding state oversight to the university, campus-police, and county-prosecutor scrutiny.
- The astrology segment says 44% of Indian adults believe in astrology and describes fund managers using astrologers' WhatsApp groups, sometimes alongside conventional analysis rather than instead of it.
- A fund manager says he gives astrology roughly half the weight in investment decisions, while astrologers also advise businesses on offerings, deals, factory construction, and executive selection.
- Studies cited by the episode found underperformance among investors favoring firms associated with lucky eight and avoiding unlucky four; anecdotes about successful trades do not establish predictive power.
- Astrology courses and platforms can earn substantial revenue even when clients lack evidence of better investment returns, creating a stronger commercial case for selling advice than an empirical case for following it.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction was adopted. The French segment extends the existing escalation mechanism but does not establish that school grievances, police action, budget pressure, or electoral fear alone caused the nationwide movement; its counts and forecast remain source-dated.
- The Cornell allegations are contested, no criminal charges were brought, and university responsibility findings are not equivalent to criminal convictions. The episode supports criticism of procedure and oversight, not a factual adjudication of the alleged assault.
- Due-process protections and complainant accessibility are not simple opposites. The source argues that the 2020 framework may discourage investigation, but does not show that weaker protections would necessarily improve accuracy, fairness, deterrence, or survivor welfare.
- Indian astrology practices may provide confidence or cultural meaning, but anecdotes, horoscope matching, and lucky-number behavior do not demonstrate reliable financial prediction. Market-size, fundraising, price, and belief figures remain source-reported.
