# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》868丨赢了也是输家？东汉后宫争艳记

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 165 CE layer before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-869-liying-weihe-bei-chengwei-tianxia-diyi-kaimo-lltckcioe1n9v91yy4c9vvwmz4mo|Hanji 869]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-870-gudai-tizhinei-buganle-meiyou-tuilu-lpwbniu9ifpt6cp4ip9czqwsh5xd|Hanji 870]]. It pairs [[WangChangShumaoLateHan|王畅]]'s turn from harsh punishment toward humane local governance in [[NanyangCommandery|南阳郡]] with the court notices of per-mu land taxation, a Luoyang earthquake, and [[LiuMaoLateHan|刘茂]]'s appointment as sikong.

The second half follows [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]'s harem politics from [[EmpressDengHuanLateHan|邓皇后]] and [[GuoGuirenHuanLateHan|郭贵人]] through the contest between [[TianShengLateHan|田圣 / 田胜]] and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙]]. The source sharpens the distinction between personal favor and formal status: Tian Sheng is loved but blocked, Dou Miao becomes empress but not beloved, and [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] rises through his daughter's selection.

## Key Claims

- Wang Chang initially attacks powerful Nanyang households harshly, but Zhang Chang persuades him that durable local order requires broad, humane governance rather than fear alone.
- Zhang Chang's argument links Nanyang's special imperial status, earlier educative administrators, and the Confucian idea that upright personal conduct can transform people.
- On August 6, 165 CE, the court first orders commanderies and kingdoms to tax landholders per mu, but the source emphasizes how compressed this major institutional notice is in the chronicle.
- The host uses the land-tax notice to argue that much traditional Chinese historical writing foregrounds people and power more than institutional mechanics, while works such as Du You's Tongdian preserve more system detail.
- The episode records a September 15 Luoyang earthquake and a winter-tenth-month office change in which Tong Jing is removed as sikong and Liu Mao is appointed.
- Emperor Huan's post-Liang harem politics first turns on Empress Deng and Guo Guiren: Guo outlasts Deng, but that victory does not produce stable affection or status.
- Huan favors Tian Sheng and wants to make her empress, but Ying Feng and Chen Fan oppose the move because her background and rank do not support the title.
- Dou Miao becomes empress with eunuch and official backing, but the host stresses the irony that winning the formal title leaves her emotionally defeated because Huan does not love her.
- Dou Wu's political rise begins here: after Dou Miao becomes empress, he is elevated to Tejin and Huaili Hou, then appointed Chengmen Xiaowei.

## Key Quotes

> "其身正，不令而行；其身不正，虽令不从" - Zhang Chang's quoted principle for governance by official example.

> "转一念" - the host's description of Wang Chang's shift away from punitive local action.

> "赢了也是输家" - the episode's harem-politics judgment on Dou Miao's formal victory without affection.
