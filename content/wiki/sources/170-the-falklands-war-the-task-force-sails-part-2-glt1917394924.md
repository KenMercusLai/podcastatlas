# 170. The Falklands War: The Task Force Sails (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the opening of the [[FalklandsWar|Falklands War]] from [[RexHunt|Rex Hunt]]'s decision to end resistance at Government House through Britain's rapid [[FalklandsTaskForceMobilization|task-force mobilization]], cross-party debate, allied support, and the first victory at South Georgia. It presents the decision to sail as politically and militarily contingent: [[MargaretThatcher|Margaret Thatcher]] was determined to recover the [[FalklandIslands]], but ministers and officials doubted Britain's capacity and international backing until [[HenryLeach|Henry Leach]] argued that a fleet could be ready within days.

The episode also qualifies the later image of an uncompromising “Iron Lady.” Britain secured UN Security Council Resolution 502 and help from Chile, France, and the United States while accepting an [[FalklandsCrisisDiplomacy|Al Haig compromise]] built around mutual withdrawal, interim administration, and later sovereignty talks. The source argues that Argentina's junta rejected this off-ramp because retreat after nationalist celebration would threaten the regime's legitimacy.

## Key Claims

- Hunt ordered resistance to stop when Argentine forces surrounded Government House, avoiding further bloodshed without offering a formal surrender, and promised Islanders that Britain would return.
- Thatcher immediately favored recovering the islands, but John Nott and other ministers initially doubted whether Britain could do so.
- Leach's assurance that a task force could be prepared in three days converted political resolve into a credible military option and framed inaction as a threat to Britain's international standing.
- Michael Foot's anti-fascist support for the Islanders helped move the Commons debate beyond blame for the invasion, although Labour and the wider left remained divided over force.
- Public support and dockside crowds were substantial rather than merely imposed by government, but writers and other critics remained uneasy about patriotic mobilization.
- UN Security Council Resolution 502 gave Britain diplomatic support for Argentine withdrawal, while Chile supplied discreet intelligence and logistics and France shared information about weapons it had sold Argentina.
- The Reagan administration divided between alliance with Britain and Cold War ties to Argentina; Haig's shuttle diplomacy sought a negotiated compromise.
- Thatcher told Cabinet that Britain had to accept Haig's mutual-withdrawal plan, which the source suggests might eventually have favored Argentine sovereignty, but the junta rejected it.
- The voyage south created time for diplomacy while keeping force credible; Britain's recapture of South Georgia supplied an early victory after a failed glacier landing and helicopter losses.
