# Dragon Psychology 101

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory|Revisionist History]] episode asks why the [[MetropolitanMuseumOfArt|Metropolitan Museum of Art]] responds to financial strain without treating its enormous collection as an ordinary pool of saleable assets. [[MalcolmGladwell]] connects the museum's acquisition, storage, and accounting practices to Smaug's guarded treasure, then uses the [[AndyWarholMuseum]] and [[AndyWarhol]]'s time capsules to show how association can turn mundane remnants into objects an institution fears discarding.

[[RandyFrost]]'s research supplies the psychological mechanism: possible usefulness, emotional attachment, aesthetic appreciation, and [[ObjectMediatedMemoryAndIdentity|object-mediated memory and identity]] can make disposal feel like the destruction of meaning or self. The comparison supports a qualified case for [[MuseumCollectionStewardship|mission-based museum collection stewardship]], while [[MuseumCollectionNoncapitalization|collection noncapitalization]] helps explain why holdings remain outside ordinary balance-sheet valuation and allocation.

## Key Claims

- The Met's financial distress coexisted with a collection of roughly two million objects, many inaccessible in storage, because the institution did not treat collection objects as ordinary assets available to solve operating problems.
- Opposition to capitalizing museum collections placed those holdings outside the usual balance-sheet language of valuation, exchange, and resource allocation.
- The Warhol Museum's preservation of time-capsule contents, boxes, paper clips, and personal effects shows how provenance and association can make distinctions between significant and disposable objects difficult.
- Frost distinguishes hoarding from a simple fear-based model by emphasizing anticipated usefulness, emotional connection, aesthetic sensitivity, and possessions' power to trigger autobiographical memory.
- The Smaug analogy argues that institutional accumulation can become self-justifying, but it does not by itself resolve museums' legal, ethical, scholarly, preservation, or public-trust duties.

## Key Quotes

> “Proust effect” - Frost's term in the episode for possessions' capacity to unlock vivid memories of people, events, and earlier life.

> “compulsive collector” - Simon Doonan's distinction between Warhol's indiscriminate accumulation and selective collecting.
