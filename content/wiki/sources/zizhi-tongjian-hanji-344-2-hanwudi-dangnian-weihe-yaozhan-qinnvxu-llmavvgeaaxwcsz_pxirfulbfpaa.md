# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》344-2｜汉武帝当年为何腰斩亲女婿？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the rise-and-fall arc of [[LuanDaWesternHan|栾大]]. [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] first makes the fangshi 乐通侯, gives him a large household and six seals, and marries him to [[WeiZhangPrincessWesternHan|卫长公主]], turning an unverified promise of access to immortals into rank, wealth, kinship, and conspicuous court favor.

The same promise later becomes a delivery and truthfulness test. After repeated failure to summon immortals, Luan Da says that he met his teacher during an outing, but imperial surveillance reportedly establishes a different itinerary. The episode therefore attributes his 112 BCE execution not merely to a failed technique but to continued fabrication after Wudi had begun checking the claim; [[DingYiWesternHan|丁义]], who had recommended him, is also executed.

## Key Claims

- Han Wudi grants Luan Da the title 乐通侯, a large fief and household, vehicles and goods, and a sequence of six seals within a few months.
- Wudi marries [[WeiZhangPrincessWesternHan|卫长公主]] to Luan Da and gives the marriage an exceptionally large gold gift, making imperial kinship part of the patronage package.
- The princess had previously married [[CaoXiangWesternHan|曹襄]], whom the episode describes as a military figure from the Cao Can and Pingyang Princess family line; she and Cao Xiang had a son.
- Wudi's personal visit, ceremonial bestowal of the “天道将军” seal, and gifts from officials and senior relatives publicly advertise Luan Da's access and encourage other coastal and Yan-Qi fangshi to offer immortal formulas.
- Luan Da repeatedly claims he can summon immortals but produces no visible result, gradually exhausting the emperor's patience and trust.
- In 112 BCE he leaves under the stated purpose of a coastal Taiyi prayer, goes instead to Taishan according to the episode, and returns claiming that he met his teacher.
- Wudi has already sent someone to observe Luan Da's movements, so the conflict becomes a falsifiable report to the ruler rather than an indefinitely postponed miracle.
- Luan Da is executed by waist-cutting for false claims and ineffective techniques; Ding Yi, who recommended him, is publicly beheaded.

## Key Quotes

> “妄言见到老师，方术多不奏效” - the episode's compressed basis for Luan Da's fall after imperial verification.

> “在刀尖上跳舞” - the host's description of continuing to fabricate while a coercive patron's suspicion grows.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source expands the compact Luan Da account in the earlier fangshu survey into a patronage, verification, and punishment sequence.
- Name normalization: the supplied file consistently writes “鸾大” and “曹湘.” The wiki uses the established historical forms [[LuanDaWesternHan|栾大]] and [[CaoXiangWesternHan|曹襄]], while preserving the supplied spellings here as transcript or editorial variants rather than treating them as separate people.
- The source is a short structured summary rather than a verbatim transcript or primary-source dossier. The fief and household totals, six-seal sequence, gold amount, marriage chronology, exact itinerary, surveillance mechanics, wording of the report, execution procedure, and Ding Yi's legal liability remain episode-attributed.
- The episode calls Cao Xiang a grandson of Cao Can and gives military and family details, but this note does not independently settle the exact genealogy, office, campaign record, age at death, or the identity and later life of the reported son “曹宗.”
- Wudi's rewards plausibly create an imitation incentive, but the source does not inventory the later claimants or prove that every new fangshi entered court solely because of Luan Da's example.
