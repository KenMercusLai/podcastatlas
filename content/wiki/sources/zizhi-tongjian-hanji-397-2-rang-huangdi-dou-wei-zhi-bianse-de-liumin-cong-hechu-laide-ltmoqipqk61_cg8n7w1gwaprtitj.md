# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》397-2｜让皇帝都为之变色的流民，从何处来的？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the late-[[HanWudi|汉武帝]] crisis behind the early [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]] regency sequence. It links large-scale Guandong displacement, rebellion, and public exhaustion to Han Wudi's costly wars, luxury, and heavy fiscal policies, then follows the shift through the Luntai self-criticism edict, [[DuYannianWesternHan|杜延年]]'s advice to [[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]], and the 81 BCE Salt and Iron Conference.

The durable synthesis is that early Han Zhao policy is presented as limited correction rather than total repudiation. Huo Guang accepts Du Yannian's call to revive a frugal, lenient Wen-era line and uses the Salt and Iron Conference to criticize [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]]'s Han Wudi-era fiscal program, but the settlement abolishes only the liquor monopoly and some local iron offices while leaving salt, iron, and junshu-style state operations largely intact.

## Key Claims

- Han Wudi's late policies produce severe social consequences: the episode cites two million Guandong displaced people, another four hundred thousand unnamed or unregistered people, rebellion, and broad disorder.
- The Luntai self-criticism edict marks an emergency policy turn in which Han Wudi acknowledges error and begins retreating from the most costly direction.
- Du Yannian advises Huo Guang that displaced people have not all returned home and that the government should revive Emperor Wen's frugality, lenience, alignment with heaven, and attention to popular feeling.
- Huo Guang accepts Du Yannian's advice and convenes the Salt and Iron Conference in 81 BCE under Han Zhao's name, with [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]] and Sang Hongyang summoning more than sixty worthy and literary scholars.
- The meeting runs for nearly four months and debates the Han Wudi-era fiscal system, especially salt and iron monopoly, junshu, and liquor monopoly.
- Sang Hongyang defends Han Wudi's domestic and foreign policies, while the worthy and literary scholars attack state operation as "competing with the people for profit" and accuse long-serving ministers of leaving commoners poor while insiders grow wealthy.
- Huo Guang does not fully accept either side: he uses the debate to weaken Sang Hongyang politically but keeps most state economic institutions, abolishing only liquor monopoly and some Guannei iron offices.
- The later text known as the [[SaltAndIronConference|Salt and Iron Debate / 盐铁会议]] is said to derive from records later polished into the sixty-chapter Yantielun.

## Key Quotes

> "关东流民二百万口，无名数者四十万" - the population-displacement figure highlighted in the episode.

> "民所疾苦、教化之要" - the episode's compact framing of the Salt and Iron Conference agenda.
