# Introducing Revisionist History Season Four

## Summary

This short [[RevisionistHistory|Revisionist History]] trailer has [[MalcolmGladwell]] preview season four through rapid shifts among tea tasting, heavy-metal music theory, law-school admissions, pronunciation, Rome, Jesuits, chess masters, organized crime, and lawyers. Gladwell explicitly declines to give the season one unifying theme, presenting its geographical and intellectual range as the organizing promise instead.

The montage points toward later full episodes without developing their evidence or conclusions. [[tempest-in-a-teacup-tempest-in-a-teacup|Tempest in a Teacup]] supplies the colonial-tea and Mafia argument, [[in-a-metal-mood-in-a-metal-mood|In a Metal Mood]] develops the heavy-metal fragment, [[descend-into-the-particular-descend-into-the-particular|Descend into the Particular]] and [[dr-rock-s-taxonomy-dr-rock-s-taxonomy|Dr. Rock's Taxonomy]] develop the Jesuit and casuist material, [[chutzpah-vs-chutzpah-chutzpah-vs-chutzpah|Chutzpah vs. Chutzpah]] provides the language and organized-crime context, and [[puzzle-rush-puzzle-rush|Puzzle Rush]] examines the timed law-school test and chess comparison.

## Key Claims

- Season four was announced to premiere on June 20, 2019.
- Gladwell presents the season as deliberately eclectic rather than organized around one stated theme.
- A tea tasting moves through forest-leaf, earthy, roasted-carrot, and “tire fire” notes, illustrating both sensory description and the unusual situations created by podcast reporting.
- A music-theory exchange identifies the flattened fifth, or “devil's interval,” as foundational to heavy metal.
- The preview names Rome, Jesuits, chess masters, mafiosi, and lawyers as subjects spanning the season.
- Gladwell says he took the law-school admissions test for listeners, while a featured exchange begins by eliminating the two least plausible answers.
- A pronunciation demonstration contrasts an “L” sound made with a flat tongue in Middle Eastern languages with the speaker's familiar pronunciation, ending in comic disagreement about whether the distinction is audible.
