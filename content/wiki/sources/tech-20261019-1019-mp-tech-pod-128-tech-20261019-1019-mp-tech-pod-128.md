## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[DavidBrancaccio]] compare [[ChatGPT]], [[Claude]], and [[Gemini]] while researching a current California ballot proposition. The exercise adds [[AIAssistedElectionResearch]] as a voter workflow built around precise temporal and jurisdictional prompts, cross-model comparison, source inspection, and awareness that agreeable answers can still be wrong or ideologically slanted.

The episode also has Cornell researcher [[DavidRand]] describe a 2024 experiment in which roughly six-minute conversations with a purpose-built chatbot shifted some participants' presidential preferences in both directions. That result motivates [[ConversationalAIPoliticalPersuasion]], but the short episode does not establish the study's methodology, durability, population-level effect, or equivalence between the research system and general-purpose commercial chatbots.

## Key Claims
- Generative AI now intersects elections as a policy issue, source of campaign funding, advertising tool, voter-research aid, and possible persuasion channel.
- Brancaccio's initial query caused [[ChatGPT]] to confuse a current California Proposition 4 with a 2024 measure and incorrectly say that he could not vote on it.
- Adding the exact proposition number, election date, year, and jurisdiction produced more relevant answers, showing that election research is unusually sensitive to temporal and geographic context.
- The episode cites a Scottish case in which a chatbot invented a nonexistent election candidate, extending [[AIHallucination]] into civic information.
- Useful citations still require inspection: official or established sources such as the California Secretary of State and Ballotpedia can appear beside outlets whose ideological positioning needs context.
- Comparing multiple chatbots can reveal disagreements worth investigating, but agreement among systems does not independently prove a claim.
- Chatbots can behave sycophantically, so users should ask for direct analysis rather than preference reinforcement and should retain independent judgment.
- [[DavidRand]] says a purpose-built chatbot moved some 2024 study participants toward either [[KamalaHarris]] or [[DonaldTrump]] after an average conversation of about six minutes.
- Rand characterizes the measured persuasion effect as roughly three to four times that of a standard political advertisement, while the episode leaves the study design and durability unexamined.

## Key Quotes
> "people pleasers" - Brancaccio's warning that chatbots may reinforce what users appear to want to hear.

> "three or four times" - Rand's comparison between the study chatbot's measured effect and a standard political advertisement.
