# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》212｜贾谊《论积贮疏》与重农主张（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[JiaYi|贾谊]]'s [[LunJiZhuShu|《论积贮疏》]] as an argument that food production and stored grain are prerequisites for moral order, disaster relief, military supply, and regime security. When producers are few, consumption and luxury grow, and neither households nor the court hold reserves, drought or war can turn material scarcity into family separation, predation, rebellion, and loss of central control.

The episode says [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] responds by performing imperial plowing, renewing agrarian exhortation, and remitting half the year's land tax. It also links his repeal of slander and “妖言” offenses to open political feedback and to Jia Yi's influence, but that influence claim and the proposed earlier restoration of the offense remain source-scoped. The durable synthesis is that [[GrainReservesAsStateCapacity|grain reserves are state capacity]], not merely idle stock, while [[HeavyAgricultureLightCommercePolicy|agrarian priority]] becomes coercive when craftspeople and merchants are treated only as labor to be driven back into farming.

## Key Claims

- Jia Yi links sufficient clothing and food to the possibility of ritual, moral distinction, and public order; chronic want undermines governance before punishment begins.
- Slow biological production makes grain and cloth difficult to replace quickly, so rising luxury and a small producer base create structural scarcity.
- A state and population without reserves are exposed to drought, crop failure, forced sale of children, military mobilization, and frontier supply shocks.
- When famine and war coincide, scarcity can escalate into robbery, cannibalism, local armed power, and rebellion where central orders are already weak.
- Stored grain supports defense, attack, relief, and frontier diplomacy, making reserve depth a common foundation for civil and military policy.
- The proposed remedy prioritizes farming and seeks to move people out of crafts, commerce, and other “secondary” livelihoods into cultivation.
- Liu Heng reportedly answers with imperial plowing and a half-remission of land tax, while separately repealing speech offenses that obstruct criticism and advice.

## Key Quotes

> “积贮者，天下之大命也。” - the episode's compact statement that reserves are a vital condition of the realm.

> “夫积贮者，天下之大命也，苟粟多而财有余，何为而不成？” - the reported link from surplus grain and resources to governing capability.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is adopted. The source supplies an earlier diagnosis that complements Chao Cuo's later incentive and granary program rather than duplicating its mechanism.
- Policy qualification: the episode presents farming as the productive “root” and crafts and commerce as “secondary,” but existing wiki evidence also treats merchants as transport, storage, and exchange infrastructure. Agrarian security does not by itself establish that suppressing every nonfarm occupation improves food supply.
- Causal qualification: Liu Heng's imperial plowing and tax remission follow the memorial in the episode, but sequence and emotional response do not establish that Jia Yi alone caused either policy.
- Speech-law qualification: the episode says the slander and “妖言” offenses blocked upward information and attributes their repeal substantially to Jia Yi. It also infers that an offense previously repealed under Lü Zhi must have been restored, perhaps to suppress opposition to Lü-family enfeoffment; the restoration, motive, and legal continuity are not established here.
- Source-scope caution: quotations, chronology, famine and war scenarios, social outcomes, enfeoffments, the籍田 rite, exact tax remission, statutory categories, and policy effects require comparison with 《论积贮疏》 and transmitted histories.
