# 490. Hundred Years' War: England Triumphant (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V]] from the aftermath of the [[BattleOfAgincourt|Battle of Agincourt]] through the conquest of [[Normandy]], the French factional crisis, and the [[TreatyOfTroyes|Treaty of Troyes]]. It argues that Agincourt joined tactical success to divine and national myth, strengthening [[LancastrianLegitimacy|Lancastrian legitimacy]] while destroying a large part of the French military and governing elite.

Henry then exploited the [[ArmagnacBurgundianCivilWar|Armagnac-Burgundian civil war]], especially the murder of [[JohnTheFearless|John the Fearless]], to secure an alliance with [[PhilipTheGood|Philip the Good]]. The 1420 settlement made Henry regent and heir to [[CharlesVIOfFrance|Charles VI]], disinherited [[CharlesVIIOfFrance|the Dauphin Charles]], and attempted an [[AngloFrenchDualMonarchy|Anglo-French dual monarchy]], but Henry's 1422 death left the arrangement to a nine-month-old heir.

## Key Claims

- Agincourt killed much of the French high command and local Picard elite, producing administrative as well as military damage.
- Henry understood the victory as divine confirmation, but the source also emphasizes battlefield killing, looting, captivity, grief, and shame.
- Agincourt strengthened Lancastrian rule at home and became a more powerful English national myth than earlier victories such as Crécy or Poitiers.
- Diplomatic support, parliamentary taxation, naval security, Burgundian pressure, and French factional division enabled Henry's 1417 return to war.
- The conquest of Normandy, especially Rouen, gave Henry territorial control and strategic access along the Seine rather than merely a symbolic claim.
- John the Fearless's murder at Montereau drove Philip the Good toward England and made the Treaty of Troyes politically possible.
- The treaty preserved separate kingdoms under one crown, but resistance by Charles VII's party and Henry's early death exposed its structural fragility.
- The episode qualifies an "overrated conqueror" judgment by emphasizing Henry's military, diplomatic, administrative, and linguistic statecraft alongside the war's enormous costs.

## Key Quotes

> "God has granted him victory" - the episode's summary of Henry's religious interpretation of Agincourt.

> "separate kingdoms under one crown" - the intended constitutional form of Henry's Anglo-French settlement.

> "blood, money, and suffering" - the costs retained in the episode's final reassessment of Henry.
