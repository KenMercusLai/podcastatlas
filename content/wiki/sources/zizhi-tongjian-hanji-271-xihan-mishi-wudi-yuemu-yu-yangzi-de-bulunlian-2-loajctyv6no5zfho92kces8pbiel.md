# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》271｜西汉秘史：武帝岳母与养子的不伦恋（2）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[DongYanWesternHan|董偃]]'s rise under [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]] and [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], then turns to [[DongfangShuo|东方朔]]'s attempt to contain it. Dongfang Shuo presents Dong Yan's relationship, its claimed effect on sexual and marital norms, and his role in imperial luxury as three capital offenses, but his operational argument is narrower: the main hall of Weiyang Palace is a state space and should not admit a private favorite for entertainment.

Wudi moves the banquet to the North Palace rather than abandoning all contact, and the episode says his favor toward Dong Yan subsequently declines. The final section reports Dong Yan's early death, Liu Piao's death in 116 BCE, her request to be buried with him at Baling, and Wudi's approval. Those death, diagnosis, feeling, estrangement, and burial details come through a dramatic popular-history summary and are not independently verified here.

## Key Claims

- After the audience described in Hanji 271-1, Dong Yan is presented as an imperial entertainment companion in riding, cockfighting, ball play, dog racing, and horse racing rather than as a regular official.
- Dongfang Shuo blocks Dong Yan's entry to a banquet in Weiyang Palace's main hall by framing the relationship, alleged damage to sexual and marital norms, and encouragement of luxury as three capital offenses.
- When Wudi answers that the banquet is already prepared, Dongfang Shuo distinguishes a state ceremonial venue from private recreation and argues that an unrelated favorite should not enter the former.
- Wudi relocates the banquet to the North Palace and rewards Dongfang Shuo; the source then says imperial favor toward Dong Yan fades.
- The historical examples invoked to connect favorites with political disorder are heavily compressed and textually unstable in the supplied summary, so their names and exact application are not promoted as settled fact.
- The episode attributes Dong Yan's death to severe depression after losing favor and Liu Piao's later death to grief; these are narrative and medical inferences rather than established diagnoses or causal findings.
- Liu Piao is said to request burial with Dong Yan rather than her husband, and Wudi is said to permit burial at Baling; exact date, family estrangement, motive, and burial arrangement remain source-scoped.

## Key Quotes

> “与国事无关的人不能进入。” - the episode's rendering of Dongfang Shuo's boundary for the main hall.

> “酒席已经摆下。” - Wudi's reported attempt to preserve the planned banquet before moving it elsewhere.

- [[LiuPiaoWesternHan|馆陶长公主刘嫖]] - patron and companion whose reported burial request closes the episode.
- [[DongfangShuo|东方朔]] - remonstrant who turns private scandal into a venue, ritual, and governance question.
- [[CourtCeremonialSpaceBoundary|宫廷礼制空间的公私边界]] - the main hall and North Palace mark different levels of institutional recognition.
- [[CourtIntimacyAsPoliticalResource|宫廷亲密关系作为政治资源]] - shows that intimate access creates visibility but remains vulnerable to correction and loss of favor.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The source continues Hanji 271-1 from recognition to restriction, so the two notes describe different phases rather than incompatible judgments.
- The supplied document again writes “董衍”; the canonical page normalizes this to the transmitted 董偃. Its allusions rendered as 树雕、一牙、魏开芳 and 季庆复 are likely affected by transcription or retelling and are not used to establish exact identities or events.
- “三项死罪” is preserved as Dongfang Shuo's attributed rhetoric, not as proof that a formal prosecution or death sentence occurred; Wudi's actual response in the episode is banquet relocation and later cooling of favor.
- Depression, causation of both deaths, Liu Piao's feelings, Chen Jiao's fourteen-year estrangement, exact dialogue, reward weight, private-life generalizations, death date, will, and Baling burial require primary-source and historiographic checking.
