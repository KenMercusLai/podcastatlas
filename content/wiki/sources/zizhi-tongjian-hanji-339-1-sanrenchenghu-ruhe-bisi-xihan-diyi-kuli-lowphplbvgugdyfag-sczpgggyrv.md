# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》339-1｜三人成虎，如何逼死西汉第一酷吏？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode fills the coalition-building stage between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-338-2-yinwei-yi-chang-anmo-yinfa-de-xuean-lp8mwe-z21lhqhg4lwnj-jmeotag|Hanji 338-2]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-339-2-yi-si-sharen-kuli-zhangtang-shi-ruhe-zuodao-de-luq3jaa9iruhiqiqlduewbj-luca|Hanji 339-2]]. As [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] continues trying to turn the funerary-park theft into a knowing-nonreporting case against Chancellor [[ZhuangQingdiWesternHan|庄青翟]], [[ZhuMaichenWesternHan|朱买臣]], [[WangChaoWesternHan|王朝]], and [[BianTongWesternHan|边通]] join the threatened chancellor in an active counter-coalition.

The episode presents that coalition as the product of both self-protection and stored resentment. The three clerks have allegedly endured repeated humiliation from Zhang, while Zhu also blames him for insisting on the execution of his benefactor [[YanZhuWesternHan|严助]]. Zhu then selects merchant [[TianXinWesternHan|田信]] as the bridge for an accusation that Zhang leaked policy information and enabled private profit. The supplied transcript ends before the accusation is fully stated, so it does not independently establish bribery, adjudicated guilt, or the death promised by the title.

## Key Claims

- Zhang Tang knows Zhuang Qingdi bears limited responsibility for the funerary-park theft but continues seeking evidence that the chancellor knew and failed to report it.
- Zhu Maichen, Wang Chao, and Bian Tong decide to help Zhuang because they resent Zhang and believe his success against the chancellor would place them in danger next.
- The episode says Zhang's rapid promotion to御史大夫 was followed by repeated command, humiliation, and status disrespect toward the three former senior officials.
- Zhu's hostility is given an additional personal basis: Yan Zhu had helped him enter court service, and Zhang later insisted on Yan's execution after the Huainan case despite imperial reluctance.
- Zhu chooses Tian Xin, a merchant described as profiting around state controls and friendly with Zhang, as the evidentiary bridge for a charge of policy leakage and official-commercial collusion.
- The episode complicates Zhang's cruel-official image by also describing him as talented, financially clean, sexually restrained, and independent of a private faction.
- The transcript breaks off while the accusation is still being listed and cannot confirm its complete content, proof, outcome, or direct causal path to Zhang's suicide.
