# 30.孟子：战国愤青的赤子之心

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode rereads [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] as a forceful Warring States thinker rather than a stale school-text representative of obedience. It places [[MenciusText|《孟子》]] inside [[WarringStatesPeriod|战国时期]] competition among mobile states, talent markets, [[JixiaAcademy|稷下学宫]], and rival teachings such as [[Mozi|墨子]] and [[YangZhu|杨朱]], then follows Mencius's conversations with [[LiangHuiWang|梁惠王]] and [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] to show his politics of renyi, benevolent government, ruler accountability, and people-based legitimacy.

## Key Claims
- The episode argues that early Confucianism, especially Mencius, should not be collapsed into later obedience-centered imperial Confucianism; it contains [[EarlyConfucianResistancePolitics|early Confucian resistance politics]] around dignity, remonstrance, and refusing unrighteous rulers.
- Mencius is framed as a "golden-sentence" thinker whose familiar sayings are culturally widespread, but the episode tries to recover the political force behind phrases such as "民为贵，社稷次之，君为轻."
- The [[WarringStatesPeriod|Warring States]] background matters because collapsed ritual order, interstate competition, population mobility, and scholar mobility made moral politics practically relevant rather than merely idealistic.
- [[JixiaAcademy|稷下学宫]] is treated as a formative intellectual environment where Mencius could encounter multiple schools before actively choosing and amplifying the Confucian renyi path from [[Confucius|孔子]].
- Against rulers who ask first about profit, Mencius develops [[RighteousnessOverProfit|righteousness over profit]]: political order decays when ruler, ministers, and common people all make private gain the supreme criterion.
- In the [[LiangHuiWang|梁惠王]] sections, the episode reads Mencius's responses as a critique of partial welfare gestures; disaster relief and water works are not enough if rule still lets people starve.
- In the [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] sections, "以羊易牛" becomes a case for finding real but limited compassion and expanding it into [[MencianBenevolentGovernment|Mencian benevolent government]].
- [[WangdaoVsBadao|王道 versus 霸道]] names the episode's contrast between rule by humane legitimacy and rule by hegemonic force, but the episode also notes that Mencius still imagines conquest and unification.
- [[ConstantPropertyConstantHeart|有恒产者有恒心]] is presented as Mencius's recognition that stable livelihood and personal property underwrite moral steadiness and productive life.
- [[MencianPeopleBasedLegitimacy]] links "民贵君轻," "暴君非君," and "天视自我民视，天听自我民听": the ruler's legitimacy depends on the people rather than on private possession of the realm.
- [[MencianIdealismLimits]] appears in the episode's discussion of Qi's failed campaign against Yan: benevolent intention, if not backed by institutions, discipline, and realistic constraints, can become destructive.

## Key Quotes
> "民为贵，社稷次之，君为轻" - the episode's central shorthand for Mencian limits on ruler supremacy.

> "何必曰利？亦有仁义而已矣" - Mencius's answer to Liang Hui Wang, used to frame the renyi-versus-profit dispute.

> "君视臣如土芥，则臣视君如寇仇" - the episode's key example of Mencius's insistence on mutual dignity between ruler and minister.

> "闻诛一夫纣矣，未闻弑君也" - the source's strongest example of the claim that a tyrant who destroys renyi loses ruler status.

- No direct contradiction found.
