# The Alabama Murders - Part 7: The Second Warrant

## Summary

The final episode of [[TheAlabamaMurders]] follows [[KennethSmith]] from the trauma of Alabama's failed lethal-injection attempt through a second warrant and the first judicial execution by nitrogen hypoxia. Court testimony reported by the episode shows that the state reused much of the earlier execution setting and team while relying on an industrial mask, limited relevant expertise, no medical role in protocol development, and incomplete planning for vomiting, leakage, seizures, or prolonged consciousness.

The episode contrasts animal research, expert warnings, family and witness observations, and official claims that the execution proceeded as expected. It presents that conflict as a case of [[NitrogenHypoxiaExecutionRisk]] and [[MedicalizedExecutionOpacity]], while closing on the continuing grief of [[ElizabethSennett]]'s family, Smith's family, his lawyer [[RobertGrass]], and psychologist [[KatePorterfield]].

## Key Claims

- Alabama selected nitrogen hypoxia after failing to establish intravenous access during its first attempt to execute Smith; the method had not previously been used in a judicial execution.
- [[KatePorterfield]] testified that the first attempt caused Smith severe post-traumatic stress and that a second warrant, isolation, masking, and reuse of the setting would aggravate it.
- [[JoelZivot]] explained that leakage, vomiting, breath-holding, rising carbon dioxide, seizures, and delayed unconsciousness complicated the state's painless and rapid model.
- The episode reports that a rat study found slower unconsciousness, seizures, stress, and lung damage under nitrogen and rejected it as a humane euthanasia alternative.
- Corrections commissioner [[JohnQHamm]] acknowledged that medical personnel had not developed the protocol, vomiting precautions were limited, and the written protocol did not specify how to ensure the mask sealed against outside air.
- Smith's fasting period was extended in response to vomiting concerns, but the request to stop the execution failed.
- [[RobertGrass]] observed seizure-like movement and apparent gasping lasting minutes, while Hamm attributed the movements to breath-holding, involuntary action, and agonal breathing and said the procedure met expectations.
- The episode argues that calling the execution historic converted a long sequence of preventable institutional failures into an official success narrative.

- The episode preserves a direct interpretive conflict: witnesses describe minutes of violent movement and apparent gasping, while Hamm says breath-holding, involuntary movement, and agonal breathing were expected and that the protocol succeeded.
