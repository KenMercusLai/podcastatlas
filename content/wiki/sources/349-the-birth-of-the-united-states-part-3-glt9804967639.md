# 349: The Birth of the United States (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[AmericanRevolution|American Revolution]] from [[ThomasPaine|Thomas Paine]]'s [[CommonSensePamphlet|Common Sense]] and the [[DeclarationOfIndependence|Declaration of Independence]] through the New York campaign, Trenton and Princeton, and the [[BattleOfSaratoga|Battle of Saratoga]]. It argues that independence joined a universal political claim to the practical need for unity and foreign recognition, while the Declaration's equality language remained in unresolved conflict with slavery and elite revolutionary leadership.

The military narrative explains how the [[ContinentalArmy|Continental Army]] survived British opportunities to destroy it, how Washington used limited victories to restore morale, and how failed British coordination at Saratoga made open French intervention more likely. French entry then transformed a colonial rebellion into [[AmericanRevolutionGlobalWar|a global imperial war]].

## Key Claims

- Paine's accessible, widely circulated pamphlet made explicit arguments for independence and republicanism while framing America as a cause with meaning for all humanity.
- The Continental Congress and local committees exercised improvised authority before independence, while the Prohibitory Act helped John Adams present separation as a response to Britain treating the colonies as enemies.
- The Declaration united the states, justified separation, and supported diplomacy; its attack on George III recast a former object of loyalty as a tyrant.
- The Declaration's equality language became a durable standard later invoked by Abraham Lincoln and Martin Luther King Jr., even though Jefferson was a slaveholder and Congress removed an anti-slavery grievance from his draft.
- British hesitation after Long Island, incompatible peace terms, and Washington's escapes prevented an early destruction of the American cause.
- Trenton and Princeton supplied morale and propaganda value when the army was shrinking and enlistments were expiring.
- British failure to coordinate Howe's and Burgoyne's 1777 movements ended in surrender at Saratoga and showed the danger of operating inland without reliable support.
- Secret French aid preceded the 1778 alliance; open French entry forced Britain to consider the Caribbean, Europe, India, Canada, and other imperial theaters as well as North America.

## Key Quotes

> "the cause of America is the cause of all mankind" - Paine's universal framing as emphasized by the episode.

> "times that try men's souls" - the phrase from Paine's *The American Crisis* used to frame the winter of 1776.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode sharpens the gap between universal equality and the slaveholding social order represented by Jefferson and other revolutionary elites.
- Claims about exact troop totals, the unprecedented scale of the British expedition, commanders' motives, missed chances to destroy Washington, British restraint, and counterfactual victory remain source-scoped.
