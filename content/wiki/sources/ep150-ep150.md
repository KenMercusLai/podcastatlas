# 150 ✪ alo入华姗姗来迟；凯乐石、之禾持续提价

## Summary

This [[FengTouQuan|疯投圈]] episode compares three premium-brand paths: [[AloYoga|Alo Yoga]] sells a celebrity-led Los Angeles lifestyle, [[Kailas|凯乐石]] builds professional outdoor credibility, and [[Icicle|之禾 / ICICLE]] joins natural materials, integrated manufacturing, and restrained Chinese aesthetics. Its central argument is that weak aggregate consumption does not eliminate premium demand when a brand can identify a sufficiently large [[CoreTribePositioning|core customer group]] and earn repeated purchases through product, culture, or professional proof. The episode treats long operating horizons as important but keeps reported revenue, growth, price, repurchase, and market-size figures source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- [[AloYoga|Alo Yoga]] entered mainland China through [[Tmall]] after trademark delays, overseas purchasing, and widespread lookalikes had already shaped local awareness; the source says its first minute of official sales exceeded RMB 10 million but leaves durable repeat demand unresolved.
- The hosts distinguish Alo's celebrity, fashion, and lifestyle positioning from [[Lululemon]]'s stronger emphasis on fabric, comfort, instructors, and actual exercise communities.
- Alo's higher pricing and access to China's dense apparel supply chain create a durability test: social attention can open the market, but nearby lower-priced alternatives may make product proof and repeat purchase decisive.
- [[Kailas|凯乐石]] is presented as having cut thousands of broad leisure and camping SKUs around 2020 to focus on climbing, mountaineering, and trail running, accepting a delayed payoff before faster growth became visible around 2023.
- Kailas reinforces professional positioning through technical materials, expedition and race sponsorship, practitioner-led marketing, premium-mall stores, and an outdoor-oriented internal culture rather than relying mainly on entertainment celebrities.
- Kailas also carries material qualifications: rapid price increases, lower secondhand retention than [[Arcteryx|Arc'teryx]], difficult European competition, and an early fabricated Australian-origin story that the brand later removed.
- [[Icicle|之禾 / ICICLE]] is presented as a Chinese light-luxury fashion brand whose natural materials, own manufacturing, high-end mall presence, print publishing, and understated aesthetics support premium pricing and a host-reported 68% annual repurchase rate.
- The episode argues that a small share of China's population can still form a large business. It estimates that roughly 400,000 customers spending RMB 10,000 annually could support ICICLE's reported RMB 4 billion sales scale.
- Kailas and ICICLE are both used as long-horizon cases: each operated for roughly two decades before more visible growth, making inventory, cash-flow survival, and coherent positioning prerequisites for receiving the benefit of time.
