## Summary
This episode pairs an opening source-scoped psychology segment on unmet maternal love with a late-Han political sequence from Jian'an 7-8. Its main historical arc shows [[SunQuan|孙权]] refusing [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s demand to send a brother or son to court after [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] and [[LadyWuSunJian|吴夫人]] frame the request as a hostage-backed dependency trap. It then turns to [[GuoJia|郭嘉]]'s reading of the [[YuanShao|袁绍]] succession split: if Cao Cao relaxes pressure, [[YuanTan|袁谭]] and [[YuanShang|袁尚]] will stop cooperating and fall into open conflict.

## Key Claims
- The opening relationship segment argues, source-scoped, that unmet maternal love can turn intimacy into anxious dependence, people-pleasing, and projection; the episode does not make this a durable wiki claim about the historical material.
- Cao Cao's demand that Sun Quan send a younger brother or son to court service functions in the episode as a demand for a political hostage, not as a neutral honor.
- Zhou Yu argues that Jiangdong's six commanderies, troops, grain, wealthy land, and stable population give Sun Quan enough base strength to refuse hostage-backed subordination.
- Zhou Yu's strategic point is that sending a hostage would bind Sun Quan to Cao Cao's summons and reduce his ability to preserve Jiangdong autonomy.
- Lady Wu endorses Zhou Yu's advice and tells Sun Quan to honor Zhou Yu like an elder brother, making the refusal a household-backed succession decision rather than only a military adviser's recommendation.
- Cao Cao is displeased by the refusal, but the episode says he cannot immediately break with Sun Quan while Zhou Yu and the Jiangdong leadership keep the regime stable.
- Guo Jia reads Yuan Shao's failure to name a clear successor, combined with comparable support for Yuan Tan and Yuan Shang, as a latent split: external pressure forces cooperation, while relaxed pressure lets rivalry surface.
- Cao Cao's post-Liyang withdrawal toward Xu and his feint against [[LiuBiao|刘表]] implement Guo Jia's [[PressureReleaseInfightingStrategy|撤压诱发内斗]] logic.
- Yuan Tan's request for armor and Yuan Shang's suspicion turn a pursuit plan against Cao Cao into fraternal conflict; [[GuoTu|郭图]] and [[XinPingLateHan|辛评]] deepen the split by blaming [[ShenPeiLateHan|审配]] for Yuan Tan's succession disadvantage.
- [[WangXiuLateHan|王修]] and [[GuanTongLateHan|管统]] show that Yuan Tan still has loyal counsel and followers, but he cannot convert that loyalty into reconciliation before sending [[XinPiLateHan|辛毗]] to ask Cao Cao for aid.
