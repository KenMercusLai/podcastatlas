# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1089丨身为孔子二十世孙，孔融如何触怒曹操？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens with a source-scoped portrait of awakening as calm, non-defensive, nonattached, compassionate participation in ordinary life, then uses [[KongRong|孔融]] as a negative contrast. Its historical core traces how Kong Rong's lineage prestige, eloquence, and contempt for [[CaoCao|曹操]] became repeated public provocation through the liquor-ban argument, mockery of a military campaign, and a letter about [[CaoPi|曹丕]] taking [[YuanXiLateHan|袁熙]]'s wife after the capture of [[YeLateHan|邺城]].

The episode's durable contribution is not that learning or truth-telling are themselves defects. It extends [[PrestigeCapacityGap|名望能力落差]] from failed government in Beihai to failed self-placement under concentrated coercive power: cultural status and verbal skill do not protect a critic who repeatedly attacks a ruler's competence, family conduct, and moral legitimacy. The host's conclusion that Kong Rong lacked humility and situational judgment is interpretive, while the exact incidents and motives remain source-scoped; the following [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1090-ta-ceng-maosi-cangxiong-wei-mu-kuxue-zhen-shi-buxiao-zhi-ren-ma-lg-z9lyx_y6dxbopsfrqxoyymdjj|Hanji 1090]] supplies the formal accusation and execution branch.

## Key Claims

- The opening describes an awakened person as calm, concise, non-defensive, compassionate, highly attentive in action, and relatively unattached to success or failure.
- It presents ordinary life and work as a field of practice rather than something spiritual awakening requires a person to escape.
- The historical section identifies Kong Rong as a descendant of [[Confucius|孔子]] whose learning, childhood fame, and eloquence support unusually strong confidence in confronting power.
- The host argues that Kong Rong sees Cao Cao as an opportunistic coercive minister rather than a legitimate ruler and therefore repeatedly mocks him in public.
- When Cao Cao restricts alcohol production to conserve grain, Kong Rong answers by comparing prohibition of alcohol with banning marriage because women had been blamed for fallen states.
- The episode says Kong Rong mocks Cao Cao during a campaign against a weaker opponent; the transcript's place or opponent name is corrupt, so the event is not normalized beyond that broad description.
- After Cao Pi takes the wife of Yuan Xi following the capture of Ye, Kong Rong sends Cao Cao a fabricated analogy claiming that King Wu of Zhou gave Daji to the Duke of Zhou.
- The host reads the fabricated analogy as an attack on the Cao household's sexual conduct and on Cao Cao's use of Zhou-dynasty moral imagery, not as an innocent antiquarian error.
- The closing judgment treats Kong Rong's failure as a mismatch between scholarship and political wisdom: he possesses cultural authority but neither humility nor reliable assessment of lethal power.

## Key Quotes

> “无为而无不为。” — the opening's shorthand for fully engaged action without attachment to outcomes.

> “最博学者，竟是最天真的人。” — the host's summary of Kong Rong's failure to convert learning into political judgment.

## Contradictions

- The spoken/transcribed “公元2008年” is incompatible with Jian'an 13 and is treated as an error for 208 CE.
- The transcript forms “误换,” “甄福,” “衍城,” and “袁细” are unreliable. The surrounding sequence supports a campaign reference left unresolved and a Ye/Cao Pi/Yuan Xi-wife episode, but the wiki does not treat the corrupt forms as canonical names.
- Hanji 1089 describes Kong Rong's wife and two sons being killed and assigns the children ages nine and seven; Hanji 1090 more cautiously records two children. Household composition, ages, gender, dialogue, and the exact execution sequence remain source-scoped.
- No settled contradiction with existing wiki synthesis is recorded. Hanji 1007 grounds a real governing and advice-tolerance failure, while Hanji 1090 shows that substantive flaws do not make the later prosecution or “unfilial” label politically neutral.
