# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1104丨曹操颁布求贤令，吸引了多少顶级人才？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode reconstructs [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s first twenty years as a three-stage capacity build. [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]] and [[Chenliu|陈留]] supply political shelter and a mobilization base; [[WeiZiLateHan|卫兹]] and Cao-clan or Xiahou-clan followers supply early money and organized manpower; and the later [[TuntianSystem|屯田制]] turns displaced population and land into a more durable grain and military base.

The episode then treats talent attraction as Cao Cao's decisive multiplier. His personnel field combines clan commanders such as [[CaoHong|曹洪]], [[XiahouDun|夏侯惇]], and [[CaoRen|曹仁]]; advisers such as [[XunYu|荀彧]], [[XunYou|荀攸]], [[ChenQun|陈群]], [[ZhongYao|钟繇]], [[GuoJia|郭嘉]], and [[JiaXu|贾诩]]; and commanders who move from other camps or bring followers with them, including [[ZhangLiao|张辽]], [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]], [[ZhangHeLateHan|张郃]], and [[LiDian|李典]]. The modern startup analogy is useful for distinguishing capital, organization, and talent conversion, but its exact numbers and simplified chronology remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- After leaving the court, Cao Cao's first usable capital is presented as a combination of Zhang Miao's protection, Chenliu's resources, Wei Zi's financial and military support, and organized Cao-Xiahou kin groups rather than a direct cash transfer from [[CaoSong|曹嵩]].
- Wei Zi is described as exhausting his wealth to help recruit roughly five thousand soldiers and then dying in the [[BianshuiBattleLateHan|汴水之战]] during Cao Cao's failed advance against [[DongZhuo|董卓]].
- Cao Hong contributes military resources, while Xiahou Dun and Cao Ren are presented as arriving with substantial self-organized followings; kinship lowers early recruitment and trust costs without eliminating later governance problems.
- The source compresses the Qingzhou surrender and屯田 into a 192 CE turning point that converts a large displaced population into production and military capacity, giving Cao Cao a stronger supply base than rivals who remain dependent on seizure.
- Cao Cao's personal profile is summarized through reading, military command, strategic intelligence, and persistent talent seeking, including three talent summonses that the short episode does not date or distinguish.
- The talent system is differentiated by function: Xun Yu holds the rear, Dian Wei and Xu Chu serve as guards, while Zhang Liao and Cao Ren receive independent command.
- Recruitment can operate at group scale rather than only person by person: the episode presents Li Dian as bringing thousands of clan members and retainers, making a personnel transfer simultaneously a force transfer.

## Key Quotes

> "太祖御军三十年，手不舍书。" - the episode's cited summary of Cao Cao's continued reading while commanding armies.

> "一人跳槽就可以带一支军队过来。" - the host's modern shorthand for recruitment that transfers an organized following as well as an individual.

## Contradictions

- Chronology qualification: this episode places the beginning of屯田 directly after the 192 CE Qingzhou surrender, whereas [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1008-ruhe-pingjia-caocao-de-tuntianzhi-ls8wvh9ox-pcjmt0z8w82opbha-k|Hanji 1008]] supplies the more specific 196 CE Xudu implementation through [[ZaoZhi|枣祗]] and [[RenJun|任峻]]. The wiki treats 192 as strategic and population prehistory, not as a replacement for the formal implementation account.
- Financing qualification: the episode explicitly says Cao Song did not directly give Cao Cao cash and instead supplied inherited connections, but this remains a source-level resolution of the question left open by Hanji 1103 rather than independently settled fact.
- Numerical qualification: Cao Song's alleged one-hundred-million-cash office purchase, the comparison with annual state revenue, Wei Zi's five-thousand recruits, Xiahou Dun and Cao Ren's follower counts, the size of the Qingzhou population, and Li Dian's thousands all require primary-text verification.
- Transcript qualification: “陈昱” is normalized to [[ChengYu|程昱]] in context, while the reference to “钟玉” is not promoted to a canonical actor because the short transcript does not securely identify the intended person.
- Scope qualification: the episode says Cao Cao issued three talent summonses but gives neither their texts nor dates, so it supports a broad recruitment pattern rather than a detailed history of the edicts.
