# 717. The Suez Crisis: Anarchy in the Middle East (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces the road from [[GamalAbdelNasser|Gamal Abdel Nasser]]'s nationalisation of the Suez Canal Company to the secret French-Israeli proposal that would supply Britain and France with a pretext for intervention. It places the dispute inside Egyptian anti-colonial sovereignty, Britain's postwar imperial contraction, France's war in [[Algeria]], Israel's security fears, and Cold War competition rather than treating the canal takeover as an isolated provocation.

The episode also presents [[AnthonyEden|Anthony Eden]] as a warning about [[MisappliedHistoricalAnalogy|misapplied historical analogy]]. His anti-appeasement reputation, political insecurity, poor health, and conviction that Nasser was a new Mussolini helped turn a legal nationalisation and functioning canal into a perceived test of British prestige requiring regime change.

## Key Claims

- Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal Company on 26 July 1956 after the United States, Britain, and the [[WorldBank|World Bank]] withdrew financing for the Aswan High Dam.
- The episode presents nationalisation as legal and accompanied by an offer to compensate shareholders, while Egypt continued operating the canal successfully.
- Eden and most of his cabinet recognized the weak legal basis for reversing the takeover but prepared military options that expanded from securing the canal to overthrowing Nasser.
- Britain's strained finances, obsolete Canal Zone base, shrinking empire, and dependence on canal-linked oil routes made Suez both strategically important and evidence of reduced imperial capacity.
- France treated Nasser's support for Algeria's FLN as a reason for regime change, while Israel feared Egyptian rearmament, the blockade of Eilat, and attacks from Gaza.
- Dwight D. Eisenhower opposed an Anglo-French attack because it could widen war and push Arab governments toward the Soviet Union.
- Negotiations moved toward guarantees of open navigation, but Britain and France continued secret planning and considered using an Israeli attack across Sinai as the trigger for intervention.

## Key Quotes

> “Nasser has got to be destroyed.” — Eden's position as reported in the episode.

> “The canal continued to work.” — the practical fact that weakened claims of an immediate navigation emergency.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode distinguishes the legality of nationalisation and effective Egyptian canal operation from British and French claims that strategic danger required force.
- Eden's health, personality, rivalry, and anti-appeasement memory are contributory explanations, not a diagnosis or a complete causal account of British policy.
- Speeches, private dialogue, approval figures, troop and oil shares, timing, personal motives, and claims about French, Israeli, American, or Egyptian intentions remain episode-attributed and source-scoped.
