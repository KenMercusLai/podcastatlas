## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty Carino]] interview [[JamieSiminoff]] about [[Ring]]'s new default encryption system, TAKE, short for Throw Away the Key Encryption. Siminoff says [[RingTakeEncryption|TAKE encryption]] is meant to let users encrypt Ring camera video while still using shared-user, AI, cloud, and safety features that older end-to-end encryption disabled.

The source extends the wiki's [[ConsumerCameraSurveillance]] and [[PublicSafetyPrivacyTradeoff]] branches by framing encryption as a governance response to public anxiety around AI-enabled home-camera networks. It presents TAKE as shifting practical unlock authority from [[Amazon]] or Ring to the user, while also keeping downstream limits visible: once a user shares footage with police, later handling depends on the relevant jurisdiction's law.

## Key Claims
- [[Ring]] is rolling out TAKE, short for Throw Away the Key Encryption, as a default encryption system for camera video.
- [[JamieSiminoff]] says Ring's earlier end-to-end encryption option disabled cloud-processed features, while TAKE keeps AI-enabled and security features active.
- Siminoff describes TAKE as a home-oriented variant of end-to-end encryption with multiple controllable keys rather than a one-to-one messaging-style model.
- With TAKE on by default, Siminoff says the user holds the key needed to unlock their videos.
- Ring community requests from police are described as anonymous and optional: users choose whether to respond and unlock footage.
- When asked whether footage shared with local police could later be accessed by a federal agency such as [[USImmigrationAndCustomsEnforcement|ICE]], Siminoff says Ring's requests come from local police departments and that downstream handling depends on county and state law.
- Siminoff cites Firewatch examples near Spokane and Dallas where shared video helped build more accurate fire maps.
- The episode revisits backlash to [[RingSearchParty|Ring Search Party]], which Siminoff frames as anxiety about future misuse rather than objection to finding lost dogs.
- Siminoff says [[Ring]] had announced it would look at integrating community requests with [[FlockSafety|Flock Safety]], but the work never started and was canceled before it began.
- Siminoff argues that AI-enabled safety products need privacy guardrails such as TAKE to maintain consumer trust.
