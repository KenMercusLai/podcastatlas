## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode opens with [[TomSass]] reporting from [[Afghanistan]] five years after the [[Taliban]] returned to power. It argues that violence and day-to-day street insecurity have fallen, but that [[TalibanHardlineRule]], poverty, hunger, restrictions on women and girls, and international isolation define the bargain Afghans are living under. The episode then turns to [[Colombia]] after a major earthquake, using regional inequality and weak logistics as a [[DisasterResponseStateCapacity]] case, and closes with [[AnneRowe]]'s obituary of [[NirmalPurja]] and the ethics around [[ProjectPossible]].

## Key Claims
- [[TomSass]] spent about ten days in Afghanistan, visiting Kabul, Herat and Kandahar, and describes a country where the [[Taliban]] has a tight grip on power and visible violence has fallen sharply.
- The source says Kabul feels busier and calmer than outsiders might expect, but music bans, dress codes, vice-and-virtue policing, poverty, hunger, unemployment and fear of speaking openly make that normality tightly constrained.
- Power is described as consolidating around hardliners led by [[HibatullahAkhundzada]], who is based in Kandahar and has taken control of revenue sources including mining contracts.
- The Afghanistan segment presents a material tradeoff: people have more peace in the narrow security sense, but weak livelihoods, a health-care crisis, aid cuts and [[ForcedRefugeeReturn]] from [[Iran]] and [[Pakistan]] have intensified humanitarian pressure.
- Women and girls are central to the episode's political judgment. The source says girls are banned from secondary school, women are pushed out of many workplaces and universities, and a Kabul madrasa operates as a partial [[GirlsEducationWorkaround]] for secondary-age girls.
- The source says [[Russia]] is the only country to have recognized the Taliban government, while [[India]], [[Turkey]], [[China]], Gulf states and [[Iran]] engage pragmatically on diplomacy and trade.
- Western coercion through withheld recognition, reduced aid, sanctions and frozen central-bank reserves is presented as failing to moderate the Taliban; the source argues for continued aid, engagement and sanctions reform under [[HumanitarianIsolation]].
- The Colombia segment says a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck four days before the episode, with hundreds dead, many missing and the crucial 72-hour rescue window already passed.
- The earthquake response is framed through [[DisasterInequality]]: richer areas such as Cali and the coffee region received better support, while remote Choco and Quibdo faced damaged hospitals, limited roads, landslides and armed-group pressure.
- [[AbelardoDeLaEspriella]] had been sworn in three days before the earthquake and is presented as taking personal command of the response, turning his new presidency into an early state-capacity test.
- [[NirmalPurja]] is remembered for compressing the climb of all fourteen 8,000-metre peaks into six months and six days through [[ProjectPossible]], compared with a previous record of more than seven years.
- The obituary keeps his achievement tied to [[HighAltitudeClimbingEthics]]: critics objected to bottled oxygen, fixed ropes and helicopters, while Purja argued that oxygen also let him rescue climbers who had been abandoned.
- The source says Purja died on July 30 while attempting Broad Peak, close to completing his third round of the fourteen highest mountains.

## Key Quotes
> "peace but empty pockets" - village elder summary of the security-material tradeoff near Kabul.

> "not hope for any future" - schoolgirl's bleak description of life under constrained education and opportunity.

> "six months and six days" - the source's figure for Purja's Project Possible record.
