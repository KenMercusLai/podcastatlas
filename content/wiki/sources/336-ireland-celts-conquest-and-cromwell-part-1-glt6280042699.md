# 336: Ireland: Celts, Conquest and Cromwell (Part 1)

## Summary

Recorded in Dublin's General Post Office, this [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode with [[PaulRouse]] begins from [[PatrickPearse]]'s 1916 proclamation and then reconstructs the longer relationship between [[Ireland]] and English or British power. It rejects a seamless story of “800 years” of oppression without denying conquest, religious exclusion, plantation, mass dispossession, or violence.

Its central synthesis is that modern Irish identities emerged through contingent layers: incomplete medieval conquest and cultural intermingling, the Reformation's confessional divide, land transfer and plantation, English security fears, Cromwellian war, Protestant ascendancy, revolutionary ideas, and the failed promises surrounding the [[ActOfUnion1801|Act of Union]]. Historical memory helped later actors organize those layers into rival nationalist and unionist traditions.

## Key Claims

- The 1169-1171 Anglo-Norman intervention established royal towns and baronial holdings but did not complete a uniform English conquest of Ireland.
- Medieval Ireland combined a politically fragmented Irish landscape with settlement, trade, bilingualism, and cultural exchange across the Pale; the Statutes of Kilkenny show how difficult it was to preserve a sealed English settler identity.
- [[PatrickPearse]] used deep Irish-language literature and an “800 years” historical frame to place the poorly supported 1916 rebellion inside a longer national tradition, although that tradition also contained invented romance.
- Henry VIII's claim as King of Ireland and the failed Reformation joined sovereignty, land, conquest, and Catholic identity, while fear of intervention by Spain or France made Ireland an English security problem.
- Plantation transferred land from existing owners to English and Scottish settlers; the episode estimates that roughly two thirds of Irish land changed from native to settler ownership between the 1560s and 1690s.
- The 1641 rebellion included the killing of about 4,000 Protestant men, women, and children, while embellished reports intensified the fear and confessional paranoia surrounding later Cromwellian violence.
- [[OliverCromwell]]'s 1649 campaign, especially Drogheda and Wexford, became the symbolic concentration point for a longer process of conquest, death, transportation, and Catholic dispossession.
- Williamite victory, the Boyne, the siege of Derry, penal laws, and Protestant ascendancy tied political power and property to confessional identity while supplying later unionist memory with heroic symbols.
- The [[UnitedIrishmen]] tried to unite Catholic, Protestant, and dissenter reform behind emancipation and an Irish parliament, but suppression, failed French aid, and the 1798 rebellion helped harden Catholic-nationalist and Protestant-unionist identities.
- The [[ActOfUnion1801|Act of Union]] was the British state's response to 1798: it abolished the Dublin Parliament and centralized rule at Westminster, but failure to deliver promised Catholic emancipation damaged the settlement at birth.

## Key Quotes

> “800 years” - the inherited struggle frame the episode examines rather than accepts as a complete explanation.

> “a free and independent republic” - the 1916 proclamation's claim, used as the episode's opening point for the longer history.

> “back door” - the recurring English security image for foreign Catholic intervention through Ireland.
