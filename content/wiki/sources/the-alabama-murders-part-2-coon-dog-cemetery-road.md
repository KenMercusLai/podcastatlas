# The Alabama Murders - Part 2: Coon Dog Cemetery Road

## Summary

This episode of [[TheAlabamaMurders]] reconstructs the planned March 18, 1988 assault on [[ElizabethSennett]] at her isolated home. An anonymous tip led investigators to Billy Gray Williams, [[JohnForrestParker]], and [[KennethSmith]]; the episode says Williams recruited Smith, Smith recruited Parker, and the men were paid to make the attack appear to be a burglary.

Investigators also suspected [[CharlesSennett]] because of his unusually elaborate alibi trail, apparent prior knowledge of multiple attackers, failure to aid his critically injured wife despite CPR training, and reaction when Smith was named. He admitted an affair but denied involvement and died by suicide seven days after Elizabeth's murder. The episode treats shame within his religious community as a possible motive, not an established account of his thinking, and uses the case to develop [[TrueCrimeResolutionFalseClosure]]: identifying culprits does not end the trauma or the institutional consequences set in motion by a crime.

## Key Claims

- Elizabeth was beaten and stabbed after Parker and Smith gained entry by claiming that Charles had authorized them to inspect the property for hunting.
- Smith said Williams offered him $1,500 for a job involving harm, Smith recruited Parker, and an unidentified man supplied a diagram and instructions to stage a burglary.
- An anonymous caller accurately named Williams, Parker, and Smith and identified where the stolen VCR could be found; all three men were arrested and confessed.
- Investigators viewed Charles's many unusual contacts during the attack, precise account of his movements, use of “they,” and refusal to touch or aid Elizabeth as evidence of planning and prior knowledge.
- Charles admitted an affair, denied involvement, reacted visibly when Smith was named, and died by suicide one week after the murder.
- The episode proposes that fear of religious and social exposure may help explain Charles's recklessness, while acknowledging that his internal reasoning cannot be established.
- Solving the immediate crime did not restore order: Elizabeth's sons lost both parents within a week, and the case initiated a decades-long sequence of trials, sentences, executions, and wider harm.

## Key Quotes

> “That cannot be.” - Charles's reported response when told that Elizabeth was still alive.

> “They shouldn't have done her that way.” - Charles's reported statement while being driven to the hospital.

## Contradictions

- Part 2 recounts Smith's claim that Parker attacked Elizabeth while Smith staged the burglary, but [[the-alabama-murders-part-3-a-peculiar-institution]] later argues that neither man inflicted the rapidly fatal chest wounds and advances Charles as the most plausible later assailant. The accounts can overlap as to assault participation but conflict with a simple claim that Parker alone caused her death.
- Charles's alibi behavior, statements, affair, reaction to Smith's name, and suicide supported investigator suspicion but did not independently prove his role. The religious-shame explanation is explicitly interpretive.
- Confessions, witness recollections, evidence recovery, dialogue, chronology, motive, and investigative conclusions remain episode-scoped because the supplied file is a structured summary rather than a transcript, confession record, police file, trial record, medical record, or independent investigation.
