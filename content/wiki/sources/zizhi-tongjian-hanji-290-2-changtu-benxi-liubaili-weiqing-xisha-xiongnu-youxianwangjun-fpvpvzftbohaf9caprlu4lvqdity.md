# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》290-2｜长途奔袭六百里，卫青袭杀匈奴右贤王军

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues the reported 124 BCE [[GaoqueCampaignWesternHan|Gaoque campaign]] after the Xiongnu Right Wise King withdraws roughly six to seven hundred li to a northern base. Believing distance, difficult grassland terrain, and the expected exhaustion of a pursuing Han force provide security, he relaxes precautions and holds a feast.

The episode's durable addition is the reported intelligence advantage behind [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]]'s pursuit: a surrendered Xiongnu prince's subordinate serves as a guide, allowing the Han force to avoid scouts and defensive zones, approach the base, and spread into an encirclement before nightfall. The supplied text stops before combat, so it does not establish the “袭杀” promised by the title, the Right Wise King's fate, captures, casualties, or the campaign's later political effects.

## Key Claims

- The Right Wise King reportedly withdraws six to seven hundred li to his northern base and assumes that distance, terrain, weather, and Han fatigue make further pursuit unlikely.
- The episode presents his confidence as grounded in real regional power: it describes his group as a major southern Xiongnu force and an important screen for the northern royal court.
- His security judgment produces a practical vulnerability: the episode says he posts no warning force while his followers feast and drink.
- A subordinate of a surrendered Xiongnu prince reportedly guides Wei Qing, knows the base's location, and provides a route around scouts and guarded areas.
- Han reconnaissance reportedly confirms the camp's relaxed condition, after which Wei Qing disperses his troops around it and waits for darkness.
- The narrative labels the operation a decapitation strike, but the supplied segment ends at deployment and does not narrate the strike or its result.

## Key Quotes

> “卫青是有备而来。” - the episode's concise explanation for why distance alone does not protect the camp.

> “静等夜幕降临。” - the closing boundary, after encirclement but before combat.
