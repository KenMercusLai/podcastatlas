# The Story of Stories

## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode has psychologist [[TaniaLombrozo]] explain why people turn observations into causal models and why the resulting feeling of coherence can support discovery while also outrunning evidence. [[ExplanationDrivenSensemaking]], [[SelfExplanationKnowledgeCalibration]], and [[ExplanatoryOverreach]] connect everyday learning to political polarization, stereotypes, jury narratives, pattern perception, and conspiracy belief. In the second half, psychologist [[JonathanRottenberg]] presents [[DepressionMoodSystemFrame]] as a contextual alternative to a simple defect story while preserving the severity, treatment, and suicide-risk boundaries of clinical depression.

## Key Claims
- [[ExplanationDrivenSensemaking]] turns observations into models that support prediction, response selection, learning, and cultural transmission.
- [[SelfExplanationKnowledgeCalibration]] can reorganize knowledge and expose the [[IllusionOfExplanatoryDepth|illusion of explanatory depth]] when a familiar mechanism proves difficult to explain precisely.
- [[ExplanatoryOverreach]] arises when simplicity, breadth, emotional satisfaction, identity protection, or narrative order is mistaken for evidential strength.
- Jury judgments, stereotypes, political beliefs, and [[ConspiracyTheoryPatternSeeking|conspiracy theories]] can be shaped by whichever story makes evidence feel coherent, particularly under uncertainty or low control.
- [[ConsiderTheOppositeBiasCorrection|consider-the-opposite correction]], detailed explanation, and asking “How do I know?” help reveal unsupported confidence.
- [[DepressionMoodSystemFrame]] treats mood as an interacting response to biology, environment, relationships, temperament, behavior, and perceived life problems without claiming that severe depression is useful or morally instructive.
- Medication, psychotherapy, activity, social support, self-understanding, time, and professional care are presented as potentially complementary.
