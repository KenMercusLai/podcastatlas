# 350: The Triumph of George Washington (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode closes an American Revolution series with historian [[AdamIPSmith|Adam I. P. Smith]], following the war from [[ValleyForge|Valley Forge]] through the southern campaign, [[Yorktown]], and the [[TreatyOfParis1783|Treaty of Paris]]. It argues that American victory depended on the [[ContinentalArmy|Continental Army]] surviving long enough for French and Spanish intervention to turn a colonial rebellion into [[AmericanRevolutionGlobalWar|a global imperial war]].

The episode also treats victory as morally and politically uneven. Independence widened participation for many ordinary white men and stimulated some northern and British abolitionism, but it reduced imperial restraints on settler expansion, left women with little formal gain, and exposed Black Loyalists and other formerly enslaved people to re-enslavement. [[GeorgeWashington|George Washington]] emerges as both the stabilizing republican founder and a slaveholder whose conduct after Yorktown sharpens the founding contradiction behind [[ContestedAmericanFoundingMyth|contested American founding myth]].

## Key Claims

- Restoring the pre-1775 imperial settlement became increasingly unrealistic, while French entry in 1778, later Spanish intervention, and wider maritime pressure stretched Britain across North America, the Caribbean, Gibraltar, and home defense.
- [[ValleyForge|Valley Forge]] mattered because the army could plausibly have dispersed there; women including [[MarthaWashington|Martha Washington]] and household networks helped sustain the war effort as well as soldiers and officers.
- Britain prolonged the war because independence for all thirteen colonies was a huge concession, British forces still won major battles, and the southern strategy appeared to offer Loyalist support and valuable territory.
- [[Yorktown]] was decisive because Washington's army and French land and naval power trapped Cornwallis, making surrender the culmination of coalition warfare rather than a purely American battlefield victory.
- The Revolution redistributed liberty unevenly: poor white men gained political power, limited female enfranchisement briefly appeared in New Jersey, Native nations lost British restraints on settlement, and Black Loyalists faced severe postwar vulnerability.
- Washington's voluntary return to private life and later presidency supported [[RepublicanRestraintLeadership|republican restraint leadership]], but his order to return escaped enslaved people after Yorktown deepened [[RevolutionaryMilitarySlaveryContradiction|the military slavery contradiction]].
- The weak [[ArticlesOfConfederation]] and subsequent Constitution created an enduring dispute over national capacity, state autonomy, and whether an elected executive might reproduce monarchical power.
- The argument between 1776 and the 1619 Project is best treated as a struggle over emphasis rather than a choice between liberty and slavery: a durable national story must include republican possibility, enslavement, Indigenous dispossession, exclusion, and later expansion of rights.

## Key Quotes

> "remember the ladies" — Abigail Adams's appeal to John Adams, used to mark women's political claims amid limited formal gains.

> "revolution in favor of government" — Smith's description of the constitutional turn toward stronger national authority.

> "three overlapping wars" — Smith's frame for the Continental conflict, Patriot-Loyalist civil war, and global Anglo-French-Spanish struggle.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode complicates celebratory accounts by pairing expanded white male politics and abolitionist momentum with Native dispossession, limited gains for women, and the return of formerly enslaved people after Yorktown.
- Its claim that defending slavery was not the Revolution's general purpose qualifies strong 1619-centered interpretations without denying that southern slaveholders feared British emancipation tactics or that slavery was foundational to colonial and national development.
- Counterfactual claims about a peaceful British-imperial path, Britain winning with different officers, or the Revolution being a mistake remain explicitly speculative.
