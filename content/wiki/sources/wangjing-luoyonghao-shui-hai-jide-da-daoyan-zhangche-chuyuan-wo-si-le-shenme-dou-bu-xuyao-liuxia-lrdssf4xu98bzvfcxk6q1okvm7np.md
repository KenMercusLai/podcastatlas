## Key Claims
- [[WongJing|王晶]] presents his director identity as commercial and audience-facing: box office, ticket buying, comedy density, genre payoff, and risk control matter more to him than prestige labels.
- [[TelevisionBroadcastsLimited|TVB]] is framed as a crucial [[TVBTalentPipeline|Hong Kong film talent pipeline]], training writers, actors, comedy timing, and production speed before feeding film companies.
- [[TaiwanMarketHongKongCinema|Taiwan market demand]] helped make Hong Kong films commercially scalable by adding high-volume distribution, star premiums, television, video, and overseas revenue to local box office.
- [[HongKongFilmGangPressure]] appears as production and contract pressure rather than pure movie myth: crews faced location fees, intimidation, gun-display pressure, and entertainment-industry coercion, while police attention constrained extreme violence.
- [[HongKongFilmMarketDecline]] is attributed mainly to the loss of the Taiwan base, piracy, cable and theater short-termism, fewer usable locations, and changing genre constraints rather than only Cantonese cultural decline.
- Wang's later mainland work, web-film period, short video, and AI comments show [[HongKongFilmIndustry]] adapting through northbound production, platform content, self-media, and AI experimentation.
- His AI view reinforces [[ActorAIPerformanceBoundary]]: AI may replace much routine audiovisual work, but he says subtle real feeling and live human performance cannot be fully substituted.
