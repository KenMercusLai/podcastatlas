# 495. Evita: The Rise of General Perón (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[JuanPeron|Juan Perón]] from the 1943 military coup through his strategic construction of a labor base, his relationship and marriage with [[EvaPeron|Eva Duarte]], and the mass mobilization of 17 October 1945. It presents [[Peronism]] less as a fixed doctrine than as a flexible combination of labor benefits, nationalism, spectacle, sentiment, personal leadership, and selective borrowing from European corporatism and fascism.

The episode also separates later political memory from contemporary evidence. Although Evita's acting, class background, and growing belief in Perón's message prepared her to become his public intermediary, the hosts find no evidence that she organized the October crowds that forced his release; her central place in that founding story was constructed retrospectively.

## Key Claims

- Perón turned the marginal Department of Labour into a mass-power base by organizing unions, cultivating leaders, and delivering benefits to underrepresented urban workers.
- His political model borrowed order, spectacle, corporatism, and leader-centered imagery from fascist Europe without fitting neatly into a Nazi or Francoist identity.
- Peronism operated as an adaptable emotional and theatrical style as well as a policy coalition, allowing later left- and right-wing currents to claim the same tradition.
- Eva entered Perón's world first as actress and partner, not as an established political operator, while her radio training and working-class identification prepared her later public role.
- Union organization and worker mobilization, rather than Eva's intervention, drove the 17 October 1945 demand for Perón's release.
- Later accounts moved Eva into the center of 17 October, turning a contested chronology into a founding myth of her bond with the descamisados.
- Eva's marriage, falsified civil details, and destruction of acting publicity marked a deliberate transition from Eva Duarte to the public identity of Evita Perón.

## Key Quotes

> "soldier of the people" - the unnamed description of Perón in Eva's 1944 radio drama.

> "there was no one Perón" - the episode's summary of his political adaptability across circumstances.

## Contradictions

- The episode explicitly rejects later Peronist and anti-Peronist accounts that credit Eva with organizing the 17 October 1945 mobilization; it says she lacked the contacts and that no contemporary evidence supports the claim.
- Source-scope caution: character judgments, private motives, Eva's exact movements on 17 October, Perón's relationship history, the scale of fascist influence, and stories about altered identity records remain the hosts' qualified reconstruction rather than independently settled findings.
