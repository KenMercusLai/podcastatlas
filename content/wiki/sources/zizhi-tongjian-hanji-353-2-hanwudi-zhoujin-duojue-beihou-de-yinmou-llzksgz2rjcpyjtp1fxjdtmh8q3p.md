# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》353-2｜汉武帝酎金夺爵背后的阴谋

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places the 112 BCE [[ZhoujinForfeiture|酎金夺爵]] beside the mobilization for war against [[Nanyue|南越]]. After [[BuShiWesternHan|卜式]] offers himself, his son, and Qi sailors for the expedition, [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] rewards him publicly, but no other official or marquis volunteers; the court then uses deficient ritual gold to deprive 106 marquises of title. The episode interprets that sequence as a political punishment for elite nonparticipation, while the surviving event sequence alone does not prove that motive.

The episode follows the purge into [[ZhaoZhouWesternHan|赵周]]'s imprisonment and suicide, [[ShiQingWesternHan|石庆]]'s appointment as chancellor, and a court in which major fiscal, legal, and literary-policy officials operate without decisions passing through the chancellor. It then records a large Western Qiang rebellion, Han suppression under Li Xi and Xu Ziwei, and [[YangPuWesternHan|杨仆]]'s advance into Nanyue, linking internal elite discipline to simultaneous southern and northwestern military pressure.

## Key Claims

- Bu Shi asks to join the Nanyue campaign with his son and Qi sailors; Wudi rewards him with guannei-hou rank, gold, and land and publicizes the example.
- No other official or marquis reportedly volunteers, after which ritual-gold inspection produces 106 title forfeitures for insufficient weight or poor purity.
- The episode argues that deficient gold is the legal occasion, while refusal to share the Nanyue campaign burden is the political reason for the mass punishment.
- Chancellor Zhao Zhou is imprisoned for knowing that the marquises' ritual gold was light and dies by suicide.
- Shi Qing succeeds him, but fiscal extraction, harsh law enforcement, and literary policy are handled by officials whose work no longer depends on chancellor decision, leaving Shi cautious but institutionally weak.
- Western Qiang forces reportedly rebel in great numbers, communicate with the Xiongnu, and are defeated the next year by a ten-wan-person Han force under Li Xi and Xu Ziwei.
- Yang Pu's route becomes the first to enter and advance in Nanyue, but his waterborne command supports transport and territorial assault rather than a modern sea-control mission.

## Key Quotes

> “天下莫应。” - the compact report that Bu Shi's publicized example draws no wider response.

> “夺爵者百六人。” - the scale of title forfeiture attributed to the ritual-gold inspection.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki is adopted. The episode adds an internal-political layer between Hanji 353-1's deployment and Hanji 354's conquest account.
- The causal claim that Wudi used the ritual-gold defects mainly to punish refusal to volunteer for Nanyue is an episode-level inference; the sequence establishes public nonresponse followed by inspection and forfeiture, not the emperor's private motive.
- The supplied note places Yang Pu's first arrival in Nanyue in 112 BCE. The received *Zizhi Tongjian* sequence places mobilization in 112 BCE and his entry and advance in 111 BCE, so the exact chronology remains qualified.
- Transcript forms 卜氏, 赵州, 桑洪阳, 王文淑, 尔宽, 李熙, and 杨溥 are normalized to 卜式, 赵周, 桑弘羊, 王温舒, 兒宽, 李息, and 杨仆.
- The claim that this is the Western Qiang's first appearance on the Chinese historical stage, the size and composition of the rebel and Han forces, the officials' private motives, and the modern characterization of Yang Pu's mission remain source-scoped.
