# Essentials: Understanding & Treating Addiction | Dr. Anna Lembke

## Summary
This condensed [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] and psychiatrist [[AnnaLembke]] explain addiction through tonic dopamine, reward adaptation, anticipatory craving, and a [[PleasurePainBalanceAddiction|pleasure-pain balance]] rather than pleasure or willpower alone. It restates the full interview's recovery frame: a clinically bounded abstinence period, advance planning for relapse triggers, [[RecoveryTruthTellingAndAmends|truth-telling]], protective barriers, and preserved offline connection. The episode also keeps psychedelic-assisted addiction treatment inside a [[PsychedelicClinicalSupervisionBoundary]] and treats social media as a potentially engineered behavioral-reward risk rather than declaring every use pathological.

## Key Claims
- Dopamine is presented as a tonic baseline plus changes above or below that baseline, with roles in reward, motivation, wanting, and movement rather than pleasure alone.
- [[PleasurePainBalanceAddiction]] describes repeated intense reward as recruiting a compensatory response that can leave ordinary activity less rewarding and produce anxiety, irritability, insomnia, dysphoria, craving, or preoccupation.
- Lembke presents roughly 30 days of abstinence as a clinical average for many patients to observe reward-system recovery, while saying early abstinence can feel worse and severe addiction may remain persistently vulnerable.
- Relapse can become reflexive and can follow distress, cues, positive events, or reduced vigilance; recovery planning therefore needs barriers and support before vulnerable moments arise.
- [[RecoveryTruthTellingAndAmends|Truth-telling]] is presented as a recovery practice that interrupts habitual concealment and rebuilds intimate social connection, while the proposed prefrontal and reward-circuit mechanism remains source-scoped.
- Small, short controlled psychedelic studies do not establish a quick cure or make unsupervised psilocybin, MDMA, or microdosing safe for people with addiction.
- Social-media use should be intentional and bounded, with physical and metacognitive distance from the phone protecting offline connection, sustained thought, and creative attention.
