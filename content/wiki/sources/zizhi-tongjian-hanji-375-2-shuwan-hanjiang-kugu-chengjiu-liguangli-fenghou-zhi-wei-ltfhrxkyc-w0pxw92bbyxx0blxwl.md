# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》375-2｜数万汉将枯骨，成就李广利封侯之位

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues the second [[DayuanWesternHan|大宛]] expedition after [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-374-2-hanwudi-qingjin-quanli-da-dayuan-zhi-bu-zhi-lmfzkllejz3m0vanmnu7gyxtwbul|Hanji 374-2]]. It contrasts the strategic shock of [[LiGuangliWesternHan|李广利]]'s return with the army's internal cost: the source says most losses did not come from food shortage or combat, but from commanders' greed, violence, plunder, and abuse of soldiers, yet [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] overlooked those failures and rewarded Li, officers, volunteers, pardoned convicts, and soldiers.

The aftermath extends beyond reward. Western Regions rulers send tribute and hostages, Han builds beacon infrastructure west of Dunhuang and stations tuntian forces around Luntai and Quli, [[DayuanWesternHan|大宛]] replaces the Han-backed Meicai while preserving annual horse tribute, and [[LoulanKingdomWesternHan|楼兰]] explains its double alignment as survival between Han and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]]. The source therefore qualifies the victory as a mix of coercive prestige, institutional reach, abusive command, unstable client politics, and small-state hedging rather than a costless conquest.

## Key Claims

- Western Regions states react to Dayuan's defeat by sending tribute missions and rulers' sons to Chang'an, with some remaining as hostages.
- The returning Han force has only a little over one thousand horses, while the source attributes much of the human loss to commanders' abusive treatment rather than battle or inadequate grain.
- Han Wudi makes Li Guangli Marquis of Haixi and distributes offices, pardons, and large cash rewards without holding the command structure accountable for those losses.
- Xiongnu avoids the main army but sends cavalry toward Loulan to attack envoys or cut the corridor; Ren Wen learns of the plan and reports it.
- The Loulan king says a small state between Han and Xiongnu cannot survive without answering both powers; Wudi releases him and uses him to gather Xiongnu intelligence, after which Xiongnu distrusts Loulan.
- Han consolidates the route through beacon posts from Dunhuang toward the salt marsh and small tuntian garrisons around Luntai and Quli that support and protect missions.
- Dayuan nobles later kill Meicai, install Wugua's younger brother Chanfeng, send Chanfeng's son as a hostage, and promise two heavenly horses each year.
- The episode closes by juxtaposing Mingguang Palace construction and immortality symbolism with Wudi's renewed anti-Xiongnu posture and the new chanyu's conciliatory release of detained Han envoys.

## Key Quotes

> "将吏贪，不爱卒，侵牟之，以此物故众。" - the episode's explanation for much of the army's loss.

> "小国在大国之间，不两属，无以自安。" - the Loulan king's defense of dual alignment.

## Connections

- [[LiGuangliWesternHan|李广利]] - returning commander rewarded despite the source's indictment of abusive command and severe troop loss.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler who converts the expedition into prestige, rewards, corridor infrastructure, intelligence leverage, and renewed pressure on Xiongnu.
- [[DayuanWesternHan|大宛]] - defeated polity whose nobles later remove Meicai but preserve hostage and horse-tribute relations with Han.
- [[LoulanKingdomWesternHan|楼兰]] - small state whose ruler explains double submission as a survival strategy between rival powers.
- [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - rival that avoids the returning army, seeks to disrupt the corridor, later distrusts Loulan, and then sends a conciliatory mission under a new chanyu.
- [[DecisiveResourceCommitment|决断型资源投入]] - the expedition's aftermath shows that sufficient resources can secure objectives while still concealing command abuse and disproportionate human cost.
- [[FrontierStrategyCostTypology|边疆战略成本分型]] - the victory supplies a concrete case where geopolitical reach and tactical success must be assessed against losses, rewards, and continuing garrison burdens.
- [[TuntianSystem|屯田制]] - the Luntai-Quli garrisons turn expeditionary success into route support and a continuing military-agricultural presence.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is recorded. The source deepens Hanji 374-2 by supplying the return, casualties, rewards, and political aftermath that the earlier episode did not fully account for.
- Evaluation tension: Hanji 374-2 presents decisive mobilization as strategically effective, while this sequel attributes much of the force's loss to abusive command and shows rewards granted without corresponding accountability.
- Political qualification: Meicai's later killing limits any claim that Han's installed settlement created stable internal legitimacy in Dayuan, even though the successor regime continues hostage and horse-tribute ties.
- Scope caution: casualty causes, horse totals, rewards, Ren Wen's intelligence, Loulan dialogue, corridor installations, Dayuan succession names, annual horse tribute, Mingguang Palace purpose, and the new chanyu's fear and diplomacy remain episode-attributed pending primary-source verification.
