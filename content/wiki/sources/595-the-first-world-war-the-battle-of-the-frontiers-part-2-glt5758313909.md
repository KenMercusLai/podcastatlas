# 595. The First World War: The Battle of the Frontiers (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the opening Western Front campaign from Britain's deployment of the small professional [[BritishExpeditionaryForce1914|British Expeditionary Force]] through France's disastrous [[BattleOfTheFrontiers1914|Battle of the Frontiers]], the [[BattleOfMons1914|Battle of Mons]], the [[BattleOfLeCateau1914|Battle of Le Cateau]], and the Great Retreat. It presents the campaign as a collision between offensive confidence, weak coalition information, conspicuous uniforms, cavalry culture, and industrial firepower.

The British force fought effectively at Mons and Le Cateau but could not hold against superior numbers while French armies retreated. At command level, [[SirJohnFrench|Sir John French]] moved from underestimating the German threat to considering withdrawal west of Paris, while Joseph Joffre abandoned the failed opening offensive and tried to preserve the Allied armies for renewed resistance.

## Key Claims

- France's Plan XVII attacks met German machine guns, howitzers, prepared positions, and inadequate reconnaissance; the episode reports 27,000 French soldiers killed on 22 August 1914.
- The roughly 80,000-strong BEF was small by continental standards but its professional rifle training enabled unusually fast and accurate fire at Mons.
- Poor intelligence and coalition coordination left the BEF near Mons without timely knowledge that the French Fifth Army was retreating or that a much larger German force was approaching.
- Mons and Le Cateau were withdrawals and German battlefield successes, yet British resistance helped the army escape and delayed the German timetable.
- Sir John French's shift from overconfidence to despair made senior command instability a threat to continued Anglo-French cooperation.
- The Great Retreat imposed extreme physical and organizational strain while deepening mutual British and French blame.
- The episode uses the campaign to show an [[OffensiveDoctrineFirepowerMismatch|offensive-doctrine/firepower mismatch]] without reducing every defeat to simple stupidity.

## Key Quotes

> "poor little Englishmen" - the warning attributed to French women as British troops moved toward the front.

> "washing his hands of collaboration with the French" - the episode's account of Sir John French's telegram to London during the retreat crisis.

## Connections

- [[BattleOfTheFrontiers1914]] - failed French opening offensives that forced the wider Allied retreat.
- [[BritishExpeditionaryForce1914]] - small professional British force whose tactical skill could not remove its numerical and coalition constraints.
- [[BattleOfMons1914]] and [[BattleOfLeCateau1914]] - delaying battles within the retreat.
- [[SirJohnFrench]] - BEF commander whose misjudgment and later despair threatened coalition continuity.
- [[OffensiveDoctrineFirepowerMismatch]] - interpretive pattern joining morale-centered attack to modern defensive fire.
- [[FirstBattleOfMarne]] - next campaign phase, where the retreat culminated in an Allied counterattack.
- [[WesternFrontFirstWorldWar]] - theater context for the shift from mobile campaigning toward entrenchment.
