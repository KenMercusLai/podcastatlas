# 131. Burgundy: Europe's Forgotten Superpower

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode with [[BartVanLoo|Bart van Loo]] presents [[Burgundy]] as a late-medieval composite state built between [[France]] and the [[HolyRomanEmpire|Holy Roman Empire]]. It follows [[PhilipTheBold|Philip the Bold]], [[JohnTheFearless|John the Fearless]], [[PhilipTheGood|Philip the Good]], and [[CharlesTheBold|Charles the Bold]] through marriage, Flemish wealth, French civil war, English and French alliances, court spectacle, territorial expansion, and dynastic failure.

The episode argues that Burgundy failed as an independent kingdom but endured through the [[LowCountries|Low Countries]], [[MaryOfBurgundy|Mary of Burgundy's]] Habsburg marriage, and the inheritance of [[CharlesV|Charles V]]. Its wider legacy joins state formation to [[BurgundianCourtArt|court art]], the [[OrderOfTheGoldenFleece|Order of the Golden Fleece]], and the later histories of Belgium and the Netherlands.

## Key Claims

- Philip the Bold's marriage to Margaret of Flanders joined the Duchy of Burgundy to a rich northern urban and cloth economy, giving the dynasty resources beyond its French territorial base.
- The [[ArmagnacBurgundianCivilWar|Armagnac-Burgundian conflict]], including the murders of Louis of Orléans and John the Fearless, weakened France and helped England advance during the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]].
- Philip the Good used alliance with England after Montereau, then reconciliation with France in 1435, to pursue Burgundian autonomy rather than permanent loyalty to either crown.
- Flemish wealth, artists working at Champmol, elaborate banquets, and the Golden Fleece made cultural patronage and spectacle instruments of quasi-royal power.
- Charles the Bold tried to connect Burgundy's northern and southern lands, but military overreach, defeat at Nancy, and the absence of a male heir exposed the fragility of the composite state.
- Mary of Burgundy's marriage to Maximilian of Habsburg preserved much of the northern inheritance even as the French crown recovered the original duchy.
- Burgundian institutions and identity helped shape the Low Countries, but the claim that the state could have survived with greater patience or a male heir remains counterfactual.

## Key Quotes

> "the great Duke of the Occident" - the episode's title for Philip the Good at the height of Burgundian power.

> "all states are artificial at first" - Van Loo's answer to the suggestion that Burgundy was doomed because it was an artificial state.
