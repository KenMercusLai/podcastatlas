# GameStop CEO Ryan Cohen's $56B Plan to Take Over eBay

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] episode interviews [[RyanCohen|Ryan Cohen]] about building [[Chewy]], becoming an activist investor at [[GameStop]], and pursuing a large cash-and-stock acquisition of [[EBay|eBay]]. Cohen presents a consistent operator thesis: low-margin retail rewards tiny execution advantages, customer trust, direct founder involvement, and owner-like incentives. The GameStop section reframes the company from a meme-stock case into a cost-cutting, pre-owned, and collectibles turnaround, while the eBay section argues that a physical store network plus a large online marketplace could strengthen resale, authentication, live commerce, and in-game-item liquidity. The episode is also a contested-transaction source: Cohen's claims about eBay costs, seller dissatisfaction, financing, shareholder support, and management incentives are preserved as source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- [[RyanCohen|Cohen]] says [[Chewy]] grew from a recurring-purchase pet category insight, combining neighborhood pet-store service with Amazon-like supply-chain discipline.
- Cohen describes low-margin ecommerce as a "game of pennies," where supplier terms, warehouse efficiency, labor, shipping, and purchasing scale decide whether growth is profitable.
- Cohen says Chewy retained customers through 24/7 support, handwritten holiday cards, pet portraits, fast problem resolution, and category-specific emotional trust.
- Cohen presents his post-Chewy investing framework as buying established, historically profitable businesses that are out of favor.
- Cohen says [[GameStop]] began as a passive position but became activist once he bought more than 5% and filed a 13D.
- Cohen's original GameStop thesis was that the company could survive until the next [[PlayStation]] and [[Xbox]] console cycle rather than immediately become a Chewy-like ecommerce company.
- After joining GameStop, Cohen says he first imported Chewy and Amazon-style ecommerce talent, then concluded that the playbook did not fit GameStop's physical-retail economics and inventory risks.
- Cohen says the later GameStop turnaround focused on cost cuts, internal operators, pre-owned products, and collectibles, including graded trading cards.
- The host cites GameStop metrics such as $9.7 billion in cash, $333 million in free cash flow, $835 million in Q1 revenue, 14% year-over-year growth, and collectibles at 42% of revenue; these figures remain source-scoped.
- Cohen argues that [[GameStop]] and [[EBay|eBay]] are complementary in collectibles, refurbished technology, secondhand goods, authentication, marketplace liquidity, and consumer resale.
- Cohen's eBay plan has three pillars: removing about $2 billion in costs, improving [[MarketplaceLiveCommerce|marketplace live commerce]], and building an [[InGameAssetMarketplace|in-game asset marketplace]].
- Cohen says GameStop's roughly 1,600 stores could serve as studios, fulfillment nodes, photography support, and authentication infrastructure for eBay sellers and creators.
- Cohen argues that useful in-game items such as skins and weapons differ from speculative NFTs because they have utility inside games.
- Cohen says eBay's board rejected the offer and did not meaningfully engage; he frames the issue as whether current management or an owner-operator would maximize shareholder value.
- Cohen says the proposed deal is 50% cash and 50% GameStop stock, because GameStop does not have roughly $60 billion of cash for an all-cash offer.
- Cohen criticizes eBay management and directors for limited personal ownership, while saying he would put $500 million of his own capital into the transaction and has not pulled money out of GameStop.
