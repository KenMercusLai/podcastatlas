# Unrest in peace: misery in Gaza

## Summary

This [[EconomistPodcasts]] episode examines three gaps between a promising headline and an operating system. [[NicholasPelham]] reports that the [[BoardOfPeace]] and its Gaza recovery plan have produced announcements without visible reconstruction, while border restrictions, divided territorial control, continuing attacks, material shortages, and blocked technocratic entry deepen the [[GazaDealImplementationGap]]. The second segment follows [[Suno]], [[WarnerMusicGroup]], [[UniversalMusicGroup]], and [[SonyMusic]] as lawsuits and licensing test [[LicensedGenerativeMusic]], while the final segment shows why export demand cannot quickly repair Japan's diminished tea capacity, leading [[Kagoshima]] and other producers toward a quality-and-origin strategy under [[MatchaSupplyTransition]].

## Key Claims

- The [[BoardOfPeace]] is described as charging $1 billion for membership, giving [[DonaldTrump]] chairmanship and veto power, and having no Palestinian members on its executive board.
- [[JaredKushner]] announced a $2.5 billion Gaza recovery plan, but [[NicholasPelham]] says residents see virtually no implementation after a year of announcements.
- Pelham has been unable to enter [[Gaza]] for three years and now relies on UN material, acquaintances, written reporting, and voice messages; he says meaningful independent reporting requires [[Israel]] and [[Egypt]] to admit journalists.
- Residents face bread queues, destroyed homes, tent living, continued bombing, and restrictions on heavy machinery and reconstruction material; the episode reports about 1,400 deaths since the announced ceasefire.
- Israel is described as controlling roughly two-thirds of Gaza while [[Hamas]] controls the remaining populated territory; the proposed technocratic body remains in Egypt because Israel has not allowed it to enter.
- Prolonged deprivation is reported to be weakening social protection, including accounts of orphaned children and child marriage; individual stories cannot be independently verified from the supplied episode summary.
- The episode sees little Gaza-policy difference among leading Israeli electoral candidates and describes Palestinian dissatisfaction with both Hamas and [[Fatah]], alongside fear that Mahmoud Abbas may again cancel elections.
- Deezer reportedly receives about 90,000 wholly machine-generated tracks each day, roughly half its uploads, while [[Spotify]] says it removed 75 million spam tracks in the prior year.
- [[UniversalMusicGroup]] and [[SonyMusic]] continue litigating against [[Suno]], while [[WarnerMusicGroup]] has settled; the plaintiffs reportedly allege copying of more than 60,000 protected recordings, and Suno argues that training to learn styles is fair use.
- Suno says its V6 family was trained only on licensed music from Warner and other sources; participating artists and songwriters may opt in and share subscription revenue when fans make remixes or mashups.
- No major music stars had opted in at the time discussed, so demand for fan co-creation and the effect on artistic integrity remain unproved.
- Japan's green-tea exports tripled over a decade as matcha demand rose, and [[Kagoshima]] benefited from flat mechanizable land and investment in larger-scale production.
- Japan's tea-growing area shrank by about one-third over two decades; abandoned fields, retiring farmers, specialized equipment, and a maturation period of at least five years constrain rapid conversion to matcha.
- [[China]] is described as producing about 70% of global matcha supply and overtaking [[Japan]] as the largest exporter, pushing Japanese producers toward premium taste, regional varieties, and terroir rather than volume competition.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The Gaza segment chronologically sharpens [[in-arms-way-gaza-deal-sticking-points-6a71b49647235c354d3c9b74]]: institutions that were previously underspecified are now described as still unable to enter or materially reconstruct Gaza.

- Export growth does not contradict the long decline of Japan's tea sector: the source distinguishes rising matcha demand from the slower recovery of land, labor, plants, and processing capacity.
