# 528. The Nazis' Road to War: Hitler Prepares to Strike (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the opening of the 1938 Sudeten crisis, showing how [[AdolfHitler]] used claims of persecuted German speakers to prepare the destruction of [[Czechoslovakia]] rather than a bounded minority settlement. Strategic industry, border defenses, alliances, the legacy of 1918, racial-struggle ideology, rearmament pressure, and Hitler's providential self-belief all made the state both a perceived obstacle and a prize.

The episode also complicates the familiar caricature of [[NevilleChamberlain]]. It presents him as forceful, rational, peace-minded, and confident in personal problem-solving, but imaginatively unable to grasp an opponent for whom war was central rather than a bargaining failure. The narrative ends with Chamberlain's Plan Z flight to meet Hitler, leading directly into [[529-the-nazis-road-to-war-showdown-in-munich-part-2-glt8359198113|Part 2]].

## Key Claims

- Hitler's September 1938 humanitarian and ethnic rhetoric concealed a prior decision to destroy Czechoslovakia, not merely secure autonomy for Sudeten Germans.
- Nazi foreign policy joined revenge for 1918, racial struggle, war preparation, rearmament, propaganda, and a leader increasingly convinced that providence favored his risks.
- Czechoslovakia was a resilient democracy with a trained army, fortified frontier, major industry, raw materials, and French and Soviet ties, making it a strategic obstacle as well as an economic and military prize.
- [[JoachimVonRibbentrop]] reinforced Hitler's gamble by insisting that Britain was weak and would not fight.
- [[KonradHenlein]]'s autonomy demands and Berlin-directed rejection of [[EdvardBenes|Edvard Beneš]]'s concessions kept the minority dispute available as a pretext for escalation.
- [[CaseGreen]] aimed to smash the whole Czechoslovak state rapidly; the May crisis and Hitler's perceived humiliation hardened the timetable even though Germany had not intended to attack during that specific scare.
- German public reluctance, economic strain, military concern, and [[LudwigBeck]]'s resistance show that the road to war was contested even as institutional opposition failed to stop it.
- Chamberlain's Plan Z arose from sincere fear of catastrophic war and confidence in rational personal diplomacy, but his model could not accommodate Hitler's ideological commitment to conflict and conquest.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode deepens the road-to-war sequence by showing that the Sudeten minority claim functioned inside a prior plan to eliminate Czechoslovakia.
- Sudeten German economic grievance and attraction to nationalism were real in the episode's account, but Czechoslovakia's legal equality and Beneš's rejected concessions qualify Nazi claims that invasion was a necessary humanitarian remedy.
- German anxiety, military opposition, and the accidental character of the May scare constrain a deterministic account without overturning Hitler's prior ideological and operational commitment to expansion.
