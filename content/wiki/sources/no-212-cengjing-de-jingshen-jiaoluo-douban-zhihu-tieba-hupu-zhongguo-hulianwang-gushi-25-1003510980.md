## Summary
This [[Banlatte|半拿铁]] China-internet-history episode uses [[Douban|豆瓣]], [[Zhihu|知乎]], [[BaiduTieba|百度贴吧]], and [[Hupu|虎扑]] to explain why PC-era Chinese communities became emotional "spiritual corners" while remaining hard to commercialize like ordinary internet companies. Its core synthesis is that [[ChinesePCInternetCommunities]] were built from relationships, norms, shared memory, and identity as much as from content. That relational density created durable culture, but also made advertising, paid services, algorithmic feeds, app fragmentation, and heavy platform intervention more likely to damage the community value users cared about.

## Key Claims
- PC-era communities grew after BBS and Web 1.0, when [[UserPoweredContentPlatform|user-powered content]], UGC, and person-to-person relationships became central to Chinese internet use.
- [[Douban|豆瓣]] began from books in 2005, quickly added groups, movies, music, same-city activity, musicians, and Douban FM, and reached a 2012 peak built around restrained design, ratings, recommendations, and cultural identity.
- [[Abei|阿北]]'s product idealism protected Douban from over-advertising, content-farm tactics, and aggressive algorithmic rewriting, but also contributed to missed mobile, ticketing, ecommerce, and social-product opportunities.
- Douban's choice to split into many mobile apps before a main app became a source-case for [[MobileCommunityFragmentationRisk]]: a strong PC community can lose daily-use position when mobile entry points are scattered.
- [[Zhihu|知乎]] grew through invitation, professional identity, early elite answerers, Zhihu Daily, columns, and financing, but its later knowledge-payment push exposed [[KnowledgeCommunityCreatorIncentives]] problems.
- The [[ByteDance]]-backed Wukong Q&A subsidy battle showed that money could pull creators away from Zhihu, but subsidies alone did not create a stable community atmosphere.
- Zhihu's later paid-reading, story, IP, and YanYan Stories route made it more commercially pragmatic, while also moving it away from its early knowledge-community self-image.
- [[BaiduTieba|百度贴吧]] used search keywords as community containers, making stars, schools, games, novels, interests, and obscure identities discoverable without requiring a centralized editorial taxonomy.
- Tieba's scale and openness produced powerful subcultures, including fan bars, Li Yi Bar, "屌丝", "弱智吧", and the Jia Junpeng meme, but also made governance, spam, pornography, gambling, scams, and old-post preservation harder.
- Baidu's aggressive commercial moves around ads, bar sales, KPI pressure on bar owners, and official bar-owner interventions weakened old-user trust in Tieba.
- [[Hupu|虎扑]] grew from [[ChengHang|程杭]]'s NBA translation work into a sports forum, then expanded through the Pedestrian Street, JR culture, ratings, "green literature", the Goddess Competition, and a broad "barbecue-stall" community mood.
- Hupu tried sports parks, games, esports, lottery/live-broadcasting, sports marketing, streetball, and IPO paths, but its most commercially valuable derivative route came from equipment-community demand that later became [[Dewu|得物]].
- The episode distinguishes [[CommunityVsContentPlatform|communities from content platforms]]: communities use content to build relationships and shared rules, while platforms such as short-video apps are closer to media consumption surfaces.
- The listener-comment segment on earlier phone-history episodes extends [[GenerationalTechnologyBridge]] and [[FeaturePhoneCulturalMemory]]: users were not only recalling devices but also a period when product possibility and personal futures felt open.

## Key Quotes
> "萝卜青菜各有所爱" - Douban's early slogan, used to capture taste plurality.

> "谢邀" - early Zhihu's invitation culture turned into a durable platform idiom.

> "烧烤摊文化" - Cheng Hang's source-described alternative to labeling Hupu only as a "straight male" community.

> "社区的核心是人与人的关系" - the episode's distinction between communities and content platforms.
