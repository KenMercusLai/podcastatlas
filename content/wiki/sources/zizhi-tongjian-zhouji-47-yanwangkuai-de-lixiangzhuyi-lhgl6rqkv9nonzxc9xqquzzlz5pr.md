# 《资治通鉴·周纪》47丨燕王哙的理想主义

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode pairs two 316 BCE decisions: [[QinState|秦国]] chooses whether to attack [[ShuStateWarringStates|蜀国]] or [[HanState|韩国]], while [[YanWangKuai|燕王哙]] lets [[ZiZhi|子之]] take over [[YanState|燕国]]'s governing authority. [[SimaCuo|司马错]] defeats [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s more dramatic eastward plan by arguing that Ba-Shu offers land, wealth, and lower diplomatic risk. The Yan branch is the opposite: Zi Zhi, [[SuDai|苏代]], and [[LuMaoshou|鹿毛寿]] read Yan Wang Kuai's idealism and desire for reputation, turning moralized abdication talk into [[RuleBreakingAbdication|破坏规则式禅让]].

## Key Claims
- The episode is framed under 316 BCE, Zhou Shenliang Wang's fifth year, and follows two main threads: Qin's Ba-Shu decision and Yan's internal power transfer.
- [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] argues for attacking Han and threatening the Zhou royal center, including the symbolic route through new cities, Yi-Luo geography, and the [[Jiuding|九鼎]].
- [[SimaCuo|司马错]] argues that Qin should first expand its real base: take Shu, gain land and wealth, feed the people, strengthen the army, and avoid looking like a direct violator of the Zhou-centered order.
- [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] accepts Sima Cuo's advice; Qin conquers Shu in about ten months, reduces the Shu ruler's status, appoints [[ChenZhuangWarringStates|陈庄]], and becomes wealthier and stronger.
- After [[SuQin|苏秦]] dies, his brothers Su Dai and [[SuLi|苏厉]] remain known as persuaders; Su Dai is linked by marriage to Zi Zhi and helps create the first trust move in Yan.
- Su Dai tells Yan Wang Kuai that Qi cannot dominate because its ruler does not trust [[ChenLiao|陈聊]], nudging Yan Wang Kuai to prove that he trusts Zi Zhi.
- [[LuMaoshou|鹿毛寿]] then uses ancient abdication stories about Yao, Xu You, Yu, Boyi, and Qi to make Yan Wang Kuai treat yielding authority as a path to moral reputation and hegemonic strength.
- Yan Wang Kuai first proposes yielding the state to Zi Zhi; Zi Zhi refuses publicly but gains awe, then receives the real appointment power over senior officials and control over people and finances.
- The episode says Yan Wang Kuai may have had benevolent personal qualities, but he did not understand politics; copying ancient abdication ideals inside a Warring States court destroyed normal succession boundaries.
- The source frames Zi Zhi as unworthy of the position he captures and treats the broken rule as a setup for later Yan disaster.

## Key Quotes
> "攻蜀如同狼入羊群" - the source's summary of Sima Cuo's confidence in the Shu campaign.

> "有仁德但不懂政治的理想主义者" - the episode's evaluation of Yan Wang Kuai.
