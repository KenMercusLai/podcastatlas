# 580. The Irish Civil War: The Assassination of Sir Henry Wilson (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the 22 June 1922 assassination of [[HenryWilson|Sir Henry Wilson]], an Irish-born British field marshal and Ulster Unionist MP, by IRA members Reggie Dunne and Joseph O'Sullivan. It uses Wilson's death to explain how political murder, British pressure, the anti-treaty occupation of the Four Courts, and a later kidnapping converged at the opening of the [[IrishTreatyCivilWarSplit|Irish Civil War]].

The episode's most consequential but least settled claim concerns authorization. Ronan McGreevey rejects several alternative theories and argues that the [[IrishRepublicanBrotherhood|Irish Republican Brotherhood]], through its president [[MichaelCollinsIrishRevolutionary|Michael Collins]], sanctioned the attack; the supplied evidence supports a historical case rather than an uncontested finding. The subsequent British ultimatum rested on weak public evidence, but it sharply narrowed the provisional government's room to leave the Four Courts occupation unresolved.

## Key Claims

- Wilson combined senior imperial military service with outspoken Irish unionism, opposition to Home Rule in Ulster, and a hard-line preference for treating the War of Independence as a military rather than political problem.
- Dunne and O'Sullivan were British-born Irish nationalists and disabled British Army veterans who joined the IRA, shot Wilson outside his Eaton Place home, wounded policemen during their escape, and were later convicted and hanged.
- The Anglo-Irish Treaty split nationalist politics and the IRA over dominion status, the oath, and the authority of the provisional government, while anti-treaty forces occupied the Four Courts from April 1922.
- Northern sectarian violence, Wilson's advisory role to the Northern Irish government, and his public hard-line image made him a symbol of coercion, although the episode describes more qualified private views on military discipline and sectarian policing.
- McGreevey argues that an IRB chain running through Liam Tobin and Sam Maguire carried a Collins-approved order to Dunne, but the episode presents this as a defended attribution rather than documentary certainty.
- [[DavidLloydGeorge|David Lloyd George]] blamed the Four Courts garrison and warned Collins that Britain would remove it unless the provisional government acted, despite having little evidence that the occupiers directed the assassination.
- The later kidnapping of Free State general Ginger O'Connell supplied a public trigger for action; pro-treaty forces then used British guns and shells against the Four Courts, opening the civil war.

## Key Quotes

> “Ireland's Sarajevo” - Ronan McGreevey's analogy for an assassination followed by an ultimatum and wider war.

> “the freedom to achieve freedom” - the episode's account of Collins's defense of the treaty as a platform for fuller independence.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode deepens the causal lead-up to the Four Courts bombardment summarized in the two existing sources on the treaty split.
- Collins's responsibility is a contested historical attribution. The episode reports McGreevey's elimination of rival theories and later corroboration but does not provide surviving written authorization.
- Wilson's public hard line coexisted with reported private opposition to undisciplined Black and Tan tactics and sectarian policing; neither qualification makes him a humanitarian opponent of coercion.
- The British claim that the Four Courts garrison directed the killing rested on weak evidence, and the kidnapping of Ginger O'Connell provided a distinct immediate pretext for the bombardment. The assassination is therefore best treated as an escalation and constraint, not a mechanically sufficient cause.
