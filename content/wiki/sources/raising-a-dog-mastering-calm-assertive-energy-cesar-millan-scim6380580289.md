## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[CesarMillan]] about dog psychology, owner energy, and practical household rituals. Millan's central frame, captured as [[CalmAssertiveEnergy]], is that dogs respond first to energy, body language, intention, movement, and timing rather than human explanation. The episode connects puppy selection, leash behavior, greetings, walks, exercise, discipline, affection, anxiety, barking, neutering, end-of-life care, and relationship leadership to a broader claim that companion care improves when humans regulate themselves before projecting needs onto the dog.

## Key Claims
- [[CesarMillan]] says many dog behavior problems begin with the human's state: unstable, guilty, anxious, or affection-first handling can reinforce the dog's unwanted state.
- [[CalmAssertiveEnergy]] organizes the practical toolkit around silence, calmness, confidence, love, joy, body language, intention, and follow-through before verbal command.
- Dogs should be understood before breed or name as living animals with species-specific needs for movement, safety, structure, and clear social signals.
- The episode treats walks, greeting rituals, and feeding as recurring places where humans establish safety, peace, trust, respect, rules, boundaries, and limitations.
- Millan's exercise-discipline-affection order argues that affection works best after the dog's body and mind have been served, not as a substitute for movement or structure.
- Puppy selection should attend to energy and social disposition: middle-of-the-pack dogs are framed as easier family fits than front-of-pack dogs that need more handling knowledge.
- Huberman uses Costello and Strummer as owner examples, linking dog-handling practices to forebrain impulse control, barking extinction, structured feeding, and calm greetings.
- The leadership branch generalizes beyond dogs: the episode uses animals as a mirror for human self-regulation, household hierarchy, relationship roles, political leadership, and end-of-life care.

## Key Quotes
> "silence, calmness, confidence, love, and joy" - Millan's sequence for good energy.

> "no look, no touch, no speak" - the calm greeting rule Huberman says helped Costello.

> "exercise, discipline, affection" - Millan's order for meeting a dog's needs.
