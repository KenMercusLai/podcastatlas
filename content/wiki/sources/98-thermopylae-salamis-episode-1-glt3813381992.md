# 98. Thermopylae & Salamis Episode 1

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces the road to [[BattleOfThermopylae|Thermopylae]] and [[BattleOfSalamis|Salamis]] through the [[IonianRevolt]], the burning of [[Sardis]], the destruction of [[Miletus]], the [[BattleOfMarathon]], Athenian fleet-building, [[XerxesI|Xerxes]]' invasion preparations, Greek disunity, and Delphic prophecy. It reframes the war as a confrontation between the era's dominant [[AchaemenidEmpire|imperial power]] and a fragmented Greek periphery rather than a timeless contest between a unified West and East.

Its strongest contribution is to join strategy to source criticism. [[Themistocles]] converts Laurion silver, Piraeus, ostracism, and the "wooden wall" oracle into a naval policy, while [[Herodotus]] supplies an unusually cross-cultural but still Greek narrative in the absence of a comparable surviving Persian account. The episode ends with the linked Greek defense at Thermopylae and Artemisium ready to meet the invasion.

## Key Claims

- The surviving account is structurally asymmetric: Greek literary sources, especially [[Herodotus]], narrate a war absent from surviving Persian narrative records.
- The [[AchaemenidEmpire]] is presented as the age's largest power and as an ideological order that moralized kingship, truth, punishment, and global rule.
- The [[IonianRevolt]], Athenian participation in the burning of [[Sardis]], and Persian destruction of [[Miletus]] make the invasions a sequence of rebellion and punishment rather than an unprovoked civilizational clash.
- Marathon becomes a major Athenian memory, but [[Themistocles]] recognizes that a larger Persian invasion still requires a maritime answer.
- Laurion silver, the ostracism of Aristides, and harbor development at Piraeus enable Athens to build the fleet later used at Salamis.
- Xerxes' bridges, canal, multinational forces, logistics, and treatment of spies display imperial capacity while the quoted force totals remain uncertain and exaggerated in Greek tradition.
- The wooden-wall oracle supports evacuation and naval defense, while Thermopylae and Artemisium form a mutually dependent land-sea position against a divided Greek political background.

## Key Quotes

> "custom is king" - the episode's Herodotean reminder that communities judge the world through inherited practice.

> "wooden wall" - the Delphic phrase Themistocles interprets as the Athenian fleet rather than an Acropolis palisade.

- [[AchaemenidEmpire]], [[XerxesI]], and [[AchaemenidImperialOrderIdeology]] - imperial scale, invading king, and moralized order claimed for Persian rule.
- [[IonianRevolt]], [[Sardis]], [[Miletus]], and [[BattleOfMarathon]] - escalation path from rebellion and punishment to the earlier Persian defeat.
- [[Themistocles]], [[Athens]], and [[AthenianDemocracy]] - politician, city, and civic order transformed by naval investment and evacuation planning.
- [[BattleOfThermopylae]], [[Leonidas]], and [[BattleOfSalamis]] - linked defensive setup, land commander, and intended naval decision point.
