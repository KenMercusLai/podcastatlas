# 487. Hundred Years' War: Henry V's Invasion of France (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the road to [[HenryVOfEngland|Henry V's]] 1415 invasion of [[France]], separating the familiar scenes of [[HenryVPlay|Shakespeare's *Henry V*]] from a political crisis already dramatic in its own right. [[CharlesVIOfFrance|Charles VI's]] recurring incapacity, [[JohnTheFearless|John the Fearless's]] murder of [[LouisOfOrleans|Louis of Orléans]], the [[ArmagnacBurgundianCivilWar]], and opportunistic English intervention made France vulnerable while Henry's inherited [[LancastrianLegitimacy|legitimacy problem]] made success abroad unusually valuable.

Henry combined dynastic claims, marriage proposals, territorial demands, unpaid ransom, and alliance feelers before presenting failed negotiation as justification for war. Parliamentary finance, a force dominated by archers, artillery, shipping, and the intended capture of [[Harfleur]] turned that case into an expedition, while the [[SouthamptonPlot]] underscored how defeat could reopen opposition at home.

## Key Claims

- Anglo-French peace under [[HenryIVOfEngland|Henry IV]] functioned more like armed competition: France supported English enemies, while Harfleur threatened English shipping.
- Charles VI's recurring mental crises weakened the crown and intensified competition between Louis of Orléans and John the Fearless for influence over royal government.
- John's 1407 killing of Louis, subsequent justification of tyrannicide, and the Armagnac response turned elite rivalry into a civil war that English rulers could exploit.
- Henry IV backed opposing French factions in successive expeditions, showing that English intervention preceded Henry V's more decisive invasion policy.
- Henry V's diplomacy mixed plausible claims and marriage negotiations with maximalist demands for money, territory, titles, and potentially the French crown.
- Henry's religious conviction and the insecurity of Lancastrian succession made war both a strategic opportunity and a high-risk test of kingship.
- Parliament, magnates, shipping, archers, and heavy artillery gave the 1415 campaign substantial state and military preparation.
- The Southampton Plot revealed continuing domestic vulnerability immediately before the fleet sailed for Harfleur.

## Key Quotes

> "Once more unto the breach" - Shakespearean memory used to open the episode's inquiry into the historical invasion.

> "busy giddy minds with foreign quarrels" - the play's formulation of war as a possible answer to domestic instability.

> "second Calais" - the strategic possibility represented by Harfleur.
