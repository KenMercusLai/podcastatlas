# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》304-2｜因跳槽引发的诸侯王谋反

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the political prehistory of [[LiuCiHengshanKingWesternHan|衡山王刘赐]] before the household crisis narrated in Hanji 305-1 and 305-2. It links his resentment of the central court to three pressures: rivalry with his elder brother [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]], confidence derived from descent from [[LiuBang|刘邦]] through [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]], and humiliation after a centrally appointed official blocks an unlawful execution and reports his land seizures.

The durable synthesis is that Liu Ci's rebellion posture develops through interaction between royal ambition and institutional restraint. A retainer's wish to enter central service becomes a status injury; Liu Ci's retaliatory killing attempt triggers review by the kingdom's 内史, and the failed counter-accusation ends with the court removing part of Liu Ci's senior appointment authority. The episode interprets that loss of control as the point where defensive preparation against Liu An becomes a more direct desire to overthrow the court.

## Key Claims

- The episode presents Liu Chang, Liu An, and Liu Ci as a royal line repeatedly drawn into conflict with the Han center, while not establishing heredity as an independent cause.
- Liu Ci first rules Lujiang and is later moved to Hengshan after maintaining frequent southern Yue contacts during the Rebellion of the Seven States despite not joining Liu Bi.
- Hostility between Liu Ci and Liu An matters because Liu Ci allegedly responds to news of Liu An's plot by recruiting talent himself rather than reporting his brother to the court.
- When a subordinate seeks central employment, Liu Ci treats the move as personal disloyalty, imprisons the person, invents a charge, and seeks execution.
- A centrally appointed 内史 rejects the death sentence, demonstrating that the vassal king's household authority remains subject to imperial law and officials.
- Liu Ci's attempt to accuse the 内史 backfires when the official reports his seizure of commoners' fields and leveling of graves for farmland.
- The episode says the 廷尉 seeks Liu Ci's arrest, but [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] instead removes his power to appoint officials at and above a stated sub-county level.
- The host treats that loss of appointment authority as an escalation point from precaution against Liu An toward rebellion motivated by wounded status and a wish to restore control.

## Key Quotes

> “封国也是汉朝的领土” - the episode's institutional boundary against treating a royal kingdom as sovereign territory.

> “为了维护自己的尊严，推翻朝廷” - the host's interpretation of Liu Ci's motive after appointment authority is reduced.

## Contradictions

- Identity normalization: the supplied transcript repeatedly says “恒山王刘次,” but the Lujiang-to-Hengshan transfer, brother Liu An, father Liu Chang, later family case, and rebellion outcome identify 衡山王刘赐. The wiki preserves the mismatch here while linking the canonical identity.
- The source says Liu Ci was moved to “恒山国”; the canonical polity is normalized to Western Han 衡山国 and remains distinct from the unrelated pre-Qin [[HengshanState|恒山国]].
- The episode describes Liu An as Liu Chang's eldest son and Liu Ci as another son, but does not independently establish every birth-order detail or the claim that three generations shared one psychological disposition.
- Liu Ci's southern contacts, private ambition, recruitment, exact offense against the job-seeking subordinate, land seizures, grave leveling, 廷尉 request, appointment threshold, and motive shift remain episode-attributed pending primary-text comparison.
- The title compresses the case into a rebellion “caused by job-hopping,” but the episode itself gives a multi-causal sequence involving sibling rivalry, royal status, unlawful punishment, official review, misconduct exposure, and loss of authority.
