# 500. The Roman Conquest of Britain: The Empire Strikes Back (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode explains the [[RomanConquestOfBritain|Claudian conquest of Britain]] as both a military invasion and a legitimacy project. [[Claudius]] inherited preparations made under Caligula, used the exile of the Roman-backed king [[Verica]] as a justification, and sent [[AulusPlautius]] to defeat the expanding Catuvellaunian power before arriving to claim the submission of British rulers and stage the victory as his own.

The campaign established [[RomanBritain]] through a mixture of disciplined force, client alliance, elite reward, colonization, extraction, and terror. [[Caratacus]] continued resistance in Wales, while [[SuetoniusPaulinus]] eventually attacked Anglesey and its Druidic center; news of the [[BoudiccanRevolt]] then revealed how quickly apparent security in the southeast could collapse.

## Key Claims

- Claudius needed military prestige after an irregular accession and used Britain to create the public identity of a victorious emperor.
- Britain was not isolated before AD 43: trade, coinage, diplomatic contact, hostages, client kings, and southeastern dynastic rivalry already connected it to Rome.
- Verica's displacement by Caratacus supplied Rome with a client-protection grievance that could serve as a casus belli.
- Caligula's abandoned preparations left Claudius legions, naval facilities, frontier reinforcement, and a more feasible invasion plan.
- Plautius's army crossed in AD 43, won at the Medway and Thames, and paused so Claudius could turn operational success into an imperial triumph.
- Conquest divided British elites between resistance and collaboration; Rome rewarded some clients with status and material advantage while subjecting other communities to occupation and violence.
- Roman control remained incomplete: Caratacus sustained Welsh resistance, the Anglesey campaign was destructive, and the Boudiccan rising exposed the fragility of early consolidation.

## Key Quotes

> "the empire strikes back" - the episode's title frame for Rome's return almost a century after Caesar's expeditions.

> "the capital of Britain" - the Roman political treatment of Camulodunon, despite the hosts' caution about imposing a unified national capital on tribal Britain.

## Connections

- [[Claudius]] - emperor who converted an inherited invasion opportunity into military legitimacy and public spectacle.
- [[AulusPlautius]] - operational commander of the AD 43 invasion.
- [[Verica]] - displaced client king whose appeal helped turn British dynastic conflict into a Roman intervention claim.
- [[Caratacus]] - Catuvellaunian leader who survived the initial defeat and sustained resistance in Wales.
- [[RomanConquestOfBritain]] - campaign joining military victory, client politics, propaganda, collaboration, and prolonged resistance.
- [[RomanBritain]] - provincial order whose foundations were laid through the invasion and its unequal settlements.
- [[SuetoniusPaulinus]] - later governor whose assault on Anglesey ended as the Boudiccan revolt began.
- [[RomanProvincialConsolidation]] - longer process that followed battlefield success through colonies, client rulers, towns, extraction, and garrisons.
- [[Tacitus]] - Roman historian used for Caratacus, Togidubnus, Anglesey, and the limits of conquest evidence.
