# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》436｜战场递小纸条？揭秘赵充国与汉宣帝的战术之争

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the tactical dispute before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-439-gongbi-weiqing-huoqubing-zhichang-guanlizhe-bangyang-zhaochongguo-lirqsoujwewu6efd-shb7mypqvu3|Hanji 439]]'s accepted Qiang屯田 policy. Xin Wuxian / 辛武贤 urges a July multi-route attack against Qiang groups, while [[ZhaoChongguoWesternHan|赵充国]] argues to preserve the original aim: split the Qiang coalition, punish the [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]] as the main culprit, pardon coerced groups, and avoid turning loose allies into a harder enemy bloc.

The durable synthesis is that Zhao's restraint is operational, not passive. He audits food, pack animals, terrain, enemy timing, alliance psychology, and postwar frontier cost; then he persuades [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] that attacking the wrong target at the wrong moment would strengthen the very coalition Han wants to break.

## Key Claims

- The episode frames the exchange between Emperor Xuan and Zhao Chongguo as a high-stakes distant-command problem: imperial instructions and frontier reports move slowly, but the disputed question is precise tactical timing.
- Xin Wuxian proposes a July attack with thirty days of grain from Zhangye and Jiuquan, aiming to strike Qiang groups, seize livestock and families, and return before a later winter campaign.
- Zhao rejects the plan on logistics grounds: heavy grain and equipment would slow fighting power, and Qiang forces could infer Han movement limits, withdraw into terrain, and cut supply lines.
- Zhao argues that the strategic aim is not indiscriminate punishment, but stopping the Qiang coalition by targeting the Xianling Qiang as the principal rebel and pardoning or reassuring coerced groups.
- When Emperor Xuan initially accepts Xin Wuxian's plan and orders coordinated attack, Zhao replies that striking groups without hostile intent would violate the original strategy and help Xianling bind allies.
- Zhao uses a Sunzi-style logic: defense can be an active way to make the enemy come on unfavorable terms, whereas premature attack lets the enemy lead Han movement.
- Zhao warns that attacking the allied groups first would let Xianling rescue them, consolidate moral leverage, and turn a two- or three-year problem into a ten-year frontier burden.
- Emperor Xuan ultimately accepts Zhao's plan on July 5 after receiving Zhao's further memorial.
