# Vol.267 如何现场看世界杯并大亏一笔 feat.鹰眼时间

## Summary
This [[ShangyeJiushiZheyang|商业就是这样]] crossover with [[YingyanShijian|鹰眼时间]] uses [[TeteFei|特特飞]]'s first-person 2026 [[FIFAWorldCup|FIFA World Cup]] trip across U.S. and Mexican host cities to explain how live World Cup cost lands on fans and cities. It argues that [[FIFA]] holds the strongest revenue rights while host-city committees absorb operating, security, transportation, venue, and fan-festival costs, making [[WorldCupHostCityEconomics]] depend on government subsidies, local sponsors, and on-site spending. The episode extends the wiki's [[SportsEventTicketing]] branch through high prices, official resale fees, no-cap resale pricing, and a $900-plus personal ticket loss that makes [[OfficialSportsTicketResale]] feel like a speculative market.

## Key Claims
- The 2026 tournament is framed as unusually city-driven: local host committees and city-level plans shape fan festivals, transport, pricing, sponsors, and event programming.
- [[FIFA]] controls core revenue: tickets, [[SportsMediaRights]], global sponsorship, and licensed merchandise mostly accrue to FIFA rather than host cities.
- Local host committees bear meaningful costs: security, transportation, operations, venue modifications, fan-festival builds and staffing, and city-service burden.
- Chicago's 2018 withdrawal from hosting is used as proof that even large cities can reject uncertain FIFA obligations when unknown costs may fall on taxpayers.
- North American stadiums reduce construction need but still require football-specific retrofits, including field dimensions, natural grass, locker rooms, temporary seating, screens, and broadcast/support systems.
- [[FanFestivalCommercialization]] shows the cost-allocation tension: sponsor exposure may support FIFA's global rights package while day-to-day build and service costs sit with the local committee.
- [[HostCitySupporterSponsorship]] gives cities a new local revenue channel, but sponsor categories are restricted and the main value is hospitality, tickets, VIP access, fan-zone presence, and city relationship rather than stadium exposure.
- The fan experience is described as expensive across the full chain: tickets, beverages, parking, shuttle rules, local activities, paid press-conference access, commemorative turf fragments, and premium keepsake tickets.
- The episode says the official resale platform charges both buyer and seller 15% and removes price caps, letting the organizer internalize part of secondary-market upside while increasing volatility for ordinary buyers.
- The personal ticket-loss story shows that resale prices can depend on match timing, team advancement probabilities, and fan budget substitution, making ticket holding resemble a risky event-outcome bet.
- The episode names [[FIFA]], [[GianniInfantino]], and the U.S. sports industry as winners, while warning that future hosts and ordinary fans may inherit the cost of the model's commercial success.
