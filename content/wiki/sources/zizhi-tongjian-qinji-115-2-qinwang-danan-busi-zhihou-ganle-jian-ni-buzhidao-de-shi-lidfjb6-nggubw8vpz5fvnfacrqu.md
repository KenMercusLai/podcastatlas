# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》115-2｜秦王大难不死之后干了件你不知道的事？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the [[JingKe|荆轲]] attempt against [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] after [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-1-jingke-ciqin-sihu-you-dianer-caoshuai-le-lgz1adsn1t8jjiaobmtfedpr-ixn|Qinji 115-1]] leaves him approaching alone with the [[DuKangMap|督亢地图]]. The map opens into [[TuqiongBijian|图穷匕见]], Jing Ke's first strike misses, Ying Zheng survives through panic, distance, a broken sleeve, running around a pillar, and finally drawing his long sword after a court reminder.

The source then treats the failure as an execution problem, not only a heroic tragedy: [[QinWuyang|秦武阳]]'s inability to join the attack leaves Jing Ke without the planned second body, and [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]]'s earlier impatience looks more costly in hindsight. Once the danger passes, Ying Zheng orders [[WangJianQin|王翦]] against [[YanState|燕国]], whose joint force with [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]]'s [[DaiState|代国]] remnant is defeated west of the Yi River; the episode closes by shifting from the failed strike into Jing Ke's literary afterlife and the narrowing space for youxia under unification.

## Key Claims
- The idiom [[TuqiongBijian|图穷匕见]] is tied to the moment when the Du Kang map is fully unrolled and the hidden dagger appears.
- Jing Ke's first strike fails after he seizes Ying Zheng's sleeve; Ying Zheng breaks free, and the broken sleeve removes the assassin's immediate leverage.
- Qin court ritual and weapon protocol matter in the scene: ministers are unarmed, palace attendants are outside, and Ying Zheng initially cannot draw his unusually long sword.
- The reminder "王负剑" changes the tactical balance by telling Ying Zheng to shift the sword to his back and draw it successfully.
- Once Ying Zheng draws the sword, the attack reverses; he wounds Jing Ke, including the left thigh, and Jing Ke's thrown dagger misses.
- The source follows the tradition that Jing Ke, badly wounded and disarmed, sits in the "箕踞" posture, curses Ying Zheng, and says the failure came from wanting to capture the Qin king alive and force a pact for Taizi Dan.
- The episode says [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] adopts the [[Shiji|《史记》]] / Qin Shi Huang annals wording that Jing Ke was "体解以徇," treating the execution as public punitive dismemberment.
- Ying Zheng's near-death shock becomes state action: after recovering, he increases forces in Zhao-occupied territory and orders Wang Jian to attack Yan.
- Wang Jian defeats the combined Yan and Dai/Zhao Jia force west of the Yi River, leaving Yan close to destruction.
- The host argues that if Qin Wuyang had been steady enough to join Jing Ke inside the hall, a two-person attack might have left Ying Zheng little chance to escape.
- Jing Ke's earlier delay for an unnamed distant friend is therefore reread as an operational judgment about assistant quality rather than hesitation.
- The episode's literary afterlife section uses [[TaoYuanming|陶渊明]] and [[GongZizhen|龚自珍]] to frame Jing Ke as a failed but emotionally durable youxia figure.
- The closing claim is that after Qin's unification, heavy-handed central rule would increasingly suppress and monitor people who valued personal义 and death-defying action above official order.

## Key Quotes
> "图穷而匕首见" - the idiom attached to the map-and-dagger reveal.

> "大王，王负剑" - the court reminder that lets Ying Zheng draw his sword.

> "乃欲以生劫之" - Jing Ke's source-scoped explanation that he wanted to capture the Qin king alive.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is asserted.
- Source-scope caution: the title and later shorthand can say "秦始皇," but the narrated assassination happens before Ying Zheng has taken the imperial title.
- Source-scope caution: the claim that Qin Wuyang's presence would have left Ying Zheng little chance is recorded as the host's counterfactual interpretation, not as a demonstrable alternate outcome.
- Source-scope caution: Jing Ke's claim that he wanted to capture Ying Zheng alive is reported as his dying speech in the source tradition; the wiki should not silently treat it as the only explanation for the failed first strike.
