# A Good Walk Spoiled

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode uses the fenced grounds of [[BrentwoodCountryClub]] to argue that private Los Angeles golf courses combine low user density and public exclusion with unusually favorable property-tax treatment. [[MalcolmGladwell]] traces that treatment from a 1960 California constitutional amendment promoted by [[BobHope]] through Proposition 13 and the county's refusal to treat gradual membership turnover as a change of ownership. With philosopher [[MarkCohen]], he uses the Ship of Theseus to show that institutional continuity is a contestable policy choice, then proposes scheduled public access as a reciprocal benefit for continued subsidy.

## Key Claims

- A typical golf course occupies roughly 200 acres while accommodating only about 72 golfers at once, making its opportunity cost conspicuous in park-poor Los Angeles.
- A 1960 California constitutional amendment exempted golf-course land from ordinary valuation at its “highest and best use”; [[BobHope]] served as the campaign's public face.
- Proposition 13 compounded the preference by anchoring assessments near pre-1978 levels until a qualifying ownership change or new construction.
- The county reportedly declined to aggregate gradual transfers of equity memberships because no single transfer exceeded 50 percent, although a Bel-Air member estimated that only about 10 percent of the club's then-members had belonged in 1978.
- [[MarkCohen]] explains that component-based and spatiotemporal-continuity accounts give different answers to the Ship of Theseus, so philosophy does not dictate one ownership rule.
- Gladwell reports an informal $9 billion valuation for [[LosAngelesCountryClub]] and contrasts a roughly $90 million ordinary-tax counterfactual with a reported payment of about $200,000; the supplied summary does not independently verify those figures.
- The episode proposes public evening and weekend use of subsidized courses, citing St Andrews and Toronto's Rosedale Golf Club as examples of private golf coexisting with public access.
- An episode-cited study associates heavier CEO golfing with poorer firm performance and a greater chance of dismissal, but the supplied document does not establish causation or provide the underlying paper.
