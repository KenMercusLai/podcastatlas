# The Worst Poet in the World | From Cautionary Tales

## Summary

This [[CautionaryTales]] episode has [[TimHarford]] revisit [[WilliamMcGonagall]], the Dundee weaver, actor, and poet remembered for his awkward verse about the [[TayBridgeDisaster|Tay Bridge disaster]]. It first treats his 25 years of ridicule, hoaxes, failed travel, poverty, and hostile performances as an extreme case of persistence, then asks whether some apparent incompetence may have been a knowingly comic public persona.

The episode's durable creative argument is that making can retain personal and cultural value without professional quality or commercial success. Its historical reversal remains deliberately unresolved: McGonagall may have been oblivious, may have knowingly endured humiliation for income, or may have helped construct a clowning act whose absurdity and uncomfortable truths kept drawing crowds.

## Key Claims

- McGonagall moved from Dundee weaving and amateur Shakespeare performance into a self-declared poetic vocation around age fifty, then continued writing and performing for roughly 25 years despite sustained mockery.
- *The Tay Bridge Disaster* became memorable through strained rhyme, broken meter, blunt description, and an engineering moral that sits uneasily beside mass death.
- [[EverydayCreativeAgency]] separates the market value of polished work from the personal value of making, expression, attention, and persistence.
- Adult fear of failure can suppress creative participation by comparing ordinary attempts with readily available masterworks.
- [[FailurePerformanceAmbiguity]] cautions against confidently inferring incompetence or intent from a comic public persona: McGonagall's Macbeth improvisation, jokes, ominous bridge praise, disaster verse, and crowd-drawing performances admit more than one explanation.
- His reported performance income and Dundee's ban on riotous recitals complicate the claim that he was simply a commercially failed artist.
- Generative AI may change the economics and scarcity of creative output without eliminating the human value of creative participation.

## Contradictions

- The episode does not settle whether McGonagall was oblivious, strategically tolerant of ridicule, or deliberately constructing a comic act. The new interpretation qualifies rather than overturns the existing profile of sincere ambition, weak craft, and partial self-awareness.
- The comic-genius reading is an explicit hypothesis. Jokes, ominous phrasing, crowd response, and reported earnings do not independently establish intention.
- Birth year, biographical anecdotes, fraudulent letters, travel, performance income, civic bans, auction value, and the precise bridge sequence remain episode-reported because the supplied document is a structured summary rather than a transcript, archive, literary edition, engineering inquiry, or sales record.
- The comparison between generative AI and earlier media technologies is an argument about creative economics, not evidence that every technology has equivalent effects on labor, skill, authorship, or culture.
