# 189. Australian Prime Ministers: Bob Hawke - Scott Morrison (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode surveys Australian prime ministers from [[BobHawke|Bob Hawke]] through [[ScottMorrison|Scott Morrison]]. Its central arc runs from the [[HawkeKeatingReformSettlement|Hawke-Keating combination of universal social provision and market-opening reform]], through [[JohnHoward|John Howard's]] long conservative government, into an era when party-room challenges repeatedly displaced [[KevinRudd|Kevin Rudd]], [[JuliaGillard|Julia Gillard]], [[TonyAbbott|Tony Abbott]], and [[MalcolmTurnbull|Malcolm Turnbull]].

The episode presents [[Australia]] as internationally consequential rather than politically peripheral. Hawke and [[PaulKeating|Paul Keating]] are treated as precursors to later “third way” politics, while Abbott's border policy is said to have influenced British conservatives. Across the period, arguments over Britain, the United States, Asia, immigration, Indigenous reconciliation, climate, mining, religion, and the republic question become rival ways of defining Australia's national direction.

## Key Claims

- [[BobHawke|Hawke]] joined trade-union authority, patriotic and pro-American Labor politics, universal healthcare, a floated currency, tariff reduction, deregulation, and globalization.
- [[PaulKeating|Keating]] combined working-class origins with cosmopolitan tastes, displaced Hawke after their handover pact broke down, advanced Aboriginal reconciliation and republicanism, and urged a stronger Asian orientation.
- The episode interprets the [[HawkeKeatingReformSettlement]] as an early model for the social-democratic and market-liberal combination later called the “third way.”
- [[JohnHoward|Howard's]] four-election government joined monarchy, Christianity, the U.S. alliance, the War on Terror, restrictive border politics, and an appeal to aspirational working- and lower-middle-class voters.
- [[KevinRudd|Rudd]] is credited with a financial-crisis stimulus and later became the first victim and then a beneficiary of the period's leadership challenges.
- [[JuliaGillard|Gillard]] governed a hung parliament, faced gendered attacks, delivered the misogyny speech, and was later removed by Rudd.
- [[TonyAbbott|Abbott]] represented a strongly British, Catholic, conservative current, opposed climate-policy arguments, and implemented offshore processing through Operation Sovereign Borders before being removed by [[MalcolmTurnbull|Turnbull]].
- Turnbull's liberal republican profile did not protect him from removal, and [[ScottMorrison|Morrison]] then faced bushfires, COVID-19, AUKUS, and the Djokovic visa dispute.
- Repeated internal removals make Australia a comparative case for [[PrimeMinisterialRegicide]], although the hosts do not establish that the spill era necessarily caused worse government.

## Key Quotes

> “as mad as a cut snake” - Hawke's insult for Abbott, used to introduce Australian political sledging.

> “I want to do you slowly” - Keating's threat to John Hewson, used as an example of his parliamentary style.

> “the recession we had to have” - Keating's politically costly description of the early-1990s downturn.

- [[HawkeKeatingReformSettlement]] - concept for combining social-democratic provision with market-opening reform.
