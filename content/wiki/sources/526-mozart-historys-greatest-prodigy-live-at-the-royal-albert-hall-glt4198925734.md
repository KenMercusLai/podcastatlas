# 526. Mozart: History's Greatest Prodigy LIVE at the Royal Albert Hall

## Summary

This live [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[WolfgangAmadeusMozart|Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart]] from child-prodigy touring under [[LeopoldMozart|Leopold Mozart]] to opera commissions, Salzburg service, Viennese freelancing, family rupture, financial strain, and early death. Its central argument is social as well as biographical: Mozart stood between the court musician treated as a servant and the independent creator later celebrated as a genius.

The episode also separates achievement from afterlife mythology. Mozart's talent and ambition were extraordinary, but the anonymous Requiem commission was not supernatural, the transcript rejects poisoning and total public neglect, and *The Magic Flute* complicates a simple story of terminal failure.

## Key Claims

- Leopold converted Mozart's precocity into a European touring enterprise, giving the child access, training, commissions, and fame while binding career development to parental authority and family income.
- As child novelty faded, the enterprise shifted from performed prodigy to commissioned composer, including *Mitridate, re di Ponto* in Milan.
- Salzburg offered security within household service, but Mozart experienced the Archbishop's authority as a constraint and eventually chose the greater agency and volatility of freelance life in [[Vienna]].
- Mozart's independence did not end dependence: commissions, patrons, audience demand, illness, war, and elite spending still shaped income and opportunity.
- *The Marriage of Figaro* is presented as a stronger expression of Mozart's frustration with aristocratic hierarchy than a biographical identification with *Don Giovanni*.
- Financial difficulty late in Mozart's life coexisted with continuing success, making the image of a forgotten, merely irresponsible pauper inadequate.
- The Requiem story shows how anonymous patronage and early death could be reorganized into the [[RomanticArtistMyth|Romantic myth of doomed genius]].
