## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places [[CuiYanLateHan|崔琰]]'s execution after [[CaoCao|曹操]] becomes King of Wei in 216 CE. A consoling letter to Yang Xun is detached from its immediate purpose and read as political dissatisfaction; Cui Yan's moral standing, support for [[CaoPi|曹丕]], and place in the succession conflict then make the accusation more dangerous than its wording alone.

The episode connects that death to [[DingYiLateHan|丁仪]] and the [[CaoZhi|曹植]] camp, while explicitly relying on indirect attribution rather than a conclusive identification of the accuser. It then follows [[MaoJie|毛玠]] into a second accusation and highlights [[HeQiaLateHan|和洽]]'s demand that punishment rest on proof rather than an interested informant's statement.

## Key Claims

- Cao Cao's elevation from Duke to King of Wei marks a further public concentration of authority, although the episode's claim that his power was already virtually imperial remains an institutional interpretation.
- Cui Yan writes to console the mocked official Yang Xun; the phrase that circumstances can change is removed from that supportive context and recoded as dissatisfaction with Cao Cao.
- The punishment escalates from arrest and punitive hair removal to forced labor and death after a further report about Cui Yan's conduct in custody.
- Cui Yan's established moral reputation and public support for primogeniture in favor of Cao Pi make him politically consequential inside the Cao Pi–Cao Zhi succession struggle.
- The episode identifies Ding Yi as the likely accuser by combining Pei Songzhi-cited material, Ding Yi's grievance against Cao Pi, and his Cao Zhi alignment, but it does not supply direct proof that settles the attribution.
- [[CuiLinLateHan|崔林]] provides a contrast in political survival: he defends Cui Yan's worth yet later advances through a more adaptive and practical official career.
- After Cui Yan's death, Mao Jie is accused of using drought and the families of punished people to criticize Cao Cao; He Qia argues that either the statement must be proved or the accuser's falsehood punished.
- Cao Cao spares Mao Jie's life but removes him from office; the episode reads Cao Cao's use of Ding Yi as instrumental rather than as evidence of trust.
