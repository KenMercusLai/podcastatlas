# 26 君特·格拉斯《比目鱼》：但泽、德国、女性、烹饪与人类的历史

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] reading episode approaches [[TheFlounder|《比目鱼》]] through [[GunterGrass|君特·格拉斯]]'s [[DanzigGdansk|但泽 / 格但斯克]], the novel's nine-month and nine-epoch structure, and its rewriting of *The Fisherman and His Wife*. The hosts read its four-thousand-year history as an unstable argument about gender, food, technology, war, desire, and historical memory rather than a program with one correct conclusion.

The strongest synthesis joins [[CookingAsCivilizationalLabor|cooking as civilizational labor]] to [[ProgressAsCreativeDestructiveForce|progress as a creative and destructive force]]. Women obtain fire and use it to feed and sustain life; men turn fire, knowledge, and desire toward transport, industry, conquest, pollution, and ideological violence. Yet the novel also criticizes coercive matriarchy and parts of modern feminism, so its gender politics work through [[LiteraryAmbiguityAsComplexity|conflicting pressures]] rather than a clean reversal of male and female moral roles.

## Key Claims

- [[DanzigGdansk|Danzig/Gdańsk]] supplies more than a setting: changing sovereignty, mixed German, Polish, and Kashubian histories, war, the Free City, and the later Solidarity movement make place-name choice and historical belonging politically charged.
- [[GunterGrass|Grass]] is presented as a politically engaged writer whose writing on German history and Nazism is complicated by his delayed disclosure that he had served in the Waffen-SS as a young man.
- The novel's nine chapters correspond both to Ilsebill's nine-month pregnancy and to nine historical periods, placing roughly four millennia of history inside an embodied reproductive frame.
- Its rewriting of the fisherman tale transfers insatiable desire from a wife to men who want victory, buildings, vehicles, submarines, spaceflight, and systems of thought; ambition produces useful invention and large-scale harm together.
- [[CookingAsCivilizationalLabor|Cooking]] makes household labor historically causal: food, recipes, hunger, class, murder, and survival repeatedly interrupt masculine claims to war, philosophy, honor, and world rescue.
- The prehistoric maternal order also controls information and suppresses innovation, while the speaking flounder helps men build patriarchy and later faces a feminist tribunal; power remains suspect when its holder changes.
- Love is satirically described as a male invention that can make women locate worth in being loved, while the muse question asks why female inspiration for male artists is normalized more readily than the reverse.
- The episode treats the novel's criticism of feminism, motherhood, sexual violence, and difference as deliberately difficult and sometimes disturbing rather than as a uniquely settled authorial position.
- [[LiYuWriter|李渔]]'s Chinese works titled *The Flounder* provide a distinct comparison: lower-status lovers, mutual fidelity, death, transformation, revival, and reunion use the paired fish to imagine a more reciprocal relationship.
