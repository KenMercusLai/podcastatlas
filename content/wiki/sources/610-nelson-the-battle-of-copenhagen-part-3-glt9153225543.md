# 610. Nelson: The Battle of Copenhagen (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the 1801 [[BattleOfCopenhagen1801|Battle of Copenhagen]] within Britain's effort to break the [[LeagueOfArmedNeutrality|League of Armed Neutrality]]. It presents [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]] as a meticulous planner, exposed battlefield leader, selective disobeyer, theatrical celebrity, and opportunistic negotiator whose attack succeeded through shallow-draft planning, close-range gunnery, captain-level initiative, and a truce that converted Danish losses into a political settlement.

## Key Claims

- Britain treated Baltic access and naval supplies as matters of national survival because the League of Armed Neutrality could restrict British searches of neutral shipping and strengthen France's maritime position.
- Nelson shifted his flag to the shallower-draft Elephant, personally scouted the shoals, consulted captains, and planned a southern approach through the King's Deep to bring British gunnery and carronades close to the Danish line.
- Three British ships grounded and Danish defenders fought intensely before Copenhagen's population, showing that detailed planning reduced but did not remove navigational uncertainty or the human cost of attacking a defended capital.
- When [[HydeParker|Hyde Parker]] signaled withdrawal, Nelson kept the order from being repeated and maintained “engage the enemy more closely,” judging that retreat under fire was more dangerous than continuing the action.
- [[EdwardRiou|Edward Riou]] reluctantly obeyed the withdrawal signal with his frigate squadron and was killed, making the battle a sharp case of unequal information, rank, obedience, and battlefield discretion.
- Nelson's flag-of-truce note to Crown Prince Frederick combined kinship language, coercive warning, and an exit from continued destruction; the resulting ceasefire helped turn tactical advantage into negotiation.
- Denmark accepted temporary neutrality and resupplied the British fleet, while Tsar Paul I's assassination and Alexander I's accommodation with Britain made the League's collapse wider than the battle alone.
- The episode closes by joining Nelson's victory to illness, jealousy toward [[EmmaHamilton|Emma Hamilton]], estrangement from Fanny, public redemption, and the renewed French invasion threat.

## Key Quotes

> "engage the enemy more closely" - Signal 16, which Nelson kept flying after Parker ordered the action discontinued.

> "brothers of Englishmen" - the kinship language attributed to Nelson's truce note to the Danish leadership.

- [[BattleOfCopenhagen1801]] - central battle joining shoal navigation, close action, withdrawal controversy, and negotiation.
- [[Denmark]] and [[LeagueOfArmedNeutrality]] - defended kingdom and Baltic coalition Britain sought to detach from Russian influence.
