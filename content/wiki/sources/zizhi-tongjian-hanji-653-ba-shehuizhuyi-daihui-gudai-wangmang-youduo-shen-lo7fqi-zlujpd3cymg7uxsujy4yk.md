# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》653丨把社会主义带回古代？王莽有多神！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 17 CE economic-policy layer of [[WangMang|王莽]]'s [[XinDynasty|新朝]] before the later 21-22 CE crisis notes. It focuses on [[WujunLiuguan|五均六管]]: a retro-classical program of price equalization, state loans, state monopolies, and tax controls that claimed to limit merchant extraction and usury but instead deepened official predation, fiscal failure, and popular survival rebellion.

The durable synthesis is that [[WangMangReforms|王莽改制]] failed not only because its aims were archaizing or ambitious, but because implementation assigned market control to wealthy merchants and local officials with direct incentives to exploit the system. Harsh regulations, slave taxes, corvee burdens, drought, locusts, slow litigation, property seizure, and suppressed truthful feedback convert economic reform into a state-capacity and legitimacy crisis.

## Key Claims

- In 17 CE, Wang Mang establishes officials to implement [[WujunLiuguan|五均六管]], with Liu Xin naming the program through classical sources such as the Zhouli and Guoyu.
- The program's stated aims are to limit merchant exploitation of farmers and suppress predatory lending, giving the reforms a plausible pro-livelihood rhetoric.
- The "five equalizations" regulate market prices for mountains-and-marshes resources, salt, iron, money, and cloth; when prices exceed official levels, the court intervenes, and when prices fall below them, private exchange remains possible.
- The "six controls" put mountains-and-marshes resources, salt, iron, money, cloth, and alcohol under state management, making the program a broad state-monopoly and credit system.
- Implementation undermines the stated aim because many appointed operators are wealthy merchants who collude with commandery and county officials, falsify accounts, and pursue private profit.
- Fiscal and social results diverge from reform rhetoric: the state treasury is not strengthened, while common people face harsher taxes, prohibitions, corvee, disaster, legal delay, and official seizure.
- Later local rebellion is framed as survival under excessive law and taxation: officials hide the scale of disorder, truthful envoys are punished, flatterers are promoted, and groups around figures such as Gua Tianyi, Lü Mu, Wang Kuang, Wang Feng, Zhang Ba, and Yang Mu grow.
