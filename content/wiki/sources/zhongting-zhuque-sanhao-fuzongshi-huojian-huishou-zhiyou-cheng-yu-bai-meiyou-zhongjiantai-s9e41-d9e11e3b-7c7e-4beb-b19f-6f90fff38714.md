# 【重听】朱雀三号副总师：火箭回收只有成与败，没有中间态 | S9E41

## Summary
This [[WhatsNextKejiZaozhidao|What's Next｜科技早知道]] replay episode adds [[DongKai|董凯]]'s insider engineering account of [[LandSpace|蓝箭航天]]'s [[Zhuque3|朱雀三号]] after the 2025 first flight and before the August 19, 2026 Yao-2 recovery success. Its main contribution is to turn the earlier milestone into an engineering model: recovery is judged by a binary result, but a failed terminal landing can still produce real-flight data that improves the next attempt. The episode also links reusable-rocket economics to reuse-first design, recovery-distance payload tradeoffs, production cadence, safety regulation, and longer-term full-reuse plans.

## Key Claims
- The replay opening says [[Zhuque3|朱雀三号]] Yao-2 launched on August 19, 2026, put its payload into the planned orbit, and recovered its first stage on landing legs, while the interview body was recorded after the 2025 first flight failed roughly 40 meters from the landing point.
- [[DongKai|董凯]] is introduced as [[LandSpace|蓝箭航天]]'s Zhuque-3 overall deputy chief designer, responsible for the rocket's overall design.
- Zhuque-3 is described as a medium-to-large liquid-oxygen/methane reusable rocket: the complete vehicle is about 76 meters long, has nine first-stage engines and one second-stage engine, and uses stainless-steel main tanks.
- Dong treats [[Falcon9|Falcon 9]] as the only mature operational reusable-rocket benchmark and says "domestic Falcon 9" is high praise, while warning that matching Falcon 9's roughly 150 annual launches within five years is too optimistic.
- The source frames reusability as a launch-capacity multiplier rather than a replacement for manufacturing capacity: it matters because large payload, high frequency, and low cost cannot be achieved by expanding disposable production alone.
- [[RocketRecoveryBinaryOutcome]] is the source's strict recovery standard: if the booster stands at the target site and remains still for one minute, recovery succeeds; otherwise recovery fails, even if earlier ascent and return phases performed well.
- The first flight was called "basically successful" because orbit insertion met its standard, but Dong keeps the recovery portion separate and says the recovery score is zero when the booster does not land stably.
- [[ReuseFirstRocketDesign]] is a core route choice in the episode: liquid oxygen/methane and stainless steel are not treated as inherently more advanced, but as tactical choices that reduce insulation, soot-cleaning, and refurbishment burdens when reuse is designed in from the start.
- [[RecoveryRangePayloadTradeoff]] explains why the 2025 first flight used a conservative roughly 390 km recovery range while Dong says a fuller performance case would put the recovery site around 550-600 km away.
- The episode says the first flight landed with roughly 10 tonnes of unused propellant; if that reserve had instead supported payload delivery, Zhuque-3's payload could rise by roughly 2-3 tonnes in Dong's source-scoped estimate.
- The first flight's final failure is placed in the landing-ignition phase: the center engine started, but the other four engines had trouble establishing thrust before explosion and burning. Dong says official attribution should wait until all measured data support the cause.
- Dong says rapid iteration and high reliability are not opposites. In his view, the practical bottleneck is production efficiency and capacity, while domestic regulation appropriately focuses on not making the public absorb aerospace risk.
- LandSpace prepared production around a 2026 target of ten rockets and roughly one hundred engines, then expected later work on recovery, reflights, structural weight reduction, longer recovery range, the 12B engine, and a more complete Zhuque-3 configuration.
- For later reusable launch economics, Dong estimates [[Falcon9|Falcon 9]] turnaround at about 20 days and says LandSpace hopes eventually to shorten booster re-entry into service toward about seven days, which would also require more launch pads and recovery directions.
- The episode's longer-term branch says LandSpace had a 220-tonne full-flow engine test and a larger fully reusable rocket goal; at very large scale, Dong thinks landing legs may become less feasible and a tower-catch route like [[Starship]]'s chopsticks may be more deterministic.

## Key Quotes
> "工程上成就是成，败就是败" - Dong's recovery-result standard.

> "立在目标地点并停住一分钟" - the episode's simple recovery-success criterion.

> "修路" - Dong's analogy for the current commercial-launch infrastructure task.
