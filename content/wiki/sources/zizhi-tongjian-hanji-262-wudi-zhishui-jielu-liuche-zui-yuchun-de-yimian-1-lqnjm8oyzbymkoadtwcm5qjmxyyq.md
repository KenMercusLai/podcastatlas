# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》262｜武帝治水，揭露刘彻最愚蠢的一面（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode begins the Huzi flood-control sequence completed by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-262-wudi-zhishui-jielu-liuche-zui-yuchun-de-yimian-2-lu6y3wus1gnkiiewxl6a4d8uzim7|Hanji 262 part 2]]. It says the [[YellowRiver|黄河]] breached at Dunqiu and then Huzi in 132 BCE, flooding sixteen commanderies; [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] initially sent [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] and [[ZhengDangshiWesternHan|郑当时]] to mobilize one hundred thousand people, but the closure soon failed.

The episode attributes the subsequent long delay partly to Chancellor [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]], whose fief north of the river allegedly benefited while the breach sent water south, and partly to fangshi who reinforced his appeal to heaven's will. Its durable synthesis is that disaster becomes political when private advantage and unverifiable cosmology override continued repair while the exposed population lacks an effective channel to make prolonged suffering visible to the ruler. Exact hydrology, duration, responsibility, and the comparison with Rome remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- In spring 132 BCE, the Yellow River first breaches at Dunqiu and later at Huzi near Puyang, sending water toward Juye and into the Si and Huai river systems.
- The episode says flooding affects sixteen commanderies in fertile and densely populated areas.
- Han Wudi initially orders Ji An and Zheng Dangshi to mobilize one hundred thousand people to close the breach, but the repair soon fails under the force of the water.
- Tian Fen's fief at Shu County is said to lie north of the river and to benefit from the southward breach, giving him a private reason to oppose another closure attempt.
- Tian Fen invokes heaven's will, and fangshi reportedly reinforce that rationale; the episode treats this as the interaction of private interest, cosmological belief, and imperial credulity.
- The breach is said to remain unresolved for more than twenty years, imposing continuing loss on residents and reducing court tax revenue.
- The episode argues that survivors had no effective channel to transmit the scale and persistence of their suffering to the emperor, turning flood response into a problem of [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|court feedback collapse]] as well as hydraulic capacity.

## Key Quotes

> “屁股决定脑袋。” - the episode's opening phrase for a decision shaped by the decision-maker's own position and interest.

> “如果堵住决口，恐怕会违反天意，反而遭到报应。” - the rationale attributed to Tian Fen for leaving the breach open.

## Connections

- [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] - chancellor whose alleged fief benefit and heaven's-will argument anchor the private-interest critique.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler who first orders mass repair but then accepts prolonged non-intervention in the episode's account.
- [[YellowRiver|黄河]] - river whose breach joins hydrology, labor mobilization, regional exposure, taxation, and political responsibility.
- [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]] and [[ZhengDangshiWesternHan|郑当时]] - officials assigned to the initial repair mobilization.
- [[PrivateInterestDisasterNeglect|私利优先的灾害搁置]] - focused pattern in which the distribution of flood harm protects a powerful actor's property interest.
- [[CourtFeedbackCollapse|君臣反馈失灵]] - wider information failure invoked by the episode's claim that prolonged popular suffering did not reach the ruler effectively.
- [[NaturalDisasterPoliticalOmen|自然灾害政治征兆]] - adjacent reasoning field, qualified here because “heaven's will” is used to justify passivity rather than political correction.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode supplies the failed initial repair and long-delay mechanism before Part 2's broader institutional comparison and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-363-hanwudi-jiandao-shenxian-zhihou-de-chuanshuo-ll1skj3ygm2fop-pz1etkn3qexw5|Hanji 363]]'s 109 BCE closure.
- Hydraulic caution: the note does not reconstruct river channels, levee condition, repair methods, seasonal flow, or the feasibility of sustained intervention after the first closure failed.
- Causal caution: the fief benefit and fangshi advice support a political explanation in the episode, but they do not establish that Tian Fen, belief, or imperial neglect alone caused the entire delay.
- Quantitative caution: the sixteen-commandery scope, one-hundred-thousand labor force, twenty-four-year duration, tax loss, and casualty imagery require comparison with transmitted records and technical history.
- Institutional caution: the contrast between Roman representative channels and Han imperial centralization compresses different periods and institutions and does not by itself prove the flood-control outcome.
- Illustrative caution: the episode's reference to the television drama 《为了一句话》 conveys individual flood loss but is not evidence for the 132 BCE event.
