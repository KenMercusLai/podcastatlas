# 7 寻根溯源：古代东西方的大学、博士、硕士和学士

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] episode asks what universities are for by comparing Chinese institutions such as [[Taixue|太学]], 国子监, the imperial examination, and academies with the teacher-student guilds, charters, faculties, and degrees of medieval Europe. Its central warning is against treating every old center of advanced learning as a modern university: ancient and medieval institutions combined teaching, ritual, religion, official selection, professional licensing, mobility, and corporate self-government in different ways.

The episode grounds [[UniversityInstitutionalPluralism|university institutional pluralism]]. Universities have repeatedly served political formation, occupational entry, social mobility, inquiry, community life, and credential screening, but no single function exhausts the institution. It also develops [[MedievalUniversityGuildAutonomy|medieval university guild autonomy]] while qualifying a triumphalist reading: corporate privileges could protect teachers and students from local power, yet autonomous student bodies could also shelter hierarchy, violence, nationalism, or exclusion.

## Key Claims

- Modern primary-secondary-higher tiers and bachelor-master-doctor ladders should not be projected unchanged onto ancient learning institutions.
- Chinese central education joined classical learning, ritual order, political cultivation, state certification, and official formation; its institutional logic differed from a modern comprehensive university.
- The imperial examination widened mobility beyond hereditary elites while directing education toward a scarce official pathway, extending both [[ImperialExaminationOpportunityEquality|opportunity equality]] and credential competition.
- The episode presents medieval European universities as corporations of teachers and students that acquired charters, membership rules, examinations, teaching licenses, migration rights, and jurisdictional privileges.
- [[UniversityOfBologna|Bologna]] is associated with renewed legal study and demand for trained jurists, while [[UniversityOfOxford|Oxford]] and [[UniversityOfCambridge|Cambridge]] illustrate formation through scholar movement, conflict, and institutional separation rather than one uniform state plan.
- Early university autonomy was principally corporate and jurisdictional; it overlaps with but is not identical to modern [[AcademicFreedom|academic freedom]].
- University self-government is normatively mixed: it can protect inquiry and collective bargaining while also preserving closed status orders, violent practices, or reactionary politics.
- Nineteenth-century research universities and industrially oriented institutions added laboratories, seminars, journals, and applied science without eliminating older teaching, status, and occupational functions.
- Modern disciplinary efficiency can fragment integrated classical objects such as rites and the *Book of Songs*, reinforcing [[ModernDisciplinaryFragmentation|modern disciplinary fragmentation]].
- Bachelor, master, and doctor began as role and qualification terms whose meanings and hierarchy changed; their current ordering should not be read backward as timeless.
- Degree expansion can weaken credentials' distinguishing power and push university life toward examination and employment preparation, extending [[EducationSignalInflation|education signal inflation]].

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki is adopted. The source reinforces the bounded opportunity account of the imperial examination and the productive-loss account of disciplinary specialization.
- The comparison explicitly rejects institutional equivalence among ancient religious centers, Chinese state schools and academies, European guild universities, modern research universities, and contemporary mass higher education.
- Corporate autonomy is not treated as a synonym for unrestricted modern academic freedom, and neither is assumed to produce progressive politics.
- Claims about the oldest university, exact founding dates, institutional succession from 太学 to 国子监, charter rights, student numbers, curricula, degree etymologies, Roman-law rediscovery, Nazi participation, Soviet degree structures, and present-day campus management remain episode-attributed pending specialist verification.
