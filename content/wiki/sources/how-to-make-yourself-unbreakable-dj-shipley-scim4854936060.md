# How to Make Yourself Unbreakable | DJ Shipley

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview retired Navy SEAL [[DJShipley|DJ Shipley]] about combat loss, injury, medical retirement, medication dependence, marriage strain, and recovery. Its central practical contribution is [[MentalPosture]]: evening preparation, morning micro-wins, protected movement, attention boundaries, deliberate work-to-family transitions, and small controllable actions can improve readiness without making routine a cure for trauma or psychiatric illness. Shipley also describes supervised ibogaine and [[5MeODMT|5-MeO-DMT]] treatment as personally consequential, while the episode keeps those claims inside medical supervision, research, and [[PsychedelicIntegration|integration]] boundaries.

## Key Claims
- Shipley presents movement, sleep-supporting preparation, social connection, and controlled inputs as repeatable supports for mental health, especially when injury or unstructured time removes stabilizing activity.
- [[MentalPosture]] links small completed actions before stress arrives with greater perceived control, while toxic inputs, conflict, poor sleep, and rumination can make later burdens harder to carry.
- A phone-free drive home, calming music, rehearsed entry into the house, and a reciprocal evening walk are described as deliberate transitions from work intensity to family presence.
- Shipley's training history and injury recovery support [[FunctionalFitnessReadiness]] as broad, adaptable capacity: strength, pulling, carrying, rotation, running, mobility, and substitutions matter more than specialization alone.
- Combat loss, helplessness, accumulated injury, retirement, withdrawal, and family harm show that elite performance and compartmentalization can coexist with serious unprocessed distress.
- Shipley reports that supervised ibogaine and 5-MeO-DMT helped him confront guilt and change his life, but he explicitly rejects a cure-all interpretation and attributes durability to later relationship, environment, medication, and routine changes.
- The episode presents screening, monitoring, clinical support, and ongoing research as essential to psychedelic treatment; a personal account does not establish general safety, efficacy, or a self-treatment protocol.
