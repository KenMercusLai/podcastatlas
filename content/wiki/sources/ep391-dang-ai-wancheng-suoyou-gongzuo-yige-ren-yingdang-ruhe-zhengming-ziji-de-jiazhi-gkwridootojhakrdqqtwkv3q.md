## Summary

This [[WushichaYanjiusuo|无时差研究所]] episode uses travel claims, museum guidance, trip preparation, personalized family itineraries, creative work, data analysis, and multi-agent delegation to ask what remains valuable when AI performs more execution. Its durable answer is not a fixed list of protected occupations: people retain leverage by defining purposes, seeking source-proximate evidence, communicating specifications, setting acceptance standards, cultivating judgment, and learning how to assemble unfamiliar capabilities. The episode's “disposable” versus “durable” skill labels are treated as an investment heuristic rather than a settled forecast, because tool knowledge, model capability, and domain risk change over time.

## Key Claims

- Travel-insurance research, receipt organization, repeated form filling, museum explanation, and itinerary production illustrate AI's value in removing administrative and synthesis work while leaving submission and acceptance with the user.
- The speakers argue that everyday model-quality differences feel narrower than before and that product competition is shifting toward agent execution, latency, caching, cost, and model-routing ecosystems; this is practitioner interpretation rather than benchmark evidence.
- Vertical experts and highly proactive cross-domain generalists are presented as two advantaged profiles, joined by the ability to notice problems, define work, and direct AI toward a personally chosen result.
- [[DurableSkillInvestmentUnderAI]] separates rapidly automated operational procedures from capacities that continue to govern AI-assisted work: source seeking, information analysis, writing, taste, analytical questioning, communication, and self-directed learning.
- [[SourceFirstInformationPractice]] favors papers, official reports, company filings, repositories, and direct public statements over recommendation feeds and repeated secondary retellings, while still requiring scrutiny of authorship, incentives, omissions, and intended use.
- Writing is framed as operational specification: state the goal, context, structure, constraints, and observable standard instead of asking a model to “make it better.”
- In data analysis, AI can clean data and produce many patterns, but the user must decide which questions matter, what a result would change, and whether further abstraction is useful.
- [[HumanTasteCreativeBoundary]] is reinforced by examples where AI lowers production cost but topic choice, emotional beats, humor, historical checking, and final selection remain dependent on the creator's experience and taste.
- [[AIManagingAI]] appears in a “Leader” workflow where a top model decomposes tasks, routes work to differently capable subagents, and reviews delivery; the human still chooses objectives, resource tradeoffs, and final acceptance.
- The education discussion treats [[LearningHowToLearn]] as more durable than memorizing a temporary interface, while warning that premature delegation can prevent children or adults from building the judgment needed to use AI well.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted.
- The claim that model intelligence is approaching diminishing everyday returns is a speaker assessment, not a general result across benchmarks, domains, or future releases.
- “Disposable” does not mean worthless: programming, editing, office software, and other operational knowledge can remain essential for verification, high-stakes work, speed, or building the mental models needed to supervise automation.
- The episode's usage, subscription-cost, caching, product, and model-routing examples are supplied in a structured summary rather than bills, system logs, benchmark records, or product documentation, so they remain source-scoped.
