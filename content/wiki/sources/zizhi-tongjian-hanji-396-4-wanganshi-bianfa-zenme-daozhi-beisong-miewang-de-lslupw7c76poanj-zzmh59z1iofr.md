# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》396-4｜王安石变法怎么导致北宋灭亡的

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] short episode gives a strongly critical fiscal reading of the [[WangAnshiReforms|王安石变法]]. It argues that policies such as the market-exchange law and Green Sprouts law shifted from economic development and fairness rhetoric into [[StateCommercialMonopolyExtraction|官营商业垄断式汲取]], enriching the [[NorthernSong|北宋]] treasury while damaging merchants, market vitality, and ordinary people. The episode then links this logic to [[CaiJing|蔡京]], flower-and-rock transport, and a wider dynastic cycle in which state monopoly weakens commerce, worsens fiscal stress, and pushes burdens back onto the people.

## Key Claims
- The episode frames the Wang Anshi reforms as a response to fiscal difficulty in which the court saw wealthy merchants and private capital as extractable resources.
- The market-exchange law is described as quickly deforming in practice: official commerce used state capital and coercive authority to crowd out merchants, punish competitors, and dominate even small goods.
- The Green Sprouts law is interpreted as a state monopoly over credit, with the court capturing interest revenue while restricting private lending.
- The source distinguishes "rich state" results from "benefit the people" results: treasury gains can coexist with harm to merchants, consumers, and market circulation.
- [[CaiJing|蔡京]] is presented as a later new-party inheritor whose fiscal extraction and palace-supply projects, including flower-and-rock transport, accelerated Northern Song collapse.
- The final frame, attributed to Wei Sen, casts the case as part of a repeated Chinese economic cycle: post-founding tax relief and commercial recovery are followed by official monopoly, commercial damage, fiscal stress, heavier peasant extraction, and rebellion.

## Key Quotes
> "富国，不是利民" - the episode's summary contrast between treasury success and public benefit.

> "市场日益萧条，商户零落" - the source's description of the market result attributed to official commercial monopoly.
