## Summary
This [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode has [[XieDaoyu|谢道宇]] of [[HangzhouNormalUniversityAffiliatedHospital|杭州师范大学附属医院]] explain nosebleeds, nasal-hair and crust care, rhinitis, sinusitis, saline irrigation, nasal medicines, and allergen immunotherapy. Its durable contribution is a layered self-care boundary: protect the mucosa and use low-trauma measures for minor symptoms, distinguish allergic, infectious, non-allergic, and sinus-related patterns, and escalate heavy bleeding, persistent or severe disease, purulent discharge, marked head symptoms, or high-risk clinical contexts rather than relying on folk remedies or rapid-opening sprays.

## Key Claims
- Common anterior nosebleeds can follow dryness, rhinitis, or repeated picking; the episode favors direct pressure over tilting the head back and routes heavy, posterior, arterial, hypertension-associated, tumor-related, or post-radiotherapy bleeding to urgent care.
- Nasal hair and mucus crusts are part of a protective barrier. Visible hair may be trimmed conservatively, while shaving the cavity bare, repeated picking, or squeezing an infected lesion can create trauma or spread infection.
- Acute rhinitis is presented as commonly viral and time-limited, while recurrent or chronic symptoms may be allergic, infectious, dry or atrophic, or vasomotor; symptoms and duration are clues rather than complete diagnoses.
- Sinusitis becomes more likely when obstruction is joined by abundant purulent discharge, head pressure, smell loss, or related findings, with examination, endoscopy, or CT used according to clinical context.
- Isotonic saline irrigation may remove debris and secretions, but excessive concentration, pressure, or poor direction can dry or injure mucosa or affect the connected middle-ear pathway.
- Intranasal corticosteroids and antihistamines are framed as symptom-control tools rather than cures; fast topical vasoconstrictors can produce rebound or medication-induced rhinitis when used habitually.
- Allergic rhinitis is difficult to promise as permanently cured. Preseason treatment, selected allergen immunotherapy, and some nerve procedures may help particular patients, but response, recurrence, treatment duration, and long-term benefit vary.
