# 《资治通鉴·周纪》28丨韩昭侯的快乐你想象不到

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode develops two notices left compact in the prior episode: [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]] building the high gate in a bad national moment, and [[YueWangWujiang|越王无疆]]'s failed move against [[QiState|齐国]] before [[ChuState|楚国]] later defeats Yue. It then shifts to 333 BCE, where [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] treats several successions and the Chu-Qi war briefly but prepares the stage for [[SuQin|苏秦]], [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]], and [[ZonghengjiaDiplomacy|纵横家外交]]. The episode's strongest synthesis is that [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]] was not merely title exchange: by changing visible hierarchy, it triggered [[StatusOrderBacklash|名分秩序反弹]] from Chu, Zhao, Yan, and potentially Lu.

## Key Claims
- In 334 BCE, [[HanZhaohou|韩昭侯]] builds a high gate after [[QinState|秦国]] has attacked [[HanState|韩国]] at [[YiyangWarringStates|宜阳]] and after Han has suffered drought, making the project a case of [[UntimelyExtravagance|时绌举赢]].
- [[QuYijiu|屈宜臼]]'s criticism treats "time" as political circumstance rather than calendar date: a ruler must ask whether the state's condition can bear the action.
- The episode reads the high-gate story as a ruler-ethics warning: visible building pleasure becomes politically damaging when war, drought, and popular burden should command attention.
- [[YueWangWujiang|越王无疆]] attacks Qi in the same 334 BCE cluster, is diverted by Qi diplomacy, and suffers defeat; the episode stresses that this is not Yue's immediate extinction.
- [[ChuState|楚国]] defeats [[YueState|越国]] in this period, but Yue remains substantial and is destroyed by Chu only more than twenty years later.
- In 333 BCE, [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] records six events, including several succession notices and a Chu-Qi battle, but gives the fullest narrative attention to the coming [[SuQin|苏秦]] and [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] material.
- [[ChuWeiwang|楚威王]] attacks Qi at Xuzhou, fulfilling the pressure that [[HuiShi|惠施]] expected when he advised [[WeiState|魏国]] to defer to Qi.
- The backlash against Qi is broader than a Chu-Qi interest dispute: Zhao and Yan also pressure Qi, and Lu initially leans toward joining Chu.
- The episode frames this as a dignity and rank problem. Qi and Wei's mutual royal recognition made other lords feel that two peers had suddenly promoted themselves above the group.
- [[ZhangChou|张丑]] warns [[TianYing|田婴]] not to accept Wei's "crown" because it would make Qi appear to slight powers such as Qin and Chu.
- Tian Ying ignores Zhang Chou, and the later pressure on Qi and siege of Xuzhou match Zhang Chou's warning.
- Wei's refusal to help Qi during the Chu attack becomes a sequel hook: the state that helped create the title scene does not share Qi's immediate military cost.

## Key Quotes
> "时绌举赢" - the phrase the episode uses for extravagance during hardship.

> "齐王、魏王会于徐州以相王。" - the earlier chronicle notice whose consequences this episode develops.

## Contradictions

- The episode qualifies the prior [[XuzhouMutualKingship|徐州相王]] material by showing the backlash it produced; this extends rather than reverses the earlier reading of [[HuiShi|惠施]]'s low-posture tactic.
