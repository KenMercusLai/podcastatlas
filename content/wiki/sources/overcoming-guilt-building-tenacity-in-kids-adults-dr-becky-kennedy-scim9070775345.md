# Overcoming Guilt & Building Tenacity in Kids & Adults | Dr. Becky Kennedy

## Summary
In this [[HubermanLab]] conversation, [[AndrewHuberman]] and clinical psychologist [[BeckyKennedy]] extend sturdy parenting into emotional responsibility, guilt, shame, technology, and learning. Kennedy distinguishes value-violating guilt from absorbing another person's distress, argues that children need truthful and regulated adults rather than emotional perfection, and treats repair, stories, and questions as routes back to connection and learning. The later discussion develops [[FrustrationToleranceLearningSpace]] through puzzles, sports, school, and small first steps, while Huberman supplies source-scoped links to stress chemistry, sleep, dopamine, and [[Neuroplasticity]].

## Key Claims
- Children can witness adult sadness without becoming responsible for it when the adult supplies an age-appropriate story and clearly retains responsibility for regulation.
- Empathy means noticing and caring about another person's feelings; it does not require taking ownership of those feelings or changing every boundary that produces distress.
- Useful guilt follows conduct that violates one's values, whereas “not guilt” is another person's disappointment or turbulence being treated as one's own moral verdict.
- Authority remains relational when adults explain their values, listen seriously, hold necessary limits, and repair after yelling or other mistakes.
- Shame isolates the person and blocks learning; a generous interpretation, personal story, or open question can preserve accountability while making honesty safer.
- Capability grows when people experience themselves moving through manageable difficulty rather than being rescued from every frustration or succeeding immediately.
- Frustration belongs inside the learning space and can be reduced incrementally; quitting requires context rather than a universal rule that stopping is weak or persisting is always right.

## Key Quotes
> “I am the pilot, not the turbulence.” — Kennedy's grounding phrase for retaining responsibility during another person's emotional intensity.

> “Being nervous means you care.” — a short story that makes arousal understandable without promising its removal.

> “The first step is not small enough.” — the episode's test when a difficult task still feels impossible to begin.
