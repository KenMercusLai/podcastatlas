# 708. The Terror: The Reign of Robespierre (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[MaximilienRobespierre|Maximilien Robespierre]] from an anxious, academically gifted Arras childhood and an early reputation for legal principle into the [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety|Committee of Public Safety]]. It argues that his incorruptible public character, Rousseauian faith in a virtuous people, and tendency to combine France's crises into one conspiracy gave exceptional moral authority to emergency government.

The episode then shows the Terror becoming explicit state policy in September 1793. The [[LeveeEnMasse|levée en masse]], [[LawOfSuspects|Law of Suspects]], expanded [[RevolutionaryTribunal]], suspended [[ConstitutionOf1793|Constitution of 1793]], and prosecution of the [[Girondins]] joined democratic and egalitarian promises to centralized repression, broadened suspicion, and politically required convictions.

## Key Claims

- Robespierre's early abolitionism, legal work for poor clients, support for male suffrage, education, progressive taxation, rights, and abolition of slavery make his later support for exceptional violence a political transformation rather than evidence of innate bloodlust.
- Rousseau supplied a language of popular virtue, general will, and exceptional interpretation through which Robespierre could distinguish the people's true interests from their immediate opinions.
- Robespierre treated war, revolt, scarcity, hostile journalism, military betrayal, and foreign influence as branches of one conspiracy, converting a clear crisis diagnosis into widening suspicion.
- His reputation as “the Incorruptible” let supporters transfer trust in his personal integrity to the Committee of Public Safety and its emergency measures.
- The [[ConstitutionOf1793|Constitution of 1793]] promised male suffrage and social provision but was suspended until the emergency ended, allowing democratic ends and authoritarian means to coexist without a firm limiting mechanism.
- The [[LeveeEnMasse|levée en masse]] mobilized the whole society for war and helped create the military capacity that preserved the Revolution.
- The [[LawOfSuspects|Law of Suspects]] widened arrest from identifiable acts to conduct, associations, speech, status, and insufficient patriotism.
- Dividing the [[RevolutionaryTribunal]] into four chambers and increasing committee influence over judges and jurors centralized political justice and made acquittal rarer.
- At the Girondin trial, Robespierre's proposal that jurors decide whether their consciences had heard enough displaced ordinary evidentiary procedure while retaining the form of a trial.
- The Terror may have helped the Republic survive immediate military and civil threats, but effectiveness did not resolve the injustice of punishing innocent people or excluding opponents from citizenship and legal protection.

## Key Quotes

> “Terror is the order of the day.” - Bertrand Barère's 5 September proclamation as presented by the episode.

> “Liberty, what crimes are committed in your name.” - [[MadameRoland|Madame Roland]] before her execution, as reported by the episode.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with [[707-the-terror-an-assassination-in-paris-part-1-glt6580043397]]. Part 1 explains the improvised formation of emergency institutions; this episode shows their later centralization and explicit use as state terror.
- Robespierre's opposition to indiscriminate provincial killing and rejection of Couthon's proposed mass deportation from Lyon qualify, but do not erase, his support for executions and exceptional government.
- The episode's estimate of roughly 17,000 formal executions, its much larger Vendée comparison, and claims about the Terror's military effectiveness are narrative-level figures and judgments rather than audited totals or proof that terror alone saved the Republic.
- The Constitution of 1793 vote total, the reduction in acquittals, biographical psychology, alleged private motives, reported dialogue, and the causal weight assigned to Rousseau remain source-scoped.
- Robespierre's sole authorship of the Terror is explicitly rejected; the episode nevertheless treats his integrity, ideas, and committee authority as central to its legitimation.
