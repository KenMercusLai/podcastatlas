# Relationships 2.0: Why Did You Do That?

## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode connects [[LianeYoung]]'s research on [[TheoryOfMindMoralJudgment]] with [[FredLuskin]]'s practical account of forgiveness. Its first branch shows that people use beliefs and intentions to distinguish accidents from attempted harm, but outcomes, taboo, political identity, and group loyalty can distort that process through [[BiasedIntentionAttribution]]. Its second branch develops [[ForgivenessWithoutReconciliation]] and [[AccountableSelfForgiveness]]: releasing corrosive resentment need not erase danger, restore contact, or excuse wrongdoing, while forgiving oneself requires acknowledgment, remorse, apology, repair where possible, and concrete prevention of repetition.

## Key Claims
- [[TheoryOfMindMoralJudgment]] develops during childhood and supports conversation, storytelling, trust, threat assessment, and moral evaluation by representing mental states that can differ from reality.
- Innocent intent can mitigate blame for accidental harm, and malicious intent can create culpability without a harmful outcome; catastrophic outcomes and taboo violations can nevertheless overwhelm intention information.
- The episode reports right temporoparietal-junction imaging and stimulation studies as evidence that intention processing contributes causally to moral judgment, while also noting cultural, environmental, and unconscious influences.
- [[BiasedIntentionAttribution]] lets people explain their own or their group's aggression as protection while reading similar out-group conduct as hatred or attack.
- Charitable interpretation can improve ambiguous interactions when it remains a hypothesis rather than a demand to ignore observable risk.
- [[ForgivenessWithoutReconciliation]] separates releasing bitterness from renewed contact, restored trust, denial of harm, or surrender of protective boundaries.
- [[AccountableSelfForgiveness]] follows honest acknowledgment, remorse, apology, amends where possible, and corrective action rather than bypassing responsibility through reassurance.
