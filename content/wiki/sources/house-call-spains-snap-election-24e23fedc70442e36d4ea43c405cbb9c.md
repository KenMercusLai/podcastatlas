# House call: Spain’s snap election

## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode examines three systems under pressure. [[PedroSanchez]] calls an early Spanish election around housing after his government loses a tenant-protection decree; shrinking American cattle herds raise beef prices while compressing processor margins; and animal tests show that a degradable battery can power swallowable medical devices, though human safety and useful operating life remain unresolved. The episode therefore connects [[HousingScarcityElectoralAgendaSetting]], [[CattleCycleProcessorMarginSqueeze]], and [[BiodegradableIngestibleBatteries]] without treating any of their early figures or forecasts as settled beyond the source date.

## Key Claims
- The episode says [[PedroSanchez]] called a late-November snap election while trailing in the polls, betting that housing anger could displace corruption, migration, and coalition weakness as the campaign's central issue.
- It cites a Bank of Spain estimate of a 750,000-home shortage and contrasts about 80,000 annual completions with roughly 700,000 additional households attributed to immigration over three years.
- Sanchez's defeated decree would have extended rental contracts, temporarily blocked eviction of vulnerable tenants, and barred commercial firms from buying homes; the episode also warns that rent controls can reduce rental supply and hurt new tenants.
- The source says the combined Spanish right led the combined left by 15 points about seven and a half weeks before voting, while keeping the result open because Sanchez had recovered during the 2023 campaign.
- The beef segment argues that a smaller cattle herd, slow bovine reproduction, and ranchers' reluctance to rebuild explain high U.S. beef prices better than processor market power alone.
- It says cattle costs were rising faster than retail beef prices, leaving processors with excess plant capacity and shrinking margins; Tyson was reported to expect a $775 million annual loss in beef and to have removed about 15% of its processing capacity.
- The episode treats the Ranchers First initiative as too small to change national beef prices materially because smaller slaughterhouses, direct sales, and wolf protection do not quickly rebuild the herd.
- The medical-technology segment says a Milan team made a low-power edible battery from food-derived materials, while an MIT design used magnesium, gel, reactive paper, and a temporary wax coating to produce more power and then break down.
- In pigs, the degradable battery powered a radio-frequency tracking tag and a gut-motility stimulator; after about three days intestinal fluid penetrated the coating and the structure disappeared.
- Human use remains years away because researchers must identify the degradation products, test intestinal irritation, increase power, and extend useful operating time.

## Key Quotes
> "ordinary people versus property speculators" — the episode's description of Sanchez's intended election frame.

> "price-takers" — the segment's characterization of large meatpackers buying cattle and selling beef at market prices.

> "what the battery becomes" — the unresolved safety question after the device degrades inside the gut.
