# 12 Days: Death of Edward the Confessor and the Dreyfus Affair

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses 5 January to pair two political crises: [[EdwardTheConfessor|Edward the Confessor]]'s death in 1066 and the public degradation of [[AlfredDreyfus|Alfred Dreyfus]] in 1895. The first half treats a childless king's ambiguous succession management as the immediate opening of the [[NormanConquestOfEngland|Norman Conquest]], while the second presents the [[DreyfusAffair|Dreyfus Affair]] as a wrongful conviction sustained by antisemitism, military institutional pride, forged evidence, and polarized ideas of French patriotism.

## Key Claims

- Edward's childlessness left royal blood, Witan choice, political networks, military capacity, and reported deathbed words to compete rather than identify an uncontested successor.
- The source treats Edward as culturally both Norman and Anglo-Saxon and argues that tension between his royal lineage and Godwin family power shaped the succession problem.
- Edward's reported instruction that Harold should hold the kingdom could support either kingship or stewardship for the young Edgar Atheling, preserving ambiguity rather than settling the succession.
- [[HaroldGodwinson|Harold Godwinson]]'s coronation on the day of Edward's funeral suggests advance coalition-building, including marriage into the family of Edwin and Morcar, as well as urgent vacancy management.
- Dreyfus, a Jewish French artillery officer from Alsace, was publicly degraded after being wrongly convicted of passing military information to Germany and was then imprisoned on Devil's Island.
- French defeat in 1870-71, fear of Germany, Dreyfus's Alsatian origin, and [[RacialAntisemitism|antisemitism]] made him a convenient suspect; officers then protected the conviction with forged evidence even after Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy emerged as the actual culprit.
- The affair became a political identity test because accepting Dreyfus's innocence required accepting that the army had lied, while Émile Zola and other supporters framed the case around justice rather than institutional honor.
- A pardon ended Dreyfus's imprisonment without first clearing him; full exoneration came in 1906, and he later served during the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]].
