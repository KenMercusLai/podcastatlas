# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》194｜史上第一起人彘之刑，虐杀先帝宠妃（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode centers on [[LuZhi|吕雉]]'s torture of [[QiJi|戚姬 / 戚夫人]] into a “human swine” and asks how succession fear, [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s preference for Qi Ji, and the threatened replacement of [[LiuYing|刘盈]] by [[LiuRuyi|刘如意]] shaped the background to the violence. The source explains that background without treating it as an excuse: killing a rival would already be revenge, while deliberately prolonging suffering crosses the episode's [[RevengeProportionalityBoundary|复仇比例边界]].

The second line contests [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s criticism that Liu Ying had “small love but no great righteousness” because he withdrew from government and harmed himself after seeing the victim. The host instead reads Liu Ying's compassion, refusal to retaliate against former rivals, and withdrawal as a mixture of humane restraint, despair, and powerlessness before a dominant mother. The emotional diagnosis and causal account remain source-scoped; the firmer contribution is the conflict between rulerly duty, filial constraint, personal trauma, and bounded retaliation.

## Key Claims

- The episode attributes Lu Zhi's hatred and fear to Liu Bang's favor toward Qi Ji, repeated pressure to replace Liu Ying with Liu Ruyi, and the perceived survival risk to the established empress-crown-prince branch.
- That background explains a political and psychological setting but does not excuse the “human swine” torture; the host treats suffering beyond killing as revenge that has exceeded any defensible limit.
- Sima Guang criticizes Liu Ying for responding to his mother's cruelty by abandoning state business and damaging his health, calling this concern for “small love” without “great righteousness.”
- The host rejects that binary and presents compassion as a necessary basis for larger moral responsibility, while stressing Liu Ying's limited ability to reverse an accomplished atrocity or strip power from his mother without creating a filial-political crisis.
- Liu Ying's refusal to hate or retaliate against Qi Ji and Liu Ruyi, despite their place in the succession threat against him, is treated as evidence of humane restraint rather than merely weakness.
- The episode distinguishes “repaying resentment with straightness” from unlimited vengeance: redress should be proportionate, and power withheld from revenge can itself be a moral achievement.
- After the central discussion, the episode briefly records a Zhao enfeoffment change and the beginning of work on the northwestern section of [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]]'s walls.

## Key Quotes

The supplied episode document is a structured summary rather than a verbatim transcript, so no sentence is promoted as an exact quotation. The central reported formulations are Sima Guang's “有小爱而无大义” judgment and the ethical contrast between “以德报怨” and “以直报怨.”

- [[LiuYing|刘盈]], [[SimaGuang|司马光]], and [[FilialPietyLaws|孝道法制]] - the episode tests imperial duty and remonstrance against maternal authority, filial obligation, and an already completed atrocity.

## Contradictions

- No settled factual contradiction is adopted. The source reinforces existing pages that preserve Lu Zhi's dynastic protection, governing capacity, and cruelty together rather than allowing one dimension to erase another.
- The psychological claim that prolonged succession pressure transformed Lu Zhi's personality is interpretive and does not establish an exclusive cause of her violence.
- Liu Ying's withdrawal may be read as humane protest, despair, incapacity, or failure of rulerly duty; the source explicitly contests Sima Guang but does not resolve that normative disagreement as fact.
