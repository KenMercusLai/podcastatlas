# Vol.268 两个劳斯莱斯

## Summary
This [[ShangyeJiushiZheyang|商业就是这样]] episode starts from the double-R logo on aircraft engines and reconstructs why today's [[RollsRoyce|Rolls-Royce]] engine company and [[RollsRoyceMotorCars|Rolls-Royce Motor Cars]] share a name but became separate businesses. Its core case is the [[RollsRoyceRB211|RB211]] program: Rolls-Royce took an exclusive [[LockheedL1011TriStar|Lockheed L-1011 TriStar]] engine contract that combined ambitious performance promises, fixed pricing, delay penalties, and immature technology. The episode then follows the consequences into British nationalization, Lockheed's U.S. bailout, overseas bribery around Japanese aircraft sales, [[TanakaKakuei|Tanaka Kakuei]], and the legal aftermath that helped make the [[ForeignCorruptPracticesAct|Foreign Corrupt Practices Act]] possible.

## Key Claims
- The aircraft-engine and luxury-car branches began inside the same Rolls-Royce company, but the 1971 RB211 crisis split the strategic aero-engine assets from the car business.
- [[CharlesRolls]], [[HenryRoyce]], and [[ClaudeJohnson]] formed an early sales-engineering-commercial triangle: Rolls brought elite customers and flying enthusiasm, Royce brought engineering credibility, and Johnson helped turn that into a luxury brand.
- [[RollsRoyceRB211|RB211]] concentrated risk through a fixed-price, exclusive engine deal with [[LockheedCorporation|Lockheed]] for the [[LockheedL1011TriStar|L-1011]], while performance requirements and penalty clauses left Rolls-Royce with little room for delay or redesign.
- The program's three-shaft architecture and carbon-fiber fan blades were technologically ambitious, but the blade failure in bird-strike testing forced a return to titanium and worsened the schedule and cost problem.
- [[FixedPriceEngineeringRisk]] became visible when RB211 development cost rose far beyond the initial estimate and each engine was expected to cost more to build than the contract price.
- The British government rescued the aero-engine business because it was also a defense-industrial capability, making [[IndustrialCapabilityBailout]] a better description than an ordinary commercial rescue.
- [[AirframeEngineLockIn]] meant Lockheed could not easily replace RB211 after the TriStar had been designed around it, so contract clauses could not fully separate the aircraft maker's fate from the engine maker's.
- The TriStar arrived later than [[McDonnellDouglasDC10|DC-10]], sold fewer aircraft than its apparent break-even target, and pulled [[LockheedCorporation|Lockheed]] into a separate U.S. loan-guarantee fight.
- The episode says RB211 later became technically valuable after the 1973 oil shock because fuel efficiency mattered more, helping [[RollsRoyce|Rolls-Royce]] become one of the major civil aero-engine suppliers.
- The [[LockheedBriberyScandal]] segment links overseas aircraft sales to [[Marubeni]], [[KodamaYoshio|Kodama Yoshio]], [[AllNipponAirways|All Nippon Airways]], [[TanakaKakuei|Tanaka Kakuei]], and a wider U.S. investigation into foreign bribery by defense contractors.
- The episode's claims about U.S. dissatisfaction with Tanaka's diplomacy and oil-crisis positioning are explicitly treated as source-scoped political interpretation rather than proved causation.
