# 620. The Nazis at War: Hitler Strikes West (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the opening [[PhoneyWar]] after Germany and the Soviet Union partitioned Poland. It explains [[AdolfHitler]]'s demand for a rapid western offensive through struggle ideology, confidence in high-risk gambles, fear that time favored Britain and France, and a [[NaziRearmamentWarEconomy|war economy]] vulnerable to blockade, while Allied trauma and hopes of pressure without another mass offensive encouraged delay.

The second half reconstructs [[GeorgElser]]'s independent November 1939 bomb plot. Hitler escaped the Munich beer-hall explosion by thirteen minutes; the attack killed eight people and injured more than sixty. Public sympathy for Hitler, together with the narrow escape, weakened the immediate [[GermanMilitaryOppositionToHitler|army coup prospect]] and reinforced Hitler's belief that providence, popularity, and his own judgment authorized further risk.

## Key Claims

- Britain and France declared war after Poland's defeat but initially avoided a major western offensive; a brief French Saar advance ended in withdrawal.
- Hitler's 6 October peace language coexisted with orders for Fall Gelb because he treated struggle and expansion as fundamental, feared blockade and Allied rearmament, and believed France had to be defeated before Britain could be isolated.
- Senior army commanders doubted Germany was ready, yet hierarchy, personal dependence, public support for Hitler, and uncertainty about junior officers prevented private opposition from becoming a reliable coup coalition.
- Elser acted independently, spent months preparing a timed bomb inside the Bürgerbräukeller, and missed Hitler only because Hitler spoke and departed earlier than expected.
- The regime initially suggested British responsibility, while Elser's border arrest and the objects found on him led to interrogation, torture, confession, imprisonment, and execution at Dachau in April 1945.
- The failed assassination generated sympathy for Hitler, discouraged army plotters, and gave Hitler a providential interpretation of survival that strengthened his confidence before the western campaign.
- The episode applies [[WorkingTowardsTheFuhrer|working towards the Führer]] beyond persecution: broad ideological direction and anticipated wishes could radicalize policy without detailed orders for every act.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode extends the existing prewar rearmament account by making blockade vulnerability and the perceived cost of delay explicit.
- Hitler's October peace offer is presented as incompatible with a durable settlement because western attack planning continued; its sincerity and any counterfactual Allied response remain interpretive.
- The episode distinguishes conservative-nationalist military opposition from democratic resistance and does not treat private hesitation as proof of a viable or morally comprehensive anti-Nazi coalition.
- Timings, casualty totals, public reaction, commanders' private motives, coup feasibility, Elser's precise political commitments, and claims about providential belief remain episode-attributed or source-scoped.
