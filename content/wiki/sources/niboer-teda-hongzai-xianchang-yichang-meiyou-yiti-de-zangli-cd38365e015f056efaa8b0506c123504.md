# 尼泊爾特大洪災現場：一場沒有遺體的葬禮

## Summary
This [[DuanwenNewsPodcast|端聞 / 端傳媒新聞播客]] episode follows the human and institutional aftermath of the August 26, 2026 ice-rock collapse and flood moving from Tibet into [[Nepal]]. It deepens the existing [[HimalayanGlacialFloodRisk]] account through families conducting body-absent funerals, temporary burial and DNA identification, survivors displaced again after the 2015 earthquake, damaged hydropower and roads, a failed [[CrossBorderDisasterEarlyWarning|cross-border warning chain]], and an unresolved [[LossAndDamageClimateFinance|loss-and-damage finance]] request. The episode's figures remain provisional and sometimes internally inconsistent, so its strongest contribution is the connection among warning time, repeated displacement, identification, ritual, infrastructure, and climate justice rather than a final disaster ledger.

## Key Claims
- The episode says an ice-rock collapse in the highlands near the Nepal-Tibet border sent mud, rock, and water into Nepal's Rasuwa district, damaging border settlements, hydropower facilities, roads, bridges, schools, and hospitals.
- Families whose relatives remained missing began conducting Hindu funerals with kush-grass figures in place of bodies, showing how mass disappearance interrupts both identification and customary mourning.
- Nepal used temporary burial for unidentified remains after photographing bodies and preserving teeth, hair, and other samples for later DNA matching and family return, adding [[DisasterVictimIdentificationAndBodyAbsentMourning]] to the disaster-response branch.
- Some survivors had already spent roughly a decade in temporary riverside housing after the 2015 earthquake, so the flood turned incomplete reconstruction into repeated displacement and renewed bereavement.
- Centralized relief distribution reduced duplication in principle but roads, access, and an initial rescue focus left aid far from some survivors; the episode cites an assessment that at least 22,000 children urgently needed water, sanitation, and health support.
- Hydropower dependence amplified the economic shock: the episode says twelve plants were destroyed and describes Nepal's electricity trade with India and Bangladesh as an important national economic pillar.
- Nepal received warning only after the flood had entered the country, leaving roughly sixteen minutes for local response; earlier proposals for a Nepal-China monitoring and warning system had remained unimplemented.
- The episode frames the disaster through climate justice: Nepal contributes little to global emissions but bears severe warming-linked losses, while its emergency request to the UN loss-and-damage mechanism had not produced timely support.
- The flood also damaged cultural continuity by killing pilgrims traveling after Janai Purnima and disrupting traditions centered on Gosaikunda Lake.

## Key Quotes
> "活下来只是为了在悲伤中度过余生" - the episode's rendering of survivor Budi's account after losing twelve relatives.

> "气候正义" - the episode's frame for the mismatch between Nepal's emissions contribution and disaster burden.

## Contradictions
- The episode extends rather than overturns [[after-the-flood-nepals-ongoing-rescue-6a969d560cefab2730527078]], adding a later human-recovery and climate-finance view of the same August 26, 2026 disaster.
- The two episodes give different missing-person totals, and the newer transcript's statements about fund contributions, Nepal's request, and the claim that available money was less than one tenth of need do not reconcile arithmetically. Casualty, loss, aid, and finance figures therefore remain source-scoped rather than settled wiki facts.
- The transcript contains likely machine-translation errors in river and personal names and presents little Chinese-side evidence or response; cross-border responsibility should not be inferred beyond the documented warning-system gap.
