# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》456-2｜这两位圣人的论道，藏着人类的终极秘密

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places [[Confucius|孔子]]'s first visit to [[Laozi|老子]] inside the broader [[AxialAgeCivilizationalAwakening|轴心时代文明觉醒]] frame. It presents the Lu-to-Luoyi journey as both admiration for Zhou ritual learning and a personal request for instruction, then centers Laozi's riverbank teaching on life-death naturalness, [[DaoistNaturalWuwei|道家自然无为]], non-contention, and [[DaoistWaterVirtue|道家水德]].

The episode's practical synthesis is that Laozi's first correction is about Confucius's state of being before it is about doctrine: a person who wants to serve the world must first reduce sharp judgment, argumentative exposure, pride, desire, and self-display. Confucius's later "dragon" comparison makes Laozi a teacher whose depth cannot be grasped by ordinary capture metaphors.

## Key Claims
- The episode uses Karl Jaspers's axial-age concept to frame Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Shakyamuni Buddha, Confucius, Laozi, Mozi, and the Hundred Schools as part of a wider civilizational breakthrough between roughly 800 and 200 BCE.
- Confucius is said to visit Luoyi in 518 BCE / Duke Zhao of Lu year 24, at age thirty-three, because he admires Zhou ritual and already treats Laozi as a teacher.
- The source presents several traditions about Confucius meeting Laozi, including three-meeting and Zhuangzi multi-dialogue accounts, but does not settle their historicity.
- Laozi warns Confucius that acute intelligence and eloquence become dangerous when used to attack others' faults or expose others' evils.
- At the Yellow River, Laozi turns Confucius's grief over time, disorder, and unrealized service into a teaching about being born from nature, dying into nature, and not letting fame, benefit, or anxious renyi pursuit disturb one's nature.
- Laozi argues that heaven, earth, sun, moon, stars, and living beings order themselves without forced command, so human governance and self-cultivation should follow natural principle instead of compulsive moral display.
- Laozi's "上善若水" teaching makes water the episode's concrete image for non-contention, humility, downwardness, softness overcoming hardness, and benefit without demand.
- The final warning asks Confucius to remove pride, excessive clever desire, and visible self-display; otherwise he will become intimidating and unusable.
- Confucius receives the teaching gratefully and later tells his disciples that Laozi is like a dragon riding wind and cloud, deep and hard to measure.

## Key Quotes
> "富贵者送人以财，仁义者送人以言" - Laozi's farewell frame before giving Confucius advice.

> "逝者如斯夫，不舍昼夜" - Confucius's riverbank lament that opens the nature-and-time teaching.

> "上善若水" - the phrase used to anchor the episode's teaching on water virtue.

> "吾今日见老子，其犹龙乎" - Confucius's later image for Laozi's depth.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is recorded.
- Source-scope caution: the episode treats Confucius's visit to Laozi as a meaningful philosophical tradition, while explicitly noting variant accounts; it should not be treated as a verified verbatim historical dialogue.
- Source-scope caution: the axial-age comparison and Buddhist "无我" bridge are interpretive framing devices, not claims that Confucian, Daoist, Greek, and Buddhist doctrines are identical.
