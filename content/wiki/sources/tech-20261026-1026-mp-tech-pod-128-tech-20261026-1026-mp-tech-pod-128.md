## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode follows [[DonnaGuthrie]] after she asks [[ChatGPT]] to draft her obituary and finds that two years of accumulated interaction let it produce an unexpectedly accurate account of her life. [[MichelleWong]] interprets the result through [[AIAsPersonalMirror]]: personalized AI reflects and amplifies what a user repeatedly supplies. Her own [[SyntheticYoungerSelfReflection]] project, built from childhood diaries, suggests a reflective use for such systems while the episode draws a firm [[HumanConnectionUnderAI]] boundary: AI may supplement introspection, but it should not replace relationships that include mutual care, criticism, disagreement, and accountability.

## Key Claims
- [[DonnaGuthrie]] regarded the AI-written obituary as unsettling but useful: a foundation her children could revise and make more humorous.
- The obituary's specificity appeared to come from prior interactions about Guthrie's education, creative work, gatherings, and interests rather than from a single prompt alone.
- [[MichelleWong]] describes personalized AI as a mirror that amplifies recurring user-supplied material, so the quality and emotional direction of the input shape the reflection.
- Wong found a diary-grounded conversation with a synthetic version of her 14-year-old self therapeutically useful because its answers were weighted toward her own earlier writing.
- Personalized reflection becomes dangerous when AI is the user's only outlet, especially where [[SycophanticAICompanionRisk]] reinforces a single frame or displaces human support.
- Human intimacy includes disagreement and productive tension; an agreeable chatbot cannot reproduce the reciprocity or accountability of a nourishing relationship.

## Key Quotes
> "AI as a mirror" - the episode's framing of personalized systems as reflections of recurring user input.

> "AI should never replace human relationships" - Wong's boundary for constructive use, as summarized by the episode.
