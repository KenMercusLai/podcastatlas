# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》111-1｜偌大的赵国竟亡于一个妓女之手

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode moves the Qinji sequence into 236 BCE, [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s eleventh year. It follows [[ZhaoState|赵国]] attacking [[YanState|燕国]], [[QinState|秦国]] using the opening to send [[WangJianQin|王翦]], [[HuanYiQin|桓齮]], and [[YangDuanhe|杨端和]] against Zhao, and Qin taking places including [[YeWarringStates|邺城]] and Anyang.

The episode's larger contribution is to bind the external war opening to Zhao's internal succession weakness. [[ZhaoDaoxiangwang|赵道襄王]] dies, [[ZhaoYoumiuWang|赵迁 / 赵幽缪王]] succeeds after [[ZhaoJia|赵嘉]] has been displaced, and the host frames this favorite-driven heir change as a major reason Zhao is now exposed under Qin pressure.

## Key Claims
- In 236 BCE, Zhao attacks Yan and takes Liyang, leaving itself vulnerable while the campaign is still underway.
- Qin uses that timing to attack Zhao with Wang Jian, Huan Yi, and Yang Duanhe; the episode presents the move as opportunistic but strategically intelligible.
- Wang Jian attacks Eyu and Liaoyang, while Huan Yi takes Ye and Anyang; the source treats this as Wang Jian's first full appearance on the historical stage.
- Wei's earlier transfer of Ye to Zhao is explained as a way to redirect Qin pressure, comparable to Feng Ting offering Shangdang to Zhao.
- A Yan envoy warns Ying Zheng that if Zhao absorbs Yan, a larger Zhao will become a harder Qin problem; the episode says Ying Zheng accepts the reasoning.
- The host uses "鹬蚌相争，渔人得利" to explain how Yan-Zhao conflict lets Qin benefit from weaker states' mutual attrition.
- Wang Jian is compared with [[BaiQi|白起]]: Bai Qi is treated as an unmatched battlefield technician with poor political sense, while Wang Jian is presented as more prudent and workplace-savvy.
- Zhao's loss is not only military. The death of Zhao Daoxiang Wang and accession of Zhao Qian turn an external threat into a succession-stability problem.
- Zhao Daoxiang Wang is said to have favored a woman of sex-worker background, displaced the original crown prince Zhao Jia, and installed her son Zhao Qian as heir.
- The episode says Zhao Qian's bad conduct was known in Handan, making his sudden accession a sign that Zhao is close to collapse.
- The closing mention of [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] is a brief series hook rather than a developed argument in this transcript.
