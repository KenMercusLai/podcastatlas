## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode discusses [[HarrisAlterman]] and [[DaveRoss]]'s [[FakeAISubwayAds]] project, which placed fake AI company ads around New York City's transit system to mimic vague, polished tech marketing. The episode treats the parody as a cultural diagnosis: much [[AIMarketingJargon|AI marketing jargon]] looks expensive and official while failing to explain what the company sells or who the message is for.

The guests describe their method as collecting real AI ad phrases and recombining them in a Mad Libs-like style. The result connects [[AISlop|AI slop]], public ad buys, investor-facing hype, and [[StartupLegitimacyTransfer|startup legitimacy transfer]]: the ads can be confusing to ordinary riders while still making a company look real, funded, and important.

## Key Claims
- [[FakeAISubwayAds]] included fake companies such as Froggle, Dennis, Cutlery.AI, Goofstump, Ziplink, and a fake Wireflow poster, using visual conventions that resemble real AI subway campaigns.
- [[HarrisAlterman]] says the idea came from riding the subway every day and seeing tech ads whose products were difficult to identify.
- [[DaveRoss]] says the project copied common design moves from real tech ads: solid-color backgrounds, simple clip art, similar fonts, a company name, a small logo, and a short tagline.
- The guests describe AI ad copy as built from repeated formula phrases, including "from blank to blank," "in the palm of your hand," and democratization language.
- The episode suggests that many public AI ads speak less to ordinary consumers than to investors, business buyers, recruits, or the broader hype machine.
- Public transit ads can create a legitimacy signal because riders may assume that a company buying visible ad space is real, funded, and commercially serious.
- The project went viral on [[Instagram]], with the source saying its video received more than 230,000 likes and thousands of comments.
- Alterman says someone from the MTA contacted him to say they could not keep putting up the posters, even while acknowledging that people enjoyed the joke.
- The comedians say they plan another round that may move beyond banners toward a subway art-installation format.
