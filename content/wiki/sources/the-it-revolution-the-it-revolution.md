## Summary

This paid-partnership [[RevisionistHistory]] episode has [[MalcolmGladwell]] discuss healthcare and retail transformation with representatives of [[BostonChildrensHospital|Boston Children's Hospital]], [[TractorSupplyCompany|Tractor Supply Company]], and [[TMobile]]. The cases connect private 5G, connected devices, clinical ambient AI, employee-facing mobile information, home care, and staged deployment to [[ConnectivityEnabledWorkflowRedesign]], while emphasizing that technology should improve patient, customer, and employee experience rather than become an end in itself. The source is strategically useful but does not independently test costs, outcomes, security, regulatory constraints, or alternatives to the promoted network architecture.

## Key Claims

- [[BostonChildrensHospital|Boston Children's Hospital]] describes easier access, patient self-service, and more home-based care as strategic priorities, including a prospective path for cancer infusions to update the electronic health record from home.
- [[HeatherNelson]] argues that hospital Wi-Fi can fail mobile clinical workflows and that private networks or designated slices can specify latency, capacity, and reliability for critical connections.
- Hospital connectivity must support clinicians' phones, workstations, ventilators, IV pumps, shift devices, and patients' devices while protecting patient data and limiting the attack surface.
- [[TractorSupplyCompany|Tractor Supply Company]] uses employee mobile tools to surface detailed product information at the point of customer service, especially where the right agricultural product affects productivity and profitability.
- [[ClinicalAmbientDocumentation]] can capture patient conversations, summarize them, extract action items, and reduce the need for clinicians to turn away and type, but the source supplies no accuracy, consent, privacy, or outcome evaluation.
- Tractor Supply tests technology in labs and then in roughly 100 receptive stores before expanding across approximately 2,200 Tractor Supply and 200 Petsense locations.
- [[HeatherNelson]] presents the CIO as a business and culture leader who must translate infrastructure into care, continuity, and operational outcomes rather than technical specifications.
- [[CallieField]] describes the burden on cancer patients who must coordinate fragmented doctors and systems, reinforcing the need for interoperable patient information and less repetitive administration.
- [[InfrastructureInvestmentOutcomeFraming]] helps technology leaders compete for funding by connecting invisible foundations to visible service failures and organizational priorities.
