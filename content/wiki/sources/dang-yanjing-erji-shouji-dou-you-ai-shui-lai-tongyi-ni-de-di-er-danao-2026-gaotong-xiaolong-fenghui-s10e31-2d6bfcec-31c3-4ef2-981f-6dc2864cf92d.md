## Summary
This [[WhatsNextKejiZaozhidao]] episode reports from the 2026 Snapdragon Summit and uses demonstrations and interviews to connect AI glasses, earbuds, phones, PCs, pendants, and rings into one prospective personal-AI system. Its central judgment is that the phone may lose its position as the only interaction surface while remaining a [[SmartphoneAIHub|coordination hub]] for identity, private context, connectivity, payments, permissions, and shared compute. The deeper bottleneck is [[CrossDevicePersonalMemory]]: turning continuous multimodal capture into user-controlled memory that can survive across devices and support a [[RememberUnderstandActLoop]] without collapsing privacy, interoperability, and product reliability into a conference demo.

## Key Claims
- The summit narrative shifts the interaction starting point from opening an app to expressing an intent that an agent can interpret and pursue across devices.
- Glasses are presented as a strong [[WearableAIAssistant|wearable AI]] edge because they can perceive the wearer's view, display information, and run some multimodal recognition offline, although current weight and bulk remain visible.
- The phone is unlikely to disappear soon because it still concentrates identity, preferences, private data, payment credentials, connectivity, and enough compute to support lighter accessories.
- Better CPU, NPU, and sensing capability does not by itself establish consumer demand. The episode finds no current AI application compelling enough to guarantee a mass upgrade cycle, especially as memory and device costs rise.
- [[CrossDevicePersonalMemory]] requires more than file transfer: a user changing devices should be able to preserve relevant memories, permissions, context, and unfinished tasks across brands and operating systems.
- [[Qualcomm]] presents personal AI as a [[RememberUnderstandActLoop|remember-understand-act]] progression in which systems first record context, then judge relevance and privacy, and finally suggest or perform an action.
- The source favors local retention of sensitive perception and memory, with cloud models reserved for harder reasoning or agent tasks that local hardware cannot complete effectively.
- The episode says [[ModularAI|Modular]]'s Mojo and MAX software stack may help models run across heterogeneous hardware, but distinguishes that deployment problem from the harder governance and protocol problem of portable personal memory.
- Real-world usefulness remains unproven because model reliability, battery life, software bugs, service integration, privacy authorization, and everyday product flow can all fail after a polished summit demonstration.

## Key Quotes
> "手机不会消失，但角色正在改变" — the episode's summary of the phone's move from sole interface to coordination hub.

> "记忆—理解—行动" — the three-stage personal-AI roadmap attributed to Qualcomm's summit presentation.

> "从峰会 Demo 到真实产品仍有距离" — the source's explicit product-readiness qualification.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode reinforces [[SmartphoneAIHub]] while qualifying a phone-exclusive interpretation: glasses, earbuds, PCs, and other devices can become primary sensing or interaction edges even if the phone remains the trusted coordination center.
- The claimed shipment decline, price increase, acquisition value, interoperability timetable, and platform ambitions are source-scoped statements from an event report. The source does not establish that open standards, seamless cross-brand memory, or a CUDA-like Qualcomm ecosystem already exist.
