# 《资治通鉴·周纪》71丨荀子的性本恶论

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]]'s death from [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-70-xihuan-huayang-zuosi-de-qiguo-junzhu-luuyasp5-nhnikdcozzaueosb-md|Zhouji 70]] as the entry point for [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s quotation from [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s Wangba chapter. It introduces Xunzi's biography, [[XingETheory|性恶论]], and ritual-law orientation, then turns the quotation into [[XunzianWangbaThreePaths|荀子王霸三分法]]: rule through ritual and righteousness can become kingly rule, rule through trust can become hegemony, and rule through expedient deception tends toward collapse. The episode's practical case is [[QiState|齐国]]: Qi Min Wang and [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君]] held a powerful state but did not convert that strength into durable moral, institutional, or trust-based order.

## Key Claims
- [[ZhouNanwang|周赧王]]'s thirty-first year, 284 BCE, is treated as a turning point because Qi Min Wang's death changes the Warring States balance after Qi's collapse.
- [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] uses [[Xunzi|荀子]] through [[SimaGuang|司马光]] to make the episode explicitly about "资治": power is a powerful instrument, but it also makes failed rulers unable to retreat safely into ordinary life.
- Xunzi is introduced as Xun Kuang, courtesy name Qing, a Zhao figure associated with [[JixiaAcademy|稷下学宫]], Lanling office, and the later text [[XunziText|《荀子》]].
- The episode frames Xunzi as advocating a combination of kingly and hegemonic statecraft, ritual and law, and as the Confucian figure commonly contrasted with [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] through [[XingETheory|性恶论]].
- In the quoted Wangba framework, a ruler who refuses unjust gain and innocent killing, and orders ministers, laws, and goals through ritual and righteousness, can make the state secure and move toward kingly rule.
- A ruler whose moral-ritual level is lower may still achieve hegemony if rewards, punishments, commands, and interstate agreements remain credible.
- Rule through profit, expedient manipulation, and broken trust creates internal estrangement and external suspicion; enemies despise such a state and allies doubt it.
- [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] and [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君]] become negative examples because they controlled a strong Qi but failed to use it as a base for ritual order, education, credibility, and public legitimacy.
- Qi's victories over Chu, Qin pressure, Yan, and Song made it look strong, but when Yan and Zhao attacked, the state collapsed quickly because power without order had already become brittle.
- The closing transition toward [[LeYi|乐毅]] seeking a worthy person near a Qi locality is kept as a next-story setup rather than developed into a full entity, because the transcript's names are noisy.

## Key Quotes
> "人性本恶" - the episode's shorthand for Xunzi's famous contrast with Mencius.

> "以义治国可以称王" - the host's summary of the first path in the Wangba framework.

> "以信治国可以称霸" - the host's summary of credibility as the second path.
