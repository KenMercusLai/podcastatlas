# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》114-2｜燕国刺杀秦始皇为何会选荆轲

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-1-jintian-liaoliao-yingzheng-dou-you-naxie-canren-shouduan-lmqky6g3qi9nklpdnye4lk5rrbz6|Qinji 114-1]] from [[ZhaoState|赵国]]'s collapse into [[YanState|燕国]]'s desperate response to [[QinState|秦国]] pressure. With [[WangJianQin|王翦]] already positioned against Yan and ordinary [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] repair judged too slow, [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] turns from [[JuWu|居武]]'s coalition-and-[[Xiongnu|匈奴]] proposal toward a plan to strike [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] personally.

The source answers why [[JingKe|荆轲]] is selected by emphasizing social identity rather than pure sword skill. [[TianGuang|田光]] presents Jing Ke as a worthy friend whose surface image as a wandering, wine-drinking, bookish侠士 hides the temperament needed for a death mission; Tian Guang's suicide then makes secrecy, trust, and moral pressure part of the recruitment mechanism.

## Key Claims
- [[WeiJingminWang|魏景湣王 / 魏增]] dies and [[WeiWangJia|魏王假]] succeeds, but the episode treats [[WeiState|魏国]] as already unable to reverse Qin's conquest pressure.
- After Zhao's fall, Yan faces a much more immediate Qin threat because Wang Jian remains forward near [[ZhongshanState|中山国]].
- Yan Taizi Dan's hostility toward Ying Zheng is presented as both personal and political: the two were childhood acquaintances, but Dan remembers later mistreatment in Qin and now faces a state-level crisis.
- Ju Wu proposes a slower strategic answer: rebuild coalition ties with Wei, Chu, and Qi, then seek support from the northern Xiongnu.
- Taizi Dan rejects that plan as too time-consuming and uncertain, showing how a weak state under urgent pressure may choose a high-risk shortcut over slow alliance repair.
- [[FanWuji|樊无忌]]'s flight to Yan intensifies the danger because sheltering a Qin fugitive gives Qin another reason to pressure Yan.
- Ju Wu advises sending Fan Wuji into Xiongnu territory, but Taizi Dan refuses on the grounds that he must protect someone who has entrusted himself to Yan.
- The assassination plan is framed as concentrating Yan's remaining strength against one person: if Ying Zheng dies, Qin may fall into disorder and Yan may gain time.
- Ju Wu does not endorse assassination directly; after failing to redirect Taizi Dan, he recommends Tian Guang as a wiser intermediary.
- Tian Guang recommends Jing Ke, whose value is not that he looks like the most fearsome professional swordsman, but that his identity as a wandering shi and侠士 makes him both morally legible and less suspicious.
- Jing Ke's earlier encounters with Ge Nie and Lu Goujian make him look evasive or timid, but the source separates ordinary conflict avoidance from the later ability to accept a fatal political mission.
- Jing Ke's Yan social world includes [[GaoJianli|高渐离]] and a dog butcher, linking him to a market-level侠客 circle rather than a formal court or military command.
- Tian Guang's suicide functions as both a secrecy guarantee and an implied rebuke: if Tian Guang can die for the matter, Jing Ke must live up to the义士 identity now assigned to him.
- Taizi Dan then binds Jing Ke with extremely high rank, housing, gifts, daily visits, and extreme displays of favor, making the assassination plan a political transaction as well as a moral commitment.
- The episode ends before the assassination attempt itself, at the point where Jing Ke tells Fan Wuji that his head can be used to approach the Qin king and also pursue revenge.

## Key Quotes
> "燕秦不两立" - Tian Guang's summary of Taizi Dan's problem in the private meeting.

> "旁若无人" - idiom the episode attaches to Jing Ke and Gao Jianli's public drinking, music, laughing, and crying in the Yan market.

> "集全国之力攻嬴政一人" - the source's framing of Yan's assassination logic.
