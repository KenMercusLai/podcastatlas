## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills 68-71 CE / Yongping 11-14 immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-740-rang-ni-tihuguanding-de-zhichang-zhihui-zhichang-bibei-lih5b-ighkyoycawm5c-dbrnuiew|Hanji 740]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-741-weishenme-gudai-xiongnu-zongyao-ruqin-zhongyuan-lllpgozgi6yd5pscw8z5ofidk5rp|Hanji 741]]. It moves from [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]]'s affectionate separation from [[LiuCangDongpingKing|东平王刘苍]] to [[AilaoKingLiuMao|哀牢王柳貌]]'s submission, [[WangJingBianquEasternHan|王景]]'s Bianqu/Huanghe works, and the opening fall of [[LiuYingChuKingEasternHan|楚王刘英]].

The durable synthesis is that early Yongping order contains both care and coercion: Mingdi can miss a brother-like royal kinsman, praise Liu Cang's "doing good" ethic, and mobilize prosperous-state resources for frontier and water-control projects, yet the Liu Ying case quickly moves from initial family leniency to suicide, ministerial shame, and the first stage of a [[CoerciveImplicationCascade|coercive implication cascade]] later developed in Hanji 740.

## Key Claims

- Mingdi's farewell to Liu Cang gives the ruler an affective layer before the later Yuanling dream and burial-restraint sources: imperial power does not remove private loneliness.
- Liu Cang's "为善最乐" answer makes him a moralized royal kinsman before the later Zhangdi-era adviser, farewell, illness, funeral, and memory layers.
- Ailao King Liu Mao's 69 CE submission shows southwestern frontier incorporation by county creation, road opening, and crossing the Lancang/Mekong corridor rather than by rebellion alone.
- Wang Jing's Bianqu works show high Yongping state capacity: the court can mobilize enormous labor and money after earlier rulers deferred repair because the people were exhausted by war.
- The water-control episode is not costless public-works triumphalism; the source foregrounds both technical success and vast fiscal outlay.
- Liu Ying's case begins with auspicious-object and prophecy-linked rebellion claims, Mingdi's initial refusal to execute a brother, and protective treatment of Liu Ying's children and mother.
- Yu Yan's suicide and the later deaths, exiles, and imprisonments show that the case begins turning from royal punishment into broader political fear before Hanji 740's fuller correction-and-review layer.
