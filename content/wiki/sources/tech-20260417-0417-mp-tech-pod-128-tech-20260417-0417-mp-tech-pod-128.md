## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] Bytes episode uses [[MariaCurie|Maria Currie]]'s analysis to connect three technology-policy and platform stories: [[OpenAI]] and [[Anthropic]] splitting over an [[IllinoisAILiabilityBill|Illinois AI liability bill]], [[Amazon]] buying [[Globalstar]] to strengthen satellite connectivity, and [[Spotify]] partnering with [[BookshopOrg|Bookshop.org]] to sell physical books through its app.

The episode's common thread is that technology companies are trying to occupy regulated or partially offline markets without fully leaving public consequences behind. [[StateAILiabilityShield]] asks when AI developers should be protected from user-caused harm, [[DirectToDeviceSatelliteConnectivity]] links satellites to emergency access and internet availability, and [[PhysicalBookPlatformBridge]] shows a digital audio platform trying to profit from print while still routing some demand toward independent bookstores.

## Key Claims
- [[Illinois]] lawmakers are considering a bill that would limit liability for large AI model developers when critical harms are caused by models, if the developer did not intentionally or recklessly cause harm and published a safety protocol.
- [[OpenAI]] testified in favor of the bill, while [[Wired]] reported that [[Anthropic]] wanted major changes or wanted the bill killed.
- [[MariaCurie|Currie]] says federal AI legislation appears unlikely to move, leaving states as the main arena for AI governance fights.
- Currie compares the Illinois approach to social media liability shields but says powerful AI models raise distinct catastrophic-risk questions, including cyberattacks on critical infrastructure and bioweapon creation.
- [[BillCunningham]] says final Illinois law is highly unlikely to include sweeping liability relief for AI developers.
- The episode says [[Amazon]] announced a stock-and-cash deal valued at about $11.6 billion to buy [[Globalstar]] and expand low-earth-orbit satellite capacity.
- Globalstar already partners with [[Apple]] on satellite service for some phones and watches, including emergency texting when terrestrial service is unavailable.
- The episode identifies this as [[DirectToDeviceSatelliteConnectivity|direct-to-device connectivity]] and says [[SpaceX]] currently dominates the market.
- More satellite-connectivity competition could lower prices, increase options, expand availability, and help more people get online.
- [[Spotify]] is allowing [[Android]] users in the [[UnitedStates|U.S.]] and [[UnitedKingdom|U.K.]] to buy physical books through its app via a [[BookshopOrg|Bookshop.org]] partnership.
- [[PageMatch]] is described as a Spotify feature that can match a reader's place between an audiobook and a physical book.
- The episode frames physical books as resilient because they support offline attention, screen reduction, annotation, and emotional attachment.
- The Spotify-Bookshop.org model may help independent bookstores if app-driven demand flows through Bookshop.org, but it also adds technology-sector competition to bookselling.

## Key Quotes
> "sweeping liability relief" - Cunningham's caveat about what final Illinois AI law is unlikely to include.

> "direct-to-device connectivity" - the episode's term for satellite service reaching phones and watches.

> "physical and digital parts are intertwined" - Currie's description of Spotify's book strategy.
