# 530. Hitler's War on Poland: Countdown to Armageddon (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[AdolfHitler]] from the post-Munich destruction of Czechoslovakia to the first planning for the [[InvasionOfPoland1939|invasion of Poland]]. It presents the [[OccupationOfCzechoslovakia1939|March 1939 occupation of Prague]] as the point at which territorial revision justified as German self-determination became openly imperial conquest, sharply weakening the British and French case for continued accommodation.

The episode then reconstructs [[Poland]]'s interwar vulnerability and refusal to become a German client through concessions over [[DanzigGdansk|Danzig]] and the Polish Corridor. The resulting [[BritishFrenchGuaranteeToPoland|British-French guarantee]] attempted deterrence despite limited capacity for direct aid, while Hitler's invasion planning and tentative turn toward [[JosephStalin]] prepared the diplomatic crisis continued in Part 2.

## Key Claims

- Hitler remained popular in early 1939 partly because rearmament and territorial gains had been achieved without a general European war, but public enthusiasm did not imply equal support for conquering non-German peoples.
- The Four Year Plan and arms spending produced resource, foreign-exchange, and inflationary pressure that made further seizure attractive without mechanically causing war.
- Hitler's racial-struggle ideology, desire for eastern living space, fear of delay, and disappointment at Munich made consolidation after the Sudetenland unlikely.
- Germany used Slovak separatism and threats against [[EmilHacha|Emil Hácha]] to dismantle the remaining Czech state and establish the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia.
- The occupation of Prague broke the self-determination rationale attached to earlier expansion and moved Britain and France from accommodation toward deterrence.
- Interwar Poland combined restored sovereignty and strong concern for independence with disputed borders, ethnic diversity, economic weakness, authoritarian government, and geographic exposure between Germany and the Soviet Union.
- German demands for Danzig, an extraterritorial corridor, and Polish entry into the Anti-Comintern Pact threatened to turn Poland into a client state; Polish leaders refused because trade access and foreign-policy autonomy were at stake.
- The 31 March guarantee raised the cost of attack but offered no ready mechanism for saving Poland, and Hitler responded by ordering Fall Weiss while his circle began considering a bargain with Stalin.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The source supplies the missing bridge between the earlier Nazi-in-power sequence and Parts 2–3 of the Poland series.
- Prague is treated as a decisive collapse of the political case for appeasement, not as proof that every earlier compromise had been irrational or that Britain and France immediately possessed an effective military rescue plan.
- Economic pressure reinforced ideological and strategic incentives but is not treated as a single sufficient cause of war.
- Popularity, public attitudes, economic figures, threats and reported dialogue, demographic proportions, leaders' private motives, and counterfactual deterrence outcomes remain episode-attributed or source-scoped.
