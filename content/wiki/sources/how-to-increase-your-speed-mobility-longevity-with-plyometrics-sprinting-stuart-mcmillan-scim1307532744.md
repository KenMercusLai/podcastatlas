# How to Increase Your Speed, Mobility & Longevity with Plyometrics & Sprinting | Stuart McMillan

## Summary
In this [[HubermanLab]] interview, sprint coach [[StuartMcMillan]] explains walking, jogging, running, striding, and sprinting as related but mechanically distinct gaits. The practical center is [[SkippingPlyometricProgression]]: adults who lack sprint-ready tissue capacity can use skipping, hills, and later striding to rebuild rhythm, posture, coordination, hip extension, stiffness, and eccentric control. The episode also connects [[IndividualizedMovementCoaching]] with expressive movement and treats [[MaximalSpeedVitalityHypothesis]] as a provocative but unvalidated health proposition.

## Key Claims
- [[VelocityDeterminedRunningMechanics]] argues that speed and body structure should shape foot strike, eye position, posture, and force application rather than one universal running form.
- [[SkippingPlyometricProgression]] presents skipping as a lower-cost plyometric bridge toward striding and sprinting, with alternating short bouts of skipping and walking or jogging as a beginner example.
- [[EccentricSprintCapacity]] distinguishes ordinary running from the greater braking and force-acceptance demands of striding and sprinting; movement quality should end high-intensity work before fatigue degrades mechanics.
- Cross-body rotation, pelvic and shoulder counterbalance, rhythm, and a whole-body bounce are treated as useful movement features rather than noise to eliminate.
- [[IndividualizedMovementCoaching]] begins with the athlete's structure, capacities, personality, and preferred expression instead of copying an elite model such as Usain Bolt.
- Genetics and development both matter for elite sprinting: fiber type, limb and tendon structure, neural and hormonal factors interact with culture, varied youth participation, coaching, and competition environments.
- [[MaximalSpeedVitalityHypothesis]] proposes that safely expressing one's own top running speed may reflect vitality, but the episode does not establish it as a clinical measure.

## Key Quotes
> "speed should dictate foot strike" - the episode's boundary against applying one strike pattern across all running velocities.

> "pressure" and "peace" - McMillan's mood words for the first and second halves of a 100-meter race.

> "principles are few and methods are many" - the episode's case for flexible methods grounded in stable movement principles.

- The maximal-speed health proxy is a hypothesis, not a validated biomarker or substitute for cardiovascular, neurological, musculoskeletal, or clinical assessment.
