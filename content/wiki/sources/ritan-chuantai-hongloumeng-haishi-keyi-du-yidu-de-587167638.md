# 日谈串台｜红楼梦：还是可以读一读的

## Summary

This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] and [[Ritan|日谈]] crossover records a Dali live conversation whose explicit task is to make two male hosts who have not fully read [[HongLouMeng|《红楼梦》]] want to read it. The episode argues that the novel is not reducible to love story, workplace manual, family decline, or historical cipher; its entry points include [[RedChamberEntryReading]], [[TruthfulTemperamentInRedChamber|真]], female friendship, poetry games, social cruelty, and [[RedChamberDreamStructure|dream structure]]. The later discussion turns to [[Zhiyanzhai|脂砚斋]]批语, front-eighty-chapter editions, disputed sequels, and [[RedChamberVersionAndSequelReading]], while keeping the work first of all inside literary reading.

## Key Claims

- The episode's entry advice is practical: new readers can start from chapter 3, where [[LinDaiyu|林黛玉]] enters the [[JiaFamilyHousehold|贾府]], because characters, speech, etiquette, and household relations become immediately readable there.
- [[HongLouMeng|《红楼梦》]] should not be flattened into "romance" or "workplace lessons"; those frames can open attention, but they miss the novel's broader ethics, beauty, comedy, games, and mourning.
- The hosts defend [[LinDaiyu|林黛玉]] against the simple label of being petty or difficult by restoring her age, orphanhood, guest status, sensitivity, and insistence on being herself.
- [[TruthfulTemperamentInRedChamber]] appears through Daiyu, [[Qingwen|晴雯]], and [[Lingguan|龄官]]: each has self-respect and emotional directness that the adult ethical order cannot comfortably absorb.
- [[JiaBaoyu|贾宝玉]]'s "uselessness" is read as an ethical power: he notices and cherishes women who are ignored, but he also has to learn from Lingguan and [[JiaQiang|贾蔷]] that not every girl's feeling belongs to him.
- [[GrandViewGarden|大观园]] is treated as a temporary youth kingdom opened and protected by [[JiaYuanchun|贾元春]], where girls can play, write, form friendships, and briefly remain outside ordinary marriage and status discipline.
- [[XueBaochai|薛宝钗]] and [[Xiren|袭人]] are not simply condemned; the episode contrasts them with Daiyu and Qingwen to show how different temperaments survive or fail under worldly social order.
- The poetry-club material matters because [[CaoXueqin|曹雪芹]] writes both good poems and poems calibrated to character: Daiyu, [[ShiXiangyun|史湘云]], Baochai, and Baoyu have distinct poetic voices and social styles.
- Female friendship is central rather than secondary to Baoyu: the episode highlights Daiyu, Xiangyun, and [[Miaoyu|妙玉]] in the Mid-Autumn linked-poem scene, where lyric play turns toward shared fate.
- [[YouSanjie|尤三姐]] is read as active, clear-sighted, and tragically constrained; her suicide after rejected marriage shows agency under a world that gives women few recoverable exits.
- Male characters such as [[XuePan|薛蟠]] and [[JiaLian|贾琏]] are discussed with grayness rather than as pure monsters, while [[JiaYucun|贾雨村]] is named as a more typical bad actor in the novel's social world.
- The novel's mysteries come from being unfinished, taboo-bound, heavily foreshadowed, and surrounded by commentary; the fifth-chapter verdict poems and [[Zhiyanzhai|脂批]] preserve clues without solving everything cleanly.
- The hosts disagree about whether the last forty chapters are worth reading, but both treat them as a separate [[RedChamberVersionAndSequelReading|version/sequel problem]] rather than as a transparent continuation of the front eighty chapters.
- The episode briefly introduces [[SuoYinReading|索隐派]] and evidential Redology, then recommends [[YuYingshi|余英时]]'s [[TwoWorldsOfRedChamberDream|《红楼梦的两个世界》]] while warning readers to return to the novel as fiction.

## Key Quotes

> "这个世界不配" - QinZong's shorthand for why true-hearted figures such as Daiyu, Qingwen, and Lingguan cannot be comfortably contained by the social world around them.

> "旁逸斜出" - phrase used to name Baoyu, Daiyu, Lingguan, and Qingwen as figures whose lives do not align with the main road of social usefulness.

> "还是可以读一读的" - the episode's deliberately low-pressure invitation to read the classic.
