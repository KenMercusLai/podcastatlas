# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1002丨袁术称帝后，孙策成为最大赢

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the setup immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1003-sanguo-zui-bei-digu-de-moushi-dongzhao-llc7q-loennxw26v3u5cbhitikw1|Hanji 1003]] and the later [[YuanShu|袁术]] self-coronation branch. It contrasts three late-Han responses to the same legitimacy field: Yuan Shu mistakes an imperial title for authority, [[SunCe|孙策]] uses a warning letter to cut himself loose, and [[CaoCao|曹操]] accepts [[XunYu|荀彧]]'s advice to seize the [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] legitimacy window. The episode adds [[LegitimacyWindowSeizure|合法性窗口抢占]] while extending [[AntiUsurpationCoalitionPressure|讨逆同盟压力]], [[MoralizedWarPretext|道德化战争借口]], and [[XingshiqingStrategicAssessment|行势情战略评估]].

## Key Claims
- In 196 CE, Yuan Shu is already considering the imperial title; the episode frames this as a political-risk mistake rather than a strength multiplier.
- The host argues that if Yuan Shu accumulates force quietly he may still have room, but an open imperial claim gives rivals the cleanest public reason to attack him.
- Sun Ce writes to Yuan Shu and uses the examples of Shang Tang against Xia Jie and King Wu against Shang Zhou to argue that overthrowing a ruler requires visible sovereign failure.
- Sun Ce says the current emperor has not harmed the people and is instead young and constrained by powerful ministers, so the case does not match the classic tyrant-overthrow precedents.
- Sun Ce also invokes Dong Zhuo: even Dong Zhuo did not abolish the emperor and claim the throne, so Yuan Shu would be going further than a notorious usurper.
- The letter reminds Yuan Shu that the Yuan family had long served Han, and warns that prophecy-flatterers will not bear the consequences of a failed claim.
- Yuan Shu becomes angry and ill after receiving the letter, does not reply, and the source treats the non-response as a break between him and Sun Ce.
- Sun Ce's letter functions as both remonstrance and exit document: if Yuan Shu stops, Sun Ce can keep distance without open rebellion; if Yuan Shu proceeds, Sun Ce has a legitimate basis for separation.
- In Xu, Cao Cao considers bringing Emperor Xian under his protection, while subordinates warn that the east is unsettled and actors around the emperor are arrogant and difficult to control.
- Xun Yu argues for quick action by citing Duke Wen of Jin receiving King Xiang of Zhou and Liu Bang mourning Yi Emperor, examples where honoring a weakened sovereign wins public standing.
- Xun Yu's case has three linked benefits: stabilizing public feeling, using the emperor's name for larger strategy, and making Cao Cao's camp a destination for capable people.
- Xun Yu warns that if Cao Cao delays, another strong actor may seize the opportunity first.
- Cao Cao sends [[CaoHong|曹洪]] toward Luoyang, but [[DongChengLateHan|董承]] blocks him; this sets up the access problem developed by Hanji 1003.
- The episode's comparison makes late-Han competition a contest over title reality, public reason, and talent flow, not only troop count.

## Key Quotes
