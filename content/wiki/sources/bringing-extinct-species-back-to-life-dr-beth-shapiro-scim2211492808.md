# Bringing Extinct Species Back to Life | Dr. Beth Shapiro

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] interview has [[AndrewHuberman]] speak with evolutionary biologist [[BethShapiro]] about ancient DNA, comparative genomics, cloning, gene editing, and reproductive technology. Shapiro frames [[FunctionalDeExtinction]] not as copying an extinct individual but as engineering living relatives to express selected traits and possibly restore ecological functions; the same toolkit supports [[GeneticRescueConservation]] for species that remain alive. The episode's governing boundary is [[EcologicalInterventionGovernance]]: experiments should be staged, welfare-protective, reversible where possible, monitored over time, and decided with affected communities rather than justified by technical feasibility alone.

## Key Claims
- Species boundaries depend on the classification purpose, and historical gene flow shows that reproductive isolation is often incomplete rather than absolute.
- Recoverable ancient DNA and a sufficiently close living relative are prerequisites for current de-extinction work; dinosaurs remain infeasible because usable DNA has not survived.
- Mammoth, dodo, thylacine, and dire-wolf programs seek selected phenotype and ecological function rather than complete genomic identity with one extinct animal.
- Ancient genomes are reconstructed from damaged fragments against living reference genomes, but DNA sequence alone does not reproduce epigenetic state, development, microbiome, behavior, or ecological fit.
- Technologies developed for de-extinction can also restore genetic diversity, disease resistance, toxin tolerance, and reproductive options in endangered living populations.
- Inaction has ecological consequences too, but that does not remove the need for animal-welfare review, containment, monitoring, local participation, and long-term habitat planning.
- Human embryo selection, therapeutic editing, gene drives, ecosystem models, and artificial wombs show the toolkit's wider promise while raising distinct evidence and governance boundaries.
