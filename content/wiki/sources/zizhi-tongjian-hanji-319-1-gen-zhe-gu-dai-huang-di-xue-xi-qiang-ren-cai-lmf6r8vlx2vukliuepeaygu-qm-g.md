# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》319-1｜跟着古代皇帝学习抢人才

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode begins with an unnamed minister challenging [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] over personnel policy. Wudi is presented as believing that worthy people can come from any background but that talent counts only when a ruler can recognize and fully use it; the host criticizes the resulting treatment of people as disposable instruments rather than persons with dignity and agency.

The episode then contrasts [[DongZhuo|董卓]]'s threat-backed recruitment with [[LiuBei|刘备]]'s effort to win [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] through status, promise, and repeated personal visits. The transcript ends before the Liu Bei example reaches its conclusion, so that segment is retained as a partial framing rather than independent confirmation of the Three Visits story.

## Key Claims

- The quoted discussion says great virtue and ability need not come from a fixed region or hereditary elite.
- Han Wudi is presented as saying that talent is never exhausted if a ruler can identify it, but also that a talented person unwilling to be fully used is equivalent to one without talent.
- An unnamed minister openly rejects that judgment, after which Wudi dismisses him as genuinely foolish instead of answering the objection.
- The host argues that recruitment becomes political display when a regime values ability but does not respect the talented person's dignity, freedom, or rights.
- The episode treats the quality and quantity of talent as an important determinant of a polity's survival and direction.
- Dong Zhuo is summarized as forcing people into service through threats against their families, using terror rather than voluntary allegiance.
- Liu Bei's unfinished example is framed around his Xinye need for a strategist, recommendations of Wolong and Fengchu, an unverified imperial-uncle identity claim, political promises, and persistent visits to Zhuge Liang.

## Key Quotes

> “所谓贤者，有用之器也；有才而不肯尽用，与无才同，不杀何为。” - the episode's quoted formulation of talent as an instrument whose worth depends on full political use.

> “人才也是人。” - the host's compact objection to reducing skilled people to tools.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The lethal consequence attached to Han Wudi's usefulness doctrine is not tied to a named victim in the supplied text and remains source-scoped.
- The Dong Zhuo summary overlaps the more specific Cai Yong family-threat account already preserved in Hanji 947, but this episode does not identify the threatened recruit.
- The Liu Bei segment ends mid-sentence, and the host describes the imperial-uncle identity as unverifiable; neither detail is promoted into settled biography.
