# John Birch vs. the PTA

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode uses a 1962 school newsletter and the harassment of Montana superintendent [[OrvilleLeroux]] to examine how [[RobertWelchJr]] and the [[JohnBirchSociety]] carried unsupported communist-infiltration claims into local education politics. It contrasts the society's secrecy, meeting takeovers, procedural exhaustion, intimidation, and textbook campaigns with the [[ParentTeacherAssociation]]'s cooperative reform tradition and [[ZeldaFelchlin]]'s defense of evidence, fairness, and revisable belief.

## Key Claims

- Welch moved from anti-communist writing to alleging that President [[DwightEisenhower|Dwight D. Eisenhower]] consciously served a communist conspiracy, then founded the John Birch Society in 1958 as a secretive, decentralized education campaign.
- The society treated local PTAs as leverage points: members were instructed to join, recruit allies, pack meetings, prolong proceedings until opponents left, win late votes, and redirect school policy.
- The episode presents [[LocalCivicInstitutionCapture]] as more than persuasion because procedural participation was paired with harassment, surveillance, threats, property dumping, and reported violence.
- The campaign against Leroux began after he burned worn school Bibles on a clergyman's advice and escalated after the school board refused demands not to renew his contract.
- Harassment reached Leroux's family through obscene calls, surveillance, his son's beating, and an alleged attempt to force the family car off the road; the family left Darby and most teachers did not return the next fall.
- Felchlin's Welby Way PTA message articulated [[EvidenceBasedCivicCooperation]]: seek truth justly and rationally, avoid twisting it for personal purposes, and accept evidence that conflicts with prior belief.
- The episode connects both the Birch Society's rise and the PTA's decline to backlash after [[BrownVBoardOfEducation|Brown v. Board of Education]], while explicitly rejecting the claim that the society alone caused the PTA's decline.
- The closing comparison treats organizational decline and ideological afterlife separately: the society shrank, but its suspicion-driven political style remained recognizable.
