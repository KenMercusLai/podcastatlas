# Wolfgang's Law - Part One

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode uses [[BrandonScott]]'s Baltimore to explain [[ViolenceConcentration]]: serious violence is associated with a small, connected share of a much larger population rather than distributed evenly across a neighborhood. A 2021 Western District analysis identified roughly 700 people in 18 active groups, allowing a weekly coalition of police, clergy, counselors, outreach workers, and political representatives to reason about specific relationships, grievances, and retaliation risks.

The episode pairs that diagnosis with [[ExpressiveViolenceRetaliation]] and [[TrustedCommunityViolenceInterruption]]. Because many killings arise from anger, grief, feuds, and revenge among people who know one another, prevention may depend on credible local figures who can reach a person during a narrow escalation window. The account of [[TermiteBaltimore|Termite]] shows how years of ordinary care can create moral standing that becomes decisive without an arrest.

## Key Claims

- [[MarvinWolfgang]]'s Philadelphia cohort study found that about 6 percent of the boys accounted for more than half of recorded offenses and nearly all of the most serious violence, a concentration pattern the episode calls “Wolfgang's Law.”
- Baltimore's Western District analysis narrowed a population of about 40,000 to roughly 700 people across 18 connected groups associated with gun violence.
- Violence concentration supports case-specific prevention but does not make every identified network member equally dangerous or establish individual guilt.
- The episode argues that much homicide is [[ExpressiveViolenceRetaliation|expressive]]—rooted in grief, anger, personal conflict, and revenge—rather than an instrumental calculation among strangers.
- Weekly case review combines social-network knowledge with police, clergy, counseling, outreach, and political participation to identify likely retaliation pathways and possible protectors or mediators.
- [[TrustedCommunityViolenceInterruption]] can work through pre-existing relationships and moral credibility, as illustrated by Termite persuading an armed young man not to retaliate for an injury to his aunt.
- Baltimore's reported homicide decline is consistent with the strategy's promise, but the episode does not isolate its causal effect from other changes.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. Part One supplies the network diagnosis and community-interruption mechanism that precede Part Two's fuller support-and-enforcement account of [[FocusedDeterrence]].
- The 2023–2025 homicide sequence is episode-reported and does not by itself identify the effect of the weekly meetings or distinguish it from policing, demographic, economic, reporting, or other policy changes.
- Cohort and network concentration estimates are context- and measurement-dependent. Prior arrests, group association, or social proximity do not establish present dangerousness, criminal responsibility, or a justified intervention against any individual.
