## Summary
This [[LuoyonghaosCrossroads|罗永浩的十字路口]] interview has [[LuoYonghao|罗永浩]] talking with [[CuiJian|崔健]] before Cui's fortieth-anniversary concert about old-song arrangements, new work, live performance, AI, recording, albums, music education, production cost, aging, and artistic freedom. The durable tension is [[ArtisticContinuityAgainstNostalgia]]: audiences may seek stable versions of formative songs, while an active artist remains responsible for the work's present form and for continuing to learn. The discussion also develops [[LiveMusicHumanConnection]], [[AlbumAsIntegratedWork]], [[LowCostCreativeAutonomy]], and [[MusicLanguageExpansion]] as linked parts of a creative life.

## Key Claims
- [[CuiJian|崔健]] resists treating an anniversary concert as a museum reconstruction: arrangements, staging, collaborators, and set lists should reflect the current performance rather than reproduce remembered originals on demand.
- [[ArtisticContinuityAgainstNostalgia]] does not reject old listeners; it distinguishes gratitude for the relationship created by old songs from surrendering present artistic judgment to nostalgia.
- [[LiveMusicHumanConnection]] is the clearest boundary Cui draws around AI music: machine-made background music may expand, but shared bodily presence, risk, improvisation, and performer-audience feeling remain central to live music.
- [[AlbumAsIntegratedWork]] survives single-track distribution because an album demonstrates sequence, range, teamwork, and control across a whole body of work rather than only one peak song.
- [[LowCostCreativeAutonomy]] makes experimentation easier by lowering the cost of failure and reducing pressure from owners or capital, although production still depends on trusted technical collaborators.
- [[MusicLanguageExpansion]] treats jazz, blues, classical, electronic, folk, rhythm, and sound design as additional expressive letters; learning them expands perception without invalidating an existing taste.
- Aging changes sleep, recovery, voice, and social confidence, but Cui frames these as conditions to adjust around rather than reasons to stop learning, performing, or forming new artistic goals.
- Music is presented not only as a product or occupation but as a way to understand life, society, self, and the difference between surviving and experiencing life fully.

## Key Quotes
> "每张专辑都是我的孩子" — Cui's explanation for caring especially about newer work without disowning older songs.

> "专辑像一座山" — the metaphor for a peak song requiring a larger integrated base.

> "我相信音乐能改变世界" — Cui's statement of continuing creative purpose.
