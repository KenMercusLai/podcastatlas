## Summary
This [[Budongqiu|不懂球]] episode with football reporter [[ShenWeiFootballReporter|申炜]] reconstructs how [[TianjinJinmenTiger|天津津门虎]] survived a season that began with a ten-point deduction. It argues that midseason contract renewals, pragmatic tactical contraction, patient player development, and a high-trust team culture mattered more than emergency spending that the club could not afford. The discussion presents [[YuGenwei|于根伟]]'s combined coaching and management role and the club's multi-party custodial arrangement as a productive but person-dependent experiment in [[LowBudgetFootballClubCompetitiveness|low-budget competitiveness]] and [[FootballClubCustodialGovernance|custodial football-club governance]].

## Key Claims
- Starting a season below zero created a persistent psychological chase that differed from an end-of-season deduction; early defeats, disputed decisions, and public pressure compounded the burden.
- The source treats midseason renewals with core players and foreign forward Guilherme as the key stabilizing move because they reduced uncertainty and signaled shared commitment when transfer funds and exceptional bonuses were unavailable.
- [[YuGenwei]] shifted from a more ambitious early-season style toward compact defending and counterattacking, while his touchline suspension encouraged the staff to prepare more complete match and substitution contingencies.
- [[TianjinJinmenTiger]]'s two four-match winning runs are attributed to the interaction of contract stability, tactical adaptation, improved midfield fit, late-game persistence, and calmer psychology rather than to a single formation or incentive.
- The club compensates for weak wage and transfer-market leverage by scouting for fit, giving underused or young domestic players time to improve, and using trust, care, and belonging to retain both local and foreign players.
- Combining general-manager and head-coach authority gives [[YuGenwei]] incentives to develop a multi-year squad rather than optimize only the next result, but it also concentrates recruitment, tactics, culture, and public legitimacy in one person.
- The current custodial model is presented as financially stable, lightly staffed, locally connected, and potentially civic in character, while its next ownership or support arrangement remains unresolved.
