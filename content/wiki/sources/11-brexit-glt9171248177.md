# 11. Brexit

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode treats [[Brexit]] as a problem of historical analogy rather than a policy verdict. From Doggerland and the end of Roman Britain through King John, the English Reformation, the Seven Years' War, Napoleon, Irish independence, and the [[DunkirkEvacuation|Dunkirk evacuation]], [[TomHolland]] and [[DominicSandbrook]] ask which comparisons illuminate Britain's recurring tension between European entanglement and island distinctiveness.

Its strongest synthesis is methodological: historical parallels are useful when they isolate geography, sovereignty, law, identity, or memory, but none supplies a complete precedent for leaving the [[EuropeanUnion]]. The hosts ultimately treat Doggerland, the Reformation, and Dunkirk or the Second World War as revealing mirrors because they shaped British self-understanding, while stressing that both Leave and Remain select historical stories to intensify present arguments.

## Key Claims

- Britain's island geography encourages narratives of separation, but proximity means political, commercial, religious, and military entanglement with continental Europe never disappears.
- The end of Roman rule is a catastrophic-break analogy because institutional and monetary integration collapsed, although its scale makes it a warning scenario rather than a close Brexit precedent.
- King John's break and submission to papal authority show that departure from a supranational order can end in dependence or re-entry on worse terms.
- The English Reformation is the episode's strongest sovereignty analogy because crown and Parliament rejected external legal and religious authority and tied the break to national identity.
- The Reformation was itself pan-European, qualifying any story of purely insular English separation.
- The Seven Years' War, maritime empire, and “splendid isolation” helped produce a global-Britain self-image even though Britain continued to rely on continental alliances and European diplomacy.
- Dunkirk became an emotional story of standing alone, but the evacuation also depended on French and Belgian resistance and included Allied troops.
- Ireland's departure from the United Kingdom is a union-exit comparison with economic consequences, but overt imperial domination makes it morally and politically different from Britain's EU relationship.
- Brexit history is a contest of emphasis: Leave narratives foreground difference and autonomy, while Remain narratives foreground entanglement and dependence.
- No analogy is exact; useful comparison requires naming both the shared mechanism and the disanalogies.
