## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] episode has [[GuyRaz]] interview [[AmanNarang|Aman Narang]] about building [[Toast]] from a slow-restaurant-check payment app into a restaurant operating platform. The source follows [[AmanNarang]], [[SteveFredette]], and [[JohnGraham]] from [[Endeca]] and the post-[[Oracle]] transition into the early [[FirebrandSaints]] test, the painful [[DwellTime]] first install, national scaling through [[GordonFoodService]], and later leadership changes with [[ChrisComparato]]. Its main wiki contribution is a restaurant-specific [[VerticalSaaSDomainExpertise]] case where [[FounderIdeaPivot]], [[OperationalPainFounderInsight]], [[MissionCriticalRestaurantSoftware]], [[RestaurantOperatingSystem]], [[TrustHeavyInfrastructureSales]], and [[StageAppropriateHiring]] all become visible.

## Key Claims
- [[Toast]] began from the visible consumer pain of waiting for a dinner check, but the first app failed because it required both diner behavior change and reliable restaurant point-of-sale integration.
- [[AmanNarang]] met [[SteveFredette]] and [[JohnGraham]] at [[Endeca]], where they had already seen startup-like product-market fit struggles before [[Oracle]] acquired the company.
- The failed payment app became a discovery tool: restaurants were less excited by faster checkout alone than by replacing fragmented, hard-to-update point-of-sale and back-office systems.
- [[FinaleDesserts]] showed the founders how many separate systems a restaurant could need for POS, payments, loans, accounting, inventory, online ordering, gift cards, payroll, and scheduling.
- The source frames [[RestaurantOperatingSystem]] as Toast's strategic move: because vendors would not integrate easily, Toast chose to build more of the stack itself.
- [[Toast]] chose a restaurant-specific strategy instead of a horizontal small-business product, targeting busier restaurants served by incumbents such as [[Micros]], [[NCR]], and [[PosiTouch]].
- [[StevePapa]] invested about $500,000 after the pivot, while institutional investors remained skeptical of restaurants because of low margins, switching reluctance, and incumbent vendors.
- The first live customer, [[DwellTime]], went down within about 20 minutes, teaching the team that restaurant software is [[MissionCriticalRestaurantSoftware]] and failure immediately harms service.
- Early selling was high-touch: the team coded, installed, trained, supported, and learned inside restaurants, making the source a [[CustomerDiscoveryByDoingWork]] case.
- [[GordonFoodService]] helped Toast expand beyond Boston before the company had a mature national sales organization.
- [[ChrisComparato]] became CEO in 2015 after fast growth exposed support, outage, hardware-return, and customer-experience problems that founder hustle alone could not absorb.
- COVID cut Toast revenue by more than 90% in early April 2020 and led to a 55% team reduction, but restaurant demand for QR menus, ordering, and payment made [[OffPremiseRestaurantCommerce]] central.
- Toast went public in 2021; [[AmanNarang]] became CEO in 2024, framing the leadership change as continuity with a founder who had deep context and passion for the next phase.
- The episode says Toast has more than 20% of small-business restaurants and a larger share of sales volume because Toast customers are busier than average; this scale claim is source-scoped to the July 20, 2026 episode.
- [[ToastIQGrow]] is presented as part of Toast's AI platform, with the source claiming customers who switched to it saw sales rise 8%.
- Toast's future expansion is described as both geographic and adjacent-market expansion, including Canada, the UK, Dublin, Australia, grocery, convenience stores with gas, liquor stores, and hardware stores.

## Key Quotes
> "Built for Busy" - the Toast marketing campaign Aman cites when describing the company's focus on higher-volume restaurants.

> "fax machine" - the source's detail that online orders were still reaching [[FinaleDesserts]] through fax in 2013.

> "nearly one in five restaurants in America" - the episode's opening scale frame for Toast's market position.
