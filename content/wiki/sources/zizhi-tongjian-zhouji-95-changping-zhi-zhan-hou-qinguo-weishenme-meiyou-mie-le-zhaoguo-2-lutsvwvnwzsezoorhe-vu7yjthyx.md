# 《资治通鉴·周纪》95丨长平之战后秦国为什么没有灭了赵国（2）

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues the post-[[ChangpingBattle|长平之战]] question from [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-1-lq4wvkd8qx3q7zji6fnnkxi3qcl1|Zhouji 95 part 1]] by turning from [[QinState|秦国]]'s pause to [[ZhaoState|赵国]]'s next choice: whether [[ZhaoDan|赵丹 / 赵孝成王]] should honor the six-city settlement or use the respite to rebuild anti-Qin leverage. It contrasts [[LouHuan|楼缓]]'s appeasement logic with [[YuQing|虞卿]]'s proposal to transfer the same cities to [[QiState|齐国]], then treats the story with caution because the episode doubts that weakened Qi under [[QiWangJian|齐王建 / 田建]] would reliably join Zhao against Qin.

The episode's larger synthesis is that Qin's failure to destroy Zhao immediately was not settled by one pause. Zhao's inability to publicly accept land cession after Changping, Qin's anger at Zhao's nonperformance, [[FanJu|范雎]]'s unresolved grievance over [[WeiQiWarringStates|魏齐]], and renewed [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] gestures pushed Qin and Zhao toward the next confrontation. The closing Wei case adds [[KongBin|孔斌]] as a warning voice: weak states often hope a neighbor's disaster benefits them, even when the stronger state's war machine will eventually reach them too.

## Key Claims
- [[YuQing|虞卿]] argues that Qin withdrew because it could not keep fighting, not because it pitied Zhao; ceding land now would help Qin recover and sharpen its next attack.
- The episode frames Zhao Dan's hesitation as a moral and strategic problem: keeping a promise to an implacable predator may become self-harm, but breaking promises corrodes one's own ethical boundary.
- [[LouHuan|楼缓]], normalized here from the transcript's 卢缓 rendering, argues that Zhao should cede the promised land to [[QinState|秦国]] so other states will believe Qin-Zhao relations are repaired and will not join Qin in bullying Zhao.
- Yu Qing counters that ceding land to Qin advertises weakness; he proposes giving six cities to Qi instead, creating a visible Zhao-Qi alignment that should pressure Qin into seeking peace.
- The host questions the neatness of Yu Qing's plan. Qi's strength and will are doubtful by the time of Qi Wang Jian, so the claim that Qi would naturally join Zhao against Qin may be more rhetorical than realistic.
- The source presents the post-Changping settlement as politically hard for Zhao to execute: after catastrophic loss and public anger, openly handing land to Qin is no longer an easy court option.
- Zhao's nonperformance enrages [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]], while Fan Ju's enemy Wei Qi still sheltering with [[Pingyuanjun|平原君]] keeps personal revenge entangled with state war.
- Zhao's grant of Lingqiu to [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]] signals an attempt to reopen hezong through Chu.
- The episode argues that hezong is hard because weak states do not automatically unite against the strong; they may instead attach themselves to the stronger side and profit from another weak state's loss.
- [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]]'s ministers initially treat Qin's attack on Zhao as good for Wei; only Kong Bin warns that Qin's appetite will not end with Zhao.
- Kong Bin links Qin's danger to the post-[[ShangYangReforms|商鞅变法]] state machine: under [[FarmingWarStatecraft|耕战逻辑]], Qin must keep converting war into rewards, territory, and political motion.

## Key Quotes
> "帮对方磨刀" - the episode's metaphor for helping Qin recover by ceding land after it has paused from exhaustion.

> "软柿子" - Yu Qing's warning that public cession to Qin would make Zhao look easy to squeeze.

> "邻国倒霉就是本国得利" - the short-sighted logic attributed to Wei ministers watching Changping.
