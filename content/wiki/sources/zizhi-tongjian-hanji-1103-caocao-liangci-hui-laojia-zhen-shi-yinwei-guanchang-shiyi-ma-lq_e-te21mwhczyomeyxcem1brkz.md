## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode reconstructs [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s path from family-backed court entry to an independent base at [[Chenliu|陈留]]. It distinguishes his first return to Qiao after the Song-empress purge, his later preventive resignation from Jinan, and his final flight from [[DongZhuo|董卓]] as three different forms of [[InstitutionalExitEscalation|制度性退场升级]], not a single story of personal career disappointment.

The episode also joins inherited access to individual action. [[CaoTengLateHan|曹腾]] and [[CaoSong|曹嵩]] make Cao Cao's early officeholding possible; his punishment of [[JianShuoLateHan|蹇硕]]'s uncle, failed remonstrance, Yellow Turban service, and Jinan enforcement repeatedly expose him to the same eunuch and local-elite power structure. By 189 CE, [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]]'s protection and Chenliu's distance, commerce, and supplies make departure from the court into a viable political start.

## Key Claims
- Cao Cao's 174 CE langguan entry depended on the Cao family's eunuch-linked status as well as the formal recommendation route; the episode treats late-Han selection as increasingly controlled by family standing.
- [[SimaFangLateHan|司马防]] recommends Cao Cao for Luoyang north commandant, where Cao Cao's killing of Jian Shuo's curfew-breaking uncle becomes both strict enforcement and a politically survivable [[HighStatusLawEnforcementSignal|high-status law-enforcement signal]].
- The move from Luoyang north commandant to Dunqiu magistrate brought higher nominal pay but is interpreted as removal from the capital after conflict with the eunuch bloc.
- Cao Cao's first return to Qiao follows the 178 CE purge of Empress Song and Song Qi, whose marriage tie to Cao Cao's uncle draws Cao Cao into punishment by association.
- After returning as yilang, Cao Cao uses disaster language to criticize court corruption, but Emperor Ling's limited response leaves him disillusioned and unwilling to continue memorializing.
- Military service against the Yingchuan Yellow Turbans leads to appointment as Jinan chancellor; his enforcement there threatens eunuch and local-power interests, so the episode interprets his second return home as preventive withdrawal before retaliation.
- Cao Cao's period of reading and hunting at Qiao is a constrained pause rather than proof that family privilege eliminated career risk; in 188 CE he returns as a colonel in the [[WestGardenEightColonels|West Garden army]].
- In 189 CE Cao Cao refuses to serve Dong Zhuo, leaves Luoyang, and reaches Chenliu, where Zhang Miao's friendship, the city's commercial resources, and its position outside Dong Zhuo's immediate control create a plausible launch base.
- The episode denies the *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* assassination-of-Dong-Zhuo story and defers the source of Cao Cao's first recruitment capital to the next episode.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki is recorded. The episode consolidates and extends the early-career fragments already present in Hanji 904, 925, 926, and 941.
- The claim that Cao Cao resigned as Jinan chancellor chiefly to preempt eunuch and local-elite revenge is an interpretive motive claim and remains source-scoped.
- The episode's statement that Cao Cao was initially recommended as a langguan and its simplified description of eligibility should be read as a popular account of elite access, not a complete reconstruction of late-Han appointment law.
- Cao Song's financing of Cao Cao's first army is posed as an unresolved question and is not entered as fact.
