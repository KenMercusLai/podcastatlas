## Summary
This episode opens Qin Shi Huang's twenty-seventh year, 220 BCE, by contrasting the Warring States period's long warfare with its intellectual and social vitality. It interprets Qin Shi Huang's first post-unification departure from [[Xianyang]] as a westward ritual journey through [[LongxiCommandery]] and [[BeidiCommandery]] toward Qin old lands, not a simple sightseeing tour. The episode then turns to the building of [[QinJimiao]], possible [[Taiyi]] worship, [[Lishan]]-linked palace roads, and [[QinChidao]] as a road system that made imperial mobility part of Qin order-making.

## Key Claims
- The Warring States period can be read as both a violent age of prolonged warfare and a fertile age of intellectual, political, and social mobility.
- Qin Shi Huang's first recorded post-unification departure from [[Xianyang]] is source-scoped as a westward movement through Qin's old territory, likely tied to [[HighTempleRite]] at [[YongchengQin]].
- The episode uses [[Confucius]] and [[Analects]] imagery of the ruler as the North Star to explain why frequent imperial absence from the capital could look politically risky.
- [[QinJimiao]] is interpreted as a ritual building tied less to ordinary ancestral sacrifice than to a cosmic center, with [[Taiyi]] as the likely sacrificial target.
- The road-and-palace layout around [[Xianyang]], the Wei River, [[Lishan]], and Ganquan is presented as [[QinCosmicCapitalPlanning]], an earthly imitation of celestial order.
- [[QinChidao]] extended [[ImperialHomogenization]] and [[QinAdministrativeStandardization]] into movement itself through wide tamped roads, lane hierarchy, controlled crossings, and repeated roadside planting.

## Key Quotes
> "北地者，天子所居" — the episode quotes the Analects image of the ruler as a fixed northern celestial center while explaining why imperial travel away from the capital carried political meaning.

> "之陇西、北地，出鸡头山，过回中" — the source's terse itinerary for Qin Shi Huang's first post-unification journey.
