# The Fines

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode follows [[DianeAndKurtHorning|Diane and Kurt Horning]] after their son Matthew was killed in the World Trade Center's North Tower. It contrasts the extensive [[FreshKillsRecoveryOperation|Fresh Kills recovery operation]] with the family's belief that the screened dirt and dust called “the fines” may still contain human remains and therefore should not remain above a former landfill.

The dispute develops [[AmbiguousRemainsBurialEthics|the ethics of caring for materially ambiguous remains]] and a [[ForensicIdentificationClosureBoundary|boundary between forensic identification and closure]]. Science and law require operational categories and individual proof, while grief, ritual, and public memory can attach obligation to possible or commingled traces that cannot be assigned to one person.

## Key Claims

- Fresh Kills reopened after September 11 as a secured crime scene where approximately 1.5 million tons of World Trade Center material were sorted.
- Human remains and personal effects were recovered, while material passing through quarter-inch screens became “the fines,” an estimated 360,000–480,000 tons deposited in a cordoned area.
- [[DianeAndKurtHorning|The Hornings]] helped establish [[WTCFamiliesForAProperBurial]] and argued that possible remains should be moved to Ground Zero or another dignified site.
- Later discoveries of more than a thousand fragments around Ground Zero strengthened the families' inference that the recovery process could not guarantee that every trace had been found.
- A court rejected the claimed burial right because the plaintiffs could not prove that a particular relative was present in the unidentifiable mass.
- More than 20,000 fragments were recovered, identification continued with improved methods, and more than 1,000 victims reportedly still had no identified remains at the time described.
- The episode argues that culturally meaningful care for the dead can persist even when a trace is physically indistinguishable from ordinary matter.

## Key Quotes
