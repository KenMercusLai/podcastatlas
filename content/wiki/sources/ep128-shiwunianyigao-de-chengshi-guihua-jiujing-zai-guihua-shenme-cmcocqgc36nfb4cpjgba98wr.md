# EP128-十五年一稿的城市规划，究竟在规划什么？

## Summary

This [[WushichaYanjiusuo|无时差研究所]] episode uses Shenzhen, Beijing, Shanghai, Hong Kong, and other cities to explain how long-range development goals become centers, transport networks, land uses, public facilities, and everyday experience. Its central standard is neither formal order nor novelty alone: planning should fit geography and history, coordinate work and housing, retain low-cost and lived urban functions, and improve infrastructure without erasing local life. The discussion extends from [[PolycentricUrbanStructureAndJobHousingBalance|polycentric structure and job-housing balance]] through [[HumanCenteredUrbanRenewal|human-centered renewal]], [[ClimateResponsiveUrbanInfrastructure|climate-responsive infrastructure]], and the privacy boundary of smart governance.

## Key Claims

- Master planning translates economic and social goals into spatial structure, center hierarchies, development axes, land use, transport, and public facilities, then relies on shorter implementation plans.
- [[PolycentricUrbanStructureAndJobHousingBalance]] explains why single-function centers and overconcentrated cities produce tidal commuting, while secondary centers and mixed functions can distribute daily needs.
- City form has no universal optimum: coastlines, mountains, water, history, population, transport, and economic organization alter whether linear, clustered, concentric, or grid structures work.
- [[UrbanVillageAffordableHousingFunction]] treats Shenzhen's urban villages as low-cost, work-proximate housing and labor-market infrastructure, not merely visual disorder awaiting demolition.
- [[HumanCenteredUrbanRenewal]] asks whether renewal improves safety, sanitation, public services, heritage use, and everyday life without confusing uniform appearance with progress.
- Tourism projects and historic districts require continuing operation, resident participation, cultural specificity, and modern infrastructure; copied architecture cannot reproduce history by itself.
- [[ClimateResponsiveUrbanInfrastructure]] links drainage, sponge-city measures, orientation, green space, and construction intensity to local rainfall, heat, terrain, and environmental capacity.
- [[SmartCityConveniencePrivacyTradeoff]] separates the service and management value of traffic, movement, and facility data from surveillance, behavioral pressure, and cross-domain personal-data risks.
- [[FlexiblePublicSpaceResilience]] treats time-sharing, emergency conversion, and multiple ordinary uses as planning capacities made more visible by remote work and the pandemic.
- [[AdaptiveUrbanPlanningUnderUncertainty]] remains necessary because fifteen-year plans coordinate collective systems, but population, technology, health shocks, and behavior make precise linear forecasts unreliable.

## Key Quotes

> "城市是一套生命系统" - the episode's analogy connecting transport, production, waste treatment, information networks, and public order.

> "职住平衡" - the planning objective used to connect housing location with employment and commuting pressure.

> "十五分钟生活圈" - the neighborhood-scale test for access to exercise, culture, reading, services, and green space.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode argues that efficient standardization can be useful while mechanical copying is harmful, and that both preservation and redevelopment require case-specific judgment.
- The source qualifies an exclusively negative account of urban villages by emphasizing affordability and work access, but it does not deny safety, sanitation, tenancy, or infrastructure problems.
- City comparisons, project outcomes, technology effects, planning causation, and governance claims remain episode-scoped because the supplied file is a structured 2020 summary rather than a transcript, statutory plan, dataset, resident survey, or longitudinal evaluation.
