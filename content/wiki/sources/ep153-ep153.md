# 153 ✪ 从互联网到线下连锁：一家上市公司的二次创业——用信任破局复杂服务

## Summary

This [[FengTouQuan|疯投圈]] interview with [[JinXingSoYoung|金星]] explains why [[SoYoung|新氧]] moved from medical-aesthetic lead generation into directly operated light-medical-aesthetic clinics. The episode argues that information transparency cannot repair strong sales incentives, prepaid lock-in, or inconsistent offline delivery by itself, then presents limited services, centralized procurement, clinician training, visible preparation, direct physician consultation, and transaction-by-transaction payment as a [[ComplexServiceChainOperatingSystem|complex-service chain operating system]]. Reported store economics are promising but remain founder-supplied, while the proposed AI-native clinic and thousand-store network are plans rather than validated outcomes.

## Key Claims

- New Oxygen's medical-aesthetic platform reportedly peaked near RMB 1.5-1.6 billion in annual revenue before growth slowed, prompting a search for a second growth curve.
- The company concluded that greater information transparency did not remove aggressive selling, prepaid-card incentives, high prices, or uneven treatment because the platform could punish complaints only after delivery.
- A chain is defined as headquarters control over services, prices, marketing, site selection, store format, supply, processes, and shared systems rather than stores merely sharing a brand.
- New Oxygen narrows hundreds of possible treatments to several dozen repeatable services, with roughly six or seven major items reportedly contributing about 40% of revenue and improving procurement leverage.
- The clinics reject stored-value cards, emphasize single-service payment, test direct physician consultation, and judge the model through repeat purchase and lifetime value rather than one-time upselling.
- Open entrances, glass walls, displayed prices, streamed product verification and preparation, treatment-step displays, and post-service ratings make parts of delivery visible to customers and managers.
- Frequent light-medical-aesthetic procedures, training, examinations, clinician levels, and pooled treatment guidance are intended to reduce dependence on celebrity doctors without eliminating clinical judgment.
- The first users came from New Oxygen's experienced platform audience; owned and referral traffic together reportedly supply about half of new customers.
- Same-store growth is described as trust accumulation: customers begin with lower-cost services, then raise visit frequency, item count, or spend after satisfactory delivery.
- The company is exploring AI in customer service, triage, subsidy decisions, skin analysis, diagnosis support, follow-up, workflow, and compensation, but physician replacement remains constrained by capability and regulation.
- At recording, New Oxygen reportedly operated about seventy stores in seventeen or eighteen cities; most stores reached unit profitability in months seven through ten, while the group remained unprofitable overall.
- Jin Xing argues that capital-intensive headquarters systems suit only well-resourced leaders; smaller service firms should prefer a narrow position and stable repeat customers over imitating a thousand-store strategy.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends earlier internet-healthcare evidence by distinguishing improved information access from control over clinical and commercial delivery.
- The claim that direct physician consultation and visible workflows reduce sales pressure does not establish medical appropriateness, safety, or better outcomes; provider, product, indication, and complication-support checks remain necessary.
- Chinese and Korean penetration, product-price, acquisition-cost, clinician-share, store-growth, traffic, procedure-volume, cash, profitability, and payback figures are founder- or episode-reported and lack external datasets in the supplied summary.
- AI-native clinics, physician-assisting robotics, thousand-store replication, future medical-aesthetic demand, and long-run group profitability remain prospective or unverified.
