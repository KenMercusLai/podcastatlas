# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》331-3｜古人竟然用花椒自杀？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses reports of “吞椒自尽” to connect pepper's ritual, architectural, culinary, literary, medicinal, and allegedly lethal registers. Its two political examples are [[GaoYanzongNorthernQi|高延宗]], said to have died by filling his mouth with pepper after capture and humiliation, and [[LiXianTaiweiLateHan|李贤]], said to have prepared crushed pepper while threatening death if [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦皇太后]] were denied burial with [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]].

The durable contribution is historical and symbolic rather than medical. Pepper can carry auspicious meanings in [[Shijing|《诗经》]] and [[QuYuan|屈原]] while also appearing in narratives of humiliation, protest, and loyal resolve, but the supplied document does not identify a lethal compound, preparation, exposure, dose, clinical mechanism, or independently verified case record.

## Key Claims

- The episode says pepper had elite ritual and wine uses by the Western Zhou and pre-Qin periods before becoming increasingly prominent in cooking by the Wei-Jin and Tang periods.
- “Pepper room” traditions are connected to fragrant plant material mixed into walls, insect deterrence, fertility symbolism, and auspicious wishes.
- [[Shijing|《诗经》]] and [[QuYuan|屈原]] are cited for pepper's fragrance, longevity, sacrificial, and high-purity associations.
- The source presents [[GaoYanzongNorthernQi|高延宗]] as a short-lived Northern Qi claimant who died “以椒塞口” after capture and humiliation by Yuwen Yong.
- The source says [[LiXianTaiweiLateHan|李贤]] crushed and carried pepper as a threatened means of suicide while pressing for [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦皇太后]]'s joint burial with [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]].
- Li Xian's threatened self-killing functions as [[DeathDefyingRemonstrance|赴死式直谏]] and political protest rather than merely private despair.
- Pepper's medicinal use and historical reputation for danger are juxtaposed, but no toxicological mechanism or lethal threshold is established here.

## Key Quotes

> “吞椒自尽” - the episode's label for suicide using pepper.

> “以椒塞口而死” - the death wording attributed to Gao Yanzong in the episode.

## Connections

- [[GaoYanzongNorthernQi|高延宗]] - captured Northern Qi figure whose reported death supplies the episode's humiliation-and-suicide example.
- [[LiXianTaiweiLateHan|李贤]], [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦皇太后]], and [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] - threatened self-killing used to support a burial-status remonstrance.
- [[DeathDefyingRemonstrance|赴死式直谏]] - political pattern in which willingness to die raises the stakes of advice or protest.
- [[ForensicToxicology]] - evidence frame needed to distinguish a historical poison narrative from an established causal exposure.
- [[Shijing|《诗经》]] and [[QuYuan|屈原]] - literary anchors for pepper's auspicious and sacrificial meanings.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source's claim that ancient people often used pepper to cause death is broader than the evidence supplied in this short episode and remains source-scoped.
- The episode overlaps [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]], which says Li Xian carried poison but does not identify it as pepper; the two versions are compatible but do not independently verify the substance.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-331-4-chi-huajiao-neng-zisha-bie-sha-le-lhto8ydkeetl8sjwoxyxkkehi66e|Hanji 331-4]] later cautions that pepper lethality depends on identity, form, preparation, route, dose, and evidence quality. This episode supplies historical narratives, not the toxicological evidence needed to establish their mechanism.
