# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》364-2｜汉武帝打朝鲜的历史问题

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the political prehistory of the Western Han war against [[WimanJoseon|卫氏朝鲜]], tracing [[WimanJoseonFounder|卫满]] from Yan refugee to ruler recognized as a Han outer vassal and then showing his polity expand while retaining an obligation not to block neighboring leaders from reaching the Han court.

Under [[UgeoWimanJoseon|卫右渠]], growing autonomy and obstruction of that court-access route turn hierarchy into conflict. [[SheHeWesternHan|涉何]] fails to secure compliance, kills a Joseon escort officer on his return, receives a Liaodong appointment, and is then killed in Joseon's retaliation. The closing annalistic branch shifts to a Ganquan Palace lingzhi omen, amnesty, and [[GongsunQingFangshi|公孙卿]]'s court-serving reinterpretation of drought as earth being dried for fengshan; these claims illuminate [[AuspiciousOmenPolitics|祥瑞政治]] but remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- The episode presents Wiman as a Yan refugee who crosses the Pei River with more than one thousand followers, attracts local groups and additional Yan-Qi refugees, establishes a capital at [[WangxianCity|王险城]], and becomes king.
- In the early Han settlement, the Liaodong administrator recognizes Wiman as an outer vassal who should protect the frontier, prevent raids, and preserve neighboring leaders' access to the emperor.
- Wiman uses Han-recognized status, arms, and wealth to absorb or subdue nearby polities, including Zhenfan and Lintun in the episode's account.
- Ugeo never appears at Chang'an and blocks neighboring small states from petitioning or attending the Han court, turning access control into a breach of the inherited frontier arrangement.
- Han Wudi sends She He to demand compliance in 109 BCE; after Ugeo refuses, She He has a Joseon escort officer killed, reports the act as success, and receives a frontier post rather than punishment.
- Joseon's later attack on Liaodong kills She He and completes the immediate escalation chain before the invasion developed in Hanji 366-1 and Hanji 366-2.
- The closing notices show adverse and favorable signs being politically reframed: lingzhi prompts amnesty and celebration, while Gongsun Qing explains drought as useful drying after fengshan.

## Key Quotes

> “干封三年” - the compact phrase through which the episode says Gongsun Qing converts drought into a favorable ritual explanation.

> “九茎灵芝” - the auspicious image that the episode connects to Han Wudi's celebration and later poetic irony.

## Connections

- [[WimanJoseonFounder|卫满]] - refugee founder whose Han-recognized outer-vassal status anchors the polity's origin story.
- [[WimanJoseon|卫氏朝鲜]] - frontier polity whose formation, expansion, access obligation, and later conflict structure the main narrative.
- [[UgeoWimanJoseon|卫右渠]] - Wiman's grandson and successor who blocks neighboring rulers' access to the Han court.
- [[TributeAccessGatekeeping|朝见通道垄断]] - mechanism by which an intermediary frontier ruler controls other polities' relationship with the imperial center.
- [[SheHeWesternHan|涉何]] - envoy whose killing of an escort officer and later death convert diplomatic failure into reciprocal violence.
- [[WangxianCity|王险城]] - founder's capital, identified with modern Pyongyang by the episode but kept geographically qualified by the wiki.
- [[GongsunQingFangshi|公孙卿]] - fangshi who turns drought into a fengshan-compatible explanation.
- [[AuspiciousOmenPolitics|祥瑞政治]] - broader frame for the lingzhi celebration and favorable reinterpretation of drought.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is adopted. The source backfills the origin and diplomatic prehistory of the war sequence already represented in Hanji 366-1 and Hanji 366-2.
- Name normalization: the supplied summary's 魏满, 魏又渠, 涉和, and 王显城 are routed to the established received-history forms [[WimanJoseonFounder|卫满]], [[UgeoWimanJoseon|卫右渠]], [[SheHeWesternHan|涉何]], and [[WangxianCity|王险城]].
- Geography qualification: the episode identifies the Pei River with the Taedong River and Wangxian City with modern Pyongyang, but the supplied summary does not present the archaeological or textual debate needed to settle either identification.
- Political-scope qualification: “outer vassal,” the exact access obligation, the extent of Wiman's territory, and the identities of incorporated groups are episode-attributed rather than a complete reconstruction of the polity's institutions.
- Omen qualification: the lingzhi's form, the causal link to amnesty, the later poetic tradition, and Gongsun Qing's “drying the fengshan earth” explanation are evidence of reported court interpretation, not proof of supernatural causation or environmental benefit.
