# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》710丨谁死了？揭秘东汉最著名的暗杀事件

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Jianwu 10-11 / 34-35 CE between [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-709-mian-dui-bangjia-an-guren-weihe-buguan-renzhi-sihuo-luknor8yw839qwxogeyzwluo6qe2|Hanji 709]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-711-ta-zhu-liuxiu-da-xia-banbi-jiangshan-weihe-si-fa-biequ-liib38zub0-tgrbtycmlqvohv-w|Hanji 711]]. It follows [[LaiXiEasternHan|来歙]] turning the Longxi/Tianshui plan into western road-opening and military success before [[HuanAnEasternHan|环安]] answers with an assassin, Qiang-agitation plans, and a failed attempt to turn Lai Xi's wound into Han command collapse.

The second half shifts to [[CenPengEasternHan|岑彭]] in the Shu campaign. [[LiuXiu|光武帝刘秀]] backs Cen Peng's water-command judgment over [[WuHanEasternHan|吴汉]]'s proposed dismissal of water troops, Cen Peng burns through the floating-bridge defense, imposes no-plunder discipline, and splits routes toward [[Chengdu]] before Hanji 711 completes the later Wuyang/Guangdu breakthrough and Cen Peng assassination.

## Key Claims

- The episode frames "decapitation" as command targeting during war: the first completed case is Lai Xi's assassination, while the Cen Peng line is set up but not resolved inside this transcript.
- Lai Xi moves from Hanji 708 planning to execution by reducing the remaining Wei/Wang Yuan western position, stabilizing Longyou, suppressing Qiang pressure, and opening the Liangzhou road.
- Huan An treats Lai Xi as too difficult to beat frontally and proposes assassination plus stirred Qiang rebellion to damage morale and create inner-outer pressure.
- Lai Xi's deathbed response prevents the assassination from becoming immediate command collapse: he summons Gai Yan, stops the weeping, delegates military affairs, writes a final memorial, and only then removes the blade.
- Liu Xiu's reaction combines personal grief and command replacement: Lai Xi is a childhood companion and cousin, but the campaign also requires appointing Ma Cheng to continue the western command.
- Cen Peng's authority over water warfare matters because Liu Xiu recognizes that Wu Han's steppe and infantry-cavalry strengths do not make him the right judge of the Jingmen water front.
- The floating-bridge attack combines reward incentives, Lu Qi's ship assault, wind-assisted fire, and immediate pursuit, killing Ren Man, capturing Cheng Fan, and opening the deeper Shu route.
- Cen Peng's no-plunder discipline makes local surrender easier, while his later split-route design uses Zang Gong's Fu River branch and a Min River movement toward Chengdu to preserve operational tempo.
- Bao Yong's impeachment of Zhao king Liu Liang and the "two Bao" reputation add a court-law side branch: royal kinship and rank do not fully shield visible misconduct from censorial pressure.
