# 559. The Rolling Stones: Satanic Majesties of Sixties Rebellion (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[TheRollingStones|the Rolling Stones]] from the 1967 [[RedlandsDrugCase|Redlands drug case]] through [[BrianJones|Brian Jones]]'s removal and death, the Hyde Park memorial concert, and the violent [[AltamontFreeConcert|Altamont free concert]] in December 1969. It argues that the band became a symbolic battlefield for drugs, class, youth, celebrity, and changing social order while popular music moved from singles-led pop toward serious and profitable album-oriented rock.

## Key Claims

- Redlands became national news because the Stones already functioned as [[CelebrityFolkDevilMoralPanic|celebrity folk devils]] onto whom broader anxieties could be projected.
- The prosecution was punitive without proving a coordinated government conspiracy: tabloid, police, judicial, editorial, appellate, and public reactions did not form one establishment position.
- Jones founded the band but was marginalized by [[MickJagger]], [[KeithRichards]], and manager Andrew Loog Oldham, then became unreliable amid substance use, abusive behavior, romantic rupture, and police attention.
- Jones's drowning was officially attributed to drink and drugs; later murder allegations involving Frank Thorogood remain unproven.
- Hyde Park succeeded as peaceful public spectacle despite a weak performance, while Altamont's location, planning, intoxication, low stage, and Hells Angels security enabled violence and Meredith Hunter's killing.
- The [[PopToAlbumRockTransition|pop-to-album-rock transition]] changed audiences, musicianship, formats, and profitability, helping the Stones become a durable rock institution.
- Altamont became a symbolic end to the 1960s, although war, riots, Nixon's election, and a darker mood had already eroded the decade's optimism.

## Key Quotes

> "Musical differences." - the public explanation given when Brian Jones left the Rolling Stones.

> "They were not as good as people thought." - the episode's paraphrase of Mick Jagger's 1976 self-assessment.
