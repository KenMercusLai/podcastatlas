# 284: Denmark: The Great Escape

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces the [[RescueOfTheDanishJews|rescue of the Danish Jews]] from [[NaziGermany|Germany]]'s April 1940 occupation of [[Denmark]] through the breakdown of Danish-government cooperation and the attempted October 1943 roundup. It argues that [[GeorgFerdinandDuckwitz]]'s warning became effective because Danish politicians, clergy, civil servants, hospitals, households, resistance groups, and boat crews rapidly converted information into hiding and transport while [[Sweden]] publicly opened a nearby refuge.

The episode rejects a single-cause miracle. Danish civic belonging, the Jewish community's small size and social integration, geography, improvisational networks, and [[WernerBest]]'s self-interested restraint all contributed; the capture at Gilleleje and the deportation of 464 people to Theresienstadt keep the account from becoming a frictionless national morality tale.

## Key Claims

- Germany preserved much of Denmark's domestic government after the rapid 1940 capitulation because racial ideology, food supply, and occupation stability made a compliant “model protectorate” useful.
- [[ChristianXOfDenmark|Christian X]] and Danish political leaders publicly treated Jewish citizens as Danes, although the familiar story that the king wore a yellow star is identified as a myth.
- After martial law replaced the cooperation arrangement in August 1943, [[GeorgFerdinandDuckwitz]] warned Hans Hedtoft of the planned roundup; Hedtoft alerted Chief Rabbi Marcus Melchior, who urged the community to hide.
- Warning spread through decentralized trust networks, including civil servants contacting people with Jewish-sounding names and families opening homes, churches, hospitals, hotels, and summer houses.
- [[NielsBohr]] escaped to Sweden and pressed for an assurance that Danish Jewish refugees would be admitted; Sweden's 2 October broadcast made the escape route politically credible.
- Fishermen and improvised groups such as the Elsinore Sewing Club and Friends of the Sound moved refugees across the Oresund, sometimes for payment and sometimes at severe personal risk.
- The episode reports that 7,743 people reached Sweden, 464 Danish Jews were deported to Theresienstadt, and more than 99 percent of Denmark's Jewish population survived.
- [[WernerBest]] opposed disruption to Danish production and appears to have limited enforcement, but the source presents his conduct as opportunistic and ambiguous rather than humanitarian.
