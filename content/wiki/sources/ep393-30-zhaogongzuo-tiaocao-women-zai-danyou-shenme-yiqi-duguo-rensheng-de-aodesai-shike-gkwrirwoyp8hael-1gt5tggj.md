# EP393-“30+”找工作跳槽，我们在担忧什么？| 一起渡过人生的奥德赛时刻

## Summary
This [[WushichaYanjiusuo|无时差研究所]] conversation follows [[KekeWushicha|柯柯]] through a job change after five or six years in one private-market investment institution. Rather than reducing the decision to pay, it develops [[CareerOptionalityMaintenance|career optionality maintenance]] across market cycles, accumulated organizational trust, résumé diversity, stability, growth, and the cost of proving oneself again.

The episode then makes the gendered timing problem explicit through [[CareerFertilityTimingTradeoff|career–fertility timing tradeoffs]]. It also argues for [[ExperienceSpeedComplementarityUnderAI|experience–speed complementarity under AI]] in investment work and, through [[MangbabaWushicha|芒芭芭]]'s alternative life-design stance, for [[LifePortfolioDiversification|life-portfolio diversification]] beyond work.

## Key Claims
- External job conversations can test market value and expose capability gaps even when a person does not urgently need to leave.
- Leaving a trusted long-term employer costs more than salary stability: past performance opens a door, but organizational trust and decision authority must be rebuilt.
- [[KekeWushicha|柯柯]] chose a role balancing compensation, résumé breadth, stability, development, travel, and life pressure rather than maximizing any single variable.
- [[CareerFertilityTimingTradeoff|Career–fertility timing]] is asymmetric when a new employee must establish trust before pregnancy and childbirth while high-travel opportunities compete for the same years.
- A cyclical hiring rebound should be evaluated against the next downturn, not treated as proof of an economy-wide recovery.
- AI may reward faster research and younger tool fluency in investing, but complete-cycle experience, abstraction, and final judgment remain distinct sources of value.
- The “35-year threshold” is framed as a market test of whether seniority is matched by leadership, resources, networks, and differentiated judgment, not as a neutral biological cutoff.
- Relationships, health, family, hobbies, creation, and public expression can diversify identity so that career disruption does not erase an entire sense of self.
