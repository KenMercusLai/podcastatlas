# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》112-1｜姚贾是如何凭一张嘴破四国合纵？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows the Qin conquest sequence from [[LiSi|李斯]]'s proposal to attack [[HanState|韩国]] first into [[HanFei|韩非]]'s written counterargument, [[CunHan|《存韩》]], and then into [[YaoJia|姚贾]]'s mission against a Zhao-Han-Wei-Chu anti-Qin coalition. The source presents Yao Jia's success as more than personal eloquence: [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] supplies conspicuous backing, resources, and diplomatic status, while Yao Jia exploits the fragility of [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] through persuasion, bribery, and selective pressure.

The episode also sharpens Li Si's late-Qin role. Li Si first answers Han Fei's attempt to protect Han by stressing Han's uncertainty as a rear risk, then turns Yao Jia from a [[ZhaoState|赵国]] envoy into a Qin asset through rumor and recommendation, before the transcript cuts off as Li Si begins accusing Han Fei of remaining loyal to Han.

## Key Claims
- [[LiSi|李斯]] argues that Qin should attack [[HanState|韩国]] first because Han is small, weak, and close to Qin.
- [[HanFei|韩非]], trying to protect Han, writes [[CunHan|《存韩》]] because speech difficulty makes oral persuasion harder for him.
- Han Fei's argument claims Han has not been Qin's real enemy and redirects Qin's attention toward [[ZhaoState|赵国]], which is associated with anti-Qin [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] planning.
- The episode says Han Fei's plan hides a dangerous uncertainty: if Qin attacks Zhao and [[QiState|齐国]] joins the war, Han might reverse course and endanger Qin from the rear.
- [[YaoJia|姚贾]] is introduced as a low-origin man from [[WeyState|卫国]] whose father guarded a gate and who was later attacked by Han Fei as having a theft record in [[Daliang|大梁]].
- Zhao initially values Yao Jia's speech talent enough to send him to [[HanState|韩国]], [[WeiState|魏国]], and [[ChuState|楚国]] to promote anti-Qin alignment.
- Li Si responds by spreading damaging information in Zhao, making Yao Jia look like a former thief using diplomatic travel for private enrichment; Zhao recalls and expels him.
- Yao Jia interprets the attack as evidence that Qin recognizes his value, goes to [[Xianyang|咸阳]], and is recommended by Li Si to Ying Zheng.
- Ying Zheng's heavy gifts of carriages, horses, money, clothing, and sword give Yao Jia more than comfort; they make him visibly backed by the Qin king.
- Over three years, Yao Jia visits Han, Wei, Chu, and Zhao, breaks their coalition plan, and uses bribes to make powerful local figures speak for him.
- The episode treats this as a concrete form of [[InterestBasedCoalitionSabotage|利益瓦解合纵]]: Qin uses money, status, and inside-court incentives to prevent coordinated anti-Qin action before armies decide the issue.
- After Yao Jia's success, the episode turns to Li Si's attack on Han Fei, but the transcript stops before completing that branch.
