## Summary

This [[WhatsNextKejiZaozhidao|What's Next｜科技早知道]] episode evaluates [[SnapSpecs|Snap Specs]] as a technically significant but commercially unresolved attempt to fit full [[SpatialComputing|spatial computing]] into ordinary-glasses-like hardware. It credits [[Snap]] with a decade of iteration and major reductions in weight while arguing that the new product still sits awkwardly between lighter [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Meta Ray-Ban smart glasses]] and more capable headsets such as [[VisionPro|Vision Pro]].

The durable contribution is a product-readiness test. A device can prove that integrated optical [[AugmentedReality|AR]], hand tracking, spatial anchoring, AI, and standalone networking are possible without proving that consumers will accept roughly 130 grams, a $2,195 pre-tax price, about four hours of stated battery life, limited field of view, interaction latency, or an unclear primary job. The episode therefore strengthens [[AIGlassesProductFit]], [[AIGlassesComputingPlatform]], [[WearableOpticalDisplayFriction]], and [[TransitionalAIHardware]] while keeping the route to phone-replacing glasses source-scoped at ten to twenty years.

## Key Claims

- The new Specs combine optical see-through display, spatial positioning, gesture interaction, mixed-reality applications, AI, and a dedicated operating system in a consumer-sold glasses form.
- The product is reported at about 132-136 grams, a 51-degree field of view, a $2,195 pre-tax price, and four hours of official battery life.
- Snap reduced weight by about 40% from a 226-gram prior developer generation, but the result remains far above the roughly 30-50 gram range associated in the discussion with ordinary daily glasses.
- Entertainment, light productivity, sports guidance, meditation, sales, training, repair, and shared AR demonstrate breadth but do not yet identify one indispensable use case.
- The networking case moves cellular connectivity into the glasses case so the device can work without a phone, trading eye-worn volume for a second object the user must carry.
- Specs Intelligence is described as available across glasses, Mac, and iPhone, so its current agent capabilities do not yet prove a glasses-exclusive advantage.
- The clearest display-specific value is spatial guidance and shared AR; camera-plus-audio wearables can already give AI first-person perception without carrying a full optical display.
- The episode estimates that phone-replacing spatial-computing glasses may remain ten to twenty years away because batteries, chips, heat, materials, communications, cloud rendering, and business models must improve together.
- A task-specific role in assembly, repair, training, navigation, or other short-duration work may be more credible than an all-day general-computing claim.
- Snap's ten-year route moved from a clear, inexpensive capture toy toward an expensive general spatial computer, creating tension with its earlier playful consumer identity.
