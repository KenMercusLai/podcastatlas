# 121. Australia Before Cook

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode with [[DavidHunt]] starts Australian history before [[JamesCook]], placing tens of thousands of years of [[AboriginalAustralians|Aboriginal Australian]] presence, environmental adaptation, linguistic diversity, oral law, and long-distance exchange ahead of the familiar British discovery story. It distinguishes Cook's genuine navigational and cartographic achievement from the false implication that Australia was unknown or historically empty before 1770.

The episode then surveys uncertain migration and megafauna-extinction debates, [[AustralianDeepTimeAndOralContinuity|Dreamtime traditions and songlines]], [[PreBritishAustralianContact|Makassan trade and pre-British maritime contact]], Dutch and Spanish voyages from 1606, and Britain's later decision to use isolated New South Wales as a convict destination. Its strongest synthesis is that British Australia was neither the continent's beginning nor an inevitable result of Cook's landing.

## Key Claims

- Aboriginal people had lived in Australia for roughly 60,000-65,000 years by the episode's mainstream estimate, with migration requiring sea crossings from Sunda into Sahul even when Ice Age sea levels were lower.
- Archaeology, genetics, more than 250 languages, trade networks, Dreamtime traditions, and songlines establish great antiquity and diversity, while exact settlement waves and the age of particular oral memories remain contested.
- Human hunting and landscape burning compete with climate change and aridity as explanations for megafauna extinction; the episode does not resolve the balance among them.
- “Aboriginality” as a single identity can hide many peoples, clans, languages, laws, and territories that maintained exchange without constituting one homogeneous society.
- Makassan trepang fishers had sustained seasonal contact with Yolngu communities by at least the early seventeenth century and probably earlier, exchanging goods, language, and processing knowledge without creating a settler colony.
- Claims of Chinese or Portuguese discovery rest on weak or disputed evidence, whereas Dutch and Spanish contact in 1606 and later Dutch charting are securely inside the pre-Cook European history.
- Cook was a major navigator and cartographer, but calling him Australia's discoverer erases Indigenous presence and earlier non-British knowledge of the continent.
- British settlement was contingent: after losing the American colonies, Britain selected New South Wales partly because distance made it useful for convict transportation.

## Key Quotes

> “everywhen” - Bill Stanner's term, used in the episode to convey a Dreamtime relation among past, present, future, place, law, and spirituality.

> “discovered this territory, 1770” - the Cook statue inscription used to expose the limits of a British-centered discovery narrative.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The episode reinforces existing pages that reject a simple Cook-discovered-Australia claim and adds much greater chronological depth before the Endeavour voyage.
- The 60,000-65,000-year settlement range, a possible 120,000-year date, single- versus multiple-wave migration, later Indian or Lapita influence, and the arrival of dingoes remain source-scoped summaries of debated evidence.
- Megafauna coexistence and extinction causes remain contested; the episode presents human hunting, fire, climate, and aridity without establishing a final causal allocation.
- Claims that particular Dreamtime stories preserve memories of megafauna or ancient coastlines are plausible interpretations in the episode, not independently proven unbroken transcripts of events.
- Makassan timing and scale, alleged pre-1606 artifacts, the Chinese-fleet theory, the Portuguese mahogany-ship theory, and counterfactual Spanish, Dutch, French, or Portuguese colonization remain qualified or rejected according to the episode's evidence assessment.
