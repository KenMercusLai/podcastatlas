# 297: The Nazis: Hitler's Triumph (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces the collapse of the [[WeimarRepublic|Weimar Republic]] from the Great Depression through [[AdolfHitler]]'s appointment as chancellor on 30 January 1933. Economic catastrophe transformed Nazi electoral prospects, but the episode argues that depression alone was insufficient: presidential government, emergency decrees, repeated election gambles, conservative hostility to parliamentary democracy, left division, street violence, and elite attempts to use Hitler converted mass support into state power.

The story joins the movement's appeal from below to bargains from above. [[HorstWessel]]'s martyr cult and the [[Sturmabteilung|SA]] offered spectacle, belonging, purpose, and violence, while [[HeinrichBruning]], [[FranzVonPapen]], [[KurtVonSchleicher]], and [[PaulVonHindenburg]] progressively weakened parliamentary and federal restraints. The final Hitler-Papen arrangement exemplifies [[LegalCoerciveDictatorshipConsolidation|legal-coercive dictatorship consolidation]] before full dictatorship: conservatives transferred office to an anti-democratic leader because they believed cabinet structure and presidential authority would contain him.

## Key Claims

- American credit withdrawal, banking crisis, unemployment, homelessness, war burdens, reparations, and hyperinflation memory made the Depression a decisive accelerator of Nazi and communist mobilization.
- Uniforms, camps, songs, comradeship, and street action offered dislocated young men belonging and purpose; [[JosephGoebbels]] turned [[HorstWessel]] into a pseudo-Christian martyr whose song sacralized the movement.
- Communist hostility toward Social Democrats and bourgeois fear of Stalinist revolution prevented a unified democratic-left response and made Nazism appear to some voters as the less threatening extreme.
- [[HeinrichBruning]] normalized rule through Article 48 emergency decree, pursued deflation and benefit cuts, and called the 1930 election in which the Nazis rose from 12 to 107 seats.
- [[FranzVonPapen]] lifted the SA ban, removed Prussia's Social Democratic government by decree, and called the July 1932 election in which the Nazis became the largest party with 230 seats.
- Hitler refused subordinate office despite failing to win a majority; after the Nazi vote fell in November, [[KurtVonSchleicher]] failed to split the movement through Gregor Strasser or build a durable government.
- Hindenburg had refused Hitler the chancellorship in August 1932, but the January 1933 bargain with Papen and Hindenburg's circle transferred it to him under the false assumption that conservative ministers could control him.
- The Nazis used elections and constitutional mechanisms rather than a direct armed seizure of the capital, showing how anti-democratic movements can use democratic legitimacy while organized violence and elite intrigue hollow out the system.

## Key Quotes

> “hired” - the episode's shorthand for Papen's belief that conservatives had recruited Hitler into a cabinet they could control.

> “political suicide” - the hosts' description of Weimar elites weakening the parliamentary system they should have defended.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. This episode supplies the 1929–January 1933 prehistory to Part 4's account of dictatorship consolidation.
- Economic collapse was necessary to the episode's explanation of Nazi growth but not sufficient to explain Hitler's appointment; institutional choices, ideology, fear, violence, and contingency remained decisive.
- Electoral participation did not make the Nazi project democratic in purpose, and Hitler never won a majority in the elections described.
