# 当科技让战争触屏可见，记者为何仍要抵达冲突现场？

## Summary
This [[BuHeShiYi|不合时宜]] discussion uses reporting experience from [[Iran]], [[Russia]], [[Ukraine]], [[Lebanon]], [[Syria]], [[Myanmar]], and [[SouthSudan|South Sudan]] to ask what field presence still contributes when social media, local creators, and remote tools already supply abundant war information. Its answer is qualified: presence can widen the interview sample and reveal bodily, environmental, and accidental evidence, but language, class, politics, fixers, surveillance, institutional access, and the reporter's own position still filter what becomes visible.

The episode's durable contribution is a practice-level account of conflict journalism. [[ConflictReportingAccessBias]] explains why language and intermediaries shape the observable sample; [[IndependentConflictReportingRisk]] tracks the transfer of cost and danger from institutions to freelancers; [[ConflictInformationVerification]] treats propaganda and contradictory testimony as evidence-boundary problems; and [[GlobalConflictAttentionInequality]] explains why severe but peripheral wars can remain underreported. Together these extend [[EmbeddedForeignReporting]] without treating physical arrival as automatic truth.

## Key Claims
- Conflict reporting includes front-line military events but also displacement, humanitarian crisis, social trauma, and geopolitical pressure behind the battlefield.
- Field presence remains valuable because streets, shops, communities, repeated visits, physical danger, and chance encounters reveal material that remote contact cannot fully reproduce.
- [[ConflictReportingAccessBias]] persists at the scene: English-speaking interviewees, trusted translators, fixers, class position, political affiliation, surveillance, and willingness to speak all shape the reporter's sample.
- Independent reporting can increase editorial freedom while transferring visas, transport, insurance, evacuation planning, security, and psychological burden onto individuals.
- In monitored or propagandized environments, responsible reporting may need to present incompatible beliefs and verification limits rather than force a single certainty unsupported by evidence.
- Repeated visits reveal war's temporal effects: fatigue, silence, numbness, economic contraction, damaged relationships, and changing willingness to speak.
- [[GlobalConflictAttentionInequality]] means conflicts in South Sudan or the Democratic Republic of the Congo can receive much less coverage than Ukraine, Iran, or Gaza despite severe human costs.
- A reporter's nationality may alter access and audience framing, but distinctive value ultimately depends on reaching overlooked evidence and explaining it responsibly to a particular public.

## Key Quotes
> "语言更像进入现场的一张门票，而不是获得完整真相的保证。" - the episode summary's boundary between access and truth.

> "报道不仅提供答案，也拓展想象。" - the closing frame for moving from battlefield outcomes toward possible social futures.

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source reinforces field presence as a knowledge method while explicitly rejecting the idea that arrival, language fluency, or outsider status guarantees objectivity.
- The guests disagree in emphasis over whether exposure to enemy deaths produces hatred or survival-driven numbness; the source preserves both as situated interpretations.
- Historical comparisons, prices for fixers, visa practices, casualty imagery, political attitudes, and national-media attention remain source-scoped rather than independently verified here.
