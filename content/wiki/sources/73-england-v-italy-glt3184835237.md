# 73. England v Italy

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode uses the Euro 2020 final between the [[EnglandNationalFootballTeam|England]] and [[ItalyNationalFootballTeam|Italy]] national teams as a frame for [[AngloItalianCulturalRelations|Anglo-Italian cultural relations]]. It presents England first as a Roman and papal periphery, then as a Protestant rival and wealthy consumer of Italian art, ruins, style, food, film, and travel, while preserving admiration, condescension, religious hostility, wartime violence, and national stereotyping as simultaneous parts of the relationship.

The episode's strongest synthesis is that cultural influence does not move in one direction or retain one political meaning. Roman memory, medieval finance, Renaissance literature, the Reformation, [[GrandTourCulture|Grand Tour culture]], [[GiuseppeGaribaldi|Garibaldi]] enthusiasm, football exchange, wartime distrust, the [[ArandoraStar|Arandora Star]] disaster, and postwar lifestyle aspiration repeatedly recast what Italy represented to England.

## Key Claims

- Anglo-Saxon rulers and writers treated Rome as an imperial and Christian center, using Roman sites, papal ties, and inherited authority to position England within a wider sacred history.
- Medieval papal power, Italian banking, wool trade, and literary influence made Italy both institutionally powerful and culturally productive for England.
- The Reformation changed Rome from spiritual center into confessional antagonist without ending English admiration for Italian settings, art, poetry, and learning.
- [[WilliamShakespeare]] and John Milton exemplify continued Italian cultural prestige, although the episode treats Shakespeare's travel and authorship theories as speculation rather than established biography.
- [[GrandTourCulture|The Grand Tour]] let affluent Britons consume Italian ruins, art, antiquities, food, fashion, and social polish while increasingly viewing contemporary Italy from a position of wealth and political confidence.
- Macaroni satire and later mod style show Italianate taste functioning as aspiration and as a target for British anxieties about class, masculinity, sexuality, and foreignness.
- [[GiuseppeGaribaldi|Giuseppe Garibaldi]] made Italian unification a popular British liberal and anti-Catholic cause; football-shirt and club histories later supplied more everyday channels of exchange.
- First World War diplomacy and Second World War hostility revived stereotypes of Italian unreliability, vanity, and military weakness.
- Italians in Britain faced looting, dismissal, internment, and lethal transport after Italy entered the Second World War; the sinking of the [[ArandoraStar|Arandora Star]] is the episode's clearest corrective to its comic national stereotypes.
- Postwar cinema, design, clothing, food, travel, and football restored Italy as an aspirational cultural reference while regional identities and the England-versus-Italy framing kept both national categories open to qualification.
