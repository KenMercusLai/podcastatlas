# 参半｜阿那亚变身热门景点，一度销售难的地产项目如何摆脱困境？

## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode explains how [[Anaya|阿那亚]] moved from a distressed seaside property project toward a recurring community-and-destination business. Its core synthesis is that a tightly defined Beijing middle-class customer base, resident services, managed homestays, merchant revenue sharing, cultural landmarks, and recurring festivals turned one-time home sales into [[LongTermPlaceOperation|long-term place operation]]. The same flywheel now creates tensions among resident exclusivity, visitor revenue, crowding, expansion speed, partner incentives, and investment returns.

## Key Claims
- [[MaYin|马寅]] reportedly took over the Qinhuangdao project in 2013 with a RMB 1 billion loan after leaving a real-estate executive role; weak location, limited amenities, property controls, and an industry downturn left early sales at roughly RMB 40 million.
- The first commercial wedge was not mass tourism but a defined group of affluent 35-to-45-year-old Beijing households seeking short-break family use, lifestyle improvement, and possible asset preservation; the episode relays Ma's statement that 99% of the Qinhuangdao owners came from Beijing.
- [[Anaya|阿那亚]] bundled dining, family activities, cultural life, and property services inside the community, reducing the practical friction of using a remote vacation home.
- A unified homestay-management system turned idle owner units into visitor inventory and recurring service revenue through listing, reception, cleaning, and housekeeper operations.
- Early merchant arrangements used no fixed rent plus revenue sharing, while Anaya screened brands, locations, and operating performance; this aligned the operator more closely with merchant sales than a rent-only model would.
- The episode says community-operation revenue at Qinhuangdao has surpassed property-sales revenue, indicating that the project is no longer dependent only on new-home inventory.
- The Lonely Library, seaside chapel, Dune Art Museum, Anaya Theatre Festival, Wave Film Week, and Xiami Music Festival form a content loop: cultural programming draws visitors, visitor spending supports lodging and commerce, and event images create further distribution.
- Reported annual traffic rose from roughly 400,000 early visitors to more than 4.6 million, including about 4 million overnight visitors; peak summer days above 50,000 people strained shuttles, dining halls, pools, and photo landmarks.
- Owner lanes, reservation systems, and access codes can feel hierarchical to visitors, while owners may view heavy visitor traffic as dilution of the scarce experience they purchased.
- Brand-and-operation partnerships let Anaya expand with less capital, but disputes over operating authority, service standards, and cost expose conflicts between the brand operator and asset owner.
- The Qinhuangdao model depends on a particular Beijing weekend-demand catchment and unified operating control; the source therefore treats nationwide replication as conditional rather than automatic.
- Side items report [[OnRunning|On Running]] signing [[KylianMbappe|Kylian Mbappe]], [[Doubao]] entering a Roewe cockpit through [[Volcengine]], [[MusePersonalAgent|Muse]] facing permission and privacy-trust constraints, and [[SnapSpecs]] demonstrating consumer and repair-oriented AR uses.
