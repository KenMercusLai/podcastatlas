## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode examines why century-old film projection remains a premium moviegoing technology around [[ChristopherNolan]]'s [[TheOdyssey]] release, especially through [[IMAX]] 70mm and standard 70mm screenings. [[MilesWilliams]] explains how the [[AlexTheatre]] in Glendale rebuilt its projection setup for 70mm, why the format differs from digital IMAX and 35mm, and how scarce projectors, parts, and trained operators make [[SeventyMillimeterFilmProjection|70mm film projection]] an event.

The source's larger claim is that analog movie exhibition has both technical and emotional value. Film stock, historic venues, visible projection booths, and apprenticeship labor make [[AnalogMoviegoingExperience|analog moviegoing]] feel hard to reproduce at home, while younger interest in tapes, LPs, and other physical media connects the case to [[AnalogMediaPreservation]] and [[PhysicalVideoMediaRevival]].

## Key Claims
- [[IMAX]] 70mm differs from digital IMAX and standard 70mm because it uses actual film running horizontally with 15 perforations per image, while standard 70mm runs vertically with five perforations.
- Standard 70mm is described as about four times the size of 35mm, giving more clarity, color, and image quality than smaller-gauge film.
- [[TheOdyssey]] made scarce 70mm exhibition capacity visible because viewers sought tickets for the intended IMAX 70mm format and because non-IMAX 70mm venues were also limited.
- The [[AlexTheatre]] renovated its historic 1920s venue for 70mm in roughly eight weeks after a green light from Universal, spending about half a million dollars on projection, sound, clarity, and booth work.
- [[MilesWilliams]] frames projected film as a special event because audiences cannot reproduce the same format, venue, and communal experience on home screens.
- Legacy film projection depends on aging projectors, scarce parts, and a small Los Angeles network of older projectionists and apprentices.
- The source suggests younger interest in analog media reflects a desire for physical connection after pandemic-era disconnection and digital saturation, but this remains anecdotal.
