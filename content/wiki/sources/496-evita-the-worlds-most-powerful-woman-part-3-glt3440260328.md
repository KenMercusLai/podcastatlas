# 496. Evita: The World's Most Powerful Woman (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[JuanPeron|Juan Perón]]'s 1946 election and [[EvaPeron|Eva "Evita" Perón]]'s transformation from campaign symbol into a political intermediary for workers, unions, women, and poor petitioners. It argues that welfare delivery, glamour, radio performance, Catholic imagery, and personal devotion were mutually reinforcing parts of [[Peronism]], not separable additions to formal policy.

The episode also tracks the 1947 European Rainbow Tour, women's suffrage, the [[EvaPeronFoundation]], and the regime's movement from a fair electoral victory toward [[ElectiveAuthoritarianism]] through court purges, press control, legal intimidation, constitutional change, and corruption.

## Key Claims

- Peronism combined social justice and welfare with nationalism, anti-elite rhetoric, spectacle, and personal loyalty rather than simply reproducing Italian fascism.
- Evita became an unusually powerful unelected bridge among Perón, organized labor, women, and people seeking direct material help.
- Her working-class voice and high glamour divided audiences by class: elites read vulgarity where many poorer supporters saw recognition and aspiration.
- The Rainbow Tour increased Evita's international visibility while exposing the polarizing and fascist-adjacent context in which outsiders interpreted the Peróns.
- Women's suffrage and a disciplined Peronist women's party expanded female political participation while subordinating that participation to loyalty to Perón.
- The Eva Perón Foundation delivered goods and built institutions at major scale, but personalized welfare as direct, theatrical compassion centered on Evita.
- Perón's government retained electoral legitimacy while weakening judicial, press, and constitutional restraints, producing an elective-authoritarian drift rather than an immediate full dictatorship.

## Key Quotes

> "glamour demagoguery" - the episode's phrase for the fusion of Evita's image and political appeal.

> "the bridge of love" - one devotional title associated with Evita's claimed connection between Perón and the people.
