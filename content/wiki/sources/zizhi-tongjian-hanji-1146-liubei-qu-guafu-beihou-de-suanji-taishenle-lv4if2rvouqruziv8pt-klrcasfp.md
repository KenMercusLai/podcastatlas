# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1146丨刘备娶寡妇？背后的算计太深了！

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[LiuBei|刘备]] from the capture of Chengdu into three post-conquest problems: replenishing a treasury emptied by promised military plunder, connecting the new regime to Yizhou elites through marriage, and handling a famous but distrusted scholar. [[LiuBa|刘巴]]'s reported “值百钱” proposal is presented as rapid fiscal recovery through state-defined currency value, controlled markets, and official purchasing, with the loss shifted toward holders of old money and ordinary suppliers.

The marriage of Liu Bei to [[LadyWuLiuBei|吴氏]] connects him to [[WuYiLateHan|吴懿]] and the former Liu Zhang political network, while [[FaZheng|法正]] uses the precedent of Duke Wen of Jin to answer Liu Bei's ritual concern. The final branch distinguishes office, honor, and operating authority: although Liu Bei reportedly distrusts [[XuJingShuHan|许靖]], Fa Zheng argues that publicly slighting a nationally famous man would damage the regime's talent reputation, so Xu Jing receives high status without control of substantive military or administrative work.

## Key Claims
- Liu Bei reportedly promised government property to the troops during the siege of Chengdu, leaving the new regime with an immediate military-finance problem after victory.
- Liu Ba proposes issuing a high-denomination coin, requiring important goods to trade through the new money, creating an official market, and using the currency to purchase grain and cloth.
- The episode interprets the arrangement as a rapid transfer of purchasing power to the state whose costs fall partly on holders of old money and civilian suppliers; inflation, enforcement, and longer-run effects are not established by the supplied summary.
- Liu Bei's marriage to Lady Wu is presented as post-conquest elite integration through Wu Yi and the former Liu Zhang network, not merely a private romantic choice.
- Fa Zheng answers the kinship and propriety objection with the precedent of Duke Wen of Jin, converting an ethical concern into an argument about political necessity and historical legitimacy.
- Liu Bei reportedly judges Xu Jing's ability, conduct, and loyalty harshly after his attempted surrender during the Chengdu campaign.
- Fa Zheng argues that Xu Jing's established reputation constrains the new ruler: visible disrespect could be interpreted as hostility to worthy people and discourage other recruits.
- Liu Bei therefore gives Xu Jing high rank and honor without entrusting him with substantive military or administrative authority.
- The closing preview says [[ZhaoYun|赵云]] opposes distributing Chengdu houses and land to officers, a dispute developed in Hanji 1147.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode's fiscal, marriage, and appointment mechanisms are retained as source interpretations rather than a complete account of early Shu-Han consolidation.
- Fiscal caution: the denomination, legal-tender rules, commodity controls, minting cost, quantities, price effects, enforcement, chronology, and distributional burden require comparison with historical and numismatic evidence.
- Marriage caution: political integration does not establish Lady Wu's preferences, Wu Yi's exact leverage, the complete negotiation, or the degree to which the marriage caused later loyalty.
- Ritual caution: the Duke Wen analogy is an argument used by Fa Zheng, not proof that contemporaries agreed on the relevant kinship category or that political benefit resolved every ethical objection.
- Appointment caution: Xu Jing's attempted surrender, competence, character, worldwide fame, offices, practical authority, and Liu Bei's private judgment are reported through a structured popular-history summary and require broader verification.
- Naming caution: this episode's [[XuJingShuHan|许靖]] is distinct from the existing [[XuJingLateHan|徐敬]].
