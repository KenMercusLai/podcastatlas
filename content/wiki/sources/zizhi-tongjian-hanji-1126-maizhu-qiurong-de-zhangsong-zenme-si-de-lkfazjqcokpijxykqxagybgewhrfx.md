# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1126丨卖主求荣的张松怎么死的？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows the collapse of the alliance between [[LiuBei|刘备]] and [[LiuZhang|刘璋]]. After [[ZhangSongLateHan|张松]] has supplied Yizhou maps and force dispositions to Liu Bei, his attempt to stop Liu Bei from leaving is exposed by his elder brother [[ZhangSuLateHan|张肃]]; Liu Zhang executes Zhang Song, and the rupture becomes open war ending in Liu Zhang's surrender at Chengdu.

The episode explains the collapse through more than individual betrayal. It connects Liu Zhang's weak personnel use and decision capacity to the defection of [[FaZheng|法正]] and other insiders, while treating the unresolved division between Dongzhou followers and older Yizhou interests as a structural cause. Its closing judgment asks listeners to distinguish moral labels such as loyalty or treachery from political realignment, survival, and the responsibilities of leadership in civil war.

## Key Claims

- Zhang Song has already given Liu Bei maps and military dispositions for Yizhou when Liu Bei announces that he may return to Jingzhou rather than attack Zhang Lu.
- Zhang Song writes urging Liu Bei not to leave; [[ZhangSuLateHan|Zhang Su]] obtains the letter, reports it to Liu Zhang out of fear of implication, and Liu Zhang has Zhang Song executed.
- The episode speculates that Liu Bei intentionally let the letter be exposed so Zhang Song's death could provide a pretext for war, but the supplied transcript does not provide further evidence for that motive.
- After the rupture, Liu Bei advances through Yizhou, receives successive defections, and reaches Chengdu; Liu Zhang accepts Jian Yong's persuasion to surrender rather than expose the population to a destructive last defense.
- Fa Zheng, Meng Da, Wu Yi, Fei Guan, Li Yan, and Ma Chao are used as examples of capable or well-connected people who cease supporting Liu Zhang or join Liu Bei.
- The episode attributes Liu Zhang's failure partly to underusing talent, weak military and strategic judgment, and inability to turn personal mildness into organizational cohesion.
- The Dongzhou-Yizhou division remains politically active: the episode says many Dongzhou-aligned figures support Liu Bei, while local figures such as Huang Quan, Zheng Du, and Yan Yan oppose his entry or retain stronger loyalty to Liu Zhang.
- Fa Zheng later becomes an important Liu Bei adviser in the conquest of Yizhou and the Hanzhong campaign, showing the strategic cost of Liu Zhang's earlier failure to use him.
- The closing transition places Cao Cao's three exceptional court privileges and Dong Zhao's proposal for a ducal title and nine bestowments in 212 CE, but leaves that succession-legitimacy branch for the next episode.

## Key Quotes

> “忠诚与历史进程之间存在难以清楚划分的人性灰色地带。” - the supplied summary's formulation of the episode's refusal of a simple loyalist-traitor binary.

> “组织若忽视人才、缺少战略方向且无法化解内部矛盾，可能走向人才流失乃至崩溃。” - the episode's modern organizational synthesis.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode continues Hanji 1109's alliance pivot and Hanji 1123's covert Zhang Song-Fa Zheng plan into exposure, war, and Liu Zhang's surrender.
- Motive caution: the claim that Zhang Song's letter “had to” reach [[ZhangSuLateHan|Zhang Su]] and that Liu Bei deliberately used Zhang Song's death as a pretext is the host's inference, not an established fact in the supplied material.
- Faction caution: Dongzhou and Yizhou alignment helps explain defections but does not determine every individual's choice; office, kinship, local security, personal prospects, and battlefield momentum also matter.
- Source-scope caution: the maps and troop dispositions, exact letter path, private calculations, three-year conquest duration, surrender motives, individual faction labels, Zhuge Liang's view of Fa Zheng, and modern management analogies require direct textual corroboration.
