## Summary
This [[RevisionistHistory]] conversation has [[MalcolmGladwell]] interview [[DiogoRau]], [[EliLilly|Eli Lilly's]] chief information and digital officer, about applying consumer-technology thinking to pharmaceuticals. Rau connects [[PatientCenteredHealthcareDelivery]], [[MedicationAuthenticationAndSafety]], [[TreatmentFeedbackAndAdherence]], patient access to health data, wearables, home-based care, and AI-assisted discovery while contrasting rapid technology cycles with the long uncertainty of [[PharmaceuticalDevelopmentTimeHorizon|pharmaceutical development]]. The episode's proposals are strategic and often prospective; cost, privacy, regulation, clinical validation, unequal digital access, and consumer acceptance remain material boundaries.

## Key Claims
- Rau says pharmaceutical development can require decisions more than a decade ahead, stage-specific probabilities of technical success, and years of work whose medicines may never reach patients.
- His operating mandate at Lilly spans molecule discovery through the consumer-facing treatment journey rather than treating technology as a maintenance-only IT function.
- [[PatientCenteredHealthcareDelivery]] begins with the intended patient experience and works backward, including shorter waits, fewer access barriers, telehealth, delivery, and same-day treatment when clinically appropriate.
- A cryptographically secure NFC tag can help authenticate medicine packaging more strongly than a copied visual label or QR code and could become a direct manufacturer-patient interaction surface.
- The proposed authentication layer could later support medication checks, duplicate-dose prevention, or wrong-drug warnings, although pill-level embedding and consumer acceptance remain speculative.
- Wearables may add value in clinical trials and continuing care when continuous measurement becomes understandable feedback rather than an isolated stream of numbers.
- Rau explicitly presents the idea that rapid feedback improves medication adherence as a hypothesis, using visible weight-management progress and less-visible cholesterol treatment as a contrast.
- He argues that patients should be able to see and govern their own health data rather than being required to receive it only after provider interpretation.
- Rau's consumer-side success test is the interval from deciding to seek help to receiving appropriate medicine, followed by support that helps patients remain engaged with treatment.
- His discovery-side ambition is to use AI to help develop medicines humans might not have conceived, while acknowledging that long development timelines delay patient impact.
