# Why one X user is microblogging history in real time

## Summary

This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode profiles [[MannyMorota]], who manually reconstructs news from 25, 50, and 100 years earlier on [[Twitter|X]] as though events were unfolding in the present. The project combines newspaper, wire-service, and [[WaybackMachine|Wayback Machine]] research with a platform-native publishing cadence, making [[RealTimeHistoricalReenactment|historical chronology]] and the uncertainty of incomplete reports part of the audience experience.

Morota's September 2001 feed also exposes the format's ethical boundaries. It reproduced contemporary misinformation alongside accurate reports to convey uncertainty, reached audiences with and without firsthand memories of September 11, and generated engagement that Morota did not want to profit from; he said any associated revenue would go to Tunnel to Towers.

## Key Claims

- [[MannyMorota]] runs separate [[Twitter|X]] timelines recounting events from 25, 50, and 100 years ago while also working as a nonprofit law clerk.
- He says the research and publishing process is manual: he uses historical newspapers, archived websites, [[CNN]], AP, and [[Reuters]], and ordinarily posts 10–20 stories per day.
- [[RealTimeHistoricalReenactment]] uses the familiar flow of breaking-news updates to make past events feel unresolved rather than retrospectively inevitable.
- During the September 11 recreation, Morota posted about twice per minute between the first plane impact and the collapse of the second tower, including both verified facts and misinformation circulating at the time.
- The story mix includes major events, international affairs, religion, and popular culture so the feed reconstructs the wider news environment rather than only a canonical headline.
- Older millennials described recovering forgotten details, while younger users said the feed helped them understand their parents' emotional experience, making the project a case of intergenerational public memory.
- Morota said [[Twitter|X]]'s prior creator-revenue program had ended, described compensation under the relevant program as roughly $20 per million views, and pledged to donate any September 11 revenue to Tunnel to Towers because monetizing the tragedy made him uncomfortable.
- He hopes an educational community can remain accurate and resist conspiracy claims despite the platform's broader combative culture.
