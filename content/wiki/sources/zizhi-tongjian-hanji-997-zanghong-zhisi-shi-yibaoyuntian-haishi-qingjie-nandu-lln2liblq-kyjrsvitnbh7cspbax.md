# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》997丨臧洪之死：是义薄云天，还是情劫难渡？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the late-Han sequence immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-998-baima-jiangjun-gongsunzan-cong-sanqian-baima-dao-kunshou-gucheng-lp-1l2gvbsbfnyznutdghxwwvqna|Hanji 998]]. It uses [[ZangHong|臧洪]]'s death after refusing [[YuanShao|袁绍]] to ask whether heroic loyalty can become politically destructive when grief, old-master obligation, and resentment take over judgment.

The core narrative is the collapse of any possible settlement after Zang Hong is captured in 195 CE. Zang Hong condemns Yuan Shao for failing to rescue [[ZhangChaoLateHan|张超]] against [[CaoCao|曹操]], while the host argues that Yuan Shao was also Zang Hong's current superior and Cao Cao was Yuan Shao's ally; the episode therefore turns the death into a case of [[EmotionalAttachmentPoliticalDistortion|情执政治判断失衡]] and [[DeathPartyLoyalty|死党之义与守职奉上]], not only a pure loyalist martyrdom.

## Key Claims
- The episode opens with "情关" as the interpretive frame: attachment, resentment, and obsessive feeling can make a person generate suffering for the self and disorder around the self.
- The source does not deny Zang Hong's courage or loyalty; it asks whether his loyal posture also carries immaturity, rigidity, and loss of political scale.
- Zang Hong's prior extreme conduct under siege, including killing a concubine to feed soldiers, is treated as evidence that his ideals could be pursued through extreme means.
- The host reads Zang Hong as a person with impressive气节 but weak political flexibility.
- Zang Hong's death is not presented as a fully unavoidable battlefield outcome: Yuan Shao initially wants to spare him if he submits, but Zang Hong's refusal closes the path.
- Zang Hong accuses the Yuan family of benefiting from Han favor while failing to support the collapsing Han order.
- Zang Hong's specific grievance is Yuan Shao's refusal to rescue Zhang Chao after Zhang Chao's family is destroyed.
- Zang Hong treats Yuan Shao's friendship with [[ZhangMiaoLateHan|张邈]] as morally extending to Zhang Chao, making Yuan Shao's inaction a betrayal of brotherly obligation.
- The host qualifies that judgment: if Zang Hong is serving Yuan Shao, Yuan Shao is also his current lord, and Yuan Shao's alliance with Cao Cao makes direct rescue politically costly.
- The episode therefore makes Zang Hong's loyalty conflict two-sided: loyalty to an old superior is real, but it overrides duty to the current organization and wider strategic field.
- [[ChengLongLateHan|成龙]]'s remonstrance intensifies the source's moral drama because he chooses death with Zang Hong rather than silent safety under Yuan Shao.
- The final judgment is source-scoped: Zang Hong can be both a righteous figure and a dangerous example of private emotion elevated above public responsibility.
