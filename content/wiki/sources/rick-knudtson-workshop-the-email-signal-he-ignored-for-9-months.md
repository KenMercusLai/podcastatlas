## Summary
This [[TheSaaSPodcast]] episode features [[RickKnudtson]] explaining how [[WorkshopInternalComms|Workshop]] found product-market fit after abandoning an initial intranet hypothesis for enterprise internal email. The case connects [[CustomerSignalDrivenPivot]], [[EnterpriseInternalEmailInfrastructure]], and [[ChannelNativeInternalCommunication]]: customers repeatedly named email as their most important internal channel, while the team spent six to nine months treating that problem as too simple. The episode also extends the wiki's SaaS growth and pricing branch through [[AudienceBeforeProductMarketing]], [[CommunityLedSaaSGrowth]], [[FounderEgo]], and [[UsageBasedVerticalSaaSPricing]].

## Key Claims
- [[WorkshopInternalComms|Workshop]] is described as a multi-channel internal communications platform for large organizations, used by communications teams, leaders, departments, and executives to distribute messages and measure reach.
- [[RickKnudtson]] says Workshop started from an internal communications pain he experienced at [[FlywheelWordPressHosting|Flywheel]] and during the company's acquisition by WP Engine.
- Workshop's first hypothesis centered on a modern intranet, but the team struggled to find a repeatable through-line after roughly nine months of sales work.
- Customers repeatedly described email as their primary employee-communication channel, creating a [[CustomerSignalDrivenPivot]] that the team initially resisted because email seemed too obvious and unglamorous.
- After rebuilding around email in September 2021, Workshop reportedly moved from about three intranet customers in nine months to about 10 email customers in 30 days.
- The episode frames enterprise internal email as infrastructure rather than a simple newsletter tool because large organizations must handle deliverability, spam filtering, security review, analytics, governance, permissions, and brand control.
- Rick argues that multi-channel internal communication should distribute through email, SMS, Slack, Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, and similar surfaces while preserving one source of control for communicators.
- Workshop's pricing avoids seat-based expansion friction and instead varies by audience size and channel volume, especially email and SMS.
- [[HappyMondayClub]] began as a day-one audience-building newsletter and resource hub for internal communicators and reportedly reached roughly 50,000 subscribers.
- Workshop's values "listen to learn" and "look on the bright side" are presented as operational responses to early product-market-fit struggle.
- Rick's prior [[FlywheelWordPressHosting|Flywheel]] exit helped with credibility and seed fundraising but did not remove the need to rediscover product-market fit from customer evidence.

## Key Quotes
> "listen to learn" - Rick's description of the value Workshop adopted after its first year of customer discovery.

> "look on the bright side" - Rick's shorthand for optimism as confidence that a workable solution exists.

> "founders should market for a year before they sell anything" - Rick's advice after reflecting on Happy Monday Club and Workshop's early audience building.
