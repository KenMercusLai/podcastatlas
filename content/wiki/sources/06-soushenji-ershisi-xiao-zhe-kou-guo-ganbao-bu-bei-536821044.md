# 06.搜神记： 二十四孝这口锅，干宝不背

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode has [[QinZong|秦总]] and [[Beimin|北明]] reread [[SouShenJi|《搜神记》]] as a mixed [[ZhiguaiArchiveValue|志怪 archive]] rather than a narrow sermon book. It argues that [[GanBao|干宝]] collected ghosts, omens, miracles, disasters, folk belief, literary prototypes, and unsettling anecdotes with a historian-like belief in the supernatural, so the book preserves [[StrangeTaleMoralPluralism|multiple moral voices]] rather than one stable doctrine. The title's claim, tracked as [[FilialPietyStoryAfterlife]], is that later traditions such as [[TwentyFourFilialExemplars|《二十四孝》]] moralized selected stories more narrowly than the original collection itself.

## Key Claims
- [[SouShenJi|《搜神记》]] is framed as an early Chinese strange-tale archive, not as a modern novel with one clean authorial program or a simple Confucian teaching manual.
- [[GanBao|干宝]] is presented as sincerely committed to ghosts, omens, and cosmic correspondences; the episode treats this as "serious superstition" that still preserves historical, religious, and narrative material.
- The book's brief shocks matter: the "I am a ghost" debate, walking dog, harmless spirits, mischievous ghosts, and oddly unexplained marvels show that not every tale needs an explicit lesson.
- [[LiJiSlaysSnake|李寄斩蛇]] becomes the episode's clearest action story: a girl succeeds because she acts where village and official systems have normalized sacrifice.
- The episode does not deny that stories later attached to [[TwentyFourFilialExemplars|《二十四孝》]] appear in or near [[SouShenJi|《搜神记》]], but it argues that the source often records them as strange events rather than commands to imitate them.
- The same collection contains revenge, remarriage after abuse, sympathy for nonhuman beings, and critiques of jealous power, so a single filial-piety label erases the book's [[StrangeTaleMoralPluralism|moral plurality]].
- Stories about the East Sea filial woman, broken promises to women, Ziyu, and Jiang Ziwen's daughter show that the episode also notices wrongful accusation, gendered vulnerability, and afterlife punishment.
- The source repeatedly connects stories to later literature: Dong Yong, Cowherd and Weaver Girl, Han Wudi's spirit-summoning, the East Sea filial woman as a Dou E precursor, and early detective-like plots all make the book a motif reservoir.
- Its religious world is layered: Buddhist and Indian elements, Daoist methods, omens, divination, underworld offices, local deity cults, and historical figures coexist without one systematic theology.
- The episode sometimes reads marvels naturalistically: asbestos-like cloth, magic tricks, conjoined bodies, parasites, and animal anomalies become traces of things ancient observers recorded but could not explain in modern terms.
- The Yu Ji, Zuo Ci, Zhang Hua fox, and Jiang Ziwen stories make power itself strange: authority often fears, kills, or coerces beings whose main offense is charisma, knowledge, or local religious force.
- The closing Jiang Ziwen romance reinforces the episode's central caution: [[SouShenJi|《搜神记》]] can be coercive, violent, funny, generous, superstitious, and humane at once.

## Key Quotes
> "因为我就是鬼" - the debate-ending ghost line used to set the book's abrupt strange-tale tone.

> "二十四孝这口锅，干宝不背" - the episode's title argument about later moralization.

> "不一定是在号召读者照做" - the hosts' boundary around filial-piety tales.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The source qualifies any simple use of filial-piety stories as evidence of a single traditional doctrine: in this reading, [[SouShenJi|《搜神记》]] preserves filial exempla beside revenge, defiance, remarrying after abuse, sympathy for monsters, and criticism of power, so later moralization should remain source-scoped rather than projected back onto the whole book.
