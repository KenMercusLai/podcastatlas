# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》982丨扒一扒刘备入徐背后的利益之争

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the [[Xuzhou|徐州]] succession immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-983-zhi-hui-pianren-de-laoban-yongyuan-bushi-hao-laoban-lknr2zydwjytjo7od-rt3zi-4k-s|Hanji 983]] by asking why [[MiZhu|糜竺]], [[ChenDeng|陈登]], and [[KongRong|孔融]] supported [[LiuBei|刘备]] instead of the stronger [[YuanShu|袁术]]. The episode reads Liu Bei as a useful but still controllable Han-clan proxy for local Xuzhou interests, while Yuan Shu's seal-backed imperial ambition and Huainan power base make him dangerous to local autonomy and Han legitimacy. It then shifts to [[Shouchun|寿春]], [[MaRidiLateHan|马日磾]], [[ZhaoQiLateHan|赵岐]], and the [[SunJianLateHan|孙坚]] household, using [[LadyWuSunJian|吴夫人]], [[QiantangWuClan|钱塘吴氏]], [[WuJingLateHan|吴景]], [[SunCe|孙策]], and [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] to show how kinship, local elites, trade routes, and soldiers became early Jiangdong state-building resources.

## Key Claims
- The episode rejects a purely moralized "仁义感召" explanation for Liu Bei entering Xuzhou and instead treats the transfer as [[LocalEliteProxySelection|地方豪强代理人选择]].
- [[LiuBei|刘备]] is presented as attractive to Xuzhou elites because he combines reputation, a Han-clan identity, and limited current force; that mixture gives legitimacy without immediately swallowing local power.
- [[YuanShu|袁术]] is presented as the rejected alternative because he controls [[HuainanRegion|淮南]], holds the [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]], shows imperial ambition, and would threaten both Xuzhou autonomy and Han-court orthodoxy.
- [[MiZhu|糜竺]] represents a Donghai economic-interest channel: if Liu Bei depends on Mi family money, Mi Zhu's local standing and influence rise rather than disappear under an outside warlord.
- [[ChenDeng|陈登]] represents Xuzhou gentry self-preservation: the episode has him prefer a weaker but legitimate governor over absorption by Yuan Shu's Huainan group.
- [[KongRong|孔融]] represents prestige and Confucian orthodoxy: his support fits Liu Bei's reputation and opposes Yuan Shu's illegitimate title direction.
- The episode frames Liu Bei's eventual acceptance of Xuzhou as political caution rather than simple virtue; he waits for local representatives, funding, soldiers, and strategic argument before taking the office.
- The source treats the later 196 CE Yuan Shu attack on Liu Bei as evidence that Chen Deng and other Xuzhou elites correctly anticipated Yuan Shu's northern pressure.
- At Shouchun, [[MaRidiLateHan|马日磾]] loses the imperial tally to Yuan Shu, showing how court objects can be seized by regional force even while they still carry [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]].
- [[ZhaoQiLateHan|赵岐]] is contrasted with Ma Ridi as the principled official whose refusal to bow makes Yuan Shu wary rather than successful.
- [[Shouchun|寿春]] is framed as a Jiang-Huai strategic node: its geography helps explain both Yuan Shu's later imperial staging and the pressure around Yangzhou and Jiangdong.
- [[SunJianLateHan|孙坚]]'s marriage to [[LadyWuSunJian|吴夫人]] is treated as [[KinshipResourceStateBuilding|姻亲资源建构]] rather than only household biography.
- [[QiantangWuClan|钱塘吴氏]] contributes trade, gentry, and water-route resources; the episode says [[WuJingLateHan|吴景]] later gives [[SunCe|孙策]] four thousand Danyang soldiers.
- The episode backfills the early [[SunCe|孙策]] and [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] relationship: Zhou Yu's invitation to Sun Ce's family and respect for Lady Wu make their tie close to a family alliance before the later military support in Hanji 992.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is recorded. The episode says the演义-style "陶谦三让徐州" did not happen historically; existing wiki pages already treat Liu Bei's Xuzhou position as a fragile succession and later base-loss problem rather than a simple moral handoff.
- The source's "Sun Quan later built a Qiantang naval base" claim is kept source-scoped because the surrounding wiki had not yet developed a dedicated Sun Quan or Qiantang Wu-clan branch.
