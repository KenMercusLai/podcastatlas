# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》347-1｜古代是如何用礼教PUA贞洁烈女的

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses “水清无鱼” to argue that integrity without tolerance or discretion can harden into isolation and severity. It presents [[ZhaoYuWesternHan|赵禹]] as a Western Han official whose cleanliness, strict textual use of law, refusal of social requests, and determination to act independently make him valuable to [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] but troubling to [[ZhouYafu|周亚夫]].

The closing turn extends the danger of extreme consistency from official conduct to women praised as 贞妇 or 烈女. Self-harm, disfigurement, lifelong widowhood, and death are named as costs exchanged for the dead honor of a chastity arch, supplying a compact case of [[GenderedChastityHonorCoercion|贞节荣誉的性别化强制]]. The supplied transcript only introduces that branch, so it does not establish a full historical mechanism, prevalence, chronology, or the women's own motives.

## Key Claims

- The opening maxim contrasts fertile dirty ground with clear water that supports little life, framing tolerance and forgiveness as part of cultivated character rather than moral weakness.
- The episode describes Zhao Yu as clean and capable but also as someone who applies legal texts strictly, treats himself and others harshly, and resists relational accommodation.
- Zhou Yafu reportedly sees those traits as a reason not to entrust Zhao Yu with major responsibility, separating personal probity from fitness for complex office.
- Han Wudi instead values Zhao Yu's sharp writing, appoints him to censorial and court posts, and has him work with [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] on legal revision.
- Zhao Yu refuses reciprocal banquets and requests from officials so that private relationships cannot influence his work; the episode treats the independence as principled but potentially one-sided.
- The host's broader judgment is that conviction remains valuable, but firmness without listening, context, or proportion departs from the mean and may harm the actor or others.
- The ending names widow suicide, bodily binding or injury, disfigurement, and lifelong widowhood as extreme costs through which women could be made into celebrated chaste exemplars.

## Key Quotes

> “地之秽者多生物，水之清者常无余。” - the opening contrast used to argue for tolerance rather than sterile purity.

> “换来的只是死气沉沉的贞节牌坊。” - the closing critique of converting women's suffering into public moral honor.
