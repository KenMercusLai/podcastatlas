# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》172｜刘邦佯游云梦泽 智擒韩信

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-171-zhangliang-jiao-ni-ruhe-mingli-shuangshou-2-lm1iff0cadjapwf2mggwyo8wcvxn|Hanji 171 part 2]] by narrating how [[LiuBang|刘邦]] neutralizes [[HanXin|韩信]] without open war. After someone reports Han Xin for rebellion in 201 BCE, [[ChenPing|陈平]] rejects the generals' direct-attack advice and proposes a [[RitualTourSummonsEntrapment|ritual-tour summons trap]]: Liu Bang will pretend to tour [[Yunmengze|云梦泽]] and gather the vassal kings at [[ChenCountyChuHan|陈县]]. The plan forces Han Xin into a loyalty performance around [[ZhongliMo|钟离眜]], whose suicide leaves Han Xin exposed; Han Xin presents Zhongli Mo's head, is arrested, taken to [[Luoyi|洛阳 / 洛邑]], spared from execution, and demoted from Chu king to a marquis title tied to [[Huaiyin|淮阴]].

## Key Claims
- The episode frames Han Xin's fall through the saying "生死一知己，存亡两妇人," using the arrest as the opening of a larger tragedy rather than a self-contained incident.
- Liu Bang's fear is not irrational panic: after [[PengchengBattle|彭城]] and [[GaixiaBattle|垓下]], he knows Han Xin's command ability exceeds his own and that no Han general is a clear battlefield match.
- Chen Ping's key contribution is diagnostic restraint. He asks whether Han Xin knows of the accusation, whether Liu Bang's troops are stronger, and whether Liu Bang has a commander better than Han Xin before advising against open attack.
- The fake Yunmeng tour and vassal gathering at Chen County exploit imperial protocol: if Han Xin treats the order as ordinary travel, he arrives without war preparation; if he refuses, he confirms central suspicion.
- Han Xin's earlier Qi-kingship demand, delayed response before the final anti-Xiang campaign, post-Gaixia command removal, Chu kingship, and sheltering of Zhongli Mo all accumulate into a trust fracture rather than a sudden rupture.
- Zhongli Mo is the immediate trigger because he is both an old Xiang-side commander and a practical Chu network for Han Xin. Protecting him looks disloyal to Liu Bang, while surrendering him looks like betrayal of friendship and public honor.
- Zhongli Mo's final argument is that Han has not attacked Han Xin because Zhongli Mo's presence still makes Han Xin harder to handle; killing him removes one of Han Xin's remaining deterrents.
- Han Xin's presentation of Zhongli Mo's head does not buy safety. It shows Liu Bang and Chen Ping that the dangerous network has already been cut, so the prearranged arrest can proceed.
- Liu Bang's later pardon language preserves imperial mercy while removing the actual danger: Han Xin keeps his life but loses kingship, territory, and political initiative.
