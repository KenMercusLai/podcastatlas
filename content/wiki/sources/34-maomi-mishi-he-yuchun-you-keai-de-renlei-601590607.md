# 34.猫咪秘史：呵，愚蠢又可爱的人类

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode discusses [[MaomiMishiBook|《猫咪秘史：从史前时期到太空时代》]] through a deliberately cat-centered history of [[Cat|猫]] and human civilization. It argues that cats should not be reduced to cute pets: through [[CatDomesticationHistory]], [[CatAsCivilizationalInfrastructure]], and [[CatReligiousMythology]], the episode makes cats legible as grain protectors, household guardians, ship workers, war companions, literary actors, and experimental space travelers. Its strongest synthesis is [[CatCulturalAmbivalence]]: human cultures repeatedly swing between reverence, practical dependence, suspicion, persecution, affection, and envy of feline independence.

## Key Claims
- [[CatDomesticationHistory]] begins with agriculture: grain storage attracts rodents and disease, making wildcats useful around human settlement before the relationship becomes intimate pet keeping.
- [[CatAsCivilizationalInfrastructure]] captures the episode's practical claim that cats helped protect granaries, homes, temple libraries, ships, and military supplies by controlling rodents and dangerous animals.
- Ancient Egypt is presented as the episode's peak cat-reverence case, with [[Bastet]], cat amulets, legal protection, Bubastis festival culture, and cat-shaped protective mythology.
- Many named cat stories in the episode are legend-heavy rather than strictly evidentiary: Pelusium, Roman soldiers, Siamese soul containers, ship rescues, and battlefield cats are best stored as source claims and cultural signals.
- [[CatReligiousMythology]] extends beyond Egypt into Islamic purity stories, Indian and Southeast Asian soul beliefs, Buddhist library-cat tensions, Japanese noble cats, and Norse or Greco-Roman goddess associations.
- The episode treats Japan as a case where symbolic status can collide with practical need: noble cats become precious, but mouse damage to books, silkworms, and cloth eventually forces cats back into pest-control labor.
- [[CatCulturalAmbivalence]] explains the European reversal from goddess-adjacent animal to suspected demonic or witch-linked companion, especially when night, blackness, pagan survivals, and social fear get mapped onto cats.
- The witchcraft section extends [[WitchHuntMechanism]] by showing how an animal can be pulled into enemy-making: persecuting cats becomes part of persecuting women, outsiders, and supposed hidden powers.
- Literature and legend help rehabilitate cats by making them clever agents in stories such as Puss in Boots, Whittington's cat, and philosophical anecdotes around Montaigne's question of whether he plays with his cat or his cat plays with him.
- [[Felicette]] and ship-cat stories extend the cat branch from premodern agriculture and folklore into modern warfare, polar exploration, naval logistics, and the space age.
