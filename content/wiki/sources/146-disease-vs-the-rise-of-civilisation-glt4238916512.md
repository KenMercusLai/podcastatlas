# 146. Disease vs. the rise of civilisation

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] interview with [[KyleHarper]] presents infectious disease as a central force in human history from hunter-gatherer mobility through agriculture, cities, and the [[BlackDeathHistoricalShock|Black Death]]. Human intelligence, technology, and social organization made people a "cosmopolitan ape" able to transform environments, but each transformation also changed contact with pathogens, waste, animals, vectors, and other human populations.

The episode's modern endpoint is the [[MortalityTransition|mortality transition]]: the historically recent and uneven shift from infectious disease toward chronic and degenerative causes of death. That transition changed childhood survival, family size, education, women's lives, and expectations about what counts as an acceptable death.

## Key Claims

- Humans acquired an unusually large and historically changing disease pool through migration, technology, food production, habitat modification, and social organization.
- Hunter-gatherers carried substantial infectious burdens even though small, mobile groups limited the persistence of some crowd diseases.
- The agricultural transition altered disease ecology long before large states and cities by creating sedentary settlements, concentrated human and animal waste, and sustained exposure to diarrheal disease.
- Pre-modern cities were generally demographic sinks whose deaths exceeded births, so they depended on migration from healthier rural populations.
- Skeletal evidence is presented as showing shorter stature and greater physiological stress in urban than rural populations, including across the Roman world.
- The Black Death likely killed around half the population across very large affected areas, while its reach into China remains contested and its apparent absence from fourteenth-century India remains unexplained.
- The mortality transition began in Britain, the United States, and Western Europe before spreading unevenly, making routine death from infection historically exceptional in much of the developed world.

## Key Quotes

> "history" - Harper's one-word explanation for why humans have accumulated a disease pool unlike that of other apes.

> "cosmopolitan ape" - his description of a species able to enter and transform radically different ecological settings.
