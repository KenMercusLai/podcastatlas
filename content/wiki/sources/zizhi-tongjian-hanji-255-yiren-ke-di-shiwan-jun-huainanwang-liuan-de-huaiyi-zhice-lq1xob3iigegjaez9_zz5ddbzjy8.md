# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》255｜一人可抵十万军，淮南王刘安的怀夷之策

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[LiuAn|淮南王刘安]]'s memorial against [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s expedition toward Yue territory. Liu An joins failed harvests and household distress to distance, terrain, river transport, heat, disease, supply, occupation, and Qin precedent, arguing that an apparently easy punitive campaign could kill soldiers before battle and turn prolonged deployment into domestic disorder.

His alternative is not simple inaction. Liu An proposes sending an envoy to console and reward the Yue communities, restoring a local ruling line, granting seals and titles, taking hostages, and converting uncertain local authority into tributary submission. The episode therefore supplies an early, source-scoped contrast in Liu An's political profile: before the later Huainan rebellion case, he appears here as a sophisticated critic of overextension who claims that one well-chosen envoy can achieve what ten thousand or one hundred thousand troops might not.

## Key Claims

- Liu An begins with domestic capacity: repeated poor harvests, hunger, sale of rank, and sons entering dependent marriages make another long mobilization especially costly.
- Southern terrain, dangerous waterways, dense vegetation, heat, animals, disease, and water-soil mismatch could produce severe noncombat losses before Han forces meet the enemy.
- He treats Yue's mountains and transport limits as a partial defensive barrier for Han as well as an obstacle to invasion, reducing the need for immediate deep conquest if the frontier is guarded.
- Nominal enemy weakness does not make occupation cheap: local forces can retreat into difficult ground, while Han needs much larger combat and supply forces to hold the region.
- Earlier southern expeditions and Qin's Yue campaign are used as cost precedents linking disease, bereaved households, prolonged garrisoning, coerced manpower, banditry, and wider political instability.
- Liu An recommends envoy contact, rewards, restoration of succession, royal recognition, hostages, and tribute as a lower-cost settlement that preserves local authority inside Han hierarchy.
- The episode's title claim that one envoy can equal a great army summarizes a comparative instrument test, not a general proof that diplomacy always replaces force.

## Key Quotes

> “将军率领十万大军能办到的事，一位清差大臣也能办到。” - the episode's concluding formulation of Liu An's lower-cost alternative.

> “不出一兵一卒、不损坏一支戟。” - the episode's summary of the hoped-for effect of recognition and envoy diplomacy.

## Contradictions

- Hanji 256 says Han Wudi proceeds despite Liu An's warning and that visible force helps induce Minyue elites to kill Ying; it later says the emperor recognizes Yu Shan partly because renewed war would cost too much. The sequel therefore qualifies both poles: coercive mobilization can change local incentives, while full conquest can still be rejected on cost grounds.
- The episode opens with the host's family memory of the modern Sino-Vietnamese war. That testimony supplies an ethical frame for war trauma, not direct evidence for Western Han conditions.
- The supplied file is a structured podcast summary rather than a primary-text collation. Harvest conditions, social distress, troop ratios, geography, disease rates, earlier expedition losses, Qin causation, quoted wording, and Liu An's proposed settlement remain episode-attributed.
- “越,” “闽越,” and the reported leaderless succession situation are not treated as interchangeable across every period or passage; the next episode supplies the more specific Ying, Yu Shan, and Chou sequence.
- Liu An's later rebellion planning does not invalidate the operational content of this earlier warning, but this source also does not prove that his advice was disinterested, fully feasible, or decisive in court.
