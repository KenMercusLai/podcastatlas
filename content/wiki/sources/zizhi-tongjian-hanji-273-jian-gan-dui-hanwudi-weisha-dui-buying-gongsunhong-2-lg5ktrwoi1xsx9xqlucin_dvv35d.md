# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》273｜汲黯敢怼汉武帝，为啥怼不赢公孙弘（2）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode contrasts [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]]'s direct public-purpose remonstrance with [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]]'s ability to wait, read [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s reaction, and convert political consequence into a punishment argument. After Ji criticizes imperial horse music as unfit for ancestral ritual, the episode says Gongsun calls the speech slander of sacred institutions and proposes clan punishment; Wudi rejects the proposal and continues to regard Ji as a state-serving minister.

The second half largely repeats the [[ZhuFuYan|主父偃]] prosecution and Gongsun Hong survival arcs developed more fully in Hanji 285, 286, and 289. Its durable addition is the juxtaposition of public concession and delayed counterattack: an official can absorb criticism in one scene yet seek institutional advantage later. That interpretation remains source-scoped because the appointments, prosecution recommendations, and favorable timing do not independently prove concealed revenge or a complete cause of political survival.

## Key Claims

- Ji An argues that royal music should continue ancestral virtue and educate the people rather than celebrate the ruler's acquisition of exceptional horses.
- The episode says Wudi becomes silently displeased and Gongsun Hong immediately labels Ji's intervention “诽谤圣制,” recommending punishment of Ji's clan.
- Wudi does not accept the recommendation; the host explains this through his familiarity with Ji's blunt character and his judgment that Ji is a useful “社稷之臣.”
- The episode attributes Ji's 118 BCE appointment to Huaiyang partly to Gongsun Hong's intervention, while also noting that Wudi grants treatment comparable to a vassal-state chancellor and that Ji dies there seven years later.
- In the Zhufu Yan branch, Gongsun first yields when Wudi supports Shuofang, then later argues that the Qi king's childless death and Qi's absorption make leniency toward Zhufu Yan publicly indefensible.
- The source says Gongsun Hong dies naturally in office in 121 BCE at age eighty and attributes his unusual Wudi-era survival to adaptability, restraint, timing, and sensitivity to imperial preference.
- The closing Wei Qing material is a preview of the next episode and does not supply a developed account of his campaigns or political survival.

## Key Quotes

> “诽谤圣制，罪当族。” - the episode's rendering of Gongsun Hong's proposed response to Ji An's ritual criticism.

> “汲黯是社稷之臣。” - the episode's summary of Wudi's continued public evaluation of a critic he often found uncomfortable.

- [[GongsunHong|公孙弘]] - court actor whom the episode portrays as absorbing criticism publicly while waiting for institutional opportunities against rivals.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler who dislikes Ji's correction but rejects the proposed clan punishment and continues to use him in office.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The horse-song remonstrance substantially overlaps Hanji 318, while the Zhufu Yan argument and Gongsun Hong survival account overlap Hanji 285, 286, and 289.
- Motive tension: this source treats the Huaiyang appointment as Gongsun Hong's opportunity to remove Ji An from the center, whereas Hanji 333-2 emphasizes Wudi's belief that Ji's reputation could stabilize difficult local governance. The purposes can coexist, but neither source proves a single hidden motive.
- Disposition wording: this episode says Wudi initially intended only to remove Zhufu Yan from office, while Hanji 285 says he considered releasing him because admitted bribery was not capital. Both preserve initial noncapital leniency, but the precise intended disposition remains source-scoped.
- The source does not independently establish the two horse episodes, exact song and temple-performance sequence, verbatim dialogue, governing offense, legal possibility or scope of clan punishment, Wudi's private emotions, Liu An's assessment, appointment chronology, Ji An's seven-year interval, or Gongsun Hong's age and complete survival mechanism.
- “Waiting for revenge,” concealed hatred, and the causal claim that adaptability produced Gongsun Hong's good end are the host's political interpretation rather than demonstrated access to private intent.
