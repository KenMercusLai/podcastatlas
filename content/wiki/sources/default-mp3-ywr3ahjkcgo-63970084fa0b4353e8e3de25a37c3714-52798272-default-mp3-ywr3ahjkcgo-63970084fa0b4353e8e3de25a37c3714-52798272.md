# Bringing Up Baby

## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode presents babies and toddlers as active participants in reciprocal communication rather than passive recipients of adult input. [[LauraCirelli]] connects synchronized movement to later helping, [[RachelAlbert]] describes babbling and caregiver response as a rapid feedback loop, and singing experiments show that soothing vocal interaction can regulate both infant and caregiver through [[DyadicLullabyCoregulation]].

The second half has [[AlisonGopnik]] contrast outcome-driven child shaping with [[GardenerParenting]]. [[ExplorationInstructionTradeoff]] explains why direct demonstration can improve narrow immediate performance while reducing discovery, and why play and apprenticeship may produce broader, more adaptable knowledge. [[FunctionSensitiveTantrumResponse]] adds a practical boundary: the same visible outburst can call for different responses depending on its emotional phase and what the behavior is accomplishing.

## Key Claims
- Fourteen-month-olds bounced in synchrony with an adult later helped that adult more often than infants bounced out of synchrony, extending [[RitualSynchronyAndProsociality]] into early development.
- [[ContingentInfantVocalFeedback]] describes how a baby's babble changes caregiver attention and speech, while timely responses and attention to the baby's current focus make later vocalizations more speech-like.
- Tantrums combine persistent distress with sharper anger peaks; reasoning during peak anger can prolong escalation, while the effect of ignoring depends on whether the behavior seeks attention or delays a required task.
- Soothing singing reduced measured arousal in both mothers and babies even without holding or rocking, supporting a bidirectional rather than one-way account of regulation.
- [[GardenerParenting]] treats caregiving as the creation of a safe, varied, responsive environment rather than a technique for producing a predetermined adult.
- In the multifunctional-toy example, explicit demonstration narrowed children's exploration; direct teaching can transmit a known solution while play supports discovery and transfer.
- Participation and apprenticeship can join guidance with agency, especially as children grow, without reducing education to either unguided freedom or constant verbal instruction.
