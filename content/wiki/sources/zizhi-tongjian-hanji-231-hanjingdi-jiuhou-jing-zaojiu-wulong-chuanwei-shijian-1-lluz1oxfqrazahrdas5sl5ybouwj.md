# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》231｜汉景帝酒后竟造就乌龙传位事件（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens the Jingdi-era succession and rebellion sequence through two family injuries. Before naming a crown prince, [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] reportedly tells his younger full brother [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]] while drinking that Liu Wu will inherit the throne; [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] welcomes the prospect, but [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] invokes Han father-to-son succession and is later excluded from court access.

The second branch backfills [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]]'s grievance against the center. Jingdi, while crown prince, reportedly kills Liu Bi's heir [[LiuXianWuCrownPrinceWesternHan|刘贤]] during a drunken board-game quarrel. Liu Bi returns the coffin to Chang'an, avoids court for years, and begins considering revolt when Wu envoys are detained; [[LiuHeng|汉文帝]] then releases the envoys, grants ceremonial gifts, and excuses Liu Bi from attendance, temporarily lowering rather than resolving the conflict.

## Key Claims

- Before a crown prince has been designated, Jingdi reportedly tells Liu Wu that the throne will pass to him, creating political expectations beyond the private setting of the remark.
- Liu Wu formally declines but is pleased, while Dowager Dou welcomes the statement and thereby gives the collateral succession route a second family patron.
- Dou Ying objects that the realm belongs to the Han dynastic order and that succession should pass from father to son; the dowager later has him removed from office and barred from palace access.
- The episode uses the King Cheng and Shu Yu willow-leaf story to argue that a ruler's speech cannot be treated as consequence-free joking.
- While crown prince, Jingdi reportedly kills Liu Xian after a drunken board-game dispute, giving Liu Bi a personal grievance against the imperial household.
- Liu Bi's refusal to attend court, the detention of Wu envoys, fear, and thoughts of rebellion form an escalation chain rather than a single-cause explanation.
- Emperor Wen releases the detained envoys, gives Liu Bi a stool and staff, and excuses future attendance because of age, temporarily restoring calm without removing the underlying autonomy problem.

## Key Quotes

> “千秋万岁后传于王。” - the succession statement attributed to Jingdi during the banquet with Liu Wu.

> “天下者，高祖天下，父子相传，汉之约也。” - Dou Ying's episode-attributed objection that dynastic succession is not a private gift between brothers.

## Connections

- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - ruler whose informal succession speech and earlier killing of Liu Xian create two separate family-political liabilities.
- [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]] - younger brother whose later succession ambition gains an early promise-and-expectation layer.
- [[EmpressDowagerDouWesternHan|窦太后]] - mother whose approval strengthens the Liu Wu route and whose anger exposes Dou Ying.
- [[DouYingWesternHan|窦婴]] - official who turns a family banquet into an institutional succession objection and suffers loss of access.
- [[LiuBiWuKing|吴王刘濞]] - vassal king whose rebellion prehistory now includes bereavement, insult, nonattendance, envoy detention, and temporary appeasement.
- [[LiuXianWuCrownPrinceWesternHan|刘贤]] - Wu heir whose death in Chang'an becomes a durable grievance between Wu and the center.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝]] - emperor whose release, gifts, and attendance exemption temporarily de-escalate Liu Bi's fear and anger.
- [[CollateralSuccessionDisorderRisk|旁支继承的连锁内乱风险]] - later warning whose earliest current stage is an informal promise made before a direct heir is designated.
- [[RebellionOfTheSevenStatesWesternHan|七国之乱]] - later conflict for which this episode supplies an emotional and court-relations prehistory rather than a complete cause.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source backfills the early promise behind Hanji 240's later warning against Liu Wu's succession and the personal grievance behind Hanji 233-235's rebellion sequence.
- Causal boundary: Liu Xian's death, returned coffin, nonattendance, and envoy detention deepen Liu Bi's grievance, but they do not replace territorial reduction, princely autonomy, military capacity, or later coalition politics as causes of the Seven States rebellion.
- Promise boundary: the banquet statement creates expectations in the episode's telling, but it is not treated as a completed designation, edict, or legally effective succession settlement.
- Source-scope caution: the banquet setting, intoxication, exact words, Liu Wu's inner reaction, the willow-leaf precedent, Dou Ying's office and dismissal mechanism, Liu Xian's conduct, the killing instrument, coffin exchange, attendance rules, envoy interrogation, speeches, gifts, exemption, omen, fire, dates, and private motives require comparison with transmitted records.
