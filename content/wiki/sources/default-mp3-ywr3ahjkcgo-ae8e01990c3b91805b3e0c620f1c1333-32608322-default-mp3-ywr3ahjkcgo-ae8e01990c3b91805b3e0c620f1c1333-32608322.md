# The Future of Human Communication: AI, Bionics and Voice Technology | Konrad Zielinski x Shekhar Natarajan

## Summary
This [[TomorrowToday]] interview has [[ShekharNatarajan]] speak with [[KonradZielinski|Konrad Zieliński]], the founder and product user behind [[UhuraBionics]], about non-invasive electronic speech after laryngectomy. Its central synthesis is that useful [[ExpressiveBionicVoiceRestoration]] must restore conversational timing, pitch, emotion, laughter, identity, and user control rather than merely make words intelligible. The episode connects on-device low-latency conversion and wearable design to [[HumanMachineVoiceAgency]], while extending [[AIVoiceCloningRights]], [[SyntheticVoiceIdentityAuthentication]], and [[AssistiveTechnologyDestigmatization]].

## Key Claims
- Conventional electrolarynx speech can be quiet, flat, or robotic, while implanted alternatives can require invasive procedures and continuing maintenance; the company's initial path is non-invasive electronic augmentation.
- Voice restoration is an interaction problem as much as a signal problem: pitch, timing, affection, anger, laughter, crying, shouting, and rapid turn-taking carry social meaning that intelligible words alone do not preserve.
- The guest reports that gaining manual control over intonation improved not only expression but his ability to formulate thoughts that flat output made difficult to articulate.
- The source presents roughly 50 milliseconds as a natural-interaction target, compared with much higher delays in many current conversion systems; it expects progress from smaller models, better chips, more data, and device-side inference.
- The proposed division of labor keeps intention and emotion under human control while AI preserves or reshapes a recognizable personal voice.
- Voice recordings are portable identity data: users should own them, control whether they are shared for training, and be able to move them between providers.
- Pricing and reimbursement shape access, so the company has aligned an earlier product with a Polish reimbursement ceiling while planning higher-priced professional and consumer versions.
- Synthetic speech breaks the assumption that audible voice proves identity; the episode proposes a separate secure credential that can verify a real person using an augmented voice.
- Assistive devices may lose stigma when people without disabilities also choose them, making the technology an ordinary wearable rather than a visible marker of illness.

## Key Quotes
> "scientist of unwords" - Zieliński's description of studying laughter, crying, shouting, affection, and other vocal signals beyond lexical content.

> "one of the first cyborgs" - his framing of electronic speech as human augmentation rather than replacement of identity.
