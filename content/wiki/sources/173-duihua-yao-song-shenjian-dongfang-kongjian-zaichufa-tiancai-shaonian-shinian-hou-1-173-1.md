# 173: 对话姚颂：深鉴、东方空间、再出发，「天才少年」十年后

## Summary
This [[LateTalk]] interview uses [[YaoSong|姚颂]]'s three hard-tech startups, [[DeePhiTech|深鉴科技]], [[Orienspace|东方空间]], and [[StridingAI|Striding AI / 正行创新]], to trace a decade of Chinese hard-tech entrepreneurship. The episode connects [[TsinghuaUniversity|清华大学]] research culture, AI-chip fundraising, strategic acquisition by [[Xilinx|赛灵思]], commercial-space launch strategy, and [[PhysicalAI|physical AI]] route choice. Its main conceptual addition is that physical intelligence is a [[PhysicalIntelligenceSystemStack|system stack]], not a single model, robot body, or demo.

## Key Claims
- [[YaoSong]] frames his startup choices around two lines: important human problems such as survival space, health and happiness, efficiency and fairness; and personal achievement from turning a technical origin into products used by many people.
- [[DeePhiTech]] began from [[TsinghuaUniversity]] AI-chip and acceleration work by [[WangYu]], [[HanSong]], Yao, and classmates, then had to translate technical promise into [[InvestorRiskNarrative]] and market-product language before financing.
- The [[DeePhiTech]] sale to [[Xilinx]] was not presented as only a price decision; Yao says the strategic fit, IPO uncertainty, company risk, and team intensity all mattered, making it a case for [[StrategicAcquirerFit]] and [[PostAcquisitionFounderIdentity]].
- Yao's post-exit "gray" period and later role at [[MatrixPartnersChina|经纬中国]] show [[FounderMotivationEvolution]] after a successful acquisition: the exit removed the first mission before the next direction was clear.
- [[Orienspace]] was chosen from a China-specific commercial-space thesis: satellite internet and future communications demand would need launch capacity, and a rocket company should prioritize high success rate, enough payload capacity, and market timing before cost.
- [[Gravity1Rocket|引力一号]] made [[Orienspace]] publicly visible through a sea launch, unusual solid-rocket configuration, and [[SpaceMissionLivestreaming|public launch livestream]], extending [[SpaceEconomyInfrastructure]] beyond SpaceX-centered cases.
- [[StridingAI]] defines physical intelligence as data, compute, models, algorithms, software, hardware, scenarios, solutions, and remote systems working together, which makes [[EmbodiedAIValueChain]] and vertical integration central rather than optional.
- Yao argues that [[VisionLanguageActionModels|VLA]] work was approaching a bottleneck by late 2025 and that some companies were shifting toward [[RobotReinforcementLearning|reinforcement learning]], [[WorldModels]], and [[WorldActionModels]].
- The company starts from retail and 3C manufacturing scenes through [[CPGroup]] and an ODM partner, using scenario access as the seed for a physical-world data loop and [[MilestoneCommercialization]].
- Yao favors [[WheelBasedDualArmRobots]] for early delivery because bipedal humanoids and dexterous hands add cost, instability, and control difficulty before enough commercial value is proven.
- The source makes [[RobotDemoAuthenticity]] explicit: Yao says all demo videos should state speed倍率 and whether the behavior is fully autonomous, because teleoperation presented as autonomy distorts the market.
- His risk posture is aggressive but bounded: he is willing to go all in on time, energy, and attention, but does not endorse selling homes or removing all fallback options.

## Key Quotes
> "boring 和 suffering" — Yao's borrowed contrast for why a comfortable but slow path felt worse than startup suffering.

> "好多快省" — his ordering for commercial rockets: reliability, payload capacity, speed to market, then cost.

> "八分进攻、两分防守" — his description of continuous-founder risk posture.

> "说出来的一定是真话" — his bottom line for demo and financing communication.
