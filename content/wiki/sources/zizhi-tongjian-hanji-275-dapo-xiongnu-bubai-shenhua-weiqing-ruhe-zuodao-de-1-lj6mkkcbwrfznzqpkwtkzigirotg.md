# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》275｜打破匈奴不败神话，卫青如何做到的？（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the first-command sequence left open by [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-274-cong-qinu-dao-jiangjun-weiqing-shi-ruhe-nixi-de-llgairjhid8rskv4jzx0yyijhmnc|Hanji 274]]. It says [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] turns an intelligence-poor search into a rapid advance on Longcheng by using wide penetration and envelopment, interpreting the absence of contact as evidence that his force has passed the Xiongnu screen, discarding baggage, and accelerating toward a lightly defended target.

The reported victory is modest in killed-enemy numbers but large in comparative and psychological significance because the three parallel columns under [[GongsunHeWesternHan|公孙贺]], [[GongsunAoWesternHan|公孙敖]], and [[LiGuangHanGeneral|李广]] either find no enemy or suffer defeat. The episode therefore treats [[LongchengCampaignWesternHan|龙城之战]] as both proof that the new Han cavalry system could generate offensive surprise and a break in the image of [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] invincibility, while its totals, seasonal theory, strategic turning-point claim, and analogy with the 1937 Pingxingguan battle remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- In the 129 BCE four-route expedition, Wei Qing reportedly treats the absence of contact not as reason to stop but as evidence that his force has moved past the expected Xiongnu defensive screen.
- He uses wide penetration and envelopment, orders baggage discarded, and accelerates roughly two hundred li toward Longcheng.
- The episode describes Longcheng as a southern Xiongnu political and ritual center associated with the White Sheep and Loufan kings and says fewer than three thousand defenders were present.
- Wei Qing's force reportedly kills more than seven hundred, captures more than two thousand, and takes very large livestock holdings; the precise figures and target description require primary-source comparison.
- Gongsun He returns without contact, Gongsun Ao loses most of his reported cavalry, and Li Guang is defeated and captured before escaping, making Wei Qing's result exceptional within an otherwise unsuccessful multi-route operation.
- Han Wudi reportedly condemns Gongsun Ao and Li Guang to death before both redeem the sentence, while Wei Qing receives the rank of guanneihou.
- The host attributes Wei Qing's success to mounted and archery skill, physical courage, respectful treatment of officials, care for soldiers, and command ability, while also praising Han Wudi's selection judgment.
- The episode argues that early spring exposed Xiongnu horse and supply weakness and that the victory broke a psychological barrier; both the seasonal mechanism and wider causal effect remain episode interpretations.

## Key Quotes

> “善骑射，材力绝人。” - the episode's rendering of the appraisal used to explain Wei Qing's personal military capacity.

> “遇士大夫以礼，与士卒有恩。” - the episode's character evidence for why officials and soldiers were willing to serve him.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the current wiki was found. This source completes Hanji 274's pre-Longcheng cliffhanger and supplies an earlier defeat-and-redemption layer for Li Guang and Gongsun Ao.
- Sequence boundary: Hanji 274 establishes the appointment and approach but not combat; this episode supplies the claimed battle and aftermath, so the two notes are complementary rather than duplicate evidence.
- Source-scope caution: route geometry, distance, discarded baggage, defender strength, casualty, captive and livestock totals, Longcheng's precise status, the four commanders' force totals, Gongsun Ao's losses, Li Guang's capture and escape details, sentences, redemption, and Wei Qing's reward require comparison with primary records.
- Strategic caution: a successful surprise attack can weaken an invincibility belief without by itself proving that the entire Han-Xiongnu balance immediately reversed.
- Seasonal caution: the account of horse weight loss, food shortage, pregnant mares, and pregnant Xiongnu women is the host's explanation of a spring window and is not independently established here.
- Analogy caution: the comparison with Pingxingguan is explanatory rhetoric about morale under a stronger enemy, not evidence that the battles had the same scale, operational setting, or historical effect.
