# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》399｜汉武帝托孤大臣生死内斗！目标：干掉霍光！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the opening phase of the anti-[[HuoGuangWesternHan|霍光]] coalition before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-400-1-taidula-hanzhaodi-jing-bipo-gege-yanwang-liudan-zisha-ln0-b64ip2kwpcpen_65e4du3vqc|Hanji 400-1]]'s banquet-assassination and suicide sequence. [[ShangguanJieWesternHan|上官桀]], [[ShangguanAnWesternHan|上官安]], [[GaiChanggongzhuWesternHan|盖长公主]], [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]], and [[LiuDanYanKingWesternHan|燕王刘旦]] first try to destroy Huo by forging a Yan king memorial that accuses him of quasi-imperial display, favoritism, expanded military staff, and possible treason.

The durable synthesis is that [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]]'s discernment prevents slander from becoming an administrative coup. He does not merely trust Huo Guang emotionally; he tests the accusation against time, distance, and motive, concluding that Liu Dan could not have learned recent Chang'an events so quickly and that Huo would not need a few extra command staff if he truly meant rebellion. The episode therefore adds a positive countercase to [[SlanderSuspicionFeedbackCollapse|谗言疑心式反馈失灵]]: where ruler suspicion makes slander actionable, ruler reasoning can keep a forged accusation from becoming state policy.

## Key Claims

- The anti-Huo coalition begins from accumulated grievance: Shangguan kin and Gai Chang Gongzhu resent Huo for refusing offices and titles, Liu Dan resents exclusion from succession, and Sang Hongyang resents Huo's political attack after the Salt and Iron Conference.
- Liu Dan sends repeated gifts and envoys to Chang'an, linking royal ambition with the Shangguan-princess-Sang network before the later open conspiracy.
- The conspirators forge a Yan king memorial accusing Huo Guang of imperial-style travel arrangements, unfair appointment of Su Wu and Yang Chang, and unauthorized expansion of great-general府 staff.
- Their immediate plan is procedural: submit the forged memorial while Huo is away, get Han Zhao to order an investigation, then have Sang Hongyang and ministers strip Huo of office and power.
- Han Zhao withholds the memorial, summons Huo after Huo stops outside the audience hall in fear, and publicly says he knows the memorial is false.
- Han Zhao's reasoning turns on information timing and motive: recent Chang'an events could not plausibly have reached distant Liu Dan, and a true rebel Huo would not need to rely on appointing several additional officers.
- Han Zhao's later public rebuke of anti-Huo gossip identifies Huo as a loyal minister entrusted by the former emperor and temporarily suppresses the coalition's open slander.
- Li Deyu's commentary is used to raise the episode from factional story to ruler-judgment theory: clarity about people and accusations keeps malicious speech from blocking loyal service.
