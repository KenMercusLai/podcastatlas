# 87.过年最适合聊玄学！探讨神仙方术发展史（接上集）

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode continues a history of Chinese immortality arts and fangshu from the Han into the Wei-Jin and Northern-Southern dynasties. It argues that official Confucianism after [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] did not simply oppose "superstition"; it absorbed yin-yang, five phases, disasters, auspicious signs, rainmaking, astrology, and prophecy into imperial political language. The episode then tracks [[ChineseAstrologicalPolitics|star-omen politics]], [[ChenweiPolitics|谶纬政治]], Daoist alchemy and body techniques, talismans, divination, fengshui, and [[BuddhistMiraclePolitics|Buddhist miracle politics]] as systems where technique, religious authority, political legitimacy, and strange-story imagination reinforce one another.

## Key Claims
- [[DongZhongshu|董仲舒]] is used to challenge the simple view that Confucian state ideology naturally restrains magical thinking; the episode presents his rainmaking and omen vocabulary as part of a wider [[ConfucianIdealGovernance|Confucian-imperial]] fusion with yin-yang and five-phase reasoning.
- [[JingFang|京房]] and Han star-omen traditions made Heaven readable as political diagnosis, turning unusual celestial events into commentary on ministers, rulers, war, disaster, and state conduct.
- [[ChenweiPolitics|谶纬政治]] made prophecy a dynastic technology: [[LiuXiu|刘秀]] could benefit from prophecy during restoration politics and later restrict uncontrolled private interpretation.
- [[WangMang|王莽]]'s North Dipper practices, including the "威斗" and crisis-time ritual orientation, show that omen politics could become embodied court behavior rather than only written propaganda.
- [[FeiChangfang|费长房]] illustrates the Han immortal-master story pattern: tests, hidden worlds, talismans, ghost control, and failed transcendence make fangshu narratable as both power and vulnerability.
- The episode's central religious history claim is [[FangshuSystematization|方术系统化]]: Daoist traditions gathered older fangshi arts, Huang-Lao language, internal cultivation, external alchemy, talismans, pacing rituals, and longevity techniques into more explicit systems.
- [[WeiBoyang|魏伯阳]] and [[ZhouyiCantongqi|《周易参同契》]] anchor the episode's alchemy branch, where coded language such as mercury and sulfur terminology links immortality discourse to material transformation.
- [[DaoistNourishingLifeTechniques|守一、行气、胎息、导引]] are presented as immortality-oriented practices, not merely health exercises; ordinary bodily benefits are secondary to the goal of becoming an immortal.
- [[DaoistRitualTechnology|Daoist ritual technology]] includes pacing the Dipper, talismans, spells, swords, mirrors, and command phrases such as "急急如律令", with Buddhist mantra transmission becoming one ingredient in later spell culture.
- [[PortableDivinationSystems|Portable divination systems]] such as 六壬, 奇门遁甲, 文王课, coin divination, and lots translate large cosmologies into smaller decision devices.
- [[ZhangHua|张华]], [[Bowuzhi|《博物志》]], [[GuoPu|郭璞]], [[Zangshu|《葬书》]], and [[GeHong|葛洪]] show the Wei-Jin stage in which strange knowledge, fengshui, and immortality theory become more textualized.
- [[Fotucheng|佛图澄]] shows Buddhist supernatural powers entering northern political life: miracle stories could support religious spread and elite patronage even under violent rulers.

## Key Quotes
> "万古丹经王" - the episode's phrase for [[ZhouyiCantongqi|《周易参同契》]].

> "我命由我不由天" - discussed through [[GeHong|葛洪]] and [[Baopuzi|《抱朴子》]] as an immortality and alchemy claim before later popular reuse.

> "急急如律令" - treated as an administrative phrase absorbed into later Daoist spell formulae.
