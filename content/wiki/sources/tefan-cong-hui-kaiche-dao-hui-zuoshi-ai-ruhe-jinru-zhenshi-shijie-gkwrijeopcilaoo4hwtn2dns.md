# 特番｜从会开车到会做事：AI如何进入真实世界

## Summary
This [[ShangyeJiushiZheyang|商业就是这样]] live episode with three [[HorizonRobotics|地平线 / Horizon Robotics]] guests explains [[PhysicalAI]] through assisted driving and robots. Its strongest synthesis is that physical-world intelligence is a closed loop of perception, decision, and control constrained by safety, latency, power, hardware, data, production, and responsibility: current L2 systems remain human-supervised, user trust increasingly depends on calm and predictable behavior, and the vehicle-first route can finance and teach a later robotics platform without making household humanoids imminent.

## Key Claims
- [[AutonomousDrivingResponsibilityBoundary]] is more stable than marketing labels: L2 remains “car drives, human watches,” L4 transfers fallback responsibility to the system, and L3 is difficult because responsibility changes under bounded conditions.
- [[HumanlikeAssistedDriving]] is a product-quality layer above collision avoidance: stable steering, early deceleration, natural cornering, decisive but restrained lane changes, and long-duration predictability determine whether users stay comfortable enough to keep the system engaged.
- Regulation can improve trust before an accident by binding L3 claims to clearer product and liability obligations and by reducing ambiguous “L2.x” marketing.
- [[HorizonRobotics|地平线]] argues that algorithms and chips must be co-designed because vehicle compute is limited by multi-year silicon cycles, power, heat, space, real-time response, and the future model load anticipated when the chip is defined.
- Platform suppliers may spread long-cycle chip and software investment across customers, while automaker self-development remains viable for a smaller group; the episode's proposed 20/80 split is a forecast, not an established market law.
- Cars provide a vehicle-first [[EmbodiedIntelligenceVehiclePath]] because mature supply chains, large annual volume, installed compute, field data, and cash flow can support later robot development.
- Robot “brain” planning and “small-brain” motion control must work together, but robot control adds many joints, balance, contact, tactile and force feedback, and action-level data that vehicle experience cannot directly solve.
- Near-term robot commercialization is more plausible in guiding, display, bounded industrial work, companionship, and simplified wheeled forms than in general household care or universal humanoids.
- Industrial robot performance is jointly limited by the body, actuators, sensors, control freedom, data quality, simulation, algorithms, and process design rather than by model capability alone.
