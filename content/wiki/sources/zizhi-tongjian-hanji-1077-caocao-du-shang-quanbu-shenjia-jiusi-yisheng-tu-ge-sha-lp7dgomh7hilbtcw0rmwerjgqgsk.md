# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1077丨曹操赌上全部身家，九死一生图个啥？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the decision phase before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1078-caocao-kao-zhe-zhao-mantian-guohai-meijun-zhijin-zai-yanjiu-lvmyikhf0lgekipw_ziebcw29zle|Hanji 1078]]'s route deception and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1079-bi-sharen-hai-du-caocao-fang-diren-neidou-zuoshou-yuli-lvzyzsytejoxu_qth-hhgvee6lrv|Hanji 1079]]'s [[BailangMountainLateHan|白狼山之战]] outcome. In Jian'an 12 / 207 CE, [[CaoCao|曹操]] treats [[Wuhuan|乌桓]] support for [[YuanShang|袁尚]] and [[YuanXiLateHan|袁熙]] as an unresolved northern threat, while [[GuoJia|郭嘉]] argues that surprise can exploit Wuhuan unpreparedness and [[LiuBiao|刘表]]'s reluctance to use [[LiuBei|刘备]] against an exposed Xu capital.

The source frames the expedition as calibrated but highly exposed risk, not effortless foresight. Guo Jia then urges Cao Cao to shed baggage and accelerate; rain, mud, and a guarded route immediately reveal the logistical cost of that choice before [[TianChou|田畴]] appears. Because the transcript stops at Tian Chou's introduction, it establishes the campaign's motive and risk model but does not independently narrate the route solution or battle result.

## Key Claims

- After defeating the Yuan position and consolidating much of the north, Cao Cao still faces a frontier problem because Yuan Shang and Yuan Xi take refuge with Wuhuan and Wuhuan cavalry continue raiding.
- The episode says [[YuanShao|袁绍]] had cultivated Wuhuan support and recognized [[TadunLateHan|蹋顿]] as a Wuhuan king, giving the Yuan refugees a pre-existing alliance rather than a neutral place of exile.
- Cao Cao rewards more than twenty contributors and tries to elevate [[XunYu|荀彧]] to one of the Three Excellencies; the episode interprets Xun Yu's repeated refusal as preserving distance between Han-court legitimacy and Cao Cao's personal authority.
- Cao Cao's officers oppose the distant expedition on two concrete grounds: long supply lines to Wuhuan and the danger that Liu Biao might use Liu Bei to strike [[Xudu|许都]] while the main force is away.
- Guo Jia argues that distance has made Wuhuan complacent, so an unexpected attack can succeed before the frontier coalition prepares.
- Guo Jia also predicts that Liu Biao will not exploit Cao Cao's absence because he both needs Liu Bei and fears giving Liu Bei enough authority to become uncontrollable.
- Cao Cao accepts the argument, and at Yi County Guo Jia recommends discarding heavy baggage for a rapid advance intended to preserve surprise.
- Summer rain, mud, and Wuhuan control of the main road then immobilize the army, showing that speed gained by reducing baggage does not abolish terrain, supply, or route risk.
- The transcript ends when Tian Chou appears; his identity, alternative-route proposal, the later march, and the campaign outcome must be taken from later source notes rather than inferred from this episode.

## Key Quotes

> "兵贵神速。" - the episode's compact principle behind Guo Jia's recommendation to reduce baggage and accelerate.

> "刘表不过是个坐谈客。" - the episode's rendering of Guo Jia's judgment that Liu Biao would not convert Cao Cao's absence into action.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is recorded. This episode supplies the decision and early-movement phase that Hanji 1078 and Hanji 1079 continue.
- The age-based claim that fifty-two was exceptionally old because average late-Han life expectancy was only in the thirties is not supported by a cited demographic source and should not be treated as a direct measure of an adult elite's remaining life expectancy.
- The interpretation of Xun Yu's refusal as an explicit gentry-versus-hanmen balance and silent resistance to Cao Cao is plausible commentary in the episode, not established access to Xun Yu's private motive.
- The “risk investment” and “prisoner's dilemma” labels are modern analogies. They help summarize Guo Jia's reasoning but do not establish that the historical actors used those formal models.
- Exact dialogue, the count and meaning of repeated refusals, the condition of the ancient road, Wuhuan mental states, and detailed motives remain source-scoped.
- The transcript is incomplete. It cannot support claims about Tian Chou's proposal, the route actually taken, the White Wolf Mountain battle, or the expedition's final outcome without the later episodes.
