# The Future of Biology, AI and Human Innovation with Michael Heltzen | Shekhar Natarajan

## Summary
This [[TomorrowToday]] episode has [[ShekharNatarajan]] interview [[MichaelHeltzen]] about his path from day trading into bioinformatics, CRISPR biosensing, and [[Exozyme]]'s attempt to manufacture scarce natural molecules with engineered enzymes outside living cells. Its strongest technical contribution is a linked platform model: [[CellFreeEnzymeEngineering]] simplifies and controls the reaction environment, while an [[ExperimentGroundedProteinModelLoop]] turns both successful and confidently wrong predictions into proprietary training data.

The conversation also discusses AI risk, cultural and language representation, scientific commercialization, leadership, and durable human skills. The supplied file is a structured Chinese summary rather than a transcript or evidence review, so product efficacy, prevalence, regulatory timing, safety, and company-performance claims remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- [[Bioinformatics]] translated high-volume sequencing output into interpretable biological knowledge and helped make new sequencing capacity practically useful.
- [[NanoSense]] combined a CRISPR-related recognition mechanism with a semiconductor sensor to detect molecular binding electronically and support source-described gene-editing quality assurance.
- [[CellFreeEnzymeEngineering]] removes the living cell from a production pathway, trading cellular self-maintenance and adaptation for a more controlled but still technically demanding enzyme system.
- [[Exozyme]] targets natural molecules that may be biologically interesting but too scarce for economical extraction, including the source-described [[NCTExozyme|NCT]] program and rare non-intoxicating cannabinoids.
- [[ProteinLanguageModels]] can propose enzyme changes, but practical progress depends on rapid wet-lab testing and an [[ExperimentGroundedProteinModelLoop]] that learns from both correct and high-confidence incorrect predictions.
- Platform value must be demonstrated through a product, manufacturing process, regulatory route, and bounded use case rather than through a broad technology claim alone.
- AI-era adaptability is framed as a combination of hard skills, listening, empathy, collaboration, purpose, and initiative rather than a bet on one permanently automation-proof occupation.
