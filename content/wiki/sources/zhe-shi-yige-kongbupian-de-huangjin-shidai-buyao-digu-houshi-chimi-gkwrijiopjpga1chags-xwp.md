# 这是一个恐怖片的黄金时代！不要低估《后室》《痴迷》

## Summary

This [[DianyingJubian|电影巨辩]] episode uses [[TheBackroomsFilm|《后室》]] and [[ObsessionCurryBarkerFilm|《痴迷》]] to argue that horror has become one of contemporary Hollywood's most active spaces for low-budget production, young directors, social criticism, bodily spectatorship, and unresolved endings. It connects [[ContemporaryHorrorFilmResurgence]] to the low-cost model of [[BlumhouseProductions]], the cultural branding of [[A24]], the festival strategy of [[NeonFilmDistributor]], and an [[InternetNativeFilmmakingPipeline]] that can move creators such as [[KaneParsons]] and [[CurryBarker]] from YouTube proof-of-concept work into features.

The two films also expose different adaptation outcomes. The episode praises the Backrooms short for making [[LiminalSpaceHorror|space itself the protagonist]], but argues that feature-length character, trauma, and monster conventions reduce its uncertainty. It reads 《痴迷》 more favorably through [[ConsentViolationHorror]]: a wish that overrides a woman's will turns romantic persistence, possession, and the “nice guy” fantasy into bodily horror.

## Key Claims

- [[ContemporaryHorrorFilmResurgence]] is enabled by the contraction of mid-budget drama, the relative affordability of horror, its low dependence on stars, and its ability to convert social anxiety into sensory experience.
- Horror's capacity to end without resolving its underlying conflict makes it unusually suited to social problems that have no simple real-world solution.
- [[BlumhouseProductions]], [[A24]], [[NeonFilmDistributor]], and film festivals occupy different but complementary positions in the newer horror ecosystem: low-risk production, cultural-event marketing, art-house prestige, and collective theatrical discovery.
- [[InternetNativeFilmmakingPipeline]] lets audience response, clicks, and short-form proof of effect supplement traditional film-school, advertising, festival, and producer routes into directing.
- The Backrooms short succeeds as [[LiminalSpaceHorror]] because its empty transitional environment remains familiar, uninhabitable, temporally unstable, and resistant to explanation.
- The episode argues that [[TheBackroomsFilm|the feature adaptation]] weakens that effect by translating spatial uncertainty into characters, trauma, psychology, and conventional commercial-horror beats.
- [[ObsessionCurryBarkerFilm|《痴迷》]] turns a wish for love into [[ConsentViolationHorror]] because the wish bypasses Nikki's consent and treats her will as something Bear may overwrite.
- Horror can function as a social-pressure outlet and as training in tolerating uncertainty, but those cultural claims remain interpretive rather than demonstrated audience effects.
