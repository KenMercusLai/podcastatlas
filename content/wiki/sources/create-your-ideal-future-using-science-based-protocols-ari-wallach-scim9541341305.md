# Create Your Ideal Future Using Science-Based Protocols | Ari Wallach

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] conversation has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[AriWallach]] about widening the time horizon of personal and collective decisions. Wallach's [[LongPathFramework]] combines [[TransgenerationalEmpathy]], plural futures thinking, and telos; the episode makes those abstractions practical through future-self letters and aged images, an empty frame for descendants, behavioral modeling, inherited-story review, [[CathedralThinking]], and [[Protopia]]. It also argues that notification and algorithmic reward environments can pull people into reactive presentism, while emotionally vivid future relationships can restore agency without pretending that one ideal future is fixed or guaranteed.

## Key Claims
- Humans can use memory to simulate possible futures and coordinate around preferred outcomes, but high-frequency notifications and short reward loops can narrow that capacity into reactive presentism.
- [[LongPathFramework]] joins empathy across generations, active consideration of multiple possible futures, and telos or ultimate aim.
- [[TransgenerationalEmpathy]] begins with compassion toward one's earlier and present selves, then extends backward to ancestors and forward to future selves and descendants.
- [[FutureSelfContinuity]] can be strengthened by making a later self concrete through an aged image, a written letter, or another vivid cue, although the episode does not establish universal effect sizes for these practices.
- [[CathedralThinking]] reframes legacy as work and modeled behavior whose beneficiaries may live beyond the actor's lifetime, rather than as personal credit.
- [[Protopia]] supplies a storytelling alternative to both utopia and dystopia: an imperfect future that is better enough to guide present action.
- Everyday behavior, family modeling, institutions, and stories are presented as the main transmission channels through which long-horizon choices scale.

## Key Quotes
> "Mental time travel" — Wallach's term for imagining possible futures from remembered experience.

> "The future with an s" — the episode's reminder that futures are plural and made through choices rather than passively awaited.

> "Casino in the pocket" — Huberman's analogy for smartphone systems organized around intermittent short-term reward.
