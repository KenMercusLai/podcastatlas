## Summary
This [[EconomistPodcasts]] episode links three ways of measuring power under change. [[GadiEisenkot]] receives the mandate to try to replace [[BenjaminNetanyahu]] through a minority-government strategy constrained by [[CoalitionExclusionDeadlock]]; satellite heat detections suggest modest Ukrainian battlefield gains even as [[Russia]] expands its [[CivilianInfrastructureAirCampaign]]; and [[WhosWho|Who’s Who]] becomes valuable less as a current directory than as a [[LongitudinalEliteBiographicalDataset|longitudinal record of Britain’s elite]].

## Key Claims
- The certified [[IsraeliElection2026|Israeli election]] result produced no majority, and the president gave [[GadiEisenkot]] up to six weeks to form a government and win confidence in the Knesset.
- Eisenkot’s initial plan joins four opposition parties holding only about one third of the seats, so it requires outside support or abstention from Arab parties, ultra-Orthodox parties, or both.
- [[AvigdorLieberman]] is separately pursuing an understanding with ultra-Orthodox parties while rejecting a minority government dependent on Arab-party support, showing that removing Netanyahu does not eliminate opposition-side vetoes.
- Failed legal challenges and ratification of the result moved [[BenjaminNetanyahu]] from contesting the election to raising the political cost of an Arab-supported alternative and proposing a unity government under his own leadership.
- The episode reports weakening unconditional support for Israel among U.S. Democrats and criticism from [[DonaldTrump]], [[JDVance]], and other Republicans, reducing the domestic value of Netanyahu’s claim to manage the American relationship.
- [[SatelliteHeatSignatureConflictMonitoring]] repurposes a [[NASA]] fire-detection system to infer explosion activity; the episode reports the most intense comparable three-month period of the war and a net Ukrainian gain of 73 square kilometres.
- [[OperationVivaldi]] is described as a four-stage May-to-October counteroffensive that disrupted the northern arm of a Russian attempt to encircle three strategically important Ukrainian cities.
- The territorial shift remains small and coexists with severe attrition: the episode gives wide ranges of 370,000–780,000 Russian military deaths and 116,000–220,000 Ukrainian military deaths.
- Russian strikes at least 100 kilometres beyond the front or border reportedly tripled from June to September, with 290 civilian-infrastructure targets and at least 62 civilian deaths in the preceding month.
- Ukrainian long-range attacks also continued, but the episode reports a much sharper Russian acceleration and treats the resulting winter blackouts, rail attacks, and damage to industry as an asymmetric campaign against state and social capacity.
- First published in 1849, [[WhosWho|Who’s Who]] accumulated more than 125,000 entries across 179 editions, making its consistent biographical fields useful for comparing elite life across long periods.
- The current directory is highly unrepresentative—reported as 80% male and 97% white, with strong London, private-school, Oxford, and Cambridge concentration—so it documents elite selection rather than British society as a whole.
- [[Bloomsbury]] is selling the publication, and uncertainty over the buyer and continuation creates a preservation risk even though British elites themselves will persist.
