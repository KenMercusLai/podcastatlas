# 图拉斯｜卖了三十年雪饼的旺旺，为何推不出下一款明星产品？

## Summary

This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode opens with short updates on [[Apple]], [[Siri]], [[ByteDance]], [[Doubao]], [[Meituan]], [[LOreal|L'Oreal / 欧莱雅]], and China's pet-insurance market, then asks why [[WantWantChina|Want Want China / 旺旺食品]] has not produced another star product after decades of rice crackers and flavored milk. Its durable synthesis is that frequent launches do not resolve [[HeritageProductInnovationDebt]] when formulation, decision-making, marketing, and channel incentives still favor old hero products. The company case also extends [[ValueSnackChannelConsolidation]]: fast-growing discount snack chains can increase distribution while using familiar classics as traffic drivers, leaving new products with weak shelf support.

## Key Claims

- The source says Want Want's fiscal-2025 revenue rose slightly while net profit fell about 11.5%, followed by a warning that April-June profit would fall by nearly 40%, employee self-assessments, reported layoffs, and an internal letter from [[TsaiEngMeng|Tsai Eng-meng / 蔡衍明]] describing a major operating crisis.
- Want Want has not stopped launching products: the episode cites a multi-brand strategy from 2015, about 50 launches in 2017, later entries into coffee, electrolyte drinks, and low-alcohol beverages, and roughly 15% of latest-year revenue from products introduced during the previous five years.
- The source argues that launch volume has not produced a successor to Want Want Milk or its rice-cracker lines. Several newer milk products reportedly exceeded RMB30 million in revenue, but remain small beside the legacy milk business.
- The company's foundational products relied on Japanese rice-cracker and recombined-milk know-how. That transfer enabled scale, but the episode argues that later innovation concentrated on flavor variants while consumers moved toward freshness, lower sugar, buffalo milk, and pasteurized-milk positioning.
- The episode describes Want Want as a family-controlled company in which several of Tsai's children hold important roles but major decisions still return to the chairman. It also says the executive bench is weighted toward production, R&D, and procurement rather than newer ecommerce and content-distribution capabilities.
- Television-era mass advertising made phrases such as “再看我就把你喝掉” and “你旺我旺大家旺” widely memorable, but fragmented attention makes the same broad-reach playbook less effective for new launches.
- Traditional wholesale still reportedly supplies more than half of company revenue but recently declined at a double-digit rate. Value-snack retail reached about 15% of revenue, yet those stores mainly choose familiar Want Want products and use Want Want Milk as a traffic item.
- The source says classic products retain prominent shelves while 2026 launches sit at shelf edges, and that low distributor margin gives supermarkets little reason to displace higher-margin competing brands. New products therefore compete both with outside brands and with Want Want's own proven sellers.
- Short news items say Apple's AI-enabled Siri began a limited, opt-in rollout; ByteDance released a consumer Doubao phone assistant with app-declared automation boundaries; Meituan tightened overseas food-delivery accountability; a L'Oreal-linked fund invested in a Chinese color-cosmetics brand; and Chinese pet insurance grew rapidly while identification and veterinary-pricing problems constrained adoption.
