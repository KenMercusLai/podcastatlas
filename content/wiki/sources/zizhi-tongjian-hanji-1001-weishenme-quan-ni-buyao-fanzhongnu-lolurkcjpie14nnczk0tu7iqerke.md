# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1001丨为什么劝你不要犯众怒？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the pre-claim setup before the [[YuanShu|袁术]] branch developed in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1002-yuanshu-chengdi-hou-sunce-chengwei-zui-da-ying-llya3c2-pefujwxdxefvqxwxjizjo|Hanji 1002]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1015-sanguo-luanshi-weishenme-yuanshu-gan-chengdi-lim0jemkwyjcesj2nvsr3jndhxfk|Hanji 1015]]. Yuan Shu interprets prophecy, five-phases language, family ancestry, and the [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]] as support for claiming the throne, but his own circle responds with silence and remonstrance. [[YanXiangLateHan|阎象]] and [[ZhangChengLateHan|张承]] make the episode's core claim: rule depends on virtue, accepted name, and people-support, not only territory, population, soldiers, or symbolic objects.

## Key Claims
- The host opens with [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s family-instruction contrast between leaving money, books, and accumulated virtue, making 德行 the interpretive frame before the late-Han story begins.
- In 196 CE, [[YuanShu|袁术]] wants to act against virtue and claim the imperial title after hearing that [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]] has been defeated at Cao Yang.
- Yuan Shu reads the phrase "代汉者当涂高" through his own name and courtesy name 公路, treating prophecy as a hint of personal destiny.
- Yuan Shu also uses [[YinYangFivePhasesPoliticalTheory|阴阳五行政治理论]]: he links the Yuan lineage to Chen and Shun's earth virtue, then treats Han's fire virtue as something earth can overcome.
- The [[HeirloomImperialSeal|传国玉玺]] strengthens Yuan Shu's illusion because he has forced it from [[SunJianLateHan|孙坚]]'s household, but the episode treats the seal as a symbol that cannot manufacture consent by itself.
- When Yuan Shu gathers his generals to discuss enthronement, no one voices support, showing that the title project lacks internal consensus even before public opposition hardens.
- [[YanXiangLateHan|阎象]] invokes [[ZhouWenwang|周文王]]: Zhou had accumulated virtue from Hou Ji onward and still served Shang when two-thirds of the realm had attached to it.
- Yan Xiang's comparison says Yuan Shu's family prestige is less than Zhou's accumulated virtue, while the Han house has not yet become a Shang Zhou-style tyranny that would justify replacement.
- Yuan Shu's silence after Yan Xiang's remonstrance signals that the warning lands but does not yet correct his ambition.
- [[ZhangFanLateHan|张范]] refuses Yuan Shu's invitation and sends his younger brother [[ZhangChengLateHan|张承]], keeping the family from directly validating Yuan Shu's project.
- Yuan Shu asks Zhang Cheng whether his territory, population, and military force could support a Qi Huan Gong-style hegemony or Han Gaozu-style founding.
- Zhang Cheng answers that ordering the realm is "in virtue, not in strength": a benevolent actor can rise from small beginnings, while an usurper moving against Heaven and people will be abandoned.
- The episode's "do not provoke public anger" conclusion joins [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]], [[TitleRealityRestraint|名实相符式名号节制]], and [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|民心型政治安全]]: symbols and force become liabilities when they outrun accepted moral and political order.
- The closing "近者悦，远者来" and "仁者无敌" discussion connects Yuan Shu's failure to [[Confucius|孔子]] and [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] as a broader people-facing legitimacy test.

## Key Quotes
> "在德不在强" - Zhang Cheng's compact answer to Yuan Shu's force-based question.

> "近者悦，远者来" - the Confucian governing formula used to explain why people-support comes before expansion.

> "仁者无敌" - the Mencian frame the host uses for legitimacy grounded in humane rule.

## Contradictions
