# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》385-2｜“巫蛊之祸”最大的幕后黑手，居然是他！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] short episode fills the missing Wugu escalation stage between the upstream support-network purge in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-384-1-fanlian-wuqing-hanwudi-cuihui-weiqing-jiazu-lp-rwqpmccvlaochibspvikhb96z|Hanji 384-1]] and the open armed crisis in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-386-2-diwang-wuqing-huanghou-weizifu-beipo-zisha-lrvdfib62oczcy7loqss4-prkgl|Hanji 386-2]]. It centers on [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]] using staged material evidence, Hu shamans, blood traces, torture, and expanding denunciation to turn [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s illness and suspicion into a case against [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]].

The durable synthesis is that the episode treats the crisis as manufactured legal terror before it becomes battle. Jiang Chong first expands the case outward through coerced confessions and mutual accusations, then moves inward through palace searches, reaches [[EmpressWeiZifuWesternHan|卫子夫]] and Liu Ju's quarters, and places Liu Ju in a no-clear-exit dilemma. [[ShiDeWesternHan|石德]] advises arresting Jiang Chong under a forged command to investigate the plot, but Liu Ju hesitates because false edicts and killing ministers violate the father-son and ruler-minister order he still hopes will protect him.

## Key Claims

- Jiang Chong's Wugu investigation is presented as evidence manufacturing rather than ordinary casework: shamans search for buried figures, blood is planted, and torture produces confessions.
- Coerced confession turns the case into a scalable terror mechanism, expanding from individuals to families, villages, and a nationwide panic.
- Han Wudi's illness and suspicion create the permission structure that lets Jiang Chong claim the palace itself contains harmful "gu qi."
- The search sequence matters politically: Jiang Chong moves from less-favored palace women toward Wei Zifu and finally Liu Ju's Eastern Palace.
- The episode treats the wooden figures and written charges found in Liu Ju's palace as a successful frame-up rather than neutral evidence.
- Shi De's advice introduces the Fusu precedent: when the emperor is unreachable and a malicious minister may control commands, waiting can be fatal.
- Liu Ju's hesitation is framed as moral and strategic weakness: he refuses false-command and minister-killing options even though access to Han Wudi is already blocked.
