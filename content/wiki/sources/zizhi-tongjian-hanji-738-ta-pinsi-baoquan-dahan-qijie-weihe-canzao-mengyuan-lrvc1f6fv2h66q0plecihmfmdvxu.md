## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills 65-67 CE / Yongping 8-10 immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-739-zuoyong-tianxia-meinv-de-huangdi-qishi-ye-hui-gudu-lsoogpawume-fpmitbigklhzdbjl|Hanji 739]]. It centers on [[ZhengZhongFrontierEasternHan|郑众]] warning [[EmperorMingOfHan|汉明帝]] that a return mission to [[NorthernXiongnuLateHan|北匈奴]] would strengthen the chanyu's prestige, unsettle [[SouthernXiongnu|南匈奴]] and [[Wuhuan|乌桓]], and force Han ritual dignity into a hostile stage.

The durable synthesis is that early Yongping rule already combines high state order with dangerous interpretive errors. Mingdi builds annual official evaluation, Confucian education, and ritual music around the court, but he first treats Zheng Zhong's frontier warning as defiance, then needs outside testimony to recognize that Zheng's refusal to kowtow protected Han dignity. The same episode gives [[LiuJingGuanglingKingEasternHan|广陵王刘荆]] as a royal-kin treason boundary case and [[DingHongEasternHan|丁鸿]] as a title-succession duty case corrected by [[BaoJunEasternHan|鲍峻]].

## Key Claims

- Zheng Zhong's objection to a Northern Xiongnu return mission is a credibility argument: visible Han contact could help the chanyu stabilize Western Regions loyalty, disrupt Southern Xiongnu trust, and make wavering groups doubt Han resolve.
- The kowtow dispute turns envoy etiquette into state dignity; Zheng's fear is that a single coerced ritual act would be read as Han submission rather than personal survival.
- Mingdi's imprisonment of Zheng shows how direct loyal remonstrance can be recoded as disobedience until external testimony restores the original meaning.
- Mingdi's annual best/worst official reporting, aristocratic classics schooling, and palace education for outer-relative children show a court trying to convert learning and evaluation into governance order.
- Liu Jing's case tests whether imperial brotherhood can protect suspected rebellion and curse rituals from public law; Fan Tiao's answer makes the empire a dynastic trust rather than the emperor's private property.
- Ding Hong's attempted title-yielding is recoded by Bao Jun as avoidance of state-assigned responsibility, not pure virtue.

## Key Quotes

> "绝不愿手持大汉符节向单于行叩拜礼" - Zheng Zhong's reason for refusing the mission under coercive ritual risk.

> "天下是高祖皇帝的天下，不是陛下私产" - Fan Tiao's answer when Mingdi objects to executing his brother.

> "不能因为个人私事放弃国家给予的责任" - Bao Jun's argument that Ding Hong should inherit the title.
