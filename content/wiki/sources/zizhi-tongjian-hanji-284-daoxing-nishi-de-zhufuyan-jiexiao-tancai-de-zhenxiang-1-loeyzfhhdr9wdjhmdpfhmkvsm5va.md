# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》284｜倒行逆施的主父偃，揭晓贪财的真相（1）

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] installment is an interpretive preface to the developed [[ZhuFuYan|主父偃]] case in Hanji 284 part 2. It uses a desire-escalation song attributed to 朱载堉, the phrase “倒行逆施,” and the instability of wealth to frame Zhufu Yan's reported greed as a mechanism that can turn talent and rapid advancement toward self-destruction.

The second half contrasts [[XunYue|荀悦]]'s orthodox criticism of youxia with [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s sympathetic treatment in [[Shiji|《史记》]]. The host links Sima Qian's admiration to political abandonment and unaffordable commutation before his castration, arguing that formal moral status did not produce aid while people acting through侠义 might. The durable contribution is a conflict between official order and extra-institutional rescue, not a claim that every youxia action is just.

## Key Claims

- The song attributed to Zhu Zaiyu presents desire as cumulative: food and clothing give way to houses, partners, transport, servants, office, kingship, emperorship, and immortality rather than stable satisfaction.
- The host introduces “倒行逆施” as conduct that disregards ordinary moral constraints and says the phrase is associated with Zhufu Yan under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]].
- The episode states its thesis before supplying the later case detail: Zhufu Yan's pursuit of money destroys both his career and his life.
- Xun Yue is presented as condemning youxia from an orthodox Confucian concern for social order and established rules.
- Sima Qian's inclusion of a Youxia biography in Shiji is read as respect for people willing to aid those in distress when status-conscious officials remain silent.
- The host connects that respect to Sima Qian's own punishment: he allegedly could not afford commutation, received no effective assistance from powerful Confucian officials, and accepted castration in order to continue Shiji.
- The episode treats侠义 as politically uncomfortable because aid and resistance organized outside official hierarchy can challenge power.
- Its closing wealth claim is conditional rather than celebratory: wealth has no permanent owner, and the ability to acquire or retain it matters more than possession alone.

## Key Quotes

> “倒行逆施” - the phrase used to preview Zhufu Yan's willingness to pursue his aims without ordinary restraint.

> “天下熙熙，皆为利来；天下攘攘，皆为利往” - the Shiji-linked maxim used to frame interest and wealth as pervasive social forces.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with Hanji 284 part 2, Hanji 285, or the existing Sima Qian punishment pages was found. This excerpt supplies moral and historiographical framing rather than a competing chronology.
- Scope caution: the excerpt does not narrate the concrete acts by which Zhufu Yan allegedly pursued wealth or reached his final punishment; those claims depend on the continuation.
- Attribution caution: the exact text and authorship of the “十不足” song, the wording and historical origin of “倒行逆施,” and the episode's characterization of Xun Yue and Sima Qian require primary-text comparison.
- Causal caution: Sima Qian's hardship can make his sympathy for youxia intelligible, but this episode does not prove that lack of money or official aid caused his historiographical choices.
- Generalization caution: criticism of official non-assistance does not establish that every Confucian official refused help or that extra-institutional侠义 is always ethically or politically beneficial.
