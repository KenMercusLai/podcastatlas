## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode completes the next phase of the 219 CE [[FanchengBattle219LateHan|Fancheng campaign]]. [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] uses a false target and rapid switch to break [[GuanYu|关羽]]'s siege works, while [[LyuMeng|吕蒙]] disguises troops as merchants, removes the river warning network, and takes [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]] after [[FuShirenLateHan|傅士仁]] and [[MiFangLateHan|糜芳]] surrender.

The episode connects battlefield relief to rear-area collapse. Guan Yu's long-running contempt for the two subordinates and his threat to punish their supply failure increase their incentive to defect, but this interpersonal explanation remains only one part of a sequence also shaped by captive-driven provisioning pressure, Wu intelligence, surprise, and coercive bargaining. Lyu Meng then combines strict discipline with food, medicine, and protection for soldiers' families, while [[ZhaoYanLateHan|赵俨]] and [[CaoCao|曹操]] stop Cao-side pursuit so Guan Yu can continue constraining Sun Quan.

## Key Claims

- Cao Cao remains at Mobei rather than personally entering the relief battle and sends twelve camps of reinforcements to Xu Huang.
- Xu Huang feints toward Weitou, draws Guan Yu's attention, then attacks Sizhong; after defeating Guan Yu's five thousand cavalry, he breaks successive defensive works and opens the Fancheng encirclement.
- Guan Yu still controls the Mian River by boat after the land siege is broken, so the relief does not instantly restore every route around Xiangyang and Fancheng.
- Lyu Meng hides elite troops below deck, uses merchant clothing and civilian-looking boats, and captures Guan Yu's riverside sentries before they can transmit warning.
- Guan Yu has repeatedly slighted Mi Fang and Fu Shiren; after the large Yu Jin prisoner capture increases supply pressure, their failure to meet provisions and Guan Yu's threat of later punishment deepen their fear and grievance.
- Lyu Meng has [[YuFan|虞翻]] pressure Fu Shiren through a letter; Fu Shiren surrenders and is then brought before Jiangling, where Mi Fang also opens the city.
- After taking Jiangling, Lyu Meng releases Yu Jin, protects Guan Yu's soldiers' families, forbids plunder, punishes a soldier who takes a civilian's hat, distributes food and medicine, and seals captured stores for Sun Quan.
- Guan Yu retreats when he learns that Nanjun has fallen, but Zhao Yan argues that Cao forces should leave him available to constrain Sun Quan; Cao Ren and Cao Cao therefore stop pursuit.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends Hanji 1166's flood and hold phase and Hanji 1168's Wu-deception setup with the actual Fancheng relief, river infiltration, Jiangling surrender, and Cao-side pursuit-restraint sequence.
- Causal qualification: Guan Yu's contempt and threat may help explain Mi Fang and Fu Shiren's surrender, but supply strain, military surprise, isolation, Wu pressure, and self-preservation remain independent causes.
- Operational qualification: exact camp counts, troop totals, place-name readings, feint sequence, fortification details, sentry capture, letters, and timing require comparison with primary texts and wider scholarship.
- Governance qualification: the execution over a civilian hat is presented as proof of impartial discipline, but exemplary severity does not by itself establish consent, legitimacy, or the absence of coercion under occupation.
- Strategic qualification: leaving Guan Yu to constrain Sun Quan is a multi-actor balance judgment, not evidence that Cao Cao preferred Guan Yu's recovery or could fully control the resulting outcome.
