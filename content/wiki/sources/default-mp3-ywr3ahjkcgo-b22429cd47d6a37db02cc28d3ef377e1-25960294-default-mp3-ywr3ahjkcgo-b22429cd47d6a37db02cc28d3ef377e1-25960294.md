# Just Sex

## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode has sociologist [[LisaWade]] distinguish the frequency of college hookups from [[CollegeHookupCulture|the culture that gives them meaning]]. Her account describes a rigid script in which alcohol, parties, peer approval, gender convention, status, and [[PerformedNonattachment|performed nonattachment]] make casual encounters appear spontaneous and emotionally empty even when participants are ambivalent or hope for a relationship.

The culture distributes rewards and risks unequally. Wade describes racialized desirability, constrained LGBTQ access, coercive encounters, and a narrow masculine ideal that can disadvantage women and men who want tenderness or commitment. A pandemic-era update reports fewer one-night stands and more repeated encounters, which some students associated with greater safety, accountability, and mutual pleasure.

## Key Claims
- [[CollegeHookupCulture]] concerns the rules and meanings surrounding casual intimacy, not proof that current students have more sex than earlier cohorts.
- [[SexualScriptPerformancePressure]] can regulate social meaning as well as bodily performance: alcohol, partner selection, peer approval, repetition, and post-encounter distance help classify an encounter as casual.
- [[PerformedNonattachment]] rewards appearing less invested than the other person and can turn emotional honesty, kindness, and romantic interest into status liabilities.
- Hookup access is stratified by race, gender, sexuality, class, disability, conventional attractiveness, alcohol-centered social life, and campus composition.
- Sexual experimentation is not liberating when demands replace discussion, consent is compromised, or participants feel compelled to accept unwanted terms to avoid exclusion.
- A culture organized around stereotypically masculine detachment can constrain men as well as women; some men want relationships while lacking permission to express that preference.
- The pandemic interviews suggest that changed party conditions and repeated contact can alter the script, but do not establish a durable post-pandemic shift.
