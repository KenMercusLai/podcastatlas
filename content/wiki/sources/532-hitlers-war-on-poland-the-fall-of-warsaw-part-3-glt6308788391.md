# 532. Hitler's War on Poland: The Fall of Warsaw (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the [[InvasionOfPoland1939|German and Soviet invasion and partition of Poland]] from Westerplatte on 1 September 1939 through the [[SiegeOfWarsaw1939|fall of Warsaw]]. It joins Germany's rapid operational advance to civilian bombing, village destruction, executions, and SS targeting of Polish officials, intellectuals, clergy, aristocrats, and Jews, presenting violence against society as part of the campaign rather than a later occupation afterthought.

The episode also examines the strategic isolation of [[Poland]]. Britain and France declared war but supplied no meaningful military relief, while the Soviet invasion on 17 September destroyed the remaining prospect of organized resistance from the east. The resulting partition placed millions under Nazi or Soviet rule and made occupied Poland an early arena in the escalation toward [[TheHolocaust|the Holocaust]].

## Key Claims

- German attacks at Westerplatte and the Polish post office in Danzig reveal both continuing military convention and the rapid abandonment of restraint toward irregular defenders and civilians.
- Poland faced attack from several directions after delaying full mobilization under British and French pressure; German air superiority, concentrated mobile forces, and operational tempo overwhelmed its defensive plan.
- [[Blitzkrieg]] in Poland depended on coordinated speed and air power, but the familiar cavalry-charging-tanks story was propaganda built from a real cavalry action against infantry.
- Bombing at Wieluń, attacks on refugees and medical transport, village burning, and mass execution show that terror against civilians began with the invasion.
- Britain and France declared war yet mounted only a limited Saar advance and leaflet raids, leaving Polish expectations of western relief unfulfilled.
- The Soviet invasion of 17 September closed Poland's remaining strategic options and enabled territorial partition between Germany and the [[SovietUnion]].
- Einsatzgruppen targeting and anti-Jewish violence made the campaign an early escalation point between prewar persecution and later systematic genocide.
- Warsaw's defense under Mayor Stefan Starzyński ended after starvation, artillery fire, and mass aerial bombardment; surrender protected no broader Polish sovereignty because occupation and partition followed.
