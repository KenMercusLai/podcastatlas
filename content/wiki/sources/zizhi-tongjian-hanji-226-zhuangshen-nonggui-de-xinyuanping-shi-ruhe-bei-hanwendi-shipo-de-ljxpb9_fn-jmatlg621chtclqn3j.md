# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》226｜装神弄鬼的新垣平，是如何被汉文帝识破的？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[XinyuanPingWesternHan|新垣平]]'s rise and fall under [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]] as a staged court-fraud sequence. Claims about five-colored vapors and sacred objects first win ritual patronage; a prearranged inscribed jade cup then turns prediction into apparent proof; and later claims about a repeated solar culmination and a Zhou鼎 reportedly influence nationwide celebration, a restart of the reign count, and new temple construction.

The title says Liu Heng “识破” the deception, but the episode's own turning point is external accusation followed by judicial investigation. It says an unnamed petitioner denounces the signs as fabricated, the廷尉 inquiry obtains an account of the scheme, and Liu Heng executes Xinyuan Ping and exterminates his three kin groups. The aftermath is narrower than total ritual rejection: the emperor loses interest in calendar, color, and spirit projects and stops attending the new altars personally, while officials continue routine sacrifices.

## Key Claims

- Xinyuan Ping enters court claiming skill in observing qi and says five-colored vapors and a sacred object appear northeast of Chang'an.
- Liu Heng orders a five-color帝 temple built north of the Wei River, performs sacrifice there, appoints Xinyuan Ping as a大夫, gives him substantial rewards, and lets him participate in discussions of institutions, imperial touring, and fengshan.
- The episode says Liu Heng interprets five briefly seen roadside figures as divine and orders another five-emperor altar built at the site.
- Xinyuan Ping has an associate present an inscribed jade cup after he has predicted the arrival of a precious object, creating a planned prediction-and-confirmation loop.
- A reported “sun twice at the meridian” sign leads Liu Heng to restart the reign count and authorize several days of public feasting in the episode's chronology.
- A further claim that a lost Zhou鼎 may appear at Fenyin prompts construction of a riverside temple before the object is produced.
- In winter 163 BCE, an unnamed accuser reports that the divine claims are fraudulent; a廷尉 investigation is said to expose the scheme.
- Liu Heng executes Xinyuan Ping and exterminates his three kin groups, then withdraws personal enthusiasm from calendar, color, and spirit projects while leaving routine sacrifices to officials.

## Contradictions

- The title implies that Liu Heng personally detected the fraud, while the body says an unnamed outsider's accusation triggered the investigation. The source note follows the body and treats “识破” as the emperor's eventual acceptance of externally initiated exposure.
- Name caution: the supplied summary calls the廷尉 “张世芝.” This note does not normalize that form into a canonical person because the identity and the added interrogation details require source comparison.
- Source-scope caution: the exact dates, Liu Heng's age, reward amount, temple sites, sacrificial forms, jade-cup inscription, solar phenomenon, chronology change, lost-ding tradition, confession, execution procedure, and three-kin punishment remain episode-attributed rather than independently verified.
- Mechanism caution: the episode clearly describes the jade cup as prearranged but does not explain how the reported solar effect was produced; the event is retained as a narrated sign, not proof of supernatural action or a known trick.
