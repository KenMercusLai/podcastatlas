# 257. Australia: The Mystery of the Somerton Man

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the 1948 [[SomertonManCase|Somerton Man case]] from the unidentified body found near Adelaide through the removed clothing labels, uncertain toxicology, suitcase, *Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam* fragment, apparent code, and disputed connection to [[JessicaThompson]]. It then presents hair-derived DNA and genealogical work led by [[DerekAbbott]] as identifying the man as [[CarlWebb|Carl “Charles” Webb]], while stressing that identity does not establish cause of death, motive, or the meaning of the case's most theatrical clues. The episode therefore separates a major forensic identification from the still-open historical explanation.

## Key Claims

- A man found dead on Somerton Park Beach on December 1, 1948 had no wallet or identifying papers, and labels had been removed from his clothes.
- Autopsy findings suggested possible poisoning to the presenters, but no poison was identified and no cause involving violence was established.
- A station suitcase was linked to the man through matching U.S.-made thread; “T Keane” markings later became intelligible through Webb's brother-in-law Thomas Keane.
- A concealed “Tammam Shad” paper fragment, its matching *Rubaiyat*, uncracked letter sequences, and phone number for [[JessicaThompson]] generated love-affair and espionage theories without proving either.
- [[DerekAbbott]] and genealogist Colleen Fitzpatrick used hair from the death mask and family-tree analysis to identify the man as [[CarlWebb]], according to the episode.
- The reported DNA result excluded the proposed paternity link between the Somerton Man and Jessica's son Robin, weakening a central doomed-love theory.
- Identification leaves the death's cause, Webb's movements, the removed labels, the altered cigarettes, and his relationship to Jessica unresolved.

## Key Quotes

> “it is done” or “it is finished” - the episode's translation of “Tammam Shad.”

> “many parts of the history remain unwritten” - the episode's closing judgment after the reported identification.
