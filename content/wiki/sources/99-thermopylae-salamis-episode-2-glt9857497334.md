# 99. Thermopylae & Salamis Episode 2

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows Xerxes' invasion from the linked defenses at [[BattleOfThermopylae|Thermopylae]] and Artemisium through the evacuation and burning of [[Athens]] to the [[BattleOfSalamis]]. It treats Thermopylae as a military disaster transformed into a story of noble sacrifice, while Salamis breaks the naval half of an amphibious Persian strategy and leaves the remaining army to be defeated at Plataea.

Its strongest contribution is the combination of strategy and memory. [[Themistocles]] uses the Athenian fleet, coalition persuasion, and a possibly reconstructed intelligence trick to force battle in narrow water, while [[Leonidas]]' death becomes Homeric, Delphic, and civic material for [[GrecoPersianWarMemory]]. The episode resists a simple good-versus-evil account and treats claims that Salamis "saved Western civilization" as counterfactual rather than demonstrated history.

## Key Claims

- Thermopylae and Artemisium form a linked land-sea defense whose purpose is to delay [[XerxesI|Xerxes]] and protect central Greece.
- The mountain path makes Thermopylae vulnerable; Persian intelligence and Ephialtes' guidance allow the Immortals to outflank the position.
- [[Leonidas]] remains with Spartans, Thespians, and Thebans to cover withdrawal, turning military defeat into a durable sacrifice narrative.
- The fall of the pass exposes Attica, forcing evacuation before Persian forces loot and burn Athens and the Acropolis.
- [[Themistocles]] keeps the Greek coalition at Salamis and, according to the episode, sends Sicinnus to induce Xerxes to enter the straits.
- Narrow water neutralizes Persian numbers, disrupts formation, and lets the trained Greek fleet shatter the naval half of Xerxes' invasion plan.
- Salamis is strategically decisive within the campaign, but claims about the survival of democracy, philosophy, Christianity, Islam, or "the West" remain counterfactual and source-scoped.

## Key Quotes

> "a cross between Churchill and Odysseus" - the episode's comparison for Themistocles as wartime leader and schemer.

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode extends the Marathon branch from the 490 BC victory to Xerxes' 480 BC invasion, Greek defeat at Thermopylae, and victory at Salamis.

- Army size, casualty scale, Persian motives, Leonidas' reasoning, Themistocles' role in shaping the Thermopylae story, the Sicinnus deception, and all long-range civilizational counterfactuals remain uncertain, literary, or source-scoped.
