# Unlocking Hidden Potential with Adam Grant

## Summary
In this live [[RevisionistHistory]] conversation, [[MalcolmGladwell]] interviews [[AdamGrant]] on publication day for [[HiddenPotential]]. Grant argues that starting ability is a weak proxy for eventual capability because visible performance reflects opportunity, learning strategy, coaching, practice, character skills, and environmental support as well as talent. Their discussion joins [[PotentialAsGrowthRate]], perfectionist avoidance, early-success risk, parental prestige pressure, admissions policy, and values-based choice while keeping the larger institutional claims source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- [[PotentialAsGrowthRate]] judges promise through learning trajectory and conditions rather than treating early polish, remedial placement, or prodigy status as a ceiling.
- Peak performance can look effortless when audiences do not see the practice history behind it; Grant uses Martin Luther King Jr.'s long speaking experience to qualify the natural-talent interpretation.
- Struggle can build tolerance for mistakes and obstacles, but difficulty is useful only when it supplies learning rather than humiliation or exclusion.
- [[PerfectionismAsAvoidance]] can narrow development by directing practice toward already-mastered tasks, minor errors, restarts, rumination, and self-shame instead of harder attempts.
- Early success may leave some learners underprepared for later obstacles when they have not needed to develop coping and learning skills.
- Parental over-identification can replace concern for character, meaning, and flourishing with institutional prestige and the child's visible achievement.
- The episode critiques inherited advantage and demographic shortcuts while arguing that admissions should examine individual obstacles; it does not supply a complete policy design or comparative outcome evidence.
- College and career choices should be evaluated against a person's values and desired life rather than status alone, but Grant declines to prescribe one institution for every student.
