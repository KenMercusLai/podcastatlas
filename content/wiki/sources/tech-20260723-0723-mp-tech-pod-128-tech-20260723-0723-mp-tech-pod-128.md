## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode centers on [[DylanThompson]]'s account of trying to resolve a missing $1,700 e-bike delivery after only one of two ordered bikes arrived. The case moves through [[FedEx]], the bicycle company, a bank, a credit-card company, and a local police department, but repeatedly runs into chatbots, phone bots, automated claims systems, and weak routes to human help.

The source's main contribution is [[CustomerServiceSludge]]: automation can become a friction layer that absorbs difficult cases, delays resolution, and makes service metrics look cleaner when customers give up before reaching a person. The episode qualifies [[CustomerSupportAutomation]] by separating helpful first-line automation from systems that block [[HumanJudgmentUnderAI]] when a case is high-value, ambiguous, or emotionally costly.

## Key Claims
- Thompson ordered two e-bikes, received only one, and then received a delivery notification for a missing $1,700 package.
- He contacted FedEx, the bicycle company, his bank, his credit-card company, and his local police department over multiple weeks.
- Nearly every organization used a chatbot or AI voice agent as the first line of contact.
- Thompson eventually reached some human representatives, but the support path did not restore the missing bike or produce a full resolution.
- The bicycle company helped refund shipping cost, while FedEx opened and closed a claim through automated channels before Thompson reached a representative.
- Thompson uses "sludge" for customer-service friction such as waits, loops, and repeated steps that cause customers to give up.
- The episode argues that AI gives old service sludge a new interface by putting another automated wall between consumers and organizations.
- The source says many consumers still prefer human support, while some businesses may assume users will eventually accept chatbot-first service.
- If difficult cases never reach humans because users give up, companies can make support metrics look better without solving the underlying problem.
- Thompson's unresolved case leaves the episode's optimism conditional: companies that make human help easier to reach may eventually differentiate themselves.

## Key Quotes
