# 海外航司纷纷拥抱星链，免费 Wi-Fi 为何逐渐成为航司新标配？

## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode opens with short updates on [[Tesla]], [[Apple]], [[Meta]], [[Tipsy]], and [[KFC]], then explains why faster in-flight internet is increasingly offered free rather than sold as a standalone add-on. Its core contribution is [[InFlightConnectivityEconomics]]: low-earth-orbit capacity makes broad access technically more credible, [[SpaceX]] uses free-to-passenger terms to accelerate [[Starlink]] adoption and lock in long aircraft contracts, and airlines convert connectivity into service differentiation, loyalty enrollment, passenger data, advertising, and credit-card distribution.

The episode also keeps the market contestable. [[AmazonLeo]] and upgraded traditional satellite providers can compete on cost and coverage, while aircraft certification, country-by-country approval, and unavailable airspace constrain any claim that one network can become a frictionless global standard.

## Key Claims
- The episode says more than 40 airlines had announced [[Starlink]] partnerships and that the commercial-aircraft order backlog exceeded 8,000 aircraft, but these deployment figures remain source-scoped.
- Low-earth-orbit satellites reduce signal distance and latency relative to traditional geostationary systems, while multiple aircraft receivers and inter-satellite links can improve capacity and ocean coverage.
- Charging for older in-flight Wi-Fi partly rationed scarce bandwidth; greater capacity makes free access technically more plausible rather than automatically costless.
- The source says [[SpaceX]] asks airline partners to make service free to passengers and may concede on price because passenger expectations can pressure other airlines to adopt [[Starlink]].
- Aircraft terminals are supplier-specific, installation requires downtime, and contracts often run for years, so early adoption can create switching costs and durable supplier lock-in.
- Airlines can use login portals and entertainment systems for advertising, require free frequent-flyer enrollment, and market tickets or co-branded credit cards, turning Wi-Fi from a direct fee into a [[CustomerLifetimeValue]] channel.
- [[AmericanAirlines|American Airlines]] is presented as a large Starlink deployment case, while [[DeltaAirLines|Delta Air Lines]] and JetBlue are described as choosing [[AmazonLeo]].
- [[ChinaEasternAirlines|China Eastern Airlines]] is described as already offering a free standard connectivity tier on wide-body direct routes even though Chinese carriers had not yet adopted low-earth-orbit satellite service.
- Certification and national regulation can interrupt coverage; the episode cites British Airways material saying Starlink service is unavailable over countries including Russia and India.
