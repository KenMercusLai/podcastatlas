# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》592丨汉代丞相是如何评价前BOSS的？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens the [[WangJiaWesternHan|王嘉]] remonstrance sequence later developed in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-593-zhenzheng-lihai-de-ren-zheyang-shuohua-luigzfyq6rtua0neci9jqi8ybxqq|Hanji 593]]. In Yuanshou 1 / 2 BCE, after [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] appoints two Da Sima generals and responds to a solar eclipse by inviting fault statements and direct-speaking recommendations, Wang Jia submits a confidential memorial praising [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]], [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]], and Aidi.

The durable synthesis is that the praise is setup, not a finished judgment. Wang Jia selects examples of Yuan's frugality and fiscal reserves, Cheng's tolerance of harsh remonstrance and nonprotection of favorites, and Aidi's early learning, austerity, and public care so that later criticism can use imperial precedent as pressure rather than begin as open accusation.

## Key Claims

- Han Aidi's Yuanshou 1 opening combines military-talent recommendations, Da Sima appointments, eclipse anxiety, invitations to criticism, and amnesty.
- Wang Jia's confidential memorial praises Yuan, Cheng, and early Aidi despite the episode's premise that Western Han decline is often associated with those reigns.
- Wang Jia presents Yuan's private restraint as a fiscal and political virtue: imperial frugality leaves reserves for disaster relief and frontier expense.
- Wang Jia presents Cheng as a ruler who could tolerate sharp criticism about palace behavior and did not ultimately shield favorites such as Chunyu Zhang, Zhang Fang, and Shi Yu from consequences.
- Wang Jia presents Aidi's Dingtao and early reign image through learning, frugality, simplified court goods, and delayed construction of Gonghuang's sleeping temple out of concern for public burden.
- The episode explicitly withholds Wang Jia's final purpose until the next installment, making this source a rhetorical prelude to Hanji 593 rather than a complete remonstrance case.

## Key Quotes

> "言不由衷，定有鬼" - the episode's frame for reading Wang Jia's praise as politically purposeful.

> "事出反常必有妖" - the closing reminder that the praise should be read as setup.
