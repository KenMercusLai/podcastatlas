# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》422-2｜古代外国人为啥爱到中国朝贡？原因啪啪打脸

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] short episode reframes ancient Chinese tribute from pure awe, fear, or cultural admiration into a mixed symbolic and economic system. It argues that [[WesternHanDynasty|西汉]] reward practices, [[MingDynasty|明朝]] high-priced贡物 purchase and reception rules, and [[QingDynasty|清朝]] resistance to resident foreign envoys all show the cost side of [[TianxiaChina|天下中国]] and [[RemoteTributePrestigeTheater|远方朝贡威德表演]].

The durable synthesis is that tribute could create prestige only by paying for recognition. The source adds [[TributeSystemEconomicIncentive|朝贡体系经济激励]] as a general concept, while [[XianfengEmperor|咸丰帝]]'s response to the Treaty of Tianjin becomes a late case of [[TributaryStatusDiplomaticRigidity|朝贡身份秩序外交僵化]]: protecting kneeling ritual and "天朝上国" status could override fiscal and diplomatic judgment.

## Key Claims

- The episode rejects a simple "foreigners came only because they feared or admired China" explanation and treats tribute as a political, cultural, and economic exchange.
- From [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] onward, the source says courts could pursue "万国来朝" prestige by giving rewards far beyond the value of incoming gifts.
- [[MingDynasty|Ming]] tribute is presented as especially institutionalized: priced贡物, generous回赐, free reception, and above-market purchase terms made tribute attractive to foreign polities and merchants.
- Fake or competitive tribute missions, including the 1374 Siamese merchant case and the 1523 Ningbo "争贡之役," show how tribute benefits could produce fraud and violence.
- The episode gives a source-scoped interpretation of Tumu and Altan Khan episodes as conflicts partly driven by broken tribute-trade expectations and blocked access to material exchange.
- [[MatteoRicci|利玛窦]] is cited for the inversion that the relationship could look less like the world paying China and more like China paying the world.
- In the late [[QingDynasty|Qing]] branch, [[XianfengEmperor|咸丰帝]] treats foreign residence in Beijing as a kneeling-and-face problem and briefly considers removing all customs duties to avoid it.
- The episode concludes that tribute's prestige function could become a fiscal burden and, in the treaty era, a source of diplomatic misjudgment.

## Key Quotes

> "赔本赚吆喝" - the episode's compact judgment on tribute economics.

> "打肿脸充胖子" - the closing judgment on rulers paying heavily to preserve the face of all-under-heaven prestige.
