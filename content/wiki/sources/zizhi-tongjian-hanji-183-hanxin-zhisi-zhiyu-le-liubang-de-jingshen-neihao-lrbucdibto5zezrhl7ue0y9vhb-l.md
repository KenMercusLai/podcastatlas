# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》183｜韩信之死，治愈了刘邦的精神内耗

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode reopens [[HanXin|韩信]]'s death after the Hanji 182 "成也萧何败也萧何" branch by directly challenging [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s judgment that Han Xin brought disaster on himself through poor ruler-minister conduct and later rebellion. The host argues source-scopedly that the rebellion case was likely a frame-up, that [[LiuBang|刘邦]] may have preserved Han Xin for years because the demoted marquis still had military value and surrender-signaling value, and that [[LuZhi|吕雉]]'s camp could only safely eliminate him while Liu Bang was away. The episode therefore converts Han Xin's death from a simple ruler-fear purge into a contested triangle among Liu Bang's pacification strategy, Lu-family/capital security, and the wider early Han rollback of autonomous powerholders.

## Key Claims
- [[SimaGuang|司马光]] praises Han Xin's decisive contribution to Western Han victory but argues that Han Xin should have known how to be deferential, not demand Qi kingship under pressure, and not miss the Guling rendezvous.
- The host rejects that moralized judgment, calling the later Han Xin rebellion case likely deliberate framing rather than a proven plot.
- Han Xin did not betray Liu Bang when he controlled armies and had [[KuaiChe|蒯彻 / 蒯通]] urging him toward [[TripartiteBalanceStrategy|三分天下式制衡]], making a weaker post-demotion rebellion look implausible in the host's reading.
- Liu Bang's tolerance of the later "多多益善" exchange is treated as evidence that he still admired Han Xin and did not kill him for ordinary verbal arrogance.
- The host argues that Han Xin as Huaiyin marquis had strategic value: he could strengthen Han military planning, deter other commanders, and serve as proof that losing kingship did not require losing life and wealth.
- Keeping Han Xin alive therefore supported [[DemotedVassalSafetySignal|降爵诸侯安全信号]]; killing him would have resembled [[PunitiveSurrenderDeterrence|杀降纳降反噬]] by warning other kings that surrender or demotion had no safe ending.
- The episode rereads the [[FanKuai|樊哙]] visit: because Fan Kuai was tied to Lu Zhi by marriage, his respectful "大王" treatment of Han Xin may have been a Lu-family attempt to pull Han Xin into its orbit.
- Han Xin's "耻与哙伍" response is treated source-scopedly as a refusal of that possible Lu-linked alignment, not merely wounded pride.
- [[XiaoHe|萧何]]'s false-victory summons remains the practical lure, but the host emphasizes that Liu Bang had not returned to [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]] when Han Xin was killed.
- Liu Bang's recorded reaction of "怜" on hearing of Han Xin's death is read as closer to genuine sorrow than to relief after removing an enemy.
- The same year also ends [[HanWangXin|韩王信]]'s branch when [[ChaiWu|柴武]] kills him at [[Canhe|参合]], letting the host say that "two Han Xins" disappeared in one year.
- The episode closes by stressing the mutual achievement between Liu Bang and Han Xin, then shifts toward [[PengYue|彭越]]'s coming end as the next early Han merit-holder tragedy.

## Key Quotes
> "韩信到底反了没有" - the episode's core reopening question.

> "多多益善" - the Liu-Han exchange used to test whether Liu Bang treated Han Xin as immediately intolerable.

> "两个韩信都没了" - the closing juxtaposition of Han Xin and Han Wang Xin's deaths in the same year.

## Contradictions
- This source directly contests Hanji 182's apparent acceptance of Han Xin's later capital plot. The wiki should preserve both: Hanji 182 records the chronicle-style conspiracy account, while Hanji 183 records the host's source-scoped argument that the case was likely a frame-up.
- This source also qualifies existing Liu Bang suspicion pages: it does not deny Liu Bang's broader rollback of different-surname kings, but argues that preserving Han Xin as a demoted marquis may have served Liu Bang's pacification strategy longer than killing him did.
- The claims about Lu Zhi trying to pull Han Xin through Fan Kuai, Han Xin's support for commandery-county centralization, and Liu Bang's protective intent are interpretive inferences from the host, not settled facts.
