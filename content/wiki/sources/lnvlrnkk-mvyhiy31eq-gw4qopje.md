# 这个破业非创不可吗？｜对谈 Articuler.ai 创始人 Jason

## Summary

This [[42Zhangjing|42章经]] interview follows [[JasonArticulerAI|Jason]] and [[ArticulerAI|Articuler.ai]] from a general people-search product into event audience acquisition and attendee matching. Its central product insight is [[EventIntentMatching]]: an event concentrates declared goals, people, time, and place enough for a matching system to recommend useful introductions before participants arrive.

The founder story connects that wedge to [[CustomerSignalDrivenPivot]], [[FounderLedSales]], and [[StartupRunwayDiscipline]]. A failed financing, team departures, and near-exhausted cash forced Jason to sell personal stock, take revenue-producing recruiting and sales-lead work, and meet users in person; those constraints exposed a clearer event problem. The episode also proposes [[RevealedConnectionPreference]] as a data asset and extends [[AIApplicationSurvivalStrategy]] by arguing that stronger base models can improve dynamic matching rather than automatically erase the application.

## Key Claims

- Articuler.ai helps event organizers identify and invite likely attendees, then recommends people for participants to meet based on background, intent, need, and resources.
- Events form a bounded matching environment because participants have committed to a common time and place and often arrive with explicit goals such as finding customers, investors, talent, or peers.
- Jason says targeted, short, personalized invitations and careful email infrastructure produced roughly 80% opens and 15-20% conversion, while matching gave registrants an additional reason to attend.
- The company treats connection requests and reciprocal acceptance as behavioral evidence about what people actually value, rather than trying to infer everything from profiles or fragmented conversations.
- A failed financing and near-zero runway pushed the team toward paid recruiting and sales-lead work; direct field contact during that survival period helped reveal the event wedge.
- Jason argues that founders should locate and sell to early customers themselves, and should search for product-market fit through existing transactions, observed behavior, and paid demand rather than internal speculation alone.
- The application thesis is that model improvement can strengthen dynamic matching, while generic productivity tools remain more exposed to direct model-provider competition.
