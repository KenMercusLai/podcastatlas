# 278.在红果做AI漫剧的人，是骆驼祥子吗？一个人、16亿播放、七位数收入

## Summary
This [[Luanfanshu|乱翻书]] follow-up returns to [[XiaoGuoGege|小果哥哥]] six months after [[AnhuiXiaoMujiang|《安徽小木匠》]]. He reports making ten [[AIShortDrama|AI漫剧]] alone, spending under RMB 150,000 in total, receiving more than 1.6 billion plays and seven-figure income, while working overnight and drawing on years of video and internet experience. The episode clarifies that producers in this [[Hongguo|红果]] route usually make the drama while distributors and traffic-buying companies fund amplification, but it repeatedly warns that most entrants may lose money and that rights, review, quality grading, story choice, platform rules, and luck prevent the case from becoming a repeatable wealth formula.

## Key Claims
- [[XiaoGuoGege|小果哥哥]] reports completing ten works in six months by himself, with total spending below RMB 150,000, aggregate plays above 1.6 billion, and seven-figure income from [[Hongguo|红果]].
- The producer does not normally finance the large traffic-buying budget in this route. Production, distribution, ad buying, advertising revenue, and settlement are separate roles inside [[ShortDramaPaidTrafficDistribution]].
- The creator began with a RMB 5,000 test funded by a friend, recovered only about RMB 500 from the first work, and expanded duration and spending only after the second work earned money.
- [[AnhuiXiaoMujiang|《安徽小木匠》]] remained his highest-play and highest-income work; later output did not surpass the early breakout.
- The source reports a large quality-linked payout difference: roughly RMB 1.5 versus RMB 12 per ten thousand plays in the creator's low- and higher-quality examples, with character, scene, editing, music, action, voice consistency, and historical detail affecting grading.
- The workflow runs from listening to [[FanqieNovel|番茄小说]], applying for adaptation rights, segmenting the novel into scripts, generating storyboards and video, checking prompts and outputs, editing, and submitting for review. [[Doubao|豆包]] and [[Codex]] support script work, while [[Jimeng|即梦]] and [[Seedance]] support generation.
- The creator rejects one-shot whole-book adaptation: he divides the story into bounded sections, reviews each stage, and uses ordinary-language correction rather than relying on ornate prompts.
- Longer generated clips and intelligent editing are reducing manual editing as a barrier, shifting scarce capability toward story selection, adaptation, directing, continuity, quality control, platform knowledge, and persistence.
- The episode reports both extreme upside and repeated failure: one peer spent nearly RMB 50,000 without profit, while another reportedly needed 49 works before a seven-billion-play breakout.
- [[ChixieRuSong|《持械入宋》]] is presented as a long-persistence team breakout, while [[GuanghuanZhixia|《光环之下》]] shows that a little-read short story can become a high-play adaptation when story fit, regional language, material, and timing align.
- Review rules favor a positive moral resolution even when conflict attracts attention; celebrity likeness or voice imitation, overused AI faces, and content centered on unqualified cruelty can fail review.
- The “rickshaw puller” criticism correctly identifies platform dependence, but the guest argues that the platform and tools supplied an opportunity and income ceiling he previously lacked. The source does not resolve the creator-bargaining question.

## Key Quotes
> "平台至少给了我一辆车" — the guest's response to the “骆驼祥子” platform-labor analogy.

> "绝大多数人做短剧都不赚钱" — the episode's repeated boundary against reading the case as a general income promise.

> "一个人、十部剧、十六亿播放" — the reported scale of the six-month solo-production case.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode revises the earlier impression that three rejected follow-up works implied a stalled route: six months later, the same creator reports ten released works and substantial aggregate income, while still saying that several works failed review and later works did not beat his first breakout.
- The source qualifies simplified claims that production companies bear 95% traffic-buying costs: in this guest's Hongguo/Fanqie route, distributors and traffic-buying companies generally fund amplification, while the producer bears production cost and execution risk.
- Plays, income, spending, payout rates, peer results, market mechanisms, and platform rules are participant-reported and not independently audited. The case is exceptional, uses a creator with earlier video and internet experience, and does not establish representative profitability.
