# EP134-从种族隔离的南非到92年的洛杉矶骚乱，全世界的平权走过了多少艰难岁月

## Summary

This [[WushichaYanjiusuo|无时差研究所]] episode connects [[Apartheid]] in [[SouthAfrica]] to Asian American identity and the 1992 Los Angeles unrest. Its central synthesis is that legal equality does not immediately erase unequal land, education, work, wealth, policing, or historical memory, so present conflict should not be reduced to a group's supposed moral character or to one triggering event.

The U.S. branch argues that “Asian” contains major national, class, migration, and generational differences. It joins the [[ModelMinorityStereotype|model-minority stereotype]] to [[RacialTriangulation|racial triangulation]]: Asian Americans may be relatively praised against Black Americans while still treated as politically weak or permanently foreign. The Los Angeles case then shows how unequal justice, media framing, neighborhood commerce, and mutual stereotypes can turn systemic grievance into [[InterminorityConflictDisplacement|inter-minority conflict displacement]].

## Key Claims

- [[Apartheid]] governed land, racial classification, residence, education, employment, family life, and political power; its effects therefore outlasted its formal abolition.
- Bantu education and occupational reservation restricted knowledge and advancement, making later inequality partly an institutional inheritance rather than evidence of innate group capacity.
- “Asian” is not a uniform political or economic category; national origin, class, language, migration history, geography, and generation shape different experiences and interests.
- [[ModelMinorityStereotype|Model-minority]] praise can be used to deny structural inequality, blame Black Americans, and obscure disadvantage inside Asian communities.
- [[RacialTriangulation]] places Asian Americans in a double position: relatively valorized through economic success and simultaneously excluded as foreign or difficult to assimilate.
- The 1992 Los Angeles unrest cannot be explained only as anti-Korean violence or only through cultural misunderstanding; policing, judicial outcomes, media circulation, economic brokerage, and neighborhood history shaped the conflict.
- [[InterminorityConflictDisplacement]] occurs when minority groups become the immediate targets of one another's anger while the institutions allocating protection, punishment, and economic power recede from view.
