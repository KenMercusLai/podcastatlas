# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》246｜金屋藏娇的鼻祖，终于当上了皇帝

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode closes [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s reign with a final agrarian order, [[HanWudi|刘彻]]'s coming-of-age and accession, and [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王娡]]'s elevation to empress dowager. The immediate enfeoffment of her half-brothers [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] and 田胜 makes the succession a redistribution of maternal-family access as well as a change of emperor.

Its larger synthesis comes from Ban Gu's retrospective on Wen-Jing rule. Low intervention, frugality, and recovery are said to turn early-Han scarcity into household sufficiency and overflowing public stores, but the same affluent order also permits land concentration, local strongmen, political purchasing, and elite competitive luxury. [[WenJingProsperityTradeoff|文景之治的繁荣代价]] therefore separates aggregate recovery from equal distribution, while the episode's “物盛而衰” bridge to Wudi remains a moral-historical frame rather than a deterministic law.

## Key Claims

- In his final illness, Jingdi reiterates agriculture as the material base of food and clothing, orders local officials to promote farming and sericulture, and threatens severe punishment for officials who divert popular labor into mining gold and jewels.
- Liu Che receives the cap ceremony shortly before Jingdi dies at Weiyang Palace, then succeeds as the sixteen-year-old seventh Western Han emperor in the episode's chronology.
- Liu Che elevates Dowager Dou and his mother Wang Zhi, then enfeoffs Wang's half-brothers Tian Fen and Tian Sheng, moving the maternal clan into the new reign's political field.
- Ban Gu's retrospective credits early-Han relaxation and Wen-Jing frugality with replacing post-Qin scarcity by household sufficiency, full granaries, and abundant treasury reserves.
- The same account says affluence coexists with land annexation, rural strongmen, wealth-based access to power, and competitive luxury among imperial kin and officials.
- The episode frames Wudi as heir to both accumulated resources and unresolved social pressures, then previews a reign of major achievement and major consumption.

## Key Quotes

> “农，天下之本也。黄金珠玉，饥不可食，寒不可衣。” - the agrarian principle attributed to Jingdi's final order.

> “物盛而衰，不其遍也。” - the episode's transition from Wen-Jing abundance to the costs associated with Wudi's reign.

## Connections

- [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]] - dying ruler whose final order joins agrarian priority, official accountability, and limits on extractive labor.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - adolescent successor who inherits Wen-Jing resources and the social tensions embedded in that prosperity.
- [[EmpressDowagerWangWesternHan|王太后（王娡）]] - mother elevated at accession after a life path the host interprets through marriage, ambition, and family political calculation.
- [[TianFenWesternHan|田蚡]] - maternal uncle enfeoffed immediately after Liu Che's accession, before his later rise to senior office.
- [[HeavyAgricultureLightCommercePolicy|重农轻商政策]] - agrarian-priority framework sharpened here by limits on official diversion of labor into precious-material extraction.
- [[WenJingProsperityTradeoff|文景之治的繁荣代价]] - distinction between broad recovery and the concentration, strongman power, and elite consumption visible inside it.
- [[ImperialRiseDeclineCycle|Imperial Rise and Decline Cycle]] - adjacent moral-historical pattern invoked by “物盛而衰,” with determinism explicitly bounded.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode's prosperous Wen-Jing picture and its account of concentration describe aggregate abundance and unequal control of wealth at the same time.
- Chronology and text caution: the astronomical reports, accession ages, dates, reign length, edict wording, burial sequence, and fiscal imagery are episode-attributed and require comparison with transmitted records.
- Biography caution: Wang Zhi's age, willingness to leave Jin Wangsun, her mother's motives and emotions, and the claim that the family “won everything” are host commentary rather than independently established interior states.
- Causal caution: the episode links fewer farmers to more commerce, and later Wudi policy to depletion, but does not quantify either mechanism or establish that commerce, luxury, land concentration, war, and court consumption formed one simple causal chain.
- Scope caution: Tian Sheng, Wang Zhi's mother, Jin Wangsun, Yangling, and the astronomical portents remain source-scoped rather than receiving canonical pages from this short transition episode.
