# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》130-2｜历史记录首次屠城！

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-130-1-guailaotou-yiqiao-san-shua-zhangliang-chuanshou-shenshu-lqwjo13zzfgtndpajzpuvrb2h4l2|Qinji 130-1]] by completing [[ChenYing|陈英 / 陈婴]]'s refusal of kingship and then following [[XiangLiang|项梁]] as he absorbs allies, defeats [[QinJiaRebel|秦嘉]] and [[JingJu|景居 / 景驹]], and gives [[LiuBang|刘邦]] the force needed to retake [[FengCounty|丰邑 / 丰县]]. The episode's main thematic contrast is between Chen Ying's survival through attachment and [[ChenSheng|陈胜]]'s exposure after claiming kingship. It closes with [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s capture and massacre of [[XiangchengQin|襄城]], extending the wiki's [[WarringStatesWarBrutalization|战国战争残酷化]] and [[NonKillingUnification|不爱杀人者能一之]] branches into the Qin-collapse field.

## Key Claims
- [[ChenYing|陈英 / 陈婴]] is presented as a cautious Dongyang local official whose local reputation lets him become head of a two-ten-thousand-person scarf-marked rebel force after younger rebels cannot agree on a leader.
- Chen Ying's mother warns that a family without prominent ancestors should not suddenly seek the highest name; the safer course is to attach to a stronger power.
- Chen Ying follows that advice, refuses kingship, and hands his military position to [[XiangLiang|项梁]] rather than trying to become another independent rebel ruler.
- The episode contrasts Chen Ying with [[ChenSheng|陈胜]]: both face warnings about premature kingship, but Chen Sheng ignores such counsel and becomes the exposed center of blame and attack.
- The source extends Chen Ying's payoff beyond the immediate rebellion: after [[XiangYu|项羽]]'s defeat, Chen Ying surrenders to [[LiuBang|刘邦]], is enfeoffed as Tangyi Hou, serves in the Chu kingdom under Liu Bang's brother Liu Jiao, dies in 183 BCE, and leaves descendants tied to the Han royal house by marriage.
- [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] and another Chu-side commander join Xiang Liang after Xiang Liang crosses the Huai, helping bring Xiang Liang's force to roughly sixty or seventy thousand people at Xiapi.
- [[QinJiaRebel|秦嘉]] and [[JingJu|景居 / 景驹]] resist Xiang Liang near Pengcheng, making post-[[ChenSheng|陈胜]] anti-Qin fragmentation open into rebel-on-rebel conflict.
- Xiang Liang attacks Qin Jia's legitimacy by arguing that Chen Sheng was the first rebel king and that Qin Jia, as a follower, betrayed him by installing Jing Ju.
- The episode reads that legitimacy attack as an "攻心计": it gives Xiang Liang's soldiers moral cover by making the campaign look like punishment of a disloyal rebel rather than mere rivalry.
- Qin Jia loses, flees, counterattacks at Huling, and dies; his troops surrender, while Jing Ju flees and later dies in Wei.
- Xiang Liang merges Qin Jia's old troops, then faces renewed pressure from [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]] near Li County.
- [[ZhuJishi|朱鸡石 / 朱吉石]] loses against Zhang Han and flees to Huling, while [[YuFanjun|余樊君]] is killed in battle; Xiang Liang then executes Zhu Jishi after entering Xue County.
- [[LiuBang|刘邦]] visits Xiang Liang at Xue with more than one hundred cavalry, receives five thousand men and ten fifth-rank commanders, and finally retakes Feng on his fourth attempt.
- [[YongChi|雍齿]] flees to Wei after Liu Bang retakes Feng.
- Xiang Liang sends [[XiangYu|项羽]] against [[XiangchengQin|襄城]] in Yingchuan commandery, and Xiang Yu kills the city's soldiers and civilians after taking it.
- The source presents Xiangcheng as the first recorded case of "屠城" in its narrative frame, while also noting that both Xiang Yu and Liu Bang have massacre records and that many historians judge Xiang Yu's cruelty as worse.
- The closing return to [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]]'s captivity and death in Qin explains why Chu resentment against [[QinState|秦国]] remains a powerful anti-Qin background.

## Key Quotes
> "不要追求史书上的虚名" - the source's compressed lesson from Chen Ying's mother.

> "历史上首次屠城的记录" - the source-scoped claim attached to Xiang Yu's capture of Xiangcheng.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction found. This episode extends Qinji 130-1 by taking Chen Ying from refusal of kingship into later Han survival, and by moving Xiang Liang from westward mobilization into coalition consolidation.
