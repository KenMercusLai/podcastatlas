# Hitler’s Olympics, Part 3: Mustache to Mustache

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode examines how American Olympic official [[CharlesHitchcockSherrill]] helped neutralize boycott pressure against the 1936 Berlin Games by securing an invitation for partly Jewish fencer [[HeleneMayer]]. It argues that Sherrill's optimism, patrician prejudice, attraction to powerful men, and investment in elite institutional continuity made symbolic inclusion look like a solution while [[NaziGermany|Nazi Germany's]] persecution remained intact.

The episode contrasts Sherrill with journalist [[DorothyThompson]], whose suffrage work, European reporting, Jewish family connection, and identification with outsiders produced a radically different reading of [[AdolfHitler]]. Sherrill admired Hitler, attended the 1935 Nuremberg Rally, and declared the Olympic dispute resolved; Thompson saw an insecure and vicious demagogue whose ascent depended partly on respectable people adapting to visible success.

## Key Claims

- Sherrill belonged to a narrow American social elite whose optimism and codes of mutual deference made hierarchy appear normal and complaints from excluded groups seem disruptive.
- His patrician antisemitism differed in degree and objective from Nazi racial persecution but still made him receptive to an Olympic solution that preserved exclusion beneath a narrow exception.
- Sherrill admired [[BenitoMussolini]] and Hitler as successful strongmen, valued recognition from powerful figures, and described his August 1935 meeting with Hitler in admiring physical and personal terms.
- Thompson's outsider-oriented career and sustained study of European fascism led her to interpret Hitler as a dangerous demagogue rather than a leader owed deference.
- Sherrill proposed Mayer as a politically convenient Jewish athlete whose appearance and partial ancestry might be acceptable to Hitler; her invitation offered evidence of inclusion without changing the regime's control over Jewish participation.
- Sherrill attended the Nuremberg Rally at which the [[NurembergLaws|Nuremberg Laws]] were announced, yet treated Mayer's invitation as sufficient reason for American participation.
- His final appeal for “peace” converted an institutional decision into pressure on persecuted people and boycott advocates to stop disturbing Olympic harmony.
