# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》192｜刘邦重伤竟甘愿等死

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode links [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s worsening wound after the campaign against [[YingBu|英布 / 黥布]] to the unresolved succession conflict among [[LuZhi|吕雉]], [[QiJi|戚姬 / 戚夫人]], crown prince [[LiuYing|刘盈]], and [[LiuRuyi|刘如意]]. It proposes that Liu Bang may have refused a physician sent by Lu Zhi because treatment had become inseparable from court trust, but explicitly acknowledges that this motive is a retrospective human inference rather than a verifiable fact.

The second half presents the [[WhiteHorseCovenant|白马之盟]] and a contemporaneous imperial edict as paired attempts to stabilize the founding coalition near Liu Bang's death. The covenant uses oath custom to restrict kingship to the Liu clan and marquis rank to merit, while the edict publicly confirms rewards and exemptions for the rulers, marquises, and soldiers who helped establish the dynasty; the episode treats customary commitment and formal law as complementary but does not independently establish the covenant's exact reconstruction.

## Key Claims

- After being wounded during the Ying Bu campaign, Liu Bang's condition worsens on the return to Chang'an; when a physician says the injury can be treated, Liu Bang refuses treatment, rewards him, and sends him away.
- The host connects that refusal to the succession struggle: because Lu Zhi benefits from Liu Ying's secure accession and supplies the physician, Liu Bang may fear that treatment offers an opportunity for harm.
- The episode marks this explanation as conjecture. It does not establish malicious intent by Lu Zhi or the physician, and the actual reason for Liu Bang's refusal remains unknown.
- The reported White Horse Covenant is placed about one month before Liu Bang's death in Chang'an, with Liu relatives, kings, marquises, generals, and senior ministers participating in a sacrificed-horse oath.
- Its reported rule combines a clan boundary for kingship, a merit boundary for marquis rank, and collective action against violators.
- The episode distinguishes the covenant's customary and personal force from a formal imperial edict issued to the realm, while arguing that both seek to reassure the founding political coalition.
- The edict recalls joint conquest and pacification, protects enfeoffed rewards, and reportedly preserves tax-and-corvee exemptions for soldiers who followed Liu Bang into Hanzhong and back east.

## Key Quotes

The supplied episode document is a structured summary rather than a verbatim transcript, so no sentence is promoted as an exact quotation. The reported covenant formula and Liu Bang's appeal to Heaven when refusing treatment remain episode-attributed renderings.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-189-liubang-siqian-zuihou-yibo-lllydj2xyk4cr7b9d2qx0hvfw-dz|Hanji 189]] by carrying the failed heir-replacement conflict into Liu Bang's final illness, and it supplies an origin-and-function account for the covenant invoked later in Hanji 199.
- The host explicitly labels distrust of Lu Zhi's physician as a human inference. The episode does not establish that Lu Zhi intended harm, that the physician was compromised, or that refusal rather than the original wound caused Liu Bang's death.
- The covenant's exact date, location, participants, wording, ceremony, legal force, and relationship to the edict are reconstructed in the episode and remain source-scoped pending direct comparison with transmitted texts.
- The episode calls the edict legally effective while describing the covenant as customary and weakly enforceable; this is presented as a distinction between two stabilizing instruments, not as proof of their precise legal status.
