# 014 正派高徒与魔教“妖女”|金庸《倚天》中存在感超强的几对配角

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode reads three supporting relationships in [[HeavenSwordDragonSaber|《倚天屠龙记》]] as conflicts among love, sect identity, family duty, reputation, and personal principle. [[ZhangCuishanJinYong|张翠山]] and [[YinSusuJinYong|殷素素]] expose the cost of treating departure from Wudang as less imaginable than death; [[YangXiaoJinYong|杨逍]] and [[JiXiaofuJinYong|纪晓芙]] preserve an unresolved coercion boundary while showing Ji's refusal to betray either her conscience or the man she loves; and [[YangBuhuiJinYong|杨不悔]] with [[YinLitingJinYong|殷梨亭]] turns care, age difference, and inherited relational debt into an ethically disputed but mutually chosen marriage.

## Key Claims

- [[SectarianIdentityMoralMisclassification|Sectarian identity is an unreliable moral shortcut]]: orthodox and heterodox affiliation does not settle conduct, character, or relational worth.
- Zhang Cuishan's suicide is interpreted less as an unavoidable answer to outside pressure than as an inability to rank marriage, brotherhood, sect belonging, parenthood, and continued life under conflict.
- Yin Susu's isolation and guilt make her suicide more psychologically legible than Zhang's, but the existence of their child leaves a serious parental-responsibility objection to both deaths.
- The source cannot settle what the novel's “forcing” between Yang Xiao and Ji Xiaofu denotes; Ji's later love and refusal to kill him do not by themselves resolve the earlier consent question.
- Ji Xiaofu neither returns to Emei nor joins Yang Xiao, preserving love, honesty, and non-harm while losing institutional protection; the hosts disagree whether this is indecision or principled refusal.
- Yang Buhui's care for the injured Yin Liting develops into a relationship not reducible to resemblance to her mother, father longing, compensation, or gratitude, although each remains a possible influence.
- Her choice of Yin over Zhang Wuji rejects repayment marriage: rescue, childhood hardship, familiarity, and moral worth do not create a romantic debt.
