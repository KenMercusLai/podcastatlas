## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode continues [[ChristopherMims]]' [[HowToAI|How to AI]] discussion, shifting from individual assistant use to organizational AI adoption. Mims argues that companies overestimate automatic productivity gains unless they redesign workflows, assign AI narrow repeatable tasks, and manage change around human work. The episode also adds [[Clorox]] and [[HiddenValleyRanch]] as non-tech AI adoption examples, while introducing [[InvisibleClassicalAI]] to show that older AI systems already shape maps, insurance claims, search, and social feeds.

## Key Claims
- AI adoption inside organizations is a collective workflow problem, not only a matter of giving individual workers chatbots.
- Bosses may assume AI saves more employee time than workers report, creating an [[AIEconomicDiffusion]] gap between model availability and realized productivity.
- Mims argues that agentic AI is most useful when treated like an assembly-line robot: assign bounded, repeatable tasks it can perform reliably.
- Sales-call recording, evaluation, and coaching are presented as examples of narrow AI assistance that can improve frontline work without replacing every human judgment step.
- [[Clorox]] used generative image tools to create simple web ad variants for [[HiddenValleyRanch]], showing how AI can enter ordinary consumer packaged goods workflows.
- Clorox also used [[MicrosoftCopilot|Microsoft Copilot]] and [[ChatGPT]] as a brainstorming participant, including in ideation around a "toilet bomb" product.
- Generative AI is only part of AI's influence; older, pre-generative systems already shape Google Maps routing, insurance-claim apps, search results, and social feeds.
- Mims is concerned that AI will compound economic uncertainty if companies justify layoffs or flat headcount by promising higher productivity from remaining workers.

## Key Quotes
> "assembly-line robot" - Mims' analogy for assigning AI specific repeatable tasks.

> "another voice in the room" - the episode's framing of AI in Clorox brainstorming.

> "invisible" - Mims' description of how many everyday AI interactions will remain.
