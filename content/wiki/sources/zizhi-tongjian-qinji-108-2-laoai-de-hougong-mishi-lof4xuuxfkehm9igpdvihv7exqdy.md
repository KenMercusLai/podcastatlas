# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》108-2｜嫪毐的后宫秘史

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the intimate and political prehistory behind [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-108-1-lishi-gan-dangmian-dingzhuang-qinshihuang-diyi-ren-lg0dssyicp387foiraohkv1x5aij|Qinji 108-1]]'s [[LaoAi|嫪毐]] rebellion branch. It links [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s hostage-childhood vulnerability, young accession, and delayed personal rule to the humiliating court scandal around [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]], [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]], and Lao Ai.

The episode also shifts from palace scandal into historiography. The host uses [[Shiji|《史记》]]'s blunt language about Zhao Ji and Lao Ai as evidence to discuss [[GenderedHistoriographicalDesireFraming|性别化史书欲望叙事]], arguing that classical accounts and later readers can reduce Zhao Ji to appetite unless one restores emotional, political, and narrative constraints through [[FemaleSubjectivityInClassicReading|经典阅读中的女性主体性]].

## Key Claims
- The source presents Ying Zheng's early life as politically exposed: born while [[QinZhuangxiangwang|异人 / 子楚]] is hostage in [[Handan|邯郸]], separated from his father around the Qin-Zhao war crisis, and returned to Qin only after Zichu becomes crown prince.
- Ying Zheng's later anger around the Lao Ai affair is interpreted as more than abstract severity: the host links it to childhood insecurity, a long period of minority rule under Lu Buwei and Zhao Ji, and the humiliation of a family scandal becoming public politics.
- The episode repeats the [[NaturalDisasterPoliticalOmen|自然灾害政治征兆]] frame from Qinji 108-1: abnormal summer cold after the suppression of Lao Ai can be read by ancient interpreters as a political sign.
- [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]]'s old relationship with [[ZhaoJi|赵姬]] becomes dangerous as Ying Zheng grows up, so Lu Buwei tries to transfer the risk by introducing [[LaoAi|嫪毐]] indirectly.
- The host stresses the indirectness of Lu Buwei's maneuver: Lao Ai is first displayed as a rumor-producing curiosity, giving Zhao Ji space to request him rather than making Lu Buwei appear to offer a lover openly.
- Lao Ai is brought into the palace through a fake eunuch arrangement: he is made to appear as if he had undergone corrosion/castration punishment, including removing facial hair.
- The episode notes that pre-Qing "宦官" and "太监" should not be treated as identical categories and that early palace service did not always require castration.
- Zhao Ji is presented as a young widow and queen mother whose continued desire is made politically dangerous by her status, Ying Zheng's coming adulthood, and court scrutiny.
- Lao Ai's favor through Zhao Ji converts private intimacy into office and faction: he receives Changxin Hou status, a [[TaiyuanWarringStates|太原]] fief, servants, and thousands of retainers.
- Lao Ai's drunken claim to be Ying Zheng's "假父" is treated as the point where sexual scandal, household humiliation, and royal authority directly collide.
- The host criticizes the ease with which [[Shiji|《史记》]] and later male-oriented reading can flatten Zhao Ji as only lustful, especially through formulas such as "太后淫不止."
- [[ZhangAiling|张爱玲 / Eileen Chang]], [[JinPingMei|《金瓶梅》]], and [[HongLouMeng|《红楼梦》]] are used as interpretive contrasts: desire can be written as animal appetite, but it can also expose feeling, social pressure, tragedy, and destruction.
- The provided transcript stops with Lao Ai's expansion and does not narrate the full rebellion and suppression, so the episode functions as a prequel and interpretive supplement to Qinji 108-1.
