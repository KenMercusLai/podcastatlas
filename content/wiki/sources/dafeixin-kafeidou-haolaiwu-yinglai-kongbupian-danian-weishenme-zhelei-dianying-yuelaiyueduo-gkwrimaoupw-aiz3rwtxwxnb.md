# 达霏欣｜咖啡豆：好莱坞迎来恐怖片大年，为什么这类电影越来越多？

## Summary
This [[ShengdongZaokafei|声动早咖啡]] episode combines short updates on Apple hardware, AI-search monetization, cloud growth, Chinese HBM development, archival tape, and Unitree R&D with a deeper explanation of the [[ContemporaryHorrorFilmResurgence|contemporary horror-film resurgence]]. Its durable synthesis is that horror combines portfolio-friendly production economics, relatively portable fear effects, an [[InternetNativeFilmmakingPipeline|internet-native IP and talent pipeline]], and a collective theatrical experience that can turn social anxiety into controlled emotional release.

The episode also adds important limits. Low cost caps downside without guaranteeing a hit; viral concepts do not remove the need for feature-length storytelling; reported company, market, yield, and box-office figures remain time-bound; and effective horror depends on tension, release, pacing, and narrative rather than maximizing jump scares.

## Key Claims
- [[Apple]] reportedly introduced a touchscreen [[MacBook]] Pro with M5-series chips and an OLED display at substantially higher starting prices, while the episode notes weak laptop demand and limited touchscreen share.
- [[Alphabet]] reportedly missed revenue and profit expectations as AI search produced fewer ads than classic search, while [[GoogleCloud|Google Cloud]] and [[Microsoft]] cloud revenue grew rapidly; the episode reads this as a shift from search-ad economics toward infrastructure revenue rather than proof that cloud growth fully offsets search pressure.
- [[ChangXinMemory|ChangXin Memory]] is described as developing [[HighBandwidthMemory|HBM]] for AI accelerators, with reported yields of roughly 20%-30%, performance nearer an older [[SKHynix|SK Hynix]] generation, and testing by Chinese chip companies. These figures remain episode-scoped qualification signals, not verified mass-production results.
- [[Fujifilm]] reportedly plans to increase magnetic-tape capacity fivefold as AI expands long-duration data retention. [[MagneticTapeColdStorage]] remains a complement for infrequently accessed archives rather than a substitute for SSDs or hard drives across all workloads.
- [[UnitreeRobotics|Unitree Robotics]] reported first-three-quarter revenue of RMB 1.8 billion and R&D spending of RMB 263 million, attributing the increase partly to embodied-model and compute investment. The episode also says research, education, and performance still represent about 70% of humanoid shipments.
- Large models can help robots plan actions and routes, but translating model input and instructions into precise, reliable physical execution remains an [[EmbodiedAI|embodied-AI]] systems problem.
- Horror's low dependence on stars, large action sequences, complex effects, or famous IP can cap production losses while leaving upside from rare hits, making the genre attractive when film finance is cautious.
- Fear, visual motifs, sound, and bodily response can travel across markets more readily than humor or locally specific dialogue, although cultural interpretation and regulation still limit any claim of frictionless portability.
- Online images, memes, shorts, independent games, and YouTube creators can supply new concepts and directors; audience attention provides discovery evidence without proving feature-length narrative skill.
- Collective darkness, anticipation, screaming, and relief make horror unusually suited to cinema as a social event that is difficult to reproduce at home.
- Horror can embody economic, political, relational, racial, and technological anxiety in a safe fictional frame, but the episode's social-pressure account is interpretive rather than a measured causal effect.
- Better-rated horror is not defined by more jump scares. Pacing, contrast between tension and release, character, and story remain necessary for sustained audience approval.
