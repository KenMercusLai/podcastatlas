# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》571丨汉成帝亲手杀子，是真是假？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens the [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] heir-killing accusation sequence later questioned in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] and politically closed in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-574-gudai-de-manfen-zuowen-shi-shenme-yangzi-lotqprwr3rk5lke0rjn94riljuvh|Hanji 574]]. It starts from Chengdi's 7 BCE sudden death at [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]], [[ZhaoHedeWesternHan|赵合德]]'s suicide, and [[WangZhengjun|王政君]]'s demand for investigation, then centers on [[XieGuangWesternHan|谢光]]'s 6 BCE report to [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] about missing imperial sons.

The durable synthesis is procedural rather than conclusive. The episode does not yet settle whether Chengdi personally killed a son; it reconstructs the accusation chain around [[CaoGongWesternHan|曹宫]]'s newborn boy: confinement under Chengdi's handwritten order, questions about whether the infant was dead, Ji Wu's attempt to preserve the imperial bloodline, Cao Gong's forced poisoning, the six attendants' deaths, and the boy's removal by edict on the eleventh day.

## Key Claims

- In March 7 BCE, Han Chengdi dies suddenly at Weiyang Palace, popular rumor blames Zhao Hede, Wang Zhengjun orders investigation, and Zhao Hede kills herself.
- In 6 BCE, Xie Guang reports to Han Aidi that Xu Meiren and Cao Gong had both borne sons by the former emperor and that those sons had disappeared.
- Cao Gong, a former central-palace woman who taught the `Shijing` to Zhao Feiyan, becomes pregnant after contact with Chengdi and gives birth to a boy in October 12 BCE.
- Chengdi's handwritten note has Cao Gong, the infant, and six attendants confined, while forbidding questions about the child's sex or parentage.
- When Zhao Zhaoyi's driver asks whether the child has died, Ji Wu reports that the boy is alive and argues by memorial that an emperor without sons should preserve this bloodline regardless of the mother's rank.
- Cao Gong is forced to take pills delivered with an imperial note; before dying she accuses the Zhao sisters of wanting to dominate the realm and says the boy looks like Emperor Yuan.
- The six attendants are told by Zhao Zhaoyi that they cannot live despite being innocent, then all hang themselves; the infant is later taken away by Li Nan on the eleventh day and disappears from the record.
- The episode ends before fully answering the title question, leaving the exact mechanism and responsibility for the alleged killing to the next installment.
