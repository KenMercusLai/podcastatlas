# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》328-1｜中国足球世界第一！居然是2000年前

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[HanDynastyCuju|汉代蹴鞠]] as an already flourishing social practice through [[LiuTaigong|刘太公]]'s reported attachment to the game and [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s construction of [[XinfengHan|新丰]] near Chang'an to recreate his father's Pei County social world. The second half turns to [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] and argues that inherited doctrine must be judged against changing force structure: the episode characterizes cavalry in [[SunBinBingfa|《孙膑兵法》]] as auxiliary, whereas Huo's long-range war against [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] required more independent mounted operations associated with [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]]'s organizational innovations.

## Key Claims

- The episode says cuju was attributed to the Yellow Emperor era and became especially prominent in the Han, but it does not reproduce the claimed ancient passage.
- It reports that FIFA formally recognized China as the origin of football in 2004 and treats cuju as the earliest historically documented football activity.
- Liu Taigong is presented as an enthusiastic cuju player whose move from Pei County to Chang'an separated him from familiar teammates and daily routines.
- Liu Bang responds by building Xinfeng east of Chang'an in imitation of Pei County and relocating local people so that his father can resume his social and sporting life.
- The episode characterizes Sun Bin-era cavalry as an auxiliary force for seizing key positions, disrupting supplies, burning grain, and raiding rather than conducting independent frontal shock action.
- Meng Tian's anti-Xiongnu warfare is likewise described as relying mainly on infantry and chariot-infantry formations, with cavalry embedded in a larger formation.
- Wei Qing is presented as an important cavalry reformer whose five-part arrangement joined front, rear, left, right, and central units for long-range movement and battle.
- The Huo Qubing comparison supports a broader claim that military doctrine loses authority when weapons, force structure, terrain, opponent, and operational range have changed.

## Key Quotes

> "以蹴鞠为欢。" - the episode's phrase for Liu Taigong resuming play after the move to Xinfeng.

> "大人，时代变了。" - the host's modern rhetorical compression of Huo Qubing's rejection of mechanically copied older doctrine.

## Connections

- [[HanDynastyCuju|汉代蹴鞠]] - social, recreational, and origin-story branch preceding the military-training material in Hanji 328-2.
- [[LiuTaigong|刘太公]], [[LiuBang|刘邦]], and [[XinfengHan|新丰]] - family adaptation through reconstruction of a familiar local community and cuju network.
- [[HuoQubing|霍去病]] and [[WeiQingHanGeneral|卫青]] - later mounted-war practice contrasted with older auxiliary-cavalry doctrine.
- [[SunBinBingfa|《孙膑兵法》]] and [[MengTian|蒙恬]] - earlier doctrinal and campaign baselines used by the host to mark changes in cavalry roles.
- [[MilitaryDoctrineEraFit|兵法的时代适配]] - synthesis that doctrine must be re-evaluated when the material and organizational conditions of war change.
- [[WarringStatesMilitaryTransformation|战国军事形态转变]] - broader transition from chariot-centered war toward infantry specialization and increasingly capable cavalry.
- [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] - mobile frontier opponent whose operational environment makes older agrarian-theater assumptions less transferable.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. The cuju material extends Hanji 328-2 backward from rules, military conditioning, and court spectacle into a family and community case.
- The Yellow Emperor origin, attribution to the *Shiji*, 1993 Olympic-bid display, exact 2004 FIFA recognition, and "earliest documented football" formulation are not supported here with reproduced documents and remain episode-attributed.
- The Xinfeng account is presented through a compact retelling of the *Xijing Zaji* tradition. Construction scale, distance from Chang'an, population transfer, exact location, and the modern "league" analogy remain source-scoped.
- The descriptions of Sun Bin's cavalry tasks, Meng Tian's force mix, Wei Qing's five-part formation, and Huo Qubing's attitude are interpretive summaries rather than reproduced passages or a complete comparative military study.
