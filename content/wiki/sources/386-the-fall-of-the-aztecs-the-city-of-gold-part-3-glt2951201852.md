# 386. The Fall of the Aztecs: The City of Gold (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode follows [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]] from the Gulf coast through fighting and alliance with [[Tlaxcala]], the [[CholulaMassacre|Cholula massacre]], and the first Spanish-led entry into Tenochtitlan. It centers [[Malinche]]'s interpreting role and treats the meeting with [[Moctezuma]] as a problem of translation, courtly convention, strategic hospitality, and later Spanish self-justification rather than a demonstrated voluntary surrender. The result is a contingent coalition story in which Indigenous political choices, regional rivalries, violence, and source construction matter more than inevitable Spanish mastery.

## Key Claims

- Cortes marched inland with only several hundred Spaniards, many of them tradesmen rather than professional soldiers, while depending on porters, enslaved Tainos, Totonacs, local provisioning, and Malinche's translation.
- Tlaxcala first resisted the expedition, then entered a mutually instrumental relationship in which Tlaxcalan leaders may have recruited the Spaniards as useful armed partners against the [[MexicaEmpire|Mexica Empire]], not simply submitted as Spanish auxiliaries.
- Cortes and Pedro de Alvarado used night attacks, torture, mutilation, cannon, and exemplary violence during the Tlaxcalan campaign, making coercive signaling part of the alliance's formation.
- Cortes and Tlaxcalan allies massacred and sacked Cholula after a reported warning of attack whose transmission and reliability cannot now be established; the violence served both local rivalry and Cortes's effort to project terror.
- The scale, urbanism, agriculture, lake transport, and causeways of the Valley of Mexico astonished the Spaniards and contradicted assumptions of a culturally or politically simple American world.
- Moctezuma's formal welcome did not establish political submission: courtly hospitality may have been mistranslated or later reframed by Cortes, whose legal position rewarded a story of voluntary transfer.
- The expedition entered Tenochtitlan without knowing whether it had become an honored guest, a contained dependent, a prisoner, or some unstable combination of those positions.

## Key Quotes

> "conquer or die" - the children's-history formulation used to introduce Cortes's inland march and its later heroic framing.

> "guests, prisoners, or something else" - the episode's unresolved question about the Spaniards' position inside Tenochtitlan.

## Connections

- [[HernanCortes]] - expedition leader whose audacity, violence, dependence, and legal self-interest shaped both the campaign and its later narrative.
- [[Malinche]] - interpreter and possible strategist central to alliance-making, warnings, and the meeting with Moctezuma.
- [[Tlaxcala]] - independent Nahua confederation that moved from armed resistance to a mutually instrumental anti-Mexica alliance.
- [[Moctezuma]] - experienced ruler whose formal welcome was later represented as surrender despite deep linguistic and political ambiguity.
- [[MexicaEmpire]] - Tenochtitlan-centered regional power encountered through rivals, emissaries, tributary claims, court ceremony, and monumental urbanism.
- [[CholulaMassacre]] - coalition atrocity that joined Tlaxcalan rivalry to Cortes's use of exemplary terror.
- [[SpanishConquestOfMexico]] - wider process shown here as coalition formation, regional politics, mediated encounter, and coercive improvisation.
- [[ConquestCaptivityNarrativeUncertainty]] - framework for separating hospitality, containment, dependency, and surrender in the entry to Tenochtitlan.
- [[ColonialSourceMediation]] - adjacent source-critical problem involving translation, retrospective memory, political defense, and imported literary models.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with [[387-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-prisoners-of-montezuma-part-4-glt8017930427|Part 4]] was found; this episode supplies the inland campaign, coalition formation, and first meeting that precede Part 4's unstable accommodation and captivity dispute.
- Cortes's and Bernal Diaz's surrender narrative conflicts with the episode's reading of Moctezuma's words as courtly hospitality filtered through translation and later Spanish legal need.
- Older Spanish-centered accounts describe Tlaxcalans as recruits, while the episode's newer interpretation treats the Spaniards as mercenary-like partners recruited into Tlaxcalan strategy; the exact balance of initiative and leverage remains interpretive.
- Malinche's intentions, the alleged Cholulan plot, Moctezuma's private calculations, exact dialogue, troop and population estimates, reported speeches, literary borrowings, and the location and status of Tlaxcalans inside Tenochtitlan remain source-scoped or unresolved.
