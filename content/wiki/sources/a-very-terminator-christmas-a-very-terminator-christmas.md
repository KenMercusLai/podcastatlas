# A Very Terminator Christmas

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] oral history reconstructs how producer [[StanBrooks]]'s modest remake of the 1945 romantic comedy became [[ArnoldSchwarzenegger]]'s only feature-length directing project. Schwarzenegger used the low-risk television production to test directing, asked for more humor and danger, sought advice from established filmmakers, and helped turn [[ChristmasInConnecticut1992|Christmas in Connecticut]] into an improbable mixture of screwball romance and action-movie ambition.

The finished film drew mixed reviews and did not establish a directing career, yet many participants remembered the chaotic 20-day production with affection. [[BenNaddaffHafrey]] uses that tension to extend [[OutcomeIndependentCreativePractice]]: making can retain value through commitment, friendship, and shared experience even when the artifact is flawed, while screenwriter Janet Brownell's distress prevents the pleasure of some collaborators from standing in for everyone involved.

## Key Claims

- Brooks developed a small TNT remake in which the original film's fraudulent domestic columnist became a Martha Stewart-like television personality.
- Schwarzenegger volunteered to direct after completing *Terminator 2*, accepted a reported $100,000 fee, and approached the family-friendly television movie as a lower-risk directing experiment.
- His requests for more jeopardy, action, and comedy produced a large opening rescue, further rewrites, and a project substantially different from Brownell's initial conception.
- The approximately $3 million film paired Dyan Cannon, Kris Kristofferson, and Tony Curtis and was completed in 20 shooting days, largely in story order to support its inexperienced feature director.
- Crew recollections identify visible production limits and specific directing failures, including the unconvincing rescue and a baby-bath scene whose selected take undermines the intended competence and romance.
- Schwarzenegger nevertheless remained intensely engaged while *Terminator 2* was becoming a major hit, and many collaborators remembered the production's improbability and enthusiasm fondly.
- The film's mixed reception ended the directing experiment, but Brooks and Schwarzenegger remained friends; Schwarzenegger later appointed Brooks to the California Film Commission.
- The episode argues that creative process, relationships, and the chance to keep making can matter independently of prestige or finished-work quality.
