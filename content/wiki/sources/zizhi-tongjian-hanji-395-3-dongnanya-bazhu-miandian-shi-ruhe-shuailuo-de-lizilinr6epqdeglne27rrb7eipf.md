## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses the fear around northern Myanmar and the film 《孤注一掷》 as an entry point into nineteenth-century [[Myanmar]] history. It argues that Burma was not historically a weak state: it had been a major Southeast Asian military power, but its court badly misread British power after expansion brought it into contact with British India. The episode treats the First Anglo-Burmese War and the Treaty of Yandabo as a key starting point for Myanmar's modern decline and as background for later instability.

## Key Claims
- The episode says contemporary Chinese impressions of Myanmar often center on northern Myanmar scams, violence, jade trade, civil war, and the World War II China Expeditionary Force battlefield.
- The host argues that modern Myanmar instability should be read through a longer historical frame rather than only through recent public-safety fears.
- The episode compares Burma before the First Anglo-Burmese War with China before the First Opium War: both are portrayed as powers that misunderstood the world situation and the enemy's strength.
- The source says Burma's 1785 annexation of Arakan made it directly adjacent to British India and set up a new conflict frontier.
- The episode presents Burmese demands toward British Bengal and the plan to conquer Calcutta as signs of serious [[ColonialPowerMiscalculation|colonial-power miscalculation]].
- The source says the First Anglo-Burmese War ended in a rapid Burmese defeat, near existential danger, and the Treaty of Yandabo, which required territorial concessions, indemnity, and acceptance of a British envoy.
- The host stresses that Burma's defeat was historically striking because Burma had previously been a formidable Southeast Asian military power.
