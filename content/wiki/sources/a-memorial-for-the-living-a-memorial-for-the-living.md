# A Memorial for the Living

## Summary

[[MalcolmGladwell]] contrasts the engineered permanence of the [[NationalSeptember11Memorial]] with [[ChangingHomelessness]]'s continuously updated Jacksonville by-name list. Both systems restore identity and relational context, but one commemorates the dead while the other helps living people obtain housing.

The comparison develops [[MemorialCareAllocation]] as an ethical challenge: collective precision, money, and attention reveal what society has chosen to value, while [[ByNameHomelessnessCoordination]] can identify vulnerable people and organize casework without overcoming a shortage of deeply affordable homes.

## Key Claims

- [[MichaelArad]]'s “Reflecting Absence” uses the Twin Towers' footprints, waterfalls, names, trees, and exact location to preserve the scale and relationships of the September 11 loss.
- Opening the memorial plaza by September 11, 2011 required unusual construction sequencing around active rail infrastructure and substantial additional cost.
- [[AmandaSachs]] arranged 2,982 victims' names through “meaningful adjacency,” grouping institutional affiliations and requested personal relationships rather than using a simple alphabetical list.
- [[ChangingHomelessness]] maintains a by-name list ordered by vulnerability and time without housing, then reviews individual barriers and possible resources each week.
- The Jacksonville workflow makes homelessness visible and actionable, but the episode says scarce apartments at the price clients can afford prevent identification from becoming housing.
- [[DawnGilman]]'s annual reading of people who died while homeless turns graph points back into family members and exposes the stakes of delayed material care.
- [[MemorialCareAllocation]] asks whether the care devoted to commemoration should also shape obligations toward vulnerable people who remain alive.
