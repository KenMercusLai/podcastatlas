## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] Bytes episode has [[AnitaRamaswamy]] of [[TheInformation]] discuss three technology-business stories: [[Apple]]'s announced CEO transition from [[TimCook]] to [[JohnTernus]], [[Meta]]'s reported employee computer tracking for AI training, and [[Roblox]]'s state settlements over child-safety concerns. The episode's common thread is that AI-era strategy is moving through concrete institutions: executive succession, workplace monitoring, training-data scarcity, and platform safety rules for minors.

The strongest synthesis is that large platforms are trying to find new growth surfaces while trust constraints tighten. Apple is pushed toward a post-iPhone interface search, Meta is turning employee computer-use traces into possible agent-training data, and Roblox is responding to legal pressure with face-based age estimation and communication limits for younger users.

## Key Claims
- [[Apple]] announced that [[TimCook]] is stepping down as CEO after about 15 years and moving to executive chairman.
- [[JohnTernus]], Apple's senior vice president of hardware engineering, is named as the next CEO, which [[AnitaRamaswamy]] reads as a signal that hardware innovation may become more central to Apple's next phase.
- The episode says Apple's annual revenue nearly quadrupled under Cook, reaching more than $416 billion last year, but continued growth at that pace will be difficult.
- The Apple segment frames stagnant iPhone sales, tepid response to [[AppleIntelligence]], and mixed reaction to [[VisionPro]] as signs that Apple still needs a compelling post-iPhone form factor.
- [[Reuters]] reported that [[Meta]] is installing tracking software on U.S. employees' computers to capture mouse movements, clicks, and keystrokes for AI training.
- Meta says the data will not be used for performance reviews and that managers will not be able to access it.
- The episode links the tracking to [[ComputerUseAgent|computer-use agents]]: models need real examples of how people operate computers if they are expected to complete everyday digital tasks.
- Ramaswamy connects the move to [[AITrainingDataScarcity]], saying major AI companies are running out of easily available public web data and looking for higher-value training material.
- The episode says Meta took a big stake in [[ScaleAI]], reinforcing the value of data gathering, labeling, and process traces for model improvement.
- Ramaswamy says employee unease is heightened by reports that Meta plans to cut about 10% of its workforce next month, around 8,000 people.
- The discussion says large tech companies are pressuring employees to use AI more, including reported token-usage measurement experiments at Meta.
- Ramaswamy expects AI-driven workplace monitoring to become more common, including the possibility that smaller companies collect employee behavior data and sell it to AI model developers.
- [[Roblox]] reached $36 million in settlements with the attorneys general of [[Alabama]], [[Nevada]], and [[WestVirginia|West Virginia]] over child-safety issues.
- Cases in state and federal courts have accused Roblox of failing to protect minors from predatory behavior on the platform; Roblox says it will work with states to make online experiences safer for children.
- [[MattKaufman]] describes a Roblox age-verification tool that uses a face snapshot to estimate age, with about 1.4 years of accuracy for users under 18.
- The episode says Roblox users must be at least nine years old to communicate with other users their own age.
- Ramaswamy says age verification, harmful-content prevention, and avoiding direct marketing to children are likely to remain live platform-safety issues across tech.
- The closing promo points listeners to [[HowWeSurvive]], [[AmyScott]]'s Marketplace climate-solutions podcast.
