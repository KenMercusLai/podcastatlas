# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》198｜舅娶外甥女，这位皇帝身上事儿不简单

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[LiuYing|汉惠帝刘盈]] from early-reign construction and ritual decisions through his death and the child-successor order controlled by [[LuZhi|吕雉]]. It holds two sides of the reign together: Liu Ying is associated with tax relief, amnesty, repeal of Qin book restrictions, and concern about disturbing residents, while Lu Zhi arranges his marriage to his niece [[ZhangYanEmpressWesternHan|张嫣]] and later controls the accessions of [[LiuGongYoungEmperorWesternHan|前少帝刘恭]] and [[LiuHongYoungEmperorWesternHan|少帝刘弘]].

The ritual-road episode adds a separate governance lesson. After Liu Ying accepts [[ShusunTong|叔孙通]]'s warning that a newly built elevated road crosses Gaozu's ceremonial route, Shusun Tong redirects demolition toward construction of another ancestral temple because a ruler should not visibly admit error. [[SimaGuang|司马光]] rejects that premise: the political virtue lies in recognizing and correcting mistakes, not preserving an image of royal infallibility. The source supports [[CorrectableLeadershipError|犯错能改的领导力]], but exact ages, dates, quotations, private marital conduct, parentage, motives, and psychological explanations remain episode-attributed.

## Key Claims

- The court recognizes Zou Yao as Donghai king at Dong'ou and continues large-scale Chang'an wall construction with prisoners and later with mobilized labor.
- Lu Zhi reportedly arranges Liu Ying's marriage to eleven-year-old Zhang Yan, daughter of his sister [[LuYuanPrincess|鲁元公主]], making the spouses maternal uncle and niece.
- The episode says Liu Ying never consummates the marriage and that Zhang Yan is later found to have remained a virgin; those intimate and postmortem claims are not independently verified here.
- Liu Ying's adulthood is paired with relief measures: selected dutiful and agriculturally productive households receive tax exemption, a general amnesty follows his cap ceremony, and Qin-era book restrictions are repealed.
- Liu Ying reportedly builds an elevated road between [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]] and [[ChanglePalace|长乐宫]] to reduce disruption caused by imperial travel to visit Lu Zhi.
- Shusun Tong objects that the road crosses Liu Bang's ceremonial route, but then advises against visible demolition because an emperor should not acknowledge error; Liu Ying instead orders another Gaozu temple north of the Wei River.
- Sima Guang explicitly rejects rulerly infallibility and treats willingness to correct known mistakes as the relevant standard for sage government.
- The episode records repeated fires, unusual winter weather, drought, further labor mobilization, and the deaths or appointments of Cao Shen, Wang Ling, Chen Ping, Zhang Liang, and Zhou Bo as the reign's annalistic background.
- Liu Ying dies young, after which Lu Zhi installs Liu Gong under regency authority; the host's claim that maternal pressure, alcohol, and depression caused Liu Ying's death is interpretive rather than established medical history.
- Liu Gong is reportedly raised as Zhang Yan's son after his birth mother is killed, then confined, deposed, and killed after learning the secret and threatening revenge; Liu Yi is renamed Liu Hong and installed while Lu Zhi continues to govern.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The Liu Gong and Liu Hong sequence reinforces [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-200-lishishang-diyici-tonghuopengzhang-lgcmvklcdkv-mtxv_bei2wclvuqw|Hanji 200]] while moving the narrative backward to Liu Ying's death and Liu Gong's initial installation.
- The episode's exact ages, dates, labor totals, titles, weather reports, fire sequence, words, and ritual route require comparison with transmitted histories.
- Zhang Yan's age, Liu Ying's refusal to consummate the marriage, and the later virginity report are source-scoped; they do not establish every participant's knowledge, consent, or agency.
- The account links Liu Ying's emotional decline to Lu Zhi's pressure and alcohol, but the supplied document does not establish a clinical diagnosis or exclusive cause of death.
- Liu Gong and Liu Hong's biological parentage, their mothers' identities and deaths, Liu Gong's threat and health, and the legal procedure of deposition and accession remain source-attributed.
- Shusun Tong's exact wording may be a transmitted rhetorical formula. The durable contrast is between face-preserving ritual repair and Sima Guang's explicit defense of acknowledging and correcting error.
