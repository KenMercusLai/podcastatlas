# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》895丨血腥屠刀下的骨肉情深

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 169 CE party-prohibition branch immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-896-jiaru-luanshi-jianglin-ni-gai-ruhe-zibao-lsuvt-ksp-gfggc9riqqovjswkg|Hanji 896]]. [[ZhangJianLateHan|张俭]] flees to the home of [[KongBaoLateHan|孔褒]], where sixteen-year-old [[KongRong|孔融]] shelters him; after Zhang escapes, Kong Bao, Kong Rong, and [[KongRongMother|孔融母]] compete to assume the capital charge.

The episode's core synthesis is that political persecution turns rescue, friendship, and kinship into a [[FugitiveShelterCollateralRisk|fugitive-shelter collateral risk]]. The Kong family makes that risk morally radiant through mutual self-sacrifice, while [[XiaFuLateHan|夏馥]] and [[DuGenLateHan|杜根]] supply the darker counterpoint: survival flight can drag helpers into danger, so withdrawal and non-contact may also be ethical choices.

## Key Claims

- In 169 CE, Zhang Jian is endangered in the party-prohibition crackdown and seeks refuge with his long-time friend Kong Bao.
- Kong Bao is away when Zhang arrives, so sixteen-year-old Kong Rong decides to receive and hide Zhang Jian in the Kong household.
- After the case is exposed, Zhang Jian escapes, but local authorities imprison Kong Bao and Kong Rong and cannot easily decide who should bear the charge.
- Kong Rong claims responsibility because he personally hid Zhang Jian; Kong Bao claims responsibility because Zhang came as his friend; their mother claims responsibility because she is the household head.
- The court is moved by the family's mutual willingness to die, but [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s court still orders Kong Bao to suffer capital punishment.
- The source treats this story as a stronger basis for Kong Rong's reputation than the familiar childhood "letting pears" anecdote.
- Zhang Jian later survives the danger, returns after party prohibitions loosen, accepts a Weiwei appointment in the Jian'an period, withdraws after seeing [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s usurping ambition, and dies in [[Xudu|许都]] at eighty-four.
- Xia Fu criticizes Zhang Jian's flight because one fugitive's survival can implicate many households; he then disguises himself, withdraws into remote labor, and refuses his brother Xia Jing's money to avoid receiving disaster.
- Du Gen is used as a comparison: when hidden after offending Empress Dowager Deng, he avoids old friends because exposure would implicate them.
- [[TanSitong|谭嗣同]]'s prison poem is invoked to connect Zhang Jian and Du Gen to later memories of choosing whether to flee, stay, live, or die under political persecution.

## Key Quotes

> "望门投止。" - idiom tied to Zhang Jian's flight.

> "一人逃死，祸及万家。" - Xia Fu's criticism of the collateral cost of flight.

> "去留肝胆两昆仑。" - Tan Sitong's later poetic frame for flight and steadfastness.
