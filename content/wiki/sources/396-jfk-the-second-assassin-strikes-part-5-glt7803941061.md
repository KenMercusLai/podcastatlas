# 396. JFK: The Second Assassin Strikes (Part 5)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode reconstructs the two days between the [[KennedyAssassination|assassination of John F. Kennedy]] and [[JackRuby|Jack Ruby]]'s killing of [[LeeHarveyOswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]]. It argues that the [[DallasPoliceDepartment|Dallas Police Department]] assembled a substantial case quickly, but its failure to protect Oswald during a public transfer eliminated a trial and permanently damaged confidence in that case. Ruby's grief, erratic conduct, Western Union timing, and easy basement access are presented as evidence of an impulsive act rather than a professional silencing operation.

## Key Claims

- Police linked Oswald to an unusual Thursday visit, a long package described as curtain rods, the Depository's sixth floor, the rifle purchase, a backyard photograph, false statements, flight, and the killing of J. D. Tippit.
- Marina and Robert Oswald both found Oswald's calm demeanor troubling, although their impressions are behavioral evidence rather than proof of guilt.
- Dallas police planned heavy transfer security after threats against Oswald, but a vehicle change and an accessible basement ramp allowed Ruby to reach him.
- Ruby was sending money to an employee at Western Union minutes before the shooting, which the hosts treat as inconsistent with a precisely timed professional assassination.
- Ruby's visible grief, emotional volatility, lack of escape plan, and stated motives support an impulsive explanation without independently resolving every question about his contacts or access.
- Oswald's death at Parkland Hospital prevented a trial and fused the department's custody failure with public judgment of its underlying investigation.
- Kennedy's nationally watched funeral, Oswald's difficult burial, and the heavily attended funeral of Officer Tippit show sharply different forms of public grief and moral identification.
- The episode deliberately leaves the lone-gunman-versus-conspiracy question open for the following installments.

## Key Quotes

> "Patsy" - Oswald's description of himself while claiming that he had been framed.

> "a Jew has guts" - Ruby's reported explanation that his act also expressed ethnic pride.
