# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》230｜申屠嘉欲杀晁错，为何反被气死？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s reign through ritual titles, tax and penal measures, appointments, and the first visible struggle around [[ChaoCuoWesternHan|晁错]]. Chancellor [[ShentuJiaWesternHan|申屠嘉]] tries to turn Chao's unauthorized opening through the wall adjoining the Former Emperor's temple precinct into a capital accusation, but Chao reaches the emperor first, admits the act, and receives protection; Jingdi then defeats the impeachment by taking responsibility, and Shentu Jia dies after returning home.

The second branch establishes the pre-succession scale of [[LiuWuLiangKingWesternHan|梁孝王刘武]]'s position. Maternal favor, imperial gifts, a large and fertile kingdom, expanded palaces and roads, a broad guest network, and exceptional Chang'an access make Liang both a privileged royal household and a latent political concentration. The episode ends immediately before Jingdi's banquet promise in Hanji 231 part 1, so it supplies the material and relational preconditions for that later succession expectation rather than the promise itself.

## Key Claims

- Jingdi approves the ancestral titles 太祖 for Gaozu and 太宗 for Emperor Wen, restores half the land tax, lowers two flogging schedules from five hundred to three hundred and from three hundred to two hundred, and appoints a new group of senior officials.
- Chao Cuo receives repeated private audiences, changes numerous rules with imperial support, and discusses weakening royal kingdoms through demotion and punishment, while Shentu Jia's advice is increasingly rejected.
- After Chao opens a more convenient southern passage through a wall associated with the Former Emperor's temple precinct, Shentu Jia prepares a capital impeachment instead of first arresting him.
- Chao learns of the plan, enters the palace overnight, admits the act, and secures Jingdi's protection before the next court session.
- Jingdi says the opening was in unused outer ground and was done on his authority; Shentu Jia's impeachment fails, and the episode reports that he later dies after vomiting blood in anger.
- Liu Wu's position rests on Dowager Dou's affection, Jingdi's exceptional reception, a large and rich Liang domain, extensive building, and a guest circle including Mei Cheng, Yan Ji, Yang Sheng, Gongsun Gui, Zou Yang, and Sima Xiangru.
- The host interprets Liu Wu's privilege as a risk of pride and declining respect for court boundaries; this is a forward-looking judgment rather than proof that every privilege already constituted disloyalty.

## Key Quotes

> “此非庙垣，乃壖中垣，不致于法。朕使为之。” - Jingdi's episode-attributed defense of Chao Cuo against Shentu Jia's accusation.

> “吾悔不先斩错乃请之。” - Shentu Jia's reported regret after the failed impeachment.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source backfills Chao's early dependence on imperial access, the first stage of Jingdi's flogging reductions, and Liu Wu's material and relational base before the later succession and Seven States sequence.
- Penal chronology boundary: Hanji 230 reports reductions from five hundred/three hundred strokes to three hundred/two hundred; Hanji 243 later reports reductions from three hundred/two hundred to two hundred/one hundred plus execution standards. These are treated as successive reforms, not incompatible versions of one decree.
- Causal boundary: Chao's overnight audience and Jingdi's protection explain the failed impeachment in the episode, but they do not prove that Shentu Jia literally died from anger as a medically established cause.
- Source-scope caution: ancestral-title procedure, Xiongnu talks, tax restoration, labor-service age, royal enfeoffments, offices, wall and temple geography, legal penalty, leaked information, audiences, dialogue, cause of death, omens, Liu Wu's county count, wealth, construction dimensions, guest roster, court ceremony, and private motives require comparison with transmitted records.
