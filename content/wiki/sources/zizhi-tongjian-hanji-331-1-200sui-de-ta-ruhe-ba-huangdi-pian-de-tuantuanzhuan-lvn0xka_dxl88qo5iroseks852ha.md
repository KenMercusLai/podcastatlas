# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》331-1｜200岁的他，如何把皇帝骗得团团转？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[ShaowengWesternHan|少翁]] as an early [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] court-fangshi case. Shaoweng reportedly claims to be two hundred years old, combines historical storytelling with a screened apparition of the emperor's deceased consort Wang夫人, and turns grief and immortality desire into trust, the title 文成将军, and sacred construction at [[GanquanPalace|甘泉宫]].

The episode then supplies a checkable failure. After divine beings do not appear, Shaoweng allegedly feeds a written silk text to a cow and predicts a heavenly writing inside it; Wudi recognizes the handwriting, interrogates him, and has him killed. The host's shadow-play explanation for the apparition and the claimed origin of shadow puppetry remain unverified, while the ox-belly sequence is retained as the episode's central example of staged evidence exposing an otherwise inaccessible sacred claim.

## Key Claims

- Shaoweng wins Wudi's attention by claiming an age of two hundred years, knowledge of Qin-Han stories, and the ability to summon spirits.
- The episode says he stages a nighttime appearance of the deceased Wang夫人 behind a screen and forbids Wudi from approaching, making distance and limited visibility part of the proof conditions.
- After the apparition, Wudi grants Shaoweng the title 文成将军 and accepts advice to decorate palace space and expand sacred construction intended for divine visitors.
- More than a year of nonarrival weakens trust and moves Shaoweng from open-ended supernatural promise to a physical sign that can be inspected.
- Shaoweng allegedly hides a written silk text in a cow, predicts a heavenly text in its belly, and is exposed when Wudi recognizes the handwriting.
- The episode says Shaoweng confesses under threat of torture and is executed, while Wudi conceals the embarrassing cause and publicly attributes the death to poisoned horse liver.
- The opening maxim about concealing wisdom, eloquence, and courage is the host's moral frame: displaying extraordinary ability near coercive power can endanger both claimant and patron.

## Key Quotes

> “是也非也” - the episode's rendering of Wudi's uncertainty before the screened apparition.

> “文成食马肝” - the public explanation the episode says Wudi used to conceal Shaoweng's execution.

## Contradictions

- The episode says Shaoweng advised construction of Ganquan Palace, while existing wiki sources place a Ganquan palace or area in earlier Qin and Warring States narratives. The claim is therefore treated as construction or renovation within a broader complex, not secure evidence that Shaoweng originated the place.
- No contradiction is adopted between Shaoweng's death here and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-344-1-ta-bushi-zhoujielun-que-ping-yichang-moshu-jiang-gongzhu-pian-shangchuang-lho5giyqfpigbvfhguckpnbsy6oc|Hanji 344-1]]: the later episode explicitly treats his killing as a precedent that [[LuanDaWesternHan|栾大]] uses when negotiating protection.
- The source is a structured podcast summary rather than a primary-text dossier. Shaoweng's claimed age, historical knowledge, exact dialogue, apparition mechanics, palace works, elapsed time, handwriting recognition, confession, execution procedure, and cover story remain episode-attributed.
- The host's suggestion that the apparition was shadow play, and that Shaoweng was consequently called an inventor of shadow puppetry, is not promoted into established biography.
