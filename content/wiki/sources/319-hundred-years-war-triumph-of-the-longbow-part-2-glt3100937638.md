# 319: Hundred Years' War: Triumph of the Longbow (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[EdwardIIIOfEngland|Edward III]] from the financially strained opening of the [[HundredYearsWar|Hundred Years' War]] through [[BattleOfSluys|Sluys]], the 1346 Normandy campaign, [[BattleOfCrecy|Crécy]], and the capture of [[Calais]]. It argues that materially weaker [[England]] offset [[France]] through wool pressure on [[Flanders]], Channel security, allied access, credit, destructive campaigning, prepared positions, and archery rather than through chivalric courage alone.

Edward's Arthurian charisma and tournament fellowship helped sustain elite commitment, but the victories depended on logistics and institutions. Sluys combined ships, longbowmen, boarding forces, and Flemish intervention; Crécy combined selected ground, field obstacles, missile fire, disciplined deployment, and French command failure; Calais required a long maritime supply effort. The episode closes by showing these gains as both transformative and precarious just before the Black Death.

## Key Claims

- Edward turns the 1337 wool embargo into pressure on Flemish cloth cities, then uses his French crown claim to ease their oath problem and obtain support against Philip VI.
- England's smaller fiscal and demographic base makes a prolonged war dangerous, so Edward borrows heavily and seeks spectacular operational successes.
- [[BattleOfSluys|Sluys]] gives England a major Channel victory through longbow range and rate, infantry boarding, and a Flemish rear attack, but it does not solve Edward's debts.
- The Breton succession struggle and English possession of Brest help protect maritime routes toward Gascony after the initial Flanders campaign stalls.
- Edward's 1346 [[Chevauchee|chevauchée]] across Normandy destroys countryside and pressures Paris in order to damage Philip's prestige and force a response rather than to occupy every place crossed.
- [[CampaignLogisticsBattlefieldSelection]] links repaired bridges, the Blanchetaque ford, pursuit, and Edward's chosen ground near Crécy to the eventual battle.
- [[BattleOfCrecy|Crécy]] is explained through prepared terrain, English archery, obstacles and field cannon, weakened Genoese crossbow deployment, repeated French cavalry attacks, and command disorder rather than numerical strength alone.
- The siege and settlement of [[Calais]] convert battlefield victory into an English bridgehead, but only through sustained shipping, labor, food supply, coercive surrender, expulsion, and garrisoning.
- David II's defeat at Neville's Cross and Charles de Blois's capture widen the English success across the Scottish, Breton, and French fronts.
- The Black Death interrupts the apparent momentum created by Sluys, Crécy, and Calais.

## Key Quotes

> "win his spurs" - Edward III's reported refusal to reinforce his son at Crécy in the episode's heroic account.

> "modern warfare" - the hosts' framing of Crécy as a victory shaped by projectile weapons and prepared tactics rather than knightly hand-to-hand combat alone.

## Contradictions

- The source says the siege of Calais lasted from July 1346 to September 1347, but later dates the surrender to 4 August 1347. The wiki preserves this internal chronology conflict rather than silently choosing one version.
- No settled contradiction with Parts 1, 3, or 4 was found. This episode fills military and operational gaps that Part 1 explicitly deferred and that Parts 3-4 previously referenced only in retrospect.
- Longbow origin and armor-penetration claims, fleet and army sizes, casualty totals, speeches, the Black Prince's acquisition of the ostrich-feather badge, and precise tactical sequences remain source-scoped popular-history claims rather than independently audited conclusions.
