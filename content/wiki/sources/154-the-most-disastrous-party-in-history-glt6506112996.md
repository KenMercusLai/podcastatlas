# 154. The Most Disastrous Party in History

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses [[BorisJohnson|Boris Johnson]]'s Partygate scandal to rank ten gatherings whose failures became larger than the events themselves. Its cases move from social-media fraud and destructive elite behavior through treacherous feasts, court catastrophe, mass death, and imperial spectacle, showing how a party can expose hypocrisy, insecurity, dynastic conflict, weak government, or political distance from ordinary people.

The strongest synthesis is [[DisastrousPartyPoliticalSymbol]]: an event becomes historically durable when it condenses an existing legitimacy problem into a vivid story. [[KhodynkaTragedy]], [[BalDesArdents]], and [[BlackDinner]] connect celebration to death or betrayal, while [[Persepolis]] joins [[AlexanderTheGreat|Alexander the Great]]'s legendary drunken destruction to [[MohammadRezaPahlavi|Mohammad Reza Pahlavi]]'s later imperial commemoration. The episode repeatedly qualifies vivid anecdotes whose surviving form depends on hostile sources, propaganda, legend, or retrospective political memory.

## Key Claims

- Partygate mattered politically because gatherings during COVID-19 restrictions made an existing problem of trust and rule-setting hypocrisy visible around Boris Johnson's government.
- [[FyreFestival]] exposed the gap between influencer marketing and operational reality: luxury branding could sell an experience that lacked the promised accommodation, performers, food, and payment infrastructure.
- Peter the Great's destructive 1698 stay at Sayes Court is framed as elite license made memorable through damage to John Evelyn's house and garden.
- The Blood Feast of Roskilde, Muhammad Ali's 1811 Mamluk massacre, and [[BlackDinner]] show hospitality functioning as a weapon when reconciliation or celebration places rivals inside a controlled killing space.
- The episode treats the rose-petal death story about Elagabalus cautiously because the hostile *Augustan History* and later art make the anecdote culturally powerful without making it secure history.
- [[BalDesArdents]] turned court entertainment into lethal evidence of instability around [[CharlesVIOfFrance|Charles VI]], while [[KhodynkaTragedy]] turned coronation generosity into a mass-casualty omen for [[NicholasII|Nicholas II]].
- [[Persepolis]] ranks first because two different spectacles at the same imperial site became symbols of overreach: Alexander's reported burning of the palace and the shah's 1971 celebration of monarchy.

## Key Quotes

> "a party highlights anxieties and flaws that are already there" - the hosts' recurring explanation for why some gatherings become political symbols.
