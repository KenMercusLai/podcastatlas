## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] presentation has [[BillGurley|Bill Gurley]] compare the unresolved origin of COVID-19 with investigations of the Surfside collapse, the Boeing 737 Max crashes, Hurricane Katrina, Fukushima, and Challenger. He uses [[CAPACorrectivePreventiveAction|corrective and preventive action]] to argue that prevention requires a defensible root cause and that implicated institutions cannot be the only investigators. Gurley does not choose between animal spillover and a laboratory accident; he calls for an independent, document-driven inquiry while the source itself remains an advocacy presentation rather than a balanced adjudication of the competing evidence.

## Key Claims
- [[CatastrophicFailureRootCauseInvestigation]] is presented as a repeatable public-safety practice: overlapping technical, legislative, legal, journalistic, and independent inquiries can expose both physical failure and institutional resistance.
- [[CAPACorrectivePreventiveAction|CAPA]] requires detection, investigation, root-cause confirmation, correction, and prevention; Gurley's central methodological claim is that prevention is unreliable when root cause is skipped.
- Surfside, the Boeing 737 Max, Katrina, Fukushima, and Challenger are used as comparison cases where investigators, whistleblowers, families, journalists, or independent commissions overcame institutional blind spots.
- [[RichardFeynman|Richard Feynman]] and [[KiyoshiKurokawa|Kiyoshi Kurokawa]] represent the desired investigator profile: independent of implicated institutions, evidence-driven, publicly legible, and focused on prevention.
- Gurley argues that COVID-19's human and economic toll should have produced a larger independent inquiry than the comparison disasters, but says no equivalent newsroom team, bipartisan congressional investigation, scientific program, victims' case, or commission emerged.
- [[InvestigationConflictOfInterest]] is the episode's explanation for part of that gap: people or institutions connected to virology policy, funding, or research may have incentives that make them unsuitable as the sole judges of a possible research-related accident.
- The episode identifies [[AnthonyFauci|Anthony Fauci]], Francis Collins, Peter Daszak, Jeremy Farrar, and Ralph Baric as alleged institutional blockers; that characterization is Gurley's argument, not an independently established finding of misconduct or causation.
- [[DRASTIC]] and U.S. Right to Know are praised as decentralized searchers using scientific analysis and public-records requests when larger institutions did not investigate aggressively.
- Gurley treats the DEFUSE proposal and disputes around the Proximal Origin paper and Lancet statement as evidence requiring scrutiny, not as proof that SARS-CoV-2 came from a laboratory.
- [[COVIDOriginsInvestigationUncertainty]] remains the final position: animal spillover and a laboratory accident should both remain open until transparent evidence establishes a defensible root cause.
- The episode links unresolved origins to future preparedness and [[ScienceTrustPoliticization]] because premature certainty, partisan sorting, and perceived gatekeeping can weaken trust in the next public-health emergency.

## Key Quotes
> "root cause" - the finding Gurley says prevention must follow rather than assume.

> "searchers" - Gurley's label for independent people pursuing evidence across institutional boundaries.

> "blockers" - Gurley's label for actors he believes impeded scrutiny; the source does not independently establish the characterization.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is recorded. The episode strengthens existing wiki claims that COVID-origin questions became entangled with scientific and political trust, but it does not resolve the origin question.
- The presentation is advocacy from one speaker and does not include detailed responses from the scientists or institutions criticized. Its claims about conflicts, private-public inconsistency, animal testing, early cases, DEFUSE, institutional obstruction, death toll, and economic cost therefore remain source-scoped.
- Gurley's refusal to choose an origin hypothesis is an important qualification: defending inquiry into a laboratory accident is not the same as establishing that one occurred.
