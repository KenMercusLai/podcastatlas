# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》481丨威风！西汉冯奉世一举灭敌国！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Yongguang 2 / 42 BCE under [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]], before the later Yuan-era household, succession, and Shi Xian branches. It follows [[FengFengshiWesternHan|冯奉世]] responding to a Qiang rebellion in [[LongxiCommandery|陇西郡]] after famine and court hesitation leave senior ministers without a clear plan.

The durable synthesis is that frontier crisis decisions can become more expensive when the court tries to save the first increment of troops. Feng Fengshi argues for reconnaissance, sufficient force, and one-month resolution because a small expedition may fail, embolden the rebels, alarm other groups, and force larger later mobilization; Yuan initially compromises at twelve thousand troops, then has to send a much larger reinforcement after early defeat.

## Key Claims

- The episode frames decisive management as knowing when delay and compromise create more disorder than action.
- In 42 BCE, the Xianjie-linked Qiang rebellion coincides with poor harvests and hunger, making the court anxious about both military and provisioning cost.
- Feng Fengshi differs from silent ministers by volunteering to command and by treating deterrence, rebel scale, weapons quality, and campaign duration as linked variables.
- Feng proposes forty thousand troops rather than the one-man-per-two-enemies textbook ratio of sixty thousand because he judges Qiang weapons inferior and expects a one-month finish.
- Wei Xuancheng and other ministers object that autumn harvest timing makes large conscription too burdensome, so they recommend only ten thousand troops.
- Feng warns that too few troops may fail at attack, fail at defense, encourage other groups to join, and prolong the war.
- Yuan initially chooses a compromise of twelve thousand troops under a tuntian label, but after Han Chang's forward detachment loses, he dispatches sixty thousand reinforcements and later appoints Ren Qianqiu as Fenwu general.

## Key Quotes

> "善用兵者，役不再籍，粮不三载" - Feng Fengshi's compressed argument that good command avoids repeated levies and repeated grain transport.

> "当断不断，必受其乱" - the host's opening maxim for hesitation in crisis.
