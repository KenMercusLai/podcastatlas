# 216. Pigeons

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses [[Pigeon]] history to connect domestication, religious symbolism, sport, news, military communication, intelligence, and changing urban attitudes. Its central case is [[OperationColumba]], a British Second World War project that dropped donated homing pigeons into occupied Europe so civilians could return intelligence about German forces, radar, and weapons sites.

The broader synthesis is that [[CarrierPigeonCommunication]] turned a biological homing behavior into a fast but one-way network whose value was greatest across hostile terrain or after electronic systems failed. The episode also contrasts long human reliance on pigeons with the destruction of the [[PassengerPigeon]], showing how utility, symbolism, abundance, and reputation can change sharply across time.

## Key Claims

- Rock doves adapted readily to human-built ledges and dovecots, supporting long use for food, breeding, sport, symbolism, and communication.
- Homing pigeons return to a trained loft rather than carrying messages freely between arbitrary locations, making the network fast but directionally constrained.
- Pigeon posts served ancient, medieval, commercial, and military communication; [[Reuters]] began with a pigeon link carrying stock prices across a telegraph gap in 1850.
- First World War forces relied on pigeons when other battlefield communications failed, with the wounded Cher Ami presented as the best-known rescue case.
- [[OperationColumba]] recruited civilian pigeon fanciers and occupied-Europe respondents into a distributed intelligence system that reportedly identified German activity, radar, and V-weapon sites.
- RAF rescue pigeons and postwar camera experiments show that pigeons could serve as fallback communications or sensing platforms alongside newer technologies.
- The destruction of the formerly abundant [[PassengerPigeon]] and modern contempt for feral pigeons reveal the instability of human judgments about other species.
