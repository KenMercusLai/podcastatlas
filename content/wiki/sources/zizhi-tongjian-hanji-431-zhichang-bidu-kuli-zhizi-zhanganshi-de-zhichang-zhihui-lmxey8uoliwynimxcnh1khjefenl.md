# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》431｜职场必读！酷吏之子张安世的职场智慧

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]] Yuankang 3 / 63 BCE layer between Hanji 430 and Hanji 432. It deepens [[ZhangAnshiWesternHan|张安世]] from reward-restraint figure into a full court-survival case: as [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]]'s son, he refuses excessive favor, fears Huo-family association risk, advises the ruler privately without displaying credit, recommends talent without creating private obligation, and sends [[ZhangYanshouWesternHan|张延寿]] outward to keep the family from looking too concentrated at court.

The episode then pairs Zhang's high-court caution with [[ShuGuangShuShouWesternHan|疏广与疏受]]'s timely retirement and wealth-distribution lesson, before returning to [[HuangBaWesternHan|黄霸]] as a humane, detailed, information-rich local administrator. The durable synthesis is that power, credit, wealth, and responsibility become safer when kept inside role boundaries: good service should not be converted into personal credit display, private favors, dynastic family accumulation, or local governance by mere punishment.

## Key Claims

- Zhang Anshi repeatedly treats excessive imperial favor as danger rather than pure benefit, asking to resign surplus revenue because father and son have both been ennobled.
- After the Huo-family disaster, Xuan's pardon of Zhang Anshi's granddaughter reduces immediate association risk, but Zhang still fears becoming another overfavored Huo Guang-style target.
- Zhang advises Xuan privately on major affairs, then withdraws or feigns surprise when decisions are announced, preserving imperial authority and reducing peer jealousy.
- Zhang's recommendation ethics forbid turning public personnel selection into private favor: when a promoted man comes to thank him with gifts, Zhang cuts off access to the household.
- Zhang also criticizes a lang official's self-promotion, teaching that known merit must be handled through the ruler's decision channel rather than loud credit-claiming.
- Shu Guang and Shu Shou retire when rank, money, and reputation are already sufficient, then disperse reward wealth rather than burden descendants with excessive assets.
- Huang Ba's Yingchuan governance is presented as detailed grassroots administration: production, thrift, relief, information gathering, education before punishment, and continuity of usable officials.

## Key Quotes

> "知足不辱，知止不殆" - the retirement maxim attached to Shu Guang and Shu Shou.

> "当官不为民做主，不如回家卖红薯" - the closing folk line tying office to public responsibility.
