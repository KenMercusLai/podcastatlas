# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》433｜西汉王褒的 《圣主得贤臣颂》说了啥？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Shenjue 1 / 61 BCE under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]], between the [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-432-kai-jinian-gongchen-zhi-xianhe-jiemi-qilinge-shiyi-gongchen-lj65dvmawrppq2iomuzarg1ah9ak|Hanji 432]] Qilin Pavilion and frontier-warning layer and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-434-yidai-nengli-dangan-zhize-huangdi-jiemi-hanchao-qiren-zhangchang-ligbhsgfymvjlhpvieqhpbkpvqk4|Hanji 434]]'s Zhang Chang and Wang Ji remonstrance layer. It centers [[WangBaoWesternHan|王褒]], whom Xuan sends toward Yizhou in the gold-horse and jade-rooster search before asking him to write [[ShengzhuDeXianchenSong|《圣主得贤臣颂》]].

The durable synthesis is that the episode turns a short annalistic notice into a political-literary theory of mutual recognition. [[ShengzhuXianchenMutualRecognition|圣主贤臣互相成就]] describes rule as a two-sided fit: the ruler must actively seek and recognize talent, while the worthy minister needs a ruler whose trust makes ability usable; [[TongyueWangBao|《僮约》]] adds a second Wang Bao layer as social-history evidence for Han-era Sichuan and early tea culture.

## Key Claims

- In 61 BCE, Xuan sacrifices at Ganquan and Hedong, then sends Wang Bao with imperial credentials to search in Yizhou for the gold-horse and jade-rooster spirits.
- The episode places Xuan's interest in Wang Bao inside a broader Xuan-era literary and scholarly culture: Xuan discusses the Six Arts and classics, admires Han Wudi, favors poetry, music, and fu, and keeps literati near the court through the Gold Horse Gate.
- [[JinmaMenCourtLiteraryAccess|金马门待诏]] is presented as both honor and access channel for talented scholars such as Gongsun Hong, Dongfang Shuo, Zhufu Yan, and Liu Xiang.
- [[ShengzhuDeXianchenSong|《圣主得贤臣颂》]] argues that worthy officials are the state's crucial instruments: as good tools, skilled artisans, proper drivers, and seasonal clothing make work easier, the right people make governance effective.
- Wang Bao's political logic is reciprocal: rulers must first labor to seek worthy people, while worthy people may remain obscure until a ruler can hear, trust, and use their counsel.
- The episode treats the essay's "flying dragon," "wind and cloud," smooth-feather, and ocean-whale imagery as the emotional and cosmic elevation of harmonious ruler-minister fit.
- Wang Bao's [[TongyueWangBao|《僮约》]] is highlighted as more valuable than the court-praised ode for historians because it records Han-era Sichuan daily life and early references to drinking, buying, and planting tea.

## Key Quotes

> "贤才是国家的工具" - the episode's summary of Wang Bao's personnel thesis.

> "圣主得贤臣，颂" - the title phrase through which the episode frames ruler-minister mutual recognition.

> "飞龙在天，利见大人" - the Yijing phrase used to elevate the ruler-minister fit.
