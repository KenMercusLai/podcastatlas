# Arvind Krishna & Nikesh Arora: Quantum Threat, Open vs Frontier AI, AI Cyber Warfare & SaaS Collapse

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] conversation with [[ArvindKrishna|Arvind Krishna]] and [[NikeshArora|Nikesh Arora]] connects the long lead time for [[QuantumCryptographicMigration]] to enterprise model economics, AI-assisted cyber defense, vertically integrated platforms, and software consolidation. Its central operating argument is that organizations should begin cryptographic migration before a capable quantum machine exists, experiment with frontier models before routing routine production work to cheaper controlled models, and verify AI-generated vulnerability findings and patches rather than equating discovery with remediation. The speakers also expect reasoning, memory, context, and applications to become more integrated while enterprises reduce a fragmented software estate to a smaller set of major platforms.

## Key Claims
- A cryptographically relevant quantum computer may arrive around 2030, but the date is uncertain; "harvest now, decrypt later" exposure and multi-year infrastructure replacement make present migration decisions consequential.
- Error correction or mitigation demonstrated on working hardware, independent verification, and sustained growth in executable gates are more useful milestones than raw physical-qubit counts.
- [[ExperimentationProductionModelSplit]] separates fast frontier-model prototyping from production deployment, where smaller or open-weight models may offer lower cost, greater control, and better protection of proprietary data.
- [[AIEnabledVulnerabilityDiscovery]] can help both attackers and defenders; reported false-positive rates of roughly 20% to 30% mean findings and proposed patches still require validation.
- Shared vulnerability intelligence, cheaper defense, and technical superiority are presented as ways to reduce attacker advantage, while product liability remains with the company that releases AI-generated software.
- [[StrategicStackIntegration]] lets a company keep differentiated layers under control while buying commodity components and shifting value between layers when a profit pool is attacked.
- [[AgentPlatformSwitchingCosts]] rise when reasoning becomes coupled to memory, personal context, APIs, and services, potentially moving the primary interface from individual apps to coordinating agents.
- [[EnterpriseSoftwarePlatformConsolidation]] could reduce hundreds or thousands of vendors to a smaller set of major platforms, but reliability-critical transactional systems may require years of parallel validation.

## Key Quotes
> "95% of the work" - the source's threshold for a cheaper model being economically sufficient for many enterprise tasks.

> "five to ten major platforms" - the source's forecast for enterprise software consolidation.

> "Encrypted data stolen today could be retained and decrypted" - the source's reason for beginning migration before a capable quantum computer arrives.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode strengthens the urgency of [[QuantumCryptographicMigration]] but leaves the 2030 midpoint, annual gate-growth path, physical-to-logical-qubit example, and cryptographic-break threshold as speaker forecasts rather than demonstrated milestones.
- The forecast that most token use will move to open-weight models qualifies frontier-model dominance without showing that self-hosted cost estimates include hardware utilization, staffing, security, evaluation, and migration overhead.
- The software-consolidation thesis is explicitly weaker for financial, banking, and other high-reliability systems, where long parallel operation and validation may preserve incumbent systems.
