# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》207｜刘邦小姨子，吕媭被棍杀（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows the immediate settlement after the anti-Lü coup. [[ZhouBo|周勃]]'s side destroys the wider Lü household, [[LuXuWesternHan|吕媭]] and her son are killed, [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|齐王刘襄]] is told to demobilize, and [[GuanYing|灌婴]] confronts [[WeiBoWesternHan|魏勃]] before withdrawing from Xingyang.

The second half shows that removing the Lü commanders did not settle succession. [[ChenPing|陈平]], Zhou Bo, and allied ministers isolate the young emperor, deny the legitimacy of the Hui-era princes, kill them, and choose [[LiuHeng|代王刘恒]] over Liu Xiang and [[LiuChangHuainanKing|淮南王刘长]]. The episode presents Liu Heng's seniority, reputation for filial kindness, and the restraint of [[BaoJi|薄姬]]'s family as positive qualifications, while Liu Xiang's powerful uncle [[SiJunWesternHan|驷钧]] is treated as a renewed outer-relative risk.

## Key Claims

- After Lü Chan's death, Zhou Bo reportedly orders the arrest and killing of the wider Lü clan, including Lü Lu and Lü Tong's households.
- [[LuXuWesternHan|吕媭]], Lü Zhi's sister and Fan Kuai's widow, is presented as a politically active Lü-family figure who had attacked Chen Ping's reputation and is beaten to death during the purge; her son Fan Kang is also killed.
- The source says Liu Zhang carries news of the capital victory to Liu Xiang and asks Qi to stop its campaign, while Guan Ying rebukes Wei Bo for encouraging the mobilization.
- Wei Bo defends acting without prior authorization through a house-fire analogy but then appears terrified; Guan Ying dismisses him as mediocre and returns from Xingyang.
- The restoration coalition reinstates Chen Ping and Shen Yiji as chancellors while removing Shen Yiji's taifu role, which the host reads as institutional isolation of the young emperor.
- Ministers justify killing the young emperor Liu Hong and the other Hui-era princes by declaring that they were not Liu Ying's biological sons; the episode treats participant self-protection against future retaliation as a central motive.
- Liu Xiang is rejected despite his senior-branch claim and anti-Lü contribution because Si Jun's conduct is presented as a risk of renewed outer-relative domination.
- Liu Heng is selected because of seniority among Liu Bang's surviving sons, a reputation for kindness and filial conduct, and the comparatively restrained maternal family around Bao Ji.
- [[LiJiWesternHan|郦寄]]'s role in separating Lü Lu from military control is defended through Ban Gu's argument that saving the ruler, state, and his coerced father could outweigh private friendship.

## Key Quotes

> “家里失火” - Wei Bo's analogy for acting before requesting authorization when the political emergency was already underway.

> “安刘氏江山、救父亲、救皇帝” - the episode's compression of Ban Gu's defense of Li Ji.

## Connections

- [[LuXuWesternHan|吕媭]], [[LuLuWesternHan|吕禄]], and [[LuChanWesternHan|吕产]] - Lü-family figures killed as the winning coalition expands military victory into household liquidation.
- [[LiuXiangQiKingWesternHan|齐王刘襄]], [[WeiBoWesternHan|魏勃]], and [[GuanYing|灌婴]] - outside mobilization is ordered to stop after the capital outcome is settled.
- [[LiJiWesternHan|郦寄]] and [[ZhouBo|周勃]] - actors in the Northern Army transfer whose conduct is reassessed after the victory.
- [[LiuHeng|汉文帝刘恒]], [[BaoJi|薄姬]], and [[SiJunWesternHan|驷钧]] - candidate selection turns on lineage, reputation, and anticipated outer-relative risk.
- [[PostRegencyInsideOutsideMobilization|摄政终结后的内外合击动员]] - the episode supplies the demobilization and settlement phase after the inside-outside coalition succeeds.
- [[SuccessionLegitimacyPurge|继位合法性清洗]] - the anti-Lü victory expands into elimination of Lü households and the young-emperor line before a replacement ruler is installed.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source is a structured popular-history summary rather than a primary-text comparison.
- The opening moral claim that warmer temperament reliably produces greater fortune is treated as framing, not an established causal rule.
- Lü Xu's alleged earlier exercise of power, the details of her execution, the full Lü-clan death list, Liu Hong's parentage, the Hui-era princes' biological status, and the precise legal procedure for their deaths remain source-attributed.
- The source's claim that Liu Heng was the eldest surviving son, the candidates' exact order of consideration, Si Jun's conduct, Bao-family restraint, dialogue, dates, titles, and motives require comparison with transmitted histories.
- Participant fear of later revenge plausibly explains the breadth of the cleanup but is an interpretation rather than direct proof of each minister's private intention.
