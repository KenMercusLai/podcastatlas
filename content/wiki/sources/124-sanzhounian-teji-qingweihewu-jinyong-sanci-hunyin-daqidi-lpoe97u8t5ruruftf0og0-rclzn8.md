## Summary

This third-anniversary [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode reconstructs [[JinYong|金庸]]'s marriages to [[DuYefen|杜冶芬]], [[ZhuMei|朱玫]], and [[LinLeyi|林乐怡]] while distinguishing documented chronology, one-sided recollection, biographical dispute, and host inference. Its strongest contribution is methodological: private experience may inform fiction, but temporal sequence, emotional resemblance, and character similarity do not establish direct causation or one-to-one prototypes.

## Key Claims

- Jin Yong and [[DuYefen|杜冶芬]] married after meeting through her brother in the late 1940s and divorced around 1953; Jin later called her conduct a betrayal, but the episode stresses that the surviving account is largely his and does not settle what occurred or when the relationship had already broken down.
- Jin Yong married [[ZhuMei|朱玫]] in 1956, had four children with her, and received her labor during Ming Pao's early years; claims that she sold jewelry to fund the paper or that he married her only after failing to win Xia Meng remain uncertain or causally unproven.
- Jin Yong's relationship with [[LinLeyi|林乐怡]] began while he was married to Zhu Mei and became a major cause of the second marriage's collapse. His later admission of responsibility does not establish whether marital conflict preceded the affair or diminish the harm of infidelity.
- Accounts that Zhu Mei died in poverty are not established by the episode's evidence: reported property and share transfers, her retained estate, and a biographer's change from “poverty” to “desolation” distinguish economic condition from loneliness and emotional injury.
- The alleged sterilization condition attached to the divorce is unverified and may be a backward inference from Jin Yong and Lin Leyi having no children.
- Stories of Jin Yong and Lin Leyi's first meeting share a bar setting and her service work but vary in the tip, food, and dialogue; their polished form suggests later narrative shaping rather than a reliable transcript of the encounter.
- A large age gap does not by itself settle the relationship's meaning, but Lin Leyi's reported age of sixteen at the beginning makes consent, maturity, and power asymmetry ethically significant rather than incidental.
- Similarities between Zhu Mei and [[HuoQingtong|霍青桐]], between Lin Leyi and [[RenYingyingJinYong|任盈盈]], or between marital pain and passages in [[FeihuWaizhuan|《飞狐外传》]] are interpretive clues, not proof that fictional characters directly reproduce real people.

## Contradictions

- Jin Yong's “betrayal” account and Du Yefen's brother's account of love having ended do not establish the same chronology or meaning; the episode leaves the first divorce unresolved.
- The claim that Zhu Mei ended her life in poverty conflicts with reports of property, shares, and an estate, while “not poor” does not contradict evidence of loneliness, estrangement, or lasting emotional harm.
- The sterilization condition, the exact first-meeting dialogue, whether Zhu Mei knew of the affair for years, and why Jin Yong ultimately divorced remain disputed or speculative.
- The source depends on biographies, interviews, family recollections, and public reporting rather than private records or testimony from every principal; even apparently firsthand memory may be selective or retrospective.
