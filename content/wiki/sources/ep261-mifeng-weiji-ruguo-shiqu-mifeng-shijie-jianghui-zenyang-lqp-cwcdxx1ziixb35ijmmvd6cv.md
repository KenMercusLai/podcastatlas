# EP261 “蜜蜂危机”：如果失去蜜蜂，世界将会怎样？

## Summary
This [[TalkSanlian|Talk三联]] episode with [[GaoYiding|高一丁]], [[MiaoQian|苗千]], [[DuanNongyu|段农裕]], and [[LiuChang|刘畅]] uses bees to make the wider insect crisis visible. It separates familiar honey-producing, human-managed bees from the many wild and often solitary bees that support [[NativeBeeEcologicalPollination|native-bee ecological pollination]] without becoming consumer products. The episode's main synthesis is that the danger is less a simple decline in managed honeybee numbers than a combined [[WildPollinatorCrisis|wild-pollinator crisis]] driven by habitat loss, [[PesticidePollinatorRisk|pesticide exposure]], climate stress, agricultural monoculture, disease, parasites, weak species data, and fragile beekeeper economics.

## Key Claims
- The source distinguishes narrow honeybees from the broader bee world: most bees do not produce harvestable honey, and many are solitary, but they still matter as pollinators.
- In [[Mengyin|蒙阴]], [[DuanNongyu|段农裕]]'s field reporting shows migratory beekeeping as a flower-chasing livelihood shaped by terrain, weather, local relationships, low honey prices, aging practitioners, and sudden pesticide or hail risk.
- In [[Xishuangbanna|西双版纳]], [[LiuChang|刘畅]]'s reporting shows why local bees matter beyond honey: Chinese honeybees, giant honeybees, and stingless bees can support rainforest plants and surrounding crops, while overharvesting and chemical-intensive cash crops can damage those relationships.
- [[PesticidePollinatorRisk|Pesticide risk]] is operational as well as ecological. A severe poisoning event can force a colony to spend one or two months rebuilding, causing beekeepers to miss flower seasons, while agricultural experiments and field observations can be ruined by nearby spraying.
- The source treats [[PollinationServiceMarket|pollination service markets]] as a possible income shift for beekeepers, especially around high-value crops such as blueberries, coffee, macadamia nuts, mangoes, and pomelos, but says the Chinese market is still uneven and often informal.
- [[BeeColonyCollapse|Colony failure]] and wild-pollinator decline are related but not identical. Managed honeybee populations can grow while wild bees and other pollinating insects still lose habitat, food sources, and ecological niches.
- China is presented as under-surveyed for bee biodiversity: the source says China has recorded more than 1,400 bee species but may have far more, and that [[XishuangbannaBotanicalGarden|西双版纳植物园]] only began a systematic local bee survey in 2024.
- The episode rejects the viral "humans live only four years after bees disappear" line as an unclear attribution, while still arguing that pollinator loss would raise food prices, reduce diet diversity, and weaken ecological chains.
- Practical responses include limiting highly harmful pesticides, connecting parks and less-mown flower areas into urban corridors, preserving habitat, and treating ordinary plantings as small support points for pollinators.
