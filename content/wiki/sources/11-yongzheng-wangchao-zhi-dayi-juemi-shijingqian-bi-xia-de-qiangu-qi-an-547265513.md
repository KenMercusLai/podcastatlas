# 11.雍正王朝之大义觉迷：史景迁笔下的千古奇案

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode uses [[JonathanSpence|史景迁]]'s [[TreasonByTheBook|《雍正王朝之大义觉迷》 / Treason by the Book]] to narrate the [[ZengJing|曾静]] case from [[ZhangXi|张熙]]'s anti-Qing letter to [[YueZhongqi|岳钟琪]] through interrogation, secret memorials, arrests, [[QingLiteraryInquisition|文字狱]], and the publication of [[DaiYiJueMiLu|《大义觉迷录》]]. The episode argues that the case was less a disciplined rebellion than a political event enlarged by rumor, anti-Manchu writing, official self-protection, and [[YongzhengEmperor|雍正]]'s need to defend both his personal reputation and Qing legitimacy. Its larger contribution is a Qing-history example of [[ImperialPublicRebuttal|imperial public rebuttal]] and [[PoliticalPropagandaBackfire|propaganda backfire]]: Yongzheng tried to defeat rumor by publishing refutations, while [[QianlongEmperor|乾隆]] later tried to restore control by destroying the book and executing Zeng Jing and Zhang Xi.

## Key Claims
- The Zeng Jing case began when [[ZhangXi|张熙]] approached [[YueZhongqi|岳钟琪]] in Xi'an with a letter calling him "天立大元帅" and urging him, as a supposed descendant of Yue Fei, to rise against Qing rule.
- [[YueZhongqi|岳钟琪]] treated the letter as a deadly political trap because he had served under [[NianGengyao|年羹尧]], occupied a sensitive Sichuan-Shaanxi post, and lived inside a post-purge official climate of fear.
- The investigation used both torture and deception: Yue arranged for Li Yuan to approach Zhang Xi, performed a false oath of rebellion, and then reported the expanding confession chain by [[SecretMemorialPoliticalIntelligence|密折]].
- [[YongzhengEmperor|雍正]] initially considered the affair absurd and hateful, but his response quickly moved from criminal investigation into reputation defense, ideological correction, and public political pedagogy.
- The supposed conspiracy thinned out during arrests across Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and Hunan; many named people had little substantive involvement, pushing the case toward books, influence, and [[LuLiuliang|吕留良]]'s anti-Qing writing.
- [[LiWeiQingOfficial|李卫]]'s search of the Lu family turned a book-buying connection into a wider [[QingLiteraryInquisition|literary inquisition]], with Lu Liuliang's descendants, students, diaries, and printed works becoming the center of punishment.
- Zeng Jing appears in the episode as a frustrated rural scholar whose anti-Qing view combined [[LuLiuliang|吕留良]]'s Hua-Yi arguments, civil-service failure, social distress, omen reading, and idealized Confucian political imagination.
- Yongzheng's strategic split was to spare Zeng Jing as a deluded commoner while making Lu Liuliang and Zhejiang literati carry the heavier guilt for ideological contamination.
- [[DaiYiJueMiLu|《大义觉迷录》]] tried to turn confession, imperial refutation, and repentance into state propaganda, but it made the emperor's family scandals and succession rumors easier to circulate.
- [[QianlongEmperor|乾隆]] reversed the settlement soon after Yongzheng's death by arresting and executing Zeng Jing and Zhang Xi and suppressing the book, which the episode reads as a different style of imperial control.
- The episode's strongest institutional claim is that rumor cannot be eliminated by command: intense suppression can make informal speech more fantastic, while official rebuttal can unintentionally preserve the accusation it tries to defeat.

## Key Quotes
> "不是一个成熟严密的造反组织" - the episode's framing of the case as inflated political panic rather than disciplined rebellion.

> "雍正既要证明自己清白，又要维护皇权秩序" - the source's central tension around personal reputation and state legitimacy.

> "管控越严格，流言可能越离谱" - the episode's broader lesson about rumor and authoritarian control.
