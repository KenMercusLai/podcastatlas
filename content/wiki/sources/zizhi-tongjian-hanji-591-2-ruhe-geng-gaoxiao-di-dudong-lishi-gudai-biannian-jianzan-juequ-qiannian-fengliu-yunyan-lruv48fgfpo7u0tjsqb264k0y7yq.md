# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》591-2丨如何更高效地读懂历史？古代编年鉴赞，撷取千年风流云烟

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] background episode steps out of the immediate Hanji narrative to explain how to read [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] inside the broader Chinese historiographical tradition. It sketches [[TwentyFourHistories|二十四史]], [[Shiji|《史记》]], [[HanShu|《汉书》]], [[HouHanshu|《后汉书》]], [[Sanguozhi|《三国志》]], and [[ZuoZhuan|《左传》]], then positions Zizhi Tongjian as a political, military, ethnic-relations, and ruler-instruction work compiled by [[SimaGuang|司马光]] and collaborators.

The episode's durable contribution is methodological rather than event-narrative. It treats history reading as a mix of source map, genre awareness, and lived interpretation: readers should know whether a text is dynastic official history, biographical history, or chronological statecraft compilation, but they should also enter the people and events rather than only memorizing conclusions.

## Key Claims
- Chinese historical writing is presented as a governance tradition: rulers and later dynasties record the past to preserve experience, teach order, and support political judgment.
- [[TwentyFourHistories|二十四史]] provides the macro-map of dynastic official history, too large to treat as a quick reading project but useful as a civilizational archive.
- [[Shiji|《史记》]] is praised for wide evidence gathering, field investigation, source comparison, and willingness to preserve doubt or competing accounts.
- [[HanShu|《汉书》]], [[HouHanshu|《后汉书》]], and [[Sanguozhi|《三国志》]] are introduced as the other "前四史" texts, with Sanguozhi noted as concise and structurally unusual because it lacks several tables and treatises found in fuller official histories.
- [[ZuoZhuan|《左传》]] is introduced as a narrative-rich chronological history with high literary value, strong war and diplomatic scenes, and some source-scoped supernatural or omen material.
- [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] is defined through its ruler-instruction purpose: political, military, and ethnic-relations episodes are selected so past affairs can illuminate governance.
- The episode emphasizes [[ChronicleBiographicalHistoryForms|史书体例意识]]: readers understand a source better when they know whether it organizes history by annals, biographies, dynasty, or long chronological sequence.
- [[ParticipatoryHistoricalReading|参与式读史]] matters because historians, later readers, and different life stages can produce different interpretations of the same event; argument is part of why history remains alive.
