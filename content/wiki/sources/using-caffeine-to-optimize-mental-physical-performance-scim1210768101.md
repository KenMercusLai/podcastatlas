# Using Caffeine to Optimize Mental & Physical Performance

## Summary
This solo [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] explain caffeine through adenosine blockade, alertness, dopamine-linked reinforcement, performance, memory, tolerance, and sleep timing. It adds [[CaffeineBehavioralReinforcement]] while extending [[CaffeineAdenosineSleepTiming]], [[DopamineToolTiming]], and [[PostLearningArousalTagging]]. Its practical synthesis is that dose, timing, adaptation, pairing context, and downstream sleep matter more than treating caffeine as generic energy.

## Key Claims
- [[CaffeineAdenosineSleepTiming]] is reinforced by the distinction between blocking adenosine receptors and clearing sleep pressure: caffeine can mask accumulated tiredness and be followed by a crash when blockade recedes.
- The episode recommends experimenting with a 90-120-minute delay after waking and avoiding caffeine roughly 8-12 hours before sleep, while allowing earlier use around early intense training and emphasizing individual sensitivity.
- A source-suggested acute dose of one to three milligrams per kilogram is linked to alertness, reaction time, coordination, memory, power, endurance, and perceived wellbeing; product concentration, tolerance, and anxiety or cardiovascular responses limit universalization.
- [[CaffeineBehavioralReinforcement]] treats caffeine as a learned-preference tool: its effects can become associated with a drink, food, container, person, place, exercise session, or other context even when caffeine is not consciously identified by taste.
- [[DopamineToolTiming]] gains a caffeine-specific warning: repeatedly stacking caffeine with other strong reward or arousal inputs may make motivation more peak-dependent and can be followed by a lower subjective state.
- [[PostLearningArousalTagging]] is extended by the claim that caffeine taken after learning can raise catecholamine-related arousal and improve later memory for recently studied material.
- Yerba mate, GLP-1, theanine, hydration, electrolytes, strategic abstinence, every-other-day use, neuroprotection, mood, hormones, headache, asthma, and ADHD-related claims remain source-scoped rather than individualized protocols.
