## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[StephanieHughes]] interview [[TonyPippa]] of the [[BrookingsInstitution]] about the political backlash against U.S. data-center development. The episode frames data centers as AI-era infrastructure whose growth is running faster than many communities' planning capacity, making electricity, water, land use, jobs, safety, and local bargaining power politically salient.

The source's main contribution is to connect [[DataCenterBacklash]] to [[DataCenterCommunityConsent]]. It argues that bans, moratoriums, elections, and local deals are becoming part of AI infrastructure governance because residents and local leaders may not feel they have enough control over projects proposed by large technology companies.

## Key Claims
- The episode says [[Maine]] passed what it describes as the country's first statewide data-center ban, pausing new construction until late 2027.
- It cites the [[NationalConferenceOfStateLegislatures]] as saying 13 other states were considering data-center development bans.
- [[TonyPippa]] says speed is a major factor in the backlash as data centers move into suburbs, peri-urban areas, and increasingly rural places.
- Pippa argues that communities often face large, well-resourced companies before they have planned for data centers or discussed the long-term future they want.
- The episode frames potential jobs as part of the community tradeoff, but says residents and local leaders may not feel they can secure durable long-term benefits.
- Pippa notes uncertainty about whether data centers built for today's technology will still be needed decades from now.
- The episode cites an Indianapolis case in which shots were reportedly fired at a city council member's home after support for data-center construction, with a note opposing data centers.
- Pippa says the industry appears to have been surprised by local pushback and is learning to engage more constructively and openly.
- Industry responses discussed in the episode include attention to energy costs, water resources, and cooling technology such as closed-loop systems.
- The source argues that data centers may affect local and statewide elections, including the Virginia governor's race as an example.
- Pippa says AI infrastructure politics will grow as AI affects jobs, industries, infrastructure, towns, and ways of life.
- The closing segment asks listeners near existing or proposed data centers to share their views and ends with a [[HowWeSurvive]] promo hosted by [[AmyScott]].

## Key Quotes
> "no data centers" - the note reportedly left after shots were fired at a council member's home.

> "at the table" - Pippa's phrase for the kind of community position needed to negotiate long-term benefits.

> "AI as a technology issue will also be a political issue" - Pippa's broader conclusion.
