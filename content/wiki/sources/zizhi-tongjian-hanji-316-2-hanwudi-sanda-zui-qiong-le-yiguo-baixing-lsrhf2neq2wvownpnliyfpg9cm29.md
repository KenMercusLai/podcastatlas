# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》316-2｜汉武帝三大罪，穷了一国百姓

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s fiscal crisis as the combined result of prolonged war, disaster relief, palace construction, and costly immortality seeking rather than one isolated expense. It follows the pressure from the exhaustion of Wen-Jing-era reserves through rank sale, flood relief, loans from wealthy households, mass resettlement, household taxes, and military service.

The durable synthesis is [[ImperialAchievementCostAccounting|帝王功业代价核算]]. The episode recognizes the strategic value of the anti-[[Xiongnu|匈奴]] wars and the opening of the Hexi corridor while insisting that territorial gain be judged beside treasury depletion and burdens shifted onto ordinary households. Its exact chronology, campaign count, tax terminology, migrant total, territorial area, and causal explanation for the post-Mobei pause remain source-scoped because the supplied file is a compact structured summary rather than a transcript or primary-source study.

## Key Claims

- The episode dates severe fiscal pressure to prolonged war by roughly 123 BCE and says the court sold military rank to replenish the treasury.
- Flooding in the Shandong region forced grain relief, encouraged lending by wealthy households, and was followed by the reported resettlement of more than seven hundred thousand people west of Hangu Pass and south of Shuofang.
- Palace construction, fangshi patronage, and immortality projects are presented as additional nonmilitary drains on the same fiscal base.
- The source treats the lack of money as an important reason Han did not immediately continue large anti-Xiongnu offensives after the 119 BCE Mobei campaign.
- Light nominal land-tax rates did not exhaust household obligations: the episode also names person-based levies and compulsory military service, with poorer households having less ability to avoid or absorb them.
- Fiscal reform is presented as a response to the contrast between an indebted state, burdened commoners, and wealthy elites who could better protect their assets.
- The episode credits the Hexi advance with weakening Xiongnu, opening a route west, and reducing pressure on several commanderies, while still counting the wars' human and fiscal costs.

## Key Quotes

> “三大罪，穷了一国百姓” - the title's moral frame for joining war, court consumption, and popular burden.

> “十五税一” and “三十税一” - the nominal land-tax formulas contrasted with additional household obligations.
