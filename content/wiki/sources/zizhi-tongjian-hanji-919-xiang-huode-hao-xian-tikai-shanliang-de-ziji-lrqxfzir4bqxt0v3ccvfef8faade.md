## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the late-Han close of Hanji 49 before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-920-mingwu-zhege-daoli-caineng-zhuan-daqian-lqmabg3bpyz6ct2v4arx-iun57ws|Hanji 920]]. It opens with a self-governance frame about [[BoundarylessKindnessTrap|boundaryless kindness]], then moves through [[EmperorLingOfHan|Emperor Ling of Han]]'s garden project, [[YangCiLateHan|Yang Ci]]'s remonstrance, Mencian "shared pleasure" rhetoric, and [[YangXuanLateHan|Yang Xuan]]'s suppression of Cangwu-Guiyang bandits. The episode links personal boundary failure, courtly flattery, local disorder, and captured reporting as parallel forms of late-Han institutional decay.

## Key Claims
- Kindness becomes self-harm when it has no boundary, no reciprocal protection, and no claim on value; the episode frames this as the [[BoundarylessKindnessTrap|boundaryless kindness trap]], not as a rejection of kindness itself.
- [[YangCiLateHan|Yang Ci]] opposes [[EmperorLingOfHan|Emperor Ling]]'s plan to build new imperial gardens because it converts fertile suburban land into private pleasure space and displaces ordinary households.
- The attendants' appeal to [[ZhouWenwang|King Wen of Zhou]] and [[QiXuanWang|King Xuan of Qi]] shows [[ClassicCitationLegitimationCapture|classic citation legitimation capture]]: a learned citation can strip a precedent of its public-use condition and make ruler desire sound principled.
- The Mencian contrast between a public garden and an exclusionary hunting park becomes a [[SharedPleasureGovernanceTest|shared pleasure governance test]]: the political question is not size, but whether common people can use and benefit from the space.
- [[YangXuanLateHan|Yang Xuan]]'s lime, fire-horse, and crossbow-wagon tactic shows [[BattlefieldInformationControl|battlefield information control]] in local pacification, while [[ZhaoKaiLateHan|Zhao Kai]]'s false accusation shows that tactical success still depends on court reporting channels.
- The episode's closing synthesis treats the late Eastern Han as a convergence of human-caused disorder, natural disasters, epidemics, and demographic collapse, making ordinary survival itself precarious.
