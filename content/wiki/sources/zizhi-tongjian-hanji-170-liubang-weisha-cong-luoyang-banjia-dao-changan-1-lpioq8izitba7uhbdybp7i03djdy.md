# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》170｜刘邦为啥从洛阳搬家到长安（1）

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode follows [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-169-pandian-liubang-dengjiqian-buweirenzhi-de-gushi-2-lqq8qhramfzii5o5icesd-uagwkc|Hanji 169 part 2]] into [[LiuBang|刘邦]]'s first post-unification political cleanup, even though the title previews the later capital-move question. It centers [[TianHeng|田横]]'s island exile, forced summons, suicide, and retainer deaths, then turns to [[JiBu|季布]]'s pardon after [[ZhuJiaLu|朱家]] persuades [[XiahouYing|夏侯婴]] to reframe former enemy service as loyalty. Its core synthesis is that founding order requires more than rewards and rites: Liu Bang must neutralize unreconciled losers, manage the moral afterlife of defeated elites, and decide which former enemies can be absorbed as public examples.

## Key Claims
- Tian Heng flees to an island with more than five hundred followers after [[PengYue|彭越]] becomes Liang king, because he fears Liu Bang and remains a potential [[QiState|齐国]] resistance center.
- Liu Bang sends envoys with pardon, title promises, and military threats, while ordering [[LiShang|郦商]] not to harm Tian Heng despite Tian Heng's role in killing Li Shang's elder brother [[LiShiqi|郦食其]].
- Tian Heng travels toward [[Luoyi|洛阳 / 洛邑]] but kills himself at Shixiang, explaining that he cannot endure the humiliation of becoming a subject after once being equal in royal status to Liu Bang.
- Liu Bang weeps over Tian Heng, praises the Tian brothers' rise and talent, appoints Tian Heng's two attendants as commandants, and buries Tian Heng with a kingly ceremony.
- Tian Heng's two attendants kill themselves beside his tomb, and the five hundred followers on the island also kill themselves after hearing of his death.
- The episode links Tian Heng's followers to [[XuBeihong|徐悲鸿]]'s painting "Tian Heng and His Five Hundred Retainers," reading the story as a later emblem of dignity and national resolve under Japanese pressure in the 1930s.
- Ji Bu, a former [[XiangYu|项羽]] general who had repeatedly endangered or humiliated Liu Bang, is first placed under severe bounty and collective-punishment threat.
- Zhu Jia hides Ji Bu in slave disguise and argues through Xiahou Ying that loyal service to one's former lord should not become a capital crime after unification.
- Liu Bang accepts the argument, pardons Ji Bu, and appoints him langzhong, converting a former enemy's battlefield loyalty into a usable founding virtue.
- The available transcript-summary does not yet develop the promised Luoyang-to-Chang'an explanation; it supplies political background for that question instead.
