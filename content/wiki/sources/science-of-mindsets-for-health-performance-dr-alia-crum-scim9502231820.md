# Science of Mindsets for Health & Performance | Dr. Alia Crum

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[AliCrum|Alia Crum]] about mindsets as domain-specific assumptions that organize expectations, explanations, goals, behavior, and some physiological responses. Milkshake, hotel-housekeeper, workplace-stress, Navy SEAL, sleep-feedback, and allergy-treatment examples connect [[MindsetPhysiologyEffects]], [[ThreatChallengeStressReappraisal]], and [[PlaceboNoceboExpectationEffects]] without treating belief as a substitute for food, activity, medication, safety, or material resources. The practical frame is to make a mindset visible, ask what effects it has, and cultivate a more useful interpretation when the evidence and situation make that credible.

## Key Claims
- A mindset is a default belief or assumption about a domain; it directs attention, expectation, explanation, motivation, and action rather than functioning as a generic positive slogan.
- [[MindsetPhysiologyEffects]] is illustrated by the episode's report that identical milkshakes produced different ghrelin responses under indulgent versus sensible labels and that hotel workers showed health changes after learning their labor met exercise guidelines.
- [[ThreatChallengeStressReappraisal]] does not claim that adversity is good; it asks whether stress-related arousal can be acknowledged, connected to what matters, and used toward a viable goal when real resources are available.
- Stress-enhancing information is associated in the episode with fewer reported symptoms and better performance among UBS employees, while a stress-enhancing mindset predicted training and peer outcomes among Navy SEAL recruits.
- [[PlaceboNoceboExpectationEffects]] can interact with active treatment: framing allergy-treatment symptoms as signs of adaptation reportedly reduced anxiety and symptoms at high doses and improved tolerance-related immune markers.
- Negative health messaging and tracker feedback can create harmful expectations, so communication should inform people without making insufficiency, impairment, or side effects feel inevitable.
- Mindset and objective inputs are joint causes; nutrition, exercise, sleep, medication, adversity, and social context remain consequential even when interpretation changes the response.
