# EP93-没有哪个孩子天生就是“熊孩子”-聊聊中美的早期教育(上)

## Summary

This [[WushichaYanjiusuo|无时差研究所]] episode has [[RebeccaEarlyChildhoodEducator|Rebecca]] use welfare-work and preschool examples to argue that difficult early-childhood behavior should be interpreted through development, emotion, environment, and available communication rather than a fixed “naughty child” identity. Its practical center joins [[PositiveGuidanceInEarlyChildhood|幼儿正向引导]] to emotion naming, separation reassurance, stable limits, and [[FamilyEducationAlignment|coordination across home, school, parents, and grandparents]].

The episode also treats preschool as a professional learning and social environment rather than custodial care. [[ResponsiveEarlyChildhoodCurriculum|回应式幼儿课程]] follows children's interest while integrating language, cognition, emotion, social participation, and motor development; early professional assessment and support can improve participation when developmental concerns persist, but the episode's individual [[AutismEarlyIntervention|early-intervention]] case does not establish a universal outcome.

## Key Claims

- Emotions are not moral failures: adults can name sadness, anger, worry, or happiness, investigate their cause, and help a child find a workable regulation strategy.
- Separation distress can be met with recognition and a concrete account of when the caregiver will return rather than a demand to stop crying immediately.
- Stable rules matter across settings, but school and home have different relationships and constraints, so alignment means legible expectations and communication rather than identical methods.
- Parents, grandparents, and teachers should explain the reasons behind a boundary, understand one another's motives, and avoid teaching that crying or escalation reliably cancels the rule.
- [[ResponsiveEarlyChildhoodCurriculum]] uses children's reactions to decide whether a theme should continue, then connects books, songs, objects, and activity across developmental domains.
- Preschool teaching includes socialization, communication, rule learning, observation, and curriculum design, not only feeding, diapering, and physical safety.
- The source's individual autism and preschool-adjustment cases suggest that early professional and educational support can coincide with substantial change, while remaining anecdotal and context-specific.
