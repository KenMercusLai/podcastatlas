# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》200｜历史上第一次通货膨胀

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places [[LuZhi|吕雉]]'s Gao Hou year-two eight-zhu coin reform against a reported field of light pod coins, merchant hoarding, and extreme grain and horse prices. It interprets the heavier issue and restrictions on moving gold, copper, and iron out of [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]] as an attempt to stabilize coin value and prices while concentrating strategic material under the regency government.

The episode also follows the deposition and killing of [[LiuGongYoungEmperorWesternHan|前少帝刘恭]], the accession of [[LiuHongYoungEmperorWesternHan|少帝刘弘]], and the rupture with [[ZhaoTuo|赵佗]] after restrictions on iron goods reaching [[Nanyue|南越]]. Its final causal claim is deliberately bounded: Zhao Tuo may have read a wider metal-control policy as a targeted hostile embargo encouraged by [[WuChenChangshaKing|长沙王吴臣]], but the supplied document does not independently establish the policy's sole purpose, implementation, or Zhao Tuo's private information.

## Key Claims

- In Gao Hou year two, the court reportedly orders circulation of an eight-zhu coin after light pod coins and unstable weights had weakened monetary confidence.
- The episode calls the reported price surge “inflation,” citing grain at ten thousand cash per shi and a horse at one hundred jin of gold, but supplies no price series or independent measurement.
- It argues that economic recovery made a heavier standard more feasible and that Lu Zhi also sought prestige and stronger central control through the reform.
- The eight-zhu issue is said to have increased coin weight relative to pod money and to have helped calm prices, although neither magnitude nor duration is demonstrated here.
- The episode links the reform to a “Metal Pass Statute” in the [[ErNianLuLing|《二年律令》]] restricting gold, copper, and iron from leaving Guanzhong.
- Liu Gong is reportedly confined, deposed, and killed after learning that [[ZhangYanEmpressWesternHan|张嫣]] was not his biological mother and threatening revenge for his birth mother's death.
- Liu Hong succeeds from Hengshan without a new reign era while Lu Zhi continues to govern, illustrating the gap between nominal accession and effective authority.
- Zhao Tuo interprets restrictions on iron goods as anti-Nanyue pressure and suspects Wu Chen, while the host instead reads them as a broader coinage-and-regime policy.

## Key Quotes

The supplied document is a structured summary rather than a verbatim transcript, so no sentence is promoted as an exact quotation. “历史上第一次通货膨胀” is retained as the episode's title claim, not adopted as a demonstrated global historical priority.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The source provides the initial eight-zhu stage for Hanji 201's account of the reform's later failure and replacement.
- The episode calls this the first inflation in history, but the supplied document neither compares earlier societies nor supplies enough monetary and price evidence to establish that priority claim.
- The reported coin standards are internally simplified: the episode describes Qin money, pod money, “five-part money,” and eight-zhu money without resolving denomination, statutory weight, actual specimen weight, metal content, or regional variation.
- The claim that heavier money significantly reduced prices is source-scoped; no duration, counterfactual, market coverage, or alternative causal explanation is supplied.
- The “Metal Pass Statute,” its date, wording, geographic scope, and relationship to the eight-zhu reform require direct comparison with the excavated text.
- The metal restriction may have served coinage, military supply, frontier control, or several aims. The source's claim that it was not specifically aimed at Nanyue does not establish a single exclusive policy purpose.
- Liu Gong's age, birth mother, words, illness declaration, confinement, deposition procedure, and death remain source-attributed, as do Liu Hong's exact status and the claim that he was denied a reign-era change.
- The claim that Lü Xu was history's first female marquis is not adopted; the existing wiki evidence supports only that her title was unusual, not unique.
