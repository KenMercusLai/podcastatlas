# 687. The Odyssey: Hero of the Trojan Horse (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode retells the first half of [[TheOdyssey]] chronologically, beginning with Troy's fall and [[Odysseus]]' role in the [[TrojanHorse]], then following the voyage through Ismarus, the [[LotusEaters]], [[Polyphemus]], [[Aeolus]], the [[Laestrygonians]], [[Circe]], the underworld, the [[Sirens]], [[ScyllaAndCharybdis]], Helios' cattle, and finally [[Calypso]]'s island. Its strongest wiki contribution is to make [[HomericNostos]] the organizing pressure behind the adventure sequence: every stop tests whether Odysseus and his crew can keep wanting home.

The episode also turns the wanderings into a pattern of failed discipline and boundary testing. Odysseus is brave and cunning, but his raid at Ismarus, his boasting to Polyphemus, and his inability to command trust around Aeolus' bag and Helios' cattle show why [[OdysseanLeadershipTrustFailure]] belongs beside his intelligence. The hosts frame the monsters and islands through [[HomericFantasyGeography]], where mythic places also express Greek fears about seafaring, strange coasts, hospitality, colonization, and peoples outside Greek agricultural and social norms.

## Key Claims

- [[TheOdyssey]] is treated as formally sophisticated rather than simple adventure: the hosts choose a chronological retelling while noting Homer's in medias res structure and layered narration.
- [[HomericNostos]] is the central pressure: lotus pleasure, Circe's delay, Calypso's confinement, hunger, pride, and fear all threaten the desire to return to [[Ithaca]], [[Penelope]], and [[Telemachus]].
- [[Odysseus]] is both tactical genius and danger source: the Trojan Horse, the "No One" trick, and the sheep escape show intelligence, while the Ismarus raid and Polyphemus boast generate losses and curse.
- [[Polyphemus]] makes [[HomericHospitalityTest]] grotesque because the stranger is not welcomed but trapped, killed, and eaten.
- [[HomericFantasyGeography]] keeps the episode from treating monsters as only fantasy; the hosts connect the Cyclops, Sicily, elephant-skull speculation, and western Mediterranean travel to Greek colonial anxiety.
- [[Circe]]'s island turns the voyage from hostile-coast danger toward magic, erotic delay, divine help, and the underworld threshold.
- [[Tiresias]]' warning and [[Helios]]' cattle make divine instruction explicit, but hunger and poor trust still lead the crew into catastrophe.
- [[ScyllaAndCharybdis]] force Odysseus into calculated loss: survival requires choosing the path that sacrifices six men rather than the whole ship.
- [[EpicHeroHumanLimits]] deepens in the underworld when [[Achilles]] says life as the poorest servant would be preferable to ruling among the dead.
- The episode ends with all ships and crew lost, leaving Odysseus alive but suspended with [[Calypso]], which sets up episode 688's homecoming and recognition sequence.
