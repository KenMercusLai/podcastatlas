# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》780丨最多灾多难的时代：东汉

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the disaster, frontier, and institutional diagnosis immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-781-yushan-cai-shi-ren-dui-ji-zui-da-de-e-luvaw93hnnxcbgfnwgsjb7x0hlqz|Hanji 781]]. It frames [[EmpressDowagerDengLateHan|和熹邓皇后]]'s opening political environment through repeated floods, droughts, earthquakes, epidemics, Qiang unrest, fiscal exhaustion, and the court's decision to give up the Western Regions despite [[BanChaoEasternHan|班超]]'s earlier achievement.

The durable synthesis is that the Deng-regency crisis is not only "natural disaster." Submitted Qiang groups revolt after local official abuse and frightening western-service conscription, while [[ZhongchangTongLateHan|仲长统]]'s `Changyan` diagnosis explains how the Three Excellencies became blame-bearing offices after real authority shifted to [[ShangshuTaiImperialAccess|尚书台]] channels, outer relatives, favored attendants, and eunuchs.

## Key Claims

- Eastern Han is framed as unusually disaster-heavy: the episode cites repeated floods, droughts, earthquakes, epidemics, solar eclipses, locust/drought events, and other anomalies across the dynasty.
- Ban Chao maintained the Western Regions for decades with limited direct troops, local balancing, and garrison farming, but the Deng court later abandoned the region because distance, expedition cost, fiscal stress, and court instability made support hard to sustain.
- Submitted Qiang groups had long been settled among Han commanderies and counties, but local official oppression and poor handling deepened resentment.
- A court levy sending Qiang cavalry westward triggers fear that the conscripts will not return; many flee at Jiuquan, local interception and destruction of homes widen panic, and Ma Nu's group also leaves the frontier.
- The Qiang rebels' improvised weapons show weakness rather than organized strength, making the revolt look like a fear-and-pressure response more than a prepared military threat.
- The court answers through amnesty and blame shifting: it pardons Qiang conspirators, then removes Taiwei Xu Fang and Sikong Yin Qin under the combined pressure of disaster, omens, rebellion, and flooding.
- Zhongchang Tong's `Changyan`, quoted by Sima Guang, diagnoses a structural power-responsibility split: Guangwu's post-Western-Han correction left the Three Excellencies with rank but little real control, while Shangshu, outer-relative, favorite, and eunuch channels held power without matching accountability.
- Empress Dowager Deng's austerity orders cut Yellow Gate musicians, redirect slots to Yulin guards, halve feed for nonessential palace horses, and pause nonessential construction, but rising power concentration also prompts Zhou Zhang's direct remonstrance.
