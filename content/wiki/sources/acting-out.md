# Acting Out

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode places the missing adolescent years in [[EllaFitzgerald|Ella Fitzgerald's]] public biography inside the [[NewYorkStateTrainingSchoolForGirls|New York State Training School for Girls]], where Black residents faced segregation, forced laundry work, exclusion, and reported physical abuse. Fitzgerald entered the Hudson institution in April 1933, shortly before the Apollo Theater performance that began her singing career; the surviving record does not establish exactly how she left.

At the same institution, psychiatrist [[JacobLevyMoreno|J. L. Moreno]] and researcher [[HelenHallJennings|Helen Hall Jennings]] asked roughly 500 residents whom they wanted and did not want to live with, observed work groups, and rendered the results as sociograms. Their [[Sociometry|sociometric]] study helped establish social network analysis and supported an early [[BoundedSocialContagion|network-contagion]] account of a cluster of escapes, but its abstraction largely omitted the coercion and racism that could also make running away intelligible.

## Key Claims

- [[EllaFitzgerald]] was confined at the Hudson training school after her mother's death and before her Apollo Theater breakthrough, a period she routinely omitted from her public rise-to-fame story.
- [[NewYorkStateTrainingSchoolForGirls]] combined Progressive Era reform ambitions with prison-like confinement, racial segregation, forced labor, punishment, and abuse.
- [[JacobLevyMoreno]] and [[HelenHallJennings]] used preference questionnaires, observations, and sociograms to map attraction, rejection, isolation, and cottage-level relationships across the institution.
- The researchers interpreted 14 escapes over 14 days in 1932 as an impulse spreading through a social network and reported that relationally informed cottage reorganization sharply reduced later runaways.
- [[BoundedSocialContagion]] treats those patterns as evidence worth investigating, not proof that interpersonal transmission was the principal cause of escape.
- [[MeasurementWithoutInstitutionalContext]] captures the episode's central criticism: a model can reveal relational structure while screening out segregation, forced labor, violence, and institutional power.
- A coded Black resident named “GA” may have been Fitzgerald, but the episode does not establish that identification.

## Connections

- [[EllaFitzgerald]] - resident whose concealed confinement complicates the conventional Apollo breakthrough story.
- [[JacobLevyMoreno]] and [[HelenHallJennings]] - collaborators who converted relationship preferences and observations into sociograms.
- [[NewYorkStateTrainingSchoolForGirls]] - institutional setting that made whole-community mapping possible while subjecting residents to coercive conditions.
- [[Sociometry]] - method for measuring and visualizing attraction, rejection, and group structure.
- [[BoundedSocialContagion]] - qualified interpretation of the clustered escapes and their network pathways.
- [[MeasurementWithoutInstitutionalContext]] - blind spot created when relational representation omits the institution's racial and punitive structure.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The episode does not reject the observed network patterns; it disputes treating them as a sufficient account of why residents escaped.
- The reported decline after cottage reorganization comes principally from Moreno and Jennings's own account and does not isolate reassignment from punishment, policy changes, seasonality, reporting, or other institutional changes.
- Fitzgerald's precise cottage, participation in the study, identity as “GA,” experience of a reported basement beating, and manner of departure remain uncertain or attributed rather than established by the supplied summary.
