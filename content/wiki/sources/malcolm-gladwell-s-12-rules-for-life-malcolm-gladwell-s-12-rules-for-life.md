# Malcolm Gladwell's 12 Rules for Life

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode turns [[CliffAsness]] and [[AaronBrownFinance|Aaron Brown's]] hockey model into the [[PullTheGoalieDecisionRule]]: optimize the chance of recovering a losing position rather than the appearance of keeping defeat respectable. A coach may rationally add an attacker much earlier than convention permits, yet avoid doing so because a visibly lopsided loss threatens reputation, employment, and audience approval.

[[MalcolmGladwell]] extends the analogy to crisis response, a fictional home invasion, and duty-to-retreat law, then narrows the lesson through [[SituationalDisagreeableness]]. Independence from approval is useful when convention blocks the outcome that matters, but it is not a permanent virtue or a license for callousness; Asness's harmless answer to his aunt's currency question illustrates choosing kindness when analytical correction has no meaningful benefit.

## Key Claims

- Asness and Brown's episode-reported model recommends pulling a hockey goalie with 5:40 remaining when trailing by one and 11:40 when trailing by two, much earlier than customary practice.
- The model maximizes the probability of recovering the game rather than preserving a close score, so its recommended strategy can produce a more humiliating visible loss.
- Coaches can therefore make mathematically inferior choices that remain understandable under personal incentives, audience expectations, player reaction, and conventional ideas of competence.
- [[PullTheGoalieDecisionRule]] generalizes the structure as choosing the action that improves recovery odds even when it increases visible downside or social condemnation.
- [[SituationalDisagreeableness]] separates context-specific independence from approval from a fixed identity of being abrasive, cold, or reflexively contrarian.
- [[SamHarris]] applies the analogy to a fictional home invasion by arguing for escape and outside help, but the episode does not establish universal real-world safety guidance.
- The episode links the duty to retreat with permission to choose survival over honor and reports higher homicide after stand-your-ground adoption, while leaving the causal estimate and proposed mechanism source-scoped.

## Key Quotes

> “Pull the goalie.” - Gladwell's rule for choosing recovery probability over respectable-looking defeat.

> “Disagreeableness is a choice.” - the episode's final qualification of its argument for independent judgment.
