# 025 闲话《鹿鼎记》之康熙与小宝|千古一帝与市井流氓

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode reads [[LuDingJi|《鹿鼎记》]] as a dual-protagonist novel built around [[KangxiEmperor|康熙]] and [[WeiXiaobao|韦小宝]]. Their childhood wrestling, capture of Oboi, secret work, conflict over Tiandihui, and attempted reunion on Tongchi Island make the relationship simultaneously friendship, sovereign service, informal apprenticeship, entertainment, and proxy action. The hosts argue that genuine affection survives inside this structure without cancelling the emperor's power to command, punish, pardon, or define the terms of return.

The discussion then widens from character to politics and genre. It treats Kangxi's favorable portrayal as qualified by calculation and lethal imperial habits, reads Wei's uncertain ancestry as a challenge to narrow Han-Manchu legitimacy, and describes the novel as anti-wuxia because a morally compromised, martially weak antihero affects history through improvisation, relationships, luck, and delegated capability. The episode values that formal and political breakthrough while keeping its historical claims, authorial motives, comic excess, and genre label open to qualification.

## Key Claims

- Kangxi and Wei function as paired protagonists whose contrast between imperial order and street-level adaptability gives the novel much of its emotional and political range.
- Their early wrestling matters because it creates a rare memory of reciprocal play before sovereign-subject hierarchy fully governs their interaction.
- Capturing Oboi converts intimacy into political trust: Wei shows courage, discretion, practical competence, and an ability to return credit to the emperor rather than threaten him through merit.
- [[SovereignFriendshipHierarchy|Friendship under sovereignty]] remains real but unequal; Kangxi repeatedly spares and seeks Wei, while retaining the authority to test loyalty, conceal violence, and decide what disobedience can be forgiven.
- Wei's service depends on [[ConcreteReciprocityOverAbstractLoyalty|concrete reciprocity]], secrecy, social judgment, and calibrated speech more than on martial or bureaucratic qualification.
- The novel's uncertain account of Wei's ancestry supports [[PeopleCenteredHistoricalJudgment|people-centered judgment]] by weakening a single-bloodline basis for identity and evaluating rule through ordinary welfare rather than ethnicity alone.
- The novel extends [[MatureGenreDeconstruction|mature-genre deconstruction]] by retaining martial arts and Jianghu institutions while denying its protagonist conventional mastery, heroic purity, and an unambiguous genre identity.
- The hosts treat the work's comedy and wish fulfillment as double-edged: they create reading pleasure while exposing a political and social order in which an adaptive rogue can prosper.
