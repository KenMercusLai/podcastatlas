# 28.聊斋志异：不为君王唱赞歌，偏向苍生说鬼话

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode has [[QinZong|秦总]] reread [[PuSongling|蒲松龄]]'s [[LiaozhaiZhiyi|《聊斋志异》]] beyond the familiar labels of ghost story, fox romance, and school-text excerpt. Its central claim is that the collection has strong moral and didactic pressure around filial piety, friendship, mercy, repayment, and retribution, but almost no interest in praising royal loyalty. The episode treats that absence as part of [[StrangeTaleSocialCritique]]: the real horror in many tales is war, official failure, examination absurdity, and human cruelty rather than ghosts.

## Key Claims
- [[LiaozhaiZhiyi|《聊斋志异》]] is presented as a cultural mine of character types, strange plots, folk skills, social details, and historical wounds rather than only a storehouse of horror images.
- [[PuSongling|蒲松龄]] is read as a writer with clear moral concerns, especially around kinship, friendship, good faith, mercy, and plain karmic return, but the episode emphasizes the near-absence of loyalist praise for rulers.
- Stories such as 《野狗》 and 《公孙九娘》 use the background of [[YuQiRebellion|于七之乱]] to make war, corpses, implicated civilians, and unburied grief more frightening than the supernatural premise.
- 《尸变》 shows the collection's detail-based realism: even an implausible zombie incident becomes narratively credible through lodging, witness behavior, door refusal, physical traces, and the problem of explaining deaths to officials.
- 《偷桃》 and 《口技》 show that the book preserves folk performance, magic-trick imagination, sound art, and fraud as much as ghosts or foxes, extending [[ZhiguaiArchiveValue]] into practical street culture.
- Stories about 二青, 九山王, 青凤, 狐梦, 红玉, and 王六郎 make snakes, foxes, ghosts, and other nonhuman beings ethically active rather than merely monstrous; this becomes [[NonhumanMoralAgencyInStrangeTales]].
- The episode contrasts [[JiYun|纪昀]] and [[YueweiCaotangBiji|《阅微草堂笔记》]] with [[PuSongling|蒲松龄]]: both write ghost and fox material, but Ji Yun is framed as more orthodox and notebook-like, while Pu enters emotion, psychology, and unruly fiction more fully.
- The source treats 《聊斋》's examination satire as part of a wider status critique: a bad-smelling essay can still pass, and the successful candidate may reveal the examiner's blindness rather than the text's merit.
- 《狐梦》 adds a reflexive reading loop: a reader inside 《聊斋》 wants fox romance because he has read 《青凤》, and a fox character wants "聊斋先生" to write her so future readers will remember her.
- The episode's closing synthesis is that 《聊斋》 contains horror history, romantic plot patterns, human variety, folk craft, nonhuman morality, and institutional satire in one field.

## Key Quotes
> "不为君王唱赞歌，偏向苍生说鬼话" - the episode's title thesis.

> "真正恐怖的是乱世" - the source's reading of 《野狗》 and related war-shadow stories.

> "恶念需要土壤" - the episode's moral reading of 《九山王》 and the fox's revenge.
