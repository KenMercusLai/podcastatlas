# 从马克·布洛赫到先贤祠：战争、史学与法国的国家记忆

## Summary
This [[HuzuoHuyou|忽左忽右]] episode connects [[MarcBloch|Marc Bloch]]'s work as a medieval historian with his service in both world wars, his French Resistance activity, and his death in 1944. It presents the [[AnnalesSchool|Annales tradition]] as a problem-led expansion of historical subjects and evidence, from ordinary soldiers and collective mentalities to economic, geographic, material, and psychological structures. Bloch's later admission to the [[FrenchPantheon|French Panthéon]] then becomes a case of [[StateCommemorationAndNationalMemory|state commemoration and national memory]] whose recognition, identity framing, and contemporary political use remain contested.

## Key Claims
- Bloch's First World War experience is presented as one source of his attention to rumor, collective psychology, ordinary soldiers, and the plurality of human historical subjects.
- The early Annales movement challenged event-centered political history and exclusive reliance on official archives by organizing research around problems and using evidence from multiple disciplines and everyday life.
- *The Royal Touch* treats a long-lived healing ritual as a historical problem about belief, sacred monarchy, and collective mentality rather than dismissing it as simple fraud.
- *Strange Defeat* reads France's 1940 collapse as political and social failure as well as military defeat, but the episode qualifies Bloch's judgment with French preparations, German operational risk, and the emotions of a recent catastrophe.
- Bloch's scholarship and Resistance activity are presented as continuous commitments: historical inquiry did not require withdrawal from civic action.
- French governments have repeatedly used Resistance commemoration and Panthéon admissions to consolidate national identity and shape presidential images.
- Bloch's admission recognizes a scholar and Resistance martyr while also raising disputes about family consent, Jewish and French identity, public familiarity, and political appropriation.
- The label “Annales school” names a recognizable intellectual and institutional tradition, but its generations and boundaries differ depending on whether one emphasizes method, journal, mentorship, or organizational succession.
