# 外滩大会线下圆桌｜敢把钱包交给AI吗？聊聊Agent交易爆发前夜的信任基建

## Summary
This [[SiliconValley101|硅谷101]] roundtable at the [[BundConference|外滩大会]], hosted by [[HongjunSiliconValley101|红君]], brings together [[HanXinyi|韩新毅]], [[YongLambert]], [[PeteLau|刘作虎]], and [[ZhouJingren|周靖人]] to examine what must exist before consumers will let agents transact. The panel's shared thesis is that model capability and payment protocols are insufficient: [[AgentPaymentInfrastructure]] needs authenticated users and agents, bounded authorization, [[VerifiableIntent|verifiable intent]], secure execution, traceability, recourse, and gradual [[AgentTrustCalibration|trust calibration]]. Early usage is still thin, but merchant demand and reported shopping growth make the trust stack a present design problem rather than a distant hypothetical.

## Key Claims
- Only one panelist reports having completed a purchase through an agent, while OPPO's source-scoped assistant-shopping data reportedly doubled from a small base, indicating early adoption rather than mature scale.
- Agent payment must authenticate the user, establish that the executing agent is trustworthy, preserve the user's actual instruction, and retain evidence for assigning responsibility when the outcome is wrong.
- [[VerifiableIntent]] records the mandate behind a transaction so disputes can distinguish user ambiguity, agent misunderstanding, transmission failure, merchant error, and unauthorized action.
- [[AgentPermissionBoundaries]] and the harness layer must preserve strong constraints, use safe execution environments, and prevent later content from overriding budget or action limits.
- KYC and KYB are no longer enough when machines act: the panel proposes KYA, or “know your agent,” alongside agent capability assessment and [[A2ATransactionNorms|A2A behavior norms]].
- Consumer trust has both capability and risk dimensions. Users need evidence that an agent can perform the task and confidence that autonomy remains bounded, traceable, and remediable when something fails.
- Agent-mediated recommendations still optimize among candidates using location, preferences, timing, and other objectives; hiding intermediate choices increases the importance of explainability, ranking integrity, and recourse.
- Agent-to-agent commerce may require permissioned machine credentials, organizational authority, working capital, and machine-speed settlement rather than simply attaching today's consumer card or wallet account.
- The panel expects commerce distribution to move from attention and time toward intent, creating opportunities for personal assistants, intent-based merchant conversion, and scalable expert services.
