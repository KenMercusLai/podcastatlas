# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》334-1｜身为酷吏的他，如何一步步沦为“夜壶”

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode begins the fall of [[YiZongWesternHan|义纵]] by placing a neglected road inside [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s illness, recovery, and repeated travel to [[GanquanPalace|甘泉宫]]. When the imperial carriage encounters an unrepaired route, Wudi treats the physical neglect not merely as poor maintenance but as evidence that Yi did not expect him to return alive or did not take him seriously.

The host generalizes the episode through [[ExecutionDetailAttitudeAttribution|执行细节的态度归因]]: small operational omissions can become symbolic evidence about respect, loyalty, or managerial control in unequal relationships. The source also uses a Starship failure claim, visiting a partner's parents, and the iceberg metaphor as explanatory analogies, but these examples do not independently verify the historical episode or a universal workplace rule.

## Key Claims

- After a serious illness at Dinghu Palace, Han Wudi accepts a spirit-medium's prediction of recovery, goes to Ganquan Palace after improving, grants a general amnesty, and keeps the medium there.
- The episode says the road to Ganquan Palace within Yi Zong's administrative responsibility is damaged and unrepaired, making the emperor's journey physically uncomfortable.
- Wudi interprets the neglected road as implying that Yi did not expect him to travel it alive again, turning a maintenance failure into a perceived statement about loyalty and respect.
- The host argues that Yi bears some administrative responsibility because he could have anticipated the emperor's repeated Ganquan travel and repaired the route.
- The alternative suggestion that Yi's enemies deliberately arranged the route is presented as speculation, not an established plot.
- The episode treats words, expressions, preparation, and action as signals from which people infer hidden attitudes, especially where a superior controls consequences.
