# How to Shape Your Identity & Goals | Dr. Maya Shankar

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] interview [[MayaShankar]] about identity after loss, uncertainty, feedback, empathy, and goal pursuit. Its central synthesis is [[PurposeAnchoredIdentity]]: roles can disappear, but underlying motives such as curiosity, connection, and improvement can carry into new domains. The episode then turns that identity flexibility into behavior design through approach framing, meaningful choice, realistic planning states, slack, fresh starts, shorter goal periods, temptation bundling, and [[PeakEndTaskMemory]].

## Key Claims
- [[PurposeAnchoredIdentity]] reduces the fragility of defining the self only by a role, occupation, skill, or fixed trait.
- Curiosity can be a lower-pressure signal for exploring a new path after loss even when the new pursuit does not reproduce the old passion.
- [[CognitiveAdvisoryFeedback]] asks trusted others to test a person's interpretation and blind spots rather than offer comfort alone.
- [[GoalPursuitBehaviorDesign]] is strengthened by approach-oriented framing, meaningful agency, state-matched planning, emergency reserves, fresh starts, shorter horizons, and temptation bundling.
- [[MotivationCrowdingOut]] remains a risk when external rewards replace an activity's intrinsic meaning, while bounded rewards can support an otherwise unpleasant task.
- [[EmpathyModeDifferentiation]] separates emotional resonance, perspective-taking, and concern or compassion; understanding another person need not require agreement.
- [[AweEmotion]] involves perceived vastness and a need to update one's mental model, and it may become identity-forming when a person can imagine participating in the inspiring domain.
- [[PeakEndTaskMemory]] predicts that a task's emotional peak and ending disproportionately shape remembered experience and willingness to return.
