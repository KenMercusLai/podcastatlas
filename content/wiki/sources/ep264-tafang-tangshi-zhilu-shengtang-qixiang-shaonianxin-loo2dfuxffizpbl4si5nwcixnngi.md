# EP264 踏访“唐诗之路”：盛唐气象少年心

## Summary
This [[TalkSanlian|Talk三联]] episode with [[GaoYiding|高一丁]], [[AiJiangtao|艾江涛]], [[BoJingSanlianCulture|薄静]], and [[ZhangXingyun|张星云]] discusses a [[SanlianLifeWeekly|三联生活周刊]] cover report on [[TangPoetryRoad|唐诗之路]]. Rather than treating Tang poetry as a set of isolated famous lines, the episode reads it through three spatial systems: [[ChangAnTang|唐代长安]] and [[WangWei|王维]], [[ZhedongTangPoetryRoad|浙东唐诗之路]] and [[MengHaoran|孟浩然]], and western-frontier experience through [[CenShen|岑参]]. Its strongest contribution is methodological: AI tools can help organize routes and materials, but [[HistoricalReportingAIHallucination|历史报道中的AI幻觉]] makes poetry, chronology, archaeological evidence, and site-based reporting more necessary, not less.

## Key Claims
- The episode frames [[TangPoetryRoad|唐诗之路]] as literary geography: poems gain meaning from capital sociality, pilgrimage, waterways, border posts, monasteries, court ritual, and real travel conditions.
- [[ChangAnTang|唐代长安]] is presented as both a cosmopolitan poetry center and a lost city; Tang buildings mostly disappeared after late-Tang warfare, while towers, inscriptions, and poems preserve fragments of public life.
- [[WangWei|王维]] can be read as a Chang'an poet because his career, patronage, court writing, Buddhist-monastery public spaces, and later [[Wangchuan|辋川]] landscape practice all remain tied to the capital region.
- "京城诗" is used to explain how early Tang and high-Tang poetry often grew from courts, princely mansions, banquets, send-offs, exams, official selection, and social exchange before poets developed more personal expression.
- [[Wangchuan|辋川]] shows the limit and value of site-based reading: many relics are gone or inaccessible, but local scholarship can still connect Wang Wei and Pei Di's "辋川二十景" to concrete terrain.
- [[CenShen|岑参]] is separated from poets who only used frontier images from afar because he twice served in Anxi and Beiting, leaving poems that can be checked against western-region places, seasons, offices, and travel routes.
- Frontier poems such as "白雪歌送武判官归京" are read against the real institutional background of Anxi, Beiting, Gao Xianzhi, Feng Changqing, Talas, the An Lushan Rebellion, and repeated send-offs from border posts.
- Archaeological materials around Beiting, Jiaohe, Gaochang, and the Astana tomb documents can turn a poem's scene into dated movement evidence when names, horse-feed accounts, offices, and routes line up.
- [[ZhedongTangPoetryRoad|浙东唐诗之路]] is described as a route from the Qiantang crossing through Shaoxing, Shengzhou, Xinchang, Tiantai, Linhai, and toward Wenzhou, shaped by waterways, mountains, Buddhist/Daoist sites, and Six Dynasties cultural memory.
- [[MengHaoran|孟浩然]]'s Zhejiang travel is important but hard to fully fix; the episode keeps competing scholarly route and date claims source-scoped rather than forcing a clean itinerary.
- Tang literati roaming is not pure leisure. It can mix landscape pleasure, cultural pilgrimage to Wang Xizhi and Xie Lingyun, Daoist recommendation networks, official ambition, reputation building, and the cultivated image of reclusion.
- The episode treats Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek, Gemini, and Qwen as useful but risky assistants in historical-cultural reporting: they can help sort material but may invent books, scholars, classical phrasing, routes, or chronology.

## Key Quotes
> "长安没了，但诗还在" - the emotional frame for reading surviving poems against a materially vanished capital.

> "京城诗" - Stephen Owen's concept used by the episode to explain capital-centered poetic sociality.

> "唐诗之路" - the route concept that moves the episode from single-poet biography into cultural geography.
