# 43 东西方的地震：1556年嘉靖大地震 vs 1755年里斯本大地震

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] episode has [[LuDapeng|陆大鹏]] and [[ZhangDabai|张大白]] compare the [[HuaxianEarthquake1556|1556 Huaxian earthquake]] with the [[LisbonEarthquake1755|1755 Lisbon earthquake]]. Its central synthesis is that seismic energy becomes historical catastrophe through housing, season, urban concentration, fire, tsunami, food, disease, communications, relief priorities, and political institutions rather than magnitude alone.

The comparison also joins disaster response to knowledge production and legitimacy. Ming records preserve local destruction but central reporting and relief arrive slowly within a heaven-human correspondence framework; Pombal's Portugal combines coercive emergency rule and planned reconstruction with a parish questionnaire that turns local observation into systematic evidence. Neither case supports a simple East-versus-West progression: informal mutual aid matters in the Ming case, while Lisbon's administrative speed remains inseparable from force and political consolidation.

## Key Claims

- The [[HuaxianEarthquake1556|Huaxian earthquake]] became exceptionally lethal through winter midnight timing, widespread loess cave-dwelling collapse, prior drought, hunger, exposure, aftershocks, and epidemic disease.
- The reported 830,000 deaths are presented as a cumulative post-disaster count rather than a secure direct-death total; population, registration, and later-mortality assumptions remain disputed.
- Ming local gazetteers, inscriptions, personal accounts, poems, and memorial writing preserve material damage, survivor experience, belief, mourning, and rebuilding as different evidence layers.
- Slow and distorted reporting, ritual response, misdirected early spending, epidemic exposure, and priority for official or ceremonial structures limited central relief, while gentry and wealthy households supplied some local mutual aid.
- The [[LisbonEarthquake1755|Lisbon earthquake]] was a compound earthquake-tsunami-fire disaster amplified by All Saints' Day occupancy and candles, waterfront exposure, dense construction, and multi-day burning.
- [[MarquisDePombal|Pombal]] joined body disposal, medical care, military order, movement controls, and planned reconstruction to coercive centralization; administrative effectiveness does not erase the authoritarian methods.
- A nationwide parish questionnaire turned priests into local information nodes and preserved comparable observations about shaking, water, buildings, deaths, and fire, making [[DisasterKnowledgeInfrastructure]] part of response capacity.
- [[Voltaire]], [[JeanJacquesRousseau|Rousseau]], and [[ImmanuelKant|Kant]] used the Lisbon catastrophe differently: against optimistic theodicy, as a critique of urban concentration, and as an occasion for natural explanation.
