# Vol.270 大食代留在了它的时代

## Summary
This [[ShangyeJiushiZheyang|商业就是这样]] episode uses the closure of [[FoodRepublic|大食代 Food Republic]] in [[BeijingOrientalPlaza|北京东方新天地]] to explain why mall food courts once matched China's commercial-real-estate boom and why that fit has weakened. It traces the model from [[HawkerCentre|Singapore hawker centres]] through [[GuoMingzhong|郭明忠]], [[FoodJunction]], [[BreadTalk]], and the first Shanghai Food Republic, then frames 大食代 as a [[FoodCourtSecondLandlord|food-court second landlord]] that solved early mall foodservice gaps. The core synthesis is that [[CommercialRealEstateFoodserviceGap|early mall foodservice scarcity]] created the opening, while chain restaurants, stronger landlord leasing teams, supermarket meals, platform canteens, and scene-led markets turned the old food-court format from a mall necessity into a shrinking niche.

## Key Claims
- The episode presents the June 15 closure of the Beijing Wangfujing Oriental Plaza Food Republic as a symbolic endpoint because it was described as Beijing's last 大食代.
- [[FoodRepublic|大食代]]'s source-scoped China footprint is described as having peaked above 40 mainland stores and fallen below 20 stores when Hong Kong and Taiwan are included.
- The food-court lineage starts in Singapore's government-managed hawker relocation: shared water, sanitation, seating, utilities, and stalls made small vendors cheaper and cleaner to operate.
- [[GuoMingzhong|郭明忠]] commercialized that public hawker-centre logic through [[FoodJunction]] in 1993 and copied the model to Shanghai in 1997 as 大食代.
- 大食代's early value was not only cheap food; it rented a large awkward mall area, handled stall contracts, fire, hygiene, licences, seating, and cashiering, then gave malls stable rent with less operational complexity.
- The source says 大食代 usually charged stalls a sales commission, often around 20%, supported by unified prepaid-card or cashier systems, plus property, cleaning, promotion, and entry fees.
- The model produced a four-sided fit when malls needed food tenants, small vendors needed mall traffic, consumers wanted cheap indoor meals, and 大食代 could collect the intermediary spread.
- [[ShoppingCenterRestaurantization|Shopping-center restaurantization]] weakened the fit: stronger chain brands and landlord foodservice teams made the second-landlord layer less valuable.
- Chain restaurants such as 老乡鸡, 和府捞面, and 喜家德水饺 could compete with anonymous food-court stalls because central kitchens, procurement scale, and brand recognition improved margins and traffic.
- The episode treats closure risk as landing hardest on small vendors: equipment purchases, deposits, unpaid turnover, and sudden nonrenewal can become losses when the food-court operator exits.
- Supermarket meals, [[PlatformCanteen|platform canteens]], and [[SceneLedFoodMarket|scene-led food markets]] keep the food-court function alive but change the operator, subsidy logic, and experience design.
- The source presents 大食代 Elite and enterprise or education canteen cooperation as experiments, not yet as a proven replacement for the old mall model.
