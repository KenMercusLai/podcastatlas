# 626. Jack The Ripper: The Killer Strikes Again (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the September 30, 1888 “double event,” when [[ElizabethStride|Elizabeth Stride]] and [[CatherineEddowes|Catherine Eddowes]] were killed less than an hour apart in adjacent police jurisdictions. Witnesses, injuries, a bloodied apron fragment, erased graffiti, anatomical inferences, and the killer's rapid escape support several possibilities without proving that Stride had the same killer or that the offender had medical training.

The episode also shows an investigation overwhelmed by jurisdictional division, public pressure, broad searches, hundreds of leads, hoax-prone letters, and a conflict between evidence preservation and fear of anti-Jewish violence. Newspapers, ballads, patrols, rumors, postcards, and the unresolved “From Hell” letter helped create [[JackTheRipperMythFormation]] while the murders exposed [[VictorianWomenPrecarity]] and accelerated demands for more systematic policing.

## Key Claims

- Stride's cut throat without mutilation is consistent with an interrupted attack, but it does not prove that she was killed by the same offender as Eddowes.
- Eddowes was killed and mutilated rapidly in Mitre Square; removal of a kidney and part of the uterus suggests practical bodily familiarity but does not require a doctor.
- Similar peaked-cap descriptions across several witnesses are suggestive, yet darkness, timing, and ordinary clothing make them weak identification evidence.
- The bloodied apron fragment links Eddowes to Goulston Street, but the nearby “Juwes” graffiti may have been unrelated, written by the killer, or intended as misdirection.
- [[CharlesWarren|Sir Charles Warren]] ordered the graffiti erased before daylight because he feared anti-Jewish violence, preserving an explicit tradeoff between evidentiary value and immediate public safety.
- The “Dear Boss” letter and “Saucy Jacky” postcard were probably hoaxes; the kidney sent to [[GeorgeLusk|George Lusk]] with the “From Hell” letter is harder to dismiss but cannot be tied to Eddowes.
- Police searches, experimental bloodhounds, disguises, patrols, and large case files show both institutional effort and overload rather than simple inactivity.
- Songs, special editions, outsider rumors, imperial imagery, vigilante activity, and press criticism turned an unknown killer into a public character before his identity was known.

## Connections

- [[ElizabethStride]] - victim whose uncertain inclusion in the canonical series makes interruption evidence central.
- [[CatherineEddowes]] - victim whose rapid murder, mutilation, apron fragment, and difficult identification anchor the second half of the double event.
- [[JackTheRipper]] - unidentified offender associated with the murders but not proved by the episode to have killed Stride.
- [[CharlesWarren]] - Metropolitan Police commissioner who prioritized prevention of anti-Jewish disorder over preserving the Goulston Street graffiti.
- [[GeorgeLusk]] - vigilance-committee leader who received the “From Hell” letter and human kidney fragment.

## Contradictions

- The episode leans toward Stride as a Ripper victim interrupted before mutilation, but her inclusion remains an inference rather than a settled identification.
- Anatomical targeting can fit a doctor, medical student, butcher, slaughterman, or practiced amateur; it does not establish a profession.
- The Goulston Street graffiti, “Dear Boss” letter, “Saucy Jacky” postcard, “From Hell” letter, and kidney fragment have different provenance problems and should not be treated as one authenticated communication set.
- The vigilance patrols may have deterred the killer or shifted later violence indoors, but timing alone does not establish that causal effect.
