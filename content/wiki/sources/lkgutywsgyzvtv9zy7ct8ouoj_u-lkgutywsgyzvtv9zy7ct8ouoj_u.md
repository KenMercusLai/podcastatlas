# EP294 一款救命药，要等多少年：中国创新药的崛起之路

## Summary
This [[TalkSanlian|Talk三联]] episode explains innovative drugs as products of a long [[InnovationDrugTranslationSystem|translation system]] spanning mechanism discovery, engineering, preclinical work, phased trials, regulation, patents, capital, manufacturing, commercialization, and payment. It uses [[Ivonescimab|伊沃西]] and [[Keytruda|K药]] to connect PD-1/VEGF oncology evidence with multinational patent pressure and Chinese globalization, while keeping efficacy, safety, trial-team competence, population transferability, and affordability as distinct gates. The patient-level conclusion is qualified: domestic development can offer earlier access to experimental treatment, but participation remains risky and broad access still depends on indication approval, reimbursement, and sustainable financing.

## Key Claims
- Innovative drugs now extend beyond chemical tablets and injections to biologics and cell therapies, and unmet need includes survival, symptom control, and quality of life.
- Drug development is a chain rather than a single discovery: engineering, manufacturing, indication choice, dose, endpoints, recruitment, statistics, regulation, patents, and commercialization can each determine whether a promising mechanism reaches patients.
- Phase 1, 2, and 3 trials progressively test safety, dose, efficacy, and confirmatory evidence, but failure remains possible at every stage; portfolio development spreads rather than eliminates that risk.
- Strong Chinese recruitment efficiency comes from large patient pools and concentrated hospitals, yet [[GlobalClinicalTrialCapability|global trials]] remain necessary when population, disease-cause, regulatory, ethical, and market differences limit transferability.
- Patent cliffs increase multinational demand for external pipelines. Licensing overseas rights can finance global development for smaller Chinese firms, while self-globalization requires substantially deeper regulatory, legal, clinical, and commercial capacity.
- [[InnovativeDrugClinicalBenefitThreshold|Efficacy and safety]] remain the first evaluation gate. For patients entering trials, [[ClinicalTrialAdverseEventResponse|the research team's ability to recognize and manage adverse events]] is part of treatment risk, not an administrative detail.
- Public insurance, commercial insurance, company returns, and overseas revenue must be considered together: very high prices exclude patients, while indiscriminate price compression can weaken the funding of later research.
