# Spencer Pratt on Fixing LA: Wildfires, Homelessness, Corruption & the Fight to Take It Back

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] episode presents [[SpencerPratt|Spencer Pratt]]'s [[LosAngeles|Los Angeles]] mayoral campaign as a response to losing his home in the [[PacificPalisades|Pacific Palisades]] fire. Pratt argues that reservoirs, warnings, fire deployment, public safety, permitting, schools, transit, and nonprofit spending all reveal a city-government capacity failure.

The conversation extends the wiki's existing California-governance branch from [[AdamCarolla|Adam Carolla]]'s rebuilding-delay critique into a candidate platform built around enforcement, audits, treatment, dashboards, faster permitting, and independent-production support. Many of the most serious claims are Pratt's allegations or campaign arguments, not independently verified by the transcript.

## Key Claims
- Pratt says the Palisades fire turned him from a politically interested resident into a candidate because he experienced fire response and recovery as personal abandonment.
- He alleges that a nearby reservoir was drained, warnings were inadequate, assets were unavailable, and he did not hear sirens or see emergency vehicles warning residents to evacuate; these claims extend [[EmergencyWarningInfrastructure]] and [[DisasterResponseStateCapacity]].
- Pratt says whistleblowers came to him with claims about earlier fire warnings, after-action reports, and department response; the page treats those as source-scoped claims.
- His homelessness platform frames street disorder primarily as a drug and treatment problem, calling for [[MandatoryTreatmentHomelessnessPolicy]] rather than only housing supply.
- He alleges that nonprofit and contractor systems absorb public or donated money without enough visible help for intended recipients, making [[MunicipalNonprofitAccountability]] a central campaign theme.
- Pratt argues that [[KarenBass|Karen Bass]] is politically weak, that [[NithyaRaman|Nithya Raman]] can split opposition, and that local socialism distracts from basic city services.
- His public-safety plan is [[PublicSafetyFirstUrbanGovernance]]: warn first, then enforce existing laws against public drug use, theft, disorder, animal abuse, and visible street danger.
- He says he would recruit experienced operators, wealthy supporters, law enforcement, and domain experts because he has not run a city himself.
- He proposes [[MunicipalTransparencyDashboard|public dashboards]] for city and school spending, including audits of departments and schools such as [[LosAngelesUnifiedSchoolDistrict|LAUSD]].
- He links Los Angeles recovery to safer streets, faster rebuilding, film production, small-business permitting, and safer [[LosAngelesMetro|Metro]] use.
- He supports [[PermitAutomationForZonedProjects]], including AI or automatic approval when a project already satisfies zoning criteria.

## Key Quotes
> "facts and the truth" - Pratt's description of his debate posture.

> "go after Bass" - Pratt's account of advice he says he received from [[RickCaruso|Rick Caruso]].

> "lock in" - Pratt's phrase for needing two terms to make recovery durable.

> "nightmare" - Pratt's closing label for Los Angeles conditions.
