## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty Carino]] interview [[WillOremus]] of [[TheAtlantic|The Atlantic]] about [[NegativeParallelism]], the "not X, but Y" construction that has become a recognizable AI-writing tell. The discussion connects [[Pangram]]'s frequency comparison, older AI tells such as chatbot disclaimers and "delve," [[Anthropic]]'s [[Claude]] watermarks, and synthetic-data feedback loops to [[AIWritingDetection]], [[AITextWatermarking]], and [[ModelCollapse]].

The source's main contribution is to separate statistical suspicion from authorship proof. Negative parallelism is a real human rhetorical move, including in [[WilliamShakespeare|Shakespeare]]'s [[JuliusCaesarPlay|Julius Caesar]], but repeated model use can make it feel generic, detector-like, and contagious enough that frequent AI users may begin writing more like AI themselves.

## Key Claims
- Some AI writing can be recognized without detector software because chatbots repeat recognizable language patterns.
- [[Anthropic]] is mentioned as having announced watermarks for text output by [[Claude]].
- [[WillOremus]] identifies [[NegativeParallelism]] as a structure that rejects one framing before replacing it with another.
- [[Pangram]] found the construction about three times more often in AI-generated prose than in entirely human writing.
- Earlier AI tells included leftover chatbot disclaimers, the word "delve," and overused em dashes.
- Negative parallelism is not inherently artificial; the episode points to human rhetoric and [[WilliamShakespeare|Shakespeare]] as evidence.
- Oremus presents the next-word-prediction explanation cautiously: starting with "not" may guide a model into a familiar, lower-risk sentence structure.
- AI-generated material and synthetic data can create a feedback loop if models train on earlier model outputs that already overuse the pattern.
- Verbal tics can act like informal watermarks, but they are not foolproof because humans can use the same devices.
- The closing concern is that people who use AI heavily may begin talking and writing more like AI systems.

## Key Quotes
> "negative parallelism" - Oremus's label for the highlighted AI-writing pattern.

> "not just a photograph, it's a moment" - one example used to show the construction.

> "verbal watermark" - the episode's metaphor for recognizable but imperfect AI style tells.
