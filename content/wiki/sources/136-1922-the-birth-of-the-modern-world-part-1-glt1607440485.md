# 136. 1922: The Birth of the Modern World Part 1

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses 1922 as an interpretive hinge between the destruction of the [[FirstWorldWar|First World War]] and a recognizably modern cultural and political order. It joins [[JamesJoyce]], [[TSEliot|T. S. Eliot]], and [[LiteraryModernismPostwarFragmentation|literary modernism]] to the [[WeimarRepublic|Weimar Republic]], [[BenitoMussolini|Mussolini's]] appointment, and the formal creation of the [[SovietUnion|Soviet Union]].

Its central synthesis is not that one calendar year cleanly created modernity, but that cultural fragmentation and political extremism became unusually visible together. Modernist difficulty, inflation, paramilitary violence, fear of Bolshevism, fascist political theatre, famine, and imperial collapse are treated as different expressions of a postwar world whose old institutions no longer appeared secure.

## Key Claims

- The publication of *Ulysses* and *The Waste Land* made 1922 a landmark for English-language modernism, with formal difficulty and fragmentation presented as responses to modern complexity and postwar disorientation.
- Modernism broke apart inherited forms without simply abandoning tradition; Joyce and Eliot depended on dense historical and literary reference even while producing radically new structures.
- The [[WeimarRepublic|Weimar Republic]] survived its revolutionary opening but remained burdened by political violence, betrayal mythology, antisemitic scapegoating, and inflation that damaged middle-class confidence.
- [[BenitoMussolini|Mussolini's]] March on Rome succeeded because King Victor Emmanuel III refused emergency action and invited him to form a government, illustrating how conservative elites could enable authoritarian radicals they expected to control.
- The [[SovietUnion|Soviet Union's]] formal creation and [[JosephStalin|Stalin's]] rise inside the party machine made 1922 important to the later communist-capitalist geopolitical order.
- Fear of exported Bolshevism helped energize fascist and paramilitary responses in Italy, Germany, and British popular culture rather than producing only passive anxiety.
- The glamorous “Roaring Twenties” image obscures a period saturated by war loss, disease, famine, inflation, revolutionary fear, and institutional fragility.

## Key Quotes

> “post scriptum Ulysses” - Ezra Pound's phrase, used by the episode to mark a literary threshold after Joyce's novel.

> “must be difficult” - Eliot's defense, quoted by the hosts, of poetry responding to modern civilization's variety and complexity.

- [[LiteraryModernismPostwarFragmentation]] - connection between experimental form, inherited tradition, and postwar cultural rupture.
- [[PostwarCrisisModernity1922]] - broader synthesis joining cultural innovation to institutional breakdown and ideological reordering.
- [[WeimarRepublic]] - democracy facing inflation, paramilitary violence, assassination, and anti-republican myth.
- [[BenitoMussolini]], [[ItalianFascism]], and [[GabrieleDAnnunzio|Gabriele D'Annunzio]] - fascist rise and its mixture of modern technique, reactionary aims, and political theatre.
- [[SovietUnion]] and [[JosephStalin]] - communist state formation and party-machine consolidation.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found.
- The “birth of the modern world” label is an organizing interpretation, not a literal claim that the developments began or became inevitable in 1922.
