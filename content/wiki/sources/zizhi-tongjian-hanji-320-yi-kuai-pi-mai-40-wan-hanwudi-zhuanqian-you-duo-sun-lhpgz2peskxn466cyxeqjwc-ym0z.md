# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》320｜一块皮卖40万，汉武帝赚钱有多损？

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[WhiteDeerSkinCurrency|白鹿皮币]] as a fiscal device created under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] amid war-driven treasury pressure and concentrated merchant wealth. In the episode's account, a one-square-foot piece of embroidered white-deer skin is assigned a price of forty wan cash, and kings, marquises, and imperial relatives must place court, sacrificial, or tribute gifts on it before the emperor will accept them.

The durable synthesis is that the object works through ritual obligation rather than ordinary monetary circulation. [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] is credited with reusing an older elite gift convention so that access to required ceremonies carries a compulsory purchase price; the episode itself says the skin cannot circulate or be transferred as money. Its further claim that this makes white-deer skin currency a precursor to Chinese paper money remains a limited analogy based on state-assigned nominal value, not proof of full monetary function.

## Key Claims

- The episode places the measure around 119 BCE, when long warfare and government expenditure make additional revenue politically urgent.
- It says wealthy mining, iron, minting, and salt interests hold large fortunes while the court seeks ways to draw more wealth into the treasury.
- A square-foot piece of white-deer skin is edged with multicolored embroidery and priced at forty wan cash.
- Kings, marquises, and imperial relatives must use the skin as the base for gifts presented during audience, sacrifice, gift-giving, or tribute, converting participation in elite ritual into a purchase requirement.
- The episode attributes the design to Zhang Tang and treats it as an adaptation of an older skin-and-textile gift practice described through the `Guanzi` and the older meaning of 币.
- It explicitly distinguishes the device from ordinary currency because it does not circulate or perform normal exchange functions.
- The later paper-money analogy rests only on a lightweight object receiving a very high state-backed nominal price; the episode does not establish convertibility, transferability, general acceptance, or sustained circulation.

## Key Quotes

> “一张白鹿皮币定价四十万钱。” - the episode's central price claim.

> “白鹿皮币不能算实际货币，也没有发挥货币作用。” - the source's own boundary on the paper-money analogy.

## Connections

- [[WhiteDeerSkinCurrency|白鹿皮币]] - ritualized fiscal device whose compulsory ceremonial use, assigned price, and monetary limits form the central case.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] - ruler whose fiscal pressure and control of court ritual make the extraction mechanism possible.
- [[ZhangTangWesternHan|张汤]] - official credited by the episode with adapting an older gift convention into a revenue measure.
- [[YanYiWesternHan|颜异]] - later opponent whose objection to the forty-wan valuation is supplied by Hanji 337 rather than this episode.
- [[StateCommercialMonopolyExtraction|官营商业垄断式汲取]] and [[RewardedDenunciationFiscalEnforcement|告缗式奖励告发财政执法]] - adjacent Han Wudi-era mechanisms aimed more directly at markets, property reporting, and confiscation.
- [[CentralizedWuzhuCoinage|五铢钱中央统一铸造]] - related monetary centralization that should not be conflated with this noncirculating ritual purchase.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was found. Hanji 337 independently supplies Yan Yi's objection that a forty-wan skin was disproportionate to tribute worth only several thousand cash, while this episode supplies the mechanism and the attribution to Zhang Tang.
- Monetary-scope caution: the source explicitly says the object was not ordinary money and could not circulate, so its description as a precursor to paper money is an analogy about assigned nominal value rather than a demonstrated monetary genealogy.
- Historical-scope caution: the exact chronology, legal mandate, revenue, enforcement, number of required buyers, relationship to white-metal coinage, and date of abolition are not established here. The host says the abolition date is unknown.
- Textual-scope caution: the `Xiaowu Benji`, `Hanshu`, `Guanzi`, `Shuowen Jiezi`, and later deer-skin presentation claims are reported through a short popular-history episode and require direct textual comparison before being treated as a complete institutional history.
