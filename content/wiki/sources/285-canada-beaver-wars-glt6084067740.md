# 285: Canada: Beaver Wars

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses the beaver to connect Canadian environmental history with Indigenous subsistence, Atlantic trade, colonial rivalry, imperial mapping, industrial trapping, and conservation. Its central argument is that beavers were both [[BeaverLandscapeEngineering|landscape engineers]] and the basis of a [[BeaverFurTrade|commodity system]] that helped draw European states and companies inland, intensify conflict, and drive a severe population collapse.

The closing account shifts from extraction to recovery through [[GreyOwl|Grey Owl]]'s public campaign, tighter trapping controls, and later reintroduction. That recovery qualifies rather than erases the earlier history: the episode treats wildlife conservation as a response to a human-made ecological crisis while acknowledging that Grey Owl's assumed Indigenous identity complicates his legacy.

## Key Claims

- Beavers reshaped rivers, wetlands, and habitats at continental scale before the commercial fur trade sharply reduced their numbers.
- Indigenous communities used beavers for food, insulation, tools, and ceremony without producing the near-extermination associated with later export demand and industrial trapping.
- European demand for felt hats turned pelts into a medium of exchange and helped connect the St. Lawrence, inland trade routes, and Atlantic markets.
- The [[BeaverWars|Beaver Wars]] joined Indigenous rivalries and commercial strategy to French, Dutch, and English colonial competition.
- The 1670 charter of the [[HudsonsBayCompany|Hudson's Bay Company]] and later British victory in the [[SevenYearsWar|Seven Years' War]] converted fur access into territorial and imperial power.
- [[DavidThompsonExplorer|David Thompson]]'s mapping and [[JohnJacobAstorFurTrader|John Jacob Astor]]'s trading fortune show how knowledge, transport, monopoly, and finance grew around the fur economy.
- [[GreyOwl|Grey Owl]] helped make beaver protection emotionally legible to a mass audience, despite serious qualifications around his identity and earlier work as a trapper.

## Key Quotes

> “the place of the Beaver Dam” - the episode's translation of Hochelaga, the settlement at the future site of Montreal.

> “two races of beings: humans and beavers” - the episode's rendering of David Thompson's description of pre-European Canada.

> “both Indigenous people and strangers poor” - the episode's paraphrase of a Cree warning about the consequences of destroying beaver populations.

- [[BeaverWars]], [[HudsonsBayCompany]], and [[SevenYearsWar]] - conflict, chartered company power, and imperial transfer.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends the existing [[SevenYearsWar]] account from postwar fiscal and constitutional consequences to British control of Canada and its fur trade.
- Population estimates, the exact chronology of human overlap with giant beavers, Indigenous oral-memory interpretations, Astor wealth comparisons, castoreum uses, and modern beaver totals remain source-scoped.
- The episode's broad account of the Beaver Wars compresses distinct Indigenous nations, changing alliances, local causes, and phases of conflict; European trade rivalry is treated as an important driver rather than a complete explanation.
- Grey Owl's conservation importance does not resolve the ethical and historical problems created by Archibald Belaney's assumed Indigenous identity.
