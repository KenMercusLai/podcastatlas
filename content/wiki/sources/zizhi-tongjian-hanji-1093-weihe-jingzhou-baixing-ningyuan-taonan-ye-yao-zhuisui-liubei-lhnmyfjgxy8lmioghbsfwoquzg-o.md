# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1093丨为何荆州百姓宁愿逃难也要追随刘备？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the civilian-retreat and logistics prelude to [[ChangbanBattle|长坂坡之战]]. After [[LiuCongLateHan|刘琮]] surrenders [[Jingzhou|荆州]] to [[CaoCao|曹操]], officials and civilians join [[LiuBei|刘备]] on the southward movement; the host explains their choice through Liu Bei's reputation for humane conduct and fear produced by Cao Cao's earlier violence in [[Xuzhou|徐州]].

The episode then interprets mass followership as [[CivilianFollowershipPoliticalCapital|百姓追随型政治资本]]: the civilians slow the column and increase military exposure, but their visible willingness to share danger also advertises Liu Bei's claim to rule through popular trust. A separate [[GuanYu|关羽]] flotilla carries troops, grain, and other core assets toward [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]], while Cao Cao leaves baggage behind and sends or leads elite cavalry in rapid pursuit, ending where the next episode's Changban battle begins.

## Key Claims
- The source presents Jingzhou civilians' choice as comparative risk management: Liu Bei is associated with humane treatment and non-massacre, while Cao Cao's Xuzhou reputation makes staying under an uncertain conqueror appear dangerous.
- Liu Bei's earlier hospitality toward an assassin and his repeated effort to reassure local people are used as evidence for a durable reputation rather than a one-time gesture.
- The civilian-heavy movement grows to a reported scale of more than one hundred thousand people and thousands of carts, reducing daily progress and making military escape extremely difficult.
- Liu Bei's refusal to abandon followers is framed both as a sincere people-first commitment and as a politically valuable public performance; the episode leaves the sincerity-versus-performance question unresolved.
- Mass civilian followership is treated as a legitimacy signal whose long-term reputational value may exceed the immediate safety benefit of a faster retreat.
- Guan Yu's ships are presented as carrying Liu Bei's accumulated military core and supplies rather than serving primarily as civilian transport.
- Cao Cao prioritizes preventing Liu Bei from reaching Jiangling's stores, abandons heavy baggage, and makes a rapid elite-cavalry pursuit that reaches the column at Changban.
- The opening Maslow, fame, profit, and “造场造势” framework is the host's modern interpretive lens rather than evidence from the historical episode itself.

## Key Quotes
> “夫济大事必以人为本，今人归吾，吾何忍弃去。” — Liu Bei's refusal to abandon the people as rendered by the episode.

> “乱世当中，民心比城池值钱。” — the host's summary of mass followership as political capital.

## Connections

- [[CaoCao|曹操]], [[Xuzhou|徐州]], and [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|民心型政治安全]] - remembered violence and humane reputation as competing civilian safety signals.

- [[GuanYu|关羽]] and [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]] - flotilla, core assets, and the supply objective shaping the retreat.
- [[ChangbanBattle|长坂坡之战]] and [[CaoChunLateHan|曹淳]] - rapid cavalry pursuit and the battle sequence continued in Hanji 1094.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is recorded. The source adds the civilian and logistics prelude to Hanji 1094's battle-and-recovery account.
- The episode explicitly preserves disagreement over whether Liu Bei's conduct reflects humane conviction, political performance, or both; it does not resolve the actor's private motive.
- The reported civilian total, cart count, ship count and capacity, daily pace, pursuit distance, age figures, dialogue, flotilla cargo, and Jiangling supply calculation remain source-scoped pending primary-source verification.
- The transcript's “丹阳” is normalized contextually to Dangyang / 当阳, while “在甲盟” is treated as a likely transcription error for Jiameng / 葭萌.
- The claim that displaced Xuzhou people transmitted fear of Cao Cao in Jingzhou is plausible within the episode's argument but is not independently established here.
