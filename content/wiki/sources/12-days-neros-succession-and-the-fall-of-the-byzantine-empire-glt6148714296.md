# 12 Days: Nero's Succession and the Fall of the Byzantine Empire

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode uses New Year's Day to pair two crises of [[RomanImperialLegitimacy|Roman imperial legitimacy]]. In AD 69, ritual oaths exposed the weakness of [[Galba]]'s rule when the Rhine armies rejected him and elevated [[Vitellius]], opening the decisive military phase of the [[YearOfTheFourEmperors|Year of the Four Emperors]].

The second half follows [[RomanusIV]] from accused conspirator to eastern Roman emperor, then through defeat and capture at the [[BattleOfManzikert|Battle of Manzikert]], civil war, blinding, and death. It treats Manzikert less as an instant fall than as a military setback whose destructive aftermath weakened Byzantine control of Anatolia.

## Key Claims

- January 1 joined civil time, consulship, sacrifice, augury, and loyalty oaths, making New Year ritual a public test of imperial authority.
- Omens later attached to [[Nero]]'s last New Year belong to a retrospective downfall narrative; rebellion, elite defection, military pressure, and suicide ended his reign.
- Galba alienated the Rhine legions by restricting their plunder and failing to turn stern command into durable military allegiance.
- The Upper German mutiny temporarily invoked the Senate and Roman people before commanders selected Vitellius as an imperially credible candidate.
- Galba's assassination and [[Vespasian]]'s eventual victory show that provincial armies could make and unmake emperors faster than formal ritual could stabilize them.
- Romanus IV's marriage to the regent Eudokia transformed a defeated coup suspect into emperor, but Manzikert and the internal challenge during his captivity destroyed his position.
- The episode treats post-Manzikert civil conflict and Turkish movement into Anatolia, rather than the battle alone, as the deeper cause of long-term Byzantine territorial loss.
