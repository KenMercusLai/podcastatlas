# 567. The Great Northern War: Murder in Moscow (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode closes its Peter the Great sequence by joining [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]]'s state-building, scientific curiosity, court grotesquery, and personal violence to the destruction of his son [[TsarevichAlexei|Tsarevich Alexei]]. It presents Saint Petersburg, war-driven administration, industry, taxation, and the Table of Ranks as parts of [[ModernizingAutocracy]]: reforms that raised the [[RussianEmpire|Russian Empire]] to great-power status while strengthening centralized coercion. Alexei's flight, forced return, interrogations, torture, trial, and death then turn a succession conflict into a family tragedy and a state-security purge.

## Key Claims

- Peter's Saint Petersburg court combined rational urban planning, international trade, and scientific curiosity with drunken parody, ritual inversion, physical humiliation, and grotesque spectacle.
- The Great Northern War accelerated factories, metallurgy, canals, taxation, the Senate, government colleges, and the Table of Ranks, but these measures built a service state under autocratic command rather than a liberal or reliably meritocratic order.
- [[PeterTheGreat|Peter]] resembles a modernizing or enlightened despot in his suspicion of organized religion, practical learning, science, and top-down reform, but his violence and fascination with cruelty make the label incomplete.
- [[TsarevichAlexei|Alexei]] was politically dangerous to Peter less because a proven conspiracy existed than because his Orthodox conservatism, love of old Moscow, weakness, and circle of clerical and family supporters made him a possible symbol for reversing reform.
- Alexei's dynastic role became a trap: fear of his father, incompatible temperament, arranged marriage, succession pressure, and the demand to become either a worthy heir or a monk left flight as his remaining perceived exit.
- After Alexei returned under a promise of pardon, Peter used his disclosures and Afrosenia's testimony to widen the case into arrests, torture, exile, mutilation, and execution even though the hosts say no organized conspiracy was found.
- The episode treats Alexei's death as the result of renewed torture involving Peter and senior officials, rejecting the official account of stroke, repentance, and peaceful reconciliation.
- Peter's state survived an unstable sequence of women, children, favorites, and weaker rulers after his death, which the hosts take as evidence that his institutions made autocracy more durable than any one successor.

## Key Quotes

> "leave everything to..." - Peter's unfinished succession instruction shortly before his death.

> "window on the West" - the familiar phrase used for Saint Petersburg, qualified by Weber's impression of a hybrid European-Asian court.
