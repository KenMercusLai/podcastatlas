## Summary
This [[Banlatte|半拿铁]] episode uses [[Jingdezhen|景德镇]] to explain how a city becomes a durable industrial and cultural system rather than only a label attached to famous wares. Its core synthesis is that [[JingdezhenPorcelainProductionSystem]] combined raw materials, river transport, court and overseas orders, specialized labor, tacit firing knowledge, merchants, fuel supply, and later tourism and creative markets. The episode follows the arc from [[QingbaiPorcelain|青白瓷]], [[YuanBlueAndWhitePorcelain|元青花]], and the [[JingdezhenImperialKilnFactory|御窑厂]] to state-owned factories, [[JingpiaoCreativeCeramicEcology|景漂]] culture, livestream commerce risk, and advanced ceramics.

## Key Claims
- [[Jingdezhen|景德镇]] became legible as a "world porcelain capital" because it sustained a production system, not because one dynasty or single masterpiece created the city by itself.
- The city's geography mattered: [[Jiangxi|江西]] location, the Changjiang route into Poyang Lake and the [[YangtzeRiver|Yangtze River]] system, and fragile porcelain transport all made water logistics a competitive advantage.
- The source rejects the popular "China comes from Changnan" etymology as weak, while treating [[Kaolin]] from [[GaolingVillage|高岭村]] as a more reliable example of Jingdezhen entering global technical vocabulary.
- [[QingbaiPorcelain|青白瓷]] helped Jingdezhen rise in the Northern Song, but the later [[PorcelainStoneClayBinaryFormula]] expanded what local kilns could make by reducing high-temperature deformation.
- The 1278 Fuliang porcelain office made [[OfficialSupervisionCivilianKilns|官监民烧]] visible: official orders could shape private kiln production without all capacity becoming a state-owned factory.
- [[YuanBlueAndWhitePorcelain|元青花]] connected local production to overseas demand, especially large vessels and visual styles suited to West Asian and Central Asian markets.
- The [[DavidVases|大维德瓶]] changed modern scholarly understanding by giving researchers a dated 1351 benchmark for identifying Yuan blue-and-white wares.
- Traditional Jingdezhen manufacturing worked through [[DistributedCeramicProduction|distributed ceramic production]], with specialized steps in clay preparation, throwing, painting, firing, packing, selling, fuel, transport, and kiln management.
- The [[JingdezhenImperialKilnFactory|御窑厂]] did not sit outside the local economy; court demand, strict quality control, outsourcing, materials, workers, and techniques kept official and civilian production linked.
- [[TangYing|唐英]] matters because he converted administrative responsibility into close craft learning and documentation, making [[ProcessKnowledge]] visible in Qing imperial porcelain management.
- Late Qing and Republican attempts at [[CeramicIndustrializationTransition|ceramic industrialization]] were repeatedly disrupted by war, institutional weakness, and global competition after Europe built its own hard-paste porcelain capacity.
- The post-1949 [[StateOwnedCeramicFactorySystem|state-owned ceramic factory system]] rebuilt production through large factories, fuel reform, tunnel kilns, national-use porcelain, and technical collectivization before market transition exposed sales and cash-flow weakness.
- After the 1990s state-factory decline, [[JingpiaoCreativeCeramicEcology|景漂]] creators, [[LottePotteryWorkshop|乐天陶社]], markets, old factory spaces, and [[Taoxichuan|陶溪川]] made the city attractive again as a craft-and-design ecosystem.
- The modern revival carries new problems: [[CeramicLivestreamCommerceRisk|ceramic livestream commerce]] can mix real craft with fake certificates, copycat products, inflated master titles, and hard-to-enforce copyright claims.
- [[AdvancedCeramicsIndustrialUpgrade|advanced ceramics]] show that Jingdezhen's future is not only heritage tourism or studio art; the same city also tries to convert ceramic materials and process capacity into higher-value industrial output.

## Key Quotes
> "过手七十二方克成器" - the source uses this older description to explain Jingdezhen's dense division of labor.

> "官监民烧" - shorthand for official supervision over civilian kiln production in the Yuan-era Fuliang system.

> "风火仙师" - the Tong Bin legend's religious name for kiln danger and worker memory.

> "世界瓷都" - the title phrase the episode reinterprets as a production-system claim.
