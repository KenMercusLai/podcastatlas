# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》365-2｜玩的花，揭秘史上最好色的皇帝

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s harem as an institution where large numbers, delegated supervision, favor registers, allowances, pregnancy, childbirth, and titles organized unequal access to the ruler. It also links sexual activity to Wudi's immortality seeking through a reported belief in “采阴补阳,” but the episode does not establish the practice, its efficacy, or its actual role in Wudi's health.

The second half introduces [[LiJuanHanWudiCourt|丽娟]] through stories attributed to 《汉武洞冥记》 and 《琅嬛记》: falling flowers, protection from dust, a sounding amber ornament, and the “卖笑花” exchange of gold for laughter. These are useful as favorite-court literary imagery, not as securely established biography. The closing reflection turns her absence from standard history into a question of how palace women without heirs or office become historically invisible.

## Key Claims

- The episode describes Wudi's harem as a managed hierarchy rather than an undifferentiated crowd: women are grouped under eunuchs or senior women, frequently favored women are separately registered and paid more, and pregnancy or childbirth can alter reward and rank.
- A reported total of 18,000 registered palace women and a 200-woman travel entourage emphasize scale, but the figures come through popular retelling and are not independently verified here.
- Large harem scale makes access scarce even inside the palace, so entry into the harem does not itself mean repeated intimacy, durable favor, wealth, or historical visibility.
- The source connects Wudi's sexual appetite to immortality seeking through “采阴补阳” and the Yellow Emperor's legendary ascent; this is an attributed belief narrative, not medical evidence.
- [[LiJuanHanWudiCourt|丽娟]] is presented as a fourteen-year-old palace favorite whose beauty, lightness, performance, wit, and luxury are amplified through marvel-tale conventions.
- The “卖笑花” story turns intimate wit into a reward exchange: Li Juan asks whether laughter can be bought and uses one hundred jin of gold as the price of making Wudi laugh.
- The episode explicitly notes that Li Juan is absent from standard history and speculates that having no child left her politically and historiographically marginal.
- The modern-women conclusion is commentary and program preview rather than evidence about Western Han palace life.

## Key Quotes

> “笑可买乎？” — the question attributed to Li Juan in the “卖笑花” story.

> “只是汉武帝生命里的过客” — the episode's summary of palace women who left little historical trace.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content was adopted. This episode extends Hanji 365-1's appetite-and-longevity comparison but does not validate sexual restraint or sexual activity as a cause of lifespan.
- Source hierarchy caution: 《汉武故事》, 《汉武洞冥记》, 《琅嬛记》, and the nested 《甲子说林》 material are not equivalent to contemporaneous administrative records. The 18,000-woman count, palace passageways, entourage numbers, titles, allowances, and every Li Juan anecdote remain episode-attributed pending textual and historical verification.
- Medical caution: “采阴补阳,” sexual invulnerability, and any claim that frequency of sex shortened or extended a ruler's life are not adopted as medical fact.
- Biographical caution: Li Juan's age, physical description, palace placement, amber ornament, gold amount, and lack of children are preserved as transmitted story details, not as a complete verified life.
