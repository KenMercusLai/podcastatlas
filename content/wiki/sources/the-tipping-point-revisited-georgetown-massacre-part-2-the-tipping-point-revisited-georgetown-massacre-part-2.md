# The Tipping Point Revisited: Georgetown Massacre Part 2

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode concludes [[USVKhoury|U.S. v. Khoury]], in which [[AymanKhoury]] was prosecuted after paying Georgetown tennis coach Gordie Ernst $180,000 in connection with [[CatherineKhoury]]'s admission. The defense had already exposed overlap between fundraising and admissions, but Catherine's unexpected testimony and 2015 messages supplied a more direct answer to the prosecution's claim that father and daughter planned a false shoulder injury so she could avoid playing tennis.

The jury acquitted Ayman Khoury on every count. [[MalcolmGladwell]] and defense lawyer [[RoyBlack]] present the messages as evidence that Catherine quit because her parents' breakup ruptured her relationship with the father who had taught her the sport, while a withheld [[GeorgetownUniversity]] Salesforce report estimated the family's giving capacity at $1–5 million and sharpened the episode's account of [[AdmissionsFundraisingEntanglement]].

## Key Claims

- Catherine testified that she expected to train with Georgetown's team and redshirt, but stopped playing after her parents' breakup made tennis an emotionally painful reminder of her father.
- Her contemporaneous messages expressed anger and separation from Ayman Khoury, supporting the defense's argument that she was not acting under his control in a planned fake-injury scheme.
- Prosecutors relied on immunized witness Timothy Donovan for the shoulder-injury account but did not call or interview Catherine before trial, according to the episode.
- [[TrialNarrativeReframing]] moved the defense from institutional hypocrisy to a concrete alternative explanation of intent, using Catherine's testimony and messages in closing argument.
- The jury acquitted Ayman Khoury on all counts; that verdict rejects guilt beyond a reasonable doubt but does not erase the admitted $180,000 payment.
- Georgetown tracked family wealth, institutional ties, and donation potential in Salesforce and reportedly assessed the Khoury family at $1–5 million after Catherine's admission.
- The fundraising report was kept from the jury because no witness authenticated it, so its public significance and its trial admissibility are separate questions.
