# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》245｜汉景帝时期，当官为什么要倒贴钱？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode places [[HanJingdi|汉景帝]]'s 后元二年 measures against earthquake, [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] incursion, poor harvests, luxury production, and corrupt local administration. Jingdi reportedly restricts feeding cooked grain to horses, criticizes labor diverted into carving and elaborate textiles, presents imperial farming and sericulture as examples, reduces court consumption and tribute, and makes commandery-level officials answer for predatory subordinates.

Its distinctive contribution is [[AssetQualifiedOfficialEntry|赀选式财产资格入仕]]. A household asset threshold of forty thousand cash is presented as a qualification for selection because officeholding imposed substantial private costs, but wealth alone did not guarantee appointment: registered merchants, dependent sons-in-law, and officials tainted by corruption were excluded, while [[SimaXiangru|司马相如]] and 张释之 are used to argue that talent still mattered. The durable synthesis therefore separates an asset-qualified route from direct office sale while preserving its exclusionary effect on ordinary households.

## Key Claims

- In Jingdi's 后元二年 / 142 BCE, the episode reports three earthquakes in one day, a Xiongnu attack on Yanmen, the death of commandery head Feng Jing in battle, and a poor harvest.
- The court prohibits people in the interior commanderies from feeding cooked grain to horses, with confiscation of the animals as the reported sanction.
- Jingdi criticizes male carving and ornamental work and female elaborate embroidery because they pull labor from farming and ordinary weaving, thereby worsening hunger, cold, and disorder.
- Imperial ploughing, the empress's sericulture, refusal of tribute, and reduced palace food are presented as exemplary restraint intended to support lower burdens and household reserves.
- Jingdi attributes continuing subsistence failure partly to corrupt county and commandery officials and orders two-thousand-shi administrators to correct predation or face reporting and punishment through the chancellor.
- The episode renders the May order as “资算四得官”: households with forty thousand cash in assessed assets could enter a wealth-qualified selection route whose office-related costs ordinary families could not readily bear.
- Wealth was a threshold rather than the only criterion: merchants on the market register, dependent sons-in-law, and officials associated with corruption were excluded, while the episode presents Sima Xiangru and 张释之 as talented entrants.
- The host generalizes that early Western Han high office remained concentrated among founding meritorious families, nobles, and wealthy households because a mature, broadly accessible appointment system had not yet formed.

## Key Quotes

> “资算四得官。” - the episode's rendering of the forty-thousand-cash asset qualification for selection.

> “家里要有钱，但是主要还是靠个人才能。” - the host's distinction between entry qualification and final appointment.

- [[AssetQualifiedOfficialEntry|赀选式财产资格入仕]] - selection mechanism in which sufficient household assets fund eligibility and office costs without by themselves purchasing appointment.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is adopted. The episode extends Hanji 246's final agrarian order backward into a fuller late-reign sequence of scarcity response, court restraint, local accountability, and selection policy.
- The source's “资算四得官” transcription may represent a specialized or differently written historical term. The exact graph, decree wording, assessed-property method, forty-thousand threshold, covered offices, and relationship to other Han appointment routes require primary-text comparison.
- The source distinguishes asset qualification from direct office sale, but does not quantify the actual private costs of service, appointment success rate, regional reach, or how consistently identity and corruption exclusions were enforced.
- Earthquake frequency, battle sequence, official names and titles, confiscation rule, ritual example, tax effect, quoted Han text, the biographies of Sima Xiangru and 张释之, and the claim that early Han high office was broadly monopolized remain episode-attributed.
