## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three systems where headline outcomes hide deeper feedbacks: Earth's falling reflectivity, [[WestAfrica]]'s expanding cocaine-transit role, and persistent discomfort around wives out-earning husbands in the [[UnitedStates]]. [[OliverMorton]] explains [[AlbedoFeedback]], [[AerosolCoolingMasking]], [[EarthEnergyImbalance]], [[OceanHeatMonitoring]], and [[SolarGeoengineering]] as ways to understand why ocean heat and global temperatures are rising faster. [[OriOgunbiyi]] then frames [[WestAfricaCocaineTransit]] through geography, enforcement displacement, corruption, and [[MaritimeDrugInterdictionCapacity]], while [[DougDawson]] uses [[TaylorSwift]], [[TravisKelce]], survey attitudes, and census-style reporting patterns to explain [[FemaleBreadwinnerNorms]], [[ReportedIncomeGenderNorms]], and [[DomesticLaborDoubleBurden]].

## Key Claims
- [[OliverMorton]] says record ocean surface temperatures are a symptom of a wider planetary heat-storage problem, because the oceans absorb most excess energy.
- [[EarthEnergyImbalance]] is presented as a fundamental climate measure: Earth is taking in more solar energy than it emits as infrared radiation.
- The source says the energy imbalance has more than doubled in the past 20 years.
- [[AlbedoFeedback]] appears through fewer clouds, scarcer ice caps, and reduced reflective pollution, all of which allow more sunlight to be absorbed.
- [[AerosolCoolingMasking]] creates a public-health and climate tradeoff: cleaner air saves lives, but falling sulphur aerosols also remove a cooling influence.
- The episode names [[China]] sulphur reductions and 2020 [[InternationalMaritimeOrganization]] shipping rules as sources of reduced reflectivity.
- [[NASA]] satellite instruments are the main measurement channel for the imbalance, while [[ArgoFloats]] provide independent ocean-heat confirmation since about 2000.
- The climate segment treats [[SolarGeoengineering]] as a controversial response, not a replacement for emissions cuts and [[CarbonRemoval]].
- [[PaulCrutzen]]'s 2006 proposal is presented as the key reference point for placing sulphate particles higher in the atmosphere to keep cooling while reducing direct health harms.
- [[OriOgunbiyi]] says [[WestAfrica]] is geographically convenient because it sits between South American cocaine production and European demand.
- European crackdowns on direct South America-Europe routes have pushed traffickers toward [[WestAfricaCocaineTransit]] and [[CocaineRouteDisplacement]].
- The episode says cocaine seized in West Africa rose by about 50% each year between 2019 and 2025, and that 2025 average hauls exceeded five tons.
- [[Liberia]] is the central case: a raid near the airport and [[Monrovia]] found suitcase-sized cocaine packages reportedly worth more than $300 million.
- [[PauloSilva]] warns that each kilo of cocaine reaching West Africa corrupts systems and creates [[NarcoStateRisk]] for Europe as well as local states.
- [[GregoryColeman]] says Liberian public trust is low because officers and state actors have been identified or charged.
- The female-breadwinner segment says wives out-earn husbands in only about 30% of American dual-income couples despite egalitarian survey answers.
- The source says only about 10% of Americans in the [[WorldValuesSurvey]] said a woman earning more than a man would cause problems, but relationship data look less comfortable.
- [[ReportedIncomeGenderNorms]] appear through a reported drop in household-income distributions at the point where wives would begin to out-earn husbands.
- [[DomesticLaborDoubleBurden]] appears when high-earning wives still carry more housework and childcare, weakening relationship satisfaction.
