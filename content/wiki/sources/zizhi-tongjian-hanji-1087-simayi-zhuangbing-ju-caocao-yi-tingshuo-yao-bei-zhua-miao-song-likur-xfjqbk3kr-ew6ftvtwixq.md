# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1087丨司马懿装病拒曹操，一听说要被抓秒怂？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills a 208 CE personnel-governance layer immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1088-junying-yeluan-ta-dai-jishiren-jiu-zhenzhule-lu8ocnihz6arqllfcc3ujok6ujil|Hanji 1088]]. It presents [[CuiYanLateHan|崔琰]] and [[MaoJie|毛玠]] as joint selectors who reject flashy reputation and private faction-building in favor of substance, humility, and social harmony, then traces how officials visibly adopt rough carts, plain appearance, and walking as the safest response to the new standard.

The episode interprets this shift through [[SelectionDrivenVirtueSignaling|选官驱动的德行表演]]: a virtue criterion can change behavior without proving inner virtue because career risk makes visible frugality strategically useful. It then turns to [[SimaYi|司马懿]], whose claimed illness fails as a refusal once [[CaoCao|曹操]] threatens arrest. The host links this coerced entry to Sima Yi's later feigned illness against Cao Shuang, but motives, dialogue, the wider personnel reform, and the retrospective “performance decides survival” thesis remain source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- In Jian'an 13 / 208 CE, Cui Yan and Mao Jie jointly handle official selection and favor substantial, modest, harmonious candidates over famous people judged morally unreliable.
- The episode treats empty display, falseness, and private faction-building as disqualifying traits within the selection standard.
- Once appointment criteria become legible, officials collectively adopt plain carts, deliberately rough appearance, and even walking to court; the host interprets these acts as politically rational displays rather than proof of sincere frugality.
- Cao Cao's reported “吾复何为哉” response is read as confidence in rule-setting: selectors and incentives can govern conduct without the ruler intervening in every case.
- The host argues that the personnel policy both disciplines elite conduct and weakens older prestige or faction networks, but the claimed displacement of great clans by cold-family practitioners remains an interpretive generalization.
- Cui Yan's later execution and Mao Jie's imprisonment are used to argue that virtue standards can become power instruments when obedience overrides the standard's stated moral purpose.
- Cui Yan's praise of Sima Yi brings him to Cao Cao's attention; Sima Yi initially claims a disabling illness to refuse office.
- When Cao Cao threatens arrest, Sima Yi accepts appointment, making coercive power—not persuasion—the decisive condition in this account.
- The episode retrospectively compares this first illness claim with Sima Yi's later performance against Cao Shuang and with the *Book of Jin*'s warning-oriented narrative.

## Key Quotes

> “用人如此，使天下人自治，吾复何为哉。” - the episode's formulation of Cao Cao's confidence in rule-mediated governance.

> “表演决定存亡。” - the host's summary of Sima Yi's coerced entry and later feigned-illness reputation.
