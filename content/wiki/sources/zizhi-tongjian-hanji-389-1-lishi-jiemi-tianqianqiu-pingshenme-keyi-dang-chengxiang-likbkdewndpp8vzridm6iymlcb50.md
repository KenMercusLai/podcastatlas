## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode opens the Hanji 389 sequence by explaining why [[TianQianqiuWesternHan|田千秋]] could rise suddenly from Dahonglu to chancellor and Fumin Hou under [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]. The source places his promotion inside Han Wudi's late-life awakening: after failed sea-searching, repeated sacrifice, and public remorse, Wudi accepts Tian's proposal to dismiss ineffective fangshi and later declares that immortal claims are empty.

The episode also previews the [[LuntaiSelfCriticismPolicyTurn|轮台罪己诏政策转向]] by ending at [[SangHongyangWesternHan|桑弘羊]]'s Luntai屯田 proposal. Its main synthesis is that Tian's "one sentence" mattered because it gave Wudi a concrete action matching his new posture: stop waste, reject fangshi fantasy, and redirect authority toward the people rather than immortality projects.

## Key Claims

- Han Wudi's late remorse is presented as a real public turn: he admits that many earlier acts were wild, mistaken, harmful to the people, and financially wasteful.
- The failed coastal search for immortals, strange omen reports, imperial plowing, and repeated sacrifices form the immediate background for Wudi's disillusionment.
- Tian Qianqiu advises dismissing the many fangshi who talk about immortals but produce no result; Wudi accepts and sends them home.
- Wudi's later private remarks go beyond administrative dismissal: he says he had been deceived, that there are no immortals, and that diet restraint and medicines can at most reduce illness.
- Tian's rapid elevation to chancellor and Fumin Hou is explained less by prior merit than by his alignment with Wudi's late policy and psychological turn.
- The source still treats Tian as capable after promotion: he is described as gentle, wise, and comparatively fitting as chancellor.
- The closing Luntai屯田 setup links Sang Hongyang's distant frontier proposal to the next episode's fuller Luntai self-criticism and recovery policy.
