# 513. America in '68: Nixon's Great Comeback (Part 6)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode explains [[RichardNixon|Richard Nixon]]'s victory in the [[UnitedStatesPresidentialElection1968|1968 U.S. presidential election]] as the result of patient party rebuilding, tight campaign control, television packaging, and emotionally resonant appeals to a threatened "middle America." It presents Nixon's message as deliberately ambiguous: he could promise national reconciliation and speak about poverty while using law-and-order, judicial, busing, and federal-power signals that reassured white Southern and suburban voters.

The episode also examines the disputed [[AnnaChennault|Anna Chennault]] backchannel. It accepts that Nixon's campaign had contact with Chennault and sent improper signals to South Vietnam, but rejects the stronger claim that this intervention decisively destroyed a peace agreement that South Vietnamese president Nguyen Van Thieu was already unlikely to accept.

## Key Claims

- Nixon rebuilt his national position after the 1960 and 1962 defeats by campaigning for Republican candidates, working local party circuits, and accumulating obligations among delegates and activists.
- The campaign solved both an electability problem and a character problem by entering primaries, projecting a calmer centrist image, and placing loyal Nixon aides rather than the traditional party apparatus in control.
- [[HRHaldeman|H. R. Haldeman]] and television producer Roger Ailes built controlled question-and-answer events, while advertising converted disorder, Vietnam, and unrest into a visual case for Nixon without exposing him to uncontrolled campaigning.
- "Peace with honor," "law and order," "the forgotten Americans," and "bring us together" worked as emotionally legible but policy-light language that let different audiences hear different promises.
- Nixon avoided [[GeorgeWallace|George Wallace]]'s open racial rhetoric, yet opposition to forced busing, promises of conservative judges, reduced federal intervention, and private campaign calculations show that racial anxiety remained part of the appeal.
- [[HubertHumphrey|Hubert Humphrey]] narrowed Nixon's large post-convention lead after publicly breaking with [[LyndonBJohnson|Lyndon B. Johnson]] over bombing North Vietnam, but Nixon held on in a very close popular vote.
- The episode treats Chennault's contacts and Johnson's suspicion as real while keeping decisive peace-talk sabotage unproven because South Vietnam had its own objections, agency, and preference for Nixon.

## Key Quotes

> "The forgotten Americans" - Nixon's convention-speech label for the quiet, working, taxpaying majority he claimed to represent.

> "Peace with honor" - the campaign's effective but deliberately vague Vietnam formula.

> "Bring us together" - the reconciliation slogan Nixon invoked after winning the election.
