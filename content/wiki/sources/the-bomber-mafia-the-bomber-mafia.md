# The Bomber Mafia

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode introduces the interwar U.S. air-power circle known as the Bomber Mafia and its belief that long-range aircraft could disable a few critical systems, compel surrender, and avoid another war of mass ground attrition. It follows [[CurtisLeMay]] from childhood fascination with flight through engineering-trained, evidence-led combat command, where personal courage and improved bombing accuracy coexisted with emotional distance from destruction.

The B-29 appeared to provide the reach and bomb load that the doctrine required, but the episode stops before the promised technical system is tested over Japan. Its central warning is that attachment to a machine and mastery of measurable performance can narrow attention to what can be hit while leaving civilian pressure, political response, operational friction, and moral consequence underexamined.

## Key Claims

- The Bomber Mafia challenged the Army Air Corps's supporting role by arguing that independent long-range bombing could win wars without repeating First World War trench slaughter.
- Its precision doctrine treated an enemy economy as a system of critical nodes: destroying bridges, aqueducts, power distribution, or selected industrial targets was expected to pressure civilians and political leaders into ending a war.
- The doctrine was comparatively discriminating in aspiration but not civilian-neutral, because it still proposed imposing severe deprivation on urban populations to produce political coercion.
- Air Corps Tactical School graduates later occupied much of senior U.S. air leadership, giving a small interwar movement disproportionate institutional influence.
- LeMay reduced aborts and improved bombing accuracy through personal risk-taking, strike-photo analysis, probability estimates, and long straight approaches rather than evasive action.
- His description of a tightly grouped factory strike as beautiful illustrates how technical and aesthetic attention to accuracy could displace attention from bombing's human meaning.
- The B-29 supplied range, altitude, and payload, but capability alone did not establish that precision bombing could work operationally or produce the political response its advocates predicted.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode supplies the doctrinal and biographical foundation for later installments on napalm, failed precision bombing, incendiary area attack, and postwar memory.
- The claim that critical-node attacks could win a war without mass slaughter remains an aspiration in this installment; the doctrine still depends on civilian suffering and an unverified chain from infrastructure loss to political surrender.
- The Saint-Nazaire result is evidence that one formation improved accuracy under the reported conditions, not proof that straight approaches, precision bombing, or independent air power would generalize across targets, weather, defenses, and wars.
