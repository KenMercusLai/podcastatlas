# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》202｜历史上因冷暴力被饿死的人是他（1）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents the death of a Zhao king under [[LuZhi|吕雉]] through a sequence of imposed marriage, household accusation, summons to Chang'an, denied audience, armed isolation, blocked food delivery, starvation, and commoner burial. The supplied document does not name the king; the adjacent [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-202-lishishang-yin-lengbaoli-bei-esi-de-ren-shi-ta-2-lrs7h5hhj0m0oixd6gw8flqpi0nd|Hanji 202 part 2]] identifies the previously starved Zhao king as [[LiuYouZhaoKingWesternHan|赵幽王刘友]], so that identity remains explicitly cross-episode rather than body-internal.

The durable synthesis is that a politically imposed marriage can carry coercive consequences beyond the wedding. A spouse's report moves through her natal Lü network into ruler action, while confinement and punishment of food delivery produce death without an open trial. The episode attributes an anti-Lü statement to the queen's accusation but does not independently establish whether Liu You said it or whether that report alone caused the decision.

## Key Claims

- The episode places the event in Gao Hou year seven, after Lu Zhi's regency power and Lü-family rank have become difficult to challenge openly.
- The Zhao king is married to a Lü-family woman but favors other consorts, turning an imposed dynastic marriage into a politically exposed household conflict.
- The queen reports that the king opposed Lü-family kingship and intended to remove the Lü rulers after Lu Zhi's death; Lu Zhi then summons him to Chang'an.
- After the king reaches his Zhao residence in the capital, Lu Zhi reportedly refuses him an audience and has guards seal the residence from outside contact.
- Zhao officials who try to deliver food are imprisoned and punished, making starvation an enforced political outcome rather than an accidental shortage.
- The king dies on the eighteenth day of the first month and is buried outside Chang'an with commoner rather than royal rites.
- The episode attributes a final song to the king through the Shiji tradition, using it to condemn Lü-family rule, coerced marriage, accusation, and starvation.

## Key Quotes

The supplied episode document is a structured summary rather than a verbatim transcript, so no sentence is promoted as an exact quotation. Its central reported formulations concern Lü-family kingship, compelled marriage to Lü women, the queen's accusation, and the king's hope that the Liu house would eventually answer the wrong.

## Contradictions

- The supplied document never names its Zhao king. The Liu You identification comes from the adjacent Hanji 202 part 2 recap and is therefore marked as a cross-episode normalization rather than a direct statement in this episode.
- The title's “cold violence” is an interpretive label. The body describes deliberate confinement, prevention of food delivery, and starvation, which is more operationally specific than interpersonal emotional withdrawal.
- The queen's report, the truth and wording of the alleged anti-Lü statement, Lu Zhi's motive, the command chain, the exact date, the final song, and the burial treatment remain episode-attributed pending comparison with transmitted texts.
- The brief frontier notice naming “匈奴、韩国” is not used to create or update a canonical actor because the second name may reflect transcription or normalization error and is not developed by the episode.
