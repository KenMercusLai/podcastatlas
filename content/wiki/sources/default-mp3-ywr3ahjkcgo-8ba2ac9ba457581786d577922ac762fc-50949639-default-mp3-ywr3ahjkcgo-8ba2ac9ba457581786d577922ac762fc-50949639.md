## Summary
This [[HiddenBrain]] episode follows Detroit police officer [[DarwinRocher]] after three trusted colleagues invited him into a planned robbery that could include murder. Psychologist [[AdamWaytz]] uses the case to develop [[LoyaltyJusticeMoralConflict]]: loyalty and justice can both feel morally binding, situational cues can change which value becomes salient, and acting for one can still produce [[MoralStress]] through violation of the other.

The episode's organizational answer is [[WhistleblowingAsMissionLoyalty]]. Reporting misconduct need not be framed as abandonment of loyalty; it can express loyalty to the public, an institution's legitimate mission, or the people the institution exists to protect. Rocher's cooperation prevented the plot and led to convictions, but death threats, a firebombing, isolation, panic attacks, and lasting trauma show that moral reframing does not remove retaliation or psychological cost.

## Key Claims
- [[LoyaltyJusticeMoralConflict]] explains how the same disclosure can be judged as courageous protection of fairness or as betrayal of a group.
- The episode reports an experiment in which fairness prompts increased reporting of poor work and loyalty prompts reduced reporting, suggesting that moral judgment can shift with context and recently activated values.
- Group crisis, tight social norms, police solidarity, and mission-driven identity can raise the perceived cost of breaking ranks.
- [[MoralLicensing]] can let people or organizations use a good identity or prior virtue to excuse misconduct or reinterpret silence as service.
- [[DarwinRocher]] reported the planned robbery, then secretly cooperated with the FBI because the possibility of robbery or murder made silence incompatible with his values and police role.
- [[MoralStress]] names the distress produced when action violates an important moral intuition; choosing justice over loyalty can therefore remain painful even when the actor believes it is right.
- [[WhistleblowingAsMissionLoyalty]] asks leaders to define responsible citizenship as exposing conduct that betrays the institution's mission rather than treating criticism as personal disloyalty.
