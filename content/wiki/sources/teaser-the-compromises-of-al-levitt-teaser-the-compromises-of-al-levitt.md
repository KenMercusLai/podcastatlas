# Teaser: The Compromises of Al Levitt

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory|Revisionist History]] teaser traces [[AlLevitt|Al Levitt]] from a New York youth and Young Communist League membership through marriage, migration to Hollywood, an interrupted screenwriting ambition, and wartime service at the [[FirstMotionPictureUnit|First Motion Picture Unit]]. Its central irony is that Levitt, then aligned with communist politics, worked for a military filmmaking operation alongside the later anti-communist [[RonaldReagan|Ronald Reagan]].

Levitt's recollection gives the institutional history a personal edge: he remembers Reagan arriving from cavalry service in conspicuous riding clothes and becoming a laughingstock among colleagues. The seven-minute teaser does not explain the compromises promised by the title or provide independent corroboration for that retrospective judgment.

## Key Claims

- Levitt and his wife Helen grew up in New York, joined the Young Communist League while in college, married, and drove to Hollywood.
- A military draft interrupted Levitt just as he believed he was close to securing screenwriting work.
- Levitt spent roughly eighteen months at the former Hal Roach Studios in Culver City, where the Army Air Forces' [[FirstMotionPictureUnit|First Motion Picture Unit]] made training films and newsreels.
- Reagan narrated many films produced by the unit, placing two future political opposites inside the same wartime media institution.
- Levitt recalled that Reagan's arrival in jodhpurs and a campaign hat made him difficult for colleagues to take seriously.

## Key Quotes

> "really insane" - Levitt's description of being bused from Los Angeles to Fresno for a military physical and then returned.

> "nobody could ever take him seriously after that" - Levitt's retrospective judgment of Reagan's cavalry-dress arrival.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was found. Levitt's dismissive memory qualifies later stature and existing accounts of Reagan's wartime media formation, but it is one colleague's retrospective assessment rather than a contradiction of Reagan's documented service.
