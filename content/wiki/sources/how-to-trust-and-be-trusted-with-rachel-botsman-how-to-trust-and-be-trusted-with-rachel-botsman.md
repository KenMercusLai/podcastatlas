# How to Trust and Be Trusted with Rachel Botsman

## Summary

In this [[RevisionistHistory]] conversation, [[RachelBotsman]] defines trust as a confident relationship with the unknown and distinguishes it from visibility, surveillance, and control. High-trust leadership does not eliminate uncertainty: it makes expectations, time frames, and boundaries clear enough that people can act autonomously without continuous monitoring.

An audiobook excerpt develops [[TrustDecisionQuality]] through familiarity bias, desired-signal selection, convenience, intuition, verification, and the deliberate “trust pause.” The practical synthesis is to keep trust as a workable social default while matching evidence and scrutiny to the stakes, reversibility, and consequences of being wrong.

## Key Claims

- Trust concerns a belief about another person's future behavior or an uncertain outcome, whereas continuous checking and demanded visibility are forms of control.
- [[TrustControlTransparencyBoundary]] separates useful explanation and honest openness from blanket visibility that can become surveillance.
- Clear expectations, time frames, and boundaries can support independent work more directly than attempts to remove every risk.
- [[TrustDecisionQuality]] improves when people identify which signals attract them, test familiarity and desirability against relevant information, and notice when convenience is overriding judgment.
- Intuition can prompt inquiry but should not be the sole decision-maker in consequential hiring, delegation, contracting, or disclosure decisions.
- A trust pause asks where confidence comes from, what the decision-maker wants to believe, which information is missing, and what verification is proportionate.
- Trust can remain a practical default because ordinary cooperation would be impossible under constant suspicion, while higher-stakes claims still require verification.

## Key Quotes

> “a confident relationship with the unknown” - Botsman's concise definition of trust as represented in the supplied summary.

> “trust, but verify” - the episode's shorthand for retaining cooperative trust while adding a second stage for consequential decisions.
