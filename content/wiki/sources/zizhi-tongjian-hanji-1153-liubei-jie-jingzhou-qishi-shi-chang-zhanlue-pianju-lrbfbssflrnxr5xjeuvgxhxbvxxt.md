## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode connects [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s 215 CE advance into [[Hanzhong|汉中]] to the temporary repair of the [[SunQuan|孙权]]–[[LiuBei|刘备]] alliance and clarifies that the slogan “刘备借荆州” compresses a narrower, contested transfer centered on [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]] and parts of Nanjun rather than the whole province. It then follows [[SimaYi|司马懿]] and [[LiuYeLateHan|刘晔]] urging an immediate attack on newly conquered Yizhou, while treating Cao Cao's refusal as a lost opportunity only with strong qualifications about logistics, geography, political succession, age, and inferred motive.

## Key Claims

- Cao Cao's Hanzhong offensive raises the external threat to Liu Bei enough that Liu Bei and Sun Quan suspend their Jingzhou confrontation and divide control along the Xiang River.
- The episode distinguishes Liu Bei's independently acquired southern commanderies from the later Sun-side transfer around Nanjun or Jiangling, rejecting “borrowed the whole of Jingzhou” as an adequate territorial description.
- The transfer remains strategically important because Jiangling and Nanjun connect the middle Yangtze to northern and western routes; a narrower jurisdiction can still supply disproportionate corridor value.
- After Cao Cao takes Hanzhong, Sima Yi and Liu Ye argue that Liu Bei's new Yizhou regime is not yet consolidated and that delay will let Zhuge Liang organize government while Guan Yu and Zhang Fei secure the approaches.
- The episode says Cao Cao declines the immediate invasion, reconsiders after seven days, and is then told by Liu Ye that the panic has passed; it presents this as a closing strategic window while not establishing that invasion was operationally feasible.
- Cao Cao leaves Xiahou Yuan, Zhang He, Xu Huang, and Du Xi in or around Hanzhong before withdrawing, so refusal to invade Yizhou is strategic contraction and delegation rather than abandonment of the frontier.
- The host links withdrawal to Cao Cao's age, fatigue, poetry, and need to settle the Cao Pi–Cao Zhi succession, but these are interpretations rather than demonstrated primary causes.
- The closing preview places Sun Quan's Hefei offensive and Cao Cao's sealed instructions before the Battle of Xiaoyao Ford.
