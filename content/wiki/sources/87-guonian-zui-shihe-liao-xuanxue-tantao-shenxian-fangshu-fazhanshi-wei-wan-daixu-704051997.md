# 87.过年最适合聊玄学！探讨神仙方术发展史（未完待续）

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode opens the show's two-part history of Chinese fangshu by treating "玄学" as a mix of psychological need, political technique, religious imagination, and fraud risk. It traces early divination from [[OracleBoneDivination|oracle bones]] to [[ZhouyiDivination|《周易》蓍草占卜]], then follows [[AncientChineseSoulSummoning|招魂]], [[ChineseAstrologicalPolitics|star omens]], dream interpretation, [[YinYangFivePhasesPoliticalTheory|阴阳五行]], and early immortal belief into Qin-Han imperial politics. The second half focuses on [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]], [[HanWudi|汉武帝]], [[LiuAn|刘安]], and [[WuguPoliticalPanic|巫蛊之乱]] to show how techniques that soothe uncertainty can become dangerous when attached to court power, death anxiety, resource extraction, and accusation.

## Key Claims
- The episode's opening stance is skeptical but not contemptuous: belief, worship, and divination can answer psychological need, but [[FangshiFraudAndAuthority|伪士]] who sell certainty, status, sex, or money extraction are the central danger.
- [[OracleBoneDivination|商代甲骨占卜]] is presented as both ritual practice and social record: questions about ancestors, weather, agriculture, war, hunting, feasting, and escaped slaves preserve traces of early power and everyday life.
- [[ZhouyiDivination|《周易》蓍草占卜]] turns counting, grouping, remainder-taking, trigrams, and hexagrams into a sacred interpretive system whose authority depends on procedure and explanation.
- [[AncientChineseSoulSummoning|楚地招魂]] and soul-loss explanations make illness, fainting, dreams, and death rituals narratable within a body-soul framework rather than only as medical facts.
- [[ChineseAstrologicalPolitics|星占]] and dream interpretation make unusual events politically readable; the source treats this as specialist control over uncertainty, not as neutral observation.
- [[ZouYan|邹衍]] and [[YinYangFivePhasesPoliticalTheory|阴阳五行]] matter because they connect natural classification, historical cycles, and dynastic legitimacy before later fangshu systems become more explicit.
- Early immortal belief is separated from later institutional Daoism: the episode presents immortal islands, elixirs, flying ascent, and long-life fantasies as raw material that fangshi later combine with yin-yang and five-phase language.
- [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]] uses water-phase symbolism and immortality seeking to show how cosmology can attach to imperial style, colors, numbers, secrecy, punishment, and expensive search projects.
- [[XuFu|徐福]], 卢生, 侯生, 李少君, 少翁, and 栾大 show the recurring court-fangshi bargain: rulers fund miracle promises because they want death-defying certainty, while practitioners exploit ambiguity, distance, props, and failure reinterpretation.
- [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] is the episode's strongest example of imperial immortality obsession: the source ties his求仙, spirit-summoning displays, palaces, platforms, and alchemical expense to public-resource waste and late-life disillusionment.
- [[LiuAn|刘安]] and [[Huainanzi|《淮南子》]] complicate the picture by showing fangshu as a knowledge-compilation world: astronomy, calendars, technical lore, philosophy, and alchemy can preserve observation and experiment even when immortality claims are false or dangerous.
- [[WuguPoliticalPanic|巫蛊之乱]] is the closing warning: when curse belief, imperial illness, denunciation, torture, palace rivalry, and succession anxiety combine, superstition can become mass political violence.

## Key Quotes
> "喜欢但不信" - the host's compact stance toward 玄学.

> "迷信可存，伪士当去" - the episode's ethical boundary between belief and predatory authority.

> "众占非一，唯梦为大" - used to mark the status of dreams among early divination forms.

> "始皇帝死而地分" - omen language around [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s late reign.

> "今年祖龙死" - another prophecy framed by the episode as part of Qin court anxiety.

> "天下没有仙人，都是妖妄" - the episode's late [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] disillusionment summary.
