# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》364-1｜用鸡骨头测吉凶的秘密

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[ChickenBoneDivination|鸡骨占卜]] as a full ritual procedure rather than a casual reading of discarded bones. Its account moves from selecting and preparing a rooster through prayer, sacrifice, offering, bone cleaning, bamboo insertion, pattern reading, and consultation of a transmitted divination text; it then places the practice in 109 BCE, when [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] reportedly brought Yue ritual specialists into imperial sacrifice.

The closing chronicle branch shifts from local ritual knowledge to [[ImmortalityQuestPolitics|求仙政治]]. [[GongsunQingFangshi|公孙卿]] tells Han Wudi that immortals favor elevated buildings, after which the emperor commissions towers, halls, platforms, ritual equipment, and palace extensions while waiting for divine beings. The durable synthesis is that divination, sacrifice, specialist texts, architecture, and imperial patronage can form one uncertainty-management system without proving that the predictions or immortal claims were true.

## Key Claims

- The episode dates the imperial use of chicken-bone divination to Yuanfeng 2 / 109 BCE, when Han Wudi ordered Yue ritual specialists to sacrifice to the high god and numerous spirits.
- Its ritual sequence begins with a healthy rooster, prayer, killing, bloodied feathers attached to spirit money, and careful cooking and presentation of the bird as an offering.
- After worship, the specialist removes and cleans a hip bone, exposes its cavities, inserts sharpened bamboo pieces, and treats the resulting form as an omen pattern.
- Interpretation depends on both repeated visual inspection and a divination text, so the practice is presented as procedural and learned rather than spontaneous guesswork.
- The host says chicken-divination knowledge circulated orally before literate recorders using Chinese and old Zhuang scripts produced written manuals, with more than sixty texts surviving in Guangxi and Yunnan; these transmission claims remain source-scoped.
- In the chronicle branch, Gongsun Qing's claim that immortals prefer towers leads Han Wudi to build and equip elevated structures in Chang'an and at Ganquan while awaiting divine arrival.
- The episode therefore places a regional ritual practice and court immortality architecture in the same Han Wudi-era field while keeping them analytically distinct.
