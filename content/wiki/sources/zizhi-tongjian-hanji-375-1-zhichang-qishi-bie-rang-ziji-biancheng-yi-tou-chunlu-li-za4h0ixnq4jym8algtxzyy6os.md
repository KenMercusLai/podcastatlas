# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》375-1｜职场启示：别让自己变成一头蠢驴

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode adds a return-route incident from [[LiGuangliWesternHan|李广利]]'s Dayuan campaign. After a Han detachment is defeated by [[YuchengWesternHan|郁成]], [[ShangguanJieWesternHan|上官桀]] pursues its king to [[Kangju|康居]]; when four riders hesitate over whether to bring the captive back alive, the young rider [[ZhaoDiWesternHan|赵弟]] kills him and completes the delivery.

The host turns that episode into a [[BuridanIndecisionEffect|布利丹效应]] lesson: comparison becomes harmful when near-equivalent options consume the time and energy needed to act. The durable synthesis is not that speed is always correct, but that reflection needs a stopping rule. The program's meditation, stillness, and “sixth sense” advice remains source-scoped rather than evidence that intuition reliably produces correct decisions.

## Key Claims

- During the Dayuan campaign, a Han detachment under Wang Shensheng is defeated after reaching Yucheng, and Li Guangli sends Shangguan Jie to attack the polity.
- The Yucheng king flees to Kangju, which hands him over after learning that Han has defeated Dayuan and fearing wider military pressure.
- Four riders debate killing the captive because they fear escape or rescue, but none initially acts; Zhao Di draws his sword and kills the king.
- The host presents Zhao Di's act as an example of decision ownership under time pressure and as an early distinction in Shangguan Jie's career.
- The Buridan's donkey story illustrates how searching for a uniquely optimal choice between equivalent options can turn delay into the worst outcome.
- The episode's stronger workplace lesson is that careful thought needs a decision deadline; otherwise “weighing pros and cons” becomes opportunity loss.
- The closing return to Li Guangli describes the Dayuan victory as a costly victory and leaves his reward as the next episode's question.

## Key Quotes

> “兵贵胜，不贵久” - the Sunzi line used to frame delay as a cost rather than a neutral pause.

> “知止而后有定，定而后能静” - the Great Learning passage used to connect a stopping point with inner steadiness.
