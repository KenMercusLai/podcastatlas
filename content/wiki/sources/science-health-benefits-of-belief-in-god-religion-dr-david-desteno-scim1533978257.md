## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] interview has [[AndrewHuberman]] and [[DavidDeSteno]] separate metaphysical questions that experiments cannot settle from the measurable effects of belief, prayer, meditation, ritual, gratitude, community, mourning, mortality contemplation, and surrender. Its central synthesis is [[ReligiousPracticeAsSpiritualTechnology]]: inherited practices can coordinate physiology, attention, emotion, relationships, and conduct, but benefits depend more on repeated participation than nominal belief and the same mechanisms can be misused.

## Key Claims
- [[DavidDeSteno]] argues that science cannot experimentally prove or disprove God, but can test associations and mechanisms linking religious engagement and practice to health, meaning, anxiety, depression, loneliness, grief, and moral behavior.
- [[ReligiousPracticeAsSpiritualTechnology]] distinguishes professed belief from embodied participation; the episode says belief without practice does not show the same health pattern and treats prayer, meditation, ritual, gratitude, and community as candidate mechanisms rather than proof of doctrine.
- [[PrayerBreathRegulation]] proposes that repeated formal prayer can slow respiration, lengthen exhalation, increase vagal influence, and pair troubling thought with a bodily signal of safety.
- [[RitualSynchronyAndProsociality]] links coordinated singing, praying, kneeling, chanting, and movement to connection, compassion, and helping, while mourning rituals also organize memory, self-focus, community support, and continuing bonds.
- [[GratitudeMoralBehavior]] extends gratitude beyond subjective wellbeing: in the laboratory work DeSteno describes, a brief blessing-counting induction sharply reduced opportunistic cheating.
- [[MortalityContemplationValueReorientation]] distinguishes chronic death fear from bounded contemplation that can redirect attention toward loved ones, service, legacy, and longer traditions.
- Psychedelic experiences, addiction recovery, surrender, awe, Burning Man, and new religious forms are treated as adjacent cases in which context, guidance, community, and meaning can shape experience without making every spiritual interpretation scientifically established.

## Key Quotes
> "spiritual technologies" - DeSteno's term for religious practices that can move minds and bodies toward benefit or harm.

> "we will do and then we will understand" - the episode's closing invitation to investigate traditions through practice before demanding complete intellectual certainty.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki was found. The episode adds religious meaning and inherited practice as possible contexts for mechanisms already represented in synchrony, gratitude, grief, awe, loneliness, and mortality pages.
- Longitudinal mortality and disease associations do not establish that religion itself caused the reported differences; selection, community, behavior, health status, and unmeasured confounding remain possible contributors.
- Prayer-breath, vagal, cortisol, meditation, gratitude, cheating, loneliness, and death-anxiety claims are episode-level summaries without enough methods here to establish effect size, generality, or clinical prescription.
- Religious practices and charismatic settings can coordinate people toward care or exploitation; measurable psychological benefit does not validate metaphysical claims or excuse institutional abuse.
- Psychedelic risk percentages, AI-religion forecasts, new-religion counts, public-figure observations, and claims about particular traditions remain source-scoped.
