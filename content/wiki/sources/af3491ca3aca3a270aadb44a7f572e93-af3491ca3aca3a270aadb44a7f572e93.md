# 誰的身體值得先被研究？遲到的陰蒂史

## Summary
This [[DuanwenNewsPodcast|端聞 / 端傳媒新聞播客]] episode uses a newly reconstructed three-dimensional clitoral nerve network to explain the [[ClitoralAnatomyResearchGap|long delay in clitoral anatomy research]]. It argues that technical difficulty and scarce tissue only partly explain the gap: [[MaleDefaultMedicalKnowledge|male-default medical knowledge]], reproductive priorities, sexual taboo, and the need to justify female pleasure through some other clinical purpose shaped which anatomy was recorded, taught, and protected. The episode connects those omissions to sex education, surgical risk, [[GenderedMedicalization]], and women's ability to understand and define their own bodies.

## Key Claims
- Nineteenth-century anatomical work had already described much of the clitoris, but later textbooks removed, reduced, or failed to update that knowledge while penile anatomy continued to accumulate.
- The clitoris is mostly internal, multi-planar, closely surrounded by pelvic organs, and difficult to study with conventional dissection; scarce living tissue and the risk of destroying unrecorded branches create genuine technical constraints.
- [[HelenOConnell|Helen O'Connell]]'s late-twentieth-century work restored the organ's full internal anatomy and its relation to the urethra after medical training and surgery had long treated equivalent female sexual-function knowledge as dispensable.
- [[MaleDefaultMedicalKnowledge]] links the research gap to a wider pattern in which male bodies become the reference case for medicine and product design, while female anatomy appears as a reproductive special case or an incomplete variation.
- [[ReproductiveCenteredSexEducation]] can teach menstruation, pregnancy, and hygiene while leaving students unable to name the vulva, locate the clitoris, or understand sexual pleasure.
- [[FemaleSexualPleasureResearchLegitimacy]] captures the institutional problem that clitoral research often had to borrow legitimacy from surgery, injury, or gender-affirming care, whereas male sexual function was more readily accepted as a medical objective in itself.
- Historical clitoridectomy and the Freudian hierarchy of “mature” vaginal over clitoral orgasm show how medical authority could pathologize female desire and make male participation a condition of legitimate sexuality.
- Feminist self-examination and [[AnneKoedt|Anne Koedt]]'s critique of the vaginal-orgasm myth helped turn embodied self-knowledge and autonomous pleasure into legitimate public and research questions.
