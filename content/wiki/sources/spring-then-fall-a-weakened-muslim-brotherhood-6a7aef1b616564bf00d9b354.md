## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links three stories: the post-Arab Spring weakening of the [[MuslimBrotherhood]], the difficulty of comparing crime in Europe and America, and the rapid global spread of [[Padel|padel]]. [[GarethBrown]] argues that the Brotherhood is fragmented by repression, exile, suspicion, and leadership loss even though the grievances that once made Islamist politics attractive have not disappeared. The crime segment uses [[MattAshby]]'s comparative city dataset to show why simple online claims about unsafe Europe or violent America can misread definitions, reporting behavior, city boundaries, and long-run safety trends. The padel segment treats the sport's boom as a mix of easy entry, social play, court economics, professional ambition, and unresolved access costs.

## Key Claims
- The Brotherhood appeared to gain from the Arab Spring, especially when [[MohamedMorsi]] became [[Egypt]]'s first democratically elected president, but the episode says the movement is now weaker and more divided across the region.
- [[AbdelFattahAlSisi]]'s coup and subsequent repression in Egypt are presented as the strongest example of an authoritarian backlash against the Brotherhood.
- [[SayyidQutb|Sayed Qutb]] is used as the ideological reference point for a movement grounded in Islamic values, social services, welfare, and dignity politics rather than only electoral competition.
- Governments often view the Brotherhood through an [[IslamistDemocraticTrustProblem]]: the fear that it might use democracy tactically until it can impose an Islamist model of governance.
- Western suspicion has hardened, with the episode saying the Trump administration pushed pressure against Brotherhood-linked groups and that the last U.S. counter-terrorism strategy placed the movement near jihadist threat categories.
- The Brotherhood's absence from post-Assad [[Syria]] is treated as evidence that Islamist political space no longer automatically belongs to the Brotherhood, even where the new order has Islamist-jihadist roots.
- [[ElonMusk]]'s claim that many parts of Britain and Europe are unsafe becomes the entry point for [[CrimeStatisticsComparability]] rather than a simple Europe-versus-America ranking.
- [[MattAshby]] and colleagues at [[UniversityCollegeLondon|University College London]] built a cross-city crime dataset by selecting large cities, adjusting boundaries for population density, and harmonizing definitions against Britain.
- Homicide is the most comparable category; the episode says Chicago's 2024 homicide rate was much higher than European cities, while [[NewYorkCity|New York City's]] murder count has fallen about 90% over 35 years.
- Other crime categories complicate the picture: robbery and burglary comparisons vary by definitions, willingness to report, local conditions, and city boundaries.
- [[Padel]]'s growth is attributed to a low technical threshold, doubles play, social atmosphere, small courts, and commercial attractiveness for clubs.
- The episode says padel has roughly 35 million players in 150 countries, while Britain has grown from about 87 courts and 15,000 players five years earlier to more than 2,000 courts and about one million players.
- [[PremierPadel]], the [[InternationalPadelFederation]], and the [[LawnTennisAssociation]] show that padel is professionalizing, but the episode keeps its spectator future and accessibility unresolved.
