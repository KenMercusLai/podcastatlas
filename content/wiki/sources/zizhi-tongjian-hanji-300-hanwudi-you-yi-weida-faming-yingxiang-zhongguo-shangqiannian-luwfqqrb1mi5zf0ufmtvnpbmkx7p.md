# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》300｜汉武帝又一伟大发明，影响中国上千年

## Summary

This short [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode presents [[EraNameSystem|年号制度]] as a response to competing imperial and vassal chronologies. It says [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] retrospectively named earlier reign periods after 建元、元光 and a hunted auspicious beast, turning a court-defined era name into a chronology usable across the empire.

The episode also places the reform inside [[AuspiciousOmenPolitics|祥瑞政治]]. A one-horned animal captured near an imperial sacrifice is interpreted as Heaven's response, the sign is offered at the Five Emperors altar, and the era-name narrative converts that event into both legitimacy and administrative order. [[LiuHuJibeiKingWesternHan|济北王刘胡]] then anticipates a fengshan ceremony and offers Mount Tai and nearby territory, showing how omen interpretation could affect a vassal king's territorial behavior.

## Key Claims

- Before the era-name system, the episode identifies stem-branch dating and ruler-reign-year dating as the main chronological methods.
- Simultaneous imperial and vassal reign counts could make political, economic, and cultural exchange harder to coordinate.
- The episode credits Han Wudi with making a single court-proclaimed era name usable throughout the realm and with retrospectively naming earlier periods.
- A strange animal captured around the Yong sacrifice is interpreted by officials as an auspicious response to the emperor's ritual reverence.
- Officials use that sign and an earlier long-star report to explain why reign periods should receive auspicious names rather than numerical labels.
- Liu Hu reads the omen as a sign that Han Wudi may perform fengshan, offers Mount Tai and adjacent land, and receives other counties in compensation.
- Later rulers could change era names repeatedly, while many Ming and Qing emperors used one era name and were consequently remembered by it.

## Key Quotes

> “年号是在当时历史背景下产生的，有深刻现实因素。” - the source summary's institutional framing of the reform.

> “朝廷所定年号通用于全国各地。” - the source summary's claim about chronological standardization.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with the existing wiki is adopted. Hanji 300 supplies the origin and coordination argument that Hanji 301 later extends into legitimacy, reuse, and cross-polity comparison.
- The episode calls the hunted-beast era “元朔,” although its own explanation ties the name to hunting and an auspicious animal; the wording is preserved as a likely transcription or chronology error rather than silently normalized to “元狩.”
- The claim that Han Wudi simply “invented” era names compresses a retrospective naming process and remains source-scoped rather than a complete institutional history.
- The date, animal anatomy and identification as a qilin, official speeches, exact sacrifice, motivations, and causal link from local dating disorder to reform require primary-text or specialist verification.
- The description of era-name abolition after the 1911 Revolution, later East Asian use, ruler counts, and one-era-name generalization for Ming and Qing are broad historical summaries rather than independently verified findings here.
