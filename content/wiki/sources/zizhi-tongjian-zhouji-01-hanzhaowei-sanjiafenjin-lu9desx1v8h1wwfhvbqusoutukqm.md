# 《资治通鉴·周纪》01｜韩赵魏三家分晋

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode introduces the opening of [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] through [[ZhouWeilieWang|周威烈王]]'s 403 BCE recognition of Han, Zhao, and Wei. It presents [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s concern as a legitimacy problem rather than only a territorial one: a weakened Son of Heaven still controls public rank, and by granting vassal status to Jin ministers he ratifies the breach of the ruler-minister order. The episode then develops that judgment through `礼`, `分`, `名`, `名器`, the narrow exception of Tang-Wu revolution, and examples of actors who preserve role boundaries despite practical advantage.

## Key Claims

- The episode identifies 403 BCE recognition as the opening event of the chronicle and as one threshold in [[PartitionOfJin|三家分晋]], distinct from both earlier de facto power transfer and Jin's later extinction.
- [[ZhouWeilieWang|周威烈王]] bypasses the residual [[JinState|晋国]] ruler when he recognizes [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]], [[ZhaoLiehou|赵烈侯]], and [[HanJinghou|韩景侯]] as vassals, converting practical power into public rank through [[ThreeJinVassalRecognition|三晋受封]].
- The host reads Sima Guang's formula "天子之职莫大于礼，礼莫大于分，分莫大于名" as the programmatic political thesis of the work's opening.
- `礼` is presented as an ordering system, `分` as differentiated ruler-minister and social roles, and `名` as the publicly recognized office or rank that makes those roles legible.
- `名器` joins names with visible status objects such as ritual vessels, vehicles, and burial privileges; granting them outside the accepted hierarchy can normalize a prior seizure of power.
- The episode treats Tang and Wu's overthrow of tyrants as an exceptional case requiring extreme tyranny, unusually qualified replacers, and popular support rather than as a general license for ministers to rebel.
- [[WeiziQi|微子启]] and 季札 are used as examples of preserving role or succession boundaries despite having opportunities to take power.
- [[JinWenGong|晋文公]]'s request for a royal burial passage is presented as a failed test of the old order: 周襄王 refuses, and Jin Wen Gong does not force the issue despite superior material power.
- The host's central causal claim is that formal breaches matter before immediate collapse: once the highest naming authority rewards rather than condemns a violation, later actors face a weaker legitimacy barrier.

## Key Quotes

> "天子之职莫大于礼，礼莫大于分，分莫大于名" - the programmatic hierarchy attributed to Sima Guang's opening judgment.

> "履霜，坚冰至" - the episode's warning that small breaches of status order can foreshadow systemic collapse.

> "君子喻于义，小人喻于利" - the formula used to place right order above immediate advantage.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction found. The episode calls 403 BCE the beginning of the Warring States, while existing notes also preserve 453 BCE and 376 BCE as practical and terminal thresholds; [[PartitionOfJin|三家分晋]] therefore treats periodization as layered rather than forcing one date.
- The source is a popular-history interpretation of Sima Guang's normative argument. Its causal claim that Zhou recognition led to later collapse, its reconstructed motives, and its broad account of a stable earlier hierarchy remain interpretive or source-scoped rather than independently established here.
