# 607. Nelson’s Lover: The Scandalous Lady Hamilton

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs [[EmmaHamilton|Emma Hamilton]] before her affair with [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]], presenting her as a survivor of poverty and sexual dependence who became a model, performer, fashion influence, diplomat's wife, and politically connected European celebrity. Her rise from Amy Lyon through [[CharlesGreville|Charles Greville]]'s controlled reinvention, [[GeorgeRomney|George Romney]]'s portraits, marriage to [[SirWilliamHamilton|Sir William Hamilton]], and the live classical “attitudes” shows how beauty, performance, print, travel, patronage, and self-education could create fame without removing gendered or class vulnerability.

The episode also supplies the prehistory of the Hamilton-Nelson relationship. Emma's friendship with [[MariaCarolina|Maria Carolina]] gave her an informal channel into Neapolitan power; she helped Nelson obtain troops in 1793 and supplies at Syracuse in 1798 before celebrating the [[BattleOfTheNile]] and receiving him in Naples. The source therefore joins [[EmbodiedClassicalPerformance]], [[FashionCirculationInfrastructure]], and [[IntimateAccessInformalPower]] while resisting the later caricature of Emma as merely Nelson's seductive distraction.

## Key Claims

- Emma's childhood poverty, domestic service, possible survival sex, brothel work, pregnancy, and dependence on wealthy patrons place her rise inside the unequal [[SexWorkEconomicSpectrum]] rather than a simple rags-to-riches romance.
- [[CharlesGreville]] offered security but required her to surrender her child, change her name, break social ties, and perform respectable repentance, combining affection and patronage with coercive social remaking.
- [[GeorgeRomney]] painted Emma repeatedly, and reproduced images made her face recognizable beyond aristocratic portrait ownership, turning bodily performance and print circulation into early celebrity infrastructure.
- Emma's “attitudes” animated classical poses through dress, gesture, expression, hair, and rapid transformation; elite witnesses treated them as an original performance form, not merely passive display.
- [[SirWilliamHamilton]] provided education, cultural partnership, social security, and marriage, while Emma's languages, artistic intelligence, and performance skill made her an active participant rather than only his creation.
- Friendship with [[MariaCarolina]] converted celebrity and court trust into informal political access that assisted British interests before and during Nelson's 1798 campaign.
- Nelson's later attachment did not create Emma's fame: her artistic reputation, fashion influence, marriage, and royal friendship already made her one of Europe's best-known non-royal women.
