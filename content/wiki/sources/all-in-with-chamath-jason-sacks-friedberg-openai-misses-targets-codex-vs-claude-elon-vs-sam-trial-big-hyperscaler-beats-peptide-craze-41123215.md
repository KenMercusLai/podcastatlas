# OpenAI Misses Targets, Codex vs Claude, Elon vs Sam Trial, Big Hyperscaler Beats, Peptide Craze

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] episode treats [[OpenAI]]'s missed user and revenue targets as a test of whether demand, compute supply, product momentum, and capital commitments can align. The hosts connect [[Codex]], [[ClaudeCode]], [[GPT55Cyber|GPT 5.5 Cyber]], [[AICyberDefenseUtility]], [[DataCenterPowerBottleneck]], and hyperscaler capex into one AI infrastructure cycle, then move through [[OpenAINonprofitConversionDispute]], [[Retatrutide]], and [[ProductLiabilityFederalPreemption]] in the [[Monsanto]]/[[Roundup]] litigation.

## Key Claims
- [[OpenAI]]'s missed ChatGPT user and revenue targets may reflect both consumer competition from [[Google]]/[[Gemini]] and supply constraints from power, data-center construction, and compute commitments.
- [[DavidSacks|David Sacks]] argues [[Codex]] regained coding momentum through a GPT 5.5 base-model upgrade while [[Anthropic]] and [[ClaudeCode]] faced user complaints about rationing and regressions.
- [[GPT55Cyber|GPT 5.5 Cyber]] and Anthropic's Mythos discussion strengthen the wiki's dual-use cyber frame: models can discover existing vulnerabilities for defenders or attackers, so access and supervision matter.
- Hyperscaler capex from [[Amazon]], [[Microsoft]], [[Google]], and [[Meta]] is framed as an asset-heavy AI infrastructure buildout where power, turbines, transformers, debt, and utilization matter as much as software margins.
- [[OpenAINonprofitConversionDispute]] is presented as a material governance and IPO-timing risk because [[ElonMusk]]'s lawsuit challenges OpenAI's nonprofit-to-for-profit transition and names [[SamAltman]] and [[GregBrockman]].
- [[Retatrutide]] is framed as a next-generation metabolic drug candidate that may intensify [[GLP1Agonists|GLP-1]] competition, body-composition use, and peptide enthusiasm while remaining dependent on clinical approval and monitoring.
- The [[Monsanto]]/[[Roundup]] Supreme Court segment frames [[ProductLiabilityFederalPreemption]] as a conflict between federal EPA labeling authority and state failure-to-warn claims.
