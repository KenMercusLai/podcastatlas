# 38.安吉拉卡特精怪故事集：哦！迷人的悍妇！

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode reads [[AngelaCarter|Angela Carter / 安吉拉·卡特]]'s [[JingguaiGushiji|《精怪故事集》]] as a return to rough, comic, erotic, violent, and female-forward folk narrative. The hosts connect grandmotherly storytelling to [[FemaleOralStorytelling]], then show how Carter's selections and rewritings foreground women who are clever, desiring, cruel, practical, funny, and self-directed rather than safely moral. The episode's strongest synthesis is that Carter's feminism works through story texture: [[UnrulyWomenInFolktales|unruly women]], [[FairyTaleSanitization|desanitized fairy-tale material]], and [[CrossCulturalFairyTaleFlow|cross-cultural tale flow]] unsettle patriarchal and Disney-shaped expectations without becoming a simple slogan.

## Key Claims
- The episode begins from mothers and grandmothers telling stories, treating oral domestic storytelling as a serious transmission system rather than a childish pre-literary residue.
- [[AngelaCarter|Carter]] is framed as a "literary witch" because she combines fantasy, gothic style, psychoanalysis, feminism, and folk material against narrow postwar realism.
- [[JingguaiGushiji|《精怪故事集》]] matters because its women are active but not purified: they seduce, deceive, escape, punish, laugh, and solve problems without needing to become moral exemplars.
- The source distinguishes Carter's feminism from programmatic slogan-making. Her method is to restore possibility to the story world, so readers feel that women could always have been central actors.
- Stories such as the crab husband, the woman deceiving an illiterate husband, the old woman who insists against the current, Hairy Baby, and the blubber lover turn desire, household labor, marriage, sex, death, and revenge into grotesque comedy.
- The episode reads female desire as neither simple repression nor simple liberation; its Carter frame keeps sexuality connected to bodily autonomy, fantasy, danger, and social meaning.
- [[FairyTaleSanitization]] is a recurring target: nineteenth-century and middle-class retellings often made folk stories cleaner, less sexual, less violent, and easier to treat as children's morals.
- [[CrossCulturalFairyTaleFlow]] is used against narrow nationalist ownership of stories. The episode compares motifs across the [[UnitedStates]], [[India]], Inuit stories, [[Iceland]], [[Spain]], [[China]], and older textual examples such as [[YeXian|《叶限》]]-like Cinderella material.
- [[FeministFairyTaleRewriting]] appears most clearly when the episode moves from the anthology to [[TheBloodyChamber|《染血之室》]], where Carter changes who has agency inside the Bluebeard plot.
- The closing "悍妇" frame is deliberately admiring: the episode values women who do not wait for rescue, do not prove innocence, and do not apologize for being clever, hungry, desirous, angry, or funny.
