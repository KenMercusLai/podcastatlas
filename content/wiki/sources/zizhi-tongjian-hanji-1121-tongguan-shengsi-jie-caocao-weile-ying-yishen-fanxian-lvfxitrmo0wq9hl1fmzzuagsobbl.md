# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1121丨潼关生死劫：曹操为了赢以身犯险？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode supplies the central maneuver phase of the 211 CE [[WeinanBattleLateHan|渭南之战]]. [[CaoCao|曹操]] fixes the Guanzhong coalition at Tong Pass, sends [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] and [[ZhuLingLateHan|朱灵]] to secure the Pujin crossing, and then moves the main army across the Yellow River and Wei River through protected routes and fortified positions.

The most dramatic episode is Cao Cao's decision to remain with a small rearguard while [[MaChao|马超]]'s cavalry attacks the Yellow River crossing. [[XuChu|许褚]] shields and rows him away, while [[DingFeiLateHan|丁斐]] releases cattle and horses that distract the pursuers. The episode interprets Cao Cao's exposure as a morale-building act of command responsibility, while the same scene also shows the strategic danger created when an army's leader accepts extreme personal risk.

## Key Claims

- Cao Cao keeps increasing visible pressure at Tong Pass without accepting Ma Chao's challenges, inducing the coalition to concentrate its main force around the expected point of attack.
- Once the coalition is fixed, Xu Huang and Zhu Ling lead a detached force to seize the Pujin crossing west of the Yellow River, opening a route around the defended pass.
- Cao Cao stays on the south bank with a small guard while the army crosses, and Ma Chao attacks with cavalry before Cao Cao has embarked.
- Xu Chu protects Cao Cao with a saddle and helps move the boat after its operator is killed; Ding Fei releases cattle and horses, and the pursuers turn toward the animals long enough for Cao Cao to escape.
- The episode treats Cao Cao's survival and visible acceptance of danger as a source of soldier loyalty, but the army's dependence on its commander makes the same act a command-continuity risk.
- Cao-side control of the crossing lets the army defeat a later coalition attempt to retake it, then advance behind improvised protected corridors that preserve movement and supply against cavalry harassment.
- Cao Cao crosses the Wei River at night and stabilizes a sandy position by wetting the works before a freeze; repeated coalition attacks fail against prepared defenses.
- Once the Yellow River, Wei River, and Tong Pass lines no longer block Cao Cao's movement, the coalition's weaker food base makes prolonged concentration increasingly costly and contributes to its peace proposal.
- [[JiaXu|贾诩]] advises pretending to accept negotiations so that Cao Cao can seek a later opportunity to break the coalition.

## Key Quotes
