# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》843丨古代农民贫困的根源是什么？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 157-158 CE [[EmperorHuanOfHan|Huan-era]] layer immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-844-ruhe-liyu-bubaizhidi-lsp49uw8atyiufrf-ryo-9ctos5z|Hanji 844]]. It centers [[LiuTaoLateHan|刘陶 / 刘桃]]'s Taixue memorial against a proposal to cast larger coins, arguing that peasant poverty comes from hunger, lost cloth, agricultural damage, official extraction, and lack of household recovery time rather than coin weight.

The second half links local rebellion, omen politics, and frontier disorder to personnel failure. [[ZhuDaLateHan|朱达]]'s revolt begins with a corrupt county magistrate, [[ChenShouTaishiLingLateHan|陈寿 / 太史令]] dies after blaming a solar eclipse on [[LiangJiLateHan|梁冀]], and [[ChenGuiDuliaoGeneralLateHan|陈龟]] persuades Emperor Huan to replace officials and remit Bingzhou/Liangzhou burdens before the Zhang Huan frontier crisis continued in Hanji 844.

## Key Claims

- Liu Tao reframes peasant poverty from a currency-design problem into a food, clothing, agriculture, and extraction problem.
- Large-coin reform cannot substitute for stopping requisition and plunder, especially when locusts, insects, taxes, and officials have already weakened households.
- Zhu Da's 157 CE rebellion is presented as a local-corruption and personnel-failure signal rather than as context-free popular disorder.
- Chen Shou's death shows that blaming Liang Ji through omen interpretation can become lethal when the powerholder controls arrest and torture channels.
- Chen Gui links frontier rebellion to corrupt governors, bought office, weak commanders, disaster-hit provinces, and impossible tax/corvee burdens.
- Emperor Huan can accept practical relief and personnel cleanup in 158, but the following Hanji 844 layer shows that anti-Liang correction remains politically dangerous.
