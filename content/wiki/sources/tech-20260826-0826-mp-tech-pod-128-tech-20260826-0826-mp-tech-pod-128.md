## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has [[MeganMcCartyCorino|Megan McCarty Carino]] interview [[CarrieBatten]] of [[NewYorkMagazine|New York Magazine]] about [[Meta]]'s attempt to make [[RayBanSmartGlasses|Ray-Ban smart glasses]] culturally acceptable beyond a gadget audience. It connects AI features, live-streaming, [[Oakley]] and [[Caperni]] collaborations, celebrity promotion by [[KylieJenner]] and [[TeyanaTaylor]], and creator seeding to [[AIGlassesProductFit]], [[SmartGlassesBystanderPrivacy]], [[AIHardwarePrivacyExchange]], [[CreatorGiftingDemandManufacture]], and [[GenderedAIMarketing]].

The core tension is that hands-free, eye-level recording makes sense for travel, cooking, gardening, and creator work, but the same ease of capture makes bystanders hard to protect or reassure. The episode is skeptical about organic mainstream adoption: Batten says Meta could make the glasses more common through marketing force and gifted creator distribution even if everyday utility remains uneven.

## Key Claims
- The original Meta Ray-Ban smart glasses were conceived around 2019, launched in 2021, and were widely treated as a sales flop bought mainly by gadget-focused users.
- Meta's newer strategy combines better technical features, AI integration, live-streaming, an [[Oakley]] outdoors collaboration, runway/fashion positioning with [[Caperni]], and celebrity promotion.
- [[KylieJenner]]'s Starfire campaign increased visibility while also making misuse and privacy concerns more visible.
- Travel, cooking, and gardening are plausible use cases because first-person capture can record two-handed activities without holding a phone.
- A cooking-class example showed that even a user who warned others she was recording still captured strangers and triggered visible discomfort.
- The "creep glasses" association matters commercially because users may have to reassure people nearby that the device is not invading their privacy.
- The episode links the smart-glasses push to broader [[GenderedAIMarketing]] through women-focused AI adoption talk, celebrity endorsements, and phrases such as "Claude girly."
- Batten says mainstreaming may come through Meta's creator partnership and gifting machinery rather than clear evidence that many consumers are choosing to buy the glasses.

## Key Quotes
> "creep glasses" - shorthand for the bystander privacy stigma.

> "sheer brute force" - Batten's explanation of how the product could become mainstream.
