# Are wildfires making the future uninsurable?

## Summary
This [[PlanetMoney]] episode follows [[HeidiLang]] and [[ParadiseCalifornia|Paradise, California]] from the 2018 Camp Fire through rebuilding and a second crisis of unaffordable or unavailable property insurance. It argues that climate-amplified [[CorrelatedCatastropheInsuranceRisk]], constrained pricing and catastrophe modeling, insurer withdrawal, and imperfect property data pushed more residents toward the limited [[CaliforniaFAIRPlan]].

The episode also tests whether adaptation can reopen a private market. [[InstituteForBusinessAndHomeSafety|IBHS]] standards, stricter construction, and neighborhood mitigation initially earned weak or inconsistent rewards, but a direct visit helped [[MercuryInsurance]] recognize hardened homes that broad models had missed. The result is cautious rather than universal: [[CommunityWildfireMitigation]] and [[MitigationUnderwritingRecognition]] can restore some capacity, while higher risk-reflective prices may still make climate-exposed communities unaffordable.

## Key Claims
- California wildfires in 2017 and 2018 reportedly caused more than $20 billion in damage and erased decades of state insurance-industry profits.
- Rules limiting large rate increases and forward-looking catastrophe models protected consumers from immediate repricing but also contributed to insurer withdrawal from high-risk areas.
- The [[CaliforniaFAIRPlan]] provides a fire-focused last resort rather than a complete homeowners policy, so residents may need costly supplemental coverage.
- Insurance pooling becomes harder when climate change increases the frequency, severity, and geographic correlation of catastrophic losses.
- [[FireResilientConstruction]] and individual home hardening reduce physical vulnerability without automatically producing affordable coverage.
- Wildfire mitigation is partly collective because neighboring vegetation, fences, and buildings can transmit fire across property lines.
- Direct inspection in Paradise exposed a gap between broad risk classifications and property- or community-level mitigation; Mercury subsequently offered a limited number of private policies.
- A functioning market may require forward-looking models, higher prices, credible mitigation standards, and obligations to write coverage in exposed areas.

## Key Quotes
> “miracle” — [[CaseyTaylor]]'s description of insurance after the Camp Fire, contrasted with the later coverage crisis that impeded rebuilding.

> “The future is insurable” — the episode's qualified conclusion, paired with the expectation that insurance will become more expensive.
