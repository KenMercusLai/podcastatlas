## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode argues that wealthy-country housing markets are more exposed to another rise in bond yields because mortgage protection has shortened, pandemic excess savings have been depleted, and construction has increased from the unusually weak 2010s baseline. It then reports that [[Australia]]'s under-16 [[SocialMediaAgeBans|social-media ban]] has had little early effect on use, as easy evasion and inaccurate age checks collide with the privacy costs of stronger identity enforcement. The final segment reads [[Fauda]] season five as an emotionally immediate witness to Israeli trauma after October 7th rather than a balanced history of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

## Key Claims
- Government-bond yields feed into mortgage costs, including through the relationship between American ten-year Treasury yields and 30-year mortgage rates.
- The 2021-23 tightening cycle produced smaller housing-price declines than many forecasts because long fixed-rate mortgages, roughly $5trn in excess household savings, and limited housing supply absorbed the shock.
- [[HousingRateShockVulnerability]] is now higher because variable-rate borrowing has become more common in markets such as [[Australia]] and [[Canada]], fixed terms have shortened, excess savings have largely been spent, and recent construction leaves more supply available.
- The source does not forecast bond yields or a housing crash; it makes the conditional claim that continued rate increases would now transmit more directly into household payments and housing activity.
- Nearly nine months after Australia's under-16 account ban, early research cited by the episode found that about 85% of affected teenagers still used the same platforms and that 12- and 13-year-olds spent about as much time on social media as before.
- Teenagers described evading restrictions with false ages, shared accounts, and relatives' personal details, while facial scans and behavioral inference struggle to distinguish users close to the threshold.
- Real-ID enforcement, discussed through [[Malaysia]], can make exclusion stronger only by creating document-storage, hacking, privacy, and government-access risks.
- A conditional approach attributed to [[Canada]] would exclude under-16s until platforms meet safety criteria, aligning access rules with [[SocialMediaDesignRegulation]] rather than treating exclusion as the endpoint.
- [[Fauda]] season five narrows the empathy extended to Palestinian adversaries and sympathetic Europeans compared with earlier seasons.
- The review interprets that narrowed perspective as [[FictionAsTraumaWitness]]: the series stages Israeli rage, guilt, grief, and state failure soon after October 7th, but cannot supply the conflict's full truth.
- Fiction can reach audiences who avoid or distrust news and can communicate emotional reality forcefully, yet understanding the conflict also requires Palestinian voices and accounts critical of Israeli abuses.
