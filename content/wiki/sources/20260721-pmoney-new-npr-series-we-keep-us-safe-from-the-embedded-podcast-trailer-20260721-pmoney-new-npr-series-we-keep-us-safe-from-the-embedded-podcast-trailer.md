## Summary
This short [[PlanetMoney]] trailer handoff has [[KennyMalone]] introduce [[WeKeepUsSafe]], a new investigative true-crime series from the [[EmbeddedPodcast|Embedded podcast]], [[TheSeattleTimes|The Seattle Times]], and [[KUOW]]. The trailer frames [[CapitolHillOrganizedProtest|CHOP]] as a 2020 attempt to build a [[PoliceFreeProtestSpace|police-free protest space]] after Seattle police abandoned a precinct, then follows how fear, armed self-protection, rumor, and confusion preceded the killing of 16-year-old [[AntonioMaysJr|Antonio Mays Jr.]] The source's central question is how a place described as protecting Black life ended with a Black teenager dead and the case still unsolved six years later.

## Key Claims
- [[KennyMalone]] tells [[PlanetMoney]] listeners that [[NPR]]'s [[EmbeddedPodcast|Embedded]] has a new investigative series and plays its trailer rather than explaining the whole story himself.
- The trailer says that in 2020, after a standoff with anti-police-brutality protesters, Seattle police abandoned their precinct and protesters occupied about eight city blocks.
- The occupied area became [[CapitolHillOrganizedProtest|CHOP, the Capitol Hill Organized Protest]], with tents, free food, mutual aid, speeches, and music.
- The trailer presents the early CHOP period as both protest and experiment: a [[PoliceFreeProtestSpace|police-free community]] meant to protect people after police-brutality protests.
- Protesters feared an attack from right-wing activists, and some appointed themselves as security while carrying guns and wearing tactical gear.
- One night a Jeep drove into the camp, protesters believed they were under attack, and protesters shot at the Jeep.
- After the shooting, 16-year-old [[AntonioMaysJr|Antonio Mays Jr.]] was dead; the trailer says protesters saw him as a threat that night.
- Antonio's father challenges the threat narrative by saying Antonio was there for the cause.
- The trailer says the case remains unsolved six years later and asks how a place built to protect Black life ended up killing a Black teenager.
- [[WillJames]] and [[SydneyBrounstone]] say they spent more than a year investigating, reviewing rumors, finding new witnesses, and uncovering never-before-seen evidence.
- The series is identified as a collaboration among [[NPR]]'s [[EmbeddedPodcast|Embedded podcast]], [[TheSeattleTimes|The Seattle Times]], and [[KUOW]].
