# Josh Shapiro on Trump, Iran War Chaos, Israel's Failure, the Economy, and 2028 Race

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] interview presents [[JoshShapiro|Josh Shapiro]] as a centrist Democratic executive using [[Pennsylvania]]'s permitting speed, tax cuts, fraud enforcement, public safety, workforce development, housing, and education claims to argue for [[PennsylvaniaExecutionGovernance|execution-oriented Democratic governance]]. The domestic-policy half frames [[ServiceDeliveryDemocraticTrust]] as a way to reduce cynicism: faster permits, same-day barber licensing, anti-fraud enforcement, safe streets, and non-college workforce pathways become evidence that government can work without becoming anti-business.

The national-politics and foreign-policy half links [[DemocraticParty|Democratic Party]] positioning, [[DonaldTrump|Donald Trump]]'s tariffs and Medicaid cuts, [[ExecutiveWarPowersBreakdown]], [[UndefinedWarObjectives]], [[BenjaminNetanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], [[Israel]], [[Gaza]], [[Iran]], and [[AntisemitismIsraelCriticismBoundary]]. Shapiro's strongest synthesis is that Democrats should offer calm, competent governance while preserving institutional checks, separating criticism of governments from identity-based antisemitism, and judging foreign intervention by clearly defined American interests.

## Key Claims
- Shapiro says Pennsylvania has cut taxes, accelerated permitting, kept unemployment below the national rate, reduced violent crime, and expanded vocational pathways, making execution rather than ideology the episode's main Democratic-governance claim.
- The permitting discussion turns bureaucracy into a wage and trust issue: same-day occupational licensing and permit refund guarantees are framed as proof that government can "get to yes" for workers and businesses.
- Shapiro treats fraud enforcement as compatible with public benefits, arguing that stopping theft from programs such as Medicaid is different from dismantling the programs themselves.
- On 2024 and 2026 Democratic politics, Shapiro says Biden should not have sought another term, rejects a religion-based explanation for not becoming Harris's running mate, and frames the 2026 midterms as a referendum on Washington chaos and corruption.
- Shapiro criticizes Trump tariffs, Medicaid cuts, family self-dealing, pardon practices, and congressional deference as parts of a broader institutional breakdown.
- The Iran-war section says Trump launched a war of choice without defining objectives, exit strategy, or consistent rationale, making [[UndefinedWarObjectives]] the core strategic critique.
- Shapiro separates antisemitism from Israel policy: criticism of [[BenjaminNetanyahu|Netanyahu]], Gaza policy, Iran policy, or U.S. support for Israel can be legitimate, but blaming Jews collectively for Israeli government action crosses into antisemitic dual-loyalty logic.

## Key Quotes
> "get to yes" - Shapiro's governing shorthand for service-oriented government.

> "war of choice" - Shapiro's label for Trump's Iran war.

> "calm, competent governance" - Shapiro's contrast with Washington chaos.
