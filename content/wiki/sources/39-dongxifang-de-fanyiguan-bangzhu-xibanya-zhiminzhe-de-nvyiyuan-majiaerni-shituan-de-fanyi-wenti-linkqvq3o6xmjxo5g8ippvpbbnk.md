# 39 东西方的翻译官：帮助西班牙殖民者的女译员；马戛尔尼使团的翻译问题

## Summary

This [[WeirdHistoryPodcast|怪东西 Weird History]] episode compares [[Malinche]] in the [[SpanishConquestOfMexico|Spanish conquest of Mexico]] with the interpreters and translated documents of the [[MacartneyEmbassy|Macartney Embassy]]. In both cases, communication depends on multilingual chains, cultural judgment, and people exposed to coercion or divided loyalties rather than on a transparent transfer of words.

The episode's central synthesis is [[TranslationAsPoliticalMediation|translation as political mediation]]. Malinche's Nahuatl-Maya-Spanish work helped reveal rivalries and build alliances, while the embassy's Chinese and European intermediaries changed letters, gifts, demands, and replies to fit incompatible diplomatic orders. Translation could therefore enable negotiation and lower immediate friction while also hiding disagreement, manufacturing apparent consent, and altering later accounts of conquest or tribute.

## Key Claims

- Ancient Chinese accounts of “重译” and “重九译” show relay interpretation functioning both as a practical technique and as a political narrative in which distant peoples appear drawn toward a civilizational center.
- [[Malinche]] and [[GeronimoDeAguilar|Geronimo de Aguilar]] initially formed a Spanish-Maya-Nahuatl relay that let [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]] gather intelligence, negotiate with Indigenous polities, and intervene in regional rivalries.
- Malinche's mediation was causal to coalition-building, but enslavement, colonial violence, uncertain biography, and later nationalist memory make “traitor,” “mother,” or voluntary collaborator inadequate as complete descriptions.
- The reported [[CholulaMassacre|Cholula warning]] and [[Moctezuma]]'s supposed submission remain translation and source problems rather than secure evidence of a plot or voluntary surrender.
- The [[MacartneyEmbassy|Macartney Embassy]] relied on Chinese, Latin, Italian, Portuguese, and English intermediaries because neither side possessed a stable direct English-Chinese diplomatic channel.
- [[LiZibiao|李自标]] had European language training but limited command of Qing court language and political implication, showing that vocabulary and grammar do not by themselves create diplomatic competence.
- Translators recast British sovereign-to-sovereign language, gifts, and trade requests into Qing forms of deference, tribute, and imperial grace, while replies translated for the British could remove similarly charged Qing language.
- These revisions temporarily preserved cooperation by letting each side believe the other accepted its framework, but the more accurate Chinese version of Macartney's formal demands also shows that failure cannot be reduced to mistranslation: [[QianlongEmperor|乾隆]] could understand and reject requests on institutional and security grounds.
- Scientific instruments could not demonstrate European knowledge independently of language; when technical explanation collapsed into opaque transliteration, they became court curiosities rather than self-interpreting evidence.

## Key Quotes

> “翻译从来不是透明、中立的语言管道。” - the episode's core interpretive claim.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction is adopted. The Mexico branch reinforces the existing wiki's account of Malinche as a constrained but consequential mediator and its rejection of a secure voluntary-surrender narrative.
- The episode attributes greater causal emphasis to translation than some broader conquest accounts, but it also acknowledges Indigenous alliances, Spanish violence, disease, and institutional conflict rather than treating interpretation as a sufficient cause.
- Malinche's birth, name, childhood, motives, exact words, relationship with Cortes, role in the alleged Cholula warning, and date of death remain uncertain or source-scoped.
- The Macartney branch is based on the episode's selected letters and anecdotes. Exact wording, authorship, interpreter responsibility, court motives, and the causal weight of translation relative to trade, security, and diplomatic institutions require specialist documentary verification.
