# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1105丨关羽之死，埋葬了曹操怎样的梦想？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode offers a compressed retrospective on [[CaoCao|曹操]] from political legitimacy, talent attraction, and [[TuntianSystem|屯田制]] to late reversals in [[WeinanBattleLateHan|渭南之战]], [[Hanzhong|汉中]], and [[GuanYu|关羽]]'s northern campaign. Its closing synthesis shifts from public achievement to household memory: Cao Cao's regret over [[CaoAng|曹昂]] and [[LadyDingCaoCao|丁夫人]], followed by the practical deathbed arrangements remembered as [[FenxiangMailu|分香卖履]].

The episode treats Guan Yu's death and Cao Cao's respectful burial order as a symbolic end to Cao Cao's youthful "征西将军" self-image, but this is an interpretive frame rather than demonstrated private motive. The broader claim is that Cao Cao's rise cannot be reduced to battlefield skill: emperor-backed legitimacy, adviser use, personnel attraction, agricultural logistics, and the ability to convert people into durable capacity matter together.

## Key Claims

- The episode attributes Cao Cao's long rise to military judgment, court-backed legitimacy around [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]], broad talent attraction, and an organizational style that could retain and use capable people.
- In 211 CE, Cao Cao defeats [[MaChao|马超]] and the Guanzhong coalition in [[WeinanBattleLateHan|渭南之战]] after accepting [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s divisive strategy; the anecdotal battlefield dialogue and motive details remain source-scoped.
- The episode says Cao Cao becomes king of Wei in 216 and frames his claim that he would be "周文王" if destiny lay with his house as an implicit transfer of dynastic completion to the next generation.
- [[XiahouYuan|夏侯渊]]'s death and Cao Cao's failed recovery campaign in [[Hanzhong|汉中]] mark a late strategic reversal before Liu Bei becomes king of Hanzhong.
- Guan Yu's northern campaign and Cao Cao's alliance with [[SunQuan|孙权]] turn a direct northern threat into multi-front pressure; after receiving Guan Yu's head, Cao Cao orders respectful burial in the episode's account.
- The source presents renewed talent summons and continued屯田 promotion as evidence that Cao Cao remained focused on personnel and recovery late in life.
- Cao Cao's reported regret over Cao Ang asks how he could answer his dead son about Lady Ding, extending the Wancheng loss from military disaster into unresolved household guilt.
- [[FenxiangMailu|分香卖履]] is interpreted as a practical livelihood arrangement for surviving women, including textile and shoe-making work, but the modern claim that it establishes full economic or spiritual independence remains the host's interpretive extension.

## Key Quotes

> "若天命在吾，吾为周文王矣。" - the episode's dynastic-succession frame for Cao Cao's refusal to complete the imperial transition personally.

> "老骥伏枥，志在千里；烈士暮年，壮心不已。" - the episode's summary of Cao Cao's continued late-life ambition.

> "卖履分香" - the compact phrase used for Cao Cao's final household provisions and attachment to family.

## Contradictions

- No settled wiki contradiction is recorded. The source extends Guan Yu's page beyond the earlier capture, White Horse repayment, and departure arc into the late northern-campaign endpoint.
- Chronology caution: the episode jumps from the 211 Weinan campaign to the 216 kingship, Hanzhong, Guan Yu, and the 220 deathbed without providing a full intervening chronology.
- Source-scope caution: Cao Cao's battlefield dialogue, his words to Guan Yu's severed head, private emotional state, the idea that Guan Yu's burial symbolically buried his "征西将军" dream, and the exact wording of both final statements require broader textual verification.
- Interpretation caution: the livelihood provisions can support a practical-survival reading, but the episode's modern "economic independence leads to spiritual independence" formulation should not be treated as an unqualified late-Han social norm.
