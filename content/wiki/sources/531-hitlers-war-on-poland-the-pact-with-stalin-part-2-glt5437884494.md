# 531. Hitler's War on Poland: The Pact with Stalin (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the final diplomatic sequence before the [[InvasionOfPoland1939|invasion of Poland]]. It follows [[AdolfHitler]] and [[JoachimVonRibbentrop]] through the [[MolotovRibbentropPact|Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact]], coercive demands on Poland, failed efforts to detach Britain from its guarantee, a briefly cancelled attack order, and the [[GleiwitzIncident|Gleiwitz false flag]].

Its central argument is that the pact removed Hitler's immediate fear of a two-front coalition but did not make Britain and France abandon Poland. Hitler repeatedly interpreted delay, appeasement, and the weakness of democratic procedure as evidence that his opponents would retreat again; their declarations of war on 3 September exposed a consequential [[OpponentResolveMiscalculation|misreading of opponent resolve]].

## Key Claims

- Hitler's urgency joined ideological expansion to a personal belief that age, health, and a uniquely favorable moment made delay dangerous.
- [[JosephStalin]] used the [[MolotovRibbentropPact]] to gain time after the Great Terror had damaged Soviet military capacity, while Germany used it to reduce the immediate risk of eastern opposition during an attack on Poland.
- The pact's secret protocol divided spheres of influence across eastern Europe and prepared the political basis for the later German-Soviet partition of Poland.
- Britain reaffirmed its Polish guarantee after the pact, but Hitler treated parliamentary division, Munich, backchannel diplomacy, and prior restraint as evidence that Britain still would not fight.
- Mussolini's warning that Italy was unready helped produce the cancelled 26 August attack, while British and French pressure against early Polish mobilization reduced Poland's preparation time.
- Offers conveyed through Neville Henderson and Birger Dahlerus operated alongside fixed deadlines, extreme territorial demands, and continuing military preparations, making diplomacy increasingly coercive and theatrical.
- The SS-organized [[GleiwitzIncident]] supplied fabricated evidence of Polish aggression immediately before German forces invaded on 1 September 1939.
- Britain's unanswered ultimatum on 3 September turned Hitler's forecast failure into a wider European war.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction was adopted. The source backfills the diplomatic and propagandistic preparation assumed by Part 3 and supports its claim that delayed mobilization worsened Poland's opening position.
- The pact bought the Soviet Union time but did not prevent Germany's later invasion; Stalin's comparative clarity about the pact's temporary character did not protect him from surprise in 1941.
- Hitler's offers to Britain are treated as attempts to isolate Poland rather than proof of a durable peace program, but sincerity and counterfactual British responses remain interpretive.
