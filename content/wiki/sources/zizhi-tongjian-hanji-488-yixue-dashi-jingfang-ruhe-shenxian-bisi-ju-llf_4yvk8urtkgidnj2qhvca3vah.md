## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode explains [[JingFang|京房]]'s death under [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]] as a collision between omen-trained political diagnosis, leaked private speech, [[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]]'s court power, and a reform proposal that threatened the whole bureaucracy. The host argues that Jing Fang's [[KaogongfaReformBacklash|考功法 reform]] made him structurally vulnerable before [[ZhangBoWesternHan|张伯]]'s notes, [[LiuQingHuaiyangKingWesternHan|淮阳王刘清]]'s petition channel, and Shi Xian's accusation gave the case a punishable form. The episode then extends Shi Xian's profile through opponent cleanup, a staged palace-gate trust test, and reputation repair through [[GongYuWesternHan|贡禹]] before closing with [[XunYue|荀悦]]'s warning about distancing flatterers and testing both virtue and ability.

## Key Claims
- Jing Fang's death is presented as tragic but not simply a clean miscarriage of justice: his plan to push official performance assessment challenged protected interests across the court.
- [[KaogongfaReformBacklash|考功法]] is dangerous because it converts vague moral criticism into a system that can measure officials, threaten incumbents, and expose nonperformance.
- Jing Fang's private disclosure to Zhang Bo turns ruler-facing speech into hostile evidence once Zhang Bo records, transmits, or leaks it.
- Liu Qing's possible self-protection and Shi Xian's accusation transform reform advocacy into a conspiracy and slander case involving a regional king.
- Shi Xian's power depends not only on direct imperial trust, but also on retaliatory patience, information control, staged victimhood, and recruitment of respected opinion figures.
- The host uses Xun Yue's comment to argue that governance requires removing corrupting intimates and evaluating appointees by both character and practical ability.
