# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》382-2｜一生做过三位诸侯妃子，古代超美淖姬的悲情人生

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] short episode continues the Zhao-branch material after [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-382-1-bazhan-shumu-hanwudi-zhizi-liudan-you-duo-huangtang-loi-6-s1z2okmskqvbfwryz-qjto|Hanji 382-1]] by shifting from [[LiuDanZhaoPrinceWesternHan|赵太子刘丹]] to [[NuoJiWesternHan|淖姬]], [[LiuPengzuZhaoKingWesternHan|赵敬肃王刘彭祖]], and the unsettled Zhao succession. It traces Nuo Ji's movement from [[LiuFeiJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘非 / 刘飞]] to [[LiuJianJiangduKingWesternHan|江都王刘建]] and then Liu Pengzu, emphasizing how royal-household desire and succession deaths repeatedly expose a consort with little independent protection.

The durable synthesis is that the episode treats beauty, favor, and heir production as precarious resources inside princely households. Nuo Ji's son [[LiuNaoziWesternHan|刘淖子]] loses the chance to inherit Zhao when Nuo Ji's brother tells [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] that he has too many desires; [[LiuChangWushiHouWesternHan|武始侯刘昌]] is chosen instead as the safer, blander candidate. The closing lesson turns this into [[DesireGovernanceFitness|欲望治理适任性]]: desire can drive action, but excessive desire makes a ruler or manager self-serving and unfit to govern others.

## Key Claims

- Nuo Ji is presented as an unusually desired Western Han consort who was favored by three formal princes/kings, but the episode reads this as vulnerability rather than romantic triumph.
- Liu Fei is contrasted with later Jiangdu and Zhao figures as a brave, martial, hero-like prince whose early death removes Nuo Ji's strongest protection.
- Liu Jian inherits Jiangdu and seizes Nuo Ji before Liu Fei's burial, then later kills himself after a rebellion accusation, further showing how consort security depends on male royal power.
- Liu Pengzu is described not only as Liu Dan's father from Hanji 382-1 but also as a harsh and legally cunning Zhao king who obtains Nuo Ji after Liu Jian's death and fathers Liu Naozi with her.
- After Liu Pengzu dies without a settled heir, Han Wudi asks Nuo Ji's eunuch brother about Liu Naozi; the brother's judgment that Liu Naozi has "too many desires" blocks Liu Naozi's succession.
- Han Wudi chooses Wushi marquis Liu Chang because the report says he has no fault and no striking merit, making low-risk ordinariness preferable to desire-heavy ambition in this succession setting.
- The episode's explicit moral takeaway is that people must govern desire before they can govern a life, household, or polity.

## Key Quotes

> "欲望太多" - Nuo Ji's brother's decisive assessment of Liu Naozi.

> "能驾驭好自己的欲望，才能驾驭好自己的人生" - the closing moral lesson drawn from the succession outcome.
