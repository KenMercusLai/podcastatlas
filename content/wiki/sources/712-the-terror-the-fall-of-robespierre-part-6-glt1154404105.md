# 712. The Terror: The Fall of Robespierre (Part 6)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the [[ThermidorianCoup|fall of Maximilien Robespierre]] across 26–27 July 1794 as a contingent struggle rather than an inevitable rejection of terror. The [[LawOf22Prairial|Law of 22 Prairial]], accelerating executions, committee rivalry, personal feuds, economic discontent, and [[MaximilienRobespierre|Robespierre]]'s vague final denunciations aligned former practitioners of revolutionary violence through fear of becoming its next targets.

The attempted resistance then failed through hesitation and lost social support. [[FrancoisHanriot|François Hanriot]] withdrew an armed force that might have overwhelmed the Convention, Robespierre supplied no decisive leadership at the Hôtel de Ville, and workers alienated by wage controls and political suppression did not sustain the Commune. His execution allowed surviving participants to concentrate responsibility for the Terror on him while beginning a broader dismantling of revolutionary government.

## Key Claims

- The Great Terror accelerated after Prairial, with the episode estimating about 1,400 executions in seven weeks, while France's victory at Fleurus weakened the claim of immediate military necessity.
- The [[CommitteeOfPublicSafety|Committee of Public Safety]] combined exhausted administrators, Robespierre's close allies, and radical rivals whose political, religious, and personal conflicts made collective government increasingly fragile.
- Rivalry with the [[CommitteeOfGeneralSecurity|Committee of General Security]], especially around Robespierre's police bureau and the Catherine Théot affair, turned jurisdictional conflict into personal political danger.
- [[JosephFouche|Joseph Fouché]], [[PaulBarras|Paul Barras]], [[JeanLambertTallien|Jean-Lambert Tallien]], and other endangered deputies formed a defensive coalition without needing a shared program beyond survival.
- Robespierre's 26 July speech named few targets while denouncing many conspirators, so uncertainty enlarged rather than intimidated the coalition against him.
- The [[NationalConvention|National Convention]] recovered agency by interrupting Saint-Just, refusing Robespierre the rostrum, voting arrests, declaring the fugitives outlaws, and organizing forces under Barras.
- The Paris Commune's rescue created a real opportunity for insurrection, but Hanriot's retreat from the undefended Convention and Robespierre's indecision allowed its forces and legitimacy to dissipate.
- Economic grievance mattered: wage controls, food shortages, inflation, and suppression of section politics weakened sans-culotte willingness to defend authorities that claimed to speak for them.
- Robespierre's death did not simply transfer power to principled opponents of terror; former participants used his singular villainy to obscure distributed responsibility.
- Thermidor outgrew its plotters' initial intentions as Prairial was repealed, committee power was reduced, the Jacobin Club closed, controls ended, and former terrorists became defendants.

## Key Quotes

> “The blood of Danton is choking him.” - a deputy's reported taunt as Robespierre struggled to speak, as presented by the episode.

> “The Reign of Terror” - retrospective label whose political use expanded after Thermidor, according to the episode.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with Parts 1–5. This episode completes the sequence by showing the coalition anticipated in Parts 4–5 acting successfully and then losing control of the reaction it began.
- Robespierre was central to terror's moral and legal defence but was neither its sole architect nor the only enthusiastic participant; his opponents' own records of violence qualify the later singular-villain narrative.
- The episode treats Hanriot's withdrawal as decisive and an immediate assault as potentially successful, but this remains a counterfactual judgment rather than an established outcome.
- Robespierre's broken jaw is most likely attributed to a failed suicide attempt, while a Convention soldier's shot remains a competing account.
- Execution totals, private conversations, intended purge lists, motives, speech preparation, crowd attitudes, exact timings, wage effects, and claims about the origin and spread of “Reign of Terror” remain source-scoped.
