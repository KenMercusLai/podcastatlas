# 323-AI是否可以取代翻译？错误的翻译如何塑造现实？

## Summary
This [[DushuBuchenglin|独树不成林]] episode separates machine-level fluency from [[ConceptualTranslationJudgment|conceptual translation judgment]]. It accepts that [[AITranslation]] can perform much routine conversion and proofreading, but argues that academic translation also requires choices about history, ontology, political meaning, and the relationships among traditions.

Using [[YanFu|严复]] and terms such as power/right, Geist, liberty, economy, evolution, and revolution, the episode develops [[TranslationPathDependence|translation path dependence]]: a rendering may become standard because it spreads and stabilizes, not because it is uniquely accurate. Once established, terminology can guide how later readers understand freedom, rights, wealth, progress, and political change.

## Key Claims
- AI can replace a large share of mechanical translation and can help human translators catch grammatical or obvious errors, but fluent output does not settle contested conceptual choices.
- [[ConceptualTranslationJudgment]] asks what a term does within a source tradition and what associations a target-language rendering activates; it therefore has no purely mechanical answer.
- Models trained on accumulated language can reproduce majority usage, inherited errors, and biases, making AI an amplifier of existing translation conventions as well as a useful tool.
- [[TranslationPathDependence]] explains why established renderings can defeat more precise alternatives through familiarity, circulation, institutional use, and historical timing.
- The source uses power/right and liberty/license to argue that translation can blur normative distinctions and shape public interpretations of political concepts.
- [[YanFu|严复]] is presented as a losing but still valuable participant in terminology competition: renderings such as “群己权界” and “演” preserve distinctions that common usage may obscure.
- Reopening a translation does not require replacing the standard term; comparison can expose hidden assumptions and expand the range of interpretation.

## Key Quotes
> "群己权界" - Yan Fu's compact rendering of the boundary between individual and collective liberty.

> "演" - the source's example of change without a built-in promise of progress.

> "判断上的准确性" - the episode's name for the non-mechanical standard at stake.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction is adopted. The episode extends the wiki's practical account of [[AITranslation]] by distinguishing routine translation capability from historically situated conceptual judgment.
- Claims about Japanese-coined Chinese terms, Buddhist mediation, dialect pronunciation, title histories, and the causal effect of particular translations on Chinese political thought are interpretive claims from the episode and remain source-scoped pending linguistic or primary-source corroboration.
- The episode presents contested alternatives rather than uniquely correct replacements. “精神/心智,” “自由/群己权界,” and “进化/演化” highlight different semantic relations, so the source does not establish one universally valid rendering.
