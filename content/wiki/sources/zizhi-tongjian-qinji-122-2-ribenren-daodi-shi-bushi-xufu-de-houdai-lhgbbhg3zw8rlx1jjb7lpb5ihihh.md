## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]]'s 219 BCE eastern tour from sacred mountain ritual into sea-facing political theater. It covers the worship of [[QiEightSpirits|齐地八神]], the three-month stay at [[LangyaMountain|琅琊山]], the [[LangyaInscriptionPoliticalProgram|琅琊刻石政治纲领]], and the turn toward [[XuFu|徐福]]'s search for immortal medicine. The episode's main source-critical point is that Japanese Xu Fu legends, shrines, and local stories matter as reception history, but they do not prove that Japanese people are Xu Fu's descendants.

## Key Claims
- Qin Shi Huang's eastern tour keeps working as [[ImperialTourPoliticalTheater|帝国巡游政治剧场]] after the Taishan fengshan ceremony: sacrifice, migration, inscription, and long residence at Langya all make Qin sovereignty visible in former Qi territory.
- Worshiping the [[QiEightSpirits|齐地八神]] is read as a claim over the old [[QiState|齐国]] religious landscape, not only as private piety.
- The Langya inscription restates Qin's post-unification order: agriculture over commerce, unified writing and measures, legal compliance, peace after unification, and imperial authority over distant lands and living beings.
- Yan-Qi fangshi convert the emperor's death anxiety into a funded sea project, turning immortal-island lore around [[Penglai|蓬莱]] into personnel, ships, seeds, craftsmen, and political risk.
- Xu Fu's failure story follows the pattern of [[FangshiFraudAndAuthority|fangshi fraud and authority]]: unverifiable access to divine beings explains failure and demands more resources.
- The source separates the early [[Shiji|《史记》]] account from later overseas-kingship and Japan-descent legends. The host says Shiji does not record Xu Fu founding a foreign kingdom and says she tends to accept the Shiji version that Xu Fu returned.
- Japan's Xu Fu shrines, graves, local texts, and research associations are treated as evidence of legend and cultural memory, not as proof of broad ethnic descent.
- The closing examples of Tang emperors harmed by elixir seeking broaden the point of [[ImmortalityQuestPolitics|求仙政治]]: rulers with great power are especially vulnerable to death anxiety.

## Key Quotes
> "重农抑商" - the episode's summary of one Langya inscription theme.

> "秦人徐福来" - the wording the episode cites from a Japanese historical text.

> "一去不复返" - the moralizing coda's later shorthand for Xu Fu's sea-search story.
