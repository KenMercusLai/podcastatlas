# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》931丨人生艰难处，便是修心时

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 184-185 CE late-Han layer immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-932-weishenme-ren-yue-hao-yue-nan-dang-daguan-lvwzel782wcxs1lpt3rg2vmkdy29|Hanji 932]]. It opens with the host's adversity-and-self-cultivation frame, then follows [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] discovering correspondence between [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]]'s retainers and the Yellow Turban side, reporting it to [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], suffering Zhang Rang's retaliation, and refusing private suicide in prison.

The source's core contribution is Wang Yun's early official-integrity layer before his later anti-[[DongZhuo|董卓]] role. The episode treats Wang Yun's prison stance as a case where hardship clarifies rather than dissolves role identity: death is less important than whether the death preserves public official meaning, while the closing 185 CE epidemic, fire, famine, and revolt notes place individual resolve inside accelerating late-Han breakdown.

## Key Claims

- The opening says people can slide from youthful ambition into resentment and passivity after unfairness, but "ordinary" life should not be confused with real mediocrity.
- The host frames hardship as a test of [[VirtuePracticeInternalization|修养内化]]: one has to notice emotional movement, ask where it comes from, and transform it rather than only blame the environment.
- In 184 CE, [[WangYunLateHan|王允]] is introduced as the Taiyuan man serving as Yu Province inspector after the Yellow Turban fighting.
- Wang Yun defeats the Yellow Turban side and finds letters linking [[ZhangRangLateHan|张让]]'s retainers to the rebels.
- Wang Yun reports the letters to [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], who angrily scolds Zhang Rang but does not seriously investigate or punish him.
- Zhang Rang then counterattacks by accusing Wang Yun, leading to Wang Yun's imprisonment; a general amnesty releases and restores him, but he is arrested again ten days later on another charge.
- The episode presents Emperor Ling as personally attached to Zhang Rang and [[ZhaoZhongLateHan|赵忠]], making a report about a favorite's network difficult to convert into real action.
- Wang Yun suffers torture and humiliation in prison; the source says [[YangCiLateHan|杨赐]] is distressed by the suffering, and Wang Yun's younger staff members cry and bring poison.
- Wang Yun refuses to take poison, arguing that if a subject has offended the ruler he should await formal court execution and public announcement rather than dying privately.
- This refusal makes Wang Yun an early case for [[OfficeDutyMartyrdom|守官赴死]] and [[MingqiLegitimacy|名器合法性]]: even under injustice, the office-holder's death should not be privatized into quiet disposal.
- [[HeJin|何进]], [[YangCiLateHan|杨赐]], and [[YuanWeiLateHan|袁隗]] rescue Wang Yun from the death edge, showing that his reputation and political weight already matter before the later succession crisis.
- The source characterizes Wang Yun as ambitious, morally exacting, classically trained, and practiced in riding and archery; [[GuoTaiLateHan|郭泰]] is quoted as praising his capacity to assist a ruler.
- In 185 CE, epidemic spreads widely; the episode says the late-Han period saw repeated outbreaks from 171 to 185.
- The same closing sequence links epidemic, palace fires in [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]], famine, refugees, war, peasant uprisings, and local rebellions as mutually reinforcing forces in late-Han decline.

## Key Quotes

> "平凡并不是平庸" - the host's distinction between ordinary life and self-abandoning stagnation.

> "越是身处艰难，越是修心时" - the episode's opening lesson and title frame.

> "死并不是难事" - the host's reading of Wang Yun's refusal to take poison.

> "百姓不能饿肚子，一饿肚子天下就要乱" - the closing link between famine and political disorder.
