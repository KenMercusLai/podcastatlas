# 649. The Fall of the Incas: The Last Emperor (Part 6)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the final phase of the [[SpanishConquestOfPeru|Spanish conquest of Peru]] after Atahualpa's death. [[FranciscoPizarro]] and [[DiegoDeAlmagro]] destroy their rival factions through negotiation, betrayal, battle, execution, and assassination, while [[MancoInca]] repeatedly escapes pursuit and sustains an independent resistance from the [[NeoIncaState|Neo-Inca state in Vilcabamba]].

The ending widens from elite conflict to colonial consequence. Indigenous allies remain indispensable to Spanish victory, encomienda and mining embed coerced labor, epidemic disease combines with war and infrastructure collapse in a demographic catastrophe, and [[BartolomeDeLasCasas|Bartolomé de las Casas]] represents contemporary Spanish criticism. Vilcabamba survives Manco until [[TupacAmaruI|Túpac Amaru I]] is captured and executed in 1572.

## Key Claims

- Spanish victory did not produce immediate unity: control fractured into a civil war among Pizarro, Almagro, their heirs, and royal officials.
- Lima's access to reinforcements, supplies, communications, and the royal court gave the Pizarro faction a structural advantage over Almagro's position in Cusco.
- Indigenous forces were not passive spectators; Paullu Inca, Huanca, Cañari, and other allies were militarily important to Spanish factions, while Manco also attacked communities aligned with them.
- Manco's mobile resistance survived repeated pursuit and established a durable refuge in Vilcabamba, but his 1544 murder largely ended the possibility of broad Indigenous military recovery described by the episode.
- The New Laws reveal that colonial abuse was criticized within Spain, while settler revolt under Gonzalo Pizarro shows how difficult metropolitan restraint was to enforce.
- Disease, civil war, food-system destruction, forced labor, displacement, and violence interacted in Andean demographic collapse; the episode disputes a simple intentional-genocide explanation without minimizing catastrophe.
- Potosí connected coercive Andean mining to Spanish, European, and global monetary consequences.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is adopted. The episode's rejection of “genocide” as the best label is retained as an interpretive qualification: disease may explain most deaths without excluding Spanish violence, coercion, dispossession, or responsibility for conditions that worsened mortality.
- Force totals, death estimates, remembered speeches, intimate motives, and the upper estimate of eight million Potosí deaths remain episode-attributed rather than independently verified here.
- The episode identifies Vilcabamba with Espíritu Pampa; archaeological identification, exact Neo-Inca territorial control, and claims that longer-lived diplomacy could have preserved an independent state remain source-scoped.
