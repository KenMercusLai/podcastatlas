# Analysis, Parapraxis, Elvis

## Summary

This 2018 [[RevisionistHistory]] episode uses [[SigmundFreud|Freud]]'s concept of [[Parapraxis]] to ask whether repeated mistakes in [[ElvisPresley]]'s performances reveal emotional pressure that polished delivery normally contains. Its strongest evidence is a reported analysis of ten live recordings of [[AreYouLonesomeTonight]]: Elvis made 109 errors in the spoken bridges, with many clustering around betrayal, rejection, and emptiness and becoming most severe after his separation from Priscilla Presley.

Live experiments with [[JackWhite]] and [[CaseyBeathardBowles]] extend the argument beyond retrospective biography. Their substitutions and memory lapses suggest that error can accompany care, identification, and emotionally charged material, while rhythmic difficulty, fatigue, health, intoxication, habit, and interpretive overreach prevent any single lapse from proving an unconscious cause.

## Key Claims

- [[Parapraxis]] treats slips, misreadings, and memory failures as potentially meaningful expressions of ideas or feelings that conscious control does not fully contain.
- [[ElvisPresley]]'s substitution of “struck” for “stroked” in “Old Shep” changes consolation into guilt, but the episode's biographical explanation remains interpretive.
- Elvis generally remembered songs well yet repeatedly failed in the exposed spoken bridge of [[AreYouLonesomeTonight]], not its more composed sung questions.
- Alan Elms and Bruce Heller reportedly found 109 bridge errors across ten recordings, with 29 concentrated around love, changed behavior, rejection, and emptiness.
- The worst reported performances followed Priscilla Presley's 1972 departure, making betrayal and abandonment a plausible context without establishing a unique cause.
- [[JackWhite]]'s “carefully” substitution and [[CaseyBeathardBowles]]'s repeated loss of a lyric about her mother show that error can reflect emotional investment rather than indifference.
- [[MalcolmGladwell]] turns the inquiry toward his own fear of losing control and argues that a meaningful mistake can disclose the performer behind a polished public role.
- Psychoanalytic meaning remains underdetermined because rhythm, fatigue, illness, drug use, performance habit, and retrospective selection can also shape mistakes.
