## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the Hongjia 3-4 / 18-17 BCE [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] period through [[ZhengGongWesternHan|郑公]]'s Guanghan uprising and a contemporaneous [[YellowRiver|黄河]] flood. It treats Zheng Gong's self-title "山君" as a remembered root for later "山大王" language, while placing the revolt in a court environment marked by Wang-family dominance, eunuch disorder, official failure, heavy burdens, hunger, and "官逼民反."

The second half shifts from rebellion to flood policy. After the Yellow River floods Bohai, Qinghe, and Xindu and damages dozens of counties and tens of thousands of buildings, [[LiXunFloodControlWesternHan|李勋]] advises [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]] to suspend forced repair, watch the river's direction, and later dredge or shape a more natural channel. Chengdi accepts the non-repair advice and sends aid to disaster victims, leaving a tension between hydraulic patience and immediate relief.

## Key Claims

- Zheng Gong gathers followers in Guanghan, attacks government offices, frees prisoners, seizes weapons, and calls himself "山君."
- The episode reads the revolt as a survival rebellion produced by official oppression, burdens, empty granaries, hunger, displacement, and mass death.
- Zheng Gong's force expands by 17 BCE, attacks four counties, and approaches ten thousand people before the court mobilizes more than thirty thousand troops from Guanghan and Shu.
- The court combines force with amnesty and defection incentives; some rebels capture or kill former companions, and [[ZhaoHuWesternHan|赵护]] is rewarded after the uprising is suppressed within about a month.
- In the same broad period, the Yellow River floods Bohai, Qinghe, and Xindu, submerges 31 counties, and destroys more than 40,000 official pavilions and houses.
- Li Xun argues against hasty artificial repair, recommending that the court first observe the river's self-formed flow and later shape the channel with less waste of time, money, and labor.
- Chengdi accepts Li Xun's hydraulic advice while also sending envoys to settle and assist victims, so the source keeps disaster governance split between long-term river judgment and immediate human suffering.

## Key Quotes
