# 515. Nelson: Attack the French! (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]] from the outbreak of war with revolutionary France in 1793 through Mediterranean operations, the [[SiegeOfToulon|fall of Toulon]], campaigns in Corsica, his first fleet action, and Britain's 1796 withdrawal toward Gibraltar. It presents his later tactical aggression as inseparable from provisioning, sailor health, religious observance, recruitment, morale, and close-range gunnery, while [[NapoleonBonaparte|Napoleon Bonaparte]]'s Italian victories expose the limits of British [[SeaControlStateCapacity|sea control]] against continental success.

The episode also supplies the immediate prehistory of Cape St Vincent. [[JohnJervis|John Jervis]] replaced a cautious Mediterranean command with stricter discipline and an appetite for action; Spain then joined France, Nelson formed an early bond with [[ThomasMastermanHardy|Thomas Masterman Hardy]], and he carried news of the Spanish fleet to Jervis before the [[BattleOfCapeStVincent1797|Battle of Cape St Vincent]].

## Key Claims

- Nelson prepared *Agamemnon* through detailed attention to food, drink, water, citrus, religious books, recruitment, crew health, and morale, making logistics part of combat readiness rather than a background concern.
- Britain's early war strategy relied on a large [[RoyalNavy]] and subsidies to continental allies, but leaders underestimated the duration and scale of the revolutionary conflict.
- Toulon's royalists transferred the naval base to Admiral Hood in exchange for protection; Napoleon's artillery on the harbor heights helped force the British withdrawal, after which revolutionary forces retook the city violently.
- Nelson's visible leadership at Bastia and Calvi strengthened authority while exposing him to injury; debris at Calvi destroyed the sight in his right eye, an injury he minimized rather than officially foregrounding.
- Against *Ca Ira*, Nelson used close-range broadsides and British gunnery to attack a larger ship, then condemned Admiral Hotham's refusal to pursue the remaining French fleet as a lost chance for decisive victory.
- Jervis tightened discipline and encouraged aggressive captains, but Napoleon's Italian campaign closed ports, overturned allied positions, and helped force Britain out of Corsica and the Mediterranean in 1796.
- Shared danger during the Elba withdrawal established Nelson's bond with Hardy before Nelson passed through the Spanish fleet in fog and warned Jervis of its approach.
