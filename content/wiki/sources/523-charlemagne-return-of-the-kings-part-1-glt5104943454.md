# 523. Charlemagne: Return of the Kings (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the political and religious inheritance that preceded [[Charlemagne]]'s conquests. [[CharlesMartel]] converted palace-mayoral office into territorial and military dominance while weakening the old Gallo-Roman episcopal elite; [[PepinTheShort|Pepin the Short]] then used papal approval and [[RoyalAnointingTradition|royal anointing]] to replace the symbolically royal but politically weak [[MerovingianDynasty]].

The second strand explains the [[PapalFrankishAlliance]] as a reciprocal response to vulnerability. As Byzantine protection receded and Lombard power threatened Rome, popes sought Frankish military protection; Pepin gained sacred validation, defeated the Lombards, and transferred conquered Italian territory to Saint Peter. When his son Charles became sole king in 771, the military, dynastic, and ecclesiastical foundations of later western empire were already in place.

## Key Claims

- [[Einhard]]'s portrait of Chilperic III distinguishes Merovingian royal ceremony from the effective authority exercised by the mayors of the palace, while also serving Carolingian retrospective legitimation.
- Charles Martel expanded Frankish military reach, subordinated independent bishops, confiscated church wealth, and helped shift sacred and cultural influence from older Gallo-Roman episcopal families toward ruler-dependent clergy and monasteries.
- Papal respect did not yet amount to later medieval command: Rome remained inside the eastern Roman imperial order and depended on Byzantine protection even as that protection weakened.
- Lombard capture of Ravenna in 751 made a Frankish protector increasingly attractive to the papacy.
- Pope Zachary's answer to Pepin's question about powerless kingship supplied religious approval for deposing Chilperic III; anointing translated military control into consecrated kingship.
- Pope Stephen II's journey, second anointing of Pepin, and praise of the Franks as a chosen people strengthened both Carolingian rule and a Frankish sacred mission.
- Pepin's campaigns against the Lombards joined protection of Rome to territorial grants for Saint Peter, while his wider conquests and reforms supplied Charles with an unusually strong inheritance.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with existing wiki content is adopted. The episode supplies the missing prehistory for later accounts of Charlemagne's conquest and coronation.
- Charles Martel's attack on independent episcopal power coexists with later papal outreach to him; “the Church” did not act as one unified political interest.
- Papal approval strengthened Pepin's accession but did not by itself create his military control, and Frankish protection did not yet make popes sovereign in the later high-medieval sense.
- Einhard's Merovingian portrait, the Eucharius dragon story, Carloman's motive for monastic retirement, exact papal calculations, Stephen's chosen-people language, and straight-line claims from these events to the empire of 800 remain source-scoped or interpretive.
