## Summary
This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the origins and opening landings of [[GallipoliCampaign|the Gallipoli campaign]], from [[WinstonChurchill|Winston Churchill]]'s Dardanelles naval scheme through the failed March 1915 attack and the 25 April landings at Anzac Cove and Cape Helles. It presents the operation as strategically tempting on paper but weakened by wishful assumptions, inadequate scrutiny, and escalation after failure.

The episode's strongest wiki contribution is the prehistory of the later calamity covered in [[676-the-first-world-war-churchills-calamity-part-6-glt1275431911]]. It adds the Ottoman entry into the [[FirstWorldWar]], [[EnverPasha]]'s Caucasus disaster and Russian diversion request, [[JackieFisher|Admiral Lord Fisher]]'s ignored warnings, [[IanHamiltonGallipoli|Ian Hamilton]]'s constrained landing command, and [[MustafaKemalAtaturk|Mustafa Kemal / Ataturk]] stopping the ANZAC advance on the first day.

## Key Claims
- Churchill sought a bold eastern alternative to Western Front stalemate by using naval power to force the Dardanelles, threaten Constantinople, open a route to Russia, and possibly knock the [[OttomanEmpire|Ottoman Empire]] out of the war.
- The [[DardanellesStrategicShortcut]] had known hazards: Ottoman forts, artillery, mines, narrow waters, prior British warnings, and the basic problem that ships could not hold territory.
- The War Council approved the naval expedition on 13 January 1915 in an atmosphere of optimism, weak challenge, and incomplete presentation of Admiralty objections.
- The March 18 naval attack failed badly when mines sank or disabled Allied ships, but British leaders escalated into landings rather than abandoning the operation.
- Hamilton had about a month to plan an unprecedented amphibious landing against modern weapons while Ottoman forces strengthened the peninsula.
- The episode rejects the assumption that Ottoman troops would collapse; they were battle-hardened, defending home territory, and prepared under Otto Liman von Sanders.
- The ANZAC landing at Anzac Cove and the British landings at Cape Helles produced extraordinary courage but only limited footholds, heavy casualties, and rapid movement into trench warfare.
- Mustafa Kemal's intervention against retreating Ottoman troops helped stop the ANZAC breakthrough and became part of Turkish nationalist memory.
- The episode presents Gallipoli as foundational for Australian and New Zealand identity and significant for modern Turkish memory, while keeping the campaign's disastrous planning visible.
