# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》99-3｜荀子兵法里藏着人性的秘密

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode develops the [[LinwuJun|临武君]] debate previewed in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-2-mingming-yilian-baixiang-de-caize-ping-shenme-baixiang-lhfyb6hzcqkpbzdrwsxajb7hhmhe|Qinji 99-2]]. Before [[ZhaoDan|赵丹 / 赵孝成王]], Linwu Jun treats military success as a matter of timing, terrain, speed, and deception, while [[Xunzi|荀子]] argues that the deepest basis of victory is unified people-support and ruler-subject alignment.

The source turns Xunzi's military argument into a hierarchy of armies: reward-and-punishment forces such as [[QinState|秦国]] can be very strong, but profit-driven soldiers and deceptive tactics remain below hegemonic armies and below the fully legitimate [[XunzianMilitaryLegitimacy|renyi army]]. The closing "五权" and "三制" section extends the same thought into command ethics: a general must judge risk independently, resist empty victory-chasing, and refuse orders that endanger the army or abuse the people.

## Key Claims
- Linwu Jun defines military success through天时, 地利, speed, enemy-movement reading, and deceptive maneuver.
- Xunzi rejects that as insufficient and says ancient military method depends on making the people one: bow and arrow, horses, soldiers, and civilians must all coordinate before force can be reliable.
- The episode uses [[ZaoFu|造父]] to connect Zhao's origin story to [[QinFeizi|非子]], making the Zhao/Qin horse-and-lineage aside part of the debate setting rather than the main argument.
- Linwu Jun invokes [[SunziBingfa|《孙子兵法》]]-style deception and [[WuQi|吴起]]-style military success to argue that battlefield technique matters more than popular support.
- Xunzi answers that deception works only against loose, disordered, careless, or divided forces; against a well-ordered renyi community it becomes useless.
- A legitimate ruler's people become "ears and eyes": they report plots, leak hostile secrets, and deprive tyrants of the hidden information that deception requires.
- The episode compares army types in rising order: Qi's技击, Wei's武卒, Qin's锐士, the hegemon armies of [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]] and [[JinWenGong|晋文公]], and the renyi armies of [[ShangTang|商汤]] and [[ZhouWuwang|周武王]].
- Qin's strength is treated as real, accumulated, and institutionally built through harsh geography, surveillance, reward, punishment, and status incentives; Xunzi's point is that this remains below kingly legitimacy.
- Xunzi criticizes armies built mainly from recruitment, selection, rewards, and profit incentives as unstable "盗兵" that can win and lose but cannot produce the highest loyalty.
- The host applies the same distinction to modern organizations: a group held only by incentives is a gang-like aggregate, while shared culture can make it a team; this is a source-scoped analogy rather than chronicle content.
- The "五权" section asks generals not to cling to position, rush after victory, chase internal prestige, see benefit without harm, or deliberate carelessly while being stingy with resources.
- The "三制" section says a general may refuse the ruler's order rather than station troops in dangerous terrain, fight an unwinnable battle, or bully the people.

## Key Quotes
> "上下同欲" - the episode's compressed version of Xunzi's military principle.

> "盗兵" - Xunzi's label for armies joined mainly by recruitment, selection, and reward incentives.

> "宁可被杀" - the closing command-ethics formula behind the three refusals.
