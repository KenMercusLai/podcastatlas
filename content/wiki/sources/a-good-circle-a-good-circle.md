# A Good Circle: Hope College’s Radical Experiment in Generosity

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode follows [[MattScogin]] and [[HopeCollege]] as they test [[HopeForward]], a tuition-free covenant in which participating students promise to give voluntarily after graduation without a prescribed amount. The model joins [[DebtConstrainedEducationAndCareerChoice]] to [[VoluntaryPayItForwardEducation]]: shifting financial risk away from individual students may widen educational and career choice, but the institution must first finance the transition and later depend on alumni gratitude rather than enforceable repayment.

[[MitchAlbom]] supplies the episode's moral comparison through Morrie Schwartz and a Haitian orphanage where care received creates an expectation of later care for others. The resulting “good circle” broadens repayment beyond money to service and responsibility, while the small cohort, roughly $1 billion transition estimate, uncertain donation stream, and Hope College's Christian culture keep the experiment from serving as a proven general blueprint.

## Key Claims

- [[HopeForward]] students pay no tuition and sign a covenant rather than a legally enforceable repayment contract; they promise to take their education seriously and give something annually after graduation.
- The college specifies no donation amount because it wants later contributions to remain gifts rather than function as income-contingent loan payments.
- [[DebtConstrainedEducationAndCareerChoice]] can arise when tuition debt pushes students toward higher-paying fields and jobs instead of service, ministry, philosophy, classics, or other personally meaningful paths.
- [[HopeCollege]] receives about $55 million in tuition annually and would need roughly $1 billion to extend the model across the institution, according to the episode.
- Early participants entered with slightly lower academic credentials but reportedly outperformed non-participants after their first year; the small, selected cohort does not establish long-term academic or financial effects.
- [[VoluntaryPayItForwardEducation]] transfers risk from students to the institution and donor community while giving the college a stronger incentive to make education valuable enough to inspire future support.
- [[MitchAlbom]]'s orphanage example treats reciprocity as service and care as well as money: beneficiaries support younger children, serve the surrounding community, and return after college for two years of work.
