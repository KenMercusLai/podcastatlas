# 55. The World Cup of Gods - Preview

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode launches the [[WorldCupOfGods]], a listener-voted mock tournament among sixteen historical deities and deified people. Its comic sporting frame introduces [[Anubis]], [[OctavianAugustus|Augustus]], [[BrigidOfKildare|Brigid]], [[Cybele]], [[IshtarInanna|Ishtar]], [[Moloch]], [[PrincePhilip]], [[XipeTotec]], [[Mithras]], [[Zeus]], [[Odin]], [[IsisGoddess|Isis]], [[Dionysus]], [[ApolloGod|Apollo]], [[Loki]], and [[Athena]], while the later tournament episodes supply the fuller religious history.

The preview's main historical contribution is its deliberately unstable eligibility rule. Major continuously worshipped gods are excluded, but a revived cult such as modern Odin worship does not necessarily disqualify a figure, and the field includes disputed deities, a deified emperor, a saint-goddess boundary case, and a modern royal worshipped on Tanna. The tournament therefore turns continuity, revival, category, fame, and audience preference into visible parts of how ancient divine identities are selected and remembered.

## Key Claims

- The field is limited to gods whose historical worship is treated as discontinued, while later revival or small-scale worship may remain eligible.
- The rule avoids direct competition involving major living religions but does not produce a neutral or globally representative sample of divinity.
- The unseeded field deliberately mixes ancient cult figures with [[OctavianAugustus|Augustus]] and [[PrincePhilip]], making the boundary between deity, deified ruler, saintly figure, and modern local worship part of the joke.
- Seeding reflects recognizability, host judgment, celebrity endorsement, and anticipated fan support rather than a formal measure of historical importance.
- The sports format uses imagined matchups, predictions, and style clashes to turn a compressed mythology survey into participatory public history.
- Twitter voting makes the event evidence of modern reception and fandom, not of ancient religious prestige or devotion.
