# 563. Peter the Great: Bloodbath in the Kremlin (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[PeterTheGreat|Peter the Great]] from youthful military play, maritime curiosity, and grotesque court ritual through the [[GreatEmbassy|Great Embassy]] and into coercive reform. Practical study in Dutch and English shipyards, recruitment of foreign specialists, and interest in science and government become inseparable from [[CoerciveWesternization|forced Westernization]], removal of his wife Eudokia, and the destruction of the [[Streltsy]]. The closing diplomatic pivot from the Ottomans toward [[SwedishEmpire|Sweden]] supplies the immediate prehistory of the [[GreatNorthernWar|Great Northern War]].

## Key Claims

- Peter's early court combined hands-on interest in soldiers, tools, ships, foreign communities, and Protestant Europe with heavy drinking, ritual parody, physical humiliation, and exemplary violence.
- The Great Embassy was both diplomacy and apprenticeship: Peter sought anti-Ottoman support and recruited expertise while personally studying shipbuilding, factories, anatomy, collections, coinage, and maritime institutions.
- Dutch practical shipbuilding disappointed Peter because it relied less on systematic plans, helping redirect him to England for more formal instruction and technical recruitment.
- Peter's admiration for English commerce, science, Parliament, and open speech did not imply acceptance of limits on his own monarchy.
- On returning to Russia, Peter made Westernization visible through compulsory beard shaving, clothing rules, calendar reform, coinage ambitions, and the removal of Eudokia, joining cultural reform to personal command.
- The Streltsy mutiny became the pretext for torture, mass execution, Sophia's final confinement, and the dissolution of an old military-political institution.
- [[AugustusTheStrong|Augustus the Strong]]'s proposal to partition Sweden's Baltic possessions helped turn Peter away from the Black Sea; after a truce with the [[OttomanEmpire]], Russia declared war on Sweden in 1700.

## Key Quotes

> "carpenter Peter" - the name Peter asked to use while working in the Amsterdam shipyard.

> "burning the disease out" - the episode's paraphrase of Peter's defense of violence against the Streltsy.
