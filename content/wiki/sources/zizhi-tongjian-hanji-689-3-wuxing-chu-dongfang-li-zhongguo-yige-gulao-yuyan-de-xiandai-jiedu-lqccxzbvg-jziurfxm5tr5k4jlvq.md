# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》689-3丨五星出东方，立中国：一个古老预言的现代解读

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses the [[FiveStarsOutOfTheEastBrocade|五星出东方织锦]] from the [[NiyaSite|尼雅遗址]] to connect archaeology, ancient astronomy, Western Han frontier war, and the later memory of [[JingjueKingdom|精绝古国]]. Its main synthesis is that the same formula could operate as astronomical omen, military slogan, diplomatic gift or warning, and retrospective national memory, while its strongest readings remain source-scoped rather than proven.

## Key Claims
- The Niya find is framed as a 1995 discovery from a likely late-Eastern-Han royal joint tomb at the Niya site in Minfeng County, Hotan, Xinjiang.
- The brocade or armguard carried the reported text "五星出东方，立中国"; a broken fragment with "讨南羌" lets the source reconstruct a campaign-facing phrase.
- The episode connects "五星出东方" to the ancient reading of five visible planets appearing in the east as an auspicious alignment, with Sima Qian and Han textual astronomy as the interpretive frame.
- The brocade's craft details, right-arm placement, and no-overseas-exhibition status make it both a material artifact and a political-symbolic object.
- The extended phrase is linked to the 63 BCE crisis in which Qiang groups influenced by [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] planned to cut the Han-Western-Regions route, and [[ZhaoChongguoWesternHan|赵充国]] volunteered under [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]].
- The Han-court gift or warning interpretation for Western Regions kings is plausible in the episode but not treated as settled proof.
- Jingjue's later decline is explained through loss of Han protection, regional annexation pressure, ecological drying around the Niya River, and long-term invasion pressure rather than omen failure alone.

## Key Quotes
> "五星出东方，立中国" - the episode's reported wording for the brocade text.

> "讨南羌" - the broken trailing fragment used to connect the artifact to a frontier-campaign formula.

> "今五星出东方，中国大利，蛮夷大败" - the omen formula quoted around Emperor Xuan and Zhao Chongguo.
