# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》502丨让老板被黑锅？是谁在找死！

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills Jianshi 1 / 32 BCE under [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]], immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-503-gei-wangjia-zhanglian-jiemi-xihan-shili-zuidade-waiqi-lgjgpal0s0he4qrwwhdrdbmp8aut|Hanji 503]]'s Wang-family enfeoffment and early-reign omen criticism. It follows the post-[[ShiXianWesternHan|石显]] cleanup: [[KuangHengWesternHan|匡衡]] rises as a favored new-court Confucian figure after reporting Shi Xian, while [[WangZunWesternHan|王尊]] impeaches Kuang Heng and [[ZhangTanWesternHan|张谭]] for having accommodated Shi Xian during [[HanYuandi|汉元帝]]'s reign and then shifting blame toward the deceased emperor after Chengdi's accession.

The durable synthesis is that early-reign cleanup is not only a moral reckoning. It creates office vacancies, exposes dangerous speech, and forces a new ruler to choose between punishing old-regime accommodation and preserving court stability. Chengdi's decision to reassure Kuang Heng while demoting Wang Zun shows [[CourtTransitionBlameManagement|新君清算中的责任安排]]: the ruler can stop an accusation that may be partly reasonable if it threatens senior-office continuity, predecessor dignity, and the supply of ministers who can absorb future disaster responsibility.

## Key Claims

- The host frames the episode through restraint after success: people in exposed positions must know when to lower their heads and when speech creates needless danger.
- Shi Xian's fall creates a cleanup and opportunity field because his former power, offices, and benefits become newly available to competing actors.
- Kuang Heng benefits from the new reign and from his anti-Shi-Xian report, but Wang Zun attacks him and Zhang Tan for having failed to confront Shi Xian when Shi Xian was powerful.
- Wang Zun's more dangerous charge is not only past inaction before the amnesty; it is that Kuang Heng and Zhang Tan explained Shi Xian's dominance by saying officials feared Shi Xian more than the emperor, thereby making Emperor Yuan look responsible.
- Kuang Heng responds with fear, shame, and formal resignation gestures, returning his chancellor and marquis seals rather than trying to argue aggressively.
- Chengdi does not remove Kuang Heng. The host reads this as early-reign stabilizing judgment: removing the chancellor and Yushi Dafu at once would unsettle the court before Chengdi's own authority was consolidated.
- Keeping Kuang Heng also preserves a responsibility-absorbing senior minister: when floods, droughts, or omens appear, the chancellor can take visible fault before responsibility reaches the ruler.
- Wang Zun's demotion does not erase the force of his argument; the episode treats the outcome as a choice about political costs, not a simple verdict that Wang Zun was wrong.
