## Summary
This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode follows [[StephenSmithBuildingPolicy|Stephen Smith]]'s argument that the United States has unusually few and unusually expensive elevators because specialized labor practices and large-cabin requirements raise the cost and space needed for installation. It treats this as an [[ElevatorAvailabilityAccessibilityTradeoff]]: a standard that improves performance for rare stretcher emergencies can reduce everyday access when small apartment projects omit elevators entirely.

The institutional case is [[GregoryAVictor|Gregory A. Victor]]'s successful proposal to require space for a seven-foot stretcher in qualifying elevators. Because the [[InternationalCodeCouncil]]'s model code is adopted widely, the episode argues that one amendment with a declared cost impact of “None” could influence housing economics far beyond its originating jurisdiction through [[ModelBuildingCodeDiffusion]].

## Key Claims
- [[StephenSmithBuildingPolicy|Stephen Smith]] says he could identify no high-income country with fewer elevators per capita than the United States and reports that Greece has roughly half as many elevators despite its much smaller population.
- A compact elevator for a three-story Italian building was estimated at €20,000–€30,000, while Smith estimated a comparable American installation at about $120,000–$150,000.
- [[ElevatorInstallationCost]] reflects both labor arrangements and design standards: the episode describes highly specialized union labor, on-site disassembly and rebuilding practices, and American cabins roughly twice the size of common European examples.
- European compact cabins can accommodate wheelchairs with less maneuvering room, whereas larger American cabins can better accommodate a fully extended stretcher and continued emergency treatment.
- [[ElevatorAvailabilityAccessibilityTradeoff]] arises when the price and footprint of the preferred cabin lead small or affordable buildings to install no elevator, excluding people who cannot use stairs.
- [[GregoryAVictor|Victor]] proposed a seven-foot stretcher requirement after a less standardized proposal was rejected; the revised amendment passed through the [[InternationalCodeCouncil]] process.
- [[ModelBuildingCodeDiffusion]] gives a technical amendment national reach when jurisdictions adopt the model code, while indirect costs such as omitted elevators or unbuilt projects can escape the proposal's stated cost-impact analysis.
