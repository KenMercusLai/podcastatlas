# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》118-2｜吃瓜了！王室的瓜 包甜！

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues the late-Chu branch opened in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu|Qinji 118-1]] by putting [[ChangpingJun|昌平君]] at the center of a genealogy and source-criticism problem. The host uses [[PreQinSurnameClanIdentity|先秦姓氏身份]] to read Xiong Qi as a Chu royal figure, challenges the [[Shiji|《史记》]] and [[Zhanguoce|《战国策》]] tradition that [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] had no sons, and treats the result as [[TextualVariantPoliticalStakes|史书异文政治重量]] rather than gossip alone.

The second half follows Changping Jun from [[QinState|秦国]] chancellor and suspected Chu-leaning exile into rebellion at Chenying, brief enthronement as Chu king under [[XiangYan|项燕]], and death during [[WangJianQin|王翦]] and [[MengWu|蒙武]]'s final campaign. The episode closes by turning conquest into administration: Qin sets up a temporary Chu commandery, later splits it into Jiujiang, Zhang, and Kuaiji commanderies, and faces the risk that [[ChuState|楚国]] and Three Jin remnants will concentrate in [[QiState|齐国]].

## Key Claims
- Pre-Qin surname and clan-name practice matters for political reading: the host says 姓 marks marriage boundaries while 氏 marks rank, so the name Xiong Qi is evidence for a Chu royal or noble identity rather than a neutral personal label.
- Changping Jun is presented as Xiong Qi, a biological son of Mi Wan born from Qin-Chu royal marriage ties, favored by [[HuayangFuren|华阳夫人]], and kin to [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] through the Qin court's Chu lineage.
- The episode challenges the received "Mi Wan had no son" premise by citing the Shiji Suoyin tradition around Xiong Qi and additional Chu-lineage material around [[ChuFuchu|熊负刍 / 楚王负刍]], concluding that Mi Wan likely had multiple sons.
- [[Shiji|《史记》]] is treated as essential but authored history: its paternity and succession scandals need to be tested against source layers, motive, chronology, and narrative force.
- After the Lao Ai crisis and Lu Buwei's implication, Changping Jun becomes Qin chancellor, but the episode speculates that Ying Zheng later distrusts him because his Chu blood could pull him toward Chu.
- Changping Jun's rebellion at Chenying helps explain why [[LiXinQin|李信]]'s first Chu campaign collapses and why late Chu conquest cannot be reduced to a single battlefield contest.
- After Xiang Yan retreats north of the Huai, he enthrones Changping Jun as Chu king; Wang Jian and Meng Wu then defeat the final resistance, Changping Jun dies, and Xiang Yan commits suicide.
- Qin's temporary Chu commandery and later division into Jiujiang, Zhang, and Kuaiji commanderies show that state destruction immediately becomes a [[ConquestCommanderyTransition|灭国后的郡县化过渡]] problem.
- The remaining strategic problem is not only Yan or Qi individually, but the possibility that officials, nobles, and families from destroyed states will gather in still-intact Qi.

## Key Quotes
> "姓的功能是分别婚姻，氏的功能是标明贵贱" - the episode's frame for reading Xiong Qi's identity.

> "螳臂当车" - the host's judgment on the final Changping Jun and Xiang Yan resistance.

## Contradictions
- This episode directly complicates [[PregnancySuccessionSubstitution|孕身继承替换]] and [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] as previously recorded from Qinji 109-1: the earlier source depends on a childless Mi Wan, while this episode argues that Mi Wan had several sons. The wiki should keep both claims source-scoped rather than silently harmonizing them.
- This episode also refines or varies [[XiangYan|项燕]]'s terminal sequence compared with [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu|Qinji 118-1]]: instead of Xiang Yan's death being compressed around the Qinan defeat and the "楚虽三户" memory line, the host narrates defeat, retreat north of the Huai, Changping Jun's enthronement, final Qin attack, Changping Jun's death, and Xiang Yan's suicide.
- Changping Jun's Xiong Qi identity is stated more confidently here than in the previous page, but the wiki still treats it as source-grounded reconstruction rather than an independently settled baseline.
