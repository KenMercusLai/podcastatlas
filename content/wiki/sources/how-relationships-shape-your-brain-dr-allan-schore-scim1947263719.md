# How Relationships Shape Your Brain | Dr. Allan Schore

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] interview has [[AndrewHuberman]] speak with [[AllanSchore]] about early attachment as nonverbal affect communication and regulation. Schore argues that infant-caregiver interaction helps organize later capacities for self-regulation, co-regulation, trust, intimacy, and psychotherapy, with misattunement repair as important as moment-to-moment synchrony. The episode extends [[EmotionalCoRegulationFit]], [[InterpersonalSynchronyAndRapport]], [[RelationshipConflictRepair]], and [[TherapyRelationshipAndBoundaries]], while adding [[AttachmentAffectRegulation]] and [[RightHemisphereRelationalProcessing]] as source-bounded frameworks.

## Key Claims
- [[AttachmentAffectRegulation]] frames secure attachment as learning both self-regulation and interactive regulation through repeated caregiver response, while avoidant, anxious, and disorganized patterns reflect different regulatory imbalances or failures under stress.
- Repair after misattunement matters because secure caregiving does not require perfect matching; it requires sufficiently frequent re-attunement and reconnection.
- [[RightHemisphereRelationalProcessing]] describes implicit relational communication through face, voice, gesture, bodily arousal, wide attention, and contextual emotional cues before or beneath verbal explanation.
- [[InterpersonalSynchronyAndRapport]] is extended from a general coordination marker into emotionally meaningful dyadic regulation, including the episode's discussion of hyperscanning and right-hemisphere synchrony.
- [[TherapyRelationshipAndBoundaries]] is presented as an active change medium in which the therapist listens below literal words, recognizes dysregulation, synchronizes without collapsing role boundaries, and helps the patient tolerate or reorganize affect.
- Music, touch, pets, nature, novelty, creativity, exercise, sleep, and close relationships are discussed as possible supports for affective regulation, but not as substitutes for individualized clinical care.
- Text communication is treated as thinner than voice or face-to-face exchange for affective cues, while reflective letters are presented as a qualified counterexample.

## Key Quotes
> "Attachment is affect communication and affect regulation." - Schore's organizing definition in the episode.

> "Misattunement and repair" - the episode's compact account of good-enough relational security.

> "The unconscious is relational" - Schore's frame for implicit communication between people.
