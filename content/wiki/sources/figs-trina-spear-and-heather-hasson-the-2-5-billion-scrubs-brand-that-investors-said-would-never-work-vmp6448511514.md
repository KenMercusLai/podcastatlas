## Summary
This [[HowIBuiltThis]] episode features [[GuyRaz]] interviewing [[HeatherHasson]] and [[TrinaSpear]] about turning [[FIGS]] from car-trunk scrub sales into a profitable public healthcare-apparel company. Their account connects direct observation of clinicians, [[TechnicalComfort]], complementary founder roles, [[EndUserPurchasedWorkwear]], manufacturing relationships, hundreds of investor rejections, founder control, litigation, pandemic response, and an IPO. Its central synthesis is that an overlooked professional uniform became a consumer brand when the founders treated healthcare workers as users with technical, aesthetic, and community needs rather than as a captive institutional market.

## Key Claims
- Heather's earlier fashion work, charitable uniform project in Kenya, clinic observations, and tailoring requests from clinicians led her to identify medical scrubs as an overlooked product category.
- Trina researched the category for roughly six months and saw a mandatory, replenishment-driven, non-seasonal market in which the source says 85% of healthcare professionals bought their own uniforms.
- Selling outside emergency rooms let the founders validate demand and learn directly about fit, pockets, tool storage, fabric, and comfort before scaling.
- [[TechnicalComfort]] combined stretch, softness, functional details, gender-specific fit, and more deliberate colors into a product position centered on clinical work.
- [[EndUserPurchasedWorkwear]] explains why hospital procurement produced little traction while direct sales to clinicians and workplace word of mouth became effective.
- The founders say distributor and licensing savings let FIGS spend roughly twice as much on production while pricing only 15% to 20% above conventional competitors.
- Heather led product and manufacturing development while Trina led operations and fundraising; they accepted unequal equity based on the company's pre-partnership history without allowing stake comparison to dominate the relationship.
- After an estimated 300 to 400 investor rejections, FIGS raised staged rounds totaling about $10 million before [[ThomasTull]] consolidated the investor base and left the founders with two of three board seats.
- The source reports revenue growing from about $1 million in 2015 to $263 million in 2020, with profitability reached by 2017, but these figures are not independently audited within the episode summary.
- [[StrategicPartnersMedicalApparel]] sued FIGS in 2019; FIGS says it spent about $40 million defending the case before a jury ruled in its favor in early 2022.
- FIGS shifted supply relationships toward masks and protective equipment during COVID-19, then went public in May 2021 with healthcare professionals participating in the [[NewYorkStockExchange]] ceremony.
- The founders use FIGS's changing market valuation to distinguish company price and paper wealth from personal worth, while treating profitability as the prerequisite for philanthropy and advocacy.
