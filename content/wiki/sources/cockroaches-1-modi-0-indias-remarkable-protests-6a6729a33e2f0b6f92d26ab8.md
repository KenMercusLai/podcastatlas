## Summary
This [[TheIntelligence]] episode links a youth protest wave in [[India]], an [[AdvanceNewsTradingExperiment]], and [[Invader]]'s urban mosaics. The India segment presents the [[CockroachMovement]] as a meme-born but serious challenge to [[NarendraModi]]'s image of political invulnerability after education grievances, exam leaks, unemployment, and crackdown politics converged. The markets segment argues that knowing tomorrow's headlines is not enough without reading expectations and sizing risk, while the culture segment treats [[FlashInvaders]] as a mobile game that pushes players back into the physical city.

## Key Claims
- The source says education minister [[DharmendraPradhan]] resigned after youth protests, making the event a rare public-pressure resignation during Modi's rule.
- [[KiraHuyu]] says the protest began after India's chief justice seemed to compare jobless youth to cockroaches, and [[AbhijitTipke]]'s tweet turned that insult into a rallying online meme.
- The [[CockroachMovement]] grew into a large youth campaign with millions of Instagram followers, AI-generated cockroach protest imagery, and serious demands around [[NationalEligibilityCumEntranceTest|NEET]], exam leaks, unemployment, and education failure.
- The source links the NEET leak to a larger exam-pressure system: intense competition, too few high-quality colleges, limited jobs, reported student suicides, and about 150 known exam leaks over the past decade.
- [[Sonamji]]'s removal from a hunger-strike site in [[Delhi]] is presented as a turning point that drew tens of thousands, perhaps hundreds of thousands, into the largest Delhi protest in more than half a decade.
- Protesters carried copies of the Constitution and framed themselves as patriotic because [[BharatiyaJanataParty|BJP]] politics often brands critics as anti-national.
- The source says riot police beat protesters, fired tear gas, used pellet guns, shut down the internet in central Delhi, and closed 17 metro stations, turning the episode into a case of [[DissentSpaceCompression]] as well as education policy.
- Modi first ignored the protests, then announced unrelated policy changes, and later issued a late-night apology video that Gen Z protesters mocked for its staging and script.
- The trading segment describes 120 participants shown historical Wall Street Journal front pages from 2008 to 2022 and allowed leverage up to 50 times; only about half finished above the starting amount and one in six went bust.
- [[JoshRoberts]] argues that participants mainly failed because markets move relative to expectations and because many participants mis-sized bets even when they had useful information.
- AI models guessed market direction better than humans in the experiment but still struggled with [[PositionSizing]], while five expert macro traders all made money by varying size sharply and sometimes refusing to trade.
- [[CaitlinTalbot]]'s segment presents [[Invader]] and [[FlashInvaders]] as a [[StreetArtGameLayer]]: the app rewards scanning mosaics, but the stronger effect is making players look up from phones and notice public space.
