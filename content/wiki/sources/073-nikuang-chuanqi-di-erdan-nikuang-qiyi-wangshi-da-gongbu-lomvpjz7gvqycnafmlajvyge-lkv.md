# 073 《倪匡传奇》第二弹：倪匡奇异往事大公布

## Summary

This [[ZhiZuiJinMiFM|纸醉金迷FM]] episode retells [[NiKuang|倪匡]]'s early experiences in the Greater Khingan forests, Huai River works, and a northern Jiangsu labor-reform farm, connecting dangerous animals, scarcity, engineering failure, fossils, and local strange tales to his later fiction. Its strongest synthesis joins [[LocalExperienceAsCreativeArchive|local experience as a creative archive]] to [[EvidenceBoundFolkloreInquiry|evidence-bound folklore inquiry]]: vivid firsthand or autobiographical stories can explain recurring settings and motifs without independently proving remembered numbers, species identifications, causal claims, or supernatural events.

## Key Claims

- [[NiKuang|倪匡]]'s forest, construction-site, and farm memories supplied durable material for later adventure and speculative fiction, including a disfigured man reused in 《木炭》 and recurring interests in strange organisms, spirits, and brain waves.
- Greater Khingan stories about ginseng taboos, dangerous bears, armored wild boar, and scarce meat join practical survival knowledge to folklore, economic value, and retrospective storytelling.
- The failed diversion channel between the Huai River and Hongze Lake illustrates the cost of acting on untested expert authority: tens of thousands of workers reportedly completed a channel that then allowed reverse flow and had to be blocked again.
- Deep excavation stories combine hazardous construction, multicolored soil, destroyed fossils, and a live eel found in an apparently dry underground cavity; the eel's origin remains unresolved because it died before study and was not preserved.
- Northern Jiangsu farm memories make scarcity concrete through bitter salt-marsh oil plants, an oil-soaked cloth used to flavor a pan, crowded shelters, and lethal snake risk.
- Reports of a moving newspaper, footsteps, a voice beneath a stove, and periodic apparent possession are preserved as experiences or stories while the hosts also consider sleep paralysis, environmental sound, mental illness, adrenaline, memory error, and dialect imitation.
- The episode's storytelling value does not establish its historical, zoological, geological, medical, or supernatural claims; most details derive from Ni Kuang's memoir or host discussion rather than independent records.
