# The Trillion-Dollar Industries AI Is Disrupting: Voice, Law & the End of the Billable Hour

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] episode pairs interviews with [[ElevenLabs]] and [[Legora]] to show how AI-native application companies are turning model capability into domain infrastructure. The voice section argues that useful voice agents depend on reliability, model orchestration, identity safeguards, licensing, and integrations rather than only human-sounding speech. The legal section frames AI as a software wedge into legal services, where structured legal data and supervised agents can change diligence, research, junior-lawyer work, and billable-hour economics.

## Key Claims
- [[ElevenLabs]] says its revenue reached $600 million after a rapid ARR ramp from its 2023 text-to-speech launch, making enterprise adoption central to the interview's AI-application thesis.
- ElevenLabs describes itself as an AI communication platform spanning audio generation, transcription, orchestration, localization, support agents, training, sales, and operations.
- ElevenLabs says production voice agents improved because reliability, model orchestration, knowledge, and integrations now make customer-support, sales, and proactive workflows more useful.
- The episode treats voice prompting, dictation, and ambient recording as ways to give AI richer context than short typed prompts usually capture.
- Voice identity becomes a platform-governance problem: ElevenLabs describes tracing generated content, moderating text and voice inputs, blocking scam-like uses, detecting AI audio, and authenticating voice owners.
- ElevenLabs says its licensed voice marketplace has paid more than $22 million to talent, and the episode also discusses restorative voice use for people who lost speech.
- [[Legora]] frames legal AI as a software opportunity inside a roughly $1 trillion legal-services market where software is presented as only about 4% of spend.
- Legora argues that AI pressures the billable hour by shifting diligence and document review from manual associate labor toward in-house tooling, fixed-fee structures, success fees, and agent supervision.
- Legora says junior lawyers will still exist, but their work shifts toward orchestrating agents, verifying outputs, and directing end-to-end legal workflows.
- Legora argues that legal AI needs complete and current legal data because missing a relevant case or jurisdictional rule can matter in high-stakes legal work.
- The episode names [[LexisNexis]] and [[Westlaw]] as legacy U.S. legal-research incumbents, while Legora positions firm data, enterprise data, cases, legislation, regulatory updates, and narrow extraction models as part of its trust and workflow stack.

## Key Quotes
> "snappy" - ElevenLabs' description of how some users speak more directly to AI voice agents.

> "legal engineers" - Legora's label for forward-deployed lawyers helping firms move from pre-AI to post-AI legal workflows.

> "80% accurate" - Legora's example level for an immediate cross-border legal answer that still needs legal review.
