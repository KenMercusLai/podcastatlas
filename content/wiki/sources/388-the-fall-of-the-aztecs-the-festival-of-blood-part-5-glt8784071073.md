# 388. The Fall of the Aztecs: The Festival of Blood (Part 5)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory|The Rest Is History]] episode follows [[HernanCortes|Hernan Cortes]] from his victory over [[PanfiloDeNarvaez|Panfilo de Narvaez]] back into a besieged Tenochtitlan, where [[PedroDeAlvarado|Pedro de Alvarado]] has ordered the [[ToxcatlMassacre|Toxcatl massacre]]. It presents the killings as the violent collapse of an already unstable occupation, driven by fear, rumor, missing translation, and possible Tlaxcalan influence rather than a demonstrated Mexica plot. The final section keeps [[Moctezuma]]'s death unresolved because Spanish and Indigenous-linked accounts assign responsibility differently.

## Key Claims

- Cortes defeated Narvaez's much larger force through bribery, night attack, and rapid absorption of defeated troops, increasing his numbers while losing control of events in Tenochtitlan.
- Alvarado interpreted disrupted supplies, rumor, and the Toxcatl festival as signs of an imminent attack, but the episode supplies no secure evidence that such a plot existed.
- Spaniards closed the temple courtyard exits and killed unarmed celebrants, transforming occupation tension into open urban war and destroying the remaining possibility of accommodation.
- Malinche's absence deprived Alvarado's force of its principal interpreter, while Tlaxcalan informants may have intensified anti-Mexica fear for reasons that cannot be established.
- Cortes re-entered the city without being attacked, then found his enlarged force trapped as Mexica resistance blocked escape routes and cut food and water.
- Moctezuma's captivity had damaged his authority, and releasing [[Cuitlahuac]] to obtain supplies instead strengthened organized resistance.
- Moctezuma may have died from a projectile, deliberate crowd action, self-exposure, or Spanish murder; the surviving accounts do not permit a settled verdict.

## Key Quotes

> "the sun" - the episode's translation of Tonatiuh, the Mexica name associated with Alvarado's appearance.

> "strangers from the sea" - the episode's phrasing for how some in the crowd may have understood the Spaniards whom Moctezuma had accommodated.

## Connections

- [[PedroDeAlvarado]] - acting commander who ordered the attack while Cortes was away.
- [[HernanCortes]] - expedition leader who defeated Narvaez and returned to a city in revolt.
- [[PanfiloDeNarvaez]] - rival commander whose defeat enlarged Cortes's force.
- [[Moctezuma]] - captive ruler whose damaged authority and disputed death mark the failure of negotiation.
- [[Malinche]] - absent interpreter whose departure narrowed communication inside the occupation.
- [[Cuitlahuac]] - released royal figure who organized resistance rather than supplies.
- [[MexicaEmpire]] - political order whose capital turned decisively against the Spaniards.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction with [[389-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-night-of-tears-part-6-glt2744313815|Part 6]], [[390-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-war-to-the-death-part-7-glt1796642529|Part 7]], or [[391-the-fall-of-the-aztecs-the-last-emperor-part-8-glt2984940365|Part 8]] was found; this episode supplies the massacre, siege, and hostage crisis immediately before the Noche Triste.
- Spanish accounts that blame a thrown stone for Moctezuma's death conflict with Diego Duran's Indigenous-linked account of a chained body with dagger wounds; the episode treats Spanish murder as plausible, not proven.
- The alleged Mexica plot, Tlaxcalan informants' motives, the reason Cortes was allowed back into the city, exact casualty totals, remembered speeches, omens, and participants' private motives remain source-scoped.
- The contrast between Spanish horror at sacrifice and the massacre's scale is a moral and narrative interpretation, not a complete reconstruction of the Toxcatl ritual or every participant's intent.
