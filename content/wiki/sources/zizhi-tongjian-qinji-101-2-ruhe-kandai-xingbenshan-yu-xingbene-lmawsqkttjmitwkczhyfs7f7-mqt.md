# 《资治通鉴·秦纪》101-2｜如何看待性本善与性本恶？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues the human-nature discussion opened in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-101-1-zhe-wei-daomei-junwang-dengji-santian-ji-cusi-lll-bcc7basaf6-nzwqtmcb5qw6w|Qinji 101-1]]. It contrasts [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]]'s [[XingShanTheory|性善论]] with [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s [[XingETheory|性恶论]], but then moves the emphasis to [[HumanNaturePlasticity|人性可变性]]: people are not fixed as simply good or bad, and conduct changes with environment, need, motive, education, ritual, and law.

The episode's modern example is a fire-safety training story in which a smoke mask should be buckled tightly not only for sealing, but to keep it from being taken by another desperate person. That example adds [[DisasterCivilizationRetreat|灾变中的文明退场]] to the wiki: disaster prevention is also preservation of the civil order that restrains raw survival impulses. The close returns to [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]], [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]], [[LuZhonglian|鲁仲连]], and [[LiBai|李白]], then asks whether a person with past wrongdoing who keeps doing good should be judged by stain, motive, or sustained action.

## Key Claims
- [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]] is presented as the representative of [[XingShanTheory|性善论]], with human beings born with moral sprouts such as compassion, shame, respect, right and wrong, and the roots of ren, yi, li, and zhi.
- The episode connects Mencius's human-nature optimism to [[MencianBenevolentGovernment|孟子仁政]] and [[MencianPeopleBasedLegitimacy|民贵君轻]].
- It notes that Mencius's political program was judged too remote from practical affairs and was not basically implemented by the rulers he visited.
- [[Xunzi|荀子]] is presented as the representative of [[XingETheory|性恶论]], with raw desires for profit, envy, resentment, sound, and beauty requiring later ordering.
- The episode stresses that Xunzi's answer is not resignation to evil; teachers, law, ritual, and moral guidance can redirect people toward deference, order, and stability.
- [[Confucius|孔子]]'s "性相近，习相远" is used as a bridge between the two positions: people may begin near one another, while habituation and environment make them diverge.
- The source says pre-Qin thinkers broadly allow for change in human nature, so the relevant question is not only original nature but transformation.
- Human behavior changes with need, motive, environment, goals, and the concrete people or events one faces.
- The fire-mask example argues that disaster can shrink the force of civilized restraint and make survival instincts salient, so safety practice should not assume stable everyday norms.
- Preventing disaster is framed as protecting life and property, but also as defending civilization itself.
- The relationship aside argues that self-care is a condition for non-coercive care of others; exhausted or unstable love can become pressure and harm.
- [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]] is criticized for letting fear and private interest override durable [[WeiState|魏国]] strategy and for using [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]] only when crisis forced him to.
- [[LuZhonglian|鲁仲连]] is held up, through [[LiBai|李白]]'s admiration, as a model of independent personality, free will, chivalric spirit, and practical intelligence.
- The final good-person/bad-person question extends [[ActionDefinesIdentity]] and [[VirtuePracticeInternalization|修养内化]] by testing whether long-run conduct can outweigh original stain or hidden motive.

## Key Quotes
> "人之初，性本善；性相近，习相远" - the popular formula the episode uses to introduce the debate.

> "性相近，习相远" - the Confucian line used for formation and divergence.

> "不要高估人性" - the disaster-training warning attached to the smoke-mask example.

> "先保护好自己" - the source's practical rule before extending care to others.
