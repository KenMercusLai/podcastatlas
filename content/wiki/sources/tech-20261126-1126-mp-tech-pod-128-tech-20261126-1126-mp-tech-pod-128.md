# AI food slop is on the menu

## Summary
This [[MarketplaceTech]] episode has cultural critic [[BradyBricknerWood]] examine uncanny AI-generated restaurant imagery: sweaty, repetitive, or insect-like food that can repel the customers it is meant to attract. He distinguishes older food-photography artifice, which used human craft to intensify appetite, from [[AIFoodAdvertising]] that cheaply imitates those conventions without reliably preserving their sensory or commercial purpose.

The discussion also qualifies the accessibility case for [[AIGeneratedAdvertising]]. Small food carts, bodegas, and festival trucks may lack advertising budgets, but a free generator is not automatically easier or more effective than an unedited smartphone photograph. Better models may remove visible defects, while food's physical and sensory character and fading novelty keep human judgment central.

## Key Claims
- AI-generated food advertising is increasingly visible and can produce sweaty, fractal, repetitive, or insect-like visual artifacts.
- Traditional commercial food photography already idealized dishes, but its artifice was organized around making food desirable through human craft.
- Low-margin businesses may adopt free image generators because professional advertising feels inaccessible, although a direct smartphone photograph may communicate the product better.
- Tool availability does not prove equal access, practical usability, or effective commercial communication.
- More photorealistic models may reduce visible defects without resolving the gap between synthetic food imagery and embodied appetite.
- Novelty is a major part of current AI imagery's appeal and may decay as its conventions become familiar.
- The episode's claims are cultural interpretation rather than customer research, restaurant-owner interviews, or comparative campaign measurement.

## Key Quotes
> "sweaty, fractal and almost insect-like" - Brickner-Wood's description of especially unsuccessful generated food imagery.

> "food slop" - the episode's label for AI food imagery that frightens or repels rather than seduces.
