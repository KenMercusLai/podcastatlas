## Summary
This [[YiLaoYongYi|一劳永逸]] episode compares Shanghai, [[Tokyo]], and [[HongKong|Hong Kong]] marathons to explain why a mass-participation race cannot be understood from its entry fee alone. It separates organizer economics, host-city spillovers, and the runner's own equipment, travel, training-time, and physical costs. The durable synthesis is that [[MassParticipationSportsEventFinancing|赛事融资]], [[MarathonDestinationConsumption|目的地消费]], and [[MarathonParticipationCostBoundary|个人能力边界]] are connected but distinct: a race may need sponsors and public services, create substantial urban spending, and still be unsuitable for an unprepared individual.

## Key Claims
- The episode estimates that Shanghai Marathon entry and charity-place revenue would not cover broadcast, venue, timing, supplies, prizes, elite-athlete support, medical care, security, road closure, and traffic restoration, making [[MassParticipationSportsEventFinancing]] a mixed system of participant fees, commercial sponsorship, in-kind supply, and public support.
- Sponsors can contribute cash, apparel, water, bibs, signs, or other inputs, while category exclusivity and media exposure help determine the value of the inventory.
- Tokyo is presented as a mature international event whose foreign-runner share, sponsor revenue, resident support, merchant activation, and destination stays connect the race to [[MarathonDestinationConsumption]] beyond the organizer's own accounts.
- The Shanghai, Tokyo, and Hong Kong comparison argues that participant count alone does not determine urban benefit; visitor share, race-distance mix, hotel cost, same-day travel, route geography, and length of stay affect local capture.
- The runner's true cost includes shoes, clothing, watches, transport, lodging, training, recovery, and time as well as entry fees, supporting [[MarathonParticipationCostBoundary]].
- Full-marathon participation requires preparation and respect for cutoff, injury, and illness boundaries; a 5-kilometer health run or half marathon can provide event participation without treating 42.195 kilometers as a universal goal.
- Prestigious race status, scarce entry, charity places, membership-based extra lotteries, standardized results, and repeat community can strengthen a [[MassParticipationSportsEventMoat]] even when the underlying act of running is easy to copy.
