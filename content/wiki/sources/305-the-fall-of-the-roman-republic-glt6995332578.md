# 305: The Fall of the Roman Republic

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows the failed political settlement after the [[CaesarAssassination|assassination of Julius Caesar]] through Caesar's funeral, [[MarkAntony]]'s recovery, [[Cicero]]'s constitutional campaign, and [[OctavianAugustus|Octavian]]'s conversion of inheritance into money, troops, office, and coercive power. It then traces the [[SecondTriumvirate]], the proscriptions and Cicero's death, and the [[BattleOfPhilippi|Battle of Philippi]], where the deaths of [[GaiusCassiusLonginus|Cassius]] and [[MarcusBrutus|Brutus]] end the organized republican cause.

The episode's strongest contribution is to connect assassination failure to institutional and military sequence. The conspirators kill Caesar but do not control Rome, public emotion, the Senate, succession, or armies; Antony, Octavian, and Lepidus then combine legal form with force, while Brutus and Cassius build a rival war machine in the East. Octavian's later Augustan settlement succeeds partly because it preserves republican presentation after destroying the republic's effective independence.

## Key Claims

- The assassins' retreat to the Capitol instead of immediately controlling the Senate and city lets Antony shape the settlement after Caesar's death.
- Cicero's amnesty protects the conspirators temporarily, but allowing a public funeral gives Antony the setting in which Caesar's wounds, will, and gifts turn popular anger against them.
- Cicero becomes the leading constitutional voice against Antony, yet his dependence on other commanders reveals the weakness of republican speech without reliable military power.
- Eighteen-year-old Octavian uses Caesar's name, fortune, promised bequests, and a privately recruited armed following to enter politics before forcing election as consul at nineteen.
- Antony's defeat at Mutina does not restore senatorial control: both consuls die, Antony joins Lepidus, Decimus Brutus is killed, and Octavian becomes the decisive surviving commander near Rome.
- The [[SecondTriumvirate]] gives Antony, Octavian, and Lepidus extraordinary legal authority, while proscriptions supply money and eliminate enemies, including Cicero.
- Brutus and Cassius are not presented as pure constitutional idealists; their eastern mobilization also includes coercion, atrocity, and mass military organization.
- At the [[BattleOfPhilippi]], Cassius kills himself before learning that Brutus has succeeded on his wing; Brutus later loses and dies, leaving the republican military cause destroyed.
- Octavian later defeats Antony and [[CleopatraVII|Cleopatra]], becomes Augustus, and presents one-person rule through more modest republican forms than Caesar had used.
- The hosts leave the collapse partly contested: Caesar's ambition mattered, but imperial scale, wealth, outdated machinery, army politics, and prior violence had already made the republic unstable.

## Key Quotes

> "the last of the Romans" - later memory of Brutus and Cassius after Philippi, as presented by the episode.

> "praised, honored, and then raised to the skies" - Cicero's hostile joke about using and discarding Octavian, whose report to Octavian deepened their mistrust.
