# Dunes and gloom: MBS's annus horribilis

## Summary

This [[EconomistPodcasts]] episode links three cases where ambitious promises meet institutional and political limits. [[MohammedBinSalman|Mohammed bin Salman]] faces direct [[Houthis|Houthi]] attacks, weak delivery against [[SaudiVision2030|Vision 2030]] targets, and limited leverage over [[DonaldTrump]]; [[SamBankmanFried]] offers a partial ethical reassessment from prison while still denying the fraud judgment behind [[FTX]]'s collapse; and Indonesian peat fires turn [[ElNino|El Nino]] drying, land-clearing incentives, smoke exposure, and disputed cross-border enforcement into a [[TransboundaryHazeGovernance]] problem.

## Key Claims

- The episode says Houthi attacks disrupted Riyadh and other Saudi cities, closed the capital's airport, caused about 20 reported deaths there, and forced cancellation of the Future Investment Initiative.
- It argues that MBS's military, economic, and Trump-relationship bets are all under strain, while distinguishing those setbacks from an immediate threat to his rule because social reforms still carry goodwill and no plausible challenger is identified.
- Saudi foreign direct investment is reported at about $30bn in 2025 against a $100bn annual target by decade's end, while the episode says the IMF moved from forecasting 4% growth to a 3.3% contraction after a year of conflict.
- The source treats Saudi spending connected to Trump and his family as evidence that transactional access does not necessarily produce strategic influence over decisions about Iran or regional war.
- [[SamBankmanFried]] says ends do not always justify means and that the path toward an outcome matters, but he continues to maintain his innocence and argues that more time could have made FTX customers whole.
- Bankman-Fried is described as pursuing a Supreme Court appeal and seeking pardon consideration, while [[EffectiveAltruism]] is presented as surviving the scandal and regaining influence through the AI boom.
- Fires on Borneo and Sumatra are linked to deliberate land clearing, combustible peat, and unusually dry El Nino conditions; the resulting haze reached [[Malaysia]], the Philippines, and [[Singapore]].
- Indonesia reportedly opened 98 corporate cases, deployed firefighters, water bombing, and cloud seeding, and announced a peatland and mangrove restoration agency, but financial incentives and enforcement gaps remain.
- Singapore's extraterritorial haze law can issue notices to overseas firms, yet similar action after the 2015 haze produced no convictions and remains contested by Indonesian officials as a sovereignty intrusion.
- Reduced rainfall could damage regional agriculture, food security, and prices; better forecasts help only when governments, firms, and farmers act on them.

## Contradictions

- The source's approximately 20 reported Riyadh deaths exceed the three deaths described in [[saudi-under-siege-houthis-attack-airport-cc6579d3f0d15fe71826fc6b995c02cc]]. This is preserved as a likely difference in reporting window or scope rather than resolved as a factual contradiction.

- The haze segment extends rather than overturns [[burning-questions-a-more-fire-prone-world-6a687ea64207b2a60119df8b]]: the earlier source identified Indonesia's ignition incentives and El Nino exposure, while this episode supplies an active regional case and details the enforcement limits.
