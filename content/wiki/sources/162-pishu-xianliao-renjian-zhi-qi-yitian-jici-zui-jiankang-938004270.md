# 162.屁书：闲聊“人间之气”！一天几次最健康？

## Summary
This [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] episode uses [[PishuBook|《屁书》]] as a playful entry into flatulence as physiology, health clue, etiquette problem, taboo-breaking joke, performance material, and animal behavior. It treats farting as normal rather than shameful, while still distinguishing private bodily acceptance from public-boundary responsibility. The episode's reusable contribution is a body-culture cluster around [[FlatulenceAsHealthSignal]], [[BodilyTabooHumor]], [[CivilizedBodyDiscipline]], [[FlatulencePerformanceHistory]], and [[AnimalGasCommunication]].

## Key Claims
- Flatulence comes from swallowed air and from gut fermentation by intestinal microbes; eating pace, smoking, straws, gum, stress swallowing, dentures, beans, sweet potatoes, cruciferous vegetables, fruit, dairy, carbonated drinks, and high-protein foods can all affect frequency or smell.
- The episode gives a normal adult range of roughly 8 to 15 farts per day, with an average around 14; more than 20 per day for over a week, especially with difficulty passing gas, bloating, abdominal pain, or abnormal stool, should prompt medical attention.
- Smell can carry health clues, but the hosts repeatedly mark the boundary between bodily observation and diagnosis; a single foul smell is not proof of serious disease.
- Scatological humor works because flatulence is universal and forbidden at the same time: children discover the comic power of taboo during toilet training, and adults keep using the body to puncture status and solemnity.
- Chinese insults such as "你放屁" turn bodily waste into a metaphor for meaningless, unpleasant speech, making flatulence a rhetorical as well as physiological object.
- Historical and religious examples show different ways of handling bodily shame: etiquette may hide the body, while Buddhist and Zen anecdotes can use excrement or farts to break attachment to respectability.
- The episode links [[SuShi|苏轼 / 苏东坡]], Foyin, Martin Luther, Jean-Paul Sartre, and court anecdotes to [[BodilyTabooHumor]] without treating every story as equally certain.
- Farting can become staged entertainment: medieval and modern performers, competitions, sound effects, and musical novelty acts turn embarrassment into paid spectacle.
- Law, etiquette, and institutional rules can look absurd when they try to control an involuntary and universal bodily function too completely.
- Animal examples extend the topic beyond human etiquette: cattle emissions, pet gas, dangerous rabbit bloat, defensive snake cloacal discharge, herring bubble sounds, whales, primates, elephants, and sloths all show gas as physiology, risk, signal, or ecological issue.

## Key Quotes
> "因屁废食" - the episode's joke warning against avoiding nutritious foods only because they may increase gas.

> "想放就放，要放得漂亮" - the closing stance, immediately qualified by attention to context and other people.

> "八风吹不动，一屁过江来" - the Su Dongpo/Foyin anecdote used to connect bodily humor with puncturing spiritual self-importance.
