# Show 72 - Mania for Subjugation II

## Summary

This [[HardcoreHistory]] episode follows [[AlexanderTheGreat|Alexander the Great]] from [[PhilipIIMacedon|Philip II's]] assassination through a contested accession, northern campaigning, and the destruction of [[Thebes]]. It presents speed as Alexander's central operating principle: rapid movement, flexible combined arms, intelligence, visible personal danger, and psychological display repeatedly restore a position that initially looks fragile.

The same methods make political consolidation inseparable from coercion. Rival claimants are eliminated, Thracian and Getae communities are killed or plundered, and Thebes is massacred, enslaved, and almost erased as a warning to Greece. [[Arrian]] and other late traditions preserve valuable tactical detail but disagree over responsibility, numbers, motives, and battle sequence, so strategic effectiveness does not settle either factual uncertainty or moral judgment.

## Key Claims

- Philip's murder removed the personal center of Macedon's alliance system and left twenty-year-old Alexander facing rival claimants, Greek resistance, northern threats, and an unfinished Persian expedition.
- Alexander's accession depended on elite and army support as well as executions; responsibility for Philip's death and for every dynastic killing remains unresolved.
- Rapid marches into Thessaly and Greece restored the League of Corinth before opposition could coordinate, while Sparta remained outside the coalition.
- In the northern campaign, combined arms, terrain adaptation, night movement, intelligence, and staged intimidation defeated Thracian, Triballian, Getae, and Illyrian resistance.
- The Illyrian campaign shows speed's double edge: Alexander entered a dangerous trap, then used a phalanx drill, covered withdrawal, intelligence, and a night return to reverse it.
- Thebes revolted after a false report of Alexander's death; other Greek states delayed or withheld support, leaving the city isolated against a much larger army.
- Ancient accounts disagree over whether Alexander or an unauthorized attack began the battle, but the sack killed thousands, enslaved tens of thousands, and functioned as exemplary terror.
- The League of Corinth's vote gave collective legal cover to Thebes's destruction, but Alexander retained the power to prevent it.
