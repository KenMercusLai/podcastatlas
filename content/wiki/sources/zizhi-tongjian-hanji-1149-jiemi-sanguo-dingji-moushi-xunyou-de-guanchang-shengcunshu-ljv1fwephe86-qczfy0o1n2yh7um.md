# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1149丨揭秘三国顶级谋士荀攸的官场生存术

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode joins a modern family-psychology opening to three late-Han personnel cases. It frames parental self-sacrifice as control when a child must repay unrealized ambitions through achievement, then uses [[JiangWan|蒋琬]]'s failed county appointment and later rise to argue that one poor role fit should not exhaust a person's potential.

The succession branch contrasts [[CaoZhi|曹植]]'s literary gifts with [[CaoPi|曹丕]]'s steadier political judgment, while [[XingYongLateHan|刑颙]] and [[LiuZhenLateHan|刘桢]] represent unwelcome corrective counsel. The closing portrait of [[XunYou|荀攸]] extends his existing strategic profile into court survival: secrecy, low visibility, and disciplined control of credit let him remain useful to [[CaoCao|曹操]] without presenting himself as a competing center of power.

## Key Claims

- Parental sacrifice becomes [[ParentalProjectionControl|projection control]] when a child must carry the parent's unrealized ambitions and experiences ordinary failure or happiness as betrayal.
- The episode's depression anecdote illustrates psychological burden, but it is not a clinical study and does not establish a single cause for depression.
- [[JiangWan|蒋琬]] performs poorly as Guangdu magistrate and nearly dies for neglecting county business, but [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] argues that he is suited to state-scale responsibility rather than a small administrative post.
- Jiang Wan's later service under Zhuge Liang and succession to senior Shu-Han government make his case one of [[TalentRoleFit|talent-role fit]], managerial correction, and bounded tolerance for early failure.
- [[CaoCao|曹操]] assigns the strict [[XingYongLateHan|刑颙]] to [[CaoZhi|曹植]], suggesting that political formation required discipline and correction rather than admirers alone.
- [[LiuZhenLateHan|刘桢]] tells Cao Zhi that ornamental brilliance cannot replace Xing Yong's useful counsel; the episode uses the contrast to separate literary talent from governing capacity.
- Cao Zhi's drinking, rule-breaking, and unstable self-command weaken political trust, while Cao Pi is presented as more controlled and better able to manage officials and succession competition.
- [[XunYou|荀攸]] keeps military plans and credit private, blocks family inquiry into secret counsel, and makes his competence legible chiefly through loyalty and results.
- The episode contrasts Xun You's accommodation to Cao Cao's new order with [[XunYu|荀彧]]'s stated Han-restoration boundary, but this is a host-level interpretive contrast rather than a complete account of either man.
