# Saigon, 1965

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode reconstructs the secret [[VietCongMotivationAndMoraleProject]] through [[MaiElliott]], [[LeonGoure]], and [[ConradKellen]]. Thousands of interviews with Viet Cong prisoners and defectors gave [[RANDCorporation]] an unusually rich archive, yet Goure read declining confidence as evidence that American bombing was breaking morale while Kellen read continued willingness to fight as evidence that the [[UnitedStates]] could not win.

The episode uses that divergence to extend [[StrategicIntelligenceInterpretation]]: more collection cannot settle a policy question when analysts weight the same testimony through different histories, loyalties, fears, and expectations. Elliott's later change of mind also shows that delayed revision is possible, while RAND's concern about Goure bypassing review and Kellen's complaint about confirmation-friendly expertise make interpretation an institutional problem as well as a personal one.

## Key Claims

- RAND recruited Vietnamese interviewers, including Mai Elliott, for a Pentagon-funded study of Viet Cong motivation and morale.
- The project produced approximately 62,000 pages of translated and edited interviews with prisoners and defectors, according to the episode.
- Goure emphasized a reported decline from 65 percent to 20 percent in interviewees who expected a Viet Cong victory after a year of heavy American bombing and used the finding to support an approaching-collapse interpretation.
- Kellen read roughly one thousand interviews and concluded that enduring commitment mattered more than expressions of hardship or declining optimism; he judged the war unwinnable for the United States.
- Elliott's interview with a principled communist officer challenged her prior image of communists, but fear for her family delayed the policy conclusion she later accepted after moving to the United States.
- Goure's refugee history and anti-communist patriotism, Kellen's wartime experience, and Elliott's displacement and family vulnerability supplied different lenses for the same archive.
- RAND recalled Goure from Vietnam in April 1967 after internal concern about his conclusions and review practices; the episode says Kellen's warning was not heeded until too late.
