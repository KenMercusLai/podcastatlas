# Essentials: How Humans Select & Keep Romantic Partners in the Short & Long Term | Dr. David Buss

## Summary
This [[HubermanLab]] Essentials episode condenses [[AndrewHuberman]]'s interview with [[DavidBuss]] on [[EvolutionaryMatingStrategies]], [[MateValueAndMutualChoice]], deception, and [[JealousyAndMateRetention]]. It preserves the distinction between long- and short-term mating, the role of social and sensory information in attraction, and the escalation path from relationship threat to [[MatingConflictRisk]]. Because it is an edited version of [[how-humans-select-keep-romantic-partners-in-short-long-term-dr-david-buss-scim4159413839|the full interview]], it adds editorial continuity rather than an independent evidence line.

## Key Claims
- Sexual selection combines intrasexual competition with preferential mate choice, and long-term selection differs from short-term mating in the relative weight placed on traits and risks.
- Shared long-term preferences include intelligence, kindness, mutual attraction, health, dependability, and emotional stability, while average sex differences and cultural variation qualify any universal ranking.
- [[MateValueAndMutualChoice]] is multidimensional: broadly valued traits coexist with person-specific fit, social attention, mate-choice copying, and imperfect self-assessment.
- Online profiles can magnify appearance and strategic self-presentation, whereas in-person contact supplies auditory, olfactory, behavioral, and stress-response information that messaging cannot establish.
- [[JealousyAndMateRetention]] can respond to infidelity cues, emotional distance, rivals, or changing perceived mate value, but vigilance can escalate into surveillance, stalking, coercion, or violence.
- [[MatingConflictRisk]] is associated in the episode with deception, narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy, sexual coercion, stalking, and intimate-partner violence; these population claims do not diagnose an individual.
- Evolutionary psychology proposes functional explanations, while neuroscience may investigate proximate mechanisms; neither level alone supplies a complete causal or moral account.
