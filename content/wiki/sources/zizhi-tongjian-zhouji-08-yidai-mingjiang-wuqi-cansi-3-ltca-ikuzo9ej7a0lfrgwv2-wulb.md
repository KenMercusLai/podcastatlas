# 《资治通鉴·周纪》08丨一代名将吴起惨死（3）

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode continues [[ZhouAnWang|周安王]]'s chronology from the Qin coup that installs [[QinXianGong|秦献公]] through the Tian-family Qi succession problem, [[JixiaAcademy|稷下学宫]], Wei-Zhao conflict, a solar-eclipse omen notice, and [[WuQi|吴起]]'s death. Its central synthesis is that early Warring States power was being remade at several scales at once: coups and succession drift unsettled rulership, state sponsorship turned scholars into strategic resources, and reformers such as Wu Qi could strengthen a state while making themselves fatally dependent on ruler protection.

## Key Claims
- In 385 BCE, the Qin shuzhang Gai reportedly brings [[QinXianGong|秦献公]] back from exile, kills [[QinChugong|秦出公]] and his mother, and exposes how weak regency legitimacy can open a path for a returning royal.
- The same year cluster includes [[QiState|齐国]] attacking [[LuState|鲁国]], [[HanState|韩国]] attacking [[ZhengState|郑国]] and taking Yangcheng, and a brief notice that [[SongState|宋国]]'s ruler is captured, keeping the source inside [[EarlyWarringStatesInterstateWar]] rather than a single clean campaign.
- The episode flags a Tian-family Qi succession problem: after [[TianHe|田和]], the source's successor line is treated as muddled because [[TianYan|田衍]], [[TianQiHuanGong|田武 / 田氏齐桓公]], and [[GongziXiQi|公子喜]] belong to a violent internal transition.
- [[TianQiHuanGong|田武]] is said to kill [[TianYan|田衍]], install [[GongziXiQi|公子喜]] as a puppet, then kill Gongzi Xi and rule as the Tian-family Qi Huan Gong; the episode explicitly distinguishes him from the older Spring-Autumn [[QiHuanGong|齐桓公]].
- The episode credits Tian Wu with the institutional legacy of [[JixiaAcademy|稷下学宫]] near [[Linzi|临淄]]'s Ji gate, treating Qi's ability to support "idle" scholars as a long-run talent and intellectual-capital strategy later visible under [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] and [[Mengzi|孟子 / Mencius]].
- The [[BianQue|扁鹊]] story attached to Tian-family Qi Huan Gong is used as a ruler-judgment caution: refusing repeated expert diagnosis lets a small illness become fatal.
- In 383 BCE, [[WeiState|魏国]] attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]] and defeats Zhao at Tutai, which the host reads as evidence that tensions within the Three Jin world are now visible even when the chronicle gives little motive or aftermath.
- In 382 BCE, a solar-eclipse notice is read through [[CelestialOmenPoliticalResponsibility]], but the episode also notes that the associated prediction of Qin Xian Gong's death does not fit his actual later survival.
- In 381 BCE, [[ChuDaoWang|楚悼王]] dies and the nobles who hate Wu Qi's reforms immediately attack; [[WuQi|吴起]] clings to the dead ruler's body and is shot along with it in the [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] account.
- After [[ChuSuwang|楚肃王]] succeeds, more than seventy noble households are reportedly exterminated for damaging the ruler's corpse, while [[LushiChunqiu|《吕氏春秋》]] gives a sharper version in which Wu Qi deliberately uses the corpse-damage rule to pull his enemies into punishment.
- The episode notes variant traditions in [[HanFei|韩非]], [[Mozi|墨子]], and [[Huainanzi|《淮南子》]] that say Wu Qi was torn apart, preserving a harsher afterlife for the same reform-backlash story.
- Wu Qi's final evaluation is deliberately double-sided: he is presented as an extraordinary general and reformer whose service helped Lu, Wei, and Chu, but whose ruthlessness and trust deficit prevented him from building the political base needed to survive after his patron died.

## Key Quotes
> "国家的宝藏是德，而不是天险" - part 1's Wu Qi theme returns here as a tragic contrast to his own political survival problem.

> "伏在楚悼王遗体上" - the episode's core image of Wu Qi using the dead ruler's body as his last political shield.

> "养闲人" - the host's shorthand for Qi's state-sponsored scholar policy around [[JixiaAcademy|稷下学宫]].
