# 452. Custer's Last Stand: The Battle of the Little Bighorn (Part 7)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the opening of the [[BattleOfLittleBighorn]] from the Lakota and Cheyenne encampment on the Greasy Grass through [[MarcusReno]]'s failed assault and [[GeorgeArmstrongCuster]]'s last known message to [[FrederickBenteen]]. It argues that Custer treated escape as the main danger even as scouts warned that the village was exceptionally large, then divided a tired regiment whose ammunition train was already falling behind.

The episode gives the village and its defenders causal agency rather than treating the battle only as a U.S. mistake. It also shows how the absence of survivors with Custer, conflicting recollections, and the gap between urgent orders and observed conduct turn the battle into both a command problem and an exercise in [[BattlefieldReconstructionUnderUncertainty]].

## Key Claims

- The village had grown to roughly 7,000-8,000 people and perhaps 1,800-2,000 warriors through reservation pressure, food constraints, the Rosebud victory, and [[SittingBull]]'s influence.
- Custer advanced with vague operational freedom, strong confidence, fatigued troops, limited reconnaissance, and a mistaken expectation that the village would scatter rather than fight.
- Reports that the Seventh Cavalry had been detected led Custer to attack on 25 June instead of resting and attacking the next morning.
- Dividing the regiment among Reno, Benteen, Custer, and the pack train weakened mutual support while the force approached a much larger opponent.
- Reno halted his initial charge, withdrew into timber, and then lost command cohesion after [[BloodyKnife]] was killed beside him; the retreat became a deadly rout to Reno Hill.
- Native fighters mobilized rapidly under leaders including [[CrazyHorse]] and [[GallLakota|Gall]], while gunfire into the village killed noncombatants and intensified the response.
- Custer's message asking Benteen to hurry and bring ammunition was urgent but not clearly panicked; it is the last firm evidence before his command disappeared from surviving U.S. observation.

## Key Quotes

> "What the Seventh Cavalry can't defeat, the U.S. Army can't defeat." - the episode's rendering of Custer's confidence after scout warnings.

> "Come on. Big village. Be quick. Bring packs." - William Cook's written message conveying Custer's order to Benteen.

## Contradictions

- The episode presents Reno's halt as potentially decisive but also acknowledges that continuing the charge against a much larger village may have produced a different disaster rather than victory.
- Reno is described as drinking, erratic, and panicked, but exact intoxication and motive are retrospective claims rather than directly measurable facts.
- Accounts disagree over whether Sitting Bull gave the wounded Isaiah Dorman water and tried to protect him.
- The hosts infer that Custer still expected a major victory when he sent for Benteen, while the message itself establishes urgency and village size but not his private judgment.
- Village population, warrior numbers, exact timings, speeches, individual actions, and graphic death details remain source-scoped in a popular-history reconstruction.
