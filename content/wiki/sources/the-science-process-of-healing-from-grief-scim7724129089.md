## Summary
This full-length [[HubermanLab]] episode has [[AndrewHuberman]] frame grief as [[GriefAttachmentRemapping]] rather than simple sadness, forced detachment, or a mandatory sequence of emotional stages. The central claim is that healthy grieving can preserve closeness while the nervous system relearns where and when a lost person, animal, or meaningful thing can be encountered. The episode connects that model to [[StructuredGrievingPractice]], [[GriefPhysiologySupport]], memory, motivation, sleep, circadian timing, and professional support while keeping mechanistic and treatment claims source-scoped.

## Key Claims
- [[GriefAttachmentRemapping]] describes attachment through space, time, and emotional closeness; loss disrupts learned predictions of access even after explicit knowledge has registered the absence.
- Yearning is presented as an active motivational and seeking state involving reward-related circuitry, not merely sadness and not proof that one person loved more deeply than another.
- Healthy adaptation does not require weakening the bond: it requires uncoupling emotional closeness from obsolete expectations that the lost attachment figure will appear, call, or remain physically reachable.
- The five stages of grief are treated as historically influential descriptions, not a universal linear sequence through which every bereaved person must pass.
- [[StructuredGrievingPractice]] uses bounded periods of deliberate contact with attachment while limiting guilt-producing counterfactual loops and staying oriented to present space and time.
- [[GriefPhysiologySupport]] includes sleep, morning light, autonomic regulation, NSDR, social care, and professional help as supports for remapping rather than cures for grief.
- Complicated or prolonged grief, depression, trauma, suicidality, and medically significant symptoms require qualified assessment rather than self-directed neuroscience tools alone.

## Key Quotes
> "space, time, and closeness" - the episode's three dimensions of attachment.

> "uncoupling" - the episode's term for separating attachment from prior spatial and temporal expectations.

> "rational grieving" - deliberate acceptance of changed reality while preserving the emotional bond.

## Contradictions
- No settled contradiction found. The full episode corroborates the later Essentials condensation and the Mary-Frances O'Connor interview; because the Essentials episode condenses this material, the two are shared provenance rather than independent replication.
- The episode distinguishes grief from depression but should not be read as a diagnostic test or as a categorical rule about antidepressant response.
- Inferior-parietal, nucleus-accumbens, hippocampal-cell, prairie-vole, oxytocin, epinephrine, vagal-tone, written-disclosure, cortisol, light, sleep, and NSDR claims remain source-scoped research summaries rather than individualized medical or bereavement guidance.
- Dedicated grieving blocks may not fit acute, traumatic, disabling, or prolonged grief without professional support, and the episode explicitly positions its tools as complements rather than replacements for care.
