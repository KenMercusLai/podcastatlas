# Bryan Johnson: I Just Took the Most Powerful Dose of DMT in the World... Here's What It Was Like

## Summary
This [[AllIn|All-In]] episode has [[BryanJohnson|Bryan Johnson]] describing a recent [[5MeODMT|5-MeO-DMT]] self-experiment as part of his broader longevity program. He frames psychedelics not only as ritual, recreation, or psychiatric therapy, but as possible interventions for [[DefaultModeNetwork|default-mode-network]] reset, [[Neuroplasticity|neuroplasticity]], inflammation, metabolism, and subjective psychological rejuvenation.

The hosts press the risk side: psychosis, bad trips, founder life disruption, changed values, family responsibility, and whether radical self-modification preserves personal continuity. The episode then broadens from psychedelics into [[MitochondrialTherapy|mitochondrial therapy]], gene and cell therapies, [[PatientDerivedOrganoids|patient-derived organoids]], [[CellularReprogrammingControlProblem|cellular reprogramming control risk]], [[NewLimit]], and [[GLP1Agonists|GLP-1 agonists]] as signals of a wider biological-intervention era.

## Key Claims
- [[BryanJohnson|Johnson]] says he took [[5MeODMT|5-MeO-DMT]] shortly before recording, using a 9 mg intramuscular dose followed by vaporized dosing, and describes the effect as faster and more overwhelming than prior psychedelic experiences.
- He presents the experiment as part of a ranked longevity-intervention project that had already emphasized exercise, nutrition, sleep, sauna, hyperbaric oxygen, rapamycin, and metformin before psychedelics entered the frame.
- The source treats Johnson's [[PsilocybinMushrooms|psilocybin]] work as the bridge case: he says three 25 mg doses were measured through [[KernelCompany|Kernel]], MRI, and other tools and appeared to change brain connectivity, glucose percentile, inflammation, and microbiome markers.
- Johnson argues that psychedelic value may come partly through disrupting or relaxing the [[DefaultModeNetwork|default mode network]], which he associates with ego, rumination, defensive thought loops, and adult psychological rigidity.
- The source keeps the 5-MeO evidence boundary clear: Johnson says objective 5-MeO results were not available yet, so the episode's strongest claims are immediate subjective reports rather than completed biological data.
- Johnson describes aftereffects as childlike playfulness, faster conversational access, reduced defensiveness in conflict, and a sense of psychological renewal.
- The hosts and Johnson repeatedly stress [[MedicalRiskManagement|medical risk management]]: unknown doses, poor set and setting, predisposition to psychosis, and lack of supervision can make psychedelic use dangerous.
- The episode creates a [[PsychedelicIdentityDisruption|psychedelic identity disruption]] branch by asking whether intense experiences can make founders leave companies, people leave families or religions, or values change enough to alter obligations.
- Johnson says the experience made him more motivated to continue his work, not less, but also says he has no fixed personal limit on future self-modification through neural devices, transgenic systems, plasmids, or gene-expression changes.
- The longevity section links [[MitochondrialTherapy|mitochondrial augmentation]], cell therapy, gene therapy, [[PatientDerivedOrganoids|organoid pretesting]], and Yamanaka-factor reprogramming to the same frontier-health mindset.
- The source frames [[GLP1Agonists|GLP-1 drugs]] as a possible public normalization step for injectable biological interventions, while treating deeper rejuvenation therapies as still emerging and safety-constrained.

## Key Quotes
> "raw consciousness" - Johnson's phrase for the 5-MeO-DMT experience.

> "panic and surrender" - the central choice Johnson says the experience presents.

> "30 or 40 years of psychological rejuvenation" - Johnson's subjective description of the aftereffect.
