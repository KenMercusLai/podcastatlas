## Summary
This [[Banlatte|半拿铁]] China-internet-history episode reconstructs [[ZhouHongyi|周鸿祎]] as a founder shaped by technical curiosity, direct conflict, and repeated fights over internet entry points. It follows him from early computing and failed student ventures into [[3721]], [[YahooChina|Yahoo China]], [[Qihoo360|Qihoo 360]], [[ThreeQWar|3Q 大战]], search, mobile distribution, enterprise security, investing, and founder-media identity. The core synthesis is that Zhou and 360 helped make free security and user-protection rhetoric central to Chinese PC internet competition, while also carrying the trust damage of browser-plugin tactics, public attacks, legal disputes, and hard-edged platform warfare.

## Key Claims
- [[ZhouHongyi|周鸿祎]] is framed as a non-mainstream but central China-internet figure whose career crosses early PC software, Chinese keyword access, security tools, browser traffic, search, smartphones, enterprise security, investing, and livestream-era personal branding.
- [[3721]] began as a solution to the English URL barrier for ordinary Chinese users, but its browser-plugin distribution, popups, difficult uninstall flow, and competitor-blocking behavior made it a defining controversy in [[ChineseInternetEntryPointCompetition]].
- Zhou's refusal to define 3721 as search, including removing search functionality, becomes a missed-opportunity case beside [[Baidu]] and later [[PageRankSearchRelevance]]-style search markets.
- The sale of 3721 to [[Yahoo]] and Zhou's [[YahooChina|Yahoo China]] tenure show both operational gains in mail/search revenue and the limits of operating a fast local internet business inside a slower global company.
- [[Qihoo360|Qihoo 360]]'s 360 Security Guard reversed the 3721 controversy by attacking "rogue software," then used [[FreeSecuritySoftwareStrategy]] to break the paid-antivirus model and build a browser/navigation/software-manager traffic system.
- [[ThreeQWar|3Q 大战]] turned security-software authority and platform control into a public "choose one" crisis with [[Tencent]], after which the episode says Tencent leaned more toward openness and investment while 360 experimented with platformization.
- 360's later search, mobile, content, finance, auto-investing, enterprise-security, and influencer turns show both the value and the limit of a PC-era entry-point company after mobile hardware vendors, closed apps, and new feed platforms changed user distribution.
