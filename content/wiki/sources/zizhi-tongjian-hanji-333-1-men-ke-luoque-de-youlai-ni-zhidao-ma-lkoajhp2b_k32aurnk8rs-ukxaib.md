# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》333-1｜“门可罗雀”的由来，你知道吗？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses [[Shiji|《史记》]] material about [[JiAnWesternHan|汲黯]], [[ZhengDangshiWesternHan|郑当时]], and [[ZhaiGongWesternHan|翟公]] to explain “门可罗雀.” Its central claim is that office, wealth, and rank can attract visitors whose attachment disappears with the advantage, so a reversal of fortune reveals whether a relationship was reciprocal or merely [[PowerContingentSocialTies|权势依附型交往]].

## Key Claims

- The episode presents Ji An and Zheng Dangshi as respected, upright senior officials whose visitors disappear after dismissal and impoverishment.
- It attributes to Sima Qian the judgment that even worthy officials gain guests when powerful and lose them when powerless, making ordinary people's status-dependent treatment unsurprising.
- Zhai Gong is said to have a crowded residence while serving as 廷尉, a nearly empty gate after removal, and returning visitors after restoration to office.
- The episode connects that empty gate to “门外可设雀罗” and the later idiom “门可罗雀.”
- Zhai Gong's gate inscription uses life and death, poverty and wealth, and high and low rank as tests of friendship and social conduct.
- Zheng Dangshi's earlier habit of meeting and seeing off guests around Chang'an makes his later abandonment especially stark.
- The closing discussion generalizes from the stories to modern networking and claims that a clean official who refuses informal rules can face exclusion; this is the host's interpretation rather than a demonstrated institutional rule.

## Key Quotes

> “一死一生，乃知交情；一贫一富，乃知交态；一贵一贱，交情乃见。” - the episode's version of the inscription attributed to Zhai Gong.

> “门外可设雀罗。” - the image the episode connects to the later idiom “门可罗雀.”
