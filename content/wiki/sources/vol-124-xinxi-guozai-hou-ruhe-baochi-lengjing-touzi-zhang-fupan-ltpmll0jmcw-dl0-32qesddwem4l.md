# vol.124.信息过载后如何保持冷静？ | 投资账复盘

## Summary
This [[QizhulouYanBinke|起朱楼宴宾客]] episode is [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]]'s first-quarter investment review, built around information overload, past allocation decisions, and two successful cases in mainland [[RealEstateInvestmentTrust|REITs]] and [[ConvertibleBond|convertible bonds]]. The core argument is that market information, especially around tariffs, geopolitics, and violent price moves, should not be confused with knowledge or immediate action; ordinary investors need a process that separates signals, uncertainty, and behavior. The episode adds a practical 1:1:1 allocation anchor and a [[SleepWellPortfolioTest|sleep-well portfolio test]]: a portfolio is only useful if it lets the investor live normally, keep judgment, and act selectively.

## Key Claims
- [[HowardMarks|Howard Marks]]' April 2025 memo is used as the episode's opening discipline: understand information, ask whether anyone truly knows, and then decide whether action is warranted.
- The host argues that ordinary investors should treat most short-term market shock as [[MacroEventTrendDistinction|macro events rather than macro trends]], especially when the topic is tariffs, trade war, or geopolitical escalation.
- The source extends the show's earlier [[MarketRegimeShift]] thesis from the "伊利里亚计划": deglobalization, labor shortage, higher-for-longer inflation or rates, and commodity exposure remain useful background, but not every expression worked.
- The host reviews earlier emphasis on shareholder returns and Chinese dividend assets: [[Tencent]], [[Alibaba]], and other China internet names improved buybacks, but the first-quarter price catalyst also came from [[DeepSeek]] and AI narrative repricing.
- [[LeveragedETF|Leveraged ETFs]] remain unsuitable as ordinary long-term holdings; the episode revisits [[TQQQ]] and [[NVDL]] to show how [[VolatilityDecay|volatility decay]] can make realized performance worse than a simple multiple of the underlying move.
- The new [[OneToOneToOneAllocation|1:1:1 allocation anchor]] divides the portfolio into China-related risk assets, cash-like defensive assets such as U.S. Treasuries or money-market funds, and income-or-hedge assets such as gold, REITs, structured notes, or other passive-income candidates.
- Mainland [[RealEstateInvestmentTrust|REITs]] produced roughly the expected return, but the review says the host underestimated economic-cycle exposure in highways and logistics REITs, issuer forecast risk, and the possibility that stable-income assets could become scarce and overbid.
- [[ConvertibleBond|Convertible bonds]] fit the host better than options because they combine a credit-risk floor with upside participation; the host still reduced about 70% of the position after large gains while keeping some exposure for possible late-stage overvaluation.
- [[OptionContractMechanics|Options]] were a weaker fit because expiry pressure pushed the host into short-horizon anxiety, showing that a correct direction view can fail if the instrument's time structure conflicts with [[PortfolioSuitability]].
- The final rule is [[SleepWellPortfolioTest|睡眠理论]]: when market exposure disturbs sleep, life, and judgment, the allocation is too aggressive even if the thesis might later prove correct.

## Key Quotes
> "了解信息，不代表你真的知道。" - the episode's warning against mistaking information access for knowledge.

> "不采取行动也是一种行动。" - the source's Marksesque reminder that inaction should still be deliberate.

> "宏观事件更多只能做谈资，能做投资的是宏观趋势。" - the host's boundary between news and allocation.

> "能让自己睡得好的配置比例。" - the closing behavioral test for portfolio sizing.
