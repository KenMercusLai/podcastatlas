# 468. The Murder of Franz Ferdinand: The Crime (Part 4)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode reconstructs the 28 June 1914 assassination of [[FranzFerdinand|Franz Ferdinand]] and [[SophieChotek|Sophie]] in Sarajevo. It presents the murders not as one cleanly executed plan but as a [[SarajevoAssassinationContingency|contingent chain]] of symbolic timing, ignored warnings, light security, a failed bomb attack, the decision to continue the visit, an uncommunicated route change, and a wrong turn that gave [[GavriloPrincip]] a second opportunity.

The episode also centers the couple rather than treating them only as triggers for the [[JulyCrisis|July Crisis]]. Their public tenderness, Sophie's calming role, her refusal to leave her husband after the bombing, and the court's continued enforcement of her lower dynastic status shape both the fatal sequence and its aftermath.

## Key Claims

- The visit took place on Vidovdan, a politically charged Serbian commemorative date, despite police warnings and official awareness that the city would be crowded.
- [[FranzFerdinand]] preferred mobility over heavy protection, while Austro-Hungarian officials limited military presence and relied on roughly 120 Sarajevo police.
- Nedeljko Čabrinović's bomb failed to hit the royal car directly, but Franz Ferdinand chose to continue to City Hall and then insisted on visiting a wounded officer.
- [[SophieChotek|Sophie]] calmed Franz Ferdinand at City Hall and later refused to separate from him, making their relationship operationally relevant as well as emotionally central.
- A revised riverside route was apparently not communicated to the lead driver; the convoy turned near [[GavriloPrincip]], stopped to correct course, and placed the couple within pistol range.
- Princip's two shots fatally wounded Sophie in the abdomen and Franz Ferdinand in the neck; both died soon afterward at the governor's residence.
- The post-assassination funeral preserved the court hierarchy of the couple's morganatic marriage by lowering Sophie's coffin and excluding their children from the ceremony.
- Princip avoided execution because he was nineteen, received a twenty-year sentence, and died of tuberculosis in prison in April 1918.

## Key Quotes

> "Stay alive for our children" - Franz Ferdinand's reported plea to Sophie after the shooting.

> "It is nothing" - his repeated response while dying from the neck wound.
