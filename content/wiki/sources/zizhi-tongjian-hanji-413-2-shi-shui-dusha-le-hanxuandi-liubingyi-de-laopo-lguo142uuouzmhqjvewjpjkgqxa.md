# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》413-2｜是谁毒杀了汉宣帝刘病已的老婆？

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] short episode backfills the origin scene behind the later [[HuoFamilyPurgeAfterHuoGuang|Huo-family purge]] arc. It narrates the poisoning of [[XuPingjunWesternHan|许平君]] in 71 BCE: [[HuoXianWesternHan|霍显]] wants her daughter [[HuoChengjunWesternHan|霍成君]] to become [[EmperorXuanOfHan|汉宣帝]]'s empress, identifies the palace physician [[ChunyuYanWesternHan|淳于衍]] as an access point, and presses her to place toxic aconite into the empress's medicine during childbirth.

The durable synthesis is that the Huo-family crisis begins before Huo Guang's death as a hidden household crime. Huo Xian's ambition, Chunyu Yan's medical access and job-seeking vulnerability, Xu Pingjun's maternity setting, and the use of medicine as the murder vehicle make the later confession, fear, and purge episodes intelligible as consequences of a concealed palace murder rather than only a post-regency power struggle.

## Key Claims

- Xu Pingjun's death in 71 BCE is presented as a planned poisoning rather than a natural childbirth death.
- Huo Xian's motive is to remove the existing empress so Huo Chengjun can become empress and enlarge Huo-family court power.
- Chunyu Yan's palace medical access makes her the operational tool because she can approach Xu Pingjun during pregnancy and delivery.
- Huo Xian recruits Chunyu Yan through a request about her husband's job transfer, combining promised benefit with coercive pressure from Huo-family power.
- The episode explicitly says the poisoning plot is Huo Xian's private action and is hidden from Huo Guang at this stage.
- The murder method turns an approved palace medicine process into a bypassed risk: Chunyu Yan secretly mixes toxic附子 into medicine after other physicians have presented medication.
- Xu Pingjun suspects poison after dizziness and chest distress, but Chunyu Yan denies it before the empress dies.

## Key Quotes

> "这不是自然死亡，而是一场蓄谋已久的谋杀" - the episode's framing of Xu Pingjun's death.

> "汤药变成毒药" - the medical-ethics reversal used to introduce Chunyu Yan.
