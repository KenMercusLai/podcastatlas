# 516. Nelson: God of War (Part 3)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[HoratioNelson|Horatio Nelson]] through the sharply opposed outcomes of 1797: his decisive initiative at the [[BattleOfCapeStVincent1797|Battle of Cape St Vincent]] and the failed [[TenerifeExpedition1797|Tenerife expedition]] that cost him his right arm. It presents Nelson's daring as a product of [[JohnJervis|John Jervis]]'s disciplined but initiative-friendly [[RoyalNavy]] command culture, while using Tenerife to show that courage, honor, and personal leadership could not rescue weak intelligence, difficult terrain, and repeated operational misjudgment.

## Key Claims

- Britain needed to prevent the larger Spanish fleet from joining France, and Jervis accepted battle off Cape St Vincent with 15 ships of the line against a reported 27.
- Jervis's captains were trained to understand the spirit of orders, giving Nelson a command culture within which he could break formation when the Spanish divisions threatened to reunite.
- Nelson's unsupported turn, the arrival of *Culloden* and *Excellent*, and the successive boarding of *San Nicolas* and *San Jose* made Cape St Vincent his first major national breakthrough.
- The episode locates victory in British drilling, seamanship, gunnery, sea experience, and delegated initiative rather than in Nelson's heroism alone, while preserving the battle's extreme violence.
- The Tenerife plan mixed strategic hopes and prize-money incentives with poor local knowledge, strong currents, cliffs, heat, shore defenses, and a failed first landing that should have prompted reassessment.
- Nelson instead invoked national honor, led the second assault conspicuously from the front, was shot in the right elbow, and survived after Josiah Nisbet applied a tourniquet and the arm was amputated aboard *Theseus*.
- Spanish commander Antonio Gutierrez treated the defeated British honorably; the expedition gained nothing, cost about 150 British casualties in the episode's account, and left Nelson fearing professional uselessness before Jervis reassured him.

## Connections

- [[HoratioNelson]] - captain whose initiative produced victory at Cape St Vincent and whose persistence at Tenerife produced defeat and disabling injury.
- [[JohnJervis]] - fleet commander whose discipline, battle plan, and tolerance of intent-based initiative frame the Cape St Vincent victory.
- [[BattleOfCapeStVincent1797]] - naval victory where British preparation and Nelson's formation-breaking turn prevented Spanish concentration.
- [[TenerifeExpedition1797]] - failed amphibious operation exposing the limits of courage and honor under weak preparation.
- [[RoyalNavy]] - institution whose drilling, sea experience, gunnery, and delegated command underpinned the victory but did not prevent expedition failure.
- [[NavalBreakthroughTactics]] - tactical pattern broadened by Nelson's local disruption of an enemy fleet before the later Trafalgar design.
- [[CommandDisobedienceUnderBattlefieldUncertainty]] - framework for judging Nelson's successful reinterpretation of orders without treating outcome as proof that disobedience is generally sound.
