## Summary
This [[PlanetMoney]] episode uses [[AlienFilm|Alien]] and [[AlienRomulus|Alien: Romulus]] to explain why bad jobs are not explained by simple supply and demand alone. [[ArinDube|Arin Dube]] reads [[WeylandYutani]] as an extreme science-fiction version of [[LaborMonopsony]], where workers face hidden contract risks, weak outside options, and employer control over pay and conditions.

The episode turns the franchise's horror into a labor-economics lesson about [[NegativeJobAmenities]], [[CompensatingDifferentials]], [[ShroudedJobAttributes]], [[LaborSearchFrictions]], [[NonCompeteAgreements]], [[MonopsonyByArtifice]], and [[CompanyTownLaborControl]]. [[FedeAlvarez]] then explains that the strongest [[AlienFranchise|Alien]] stories work because they foreground individual powerlessness before a larger corporate machine.

## Key Claims
- [[WeylandYutani]] is treated as a fictional mega-employer whose power makes the visible alien threat inseparable from workplace coercion.
- The Nostromo crew's job has [[NegativeJobAmenities]]: danger, isolation, long time away from home, and exposure to risks the workers did not meaningfully control.
- In a competitive labor market, workers should receive [[CompensatingDifferentials]] for such risks, but the episode questions whether Alien's labor market is competitive.
- The contract clause requiring the crew to investigate possible intelligent transmissions is framed as a [[ShroudedJobAttributes|shrouded job attribute]]: a hidden obligation that becomes costly only after workers are already committed.
- [[LaborMonopsony]] can appear through local market concentration, [[LaborSearchFrictions]], and employer-created barriers, not only through one literal company owning everything.
- [[NonCompeteAgreements]] are presented as a form of [[MonopsonyByArtifice]] because they restrict workers' outside options by design.
- [[LaborMarketCounterforces]] such as minimum-wage law, antitrust enforcement, and unions can push back against employer power.
- The episode's imagined unionized version of Alien shows that a grievance procedure and collective bargaining would make the original horror plot much harder to start.
- [[AlienRomulus]] begins from a mining and farming colony where [[CompanyTownLaborControl]] turns leaving into an administrative impossibility for workers such as Rain.
- [[FedeAlvarez]] connects his attention to labor themes partly to growing up in [[Uruguay]] and later noticing weaker baseline labor protections in the [[UnitedStates]].
