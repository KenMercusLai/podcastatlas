# 151. Valentine's Day

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode traces [[ValentinesDayHistoricalFormation|Valentine's Day]] from uncertain late-antique saint traditions through [[GeoffreyChaucer]] and late-medieval English courtly literature into Georgian and Victorian social custom. Its central claim is that the familiar direct descent from the Roman Lupercalia is weak: the stronger documented path joins birds choosing mates, poetic convention, social games, and surviving Valentine correspondence.

The episode then follows [[ValentineCardCommercialization]] through pre-printed cards, the Penny Post, anonymous correspondence, and British and American card businesses. [[VinegarValentines]] complicate a purely sentimental history by showing that commercially produced Valentines could also ridicule, shame, and insult their recipients.

## Key Claims

- Several late-antique or later saint traditions were attached to the name Valentine, but the earliest martyr narratives do not securely explain the feast's romantic meaning.
- The Roman Lupercalia and Valentine's Day share a mid-February setting, yet the episode finds no clear direct line from the fertility festival to the modern holiday.
- Chaucer's *Parliament of Fowls* and John Gower's bird-mating imagery provide an important late-14th-century English literary bridge between St Valentine's Day, spring, choice, and love.
- Charles, Duke of Orleans, John Lydgate, and the Paston correspondence show Valentine language functioning as romantic address and social selection by the 15th century.
- By the 17th century, a Valentine could be selected romantically, by lot, or through the first suitable encounter of the morning, making the day both courtship and social game.
- Late-Georgian printed cards and verse manuals lowered the skill needed to send a Valentine before the 1840 Penny Post lowered delivery cost and increased anonymity.
- British card production and Esther Howland's American adaptation helped turn the custom into a transatlantic commercial industry later associated with Hallmark.
- Vinegar valentines used the same cheap print and anonymous delivery system for mockery, showing that mass romantic media also enabled social punishment and trolling.
- The 1929 St Valentine's Day Massacre is a later calendrical association rather than evidence about the holiday's romantic origins.

## Key Quotes

> "mechanical" - period label used for machine-made or pre-printed Valentine cards.

> "vinegar valentines" - commercially produced insulting cards that inverted the sentimental form.

## Contradictions

- The episode rejects a simple direct-survival account from Lupercalia to modern Valentine's Day; this is a qualification of a popular origin story rather than a contradiction with an existing canonical page.
- The identities and merger of the saint traditions, the intended feast date in Chaucer, the political occasion of *Parliament of Fowls*, the reason vinegar valentines disappeared, and many precise anecdotes remain disputed or source-scoped.
