# 458. Helen of Troy: A Family of Blood (Part 2)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[HeinrichSchliemann]] from his claim to have found Troy to his excavations at [[Mycenae]], using his spectacular finds and self-mythologizing identifications to test the boundary between archaeological evidence and a desired Homeric story. It retells [[Clytemnestra]]'s revenge in [[TheOresteia|the Oresteia]], where the killing of [[Agamemnon]] and the trial of Orestes turn inherited blood vengeance into a drama of civic judgment.

The episode's strongest synthesis is qualified rather than literal. Schliemann did not find the historical faces or possessions of Homeric individuals, but Linear B titles, palace organization, fortifications, Hittite records, textiles, weapons, and elite female burials may support [[HomericBronzeAgeMemory|Homeric Bronze Age memory]]: later epic can preserve fragments of an older social world without proving that [[HelenOfTroy|Helen]] or Clytemnestra existed as recoverable people.

## Key Claims

- [[HeinrichSchliemann]] joined genuine archaeological discovery to literary obsession, publicity, and false or embellished personal stories.
- The gold mask popularly linked to [[Agamemnon]] predates the conventional Trojan War period by centuries and cannot identify a named Homeric king.
- [[Clytemnestra]]'s killing of Agamemnon is framed as revenge for Iphigenia's sacrifice and as the latest act in a multigenerational family curse, not as motiveless villainy.
- [[TheOresteia|The Oresteia]] uses Orestes' trial to dramatize [[BloodVengeanceToCivicJustice|a transition from blood vengeance to civic adjudication]].
- Linear B's *wanax*, Mycenaean fortifications, Hittite great-power records, palace production, and elite burials make parts of Homer's social world historically plausible without verifying the epic plot.
- [[HelenOfTroy|Helen]] and Clytemnestra may preserve a composite memory of visible, wealthy, diplomatically important Bronze Age women rather than portraits of identifiable individuals.
- Modern readings should preserve the women's agency, coercion, sexuality, violence, and political significance without reducing them to either victims or uncomplicated heroines.

## Key Quotes

> "the face of Agamemnon" - the famous identification attached to Schliemann's Mycenae discovery and rejected by the episode.

> "lord of men" - Agamemnon's Homeric title, connected by the hosts to the Bronze Age term *wanax*.

## Contradictions

- No settled contradiction found. The source strengthens the existing wiki distinction between suggestive historical memory and direct documentary proof.
- Schliemann's Agamemnon identification is rejected because the grave and mask are substantially too early; his Sophia-and-treasure story is also treated as fabrication.
- A historical Helen, Clytemnestra, Agamemnon, or Trojan War is not established. The Hittite-name equivalence, dating, gender-role reconstruction, hair and cosmetic interpretation, political motives, and modern scholarly pendulum remain this episode's source-scoped account.
