# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》933丨为官之道：有所为，有所不为

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the 185 CE late-Han layer before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-936-luanshi-xian-sha-shengmu-xin-zhehua-dui-ma-lml-kuyc7yys2ijh2kt46pwnugar|Hanji 936]] and the later [[HeishanArmy|黑山军]] campaign branch. It starts from the host's officialdom frame of flexibility with boundaries, then follows [[ZhangYanLateHan|张燕]] from [[ZhangNiujiaoLateHan|张牛角]]'s successor into court-recognized mountain administrator, before turning to [[CuiLieLateHan|崔烈]]'s purchased situ appointment and the reputational judgment delivered by [[CuiJunLateHan|崔钧]].

## Key Claims

- The opening frames official life as a balance: a person in office needs flexible posture, attention to superiors, colleagues, and trends, but also a non-negotiable boundary against private greed and power abuse.
- [[ZhangNiujiaoLateHan|张牛角]] and Chu Yan / Chu Feiyan rise during the Yellow Turban disorder; when Zhang Niujiao is hit by a stray arrow during the Yingtao attack, he tells followers to accept Chu Yan as leader and have him take the Zhang surname.
- Chu Yan's agility and battlefield skill explain the "Feiyan" name, while the succession makes him [[ZhangYanLateHan|张燕]] in the episode's narrative.
- Under Zhang Yan, mountain groups gather into the [[HeishanArmy|黑山军]], described as nearly one million people and able to disturb commanderies and counties north of the Yellow River.
- [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s court no longer has enough force to suppress the group, so Zhang Yan's envoy to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]] produces an official grant rather than a punitive campaign.
- The court appoints Zhang Yan to a Zhonglangjiang role, delegates order in the northern Yellow River mountain zone to him, and lets him recommend xiaolian annually and send officials to Luoyang.
- This makes Zhang Yan's early role a case of [[OfficeBasedRebelIncorporation|授官式招安]]: an armed disorder field is partly absorbed through title and delegated administration because direct suppression is unavailable.
- In the same year [[YuanWeiLateHan|袁隗]] is removed as situ and [[CuiLieLateHan|崔烈]], then tingwei, becomes situ after paying five million cash through [[ChengFurenLateHan|程夫人]].
- The episode says senior office purchase normally routes money into West Garden through eunuchs or palace intimates, showing high appointment as a priced court access channel.
- Emperor Ling privately regrets not asking Cui Lie for ten million cash, making the office market look like bargaining over a commodity rather than selecting a minister.
- [[CuiJunLateHan|崔钧]] tells Cui Lie that people once considered him worthy of the Three Excellencies, but after he actually became one they felt disappointed and said he carried a "stink."
- Cui Lie's office purchase therefore becomes [[OfficeSaleLegitimacyDamage|买官名节损耗]]: the transaction obtains rank while destroying the public reputation that made the rank socially credible.
- Cui Jun's Shun analogy, "small stick endure, big stick run," turns the father-son quarrel into a survival-and-judgment joke rather than only a moral accusation.

## Key Quotes

> "有所为而有所不为" - the host's headline rule for official conduct and personal boundaries.

> "臭味" - Cui Jun's reported public judgment on Cui Lie after the purchased office.

> "小杖则受，大杖则走" - Cui Jun's Shun-based answer when Cui Lie calls his flight from beating unfilial.
