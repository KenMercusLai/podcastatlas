# 227. Portugal: On the Edge of the World (Part 1)

## Summary

This [[TheRestIsHistory]] episode follows [[Portugal]] from Roman Lusitania, Visigothic and Muslim rule, and [[AfonsoHenriques|Afonso Henriques's]] kingdom through the 1147 capture of Lisbon, the [[AngloPortugueseAlliance]], and the 1415 conquest of [[Ceuta]]. It presents [[HenryTheNavigator|Henry the Navigator's]] project as a fusion of crusading Christianity, trade, dynastic ambition, navigation, and Atlantic geography rather than as an isolated burst of exploration.

The episode also makes coercion foundational to [[PortugueseMaritimeExpansion]]. Atlantic islands, caravels, African raiding, sugar cultivation, and [[EarlyPortugueseAtlanticPlantationSlavery|early plantation slavery]] developed together, placing Portuguese expansion before Columbus while refusing to separate maritime innovation from enslavement and violence.

## Key Claims

- Portugal's position between Mediterranean invasion routes and the Atlantic helps explain its later sea-facing identity without making geography a complete cause.
- [[AfonsoHenriques]] turned a county around Porto into a kingdom through the victories associated with São Mamede, Ourique, and the 1147 capture of Lisbon.
- The Lisbon siege joined Portuguese royal expansion to the Second Crusade; the episode distinguishes the chronicler's praise of English restraint from reported Flemish and German violations after surrender.
- Commercial treaties, the 1386 Treaty of Windsor, and [[PhilippaOfLancaster|Philippa of Lancaster's]] marriage to [[JohnIOfPortugal|João I]] made the [[AngloPortugueseAlliance]] both economic and dynastic.
- The 1415 capture of [[Ceuta]] moved Portuguese expansion into North Africa but disappointed commercially when trade shifted toward Tangier.
- [[HenryTheNavigator]] joined austere Christian militancy, the search for Prester John, commercial ambition, cartographic knowledge, and maritime experimentation while also failing at Tangier in 1437.
- Sagres, the caravel, and knowledge crossing Christian, Jewish, and Arab traditions helped make Atlantic exploration technically possible, though the episode's institutional picture remains compressed.
- Madeira, the Azores, Cape Verde, and São Tomé became sites where island settlement, sugar, captive trading, and plantation labor formed an [[EarlyPortugueseAtlanticPlantationSlavery|early Atlantic coercive system]].

## Key Quotes

> "oldest ally" - the episode's shorthand for Portugal's unusually durable relationship with England and Britain.

> "illustrious generation" - the label used for the children of Philippa of Lancaster and João I.
