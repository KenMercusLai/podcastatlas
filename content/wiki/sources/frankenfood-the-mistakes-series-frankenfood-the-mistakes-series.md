# Frankenfood | The Mistakes Series

## Summary

This [[RevisionistHistory]] episode uses the backlash against genetically modified tomatoes to argue that product language can make an unfamiliar food technology feel threatening before consumers evaluate its benefits. [[BarbaraStuckey]] treats “lab-grown meat” and “cell-based meat” as risks of the same kind and advances “cultivated meat” as a more appetizing but still differentiating name.

A tasting at [[UpsideFoods]] turns the naming argument into a product-experience test: the cultivated chicken is presented as indistinguishable in taste from conventional chicken, and restaurant sampling is proposed as a way to let sensory evidence precede abstract fear. The episode strengthens [[ProductNamingAsPositioning]] and introduces [[CultivatedMeat]] while leaving cost, regulation, energy use, scalability, and comparative environmental impact largely unexamined.

## Key Claims

- The episode presents the Flavr Savr and Zeneca tomato cases as warnings that technical benefits can be overwhelmed by unfamiliar or frightening language.
- Focus-group participants reportedly reacted negatively to each part of “genetically modified organism,” while “Frankenfood” supplied a vivid metaphor of unnatural scientific overreach.
- [[BarbaraStuckey]] saw similar imagery in “lab-grown meat” and “cell-based meat,” especially when the product was shown in a Petri dish.
- Her team tested roughly one hundred alternatives and selected “cultivated meat” as a term that signals novelty without centering the laboratory.
- [[CultivatedMeat]] is presented as potentially reducing animal suffering, factory farming, water use, methane emissions, and agricultural runoff.
- At [[UpsideFoods]], cultivated chicken made from cells originally taken from an egg reportedly tasted like conventional chicken to the visiting host.
- Restaurant tasting is proposed as an adoption strategy because direct sensory experience may weaken fears created by abstract production language.
- Claims about salmonella, antibiotics, future cholesterol modification, environmental benefit, and large-scale viability remain episode-scoped and are not developed through comparative evidence.

## Key Quotes

> “Frankenfood” - the negative metaphor used to show how a vivid label can dominate a technical product story.

> “cultivated meat” - the preferred term intended to balance differentiation, accuracy, and appetite.

> “tasting is believing” - Upside Foods' stated logic for introducing the product through restaurant experience.
