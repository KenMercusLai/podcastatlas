# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》1000丨郝萌叛乱，竟逼吕布翻墙逃跑？

## Summary
This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode backfills the late-Han sequence immediately before [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1001-weishenme-quan-ni-buyao-fanzhongnu-lolurkcjpie14nnczk0tu7iqerke|Hanji 1001]]. In 196 CE, [[HaoMeng|郝萌]] launches a night revolt at [[Xiapi|下邳]], forcing [[LyuBu|吕布]] to flee to [[GaoShun|高顺]], after which [[CaoXingLateHan|曹性]] turns against Hao Meng and accuses [[YuanShu|袁术]] and [[ChenGongLateHan|陈宫]] of involvement. The episode's main interpretation is [[LyuBuFactionalBalance|吕布集团派系平衡]]: Lü Bu leaves Chen Gong unpunished because he needs Chen Gong's Yanzhou gentry channel to balance the Bingzhou military core represented by Gao Shun and [[ZhangLiao|张辽]].

The second half follows [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s return to [[LuoyangLateHan|洛阳]]. [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]], [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]], [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]], and [[DongChengLateHan|董承]] shape the ruined court's immediate power field, while burned palaces, starvation, and regional non-payment show why the Han court is materially hollow even though the imperial title still restrains open usurpation and sets up Yuan Shu's ambition.

## Key Claims
- In 196 CE, [[HaoMeng|郝萌]] attacks [[LyuBu|吕布]]'s Xiapi headquarters at night, reaching the outer residential area before being stopped by the inner defenses.
- Lü Bu does not initially know who leads the revolt and escapes with his wife over a wall from near the privy area, making the episode an unusually vivid case of internal command fragility.
- [[GaoShun|高顺]] identifies Hao Meng from the rebels' Henei accent, brings troops, and breaks the rebel force before dawn.
- [[CaoXingLateHan|曹性]], a subordinate of Hao Meng, turns against him; Hao Meng wounds Cao Xing, Cao Xing cuts off one of Hao Meng's arms, and Gao Shun kills Hao Meng.
- Cao Xing tells Lü Bu that [[YuanShu|袁术]] encouraged the revolt and names [[ChenGongLateHan|陈宫]] as another participant, while Chen Gong is sitting beside Lü Bu and reportedly blushes.
- The source treats Cao Xing's allegation as politically important but evidentially limited: he was part of Hao Meng's unit and benefited from defection, so his statement cannot by itself settle Chen Gong's exact role.
- The host argues that Lü Bu does not punish Chen Gong because Chen Gong is a major adviser and represents Yanzhou士族 support that Lü Bu cannot discard without leaving the Bingzhou military group too dominant.
- [[LyuBuFactionalBalance|吕布集团派系平衡]] therefore makes survival pressure outrank moral judgment: the immediate question is not only who was guilty, but whether Lü Bu can still hold together the factions needed for rule.
- The source presents Chen Gong's blush and silence as possible political performance or embarrassment over gray contact with Yuan Shu, not as independently proven confession.
- The episode foreshadows Lü Bu's later collapse by noting that Chen Gong will stop advising, Gao Shun will lose troops to others, and Lü Bu will mismanage the very people who once kept him alive.
- In the court branch, [[YangFengLateHan|杨奉]] and [[HanXianLateHan|韩暹]] escort Emperor Xian east while [[ZhangYangLateHan|张杨]] supplies grain and meets the emperor on the road.
- Emperor Xian reaches Luoyang, stays first in the former residence of Zhao Zhong, then moves to the South Palace's Yang'an Hall after a general amnesty.
- Zhang Yang withdraws to Yewang after saying the emperor belongs to the realm and should be assisted by court ministers, while generals defend outside.
- Yang Feng leaves Luoyang for Liang, leaving Han Xian and [[DongChengLateHan|董承]] to guard the ruined court and dominate affairs around the emperor.
- The ruined Luoyang court has symbolic authority but almost no material base: burned palaces, makeshift shelters, starving officials, and non-contributing provinces make the Han house weak without making imperial usurpation publicly easy.

## Key Quotes
> "天子是天下百姓的天子" - Zhang Yang's reported explanation for leaving the court to ministers while armed men guard the outside.

> "生存压力压倒道德审判、派系平衡重于事实真相" - the episode's compact interpretation of Lü Bu's non-punishment of Chen Gong.
