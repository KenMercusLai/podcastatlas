# 《资治通鉴·汉纪》268｜河间献王刘德，如何与《诗经》结缘？（2）

## Summary

This [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇讲透资治通鉴]] episode uses [[LiuDeHejianKingWesternHan|河间献王刘德]]'s death to explain [[PosthumousNamePolitics|谥号政治]] as retrospective official judgment, then argues that such judgment can become conventional praise under pressure from powerful descendants. It reads [[HanWudi|汉武帝]]'s “汤以七十里，文王百里，王其勉之” as a threatening insinuation against a learned vassal king and attributes Liu De's withdrawal and death to that suspicion, while leaving the causal interpretation source-scoped.

## Key Claims

- Posthumous titles are assigned after death as compact official evaluations of a ruler or noble's conduct, rather than names used during life.
- The episode glosses 刘德's “献” as recognition of intelligence and learning and presents early one- or two-character titles as more compact than later extended imperial titles.
- Because officials may fear a deceased ruler's descendants, the program argues that the system can drift toward formulaic praise and away from candid judgment.
- The episode says the *Zizhi Tongjian* does not state Liu De's real cause of death, but it interprets Han Wudi's reference to Tang and King Wen as a warning that Liu De's virtue and cultural standing could be read as ambitions for rule.
- In this account, Liu De understood the warning, stopped reading and teaching after returning to his fief, turned to drinking and entertainment, and died in dejection.
- The host places the confrontation within unresolved central-court conflict with vassal kings and treats cultural and educational influence as a possible source of political mobilization.
- Liu De's relation to the [[Shijing|《诗经》]] promised by the title is not developed in the supplied installment.
