# VOL.189路怒症真相：你忍住了生活的情绪，却在开车时失控

## Summary
This [[ZheBingShuoLaiHuaChang|这病说来话长]] episode has psychiatrist [[CuiJiabinShanxi|崔嘉斌]] and psychological therapist [[ZhangYanshan|张衍山]] explain [[RoadRageAndDrivingEmotion|road rage and driving emotion]] as a safety problem shaped by traffic uncertainty, perceived rule violations, accumulated life stress, temperament, arousal, and the partly anonymous enclosure of the car. It distinguishes ordinary anger from persistent or intense anger that impairs functioning or turns into dangerous action, while noting that “road rage” is not a standalone DSM-5 or ICD-10/11 diagnosis. Its practical emphasis is to restore decision space through slower breathing, lower stimulation, route and time planning, fewer retaliatory maneuvers, ordinary emotional outlets, and adherence to traffic rules.

## Key Claims
- Anger while driving is not automatically pathological; the important boundary is intensity, persistence, functional impairment, and conversion of impulse into unsafe action.
- Congestion, uncertain arrival time, cutting in, phone use, slow obstruction, horns, glare, and perceived unfairness can create a feedback loop in which drivers amplify one another's arousal.
- Strong anger can narrow attention and degrade distance, speed, and risk judgment; intense excitement, conversation, or phone calls can also distract, so the safety issue is broader than anger alone.
- Driving emotion carries conditions from outside the car: work pressure, family demands, weak recovery routines, online hostility, and using speed or overtaking as emotional discharge can all raise risk.
- The enclosed car can produce a limited form of [[Deindividuation|deindividuation]] because other road users are encountered through vehicles, horns, and maneuvers rather than ordinary face-to-face feedback.
- Slow breathing, opening a window when safe, reducing repeated lane changes and horn use, allowing more time, and choosing a better route are presented as immediate regulation aids rather than cures.
- Traffic rules, enforcement, road layout, signs, lighting, and glare control form a system-level prevention layer alongside individual emotion regulation.

## Key Quotes
> “急也急不来。” - Zhang Yanshan's compact reminder that aggressive urgency does not reliably overcome urban traffic constraints.

> “欲速则不达。” - the episode's route-planning and anti-escalation principle.
