# Henry Wilson's Assassination and the Irish Civil War

## 概览

This episode examines the assassination of Field Marshal Sir Henry Wilson in London on 22 June 1922 and why Ronan McGreevey describes it as "Ireland's Sarajevo": a killing followed by an ultimatum that helped trigger a wider conflict.

The discussion first reconstructs Wilson's identity: an Irish-born unionist, senior British soldier, former Chief of the Imperial General Staff, and newly elected Ulster Unionist MP. His hard public line on Ireland made him a hated figure among Irish nationalists, even though the episode notes more nuance in his private views.

The second half turns to responsibility for the killing. McGreevey rejects several explanations and argues that the Irish Republican Brotherhood, ultimately under Michael Collins, most likely sanctioned the attack. The episode then traces how Britain's reaction pushed the Irish provisional government toward action against the anti-treaty occupation of the Four Courts, marking the start of the Irish Civil War.

## 分段落总结

[01:00] **A Shocking Assassination Framed by The Times**

[事实] The episode opens with a 26 June 1922 Times editorial written for Wilson's funeral, portraying his assassination as a crime that horrified the civilized world.

[事实] Wilson had recently stepped down as Chief of the Imperial General Staff and had become MP for North Down in February 1922.

[事实] He was shot outside his own front door in Eaton Place, Belgravia, by two veterans of the British Army who had joined the IRA.

[推测] The editorial's language shows how quickly the killing was interpreted in Britain not only as murder but as a crisis in the Irish settlement.

[03:00] **Why Wilson's Death Matters**

[事实] Dominic describes Wilson's killing as one of the most shocking assassinations in British history and as the trigger for the Irish Civil War.

[事实] Ronan McGreevey's book describes the episode as "Ireland's Sarajevo."

[事实] The discussion is recorded in Dublin's Four Courts, a location central to the outbreak of the Irish Civil War.

[推测] The episode's central argument is that the murder mattered less as an isolated crime than as the event that forced governments and armed factions into open confrontation.

[05:41] **Henry Wilson: Irish Unionist and Imperial Soldier**

[事实] McGreevey says Wilson was born in Dublin in 1864 and raised in County Longford.

[事实] Wilson came from an Irish unionist background linked to the plantations of the seventeenth century, and one ancestor had been with King William at the Battle of the Boyne.

[事实] Wilson served across the British Empire, fought in Burma and the Boer War, and helped prepare the British Expeditionary Force before the First World War.

[事实] In June 1919 he became Field Marshal, the youngest to hold that rank in the British Army since the Duke of Wellington.

[08:31] **Home Rule, the Curragh Incident, and Wilson's Politics**

[事实] Wilson was passionately attached to the union of Ireland and Great Britain.

[事实] During the 1914 Home Rule crisis, he was involved in opposition to implementing Home Rule in Ulster, despite being a soldier expected to follow the government's orders.

[事实] McGreevey says this earned Wilson the lasting enmity of Prime Minister Herbert Asquith and slowed his career until late in the war.

[推测] Wilson's political activism blurred the line between soldier and partisan, making him a more provocative figure in later Irish politics.

[09:46] **Wilson's Hard Line During the War of Independence**

[事实] Wilson believed Britain should respond more forcefully to the IRA in 1919 and 1920.

[事实] He wanted the conflict treated as a military operation and wanted large numbers of British soldiers sent to Ireland.

[事实] David Lloyd George increasingly saw the Irish conflict as requiring a political solution, while Wilson opposed negotiation with people he called murder gangs.

[推测] The clash between Wilson's military framing and Lloyd George's political framing helps explain why Wilson became isolated from the British government.

[11:31] **From Army Chief to Ulster Unionist MP**

[事实] Wilson's term as Chief of the Imperial General Staff ended in February 1922, and he was soon elected unopposed as an Ulster Unionist MP.

[事实] This happened two months after the Anglo-Irish Treaty was signed.

[事实] McGreevey says Wilson became a focal point for Conservative diehards who did not accept the Irish settlement.

[事实] In Ireland, Wilson was viewed as a symbol of British imperialism who argued that Irish self-government would produce anarchy.

[14:13] **The Murder on 22 June 1922**

[事实] On 21 June, Irish nationalists in London saw a newspaper notice that Wilson would unveil a war memorial at Liverpool Street Station the next day.

[事实] Reggie Dunne, Joseph O'Sullivan, and Dennis Kelleher considered killing him at the station, but decided instead to wait outside his home.

[事实] At about 2:30 p.m. Wilson returned to 36 Eaton Place, where Dunne and O'Sullivan shot him six times.

[事实] A later story claimed Wilson drew his sword, but McGreevey says the reliable eyewitness evidence does not support that.

[16:38] **The Killers' Escape and Capture**

[事实] Dunne and O'Sullivan were disabled veterans of the First World War; O'Sullivan had lost his right leg at Passchendaele in 1917.

[事实] As they fled, they shot two policemen, hijacked a taxi, and were pursued by an angry crowd.

[事实] They were eventually beaten, surrounded, taken to George Street Police Station, and later brought to court.

[推测] Their identity as British Army veterans intensified the paradox and shock of the assassination.

[17:25] **Dunne and O'Sullivan: British-Born Irish Nationalists**

[事实] McGreevey calls the assassination a paradox: an Irish-born British imperialist killed by two British-born Irish nationalists.

[事实] Reggie Dunne was 24, Jesuit educated, served in the Irish Guards, was wounded in 1918, joined the Gaelic League, and then joined the IRA in September 1919.

[事实] Joseph O'Sullivan was 25, came from a Fenian family background in West Cork, was educated at St Edmund's College, and came from a family in which six of seven brothers served in the war.

[事实] O'Sullivan later used his hollow artificial leg to hide ammunition cartridges when travelling to Ireland.

[21:02] **Treaty Split and Northern Violence**

[事实] The Anglo-Irish Treaty split nationalist Ireland between pro-treaty and anti-treaty sides.

[事实] In the June 1922 election, pro-treaty or treaty-neutral candidates won almost 80 percent of the vote.

[事实] In Northern Ireland, sectarian violence and conflict involving the IRA and state forces were occurring during what McGreevey calls the original Troubles.

[事实] Anti-treaty forces had occupied the Four Courts from April 1922, and the provisional government had not yet removed them.

[22:20] **The Ultimatum to Collins**

[事实] After Wilson's assassination, Lloyd George blamed the anti-treaty garrison in the Four Courts.

[事实] Lloyd George wrote to Michael Collins saying that if the provisional government did not remove the Four Courts garrison, the British would do it.

[事实] McGreevey compares this sequence to Sarajevo: an assassination followed by an ultimatum.

[推测] This ultimatum made the Four Courts occupation impossible for the provisional government to tolerate indefinitely.

[23:13] **Why the Treaty Split Was So Bitter**

[事实] Eamon de Valera sent negotiators to the treaty talks but then opposed the agreement they signed.

[事实] The treaty was approved in the Dail by 64 votes to 57, but the anti-treaty side did not accept that vote.

[事实] The oath of allegiance was a central issue because IRA members had sworn to establish an Irish Republic and rejected allegiance to a foreign monarch.

[事实] McGreevey says partition was not the main issue in the Civil War debate, partly because many assumed the Boundary Commission would alter Northern Ireland's viability.

[26:25] **The IRA Split and the Four Courts Occupation**

[事实] McGreevey says a majority of the IRA opposed the treaty and, in March 1922, rejected the legitimacy of the provisional government.

[事实] Some senior IRA officers backed the treaty because they saw it as giving real command over an Irish army.

[事实] Collins viewed the treaty as "the freedom to achieve freedom," and McGreevey says Ireland gained substantial independence within twenty-five years.

[事实] Anti-treaty IRA men occupied the Four Courts on 14 April 1922 and remained there when Wilson was killed.

[29:26] **Northern Ireland and Wilson as a Symbol**

[事实] The treaty included Article 12, which provided for a Boundary Commission to examine the border between north and south.

[事实] Northern Ireland had already been established in June 1921, before the treaty was drawn up.

[事实] In March 1922, the northern government appointed Wilson as military adviser and introduced the Special Powers Act.

[事实] McGreevey says Catholic expulsions, burnings, sectarian killings, and massacres were blamed on the northern government, while Wilson became the public face of the crackdown for many Irish nationalists.

[32:07] **Wilson's Public Image and Private Nuance**

[事实] The hosts note that Wilson was more nuanced in private than in his public statements.

[事实] Wilson opposed the Black and Tans because he believed in regular military discipline, not because of humanitarian objections.

[事实] He also opposed sectarian violence and believed the Ulster Special Constabulary should not be a sectarian force, though McGreevey says that was unrealistic at the time.

[事实] Collins publicly accused Wilson and British diehards of trying to restore British domination in Ireland.

[34:07] **Who Ordered the Killing?**

[事实] McGreevey rejects theories that the British government ordered the killing, that Dunne and O'Sullivan acted alone, that the anti-treaty IRA ordered it, or that the pro-treaty National Army ordered it.

[事实] He argues the remaining likely organization is the Irish Republican Brotherhood, a secret oath-bound organization committed to an independent republic.

[事实] Sam Maguire had sworn Dunne and O'Sullivan into the IRB and was connected to Michael Collins.

[事实] McGreevey says the one man who could sanction the attack as IRB president was Michael Collins.

[36:22] **McGreevey's Case Against Collins**

[事实] McGreevey says nobody has been able to contradict his argument and that later evidence from Martin Wallace corroborated Collins's role.

[事实] He believes Collins ordered the assassination in June 1922 after the Pettigo and Belleek incident.

[事实] McGreevey describes a chain in which a woman delivered a letter to Liam Tobin, Tobin delivered it to Sam Maguire, and Maguire delivered it to Reggie Dunne.

[推测] The episode treats Collins's responsibility as a strong historical argument rather than an uncontested fact.

[37:49] **British Shock and the Four Courts Crisis**

[事实] Wilson's funeral was a major public occasion, and politicians in the House of Commons were horrified by the news.

[事实] McGreevey says Wilson was the first British MP assassinated in Britain since Prime Minister Spencer Perceval in 1812.

[事实] Britain used the killing as the basis for an ultimatum to remove the anti-treaty garrison from the Four Courts.

[事实] McGreevey says Lloyd George's evidence linking the assassination to the Four Courts was only a newspaper available on Dublin streets.

[39:14] **The Shelling of the Four Courts**

[事实] The Four Courts had been occupied for over two months and was seen as a standing provocation.

[事实] The anti-treaty side kidnapped Free State general Ginger O'Connell two days after Wilson's killing, giving the provisional government a public trigger for action.

[事实] The pro-treaty side borrowed two British 18-pounder field guns and British shells, positioned them near the Four Courts, and gave the garrison until 3:30 a.m. to leave.

[事实] When the garrison did not leave, shelling began; McGreevey identifies this as the beginning of the Irish Civil War.

[42:20] **The Fate of Dunne and O'Sullivan**

[事实] Irish nationalists discussed rescuing Dunne and O'Sullivan, but McGreevey says they were too closely guarded.

[事实] Collins sent many men to try to rescue them, according to McGreevey, but the attempt was judged hopeless.

[事实] Three men instead considered kidnapping the Prince of Wales at Cowes Regatta, but failed because of Irish accents and faulty intelligence; the Prince was not there.

[事实] Dunne and O'Sullivan's trial at the Old Bailey lasted one day, on 18 July 1922, and they were convicted and hanged.

[43:52] **Echoes in Irish Republican Memory**

[事实] The prosecutor Travis Humphreys had previously acted for Oscar Wilde and prosecuted Sir Roger Casement.

[事实] The hangman John Ellis had also hanged Roger Casement and Kevin Barry.

[事实] Kevin Barry is described as an important figure in Irish Republican mythology, commemorated in song by artists including Paul Robeson, Leonard Cohen, and the Dubliners.

[推测] The episode uses these connections to show how Wilson's assassination sits inside a wider memory of executions, martyrdom, and Anglo-Irish political violence.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode's main value is its ability to connect a dramatic street assassination to the larger mechanics of state formation, treaty politics, and civil war. It turns a single event into a lens on Irish nationalism, British imperial anxiety, and the unstable aftermath of the Anglo-Irish Treaty.

[事实] The strongest material comes from Ronan McGreevey's detailed reconstruction of Wilson, Dunne, O'Sullivan, the Four Courts crisis, and the competing theories about who ordered the murder.

[推测] A limitation is that the Collins argument depends on McGreevey's interpretation of evidence, so listeners should treat it as a historically argued case rather than a universally settled conclusion.

[推测] This episode is especially suited to listeners interested in Irish independence, the origins of the Irish Civil War, political assassination, and the uneasy transition from revolutionary violence to state authority.
