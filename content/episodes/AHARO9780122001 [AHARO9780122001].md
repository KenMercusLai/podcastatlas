# Workshop: Finding Product-Market Fit by Listening to the Email Problem

## 概览

This episode interviews Rick Knudsen, co-founder and CEO of Workshop, an internal communications software company for large organizations. Workshop helps companies coordinate employee communications across channels such as email, SMS, Slack, Microsoft Teams, and SharePoint while giving communicators visibility into reach and engagement.

The core story is Workshop’s slow path to product-market fit. The team initially tried to build a better intranet, but customers repeatedly said their real pain was internal email: deliverability, governance, analytics, and scale. After ignoring that signal for six to nine months, the team rebuilt around email and quickly saw traction.

The episode also covers how Workshop used early marketing before it had a finished product: a weekly newsletter, ungated resources, webinars, and co-marketing with industry experts. Rick frames this as a major reason the company could generate early leads once the product direction became clear.

A recurring lesson is humility after a prior successful exit. Rick says his network, credibility, and seed funding helped, but they did not solve product-market fit; only repeated customer conversations and a willingness to change direction did.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **The Pivot That Changed Workshop**

[事实] Rick says it took about nine months to get roughly three customers for the intranet product, but only 30 days to get 10 customers for the email product.
[事实] He says the team had raised about $3 million and was close to giving money back to investors because the original product was not working.
[事实] His co-founder Derek pushed the team to try solving the email problem and to “listen to the customers.”
[推测] The opening frames the episode around the contrast between founder assumptions and customer demand.

[01:45] **What Workshop Does Today**

[事实] Workshop is a multi-channel internal communications platform for large companies.
[事实] The product helps communications teams collaborate, reach employees across channels, and understand engagement data.
[事实] Rick says Workshop has about 140 employees, is based in Omaha, has a small team in Ireland, serves just under 1,000 customers, and is at eight-figure ARR.
[推测] Workshop is positioned less as an employee-facing destination and more as infrastructure for communications teams.

[03:31] **Flywheel as the First Company**

[事实] Rick founded Flywheel in 2012 as a WordPress hosting platform focused on creative agencies.
[事实] Flywheel helped developers build websites, collaborate with clients, bill clients, and manage website projects through its hosting platform.
[事实] Flywheel was sold in 2019 with about 30,000 customers and more than 200 employees.
[推测] Rick’s experience scaling Flywheel gave him operational knowledge but did not directly reveal the right Workshop product.

[04:55] **The Origin of the Internal Communications Idea**

[事实] At Flywheel, fast growth and remote support teams made internal communication a constant challenge.
[事实] Rick used HubSpot for internal emails to make communication more visual and measurable, but that interfered with marketing data.
[事实] The company later used a duct-taped mix of Mailchimp, HubSpot, and Slack for internal communication.
[事实] After Flywheel was acquired by WP Engine, Rick saw internal communication at a larger scale during company integration and the shift to remote work in March 2020.

[08:06] **A Market Built Around Destinations**

[事实] Rick observed that many internal communications tools were built around employee destinations such as intranets or portals.
[事实] He felt many tools came from a pre-Slack and pre-Teams era, before companies had many always-on communication channels.
[事实] This led him to see an opportunity for a more modern communications platform.
[推测] The market gap was not simply another intranet, but a way to coordinate existing channels.

[09:07] **Starting Workshop With a Mission First**

[事实] In mid-2020, Rick reconnected with two Omaha-based co-founders who had recently sold their own bootstrapped software company.
[事实] They believed remote and flexible work would make digital-first employee communication more important.
[事实] Before choosing the exact product, they wrote a mission: to create more happy Mondays for employees everywhere.
[推测] The broad mission gave the team room to change product direction without abandoning the company’s purpose.

[11:12] **The Failed Intranet Hypothesis**

[事实] The team initially believed no one loved their intranet and that a better-designed intranet could be a meaningful product.
[事实] Customer conversations showed intranets were often a catch-all for different problems such as employee directories, files, and communication gaps.
[事实] Rick says they could not find a clear through line for the specific problem an intranet solved.
[事实] Customers kept saying email was their number one channel and that they lacked visibility into performance.

[14:03] **Why They Ignored Email at First**

[事实] Rick says he initially thought email was too simple and that customers could use tools such as Mailchimp.
[事实] He attributes part of the delay to ego after a successful exit and a desire to build something more novel.
[事实] The team ignored the email signal for six to nine months even though it kept coming up in customer conversations.
[推测] The episode presents this as the main founder mistake: dismissing a repeated, unglamorous customer pain.

[15:03] **Why Enterprise Email Was Actually Hard**

[事实] Rick says large companies face problems with spam filtering, security layers, governance, brand control, analytics, and deliverability.
[事实] Sending important messages to tens or hundreds of thousands of employees within minutes is technically difficult.
[事实] External marketing tools are not built for the internal governance and infrastructure needs of large enterprises.
[推测] Workshop’s opportunity came from treating internal email as an enterprise infrastructure problem rather than a simple newsletter problem.

[16:48] **Workshop as a Distribution Layer**

[事实] Rick says existing tools such as intranets and mobile apps often require employees to log into a destination, but engagement is low.
[事实] Workshop focuses on channels employees already use, including email, text, Slack, Microsoft Teams, and SharePoint.
[事实] Internal communication teams, department heads, leaders, and executives use Workshop, while employees may not know Workshop exists.
[事实] Workshop provides distribution and analytics across these channels.

[18:43] **Early Customers and the Happy Monday Club**

[事实] Rick believes founders should market for a year before they sell anything.
[事实] Workshop started the Happy Monday Club newsletter on day one; Rick says it has 50,000 subscribers now.
[事实] Early prospects came through the newsletter, social networks, co-branded opportunities, other newsletters, and communities.
[事实] Huddle, a Nebraska sports technology company, acted as an early design partner and did not pay Workshop at first.

[20:55] **Rebuilding Around Email**

[事实] In September 2021, the team decided to rebuild the platform around email after struggling with the intranet product.
[事实] When they emailed prospects about the email product, many people showed interest.
[事实] Rick says the team got 10 customers in 30 days for the email product after getting only about three customers in nine months for the intranet.
[推测] This was the clearest product-market fit signal in the company’s first year.

[22:24] **Listen to Learn**

[事实] Rick says one of Workshop’s core values, “listen to learn,” came from the first 12 months of ignoring then finally accepting customer feedback.
[事实] He says the second company was still hard despite the prior exit.
[事实] He emphasizes leaving ego aside and listening because customers will tell founders what to build.
[推测] The lesson is especially relevant for repeat founders who may over-trust their previous pattern recognition.

[23:34] **Inbound Marketing as the Early Growth Engine**

[事实] Rick brought an inbound marketing mindset from the hosting industry.
[事实] Workshop consistently published the newsletter every week and still does.
[事实] The company built ungated resources such as internal communications calendars and benchmark reports instead of relying on gated eBooks.
[事实] Rick says long-tail SEO and a large resource library created many inbound leads over time.

[26:01] **Webinars and Co-Marketing**

[事实] Workshop runs webinars every month, sometimes twice a month.
[事实] Rick says the first webinar had about five attendees, while a recent one had about 500.
[事实] He recommends inviting industry experts with audiences and committing to the format for a long period.
[推测] Workshop’s content strategy depended more on consistency and audience borrowing than on one-off campaigns.

[27:17] **Creating Content Without Being the Original Expert**

[事实] Rick says founders may feel imposter syndrome when creating content for an industry they did not come from.
[事实] Workshop’s CMO Jamie Bell, who came from Flywheel, helped lead content.
[事实] The team learned from sales calls, demos, and conversations, then converted repeated customer pains into content.
[事实] Rick says after talking to 100 people, a team can develop broader perspective than any single customer.

[29:43] **Mission, Values, and Culture**

[事实] Rick says Workshop intentionally wrote the mission early but waited about 12 months to write core values.
[事实] He learned from Flywheel that real values often emerge organically through repeated behavior.
[事实] Workshop’s broad mission also supports resources, community building, and an internal communications conference in Omaha.
[事实] Rick says values help later teams make autonomous decisions by reflecting how the founding team approaches problems.

[34:07] **Optimism as a Hiring Trait**

[事实] One of Workshop’s core values is “look on the bright side.”
[事实] Rick says optimism does not mean ignoring problems; it means believing there is a solution.
[事实] He considers optimism especially important in early employees because hard customer problems require people who want to solve them.
[推测] For Rick, optimism is an operating trait rather than a decorative culture statement.

[35:09] **Pricing Around Audience and Channels**

[事实] Workshop does not use seat-based pricing as the main model.
[事实] Pricing is based on audience size and the communication channels a customer buys, such as email or SMS.
[事实] Workshop previously charged for additional users past a limit but removed that friction.
[事实] Rick says retention improves when multiple departments use the product weekly and when automation is embedded in the customer’s workflow.

[37:40] **What Prior Success Did and Did Not Buy**

[事实] Rick says his prior exit made the seed round easier, and Workshop raised about $3 million early.
[事实] He chose to raise money partly because investors create accountability and velocity.
[事实] Around month nine, with almost no customers, he considered returning the money.
[事实] Rick says capital, relationships, and credibility could not solve product-market fit.

[40:22] **Recognizing Product-Market Fit**

[事实] Rick says product-market fit is hard to define but that founders often know it when they see it.
[事实] He describes the shift as a moment when nothing worked and then suddenly something worked.
[事实] He says founders need to notice why something is working and double down on it.
[推测] Workshop’s product-market fit came from narrowing the problem, not expanding the product vision.

[42:36] **Lightning Round Advice**

[事实] Rick’s business advice is to protect A players and make sure great people get to work with great people.
[事实] He recommends hiring slowly and firing quickly.
[事实] He recommends business memoirs, especially “Shoe Dog” and Bob Iger’s “The Ride of a Lifetime.”
[事实] He says quarterly company offsites are expensive but valuable for planning and alignment.

[44:49] **Productivity and Life Outside Work**

[事实] Rick says he treats email like a to-do list and recently built a Claude agent that turns saved Slack items into emailed tasks.
[事实] Omar describes using AI tools to categorize and move emails out of his inbox.
[事实] Rick says his main passion outside work is spending time with his nine-year-old daughter.
[推测] The closing reinforces Rick’s practical bias toward systems that reduce noise and improve follow-through.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it gives a concrete account of product-market fit as a messy process rather than a clean framework. The most useful parts are the details around ignoring customer signals, discovering why enterprise email was harder than it looked, and seeing traction only after the team narrowed its focus.

A strong theme is that marketing before product clarity can still be productive if it builds audience, customer access, and market learning. The newsletter, ungated resources, webinars, and community efforts were not just acquisition channels; they also helped Workshop understand the buyer and the problem.

The limitation is that Workshop’s starting position was not typical: Rick had a prior exit, a network, funding access, and an experienced team. The episode acknowledges this, but early-stage founders without those advantages may need to adapt the tactics to a smaller budget and longer timeline.

[推测] This episode is especially useful for SaaS founders working through early positioning, second-time founders at risk of overconfidence, and teams selling into enterprise workflows where the real pain may be hidden behind a seemingly boring problem.
