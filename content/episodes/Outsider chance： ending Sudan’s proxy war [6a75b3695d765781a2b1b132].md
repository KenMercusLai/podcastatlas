# Outsider chance: ending Sudan’s proxy war

## 概览

This episode examines Sudan’s civil war as a proxy conflict sustained by foreign backers, even after the Sudanese armed forces retook Khartoum. The discussion stresses that the capital remains devastated, while fighting and drone attacks continue elsewhere.

The key conclusion is that outside powers may be necessary for any settlement, but their competing interests in Sudan’s resources, Red Sea position and political future also make peace hard to reach. Even a negotiated ceasefire would be difficult to enforce because both main armed camps have become fragmented coalitions.

The episode also covers the Gordie Howe Bridge between Detroit and Windsor as a symbol of strained US-Canada trade relations, and closes with a tribute to assemblage artist Betye Saar, whose work transformed discarded objects into art about slavery, race and liberation.

## 分段落总结

[01:12] **Episode setup**

[事实] Jason Palmer introduces the episode of The Intelligence from The Economist.

[事实] The episode’s stated topics include Sudan’s war, a Canadian bridge caught in a Trump-era trade dispute, and a tribute to artist Betye Saar.

[01:45] **Sudan’s ruined health system**

[事实] Before the war, the hospital described had almost 1,000 beds, many services, doctors, nurses and patients.

[事实] Engineer Hafiz Ibrahim says RSF members were stationed in the hospital and fighting with the army left parts of it destroyed.

[事实] The segment describes Sudan’s war as the world’s worst humanitarian crisis, with millions displaced and possibly hundreds of thousands killed.

[03:07] **Khartoum after the army’s return**

[事实] Tom Gardner says Khartoum feels like a quiet, empty, pitch-black “ghost city.”

[事实] The army retook the capital from the Rapid Support Forces, but large areas around the presidential palace and airport remain devastated.

[事实] Buildings are often still standing, but many have been methodically looted.

[事实] Some poorer neighborhoods have more life, while richer areas remain largely empty because services are absent and safety is uncertain.

[05:21] **Balance of forces**

[事实] The Sudanese armed forces controls Khartoum and much of the east and north.

[事实] The Rapid Support Forces still controls much of western Sudan, especially Darfur.

[事实] Gardner says evidence points to significant UAE backing for the RSF, though the UAE denies this.

[事实] The army is described as having support from Saudi Arabia, Egypt and especially Turkey.

[推测] The war is prolonged because external sponsors are unlikely to allow their preferred Sudanese partners to lose outright.

[06:22] **Drone war and El Obeid**

[事实] Gardner says no part of Sudan is entirely safe because both sides have significant drone arsenals.

[事实] He saw civilian infrastructure including hospitals, markets and gas stations destroyed by drones.

[事实] El Obeid in North Kordofan is described as a major hotspot under sustained RSF aerial assault.

[推测] If the RSF captured El Obeid, it could reconnect western fronts and threaten Khartoum from the west again.

[07:47] **Displacement and camp conditions**

[事实] Millions of Sudanese have fled to neighboring countries such as Chad, Egypt and South Sudan.

[事实] Most displaced people remain inside Sudan, many in camps with very limited food, health care and water.

[事实] A woman named Raja, who fled El Obeid, says shelling happened two or three times a day and drones struck in the evening.

[事实] She says people who missed food distribution before drone strikes could be left without food until the next day.

[08:46] **Foreign interests in Sudan**

[事实] Foreign backers see Sudan as strategically important because of its Red Sea coastline, gold reserves and arable land.

[事实] Sudan has around 800 kilometers of Red Sea coastline along a vital and contested waterway.

[事实] Gardner links the army’s recent battlefield successes to weapons supplies, including drones, from Turkey.

[推测] External powers are trying to preserve influence over Sudan’s future economy, security position and resources.

[10:10] **The Quad and peace obstacles**

[事实] The “quad” consists of America, Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the UAE.

[事实] Its purpose is to seek an agreed political future for Sudan and then push local partners toward a ceasefire.

[事实] Gardner says the war in Iran has absorbed attention and complicated the regional picture.

[事实] He says any ceasefire would be hard to implement because both the SAF and RSF are fragmented coalitions rather than tightly controlled blocs.

[推测] Even if outside powers reach a diplomatic breakthrough, enforcement inside Sudan may remain the central obstacle.

[13:31] **Gordie Howe Bridge opens amid tension**

[事实] Annie Crabill describes crossing the Gordie Howe Bridge, which connects Detroit, Michigan with Windsor, Ontario.

[事实] The bridge took more than 14 years to develop and was meant to symbolize cross-border cooperation.

[事实] It is named after Canadian hockey legend Gordie Howe, who played 25 seasons with the Detroit Red Wings.

[15:16] **Why the bridge matters**

[事实] The Detroit-Windsor crossing handles about $94 billion in annual trade.

[事实] Before the new bridge, trucks mainly relied on the privately owned Ambassador Bridge.

[事实] The Ambassador Bridge charges high tolls and does not directly integrate with Canada’s highway system.

[事实] A trucking company owner says the new bridge already saves time, fuel, idling costs and wear on trucks.

[16:31] **Trump’s bridge dispute**

[事实] In February, Donald Trump attacked the bridge on Truth Social, threatened to block it and demanded a larger American ownership stake.

[事实] Canada paid the bridge’s six billion Canadian dollar construction cost upfront and planned to recover costs through tolls.

[事实] A later agreement in principle would send 50% of net profits into an American fund over 15 years.

[推测] Crabill suggests Trump may not have gained much beyond delaying the project and making Canada appear to renegotiate.

[18:18] **Trade war symbolism**

[事实] Trump later announced new 50% tariffs on several Canadian goods starting in August.

[事实] Canadians uninvited Americans from the July 24 bridge opening ceremony.

[事实] Crabill says the bridge should have been a case study in mutual benefit.

[推测] The bridge dispute may have hardened Canadian public opinion and encouraged Mark Carney to take a tougher stance in trade talks.

[21:03] **Betye Saar and speaking objects**

[事实] Anne Rowe says Betye Saar found meaning in discarded objects from flea markets, yard sales and market stalls.

[事实] Saar believed these objects carried the energy of previous owners and could be made into art.

[事实] She became one of America’s most famous assemblage artists in the mid-20th century.

[21:59] **Childhood influences**

[事实] Saar grew up in Watts, then separate from Los Angeles.

[事实] As a child, she searched for beads, coins, shells and other small treasures to transform.

[事实] The Watts Towers influenced her by showing how discarded plates, tiles and other materials could become something beautiful.

[22:58] **Art, slavery and Black history**

[事实] Objects such as rope, ships, chains and birdcages carried associations of slavery for Saar.

[事实] As a Black woman, she felt the weight of history in these objects.

[事实] She increasingly incorporated such objects into artworks as commentary on slavery.

[23:38] **The liberation of Aunt Jemima**

[事实] Saar became especially known for reworking the image of the Black mammy figure.

[事实] In 1968 she transformed an Aunt Jemima notebook-holder figurine by adding a rifle, a hand grenade and a Black Power fist.

[事实] She called the work The Liberation of Aunt Jemima.

[事实] The piece touched a national nerve and made Saar feel she had become an artist.

[25:16] **Recognition and continued themes**

[事实] Saar studied design in college because Black students were not allowed to study pure art at the time.

[事实] She taught herself printmaking, enamelling and watercoloring.

[事实] In 1974, the National Endowment for the Arts gave her a scholarship and grant.

[事实] She continued exploring Black slavery and restricted life for decades.

[26:58] **Late work and final vision**

[事实] Saar kept working into her 90s.

[事实] Some late immersive works used canoes carrying pleading Black hands or birdcages filled with branches and stones.

[事实] Saar saw these works as journeys from one place or state of consciousness to another.

[事实] The segment says she preferred working in silence while still imagining the objects in her studio asking to be given life.

## 播客点评/总结

The strongest part of the episode is the Sudan segment, which connects battlefield reporting, civilian suffering and regional geopolitics without treating the war as a distant abstraction. It gives listeners a clear sense of why Khartoum’s recapture does not mean the conflict is close to ending.

The bridge segment is valuable as a concrete example of how symbolic political fights can disrupt infrastructure and trade that both countries benefit from. Its limitation is that it focuses mainly on the political dispute rather than offering much detail on the longer-term economic effects.

The Betye Saar tribute gives the episode a different register, moving from geopolitics to cultural memory. It is especially effective in showing how Saar used discarded materials to challenge racial stereotypes and make historical violence visible through objects.
