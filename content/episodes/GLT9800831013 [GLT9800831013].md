# The Rest Is History: Luther’s Excommunication and Tolkien’s Birthday

## 概览

This episode marks 3 January in the show’s anniversary series, pairing Martin Luther’s formal excommunication in 1521 with the birth of J.R.R. Tolkien in 1892.

The first half explains why Luther’s break with Rome became irreversible: his challenge was not just political or institutional but theological, aimed at the authority of canon law, the papacy, and the Catholic Church’s claims about salvation.

The second half turns to Tolkien’s childhood, Catholicism, and the world behind *The Lord of the Rings*, especially its suspicion of industrial modernity and its Christian themes of weakness, mercy, providence, and grace.

## 分段落总结

[00:56] **A 3 January Anniversary Episode**

[事实] The hosts introduce the episode as part of their “12 days of Christmas” anniversary series, with each host choosing one event connected to the date.
[事实] Tom’s first subject is Martin Luther, described as central to the Reformation and to an earlier episode comparing Reformation print culture with social media.
[事实] The episode frames 3 January 1521, Luther’s excommunication, as a moment when the Reformation schism was formally marked.

[02:23] **Luther’s Road To Excommunication**

[事实] The hosts note that Luther’s 1517 “95 theses” episode is commonly seen as the beginning of the Protestant Reformation, though they also mention he probably did not nail them to the church door.
[事实] Luther’s objections are described as theological and doctrinal challenges to the structures upheld by the Catholic Church and the papacy.
[事实] Cardinal Cajetan attempted to negotiate with Luther, but Luther returned to Wittenberg, where Frederick of Saxony’s protection made him relatively safe from papal reach.
[事实] Pope Leo X issued the papal bull *Exsurge Domine* on 15 June 1520, warning Luther that he would be excommunicated unless he retracted heretical statements.

[05:03] **Burning The Papal Bull**

[事实] On 10 December 1520, Luther publicly burned the papal bull at one of Wittenberg’s city gates.
[事实] The hosts describe the site as a kind of municipal rubbish-burning place, where Luther also burned books of canon law.
[事实] Luther is said to have called the pope Antichrist and to have been actively courting excommunication.
[事实] His formal excommunication followed on 3 January 1521.

[06:16] **Luther’s Theology And The Schism**

[事实] Luther’s motive is summarized as his belief that he was doing God’s will.
[事实] The hosts describe Luther’s core challenge as the idea that salvation is God’s gift and does not require the Catholic Church’s institutional “superstructure.”
[事实] Luther emphasized personal relationships with God, the spirit, and scripture, and came to see the papacy as Antichrist.
[事实] The hosts list doctrines associated with Luther’s challenge, including *sola scriptura*, private judgment, rejection of papal authority, and attacks on canon law and Catholic teaching.
[推测] The episode presents Luther’s excommunication as inevitable once his theology rejected the premises on which medieval Catholic authority rested.

[09:00] **Modern Ecumenical Efforts**

[事实] The hosts mention a 2020 proposal from the Altenberg Ecumenical Round Table connected to the anniversary of Luther’s excommunication.
[事实] The proposal imagined the pope withdrawing the excommunication while the Lutheran World Federation would agree that the pope was not Antichrist.
[事实] The hosts say this did not happen and note that the Lutheran World Federation could not necessarily speak for all Lutherans.
[推测] The discussion uses the proposal partly to show that the Luther-Catholic divide remains historically alive, even when treated humorously.

[10:07] **Luther In Popular Memory**

[事实] The hosts discuss a Martin Luther Playmobil figure sold in Germany around the 500th anniversary of the theses.
[事实] They say the Luther Playmobil figure sold extremely well, possibly becoming one of Germany’s best-selling Playmobil figures.
[事实] Dominic describes using the figure in childhood-style battles involving Protestant armies and papal forces.
[推测] This section lightens the theological discussion while showing Luther’s continuing symbolic force in German and Protestant memory.

[11:25] **Transition From Luther To Tolkien**

[事实] The hosts joke that Catholic listeners may have been alienated by the Luther section.
[事实] They introduce J.R.R. Tolkien, born on 3 January, as the Catholic counterweight to the Lutheran first half.
[事实] They say Tolkien was Dominic’s choice for the day’s anniversary.

[12:55] **Tolkien’s Birth And Family Background**

[事实] Tolkien was born on 3 January 1892 in Bloemfontein, in the Orange Free State.
[事实] The hosts describe Tolkien’s family as having German roots, tracing them to East Prussia, around Königsberg, now Kaliningrad.
[事实] Tolkien’s father, Arthur Tolkien, was a bank manager posted to Bloemfontein with his wife, Mabel.
[事实] Tolkien’s younger brother was named Hilary and was born in 1894.

[15:06] **Loss, Childhood, And Birmingham**

[事实] When Tolkien was three, his mother took him and his brother back to England for a visit while his father stayed behind temporarily.
[事实] Arthur Tolkien died of rheumatic fever before he could join them, so Tolkien did not remember his father.
[事实] The family stayed in England, living first with Mabel’s parents and later in Sarehole, then a village in north Worcestershire and now part of suburban Birmingham.
[事实] The hosts identify the local mill and landscape around Sarehole as inspirations for elements of *The Lord of the Rings* and the Shire.

[16:33] **The Lost Shire And Industrial Modernity**

[事实] The hosts connect Tolkien’s childhood landscape to the Shire and the later spread of Birmingham suburbia to the “scouring” or loss of that world.
[事实] Tolkien’s mother died when he was 12, and a Catholic priest helped arrange for the Tolkien boys to live with an aunt in Edgbaston.
[事实] From that urban setting, Tolkien looked out onto factories, warehouses, shops, and the industrial city.
[推测] The hosts interpret Tolkien’s nostalgia, horror of industry, and sense of lost paradise as rooted in these childhood experiences.

[17:54] **The Lord Of The Rings As A Green, Anti-Urban Novel**

[事实] The hosts describe *The Lord of the Rings* as one of the great anti-urban novels and as a “green” novel.
[事实] They connect Mordor and Saruman with industrialization, machinery, fire, and destructive ingenuity.
[事实] They argue that even listeners who dislike Tolkien or fantasy should recognize the book’s importance as a mirror of 20th-century concerns.
[推测] The episode treats Tolkien’s fantasy not as escapism alone, but as a way of addressing modernity, industrialization, and power.

[18:55] **Tolkien, War, And The 20th Century**

[事实] One host says he wrote about Tolkien in a chapter dealing with the First World War, fascism, the Second World War, and the Holocaust.
[事实] The discussion notes Tolkien’s attitudes to Jews, Nazis, and RAF firebombing, while also saying Tolkien denied that *The Lord of the Rings* was an allegory.
[事实] Tolkien fought in the trenches at the Somme, and his son flew over occupied Germany.
[事实] The host argues that *The Lord of the Rings*, more than *1984*, may be the great novel about the horrors of the 20th century.

[20:34] **The Last Great Popular Christian Work**

[事实] The hosts describe *The Lord of the Rings* as the bestselling novel of the 20th century.
[事实] They argue that the book gave readers suspicious of Christian orthodoxy, scripture, or belief a way to experience Christianity’s appeal and power.
[事实] They identify distrust of power and strength, and the celebration of smallness, as central Christian elements in the work.
[推测] The episode frames Tolkien’s Christianity as embedded in the moral structure of the story rather than expressed through direct religious instruction.

[21:07] **Frodo, Gollum, Mercy, And Grace**

[事实] The hosts discuss the ending of *The Lord of the Rings*, where Frodo does not destroy the ring but claims it for himself.
[事实] They describe Frodo as weak, human, and fallible, and connect the resolution to divine grace in Tolkien’s letters.
[事实] They explain that mercy toward Gollum becomes crucial, because Gollum bites off Frodo’s ring finger and falls, destroying the ring.
[事实] The destruction of the ring occurs on 25 March, which the hosts identify as traditionally linked to both the crucifixion and the incarnation.
[推测] The hosts treat this date and the Gollum episode as a rare but highly significant Christian signal inside a work with few overt religious references.

[22:28] **Closing And Future Episodes**

[事实] The hosts stop short of a fuller Tolkien discussion because they say multiple Tolkien episodes are planned for later.
[事实] They joke that the episode has won back Catholics while keeping Protestants on side.
[事实] They preview the next episode as having existentialist and abolitionist themes.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strength is its ability to make two anniversaries feel connected: Luther and Tolkien become a Protestant-Catholic pairing, but also two ways of discussing faith, authority, modernity, and cultural memory.

[推测] Its best moments come when the hosts move from factual chronology into interpretation, especially Luther’s theology of salvation and Tolkien’s use of mercy, smallness, and grace in *The Lord of the Rings*.

[推测] The limitation is compression. Luther’s excommunication and Tolkien’s religious imagination are both enormous topics, and the episode treats them as anniversary sketches rather than full historical studies.

[推测] This episode suits listeners who enjoy brisk historical conversation, religious history, Reformation debates, Tolkien, and literary readings of modernity, but it may feel too fast or joking for anyone wanting a systematic theological account.
