# Planet Money’s “Sell Me a Sasquatch” Enters Big-Box Retail

## 概览

This episode follows Planet Money’s attempt to turn its economics-inspired board game, **Sell Me a Sasquatch**, into a real retail product. The game was created with Exploding Kittens and is built around the idea of asymmetric information from “The Market for Lemons,” but translated into a party game about trading mythical creature cards.

The central story is the path into big-box retail: a high-pressure line review pitch, buyer concerns about audience and price, months of waiting, and finally orders from two major U.S. retailers. Along the way, the episode explains how scarce shelf space, planograms, pricing, packaging, manufacturing deadlines, royalties, and retail placement all shape whether a product reaches store shelves.

The episode ends with the game being manufactured in China and later appearing in major stores including Target and Walmart, with listeners credited for playtesting and contributing creature ideas.

## 分段落总结

[00:25] **The Game Project And The Retail Stakes**

[事实] Planet Money worked with Exploding Kittens to create a board game called **Sell Me a Sasquatch**.

[事实] The game is inspired by the Nobel Prize-winning economics paper “The Market for Lemons,” especially the problem of asymmetric information.

[事实] The hosts describe the game as a way to report on how products get onto shelves at major big-box retailers.

[推测] The board game functions both as a consumer product and as a reporting device for understanding retail gatekeeping.

[05:58] **Why Big-Box Retail Matters**

[事实] The episode says 81% of U.S. retail buying still happens at brick-and-mortar locations, citing a Capital One report.

[事实] The hosts name Walmart, Kroger, Costco, Home Depot, and Target as major parts of American retail life.

[事实] Previous Planet Money projects did not give the team this kind of access to big-box retail.

[推测] The episode frames physical retail as still economically powerful despite the prominence of online shopping.

[07:23] **Preparing For The Pitch**

[事实] Erika Barris flies to Los Angeles to visit Exploding Kittens’ headquarters before a meeting with representatives from an unnamed big-box retailer.

[事实] Exploding Kittens is preparing 13 prototype games to pitch, including Planet Money’s game.

[事实] The retailer’s name and the actual pitch meeting are withheld because Exploding Kittens said those business conversations are sensitive.

[推测] The secrecy reinforces how commercially important buyer meetings are for game companies.

[09:09] **Line Review And The Buyer’s Job**

[事实] The pitch is part of a process called line review, where retail buyers decide what products stay, leave, or get added for a coming season.

[事实] The episode says line review happens twice a year.

[事实] Erika is told she may have only three to five minutes to make the case for the game.

[事实] Exploding Kittens wants Erika to emphasize Planet Money’s audience, project history, and commitment to supporting the game.

[12:34] **Betting On The Person Behind The Product**

[事实] Exploding Kittens’ co-founder says buyers are trying to predict the future, which is not possible.

[事实] He tells Erika that buyers often have to bet on the person behind a product and whether that person will help make it successful.

[事实] Erika reviews her notes before being called into the meeting and turns off her recorder.

[推测] The pitch is less about explaining game mechanics than proving that Planet Money can create demand.

[13:30] **Immediate Reaction After The Pitch**

[事实] After the meeting, Erika tells Kenny that the buyers did not strongly respond to the actual game or to Planet Money itself.

[事实] The buyers raised concerns about who the game was for: kids, teens, or adults.

[事实] They also asked about player count and whether there was a two-player version.

[事实] A major concern was whether the game belonged near a $9.99 card-game price point or closer to a $20 game.

[15:33] **Pricing As A Product Decision**

[事实] Exploding Kittens’ co-founder interprets the buyers’ price question as a positive sign because they were discussing how to sell the game, not whether to sell it.

[事实] The episode explains that pricing affects what the final product can include, from simple cards to better boxes, tokens, or figures.

[事实] The co-founder says choosing a price effectively sets the stage for the global market for that product.

[推测] The pricing discussion shows that retail economics and product design are tightly linked.

[18:15] **A Former Buyer Explains The Odds**

[事实] The hosts interview Matt Adelman, a former Target buyer who sat through hundreds of line review meetings.

[事实] Matt says that if there are 100 line review submissions, about 30 get meetings, and only about three to six brands ultimately get on shelves.

[事实] Although Matt bought deli and prepared foods rather than board games, he says the retail decision process is broadly similar across categories.

[事实] Buyers focus on whether a product will sell better than the item it replaces.

[20:27] **Shelf Space, Planograms, And Internal Pitches**

[事实] Big-box retailers use shelf blueprints called planograms.

[事实] Matt says buyers mentally evaluate where a product would fit and how productive the shelf space would be.

[事实] If buyers like a product, they still have to pitch their planogram and decisions to senior leaders.

[事实] Those leaders challenge the buyer’s choices, and the buyer must defend the decision.

[21:52] **Creators And Retail Novelty**

[事实] Matt says creators, influencers, and people with followings increasingly show up to pitch products.

[事实] The episode later says this is more common in food and cosmetics than in board games.

[事实] Exploding Kittens believes Erika’s presence made the board-game pitch feel unusual and memorable.

[推测] Planet Money’s audience and Erika’s in-room presence gave the game a marketing story beyond the product itself.

[22:26] **The Long Wait And The Retail Win**

[事实] After the pitch, the team waits for months while Exploding Kittens also pitches other big-box retailers.

[事实] Nearly five months later, Exploding Kittens tells Planet Money that two major U.S. big-box retailers accepted **Sell Me a Sasquatch** full chain throughout North America.

[事实] Exploding Kittens says the first round of orders is about 100,000 units.

[推测] The acceptance validates the pitch strategy, but it also starts a new operational challenge: making and delivering the product.

[27:03] **Final Prototype Decisions Under Deadline**

[事实] One month after the retail news, Kenny and Erika go to Exploding Kittens’ headquarters and see a conference room devoted to the Planet Money game.

[事实] The team has to finalize cards, boxes, logos, and instruction booklets.

[事实] Exploding Kittens says files must go to the factory the next day.

[事实] The rush is partly because of Chinese Lunar New Year, when factories shut down or slow production for weeks.

[29:29] **Royalties And Who Takes The Risk**

[事实] The episode says board-game royalty and licensing deals tend to be around 3% to 7% of net revenue.

[事实] Planet Money and NPR land within that industry-standard range after months of negotiation.

[事实] The deal includes a small guaranteed upfront fee and a royalty increase if sales reach 200,000 units.

[事实] Exploding Kittens takes virtually all the risk by paying for development, prototyping, and manufacturing.

[31:09] **Price, Materials, And Packaging**

[事实] Exploding Kittens gets the big-box retailer to agree to a suggested retail price of $19.99.

[事实] The team chooses to put the extra price flexibility into a nicer product rather than simply taking more profit.

[事实] Upgrades include higher-quality cards, a better box, and a Pantone purple color instead of standard CMYK purple.

[推测] The team treats packaging quality as part of how the game signals value on a crowded shelf.

[32:49] **Testing The Box On A Real Shelf**

[事实] Exploding Kittens tests the box by placing it on a real big-box store shelf to see how it looks.

[事实] They evaluate whether it stands out, reads from far away, and still works when pushed back into shadow.

[事实] The Pantone purple appears distinct from nearby products and is judged positively.

[推测] The shelf test is a practical check of retail visibility before committing to mass production.

[33:58] **Last-Minute Production Choices**

[事实] The team switches the box from glossy to matte finish to signal a more grown-up game.

[事实] They settle on a comic-strip format for the back of the box.

[事实] They also finalize instruction tweaks, QR codes, and punctuation details on the front of the box.

[事实] After finishing, Exploding Kittens rings a bell used when a game is completed and shipped.

[35:27] **Manufacturing In Shanghai**

[事实] The game files are sent to a factory in China.

[事实] Kenny FaceTimes with Exploding Kittens’ global sourcing person, Tori Welch, from the factory floor in Shanghai.

[事实] The factory is printing thousands of cards and boxes.

[事实] The factory uses a quality-control tool to check whether the Pantone purple is correct.

[36:48] **The Game Reaches Stores**

[事实] Four months later, Kenny visits a big-box retailer with his six-year-old daughter and sees the game on the shelf.

[事实] The hosts still do not name the specific retailer Erika pitched to.

[事实] They say **Sell Me a Sasquatch** is available at Target, Walmart, other board-game retailers, and the NPR Shop.

[推测] The shelf appearance is presented as the culmination of the entire reporting and product-development project.

[37:39] **Listener Contributions**

[事实] Listeners spent hundreds of hours testing prototypes and sending feedback.

[事实] Listeners submitted more than a thousand ideas for mythical creatures.

[事实] The final game includes listener-inspired creatures such as the Veblen, Bull Bear, Laffer, Ogo Pogo, and Drop Bear.

[事实] Exploding Kittens added a symbol to show which cards came from listener ideas.

[39:00] **Launch Support And Credits**

[事实] The hosts ask listeners who buy the game to rate and review it where they purchased it.

[事实] They say verified-purchase reviews matter more in rating and review systems.

[事实] They encourage listeners to host game nights and tell friends about the game.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is that it turns a product launch into a concrete lesson about retail economics. Instead of explaining big-box shelves abstractly, it follows one product through pitch meetings, buyer psychology, pricing, packaging, manufacturing, and store placement.

[推测] A major highlight is the access: the show captures the pressure around line review and explains why buyers care less about whether something is charming and more about whether it can outperform whatever loses shelf space.

[推测] The main limitation is that the most sensitive part of the story remains partially hidden: the unnamed retailer and the unrecorded pitch meeting. The episode compensates by using Erika’s recollection, Exploding Kittens’ interpretation, and a former buyer’s explanation.

[推测] This episode is especially suited for listeners interested in consumer products, retail strategy, board games, manufacturing timelines, or how media brands turn audience trust into commercial leverage.
