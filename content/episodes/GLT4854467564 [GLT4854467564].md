# Costa Rica: Civil War, Don Pepe, and the Choice to Abolish the Army

## 概览

This episode uses Costa Rica’s unexpected football success as the entry point to a larger historical question: why has Costa Rica become such an outlier in Central America for stability, safety, prosperity, education, health care, and democratic continuity?

The hosts trace the answer back to Costa Rica’s unusual colonial and 19th-century background, then focus on the 1948 civil war. They explain how a disputed election, the cancellation of results, and political violence led Jose Figueres Ferrer, known as Don Pepe, to launch an uprising.

The central conclusion is that Figueres’s choices after victory shaped modern Costa Rica. Rather than becoming a dictator, he governed temporarily, expanded political and social rights, wrote a new constitution, and abolished the army, redirecting the country’s identity toward civilian democracy and public institutions.

## 分段落总结

[00:57] **Costa Rica as a World Cup and historical outlier**

[事实] The episode is part of a World Cup-themed series covering the 32 countries qualified for the Qatar World Cup.
[事实] The hosts introduce Costa Rica as a country whose history may be little known to their audience.
[事实] They note Costa Rica’s reputation for wildlife, reforestation, ecotourism, and jokingly, Jurassic Park.

[02:04] **Football success as a clue to national stability**

[事实] In the 2014 World Cup, Costa Rica topped a group containing England, Italy, and Uruguay, while England finished last.
[事实] Costa Rica reached the quarterfinals in 2014 and came within a penalty shootout of the semifinals.
[事实] The hosts argue that Costa Rica’s sporting success reflects broader advantages of money and stability.
[事实] Costa Rica is described as safer and more affluent than many Central American neighbors.

[04:20] **Why Costa Rica became different**

[事实] The hosts ask why Costa Rica became an outlier despite sharing language, customs, and broad ethnic similarities with neighboring countries.
[事实] They identify the Costa Rican Civil War of the 1940s as the key turning point.
[推测] The episode frames Costa Rica’s later exceptionalism less as geography or culture, and more as the result of political choices.

[05:18] **Colonial backwater and weak inherited hierarchy**

[事实] Costa Rica is described as historically a “backwater,” located between Nicaragua and Panama.
[事实] Before Spanish conquest, it did not have great empires or cities like those in Mexico or Peru.
[事实] The Spanish name “Costa Rica,” meaning rich coast, reflected hope for gold more than reality.
[事实] Indigenous populations were devastated by disease, and the colony remained poor, sparsely populated, and short of labor.
[推测] The lack of large estates and dense colonial hierarchies may have reduced some later social conflicts seen elsewhere in Latin America.

[06:56] **Coffee, Britain, and 19th-century progressivism**

[事实] Costa Rica passed through several political entities after independence before becoming an independent republic.
[事实] Coffee became important in the 1820s, with Britain described as the biggest customer.
[事实] Costa Rica developed an Anglo-Costa Rican bank for coffee growers and built a railway from coffee estates to the coast.
[事实] The hosts say Costa Rica abolished the death penalty in 1871 and had freedom of religion, separation of church and state, and separation of powers.
[推测] The country’s relatively low level of conflict over cities, estates, and ideology helped make its politics less explosive than in some neighboring states.

[08:22] **Calderon, populism, and rising tension**

[事实] Rafael Angel Calderon, a doctor educated in Belgium, was president at the turn of the 1940s.
[事实] Calderon initially had support from coffee oligarchs but became increasingly populist and promoted welfare policies.
[事实] Because Costa Rica barred consecutive presidential terms, Calderon backed Teodoro Picado Mikalski as successor.
[事实] Picado is described as a historian, a polyglot, and a weak figure.

[10:16] **Authoritarian drift and the 1948 election crisis**

[事实] Calderon and Picado’s populist policies provoked unrest among professional classes, coffee interests, merchants, and manufacturers.
[事实] The government increasingly relied on militias linked to the Communist Party.
[事实] A general strike occurred in 1947, and the government responded by intimidating merchants and manufacturers.
[事实] Calderon returned as a candidate in the 1948 election against newspaperman Otilio Ulate Blanco.

[12:27] **Disputed results and violence**

[事实] The election of February 8, 1948, reportedly gave Ulate Blanco 55 percent of the vote and Calderon 45 percent.
[事实] Calderon rejected the result, claiming fraud.
[事实] A suspicious fire destroyed many ballots the day after the election.
[事实] A Calderon-controlled Congress canceled the election three weeks later.
[事实] Ulate Blanco’s ally Dr. Valverde was killed when police surrounded his house, and Ulate later escaped before being captured.

[16:31] **Introducing Don Pepe**

[事实] Jose Figueres Ferrer, known as Don Pepe, is introduced as the central figure in Costa Rican history and “the father of modern Costa Rica.”
[事实] His parents came from Barcelona, and his Catalan identity remained important to him.
[事实] He was born in Costa Rica in 1906 and later studied briefly at MIT before educating himself through reading in the Boston Public Library.
[事实] He became a coffee grower and rope manufacturer, and the hosts describe him as a paternalistic employer who built housing and provided benefits for workers.

[20:05] **Figueres and the Caribbean Legion**

[事实] Figueres became alarmed by the authoritarian direction of the Calderon administration and spent two years in exile in Mexico.
[事实] He joined the Caribbean Legion, a pro-democracy group opposed to dictators such as Somoza in Nicaragua and Trujillo in the Dominican Republic.
[事实] Fidel Castro is named as one member of the Caribbean Legion.
[事实] The group saw Costa Rica as a possible democratic base for spreading democracy in Central America.

[21:57] **The National Liberation Army uprising**

[事实] After the election was canceled and political violence escalated, Figueres decided to trigger an uprising.
[事实] His National Liberation Army included anti-communists, coffee interests, and left-wing pro-democracy figures suspicious of Calderon’s authoritarianism.
[事实] Fighting broke out in March 1948, and by April 12 Figueres’s forces had entered Cartago, the second-largest city.
[事实] Government support melted away, leaving it increasingly reliant on communist militias in San Jose.

[23:30] **U.S. pressure and the end of the civil war**

[事实] The United States was crucial because the Cold War had made it strongly anti-communist.
[事实] The Americans warned that if communists took over, U.S. troops from the Panama Canal Zone could intervene.
[事实] Picado argued that street fighting in San Jose would be disastrous.
[事实] Calderon, Picado, and the communists agreed to give up.
[事实] Figueres’s forces entered San Jose on April 24, after a 44-day conflict with about 2,000 deaths.

[24:59] **Figueres’s eighteen months in power**

[事实] After victory, Figueres took power for 18 months and said he would then give it up.
[事实] During that period, he gave women the vote and removed literacy requirements for voting.
[事实] He confirmed welfare legislation, nationalized the banks, wrote a new constitution, expanded public education, and gave citizenship to children of Black immigrants.
[事实] His most famous action was abolishing the army.

[26:35] **Why abolishing the army mattered**

[事实] Costa Rica is described as the only country of its size with no army.
[事实] Figueres feared the army was untrustworthy and associated with his adversaries.
[事实] He also believed that without an army, Costa Rica would be less likely to suffer coups and civil wars.
[事实] The hosts connect his thinking to H.G. Wells’s vision of a future without armies.
[推测] Abolishing the army became both a practical safeguard against coups and a symbolic foundation for Costa Rica’s democratic identity.

[27:33] **Figueres after the revolution**

[事实] Figueres gave up power and later served two more democratic terms as president in the 1950s and 1970s.
[事实] He remained close to the United States but criticized U.S. anti-communism when it tolerated dictatorships.
[事实] The hosts say he took money from both the CIA and the KGB.
[事实] In 1971, he personally confronted Nicaraguan plane hijackers in San Jose with a submachine gun until they surrendered.
[事实] At a 1973 Central American summit, he reportedly contrasted himself with generals by saying he was the only civilian who had fought and won a war.

[29:56] **Costa Rica’s modern legacy**

[事实] The hosts credit Figueres with showing that political choices can shape a country’s destiny.
[事实] Costa Rica is described as having the lowest crime, highest literacy, best health care, greatest stability, and best football team in Central America.
[事实] In 1949, during a public ceremony abolishing the army, the commander in chief handed the key to army headquarters to the Minister of Education.
[事实] The army headquarters became a museum.
[推测] The episode presents this ceremony as the clearest symbol of Costa Rica choosing education and civilian life over militarism.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s value lies in using a light football hook to explain a serious historical argument: Costa Rica’s modern reputation was not inevitable, but emerged from a specific crisis and from decisions made after victory in the 1948 civil war.

Its strongest thread is the portrait of Figueres. The hosts present him as idealistic, canny, brave, and unusually willing to surrender power, which makes him stand out against many 20th-century strongman narratives in the region.

The episode is conversational and humorous, so it does not offer a dense academic account of every faction or social force. [推测] Listeners looking for a concise, accessible introduction to Costa Rica’s political exceptionalism will get more from it than listeners seeking a detailed specialist history.
