# Huberman Lab Podcast with Stu McMillan: Skipping, Striding, Sprinting, and Expressive Movement

## 概览

Andrew Huberman speaks with sprint coach Stuart McMillan about running as a fundamental human movement, with special attention to skipping, striding, sprinting, and how people can recover expressive, powerful movement without needing expensive equipment.

The episode’s central practical message is that many adults are not ready to sprint safely, but can use skipping as a low-cost plyometric bridge. McMillan frames skipping as a way to train hip extension, eccentric capacity, posture, rhythm, cross-body coordination, and eventually safer striding.

The discussion expands from mechanics into identity, coaching, culture, genetics, performance-enhancing drugs, and longevity. McMillan repeatedly argues that good coaching starts with the individual’s own structure and “essence,” not with copying an ideal model.

## 分段落总结

[00:15] **Guest And Episode Scope**

[事实] Huberman introduces Stuart McMillan as a coach who has worked with more than 70 Olympians across nine Olympic Games and with major professional sports.

[事实] The episode is framed around skipping and striding as zero-cost activities for improving power, speed, coordination, posture, and movement quality.

[事实] Huberman says the discussion also covers running mechanics, track and field, genetics and environment in athletic success, and performance-enhancing tools.

[03:09] **Why Sprinting Matters**

[事实] McMillan calls running fast “the ultimate human activity” because sprinting directly identifies the fastest person on the planet.

[事实] He says the 100 meters is essentially all-out, while the 200 meters includes more tactical choices about how hard to start, smooth out, and finish.

[事实] Huberman and McMillan discuss Usain Bolt’s 100-meter world record and note that elite male sprinters may cover 100 meters in roughly 40 to 45 steps.

[推测] McMillan’s fascination with sprinting comes from seeing it as a very pure test of peak human performance.

[13:04] **Recognizing Sprint Talent And Avoiding Early Specialization**

[事实] McMillan says he noticed a young sprinter at a school track by the “pop” of ground contact and ease of movement, not by polished form.

[事实] He argues that children around 12 to 14 should try many events rather than being labeled too early as sprinters, jumpers, or distance runners.

[事实] He gives Jodie Williams as an example of an athlete who dominated youth 100 and 200 meters, later moved toward the 400 meters, and placed sixth at the 2020 Olympic Games.

[推测] The episode presents event fit as something that can emerge slowly through performance, enjoyment, and self-understanding.

[24:35] **Walking, Jogging, Running, Striding, Sprinting**

[事实] McMillan describes five gait patterns: walking, jogging, running, striding, and sprinting.

[事实] He says people usually self-organize toward their most efficient walking or jogging pattern unless outside cues interfere.

[事实] His simple cue across gait speeds is “flat foot contact,” while the actual foot strike changes with velocity.

[事实] Huberman summarizes the point as: speed should dictate foot strike, not a rigid rule about heel striking or toe striking.

[30:00] **Eyes, Head, Torso, And Posture**

[事实] McMillan says eye position matters much more in sprinting than in slower jogging because the body tends to follow the eyes.

[事实] He warns that lifting the eyes and chin too early can pull the body into excessive extension and make the runner push down the track rather than bounce.

[事实] His cue for sprinters is to let the torso determine when the chin and eyes come up.

[推测] This section links running mechanics with broader movement principles used in lifting and posture.

[36:42] **Skipping As A Safer Plyometric Bridge**

[事实] McMillan says many adults no longer have the tissue and joint capacity to sprint safely, even if their cardiovascular engine is still strong.

[事实] He recommends hill running and especially skipping as safer ways to train qualities related to sprinting.

[事实] Skipping trains hip extension, knee-behind-butt position, stiffness on the ground, rhythm, coordination, and expressive posture.

[事实] A beginner on-ramp is alternating 30 seconds of skipping with 30 seconds of jogging or walking.

[53:04] **Eccentric Capacity And Striding**

[事实] McMillan says regular running is more concentric-dominant, while striding and sprinting are largely eccentric because they require handling braking forces.

[事实] He defines striding as roughly 75 to 95 percent of maximum sprint speed, with sprinting being truly maximal.

[事实] He says testing he was involved in found eccentric force capacities differentiated elite from sub-elite performers more than concentric force capacities.

[事实] For high-intensity sprint-related work, he says movement quality should govern the session more than fatigue or total work.

[60:04] **Why Striding Is Different From Sprinting**

[事实] McMillan describes walking, jogging, running, striding, and sprinting partly by how much space the person takes up.

[事实] He says jogging and running tend to happen more behind the center of mass, while striding and sprinting involve more force in front of the center of mass.

[事实] He references Ken Clark’s work on sprinting as a “two mass system,” where the shank and foot contribute additional force in elite sprinting.

[推测] Most adults should build toward striding before assuming they can sprint safely.

[63:50] **Skipping, Aging, And Longevity**

[事实] Huberman raises the risk that older adults can fall, become immobile, and then suffer serious health consequences.

[事实] McMillan says eccentric control is one of the capacities people lose with age.

[事实] McMillan’s 78-year-old father still runs regularly and includes sessions alternating walking, skipping, and striding.

[事实] McMillan suggests the ability to safely express one’s own maximal running speed may be a strong marker of vitality or health.

[70:41] **Normalizing Skipping As Plyometrics**

[事实] Huberman notes that “skipping” can sound childlike and may discourage adults from doing it.

[事实] McMillan says skipping is simply plyometrics and is a regression from bounding, which is much harder.

[事实] He says every sprint group skips because of the importance of that gait pattern.

[事实] He argues that jumping onto a box is mostly concentric and not the eccentric plyometric activity he is emphasizing.

[74:32] **Cross-Body Coordination And Rotation**

[事实] McMillan explains that the pelvis, shoulders, and spine rotate, undulate, and counterbalance each other during walking and running.

[事实] He says humans are rotational beings and should find ways to use rotational forces rather than constrain them.

[事实] He dislikes the term “anti-rotation” and prefers thinking in terms of anti-excessive rotation.

[事实] Huberman and McMillan criticize walking while looking at a phone because it creates a constrained, flexed posture.

[82:27] **Expression, Authenticity, And Elite Athletes**

[事实] McMillan contrasts earlier hyper-intense sprint personas with Usain Bolt’s playful, expressive style.

[事实] He says Bolt gave other sprinters permission to have fun while still competing intensely.

[事实] He compares Bolt with Andre DeGrasse, who is quieter and more inward but still highly effective because that matches his personality.

[事实] McMillan uses Asafa Powell as an example of a great sprinter who, in his view, struggled by trying on personas that did not fit him.

[92:43] **Movement Can Reveal Identity**

[事实] Huberman asks whether movement can teach people who they are, not just express who they already are.

[事实] McMillan says coaches can steal an athlete’s essence by forcing them into the coach’s preferred model.

[事实] He describes working with a tall, elastic, expressive sprinter who had been coached into short, choppy acceleration mechanics.

[推测] The coaching philosophy presented here is to rediscover the athlete’s best solution rather than impose a universal ideal.

[99:12] **Flow, Mood Words, Pressure, And Peace**

[事实] McMillan cites Noah Lyles’s 2022 World Championships 200-meter win in 19.31 as an example of an athlete being fully lost in performance.

[事实] He says sprinters often cannot describe what they felt during personal bests because they “just ran.”

[事实] In training, he builds technical cues, then tries to connect them to mood words.

[事实] For the 100 meters, he frames the first 50 meters as pressure and the second 50 meters as peace.

[106:26] **Exercise Versus Movement**

[事实] McMillan says modern exercise can become detached from why people originally moved their bodies.

[事实] He says he lifts weights, skips daily, runs, boxes, hikes, and tries to play and move in many ways.

[事实] He argues that a skate park shows better movement than a weight room if the goal is to observe skilled movement.

[推测] The larger message is not to abandon the gym, but to avoid treating gym exercises as a complete substitute for expressive movement.

[111:59] **Music, Rhythm, And Coaching**

[事实] McMillan says he started DJing in 1984 and had a reggae radio show in Calgary for 20 years.

[事实] He says his background in music and art helped him understand rhythm, combinations, and coaching.

[事实] He discusses Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo as athletes who are authentically themselves in very different ways.

[事实] He also mentions Steph Curry as someone who changed basketball through a style that matched who he is.

[121:36] **Genetics, Culture, And Athletic Excellence**

[事实] McMillan says sprint success involves both nature and nurture, but genetic capacity is required to enter the elite sprinting room.

[事实] He lists factors such as type 2 fibers, limb length, joint structure, tendon length, muscle belly size, hormonal factors, and neural factors.

[事实] He says Jamaica’s sprint culture and the Jamaican high school championships create a powerful environment for developing sprinters.

[事实] He says many very fast marathoners come from the same district in Kenya, combining genetic and environmental factors.

[129:15] **Sprint Mechanics And Individual Constraints**

[事实] McMillan says people should not try to copy Usain Bolt’s sprinting stride because their body structure and action capabilities differ.

[事实] He identifies key sprinting features: high thigh position, stiff ground contact, and quality hip extension.

[事实] He says elite sprinters typically bring the knee to about waist or belly-button height at top speed.

[事实] He evaluates hip extension through force, velocity, range of motion, control, and repeatability.

[137:22] **Jump Rope, Bouncing, And Flexible Principles**

[事实] McMillan says skipping rope can train coordinated flexion and extension at the ankle, knee, and hip.

[事实] He warns against jumping rope only through the ankles and toes.

[事实] His cue is to bounce like on a trampoline, with a stiff foot and contact near the balls of the feet.

[事实] He says principles are few and methods are many, and people should choose methods that align with principles and work for them.

[142:12] **Weight Training For Sprinting**

[事实] McMillan says sprinting depends on how much force is applied, how fast it is applied, the direction of force, and body mass.

[事实] He says maximum strength has diminishing returns, especially for elite sprinters.

[事实] He contrasts Ben Johnson’s extreme strength with Andre DeGrasse, who was relatively weak in the weight room but still won Olympic medals.

[事实] He emphasizes force transmission, plyometrics, jump squats, clap push-ups, Olympic lifts, and run-specific isometrics.

[151:13] **Isometrics, Staggered Stances, And Transfer**

[事实] McMillan describes run-specific isometric work with the foot under the center of mass, a flat foot, slight knee bend, and slight hip bend.

[事实] He says most of his weight-room work is staggered, split, kickstand, front-foot-elevated, or rear-foot-elevated rather than bilateral.

[事实] He looks for chances to train big-toe extension, hip extension, and cross-body force transmission.

[事实] He says many weight-room exercises have limited direct transfer to sprinting unless the coach deliberately builds more functional links.

[156:57] **Performance-Enhancing Drugs In Sport**

[事实] McMillan says androgen-related drug use was common in sprinting in the 1960s, 1970s, and 1980s, became less common as testing improved, and is not common now in his view.

[事实] He says he cannot point to an elite sprinter today and confidently say that athlete is dirty.

[事实] He identifies Russia’s state-sponsored doping history and positive drug cases from Kenya in distance running as notable pockets.

[事实] Huberman distinguishes androgen use from endurance-focused substances such as EPO or blood-related strategies.

[165:00] **Contamination, Transparency, And Overestimating Drugs**

[事实] McMillan describes bobsledder Pavle Jovanovic testing positive after supplement contamination, with devastating effects on his career and life.

[事实] Huberman discusses transparency around TRT, HCG, fitness influencers, Hollywood physiques, and peptide use.

[事实] McMillan says people often overestimate how much drugs contribute to elite success.

[事实] He says in the drug era athletes got fast first, then drugs made them faster; drugs did not create speed from nothing.

[172:49] **Maximal Speed As A Health Proxy**

[事实] McMillan asks whether safely expressing one’s own maximal sprint speed might be a better proxy for health and vitality than many common metrics.

[事实] Huberman says he had not thought this way at the start of the conversation but finds the argument compelling.

[事实] They connect the idea of pressure and peace to physical, cognitive, and creative work.

[事实] The episode ends with encouragement to attend track meets and support track and field.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it turns sprint coaching into practical advice for non-sprinters. Skipping becomes a realistic entry point for people who want power, posture, coordination, and eccentric capacity without immediately risking all-out sprinting.

[推测] Its strongest moments are McMillan’s insistence on individuality: foot strike should follow speed, technique should follow the person’s structure, and coaching should reveal the athlete’s own solution rather than force imitation.

[推测] The limitations are that many broad claims are presented through coaching experience, elite anecdotes, and practical reasoning rather than detailed study-by-study evidence in the transcript. Claims about maximal sprinting as a health marker, gym transfer, and current doping prevalence should be treated as McMillan’s informed perspective.

[推测] This episode is best suited for runners, lifters, coaches, aging adults interested in maintaining movement quality, and listeners who enjoy links between sport, creativity, rhythm, and identity.
