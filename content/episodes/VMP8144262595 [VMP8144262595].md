# Earn the Right to Grow: Customer-Led Advice for Three Emerging Brands

## Overview

This episode of *The Advice Line* features Guy Raz and Seth Berkowitz, founder and CEO of Insomnia Cookies. Berkowitz explains why the company continues to operate nearly all of its bakeries rather than franchise them, and why expansion should follow demonstrated customer love instead of preceding it.

Three founders seek advice on distinct growth challenges: rebuilding a fashion label after losing wholesale accounts, establishing sauna hats as part of a modern wellness ritual, and marketing collegiate stadium bags when the buyer and end user are different people.

Across all three conversations, the advice converges on a consistent strategy: narrow the initial focus, meet customers where the product is easiest to understand, collect evidence from their behavior, and scale only after a strong use case has been proven.

## 分段落总结

[00:07] **Introducing Seth Berkowitz and Insomnia Cookies**

[事实] Guy Raz introduces Seth Berkowitz, founder and CEO of Insomnia Cookies, as the episode’s guest adviser.

[事实] Berkowitz started the company in college by delivering fresh cookies from his dorm room to customers seeking late-night food.

[事实] Insomnia Cookies expects to finish the year with roughly 393 bakeries and aspires to grow to 1,800 locations worldwide.

[01:31] **Why Insomnia Cookies Does Not Franchise**

[事实] The 1,800-location target is based on the company’s experience expanding across college towns, cities, suburbs, train stations, stadiums, and international markets.

[事实] Insomnia Cookies operates about 390 bakeries itself, with licensing arrangements limited to an airport and three stadiums.

[事实] Berkowitz says direct operation allows the company to test innovations, deploy them in stores, and roll them out without the complexity or resistance that can accompany franchising.

[事实] He regards product quality as the company’s core priority.

[04:17] **Growth Must Be Earned**

[事实] Berkowitz believes customer love gives a company permission to grow.

[事实] Insomnia Cookies previously opened too many stores too quickly in some markets and could have detected the problem earlier by monitoring local brand-awareness metrics more closely.

[事实] His preferred approach is to confirm that a community genuinely values the brand before accelerating expansion.

[推测] The company treats growth as the replication of proven demand rather than as a way to manufacture demand.

[05:43] **From a College Brand to a Lifecycle Brand**

[事实] About 250 of Insomnia Cookies’ roughly 400 bakeries are associated with college markets, so students remain central to the business.

[事实] The company followed former student customers as they moved into cities and later into suburbs.

[事实] Berkowitz hopes the brand can eventually reach those customers’ children, creating a recurring generational cycle.

[06:47] **Cahindo: Rebuilding a Fashion Business**

[事实] Cahindo is a luxury special-occasion fashion brand rooted in its founder’s Congolese heritage and broader African upbringing.

[事实] Its garments are handcrafted by female artisans in Africa, while its colors, prints, and product names draw on African countries, peoples, and history.

[事实] The business generated $268,000 in 2023 but later lost wholesale accounts including Anthropologie, Rent the Runway, and Nuuly; annual sales subsequently fell to $50,000.

[事实] The founder is rebuilding around special-occasion clothing while selling directly to consumers and through dropshipping relationships with Nordstrom and Bloomingdale’s.

[09:48] **Choosing Customer-Acquisition Channels with Limited Resources**

[事实] As a solo founder with limited time and money, Cahindo’s founder asks whether she should invest in Meta and Google advertising or pursue other acquisition strategies.

[事实] Berkowitz cautions that broad digital advertising is difficult in the crowded apparel market and that its underlying performance data can be hard to interpret.

[事实] He recommends local activation, featuring the artisans, and emphasizing the brand’s original mission instead of immediately pursuing a mass audience.

[事实] The founder is already testing pop-ups and considering trunk shows in bridal stores so customers can try garments and hear the brand story directly.

[10:43] **Using Focused Communities and Influencers**

[事实] Berkowitz suggests working with influencers specifically connected to weddings and special events, whose audiences may offer greater amplification than general paid advertising.

[事实] The founder says earlier influencer efforts were unsuccessful but agrees that a more narrowly defined special-events focus could work better.

[推测] The recommendation is to reconsider the fit and specificity of the influencer strategy rather than dismissing the entire channel based on earlier results.

[12:26] **Build Around Proven Bestsellers**

[事实] The founder can identify four leading products and knows that customers have worn them as mothers of the bride or wedding guests.

[事实] Raz recommends focusing on three to five bestselling pieces and collecting information about who buys them and for which occasions.

[事实] Sales, sell-through, return, and review data from these products could later support pitches to smaller wholesale boutiques.

[事实] The founder plans to narrow the new collection to five to ten proven styles and place greater emphasis on smaller boutiques.

[15:02] **Differentiate Through Mission and Simplicity**

[事实] Berkowitz recommends making the website feel mission-driven by highlighting the designers and the reason the business was founded.

[事实] He relates Cahindo’s situation to Insomnia Cookies, which experimented with products such as frozen yogurt and cupcakes before reducing its assortment to the core offer.

[事实] His operating lesson is to become excellent at something simple, repeat it consistently, and add adjacent product platforms only after the core becomes well known.

[16:49] **Sauna Société and the Education Gap**

[事实] Tara Gilman founded Sauna Société to make traditional sauna hats more stylish for modern sauna users.

[事实] She says sauna hats protect hair from heat and dryness and make sauna sessions more comfortable by insulating the head.

[事实] The idea arose after frequent sauna use damaged her hair and she found the available hats aesthetically unappealing.

[事实] Most early sales have come from in-person conversations, wellness fairs, and wearing the product herself; influencer posts have produced more limited results.

[22:02] **Making a Product Synonymous with a Ritual**

[事实] Gilman asks which marketing channels deserve her attention and how Sauna Société can become synonymous with sauna culture.

[事实] Berkowitz identifies education as the central obstacle because customers may not initially understand why they need a sauna hat.

[事实] The product has an approximately 85% margin, making sampling and giveaways comparatively practical.

[事实] He argues that seeing, touching, wearing, and discussing the hat in person is the simplest way to bridge the education gap.

[24:00] **Prioritize Places Where Sauna Culture Already Exists**

[事实] Raz recommends concentrating on spas, studios, clubs, resorts, and other locations where customers already participate in sauna culture.

[事实] He suggests experimenting cautiously with social advertising because customer-acquisition costs can be high.

[事实] Local placements can also generate customer feedback, potentially collected through a QR code paired with an incentive.

[事实] Gilman views the hat as an entry product for a broader range that could include towels, robes, swimwear, and sauna-safe clothing.

[25:51] **Lower-Cost Distribution and Corporate Gifting**

[事实] Berkowitz raises the possibility of a lower-priced version that spas could distribute in larger quantities.

[事实] Raz proposes supplying co-branded hats for resort events, welcome bags, and corporate gatherings.

[事实] Gilman is already investigating corporate gifting as a holiday strategy.

[事实] Berkowitz reiterates that any inexpensive method of getting people to see, feel, and wear the product could accelerate adoption.

[28:41] **Desden’s Licensed Game-Day Accessories**

[事实] Carol Deshaun’s company, Desden, is licensed by 90 universities and sells clear stadium bags and game-day accessories designed for female fans.

[事实] The company entered the clear-bag category after the NFL introduced its stadium policy and Deshaun anticipated similar rules on college campuses.

[事实] Desden combines overseas production of clear purses with in-house manufacturing and printing in Houston.

[事实] Revenue was approximately $1.475 million in the previous year, and the company expects to reach about $2.5 million in its current fiscal year.

[31:18] **When the Buyer and User Are Different**

[事实] Desden has discovered that mothers and grandmothers often buy products for daughters and granddaughters.

[事实] Deshaun asks how the brand should market when purchasers and end users have different motivations.

[事实] Berkowitz says the end user should remain the primary consumer in the company’s thinking, regardless of who pays.

[事实] He advises ensuring that students find the products culturally relevant, socially appropriate, visually distinctive, and worth sharing.

[32:49] **Campus Ambassadors and Product Partnerships**

[事实] Desden has operated a campus ambassador program for about eight years, sending products to students who photograph and share them on game days.

[事实] The company also holds occasional pop-ups, and social media has helped grow its direct-to-consumer business.

[事实] Berkowitz sees the transparent bag itself as an opportunity to showcase another company’s product and create mutually beneficial collaborations.

[推测] A well-chosen item displayed inside the clear bag could turn the product’s required transparency into a recurring marketing surface.

[34:18] **Learning About the End User**

[事实] Raz suggests using a QR-code contest or offer to collect information from the person actually carrying the bag.

[事实] This information could help Desden understand users and reach them again around events such as graduation.

[事实] Berkowitz also suggests showing up at moments of intense student engagement, such as ticket lines and campus game-day activities.

[35:27] **Choosing the Next Growth Path**

[事实] Desden’s direct-to-consumer growth has produced attractive margins because the company manufactures its products.

[事实] When asked whether to add licenses or enter other licensing categories, Berkowitz advises examining performance data for each existing license.

[事实] He notes that 90 licenses may already be a substantial operational burden at the company’s present scale.

[事实] His suggested experiment is to select one or two teams in another sport and evaluate their performance before expanding further.

[36:58] **The Consumer Holds the Answers**

[事实] Berkowitz’s closing advice is to follow what customers reveal through their behavior and choices.

[事实] He says the affection customers demonstrate by purchasing a product should be carefully protected and developed.

[事实] He warns that relying too heavily on founder instinct can lead a business in the wrong direction.

[推测] The episode’s central principle is that disciplined observation of customers should guide product selection, marketing, distribution, and expansion.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode is especially valuable for early-stage consumer founders because it translates a broad growth question into concrete decisions: which products to emphasize, where customers should encounter them, what information to collect, and when expansion is justified.

Its strongest feature is the consistency between Berkowitz’s own experience and the advice given to callers. His principle of “earning” growth appears in different forms across fashion, wellness, and collegiate accessories: prove a focused offer, observe real behavior, and scale what customers already demonstrate they value.

The discussion also highlights the importance of matching the sales channel to the product. Visually or experientially unfamiliar products may benefit more from pop-ups, demonstrations, specialized venues, and partnerships than from broad paid advertising.

[推测] The advice is strategic rather than financially detailed: it does not deeply examine cash flow, inventory risk, advertising economics, or licensing costs. It is therefore best suited to founders seeking direction and experiments to test, rather than a complete operational growth plan.
