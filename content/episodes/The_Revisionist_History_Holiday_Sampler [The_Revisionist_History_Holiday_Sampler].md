# Bad Women: Elizabeth Stride’s Story and the COVID-Sniffing Dogs Who Went on Tour

## 概览

This holiday sampler introduces *Bad Women: The Ripper Retold*, historian Hallie Rubenhold’s series about the lives of Jack the Ripper’s victims and the misogyny that distorted their stories. Its featured narrative follows Elizabeth Stride from a relatively prosperous Swedish farm to domestic service, pregnancy, disease, and state persecution.

Elizabeth’s experience reveals how nineteenth-century authorities treated unmarried and working-class women as moral and medical threats. After being classified as a “public woman,” she endured invasive examinations, confinement, stigmatizing treatment for syphilis, and the loss of her stillborn daughter. The system then made respectable employment nearly impossible, pushing her toward the occupation it had already accused her of practicing.

A shorter *Revisionist History* bonus preview shifts to COVID-detection dogs. Originally featured for their disease-detection abilities, the dogs later joined major concert tours, rapidly screening crews backstage while becoming popular members of the touring environment.

## 分段落总结

[00:43] **Introducing *Bad Women: The Ripper Retold***

[事实] Malcolm Gladwell introduces Hallie Rubenhold’s podcast about Polly, Annie, Elizabeth, Catherine, and Mary Jane, the five women commonly identified as Jack the Ripper’s victims.

[事实] The series challenges the long-standing characterization of the victims as prostitutes and reconstructs their lives within the misogynistic conditions of Victorian society.

[事实] The preview opens with the 1878 Princess Alice disaster on the Thames and suggests that the tragedy unexpectedly created an opportunity for Elizabeth Stride.

[02:35] **A Thames Disaster and Elizabeth Stride’s Story**

[事实] The Princess Alice, carrying hundreds of pleasure seekers on a “Moonlight Cruise,” moves into the path of an iron-hulled freighter.

[事实] The resulting tragedy becomes an important turning point in Elizabeth Stride’s life before her eventual murder in Whitechapel.

[事实] Rubenhold defines the series as an account of the real lives of Jack the Ripper’s victims and of how their histories came to be misunderstood.

[04:40] **Elizabeth’s Rural Swedish Childhood**

[事实] Elizabeth was born into a comparatively prosperous farming family in rural Torslanda, Sweden, and grew up helping with crops, livestock, food production, and household work.

[事实] Her small, conservative Lutheran community placed limited educational expectations on girls, emphasizing housekeeping, childcare, and animal husbandry.

[事实] Shortly before turning seventeen, Elizabeth left for Gothenburg to work as a domestic servant, following a common form of preparation for young women’s future household responsibilities.

[06:32] **Domestic Service and Gendered Vulnerability**

[事实] Domestic servants received food, clothing, lodging, and care in exchange for near-total obedience to their employers.

[事实] Young female servants faced the possibility of coercion, assault, or relationships with men in their employers’ households.

[事实] Nineteenth-century sexual double standards allowed men to abandon such relationships while women bore the social and material consequences, particularly when pregnancy resulted.

[08:35] **Pregnancy, Abandonment, and Criminalized Sexuality**

[事实] The identity of the man involved in Elizabeth’s pregnancy, and whether their encounter was consensual, remain unknown.

[事实] By March 1865, the twenty-one-year-old Elizabeth was six months pregnant and without a man willing or able to protect her from the consequences.

[事实] Extramarital sex and pregnancy outside marriage had recently been punishable in Sweden, while authorities maintained registers of acknowledged and suspected sex workers.

[事实] Pregnant unmarried women, mistresses, and women seen alone with men or outdoors at night could be placed on the so-called “register of shame.”

[09:58] **Registered and Controlled as a “Public Woman”**

[事实] Authorities judged Elizabeth guilty of “lecherous living” and registered her as public woman number 97.

[事实] The regulations required her to attend inspections twice weekly, restricted her movements after 11 p.m., and controlled her behavior and clothing in public.

[事实] Women on the register underwent repeated genital examinations intended both to detect syphilis and to discipline them through humiliation.

[11:18] **Syphilis and the Violence of Medical Inspection**

[事实] The episode explains the primary, secondary, and tertiary stages of syphilis, including sores, rashes, neurological damage, facial deterioration, infertility, and congenital transmission.

[事实] Registered women entered the inspection house through a concealed passage, stripped naked, and sometimes waited outdoors under police supervision.

[事实] Elizabeth was diagnosed with syphilis and confined in Gothenburg’s Cure House, an overcrowded and underfunded institution where patients could be held against their will.

[事实] Examinations were often rushed and unhygienic; the same instrument could be used on many women with inadequate disinfection, potentially spreading the diseases being investigated.

[15:00] **Toxic Treatment and a Stillborn Child**

[事实] Mercury was a standard syphilis treatment despite inconsistent dosing and severe toxic effects that could resemble the disease itself.

[事实] Because Elizabeth was pregnant, she was spared mercury but treated internally with acid, while her genital warts were dehydrated or removed.

[事实] After seventeen days of treatment, she went into premature labor and delivered a stillborn daughter at seven months while confined in the Cure House.

[事实] The child’s birth certificate left the father’s name blank.

[16:24] **Public Health as a System of Gendered Punishment**

[事实] Authorities treated women, particularly sex workers, as the primary spreaders of syphilis, while male carriers were exempt from comparable regulation.

[事实] A contemporary British sex worker named Grace says the belief that sex workers are vectors of disease still persists.

[事实] Hospital confinement and police registration carried lasting stigma, preventing affected women from obtaining respectable employment.

[推测] By excluding registered women from other work, the system helped create the very prostitution it claimed merely to identify and control.

[18:32] **Elizabeth Is Pushed into Prostitution**

[事实] Unable to secure respectable work, Elizabeth joined women selling sex along a notorious Gothenburg street known mockingly as the “street of many nymphs.”

[事实] Because street solicitation was prohibited, she likely worked discreetly inside a brothel or coffee house.

[事实] After denunciation, invasive examinations, painful treatment, incarceration, estrangement, and renewed symptoms, Elizabeth faced another period of medical confinement.

[事实] The preview ends as an unspecified opportunity appears that will change her life.

[19:35] **A Second Holiday Preview**

[事实] Gladwell closes the *Bad Women* excerpt and introduces a bonus *Revisionist History* follow-up to “The Dog Will See You Now.”

[事实] The follow-up revisits biodetection canines originally featured for their ability to detect COVID-19.

[事实] Biodetection Canines had been acquired by Protection One Canine, a company specializing in security dogs.

[20:36] **COVID-Detection Dogs Join Concert Tours**

[事实] After someone in Eric Church’s organization heard the earlier podcast, COVID-detection dogs were brought onto his tour.

[事实] Eric Church said their presence probably saved the tour, and dogs also began traveling with acts including the Black Keys and Metallica.

[事实] The dogs and handlers traveled with concert crews and screened workers backstage before performances.

[22:26] **Fast, Popular Backstage Screening**

[事实] Two German Shepherd–Belgian Malinois dogs, Mindy and Lord, began working as early as 5:30 or 6:30 in the morning.

[事实] Crew members stood in a socially distanced line while each dog sniffed a person’s empty left hand, taking about one second per screening.

[事实] After screening, workers proceeded to their jobs, and the dogs became some of the most popular individuals on the tour.

## 播客点评/总结

The strongest portion of the sampler is its reconstruction of Elizabeth Stride as a person shaped by family, labor, law, medicine, and gender—not merely as a name in the mythology surrounding her murderer. The episode uses one life to expose how moral judgment and public-health policy could combine into a self-reinforcing system of exclusion.

Its medical history is especially effective because it connects institutional stigma with direct physical harm: degrading examinations, unhygienic procedures, coercive confinement, and dangerous treatments. The comparison with a contemporary sex worker also argues that the underlying stereotype has not entirely disappeared.

The transition to touring COVID-detection dogs is abrupt because this is a promotional sampler rather than a single continuous documentary. Even so, the second preview offers a concise example of a podcast story producing an unexpected real-world connection between disease detection and the concert industry.

[推测] The sampler will appeal most to listeners interested in feminist history, true-crime revisionism, medical ethics, and unusual applications of animal detection, though those seeking a fully resolved narrative may find both excerpts deliberately incomplete.
