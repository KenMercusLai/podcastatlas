# History's Greatest Dogs

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* uses dogs as a route into politics, legend, empire, war, celebrity, and sentiment. Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook frame the discussion with Byron’s tribute to his dog, then rank seven historically memorable dogs, while excluding Laika and Prince Rupert’s dog Boy for future episodes.

The dogs range from Richard Nixon’s Checkers, whose mention helped save Nixon’s place on the 1952 Republican ticket, to Greyfriars Bobby, whose story blends devotion, tourism, and possible embellishment. The episode repeatedly asks why dogs carry such emotional and symbolic force in public memory.

The darker sections focus on Blondie, Hitler’s German shepherd, and Lootie, the Pekingese taken from the Old Summer Palace and given to Queen Victoria. The lighter or more sentimental sections turn to Gellert, Bounce, and Rin Tin Tin, showing dogs as loyal companions, moral symbols, and popular entertainers.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Byron’s Dog And The Episode’s Frame**

[事实] The episode opens with an inscription for Byron’s dog Boson, praising the dog’s virtues and contrasting them with human vice.

[事实] Byron also wrote a poem for the dog, ending with the claim that he had known only one true friend and that the friend lay there.

[事实] Tom and Dominic introduce the theme as “man’s best friend” and say they have chosen seven of history’s greatest dogs.

[推测] The opening establishes the episode’s central idea: dogs become historically memorable because people project loyalty, innocence, and moral feeling onto them.

[03:27] **Checkers And Nixon’s Political Rescue**

[事实] Checkers was Richard Nixon’s black-and-white cocker spaniel and became famous through Nixon’s 1952 televised “Checkers speech.”

[事实] Nixon gave the speech after reports about a private fund used for political expenses threatened his place as Eisenhower’s running mate.

[事实] Nixon said his family had received one gift, the dog Checkers, and declared that his children loved the dog and the family would keep it.

[事实] The speech was watched by around 60 million Americans, the switchboard was jammed with supportive calls, and Nixon stayed on the ticket.

[推测] The hosts present Checkers as politically significant because the dog helped Nixon turn a financial controversy into an emotional appeal to middle America.

[09:02] **Fala, King Timahoe, And Presidential Dog Politics**

[事实] Nixon’s use of Checkers drew on Franklin D. Roosevelt’s 1944 speech about his own dog Fala, after Republicans falsely claimed Roosevelt had sent a destroyer to retrieve the dog.

[事实] Roosevelt joked that Fala, being a Scottish terrier, resented Republican attacks on him.

[事实] Checkers died before Nixon entered the White House, and Nixon later had an Irish red setter called King Timahoe.

[事实] Nixon’s staff reportedly used trails of biscuits to help King Timahoe bond with him in the White House.

[推测] The segment suggests that presidential dogs can soften or reframe leaders’ public images, even when the leaders themselves are politically divisive.

[12:18] **Greyfriars Bobby And The Making Of A Legend**

[事实] Greyfriars Bobby is described as a famous Edinburgh dog, traditionally a Skye terrier who belonged to night watchman John Gray.

[事实] The story says Bobby stayed by Gray’s grave in Greyfriars kirkyard for 14 years after Gray died of tuberculosis.

[事实] The Lord Provost of Edinburgh paid for Bobby’s dog license, and a fountain and statue were later erected opposite the graveyard.

[事实] Later versions, including Eleanor Atkinson’s 1912 novel, added details such as Bobby visiting Traill’s Temperance Coffee House after the one o’clock gun.

[推测] The hosts treat Bobby’s story as partly grounded in fact but heavily shaped by sentiment, tourism, and commercial interest.

[21:28] **Blondie And Hitler’s Disturbing Devotion To Dogs**

[事实] Blondie was a German shepherd given to Adolf Hitler by Martin Bormann in 1941.

[事实] The hosts explain that Hitler had loved dogs since the First World War and that German shepherds fitted Nazi symbolism because of their association with wolves and Germanic strength.

[事实] Blondie appeared in Nazi propaganda and accompanied Hitler into the bunker in 1945.

[事实] Hitler tested cyanide on Blondie on April 29, 1945, and was distraught when she died; after Hitler and Eva Braun died, guards shot Blondie’s puppies.

[推测] The hosts argue that Hitler’s affection for animals is unsettling because it shows emotional attachment alongside his indifference to the mass destruction of human life.

[28:50] **Gellert And The Welsh Tale Of Fatal Misjudgment**

[事实] Gellert is presented as the dog of Llywelyn the Great, the Welsh prince of the first half of the 13th century.

[事实] In the story, Llywelyn leaves Gellert guarding his baby son, returns to find the dog bloodied beside an empty crib, and kills the dog.

[事实] Llywelyn then discovers that the baby is alive and that Gellert had killed a wolf to save the child.

[事实] The story says Llywelyn buried Gellert with ceremony and never smiled again.

[推测] Dominic doubts the story’s historicity because it resembles other legendary or mythic tales, while Tom uses its emotional force as the reason for including it.

[33:01] **Lootie And The Old Summer Palace**

[事实] Lootie was a Pekingese brought to Britain in 1861 and given to Queen Victoria.

[事实] The dog was taken after British and French forces sacked the Old Summer Palace in Beijing during the Second Opium War.

[事实] John Hart Dunn found the dog, took it back to England, and presented it to Queen Victoria.

[事实] Victoria named the dog Lootie, had her painted, then sent her to the kennels at Windsor, where she was largely ignored and later buried in an unmarked grave.

[推测] The hosts treat Lootie as an uncomfortable symbol of imperial looting and the casual way Britain absorbed trophies from conquest.

[37:38] **Bounce And Admiral Collingwood’s Naval Companionship**

[事实] Bounce was Admiral Cuthbert Collingwood’s dog, given to him in 1790 before a voyage to the Caribbean.

[事实] Collingwood took Bounce to sea, and Bounce swam after him when he went out in boats.

[事实] Bounce disliked battle noise and hid below decks during naval engagements, after which Collingwood comforted him and sang to him.

[事实] Bounce was present when Collingwood went to HMS Victory to discuss battle plans before Trafalgar.

[事实] Four years after Trafalgar, the elderly Bounce fell overboard and drowned; the crew later made a symbolic coffin, draped it in a Union Jack, and gave it a burial at sea.

[推测] Bounce becomes the episode’s most affectionate example of a dog as companion, morale figure, and link between public heroism and private tenderness.

[43:45] **Rin Tin Tin And The Birth Of The Canine Film Star**

[事实] Rin Tin Tin was a German shepherd born in France on the Western Front in 1918.

[事实] Corporal Lee Duncan rescued Rin Tin Tin, his mother, and other puppies from a damaged German headquarters near Fleury.

[事实] Duncan kept Rin Tin Tin and a female dog called Nanette, smuggled them back to America in 1919, and later trained Rin Tin Tin in Los Angeles.

[事实] Rin Tin Tin appeared first as a wolf in *The Man from Hell’s River* in 1922, then starred in 27 films and became known in Hollywood as “the mortgage lifter.”

[事实] The hosts say the story that Rin Tin Tin was voted best actor at the first Oscars is probably an urban myth.

[事实] Rin Tin Tin died in August 1932 and was buried in a pet cemetery outside Paris.

[推测] Rin Tin Tin’s career shows how film turned dogs from loyal companions into mass-cultural stars.

[49:24] **Other Famous Dogs And Final Ranking**

[事实] The hosts note that Anne Frank wrote in her diary that she wanted a dog like Rin Tin Tin.

[事实] Dominic says three dogs have stars on the Hollywood Walk of Fame: Lassie, Rin Tin Tin, and Strongheart.

[事实] Strongheart was another German shepherd, served with the German Red Cross, and appeared in silent films before Rin Tin Tin’s main career.

[事实] Tom says Laika and Prince Rupert’s dog Boy were excluded because they may be covered in future episodes.

[事实] Tom chooses Bounce as his favorite of the dogs discussed.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in its unusual way of using dogs to open broader historical themes: American populism, Scottish memory, Nazi propaganda, Welsh legend, imperial violence, naval culture, and Hollywood celebrity.

[推测] Its strongest moments come when sentimental dog stories are held against darker historical contexts, especially Blondie and Lootie. Those sections prevent the episode from becoming only a light list of charming anecdotes.

[推测] The limitation is that several stories depend on legend, anecdote, or comic speculation, especially Greyfriars Bobby, Gellert, and the Oscars story about Rin Tin Tin. The hosts usually flag uncertainty, but the format favors entertainment over deep verification.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who enjoy history through vivid anecdotes and personality-driven discussion, rather than those looking for a tightly sourced academic treatment of animal history.
