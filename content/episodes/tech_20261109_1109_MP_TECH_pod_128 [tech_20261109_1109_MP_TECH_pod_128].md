# Trading Tech Careers for Time: Drew and Lindsay Haven’s Life After Work

## Overview

Drew and Lindsay Haven spent years in the technology sector before stepping away from paid employment. Lindsay left the video game industry amid concerns about its profit-driven model and subtle gender discrimination, while Drew became disillusioned with Silicon Valley’s changing culture and its growing focus on profit.

Their initial departures were not necessarily intended as permanent retirement. Lindsay planned to return to school and possibly reenter the workforce, while Drew believed time away might simply delay retirement. The pandemic, changes in their family structure, caregiving responsibilities, and the health costs of work reshaped those plans.

The couple now prioritizes family, community, health, and control over their time. They make the arrangement financially sustainable through conscious trade-offs, including simple living, limited transportation, and lower consumption.

## 分段落总结

[00:19] **Two tech workers step away**

[事实] Drew and Lindsay Haven both spent years working in technology before leaving the workforce.

[事实] Lindsay left the video game industry about a decade earlier, returned to school, and later became a full-time caregiver for a family member.

[事实] Drew left his job about a year and a half earlier because he had become disillusioned with the company culture.

[事实] Drew concluded that they could make their existing resources “enough” if they chose to live accordingly, rather than waiting to reach a predetermined retirement target.

[01:14] **Disillusionment with the culture of tech**

[事实] Lindsay said she enjoyed the game industry and might consider returning to a workplace focused on delivering a good product at a set price rather than maximizing profit.

[事实] She also experienced what she described as unspoken gender discrimination in promotions, although it was not overt or explicit.

[事实] Drew said Silicon Valley had shifted from a culture centered partly on building things toward one dominated more heavily by profit as technology companies and venture capital firms consolidated.

[事实] He struggled to find companies genuinely committed to doing good, believing many instead used social purpose as a marketing strategy.

[02:34] **Leaving the rat race—and revising the plan**

[事实] Lindsay described Bay Area congestion, long periods spent on the road, and pressure to devote more time and money to work as parts of the “rat race” she wanted to leave.

[事实] She initially viewed school as a temporary break and expected that she might eventually return to the workforce.

[事实] The pandemic and a change in family structure altered that expectation.

[事实] Drew realized that taking time off could delay retirement but did not necessarily make it impossible, and that employment was harming his stress levels, sleep, and health.

[03:48] **Identity and social pressure without a job**

[事实] Drew had recently begun describing himself as retired, although he still found the label strange.

[事实] Lindsay usually described herself as raising her child and volunteering, and felt some need to justify not holding a paid job.

[事实] Questions from women she admired about when she would return to work made her examine whether that desire was truly her own or partly driven by other people’s expectations.

[推测] Their experiences show how strongly paid employment can shape social identity, even when a household has consciously decided that other forms of work and contribution matter more.

[04:40] **Rebuilding purpose and community**

[事实] Drew said American life is often organized around employment, making the loss of workplace relationships and sources of fulfillment a major adjustment.

[事实] Lindsay became involved in a local permaculture group, while Drew joined hobby circles and worked on getting to know people in their area.

[事实] They are intentionally replacing work-based social connections with relationships rooted in their local community and personal interests.

[05:13] **The financial trade-offs of freedom from work**

[事实] Their household has one car for three adults, which sometimes creates conflict and requires careful calendar management.

[事实] They make deliberate choices about housing, vehicles, and spending to sustain a life without paid employment.

[事实] They emphasized that they are not free from financial concerns; instead, they have chosen simpler living and lower consumption.

[事实] Drew regularly asks whether a potential purchase matters more than remaining job-free, and usually decides that keeping costs low is worth the additional control over his time.

[推测] Their approach resembles a personal version of financial independence built around redefining “enough,” rather than eliminating every financial burden or maximizing wealth.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its concrete account of leaving tech: dissatisfaction with corporate values is connected to health, gender, family responsibilities, commuting, consumption, and personal identity. Drew and Lindsay avoid presenting their choice as effortless, openly discussing social pressure and the practical inconvenience of living with fewer resources.

Its compact format leaves some important questions unanswered, including the couple’s financial position, housing costs, healthcare arrangements, and the extent to which their experience could be replicated by people with fewer savings. The episode therefore works better as a personal case study than as a general roadmap for early retirement.

[推测] This episode will be most useful to technology workers questioning the relationship between income, consumption, health, and time, as well as listeners considering career breaks, caregiving, simpler living, or early retirement.
