# American Revolution: Britain, the Colonies, and the Crisis Before Independence

## 概览

This episode sets up the American Revolution from a British perspective, with Professor Adam Smith joining the hosts to explain why the crisis was not simply a story of tyranny versus liberty. The discussion starts from Britain’s limited public memory of the war and moves into the structure, prosperity, and self-governing habits of the thirteen colonies before independence.

A central argument is that the Seven Years’ War transformed the relationship between Britain and its North American colonies. British victory removed the French threat but left Britain with large debts, a need to police western expansion, and a stronger military and administrative presence in America.

The episode frames the imperial crisis as a collision of constitutional assumptions. British ministers saw taxation and regulation as reasonable steps to pay for defense and professionalize imperial governance, while many colonists saw the same measures as violations of their rights as Englishmen.

By the end, the speakers argue that independence was not inevitable in 1770. The colonists were still largely loyal to the king and wanted practical self-government within the British Empire, but mutual misunderstanding, political paranoia, economic strain, and street violence were already making compromise harder.

## 分段落总结

[00:33] **A British Warning About Losing America**

[事实] The episode opens with the Duke of Manchester’s November 1775 warning in the House of Lords that Britain risked losing America during the War of Independence.

[事实] The hosts note that the American Revolution occupies a central place in American historical memory but is much less prominent in Britain.

[推测] The opening quotation frames the episode as a retrospective explanation of how Britain moved from imperial confidence to imperial rupture.

[01:42] **Introducing the Subject and Guest**

[事实] The hosts describe the American Revolution as a large and contentious subject and say they will cover it over the next two weeks.

[事实] Professor Adam Smith, Edward Orsborn Professor of History at Oxford and director of the Rothermere American Institute, joins the discussion.

[事实] The hosts jokingly emphasize that the episode presents a British perspective rather than an American one.

[04:13] **The Colonies Before the Crisis**

[事实] Adam Smith describes the thirteen colonies as prosperous, confident, populous, and accustomed to practical self-government.

[事实] Around one and a half million people lived in the thirteen colonies that later rebelled.

[事实] Because land and property were more widely available than in Britain, a broader number of colonists could vote under property-based franchises.

[事实] New England had a strong tradition of town meetings and local participation in government.

[06:06] **British Authority Before and After the Seven Years’ War**

[事实] Before the Seven Years’ War, Britain’s governmental presence in the colonies was slight.

[事实] After the war, Britain had a military presence, distant London supervision, a Secretary of State for America, and navigation laws regulating colonial trade.

[事实] The mercantilist trade system existed but was described as leaky and difficult to enforce.

[推测] The war made imperial authority more visible at exactly the moment colonists were already used to running many local affairs themselves.

[07:14] **The French and Indian War as the Turning Point**

[事实] The Seven Years’ War in America is identified as the French and Indian War.

[事实] Britain and the colonies faced French power in Canada and the interior, as well as Native American confederations.

[事实] British victory expelled the French from much of the New World and created a much larger British military presence in North America.

[事实] Adam Smith argues that the broader American Revolution story is also a story about France and Britain’s relationship with Europe.

[09:00] **The Caribbean’s Imperial Importance**

[事实] Adam Smith says there were twenty-six British colonies in the New World in the mid-eighteenth century, and only half rebelled in the American Revolution.

[事实] The hosts contrast mainland tobacco revenues with the much larger revenues from sugar, molasses, and rum in the West Indies.

[事实] Adam Smith says the American War of Independence was not a complete disaster for Britain because Britain avoided losing the more lucrative sugar islands.

[推测] The British imperial view placed the mainland colonies within a wider Atlantic empire rather than treating them as the obvious center of imperial concern.

[10:14] **Braddock, Washington, Franklin, and the Ohio Valley**

[事实] The hosts discuss General Braddock’s failed 1755 expedition against Fort Duquesne, in present-day Pittsburgh, where Braddock died.

[事实] George Washington served as an aide-de-camp and Benjamin Franklin helped furnish wagons for the expedition.

[事实] Adam Smith says Washington was personally invested in acquiring land in the Ohio Valley and was both a surveyor and military officer.

[事实] Washington, like many colonists, had a financial interest in white European expansion beyond the Appalachians.

[12:45] **Diverging Lessons From Victory**

[事实] The British government concluded after the Seven Years’ War that Native Americans needed to remain allied and that western settlement needed restraint.

[事实] Colonists such as Washington drew the opposite lesson, seeing opportunity in crossing the Appalachians and settling rich western lands.

[事实] The Proclamation Line of 1763 was Britain’s attempt to limit westward expansion, at least temporarily.

[事实] Policing that line required British regular troops in North America, which then raised the question of who would pay for them.

[14:08] **Debt, Pontiac’s Rebellion, and the Cost of Empire**

[事实] The episode links 1763 to the end of the Seven Years’ War, the Proclamation Line, and Pontiac’s rebellion.

[事实] Britain’s debt in 1763 is given as £137 million, with annual interest of £5 million, compared with an earlier peacetime budget of £8 million.

[事实] Adam Smith describes Britain’s postwar financial burden as a crisis.

[推测] The British government’s later fiscal measures are presented as responses to imperial costs rather than as purely arbitrary provocations.

[16:00] **Whitehall’s Problem After Victory**

[事实] Adam Smith says British officials had to manage a global empire, including Quebec’s Catholic French population, with a shaky tax base.

[事实] British people were described as heavily taxed compared with other Europeans.

[事实] From Whitehall’s perspective, the thirteen colonies appeared under-taxed, rich in land, and difficult to manage.

[推测] The episode invites listeners to understand British policy as administratively rational even while showing why colonists found it threatening.

[17:29] **How Britons Viewed Americans**

[事实] Adam Smith says Britons often saw Americans as different, including through New England accents that British observers found amusing.

[事实] The colonies had developed over roughly 150 years and were no longer simply extensions of Britain.

[事实] Southern planters were compared more closely with Caribbean planters, while New Englanders were associated with Puritan and Cromwellian traditions.

[推测] The colonies were not culturally uniform, which complicates any simple “America versus Britain” explanation.

[19:00] **Religious Diversity Among the Colonies**

[事实] The discussion contrasts Puritan and Presbyterian New England, Quaker Philadelphia, and more Anglican colonies such as Virginia.

[事实] Adam Smith says George Washington had never been to New England before taking command of the Continental Army.

[事实] Washington had been to the Caribbean, which supports the point that southern planter interests overlapped with the sugar islands.

[事实] Washington saw New Englanders as alien in religion and manners.

[20:48] **The Great Awakening**

[事实] The hosts discuss the Great Awakening as an evangelical movement that affected the colonies in the early to mid-eighteenth century.

[事实] George Whitefield is identified as a famous preacher who traveled to America in 1739.

[事实] Benjamin Franklin studied Whitefield’s voice projection and calculated how many people could hear him.

[事实] Adam Smith says historians have connected evangelicalism to the coming Revolution because it emphasized individual conscience, distrust of formal authority, and direct relationship with God.

[推测] Adam Smith suggests the Revolution probably would still have happened without the Great Awakening, but it may have looked different.

[23:26] **Self-Government Within Empire**

[事实] After the break, the discussion returns to how different colonial religious cultures shaped understandings of government.

[事实] Adam Smith says the colonists wanted self-government, independence in practical matters, control of trade regulation, and self-taxation within the British Empire.

[事实] He says British government action after 1763 made that arrangement seem impossible to them.

[事实] George Grenville’s government tried to raise revenue, enforce trade laws, and professionalize imperial governance.

[26:27] **Parliamentary Sovereignty and Constitutional Misunderstanding**

[事实] Adam Smith says the crucial issue was the role of Westminster and parliamentary sovereignty after the Glorious Revolution.

[事实] British politicians saw the supremacy of Parliament as non-negotiable.

[事实] The colonists petitioned the king to override Parliament, which Adam Smith describes as constitutionally impossible for George III.

[事实] The episode emphasizes that the conflict was not initially centered on George III in the way later revolutionary memory often suggests.

[29:00] **Taxation and the Authority of Parliament**

[事实] The colonists rejected Parliament’s authority to tax their trade, land, produce, or possessions without their representation.

[事实] Before the Seven Years’ War, theoretical disputes over parliamentary sovereignty had not strongly affected daily colonial life.

[事实] After the war, imperial measures made parliamentary authority tangible and unpopular.

[推测] The crisis sharpened when an abstract constitutional disagreement became a practical burden felt in commerce and legal administration.

[30:05] **The Sugar Act and Enforcement**

[事实] The Sugar Act of April 1764 reduced the tax on imported molasses but strengthened collection and enforcement.

[事实] Adam Smith says New England merchants could be tried in vice-admiralty courts in Nova Scotia without juries for customs violations.

[事实] British officials saw this as standard practice for smuggling cases, but New Englanders saw it as a violation of their rights as Englishmen.

[事实] Adam Smith summarizes the colonial view as asking why London would interfere with a system that had been working.

[31:46] **Two Sides Talking Past Each Other**

[事实] Adam Smith says Britain and the colonies were not really having a conversation and were talking past each other almost from the beginning.

[事实] He describes both sides as acting reasonably from their own points of view.

[事实] He says paranoia increased, especially on the American side, culminating in the Declaration of Independence’s denunciations of the king.

[推测] The tragedy presented here is not that one side had no argument, but that each side’s logic made the other’s position look illegitimate.

[33:16] **The Stamp Act**

[事实] The Stamp Act required colonists to purchase special paper for legal documents.

[事实] It particularly affected lawyers and journalists, which the hosts identify as politically dangerous groups to annoy.

[事实] The Stamp Act had existed in England since 1694, so Grenville did not see it as a radical innovation.

[事实] Grenville argued that Americans were taxed far less than people in Britain and should contribute their share.

[34:21] **Townshend, Barre, and Colonial Sympathy in Parliament**

[事实] Charles Townshend described Americans as children planted, nourished, and protected by Britain who resented contributing to imperial burdens.

[事实] Adam Smith says the tone of Townshend’s speech sounded arrogant and played badly when printed in the colonial press.

[事实] Isaac Barre, who had served in the French and Indian War, defended the colonists in Parliament and was celebrated in America.

[事实] Edmund Burke is also mentioned as a parliamentary supporter of the colonial position.

[36:04] **Hypocrisy, Slavery, and Anti-Tax Protest**

[事实] The hosts discuss Richard Henry Lee, a Virginian founding figure, having enslaved people stage protests against the Stamp Act.

[事实] The protest denounced Grenville as a projector of American slavery while being performed by actual enslaved people.

[事实] The hosts also say Lee had applied to become stamp distributor in Virginia.

[推测] The episode uses this example to show that colonial rhetoric about liberty could coexist with stark contradictions.

[37:31] **Economic Stress and Political Paranoia**

[事实] Adam Smith identifies postwar credit tightening as an underlying source of economic distress in the colonies.

[事实] The hosts connect this distress to anxiety over new taxes, forms, bureaucracy, and government interference.

[事实] Patrick Henry is discussed as comparing George III to figures such as Caesar or Charles I.

[事实] Adam Smith links colonial suspicion to both Enlightenment reading and the seventeenth-century British republican tradition.

[39:36] **Boycotts and Homespun Politics**

[事实] Colonists responded to British measures with boycotts of British goods, including clothing.

[事实] American women participated by spinning homespun cloth, and Benjamin Franklin testified that colonists were preserving lambs for wool.

[事实] Adam Smith says the non-importation movement was leaky and not a massive success in many ways.

[事实] He describes it as an early example of consumer boycotts being used to affect political action.

[41:28] **Mob Violence and Political Pressure**

[事实] On August 14, 1765, a mob attacked Andrew Oliver, the stamp distributor for Massachusetts.

[事实] Stamp distributors were intimidated, helping make the Stamp Act impossible to implement.

[事实] Adam Smith says political mobs were common in the eighteenth-century British world and were sometimes understood as men of property making a political point through force.

[事实] A mob also dismantled the house of Thomas Hutchinson, acting governor of Massachusetts Bay.

[42:48] **Repeal and the Declaratory Act**

[事实] Parliament repealed the Stamp Act because it could not be implemented in the colonies.

[事实] At the same time, Parliament passed the Declaratory Act, asserting that it still had the right to legislate for the colonies.

[事实] Adam Smith describes this as an unnecessary provocation from the perspective of managing colonial rebellion.

[推测] Repeal solved the immediate crisis but preserved the constitutional issue that had caused it.

[43:22] **The Townshend Duties**

[事实] The Townshend duties came in 1767 under a new ministry.

[事实] They were designed as taxes on imports and exports rather than internal taxes like the Stamp Act.

[事实] The British government considered this approach better established and supported by precedent.

[事实] The Townshend duties nevertheless generated strong colonial resistance.

[44:15] **Troops Arrive in Boston**

[事实] Customs agents in Boston requested military assistance amid rising tensions.

[事实] Two army regiments arrived in Boston in early October 1768.

[事实] The regiments were commanded by William Dalrymple, identified as an ancestor of the presenter of the Empire podcast.

[推测] The arrival of troops turned a fiscal and constitutional dispute into a daily urban confrontation.

[44:46] **Why Boston Became Incendiary**

[事实] Adam Smith says Boston merchants were hit by postwar economic downturn and were heavily involved in smuggling.

[事实] Boston and Massachusetts also had strong traditions of self-government, articulate political leadership, a fast press, and political mobs.

[事实] British officials increasingly treated the problem as mainly a Boston or Massachusetts problem.

[事实] Adam Smith says that view was wrong and was gradually proven wrong.

[47:05] **The Boston Massacre**

[事实] In 1770, a small number of British troops confronted several hundred local inhabitants in Boston.

[事实] Tensions had built through earlier incidents, poor relations, underpaid troops, and friction between soldiers and townspeople.

[事实] Five Bostonians were shot by British troops.

[事实] British accounts emphasized stones, cudgels, and self-defense, while American accounts emphasized snowballs and British brutality.

[49:38] **Crispus Attucks and John Adams’s Defense**

[事实] Crispus Attucks is described as a towering dock worker who was half Black and half Native American and who was omitted from Paul Revere’s famous image.

[事实] John Adams defended the British soldiers in court to show that Boston respected due process.

[事实] Adams blamed the crowd in racialized and ethnic terms and singled out Attucks as terrifying.

[事实] Two soldiers were convicted of manslaughter and branded on the thumbs; the others were found not guilty.

[51:12] **Was Independence Inevitable?**

[事实] Adam Smith says American independence was not inevitable in 1770 and that the Boston Massacre could have become only a footnote.

[事实] He says John Adams in 1770 would have been appalled by the suggestion that he was on the road to independence.

[事实] Washington was not yet a leader of rebellion and did not become a member of the Continental Congress until 1775.

[事实] The colonists were still fighting for their rights as Englishmen and wanted practical self-government within the British Empire while maintaining allegiance to the king.

[53:28] **Looking Ahead to Open War**

[事实] The hosts end by saying the next episode will cover the road to open war and the Declaration of Independence.

[事实] They preview the Boston Tea Party, Lexington and Concord, Bunker Hill, George III proclaiming American rebellion, and the move toward full-scale war.

[事实] The episode closes with a promotion for the Rest Is History Club, where members can hear upcoming episodes early.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is that it complicates a familiar revolutionary story by putting British administrative logic, postwar debt, imperial strategy, and constitutional assumptions at the center. It does not deny colonial grievances, but it shows why British ministers could see their own policies as reasonable.

A major strength is the discussion of how the Seven Years’ War created the conditions for crisis: the removal of France, the pressure for western land, the Proclamation Line, troop costs, and taxation all become connected rather than treated as separate events.

The episode is especially useful for listeners who want a British constitutional and imperial reading of the American Revolution’s origins. It is less focused on ordinary colonists’ lived experience beyond boycotts, mobs, and some discussion of women’s participation, so that angle remains comparatively thinner.

[推测] Because this is framed as a scene-setting episode, it works best as an introduction to the structural causes of the Revolution rather than as a full account of the war itself.
