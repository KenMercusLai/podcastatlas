# History's Greatest Monkeys

## 概览

This episode surveys famous monkeys and apes in history, using them to explore why primates have fascinated humans: they look enough like us to become pets, mascots, performers, soldiers, artists, political symbols, and scientific mirrors.

The hosts move from Darwin and Roman macaques to Regency blood sports, Hollywood chimps, South African baboons, Hartlepool folklore, modern political comedy, art hoaxes, and finally Darwin’s encounters with Jenny the orangutan.

A recurring conclusion is that primate stories are vivid but often poorly documented. The hosts repeatedly stress uncertainty around species, origins, deaths, and the reliability of anecdotes.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Darwin and the Human-Ape Question**

[事实] The episode opens with Charles Darwin from *The Descent of Man*, where he compares monkeys, baboons, and humans.
[事实] The hosts note that the episode includes both monkeys and apes, while joking about the difficulty of distinguishing primates.
[推测] The opening frames the whole episode around humans seeing themselves reflected in other primates.

[03:56] **Roman Macaques and Military Mascots**

[事实] Tom introduces Barbary macaques, including the Gibraltar apes and macaques known across the Roman Empire.
[事实] A macaque grave found near Livia in the Pyrenees dates to the late fifth century and contained food, ornaments, and military equipment.
[事实] The hosts discuss whether the animal was linked to Roman soldiers, Visigoths, or barbarian federates.
[推测] The macaque may have been a loved regimental mascot and a marker of Roman-style prestige.

[08:25] **Yazid and the Monkey Abu Qais**

[事实] The hosts discuss Yazid, son of Muawiyah, whose bad reputation includes keeping a favorite monkey called Abu Qais.
[事实] Abu Qais was reportedly entered into horse races while riding a donkey, and Yazid allegedly ordered Syria to mourn him after death.
[事实] Zubaydah, wife of Harun al-Rashid, is also mentioned as having had a monkey.
[推测] The hosts suggest elite monkey-keeping may have carried associations of Roman or Caesar-like decadence.

[10:51] **Jaco Macaco and Regency London**

[事实] Dominic introduces Jaco Macaco, a famous fighting monkey in London in the 1820s.
[事实] Jaco’s origins differ across accounts: one says he rose through fighting pits, while another says he was sold after attacking a sailor and later defeating dogs.
[事实] His name and species are uncertain; the hosts mention macaque, baboon, mandrill, and gibbon as possibilities.
[推测] Jaco’s story illustrates the “unknowability of the past” because surviving sources preserve spectacle more than reliable biography.

[14:36] **Westminster Pit and the Fight with Puss**

[事实] The Westminster Pit featured badger baiting, monkey baiting, bull baiting, and rat-killing contests.
[事实] Jaco was advertised as willing to fight any dog in England for 100 guineas.
[事实] Richard Martin cited Jaco’s fight with Puss in Parliament while campaigning against cruelty to animals.
[事实] Martin said Jaco’s jaw was torn off and he died, while Charles Aistrop denied this and said Jaco lived 15 months longer.
[推测] The conflicting accounts show how gambling, entertainment, and animal welfare politics shaped the story.

[22:18] **Apes in Film and Television**

[事实] The hosts discuss chimpanzees and orangutans in early Hollywood, film, and TV.
[事实] Napoleon and Sally were a chimpanzee couple appearing in 1916 Hollywood shorts.
[事实] An Austrian orangutan named Pierre was reportedly said to have killed a brutal trainer, but the hosts found only brief repeated newspaper references.
[事实] Ronald Reagan starred opposite Peggy the chimp in *Bedtime for Bonzo*.
[推测] These stories show that primate performers became memorable celebrities while their actual lives remained thinly recorded.

[24:39] **Scatter and Elvis Presley**

[事实] Scatter was a baby chimp on a Memphis TV show called *Scatter’s World*.
[事实] After becoming aggressive, Scatter was given to Elvis Presley through Colonel Tom Parker.
[事实] Scatter lived at Graceland, damaged property, threw feces, chased housemaids, and interrupted parties.
[事实] The hosts give conflicting accounts of Scatter’s death, including banishment to a climate-controlled room or poisoning after biting a maid.
[推测] Scatter’s story highlights the problem of treating young chimpanzees as comic pets after they mature.

[27:42] **Michael Jackson and Bubbles**

[事实] Michael Jackson acquired an eight-month-old chimp from a Texas research facility and named him Bubbles.
[事实] Bubbles moved to Neverland in 1988, reportedly slept in Jackson’s room, wore a diaper, ate at the table, and watched films.
[事实] Bubbles became part of Jackson’s public image and accompanied him to Japan for tea with the mayor of Osaka.
[事实] Bubbles later left Jackson’s household, went to a trainer in 2003, and moved in 2005 to the Center for Great Apes in Florida.
[推测] The hosts imply that Bubbles’ discomfort with cameras may reflect the cost of fame and exploitation.

[33:42] **Jack the Signalman**

[事实] Jack the Signalman was a baboon working on the Cape Town to Port Elizabeth Railway in the 1880s.
[事实] Jack belonged to James Wide, a railway worker who lost both legs after mistiming a jump between carriages.
[事实] Wide trained Jack to push him in a trolley and operate railway signals.
[事实] Jack was officially employed for 20 cents a day and half a bottle of beer a week, and was said to have made no mistakes in nine years.
[推测] The story combines comedy with a serious example of animal training, care, and dependence.

[36:03] **Corporal Jackie**

[事实] Corporal Jackie was a baboon owned by Albert Marr in South Africa.
[事实] Marr took Jackie with him after being conscripted in 1915, and Jackie became mascot of the 3rd South African Infantry Regiment.
[事实] Jackie had a cap, rations, a pay book, and was said to salute officers and stand at ease.
[事实] Jackie was wounded by shrapnel in France, had a leg amputated, and later helped raise money for the Red Cross.
[事实] The hosts say Jackie died of a heart attack during a thunderstorm in May 1921 after suffering shell shock.
[推测] Jackie’s story extends the military mascot theme into a more emotional discussion of war trauma.

[40:30] **The Hartlepool Monkey Legend**

[事实] Tom tells the legend that during the Napoleonic Wars, Hartlepool locals hanged a shipwrecked monkey in French uniform as a suspected spy.
[事实] The people of Hartlepool became known as “monkey hangers.”
[事实] The hosts question whether the story is true, noting similar stories elsewhere and a popular mid-19th-century song.
[推测] The legend functions more as folklore and civic mockery than as secure historical evidence.

[43:33] **H’Angus and the Mayor of Hartlepool**

[事实] Hartlepool United created a monkey mascot called H’Angus in 1999.
[事实] Stuart Drummond played H’Angus and became a popular football mascot.
[事实] In 2002, Drummond ran for directly elected mayor while campaigning in the monkey costume.
[事实] His campaign promised bananas for schoolchildren and also addressed sports facilities and reducing councillor numbers.
[事实] Drummond beat the Labour candidate, won three terms, and Hartlepool later abolished the directly elected mayoralty.
[推测] The mascot campaign turned local monkey folklore into political protest and civic theatre.

[47:47] **Pierre Brasso and Chimpanzee Art**

[事实] In 1964, a Gothenburg gallery exhibited paintings attributed to French avant-garde artist Pierre Brasso.
[事实] The painter was revealed to be Peter, a four-year-old chimpanzee from Boras Zoo.
[事实] Critic Rolf Anderberg praised the paintings before the hoax was revealed and later said they were still the best pictures in the exhibition.
[推测] The story satirizes art criticism and again blurs human and ape categories.

[49:55] **Desmond Morris and The Naked Ape**

[事实] The hosts connect chimpanzee art to Desmond Morris, a surrealist painter, primatologist, London Zoo mammal keeper, and author of *The Naked Ape*.
[事实] *The Naked Ape* describes humans as the hairless exception among living monkeys and apes.
[事实] The hosts describe the book as very 1960s, sex-focused, and disliked by feminist critics.
[推测] Morris provides a bridge between primate behavior and modern attempts to explain humans as animals.

[52:06] **Jenny the Orangutan and Darwin**

[事实] Jenny was an orangutan at London Zoo in the late 1830s, arriving from Borneo at the end of 1837.
[事实] Jenny wore human clothes, drank tea, and lived in the heated giraffe house.
[事实] Charles Darwin visited Jenny on 28 March 1838 after returning from the Beagle voyage.
[事实] Darwin described Jenny in childlike terms and was struck by her emotions, tool use, jealousy, and reaction to a mirror.
[事实] Jenny died in 1839 and was replaced by another orangutan also called Jenny.
[推测] The episode closes by presenting Jenny as a crucial example of why Darwin and later observers saw humans and apes as deeply connected.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in its range: it turns a comic topic into a tour through Roman identity, Islamic court culture, Regency cruelty, modern celebrity, military history, local folklore, art criticism, and Darwinian science.

Its strongest thread is the repeated tension between fascination and exploitation. The primates are loved, dressed up, paid, celebrated, mourned, displayed, gambled on, and sometimes badly harmed.

[推测] The main limitation is that many stories depend on anecdotal or contradictory sources. The hosts acknowledge this directly, which makes uncertainty part of the episode rather than a hidden weakness.

[推测] This episode suits listeners who enjoy historical oddities with a larger interpretive point: how humans use animals, especially primates, to think about status, humor, violence, intelligence, and themselves.
