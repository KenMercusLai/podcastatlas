# Spring, then fall: a weakened Muslim Brotherhood

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* covers three stories: the Muslim Brotherhood’s decline after its Arab Spring gains, the difficulty of comparing crime in Europe and America, and the rapid global rise of padel.

The first segment argues that the Muslim Brotherhood is weaker and more divided than at any point in recent memory. Its leadership has been killed, jailed, or pushed into exile, while pressure from Middle Eastern governments and the United States has intensified.

The second segment complicates claims that Europe is becoming lawless. The discussion says homicide rates are generally much higher in American cities, though some crimes such as robbery and burglary make the comparison less straightforward. The strongest conclusion is that many major cities on both sides of the Atlantic have become safer over time.

The final segment explains why padel has become popular: it is easier to learn than tennis or squash, highly social, and profitable for clubs. But its future as a spectator sport and its accessibility remain unresolved.

[事实] Brown says the Brotherhood has struggled to respond to external pressure.

[事实] The episode introduces competing online images: Europe as violent and unsafe, and America as full of guns, gangsters, and rogue immigration agents.
[事实] Elon Musk told The Economist that many parts of Britain and Europe are not safe to walk around.

[推测] The discussion treats social-media crime narratives as politically charged and often exaggerated.

[09:12] **Why crime comparisons are difficult**

[事实] James says crime comparisons are difficult because definitions of reported crime differ by country.
[事实] He says people’s willingness to report crimes varies widely.
[事实] He also says city boundaries and population density can distort comparisons.
[事实] Large hinterlands, such as Staten Island within New York City, can make crime rates appear lower.

[09:53] **A dataset built for comparison**

[事实] James discusses a dataset by Matt Ashby of University College London and colleagues.
[事实] The researchers selected 20 global cities with populations of at least 2.5 million that publish annual crime statistics.
[事实] They adjusted city boundaries so the cities had more similar population densities.
[事实] They harmonised crime definitions using Britain as the benchmark.

[10:50] **Homicide findings**

[事实] James says homicide is the most comparable crime statistic across borders.
[事实] Chicago is described as by far the most dangerous city in the dataset, with 587 homicides in 2024, or 22 per 100,000 residents.
[事实] That rate is said to be more than twice that of Los Angeles, the next worst city in the dataset.
[事实] The average homicide rate among the ten European cities was 1.7 per 100,000.
[事实] New York City’s homicide count has fallen by 90% over 35 years, and in 2024 its murder rate was only slightly above Barcelona’s.

[12:01] **Other crimes complicate the picture**

[事实] The study looked at eight crime categories.
[事实] Personal robberies involving force happen about as often in both regions.
[事实] London and Paris appear especially bad for personal robbery.
[事实] Domestic burglaries appear more common in Europe.
[事实] Robberies of businesses, including shoplifting with force, have risen sharply in London.

[12:34] **Limits of the data**

[事实] Matt Ashby described the statistics as the “least worst” attempt to make crime data comparable.
[事实] Reported rape cases in England and Wales have doubled over the past decade, but confidential survey data suggests cases have held steady.
[事实] James says this may reflect better reporting rather than more rape.
[事实] Vehicle crime comparisons could be improved by controlling for the number of vehicles in each city.
[推测] Some apparent crime differences may reflect reporting behaviour or local conditions rather than true differences in danger.

[14:07] **Are cities getting more dangerous?**

[事实] James answers “no” to the claim that Britain and Europe are getting more dangerous.
[事实] He says comparing a city with itself over time provides the best evidence.
[事实] Murder rates in London and New York are lower than at any time since record-keeping began.
[事实] Personal robbery in Paris has nearly halved over the past decade.
[推测] The strongest trend in the segment is that many major cities are safer than they were a generation ago.

[事实] The episode says 35 million players in 150 countries are playing padel.
[事实] Padel is described as one of the fastest-growing sports in the world.
[事实] Hamish Clayton visits the London leg of the Premier Padel tournament near Wimbledon.
[事实] The professional circuit is funded by Qatar, and London was one of 26 elite tournaments on the calendar that year.

[16:08] **Professional scale and prize money**

[事实] The London tournament is described as the first elite professional padel tournament held in Britain.
[事实] Players from 15 countries competed.
[事实] The leading players are described as Spanish and Argentine.

[16:44] **Why padel is easy to pick up**

[事实] Padel’s underarm serve, forgiving rackets, and small courts make it easier to learn than tennis, badminton, or squash.
[事实] These features make rallies easier for new players.
[事实] Fans at the tournament describe the sport as friendly, social, and good for confidence.
[推测] Accessibility and sociability are central reasons for padel’s popularity.

[17:34] **Growth in Britain**

[事实] About five years ago, Britain had only 87 padel courts and around 15,000 players.
[事实] The Lawn Tennis Association now estimates Britain has about one million players and more than 2,000 courts.
[事实] One player says women’s LTA tournaments are inclusive and friendly.
[推测] The sport’s community atmosphere appears to be helping its rapid adoption.

[18:12] **Origins and Olympic ambitions**

[事实] Padel began in Mexico in 1969.
[事实] Spain and Argentina became the two centres of the sport.
[事实] The sport spread through Europe and South America.
[事实] The International Padel Federation wants to grow the sport in Asia and North America.
[事实] The federation hopes padel could become an Olympic sport, potentially by the 2032 Brisbane Games.

[19:10] **Can padel become a spectator sport?**

[事实] Matches from the London tournament streamed on YouTube and Red Bull TV.
[事实] Organisers said roughly 4,000 tickets for the final sold out quickly.
[事实] Fans interviewed give mixed views on whether padel could rival football or tennis as a spectator sport.
[推测] Padel’s participation boom does not automatically guarantee mass broadcast appeal.

[19:54] **Business model and access problem**

[事实] Roughly three padel courts can fit into the footprint of one tennis court, creating more revenue opportunities for clubs.
[事实] Britain’s padel courts are described as among the busiest and priciest in the world.
[事实] The median fee for an indoor court is £44 per booking, while some London courts cost £100 an hour.
[事实] Amir Amin says the sport still has an elitist element and needs to become more democratised.
[事实] Tennis still had about 5.6 million British players at last count in 2023.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is breadth: it moves from Middle Eastern political Islam to crime statistics and then to a cultural-business story about sport, while keeping each segment anchored in concrete reporting.

[推测] The strongest segment is the crime discussion, because it explains why simple comparisons are misleading and distinguishes between cross-city rankings and long-term trends.

[推测] The Muslim Brotherhood segment is useful for listeners seeking a concise explanation of the movement’s current weakness, though the short format leaves limited room for country-by-country detail.

[推测] The padel segment works well as a lighter closing story and is best suited to listeners interested in how social habits, sport infrastructure, and commercial incentives can turn a niche game into a global trend.
