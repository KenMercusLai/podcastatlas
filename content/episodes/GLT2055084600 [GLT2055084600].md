# The Rise of the Nazis: The Death of Weimar

## 概览

This episode traces how the Nazis moved from a violent fringe movement to the edge of power in Germany between 1929 and January 1933. The hosts use Horst Wessel and the Nazi cult built around him as an entry point into the movement’s appeal to young men seeking purpose, spectacle, belonging, and violence.

The central argument is that the Great Depression transformed Nazi prospects, but economic collapse alone did not bring Hitler to power. The episode repeatedly stresses the failures of Weimar’s conservative, military, and presidential elites, who underestimated Hitler, weakened parliamentary democracy, and tried to use the Nazis for their own authoritarian projects.

The discussion follows the breakdown of parliamentary government, rule by emergency decree, repeated election gambles, escalating street violence, and the rivalries among Brüning, Papen, Schleicher, Hindenburg, and Hitler. It ends with the January 1933 deal that made Hitler chancellor, framed as a catastrophic miscalculation by those who believed they had “hired” him.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Horst Wessel and the Episode Frame**

[事实] The episode opens with an English rendering of the Horst Wessel Song and explains that it became central to Nazi political ritual after the Nazis took power.

[事实] The hosts frame the episode as the story of the Great Depression hitting Germany, street fighting between Nazis and communists intensifying, and Hitler moving toward power.

[事实] They connect Horst Wessel’s story to Nazi adaptations and parodies of Christian symbolism.

[01:52] **From Fringe Party to Crisis Point**

[事实] The hosts recap that previous episodes covered the intellectual and political origins of Nazism and Hitler’s development through much of the 1920s.

[事实] By the late 1920s, the Nazis were still a small party but were expanding, especially in the Protestant countryside.

[事实] Hitler is described as a leader with rhetorical gifts who had become convinced he was destined to lead Germany to greatness.

[02:41] **Horst Wessel as a Nazi Type**

[事实] Horst Wessel joined the Nazi Party around 1926 at the age of 19.

[事实] He came from a middle-class Lutheran background and sought excitement, glamour, swagger, purpose, and violence in the Nazi movement.

[事实] He studied law in Berlin, became interested in street fighting, and was identified by Goebbels as useful in confronting communist areas of the city.

[推测] Wessel’s appeal to Nazism is presented as an example of how the movement could replace older sources of meaning, including church and conventional patriotism, for some young men.

[04:26] **Martyrdom and Pseudo-Christian Myth**

[事实] Wessel wrote songs with hymn-like qualities and took part in marches through communist areas.

[事实] He was shot in his room, lingered for weeks, and then died.

[事实] Goebbels turned Wessel into a Nazi martyr and explicitly used Christian imagery at his funeral, including comparison to Christ.

[事实] The hosts describe later Nazi rituals around Wessel as quasi-religious, including offerings and rally songs that elevated him above Christ.

[06:00] **Depression and Nazi Appeal**

[事实] The hosts argue that the Nazis had an existing appeal in the traumatized and brutalized landscape of 1920s Germany.

[事实] Dominic says the Great Depression transformed Nazi fortunes irrevocably.

[事实] Germany was vulnerable because it carried the burdens of war, reparations, the memory of hyperinflation, and dependence on American bank loans.

[07:01] **Economic Collapse in Germany**

[事实] After the Wall Street crash of October 1929, American banks began withdrawing money from Central Europe.

[事实] The full impact in Germany became clear in 1931, especially after the collapse of the Credit Anstalt in Austria.

[事实] By 1932, about one in three registered German workers was unemployed.

[事实] The hosts cite estimates of around 13 million people living in households affected by unemployment and about half a million people homeless.

[09:34] **Paramilitary Politics and the Communists**

[事实] Mass unemployment made uniforms, weapons, comradeship, and street action attractive to many young men.

[事实] The stormtroopers offered camps, songs, belonging, and political violence; communist groups also drew in disaffected people.

[事实] Communist membership trebled between 1929 and 1932.

[事实] Ernst Thälmann, the Communist Party leader, is described as a Hamburg manual laborer, Iron Cross veteran, revolutionary Stalinist, and loyal follower of Moscow.

[11:25] **The Left’s Division and Middle-Class Fear**

[事实] The communists hated the Social Democrats and other Weimar parties, partly because of the violence used against left-wing revolutionaries after 1918.

[事实] In Bloody May 1929, Berlin police controlled by Social Democrats attacked a communist demonstration, killing 33 people and injuring hundreds.

[事实] The hosts argue that many bourgeois voters feared communism more than Nazism because they expected communists to expropriate property and create a Stalinist revolution.

[推测] This division on the left and fear among the middle classes helped make the Nazis seem, to some contemporaries, like the less threatening extremist option.

[14:25] **Weimar’s Political Suicide**

[事实] The hosts argue that the Nazis would not have come to power without the failures of Weimar’s own elites.

[事实] The people who should have defended Weimar democracy instead undermined parliamentary government and weakened the system from within.

[事实] Dominic describes the coming sequence as one of the most shocking acts of political suicide in modern history.

[15:33] **Brüning and Government by Decree**

[事实] In March 1930, Hermann Müller’s grand coalition collapsed, leaving no Reichstag majority.

[事实] President Hindenburg and the military circle around him disliked parliamentary democracy and saw the crisis as a chance to move toward authoritarian rule.

[事实] Heinrich Brüning of the Catholic Centre Party became chancellor and increasingly governed through Article 48 emergency decrees.

[事实] The use of emergency powers is described as a ticking time bomb in the Weimar constitution.

[17:32] **Austerity, Authoritarian Instincts, and Unpopularity**

[事实] Brüning is described as clever, austere, Catholic, patriotic, monarchist, and instinctively authoritarian.

[事实] His restrictions and law-and-order measures tended to be directed more against the left than the right.

[事实] His economic policy was deflationary: he cut spending and unemployment benefits rather than pursuing public works or stimulus.

[事实] These policies pushed more people into poverty and made him extremely unpopular.

[20:46] **The 1930 Election Gamble**

[事实] Brüning asked Hindenburg to dissolve the Reichstag and call an election in 1930.

[事实] Ian Kershaw is cited as calling this a decision of breathtaking irresponsibility.

[事实] The Nazis campaigned with spectacle, marches, parades, films, brass bands, and promises of national renewal without detailed policy.

[事实] In the election, the Nazis rose from about one million votes and 12 seats to 6.5 million votes and 107 seats.

[23:42] **New Nazi Voters and Violence in Parliament**

[事实] The Nazis gained support among Protestant farmers, teachers, Protestant priests, respectable middle-class voters, and young first-time voters.

[事实] One in four Nazi voters in 1930 had never voted before.

[事实] The Communists also rose, from 54 to 77 seats.

[事实] The Reichstag increasingly absorbed the language and atmosphere of street violence, including shouting, uniforms, and abuse.

[26:12] **Destroying Democracy Through Democratic Means**

[事实] After the break, the hosts stress that the Nazis did not take power by storming the capital with guns.

[事实] Their ascent came through backroom deals, election gambits, and parliamentary mechanisms used to destroy democracy.

[事实] The hosts compare this to a continuing democratic problem: how to deal with movements that compete electorally while aiming to abolish the system.

[推测] The episode presents Weimar as a warning about anti-democratic actors using democratic legitimacy against democracy itself.

[28:22] **Schleicher and the Army’s Agenda**

[事实] After September 1930, there was no workable Reichstag majority, and Brüning governed by decree through Hindenburg.

[事实] General Kurt von Schleicher emerges as a political general and master intriguer.

[事实] Schleicher wanted an authoritarian arrangement that would free the army from parliamentary control and enable rearmament.

[事实] The army’s ambitions included reversing Versailles and restoring German power, though not necessarily launching the later scale of world war.

[31:10] **The 1932 Presidential Election**

[事实] Hindenburg stood again for the presidency in 1932, despite being a Prussian reactionary who disliked Weimar democracy.

[事实] His main rivals were Hitler and Communist leader Ernst Thälmann.

[事实] Social Democrats backed Hindenburg as the candidate of stability because they feared the extremist alternatives.

[事实] Hitler used modern campaign techniques, including air travel, mass rallies, and Goebbels’s theatrical staging.

[33:48] **Hitler’s Strong Showing and Brüning’s Fall**

[事实] Hitler won 37 percent and about 13 million votes in the presidential election, while Hindenburg won 53 percent.

[事实] Hitler won in some Protestant countryside areas, including places such as Schleswig-Holstein and Pomerania.

[事实] The result showed strong Nazi momentum, and Brüning was removed at the end of May 1932.

[推测] The election showed that Hitler had not won power, but had become too large for conservative elites to ignore.

[34:39] **Franz von Papen and the Cabinet of Barons**

[事实] Hindenburg chose Franz von Papen as chancellor rather than Hitler.

[事实] Papen was an aristocratic Catholic from the Centre Party, wealthy, well connected, and tied to army, political, and business circles.

[事实] His cabinet was called the Cabinet of Barons or Cabinet of Experts.

[事实] The hosts portray Papen as an intriguer with little credible plan for Germany’s crisis.

[38:00] **Papen’s Election Gamble and the Stormtroopers**

[事实] Papen and Schleicher persuaded Hindenburg to dissolve the Reichstag and call another election.

[事实] They lifted the ban on stormtroopers marching openly, hoping to appease Hitler and make him an ally.

[事实] In July 1932, fighting in Altona between stormtroopers and the Communist Red Front left 18 people dead and hundreds injured.

[事实] Papen used the crisis to remove the Social Democratic government of Prussia by emergency decree.

[40:00] **The Prussian Coup and Democratic Paralysis**

[事实] Prussia was Germany’s largest and most important state, and it had been run by the Social Democrats.

[事实] Papen’s takeover of Prussia destroyed the federal principle and opened the way to centralization of the state.

[事实] The Social Democrats did not resist with a general strike, partly because unemployment was so high and because they feared civil war.

[推测] The episode treats the Prussian coup as a crucial precedent that later benefited the Nazis once they held central power.

[42:12] **The July 1932 Election and Extremist Victory**

[事实] The July 1932 election campaign was saturated with violent imagery across parties.

[事实] Nazi messaging emphasized national rebirth, attacks on communists, attacks on Jewish financiers, and contempt for parliamentary politicians.

[事实] The Nazis doubled their vote to about 13 million and became the largest party with 230 seats.

[事实] The Communists also increased their strength, reaching 89 seats.

[44:18] **Hitler Refuses Subordinate Power**

[事实] Goebbels believed the Nazis may have reached their electoral ceiling at around 37 percent.

[事实] Hitler did not want to enter government as a junior partner.

[事实] The hosts argue that Hitler’s self-image as Führer and man of destiny made ordinary coalition politics impossible for him.

[事实] Hitler began secret talks with Schleicher about becoming chancellor, but the talks failed.

[45:14] **Hindenburg Says No**

[事实] Papen introduced the death penalty for political crimes, intending it mainly for use against communists.

[事实] Soon after, Nazi brownshirts killed a communist in Upper Silesia, and five were sentenced to death.

[事实] Hitler reacted furiously, saying he could not work with people who sentenced Nazis to death.

[事实] On August 13, 1932, Hindenburg refused Hitler’s demand to become chancellor and said he would not hand the Reich to an intolerant party that encouraged violence.

[47:55] **Papen’s Collapse and Nazi Decline**

[事实] Papen tried to dissolve the Reichstag and move toward permanent rule by decree.

[事实] Before he could do so, the Communists introduced a no-confidence motion, and the Nazis supported holding the vote first.

[事实] Papen’s government lost the vote by 512 to 42.

[事实] In the next election, the Nazi vote fell from 13.7 million to 12.7 million and the party lost 44 seats.

[49:59] **Stasis, Intrigue, and Schleicher’s Failure**

[事实] The Communists rose to 100 seats, but they and the Social Democrats still would not work together.

[事实] Papen and Schleicher fell out, and Schleicher eventually became chancellor himself.

[事实] Schleicher tried to deal with Gregor Strasser inside the Nazi Party, but Strasser resigned his posts and left the party.

[事实] Hindenburg’s circle distrusted Schleicher, and conservatives around Hindenburg reopened contacts with the Nazis.

[52:13] **The January 1933 Deal**

[事实] On January 22, 1933, Hitler, Papen, and Hindenburg’s son Oskar held a crucial secret meeting.

[事实] The deal made Hitler chancellor and Papen vice chancellor, with conservatives expecting to control the cabinet.

[事实] The same day, Hitler visited Horst Wessel’s grave and addressed a staged memorial service.

[推测] The juxtaposition of private bargaining and public martyr ritual captures the episode’s larger theme: Nazi power grew through both elite intrigue and theatrical political religion.

[54:45] **Hitler Becomes Chancellor**

[事实] On January 30, 1933, Hitler went to Hindenburg’s apartment at the Reich Chancellery and was appointed chancellor.

[事实] Papen believed the conservatives had hired Hitler and could control him.

[事实] The episode ends by noting that the next stage will cover the Reichstag fire and Hitler’s consolidation of power.

[推测] Papen’s confidence is presented as one of the final and most disastrous misjudgments in the collapse of Weimar.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its careful separation of causes: economic depression is shown as essential, but not sufficient. The decisive element is the chain of elite miscalculations that turned Nazi electoral strength into actual state power.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the combination of political narrative and symbolic analysis. Horst Wessel’s story gives the discussion a human and ritual focus, while the later sections explain the procedural and institutional failures that made Hitler’s appointment possible.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode is necessarily dense, with many names, elections, and intrigues compressed into under an hour. Listeners unfamiliar with Weimar politics may need extra context on Article 48, Prussian federal power, and the party system.

[推测] The episode is especially suited to listeners interested in how democracies can be hollowed out legally, how extremist movements use spectacle and violence, and how conservative elites can underestimate radical partners they believe they can control.
