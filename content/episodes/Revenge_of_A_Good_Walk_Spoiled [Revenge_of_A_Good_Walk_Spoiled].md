# Revenge of A Good Walk Spoiled

## 概览

Malcolm Gladwell revisits his 2017 episode “A Good Walk Spoiled,” which argued that Los Angeles’s private golf clubs occupy enormous tracts of valuable urban land while remaining inaccessible to the public and receiving extraordinary property-tax advantages.

The original investigation connects golf’s intensive use of land with elite privilege, corporate executives’ costly devotion to the sport, and California tax rules that allow long-established country clubs to avoid reassessment. Gladwell uses the Ship of Theseus paradox to question the legal claim that member-owned clubs have not changed ownership despite decades of membership turnover.

Nearly ten years later, Los Angeles City Council member Adrin Nazarian acts on the episode’s argument. His team proposes a parcel tax intended to approximate ordinary taxation based on the land’s highest and best use, prompting public support, alarm among country clubs, and negotiations over their future.

The episode concludes that journalism and public argument can eventually influence policy. Rather than insisting that every golf course disappear, Gladwell and Nazarian consider compromises that would return some subsidized land—or at least meaningful access—to the public.

## 分段落总结

[01:02] **The fear that an argument will never matter**

[事实] Gladwell describes a writer’s fear that carefully developed ideas will be ignored.

[事实] He recalls advocating that schools group children more fairly by birth month because relative age can be mistaken for ability, but says no superintendent adopted the proposal.

[事实] His pessimism changes when Los Angeles council member Adrin Nazarian explains that a staff member introduced him to Gladwell’s podcast.

[推测] This opening establishes the episode as a story about the delayed real-world impact of published ideas.

[04:49] **Private green space in West Los Angeles**

[事实] Gladwell describes several large, beautiful, and exclusive country clubs occupying valuable land across West Los Angeles.

[事实] Runners outside Brentwood Country Club must use a narrow, rocky track beside the road while the spacious course remains behind fencing and barbed wire.

[事实] Landscape architect Dai Zock observes the course’s mature trees and says that turning it into a welcoming park would begin with removing the barriers.

[推测] The physical contrast between the crowded path and empty private grounds embodies the episode’s broader argument about unequal access to urban space.

[08:31] **Golf’s exceptional consumption of land and resources**

[事实] Gladwell says a typical golf course occupies roughly 200 acres and requires continual mowing, pesticides, sand, and landscaping.

[事实] Because golf balls travel at high speed over long distances, a private course may accommodate only about 72 players at once.

[事实] He contrasts golf’s extremely low population density with basketball, calculating that a basketball court built to golf’s spatial requirements would cover approximately 30 acres.

[推测] Golf is presented not merely as recreation but as an unusually inefficient allocation of scarce city land.

[09:37] **When executives spend their time golfing**

[事实] Economist Lee Biggerstaff cross-referenced USGA score records with executives from 1,500 major publicly traded companies.

[事实] Among the 363 CEOs found in the database, the average was about 15 recorded rounds annually; the top quartile played at least 22, and the top 10 percent at least 37.

[事实] Biggerstaff’s research found that firms performed worse, and CEOs were more likely to be dismissed, as the CEOs played more golf.

[事实] One executive recorded roughly 148 rounds in a single year.

[推测] Gladwell’s deliberately provocative description of golf as an addiction challenges the assumption that elite leisure is harmless or deserved.

[15:43] **Bob Hope and the constitutional privilege of country clubs**

[事实] California property taxation ordinarily considers a property’s “highest and best use,” rather than accepting an artificially low valuation based solely on its current use.

[事实] In 1960, country clubs sought a constitutional amendment exempting their land from that standard.

[事实] The clubs enlisted Bob Hope, an enormously popular entertainer and obsessive golfer whose public persona represented the common man.

[事实] The proposition passed and became part of the California Constitution.

[推测] Gladwell argues that an everyman celebrity helped secure a lasting public subsidy for wealthy, exclusionary institutions.

[20:27] **Proposition 13 compounds the tax advantage**

[事实] California’s 1978 Proposition 13 fixed the property-tax rate at one percent and generally limited reassessment to new construction or a change of ownership.

[事实] Long-established country clubs consequently retained both their special golf-course valuation and assessments effectively frozen at 1978 levels.

[事实] Most major Los Angeles clubs were founded before 1978 and therefore benefited from both protections.

[推测] The combination of the earlier exemption and Proposition 13 transformed a historical preference into a durable, multigenerational advantage.

[24:36] **Membership turnover and the Ship of Theseus**

[事实] Equity-based country clubs are owned by members, with ownership shares passing to new members when existing members leave or die.

[事实] In 2010, Los Angeles County concluded that gradual turnover did not constitute a change of ownership because no single transaction transferred more than 50 percent.

[事实] Gladwell compares this reasoning to the Ship of Theseus: whether something remains the same object after all its component parts have gradually been replaced.

[事实] Philosopher Mark Cohen explains that both component-based identity and spatiotemporal continuity can produce plausible answers, depending on context.

[推测] The philosophical analogy exposes how an unresolved question of identity was treated as legally settled in a way favorable to country clubs.

[31:38] **The public subsidy behind private exclusivity**

[事实] Gladwell says Los Angeles ranks near the bottom among major American metropolitan areas for public park space, while its large golf courses remain closed to the public.

[事实] Based on an estimated Los Angeles Country Club land value of $9 billion, he calculates that ordinary taxation might produce about $90 million annually.

[事实] He says the club actually paid approximately $200,000, implying an annual difference of about $89.8 million.

[事实] Cohen agrees that continuity principles can perpetuate aristocratic rights and inequality when ownership privileges survive successive generations.

[推测] The central objection is not simply that golf is exclusive, but that taxpayers support exclusivity without receiving corresponding public benefits.

[35:42] **A proposal for reciprocal public access**

[事实] Gladwell proposes that clubs retaining their tax privileges should remove barriers and open their grounds to ordinary residents during evenings and weekends.

[事实] He cites St Andrews in Scotland and Toronto’s Rosedale Golf Club as examples of prestigious courses that permit public access at designated times or seasons.

[事实] At Brentwood, Gladwell and Zock observe more people using the narrow outside track than the course itself on a Saturday afternoon.

[推测] These examples suggest that elite golf and democratic access need not be mutually exclusive.

[38:07] **A council member turns the episode into policy**

[事实] Nearly ten years after the original episode, Nazarian listens to it and learns how little property tax the major Los Angeles country clubs pay.

[事实] Gladwell says six major clubs occupy land conservatively valued at $15 billion while collectively paying less than $1 million in annual property taxes.

[事实] Nazarian’s team develops a proposed city parcel tax of approximately four dollars per square foot, designed to approach the revenue that highest-and-best-use taxation might generate.

[事实] Applied to the approximately 325-acre Los Angeles Country Club, the proposal would produce an estimated annual bill of roughly $55 million.

[推测] The intentionally severe proposal functions as leverage to force a public debate over taxation, land use, and access.

[41:25] **Public support and country-club resistance**

[事实] Nazarian says initial polling showed 64 percent support for the proposal.

[事实] The Los Angeles Country Club warned members that it had engaged experienced advisers and invited members with useful expertise or relationships to help.

[事实] The clubs initially sought negotiations, while Bel-Air Country Club chose to fight the proposal.

[事实] The Society of Golf Historians argued that historic courses should be preserved like important works of architecture or art.

[推测] The preservation argument reframes golf courses as cultural assets, but does not by itself answer why their stewardship should receive large public subsidies without broad public access.

[44:50] **Negotiation, compromise, and a modest request**

[事实] Gladwell suggests that Los Angeles Country Club could dedicate one of its two large parcels as a public park while retaining the other as a private course.

[事实] Nazarian responds positively and presents the idea as a possible basis for negotiations.

[事实] Gladwell also asks Brentwood Country Club to move its fence inward by three feet so runners can have a safer, more usable path.

[事实] He closes by presenting the renewed controversy as evidence that speaking truth to power can eventually produce action.

[推测] The ending narrows an abstract fight over billions of dollars into tangible compromises that could deliver visible public value.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest feature is its layered argument. It moves from the everyday experience of running beside a fence to land-use economics, corporate behavior, constitutional tax policy, and a philosophical puzzle about identity. Humor keeps the investigation accessible without obscuring its core claim: private privilege becomes especially difficult to justify when the public is paying for it.

Its follow-up structure also gives the story unusual value. The original 2017 critique is not merely replayed; the new material demonstrates how it inspired a concrete ballot proposal, triggered organized resistance, and opened negotiations. That arc offers a compelling example of journalism influencing civic debate after a long delay.

[推测] The episode is openly polemical, and its selective emphasis gives limited attention to the clubs’ full legal, environmental, historical, or financial defenses. Listeners seeking a neutral policy analysis would need additional perspectives and independently verified valuations.

[推测] It is especially suitable for listeners interested in urban planning, tax equity, public space, institutional privilege, and the practical relationship between storytelling and political change.
