# Weather and Climate That Changed History

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* explores ten ways weather and climate altered history, from Ice Age conditions to battles, revolutions, literature, technology and modern British politics.

The hosts distinguish weather as day-to-day conditions from climate as long-term patterns, but their examples show both can matter when they affect food supplies, military timing, transport, political legitimacy or cultural imagination.

A recurring conclusion is that climate or weather rarely acts alone: it intensifies existing pressures such as war, hunger, weak states or poor decisions. The D-Day example stands apart because weather forecasting is presented as a life-saving intervention rather than a disaster.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Episode Setup: Weather Versus Climate**

[事实] Dominic Sandbrook says listener John Tinker suggested an episode about the history of weather, or weather’s impact on history.
[事实] Tom Holland distinguishes weather as day-to-day conditions and climate as changes across centuries and millennia.
[事实] The hosts frame the episode as their top ten examples of weather or climate changing history.

[02:25] **The Ice Age and the Conditions for Civilization**

[事实] Tom nominates the Ice Age and says humans are still living in the Quaternary Ice Age because polar ice caps exist.
[事实] He says the last glacial maximum ended about 11,700 years ago, beginning the Holocene, the period in which history unfolds.
[事实] Creswell Crags is used as an example of Ice Age Britain, with cave art and a carved horse bone from about 13,000 years ago.
[事实] Tom links the retreat of glaciers to permanent life in Britain and to early Near Eastern urban settlements such as Jericho.
[事实] The Younger Dryas is described as a roughly 1,500-year cold spell that interrupted early urbanization before conditions improved.

[08:28] **The Little Ice Age and the Seventeenth-Century Crisis**

[事实] Dominic cites Geoffrey Parker’s *Global Crisis* and says temperatures dropped by about two degrees during the Little Ice Age.
[事实] Examples include snow in Japan, drought in sub-Saharan Africa, rivers drying in the New World, harvest failures, the frozen Bosphorus and Thames frost fairs.
[事实] He connects the climate stress to famine, the Thirty Years’ War, witch crazes and major population losses.
[事实] He says the Ming dynasty collapsed amid famine and floods, and presents the period as a major global turning point.

[13:03] **Late Antique Cooling and the End of Antiquity**

[事实] Tom introduces Late Antique Cooling, beginning in the mid-sixth century AD and probably linked to volcanic eruptions in 536, 539/540 and 547.
[事实] He describes weak sunlight, bluish light, blurred seasons, summer snow and famine in sources from the Mediterranean, Ireland and China.
[事实] He says temperatures may have fallen by two or three degrees for a century or two, which would have been devastating for subsistence societies.
[事实] He links this period to Byzantine contraction, Sasanian collapse, the emergence of Islam, possible roots of the Viking Age and Teotihuacan’s decline.
[推测] The segment treats cooling as a broad pressure behind simultaneous upheavals, while acknowledging that historians are cautious about single-cause explanations.

[18:03] **Why Weather Mattered More in Earlier Societies**

[事实] Dominic argues that weather mattered more further back in history because ancient, medieval and early modern societies depended heavily on harvests.
[事实] Tom says industrialization has allowed modern societies to escape some direct dependence on weather, at least temporarily.
[推测] This exchange gives the episode a framework: weather becomes historically decisive when it strikes fragile food systems or political structures.

[19:03] **The Great Frost of 1709 and the Great Northern War**

[事实] Dominic explains the Great Northern War as a struggle between Peter the Great’s Russia and Charles XII’s Sweden for power in the Baltic and northern Europe.
[事实] He says the Great Frost of 1709 drove temperatures in western Europe to about minus 15 degrees Celsius.
[事实] Charles invaded Russia in 1708, while Peter withdrew and used scorched-earth tactics.
[事实] The frost reduced Charles’s army through hunger and famine, weakened expected Cossack support, and preceded Russia’s victory at Poltava.
[事实] Dominic says Poltava broke Sweden’s power, after which Charles spent five years in the Ottoman Empire.

[23:52] **Kamikaze Typhoons and the Mongol Invasions of Japan**

[事实] Tom discusses Kublai Khan’s two Mongol invasions of Japan.
[事实] He says the Mongols had technological advantages, including explosives, possible cannon and early hand grenades.
[事实] In both invasions, a typhoon described as a kamikaze destroyed or disrupted the Mongol fleets.
[事实] Tom notes debate over the story because of later Japanese wartime propaganda, but says recent geological work suggests thirteenth-century Japan was more typhoon-prone than today.

[29:53] **Weather and the French Revolution**

[事实] Dominic argues that weather played an underappreciated role in the causes of the French Revolution.
[事实] He says an Icelandic volcanic eruption in 1785 was followed by cooling in the late 1780s.
[事实] In 1788, France experienced drought, crop failure, a major hailstorm, heavy snow and flooding.
[事实] By summer 1789, bread costs for French workers had risen from about half their income to about 90 percent.
[事实] Dominic says he has read that bread prices peaked on July 14, 1789, the day of the storming of the Bastille, while noting that the fact sounds almost too perfect.

[33:00] **Agincourt: Rain, Mud and English Victory**

[事实] Tom picks the Battle of Agincourt in 1415, after Henry V landed in France, besieged Harfleur and marched toward Calais.
[事实] The English army was outnumbered, wet, short of supplies and demoralized, while the French expected victory.
[事实] Rain fell on recently ploughed ground, and French cavalry and men-at-arms sank into churned mud during the attack.
[事实] Tom says English archers and the mud helped turn the battle into a French disaster.
[事实] He adds that Agincourt enabled Henry to return in force, even though England ultimately lost the Hundred Years’ War.

[38:07] **D-Day and the Weather Forecast**

[事实] Dominic presents D-Day as an example of a weather forecast changing history.
[事实] The invasion needed clear skies, a full moon, calm seas and low tide, making the timing unusually complex.
[事实] Group Captain James Stagg persuaded Eisenhower to delay by 24 hours, then recommended proceeding on June 6, 1944.
[事实] Montgomery supported going ahead, and the invasion proceeded.
[推测] Dominic argues that a two-week delay could have put the fleet into a major storm on June 19 and cost many more lives.

[41:59] **The Year Without a Summer**

[事实] Tom introduces 1816, the Year Without a Summer, caused by Mount Tambora’s 1815 eruption in Indonesia.
[事实] He says volcanic ash dimmed the sun, creating a brief global winter, famine and terrible conditions.
[事实] Horse starvation in Germany damaged transport, and Karl Drais responded by inventing the laufmaschine, a prototype bicycle.
[事实] Miserable weather by Lake Geneva kept Byron, Percy Bysshe Shelley, Mary Shelley, Claire Clairmont and Dr Polidori indoors.
[事实] The hosts connect this setting to *Frankenstein*, Polidori’s aristocratic vampire prototype and Byron’s poem about darkness.

[47:14] **Britain’s Winter of Discontent**

[事实] Dominic explains the late-1970s Winter of Discontent under Labour prime minister James Callaghan.
[事实] Callaghan wanted unions to accept a five percent pay limit to fight inflation before a 1979 election.
[事实] Union resistance coincided with Britain’s worst winter since 1962, with snowdrifts, closed roads and people freezing in cars.
[事实] Strikes disrupted lorries, railways, shops, schools, rubbish collection and burials.
[事实] Callaghan’s trip to Guadeloupe and Barbados during the crisis produced damaging images, and *The Sun* ran the headline “Crisis? What Crisis?” although he did not say those words.
[事实] Dominic says the episode badly hurt Labour and gave Conservatives a political attack line for years afterward.

[53:49] **Closing Reflections**

[事实] Tom chooses Creswell Crags as his favorite example because of his recent visit and the site’s Ice Age atmosphere.
[事实] Dominic chooses the Great Northern War, especially the detail that Charles XII stayed away from Sweden for five years.
[事实] The hosts discuss auctioning a listener-chosen topic for a benefit appeal supporting homeless people and refugees.
[推测] The ending returns to the show’s usual comic style after a discussion mostly built around disaster, hunger and war.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in its breadth: it connects climate and weather to archaeology, urban origins, empire, warfare, revolution, literature, technology and electoral politics.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the comparative structure. By moving from deep time to the twentieth century, the hosts show how environmental shocks can matter at very different scales.

[推测] Its limitation is that the scientific mechanisms are often treated lightly or jokingly, so listeners looking for detailed climatology would need other sources.

[推测] It is best suited for listeners who enjoy narrative history, counterfactual moments and broad historical connections rather than a technical climate-history lecture.
