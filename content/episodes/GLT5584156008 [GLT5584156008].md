# Game of Thrones and the Road to the Hundred Years' War

## 概览

This episode opens a four-part series on the Hundred Years' War by explaining why a conflict between England and France, formally beginning in 1337, became such a long and destructive struggle. The hosts frame it through two contrasting lenses: chivalric romance, represented by Arthur Conan Doyle's *Sir Nigel*, and the suffering-focused account of Barbara Tuchman's *A Distant Mirror*.

The central argument is that the war did not begin simply because Edward III was denied the French throne. The transcript presents deeper causes: France's overwhelming wealth and prestige, England's lingering continental interests, the unstable legal status of Gascony, the French-Scottish alliance, and the personal and dynastic crises that weakened both kingdoms.

The episode ends with Edward III choosing confrontation after Philip VI confiscates Aquitaine in 1337. England is portrayed as the underdog, but Edward has parliamentary backing, a developing English national cause, and a military advantage that the hosts save for the next episode: the longbow.

## 分段落总结

[01:00] **A Chivalric Opening Through Sir Nigel**

[事实] The episode opens with a dramatic reading from Arthur Conan Doyle's *Sir Nigel*, featuring Nigel defeating the “Red Ferret.”

[事实] Tom Holland says *Sir Nigel* and *The White Company* were books his father read to him, and that Conan Doyle regarded them more highly than Sherlock Holmes.

[事实] The hosts use *Sir Nigel* to introduce the Hundred Years' War as a childhood source of fascination, full of knights, longbows, and patriotic adventure.

[推测] The opening deliberately sets up the romantic, heroic version of the war before contrasting it with a darker historical interpretation.

[03:05] **The Hundred Years' War as Catastrophe**

[事实] Barbara Tuchman's *A Distant Mirror* is introduced as offering a very different view of the 14th century and the Hundred Years' War.

[事实] The war is described as involving mercenaries, freebooters, pillaging, destruction, and the Black Death in the background.

[事实] The hosts state that the Hundred Years' War lasted 116 years, from 1337 to 1453, rather than exactly one hundred years.

[推测] The episode frames the war as both a story of chivalric spectacle and a long period of social devastation.

[04:38] **Structure of the Series**

[事实] The hosts say the war can be imagined in two halves, with England appearing dominant early in each half before the outcome turns.

[事实] This episode focuses on the build-up to the Hundred Years' War, while the following three episodes will cover the first half of the conflict.

[事实] The coming story includes the battles of Crécy and Poitiers, the collapse of France, and wider violence in Europe.

[推测] This episode is designed as a political and dynastic foundation for later battle-focused episodes.

[05:34] **France as the Medieval Superpower**

[事实] France in the early 14th century is described as far richer and more populous than England, with about six times England's income and three times its population.

[事实] Paris is described as the largest city in Western Europe north of the Alps, with a prestigious university and effective civil servants.

[事实] France is presented as the home of chivalry, with both hard power and soft power.

[推测] The English challenge to France is framed as especially dramatic because England is materially much weaker.

[07:48] **The Capetian Dynasty and the Growth of Royal France**

[事实] The French royal dynasty in this period descends from Hugh Capet, elected in 987.

[事实] Fourteen Capetian kings succeed one another from father to son, helping avoid repeated inheritance conflicts.

[事实] The French kings originally rule only a small royal domain around Paris and Orleans, while much of France is controlled by powerful vassals.

[事实] Over the 13th century, French kings expand royal authority by reducing Angevin power, absorbing lands after the Albigensian Crusade, and annexing Champagne and Brie.

[12:05] **Philip the Fair and the Height of French Power**

[事实] Philip IV, known as Philip the Fair, rules from 1285 to 1314 and is described as a hard ruler.

[事实] He defeats the Flemings after the Battle of the Golden Spurs, extracts reparations, and takes Lille into the royal domain.

[事实] He expels the Jews from France, destroys the Knights Templar, and pressures Pope Boniface VIII, leading toward the papacy's move to Avignon.

[事实] Froissart is cited as describing France under Philip the Fair as rich, strong, and unfamiliar with war.

[推测] Philip IV represents the high point of French royal confidence before dynastic failure destabilizes the kingdom.

[13:54] **England's Diminished Continental Position**

[事实] The English kings had once ruled major French holdings under the Angevin kings, but by the 13th century their horizons had shrunk.

[事实] Italians are said to have described the English king as “little” compared with the giant King of France.

[事实] The hosts argue that England's reduced continental holdings helped consolidate a stronger sense of English national unity.

[事实] English elites are still influenced by French culture, but increasingly lose French as a first language and adopt English or Anglo-Saxon names such as Edward.

[16:49] **Flanders and Brittany as English Leverage Points**

[事实] Flanders is described as a major weaving center dependent on English wool.

[事实] English kings can influence Flanders through control of the wool supply and trade blockades.

[事实] The dukes of Brittany have close links with England, including English lands and participation in English politics and warfare.

[推测] England's remaining influence in France is not only territorial but also economic and aristocratic.

[19:21] **Gascony and the Problem of Homage**

[事实] England still holds Gascony, the reduced remnant of Aquitaine centered on Bordeaux.

[事实] The Treaty of Paris in 1259 has the French king acknowledge English rule over Gascony, while requiring the English king to pay homage for it.

[事实] This arrangement allows Gascon subjects to appeal over the English king's head to the French king.

[事实] Edward I finds this humiliating and sees it as limiting his authority over his own domain.

[推测] Gascony becomes a structural source of conflict because it makes the English king both sovereign in England and subordinate in France.

[21:31] **Identity, Elite Culture, and Wine**

[事实] The hosts say English kings identify as kings of England but also see themselves as major players in French politics.

[事实] England is described as closer to a nation state than France, though neither fits the modern category fully.

[事实] Around 80,000 tons of wine are said to be exported annually from Bordeaux to England.

[事实] The two major export economies discussed, Flemish weaving and Bordeaux wine, are both closely tied to England.

[推测] Economic integration makes Anglo-French rivalry harder to disentangle, not easier.

[25:33] **Edward II, Isabella, and the Old Alliance**

[事实] After the break, the hosts turn to Edward II, describing him as a weak and unsuccessful king.

[事实] Edward II is defeated at Bannockburn and faces domestic instability involving favourites such as Piers Gaveston and the Despensers.

[事实] Scotland has been allied with France since 1295, with the agreement that either Scotland or France should invade England if the other is attacked by England.

[事实] Edward II is married to Isabella, the youngest daughter of Philip IV of France.

[27:49] **French Pressure on Aquitaine**

[事实] Philip IV uses Edward II's weakness as an opportunity to investigate disorder in Aquitaine.

[事实] The hosts describe Philip's claims of violence and anarchy in Aquitaine as exaggerated.

[事实] In 1324, the French invade Aquitaine and overrun most of it except Bordeaux.

[事实] Edward II is told that unless he performs homage, the duchy will be forfeited.

[29:03] **Isabella, Mortimer, and the Fall of Edward II**

[事实] Isabella goes to Paris to negotiate and becomes involved with Roger Mortimer, an English exile who also hates Edward II.

[事实] Edward II sends his son Edward to Paris, where Isabella and Mortimer keep him and refuse to send him back.

[事实] Isabella and Mortimer invade England, overthrow Edward II, and rule through the young Edward III as a puppet.

[事实] Edward II abdicates and is later said to have been murdered at Berkeley Castle, though the hosts note disputed theories about his death.

[31:02] **Young Edward III Takes Power**

[事实] Edward III is described as energetic, tournament-obsessed, and surrounded by a group of close male friends.

[事实] Mortimer signs a treaty with the Scots in 1328 recognizing Scottish independence, which becomes known in England as the “shameful peace.”

[事实] In 1330, Edward III and his friends stage a coup, overthrow Mortimer, and have him executed.

[事实] The friends who help Edward III will play important roles in his reign.

[33:34] **The Capetian Succession Crisis**

[事实] After Philip IV dies, his three sons succeed one another in rapid succession and none leaves a surviving son.

[事实] Louis X's posthumous son John I reigns for only five days, the shortest reign in French history.

[事实] Joan, Louis X's young daughter, is passed over in favor of her uncle Philip V.

[事实] With Charles IV's death, the direct Capetian male line ends.

[35:49] **Edward III's Claim and the Valois Choice**

[事实] Edward III has a claim to the French throne through his mother Isabella, daughter of Philip IV.

[事实] The French reject Edward's claim because it comes through a woman, although the hosts say this was not yet settled law.

[事实] Philip of Valois becomes Philip VI after Charles IV's widow gives birth to a daughter rather than a son.

[事实] Philip VI is described through Jonathan Sumption's judgment as an extremely poor soldier.

[39:03] **Edward Initially Accepts Philip VI**

[事实] The hosts say Edward III does not immediately go to war over the French succession.

[事实] Edward turns down Flemish offers to recognize him as king of France.

[事实] Edward is focused on stabilizing England and is not yet ready for a massive war with France.

[事实] Philip VI nevertheless demands homage from Edward for his French lands.

[40:44] **Scotland and Gascony as the Two Sore Points**

[事实] The hosts identify Scotland and Gascony as the two major unresolved problems in Anglo-French relations.

[事实] Scotland gives French kings a way to pressure England through the Franco-Scottish alliance.

[事实] Gascony remains vulnerable because the French king can threaten confiscation.

[推测] Edward III's problem is strategic: defending claims in Scotland risks provoking France against Gascony.

[41:59] **Hallidon Hill and the Breakdown with France**

[事实] Edward III supports the Balliol cause in Scotland and responds when Scottish forces besiege Berwick.

[事实] At Hallidon Hill in 1333, Edward wins a crushing victory over the Scots.

[事实] David II of Scotland flees to France, which forces Philip VI to support him under the Scottish-French agreement.

[事实] Philip tells English ambassadors there will be no Anglo-French treaty unless Scotland is included and Scottish rights are upheld.

[43:49] **Invasion Scare and Confiscation of Aquitaine**

[事实] By 1336, England is experiencing a major invasion scare and preparing defenses.

[事实] The French king is planning an invasion of England through Scotland, though the hosts describe the plan as complicated and abortive.

[事实] In May 1337, Philip declares the confiscation of the Duchy of Aquitaine.

[事实] The hosts describe this confiscation as effectively a declaration of war.

[44:49] **Edward's Manifesto and National Mobilization**

[事实] Edward III goes to Parliament and secures the backing of the peers of England.

[事实] On 28 August 1337, Edward issues a public manifesto accusing Philip of supporting the Scots, backing raids on the English coast, and unlawfully stripping him of Aquitaine.

[事实] The hosts stress that Edward frames the conflict not just as a personal dynastic dispute but as a cause involving England more broadly.

[推测] The manifesto suggests Edward understands the political value of linking royal rights to English identity.

[45:51] **The Longbow and Taking the War to France**

[事实] Edward is said to have a new military advantage: the longbow, already used devastatingly at Hallidon Hill.

[事实] The hosts save detailed discussion of the longbow for the next episode.

[事实] Edward's strategy is to take the war to France, clear French control of the Channel, and establish footholds in France.

[事实] Most of Europe expects Edward to lose and is asking how he can avoid invasion or the conquest of Gascony.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode's main value is that it treats the Hundred Years' War as more than a simple dynastic quarrel. It connects succession law, feudal homage, trade, national identity, Scotland, Gascony, and military change into a coherent explanation of why war became likely.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the contrast between romance and catastrophe: the hosts enjoy the chivalric glamour of knights and longbows, but repeatedly return to the war's long-term violence and instability. The conversational jokes make complex dynastic material easier to follow, though the many Philips, Edwards, Joans, and territorial claims still demand attention.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners who want narrative history with personality and are comfortable with modern analogies. It may be less satisfying for listeners seeking a tightly formal academic account, because the style relies heavily on banter, comparisons, and selective scene-setting.
