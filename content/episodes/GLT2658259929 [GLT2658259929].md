# Napoleon Becomes French: Toulon, Grapeshot, and Josephine

## 概览

This episode follows Napoleon Bonaparte from the collapse of his Corsican position to his emergence as a French revolutionary military figure. The hosts frame the story around his gradual abandonment of Corsican identity and his commitment to France, helped by the universalist language of the Revolution.

The discussion moves through his disastrous Corsican entanglements, his family’s flight to France, his Jacobin self-presentation, and his breakthrough at the siege of Toulon. Napoleon is presented as ambitious, energetic, strategically gifted, physically brave, and already drawn to order, authority, and violence as political tools.

The second half focuses on post-Terror Paris, Paul Barras, the Vendémiaire uprising, and Napoleon’s use of artillery against the Parisian mob. It ends with Josephine: her turbulent past, Napoleon’s obsessive attraction to her, their flawed wedding, and his departure to command the Army of Italy under the newly French spelling “Bonaparte.”

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Napoleon’s Letter to Josephine and the Episode’s Frame**

[事实] The episode opens with a passionate letter Napoleon wrote to Josephine on 17 July 1796, complaining that she wrote too little and calling her “adorable Josephine.”
[事实] The hosts say the episode will later return to Napoleon’s relationship with Josephine.
[事实] They frame the episode as the story of Napoleon jettisoning his Corsican identity and committing himself to France.
[推测] The opening letter is used to connect Napoleon’s emotional life with the broader story of his self-fashioning as a French figure.

[02:21] **Corsica in Chaos**

[事实] In autumn 1792 Napoleon returned to Corsica while France was moving toward republic, terror, factional conflict, and war with its neighbors.
[事实] Corsica was described as being in greater chaos than before, with faction fighting, lynchings, French troops causing trouble, and Napoleon himself briefly captured.
[事实] A French attempt to invade Sardinia became Napoleon’s first experience of armed combat and ended in humiliation after about ten days.
[推测] The Corsican violence reinforced Napoleon’s later hostility to disorder and his attraction to centralized authority.

[04:15] **Break with Paoli and Flight from Corsica**

[事实] Pasquale Paoli, once a father figure to Napoleon, turned against the Bonapartes after the failed Sardinian expedition.
[事实] Napoleon’s brother Lucien, who had Jacobin sympathies and changed his name to Brutus, sent a denunciation of Paoli to the National Convention in Paris.
[事实] Napoleon was arrested, freed, pursued, and eventually sheltered outside Ajaccio before his family was denounced and forced to flee Corsica.
[事实] The Bonaparte family left the island by boat for France after roughly two and a half centuries of family presence there.

[07:23] **Corsican Identity and French Universalism**

[事实] Napoleon visited Corsica only once more in his life, when returning from Egypt in 1799.
[事实] The hosts discuss him as an outsider who came to lead an expansionist imperial nation, comparing this pattern to other historical examples.
[事实] They argue that French revolutionary universalism allowed Napoleon to present becoming French as also becoming a citizen of the world.
[推测] This ideological universalism helped him psychologically convert a personal break with Corsica into a larger historical mission.

[09:30] **Napoleon as a Jacobin**

[事实] After arriving in France, Napoleon wrote a strongly Jacobin tract called *Le Souper de Beaucaire*.
[事实] The tract is described as a political manifesto emphasizing strong government, bloodshed, and a grim view of human nature.
[事实] The hosts connect its tone to the atmosphere of the Terror and to Napoleon’s earlier adolescent intensity.
[推测] Napoleon’s Jacobin writing helped mark him as politically reliable at a time when ideological credentials mattered for advancement.

[11:24] **France at War and Toulon**

[事实] Britain and France were at war, and French royalists were collaborating with the Royal Navy.
[事实] Royalists and the British occupied Toulon, including its naval dockyards, which effectively damaged French naval power.
[事实] The Convention sent an army to recapture Toulon, and Napoleon became attached to it as an artillery officer.
[事实] After the artillery commander was wounded, Napoleon was promoted to replace him.

[13:18] **Opportunity Through Revolution**

[事实] The hosts emphasize that the Revolution cleared the way for young officers because many older leaders had defected, rebelled, or been executed.
[事实] Some doubted Napoleon’s experience, but others accepted him because his Jacobin pamphlet showed he was politically “one of us.”
[事实] Napoleon distinguished himself at Toulon through strategy, energy, attention to detail, and artillery organization.
[推测] Toulon gave Napoleon the first major setting in which his talent and ideological usefulness aligned.

[14:08] **Napoleon’s Performance at Toulon**

[事实] Napoleon recognized that taking the heights commanding the harbor would force the Royal Navy to retreat.
[事实] He sourced large quantities of artillery from across southern France and concentrated firepower around Toulon.
[事实] He showed physical bravery, including taking over a cannon after an artilleryman was killed.
[事实] The hosts say he contracted scabies from the dead artilleryman’s gloves and suffered from it for years.
[事实] He impressed future supporters including Junot, Marmont, and Augustin Robespierre.

[17:37] **Victory and Atrocity at Toulon**

[事实] By December 1793 Napoleon’s strategy had worked: the commanding heights were seized and Admiral Hood had to retreat.
[事实] Napoleon later remembered the explosions and burning ships at Toulon as a spectacular scene of flames, smoke, and fireworks.
[事实] After Toulon fell, the Revolutionary Army committed atrocities, and the hosts say thousands were raped, killed, or mutilated.
[事实] Napoleon later claimed he was revolted by the atrocities.
[推测] The hosts suggest he may have been more shocked by disorder than by violence itself.

[20:17] **Promotion and the Robespierre Risk**

[事实] After Toulon, Napoleon was promoted to brigadier general at age 24.
[事实] He drew up plans for an Italian campaign that would pass through Italy toward Vienna and knock Austria out of the war.
[事实] Augustin Robespierre invited Napoleon to Paris, but Napoleon did not go.
[推测] Had Napoleon gone to Paris with Augustin Robespierre, he might have been executed after the Thermidorian coup.

[21:29] **Thermidor and the Search for a New Patron**

[事实] In August 1794 Napoleon was in Nice when he learned that Augustin Robespierre had been guillotined.
[事实] Napoleon was briefly arrested after the Thermidorian reaction, though the hosts say there was probably no serious threat to him.
[事实] Junot proposed breaking him out, but Napoleon refused and expected the matter to resolve.
[事实] The hosts introduce Paul Barras as the new patron who would become crucial to Napoleon’s rise.

[23:27] **Post-Terror Paris and Desiree Clary**

[事实] After the break, the hosts describe Paris during the Thermidorian reaction as a place of partying, sex, money, and strange rituals linked to the guillotine.
[事实] Napoleon spent time in southern France and became engaged to Eugenie Desiree Clary.
[事实] He wrote controlling letters to Desiree about music and reading.
[事实] Desiree later married Bernadotte and became queen of Sweden.

[26:28] **Paul Barras and Napoleon Adrift**

[事实] Paul Barras had fought the British in India, served in the Convention, helped bring down Robespierre, and became a leading figure in the Directory.
[事实] Barras is described as corrupt, cynical, rich, and extremely well connected.
[事实] Napoleon was technically posted to the Vendee but did not want to fight fellow Frenchmen.
[事实] During this uncertain period he wrote a romantic novel and considered possibilities such as Constantinople, India, or the East India Company.

[28:27] **The Crisis of Vendémiaire**

[事实] In summer 1795 Paris faced uncertainty over a new constitution and the possibility of royalist revival.
[事实] Louis XVI had been executed, his son had died, and Louis XVIII was outside France.
[事实] On 3 October 1795 Barras summoned Napoleon and asked if he could rely on him.
[事实] Napoleon agreed, then returned to Paris and went to the theater before hearing that the Le Pelletier section had revolted.

[30:09] **Napoleon Chosen Against the Mob**

[事实] General Menou was sent to suppress the revolt but hesitated after entering narrow streets and realizing his troops were outnumbered.
[事实] The hosts contrast Menou’s reluctance with Napoleon’s lack of sentimental attachment to the Paris mob.
[事实] Napoleon’s own version made him sound like a man of destiny demanding total authority.
[事实] Barras’s version was that Barras recommended “a little Corsican officer” who would not be squeamish.
[推测] The hosts find Barras’s version more plausible because Barras was then much better known than Napoleon.

[33:03] **The Whiff of Grapeshot**

[事实] On 5 October 1795 Napoleon had already positioned guns around key government sites including the Tuileries, bridges, and the Place de la Concorde.
[事实] He had about 5,000 men and was outnumbered by the mob and National Guardsmen who joined it.
[事实] After hours of waiting and rain, Napoleon ordered cannon fire using grapeshot.
[事实] The hosts explain grapeshot as hundreds of musket balls packed into a case and fired from a cannon, spreading violently when fired.
[事实] The number killed is uncertain, but the hosts say hundreds and possibly up to a thousand.

[35:03] **Order, Power, and the End of Mob Politics**

[事实] The firing dispersed the mob, ended the royalist uprising, and secured the Directory’s control.
[事实] Barras had Napoleon appointed commander of the Army of the Interior, effectively making him military governor of Paris.
[事实] Napoleon closed clubs, reformed the National Guard, found posts for allies and family, and became a public celebrity.
[事实] He also moved to confiscate privately held weapons.
[推测] The episode presents this as a turning point in which the state reasserted a monopoly of violence after years of revolutionary chaos.

[37:04] **The Sword Story and Josephine’s Entrance**

[事实] A story says a 14-year-old boy asked Napoleon to keep his guillotined father’s sword during the disarmament campaign.
[事实] The hosts note that different versions exist and that the story may be invented.
[事实] The boy was Eugene de Beauharnais, son of Marie-Joseph Rose de Beauharnais, better known as Josephine.
[事实] Josephine grew up connected to a plantation family in Martinique and had lost her teeth, which the hosts connect to sugarcane.

[38:36] **Josephine’s Past**

[事实] Josephine had been married to Alexandre de Beauharnais, described as abusive, jealous, unfaithful, and militarily inept.
[事实] Alexandre was executed after being accused of treason following the loss of Mainz.
[事实] Josephine was imprisoned in Les Carmes, where prisoners lived amid terrifying conditions and some women tried to become pregnant to avoid execution.
[事实] She had affairs with General Lazare Hoche and later with Paul Barras.
[推测] Andrew Roberts’s suggestion that she may have suffered post-traumatic stress disorder is presented as an interpretation of her prison experience.

[40:12] **Napoleon’s Obsession with Josephine**

[事实] Barras described Josephine as highly accomplished in the arts of the courtesan.
[事实] Napoleon was gauche and inexperienced with women, while Josephine was socially and sexually sophisticated.
[事实] The hosts discuss unexplained references to “zigzags,” Napoleon’s explicit letters, his attraction to her not washing, and his obsessive attachment.
[事实] Josephine was not initially keen on Napoleon and called him “Puss in Boots” and from a family of beggars.
[推测] Barras may have encouraged the relationship to get rid of Josephine, bind Napoleon to him, and distract Napoleon from constant political and military demands.

[42:49] **Josephine’s Children and the Italian Plan**

[事实] Napoleon was kind to Josephine’s children, Eugene and Hortense, telling them ghost stories and playing with them.
[事实] At the same time, he was focused on a campaign against Austria through Italy and toward Vienna.
[事实] He argued for a two-pronged attack, with one army through Germany and his army through Italy.
[事实] He read extensively about Italy, including maps and books, and shut himself away for a week preparing.

[44:47] **Marriage, Destiny, and Bonaparte**

[事实] Napoleon and Josephine married before he left for Italy, though she was ambivalent and he arrived two hours late.
[事实] Both lied about their ages at the wedding, and the official who performed it was not legally qualified after the original official went home.
[事实] Napoleon gave Josephine a medallion inscribed “To destiny.”
[事实] On the wedding night, Josephine’s pet pug bit Napoleon and kept him from getting into bed.
[事实] After leaving for the Army of Italy, Napoleon first signed his name “Bonaparte” rather than “Buonaparte” in a letter to Josephine.
[推测] The hosts treat this spelling change as the symbolic moment when Napoleon fully becomes French.

[47:19] **Departure for Italy and What Comes Next**

[事实] Napoleon’s Italian expedition is linked to David’s later image of him as a Hannibal-like figure crossing the Alps.
[事实] The hosts say this is the point where Napoleon becomes the war hero and embodiment of France carrying the flag of the Revolution.
[事实] When told he was too young to command the Army of Italy, Napoleon said he would be old when he returned.
[事实] The hosts say they will return to Napoleon later and also plan a larger series on the French Revolution.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is that it connects Napoleon’s early military rise with identity, ideology, patronage, and emotional life rather than treating him simply as a battlefield prodigy. Toulon, Vendémiaire, Barras, and Josephine are presented as linked steps in the making of “Bonaparte.”

[推测] A major highlight is the balance between narrative detail and interpretation: the hosts keep returning to how Corsica shaped Napoleon’s suspicion of disorder, how revolutionary universalism helped him become French, and how violence became a route to political legitimacy.

[推测] The main limitation is that the tone is conversational and often comic, especially around Josephine and sexual anecdotes, so listeners wanting a sober military or archival account may find parts of it deliberately irreverent.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in Napoleon’s formation, the French Revolution’s political culture, and the relationship between private ambition and public violence.
