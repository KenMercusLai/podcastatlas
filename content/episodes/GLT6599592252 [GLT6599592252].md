# Lorenzo the Magnificent and the Pazzi Conspiracy

## 概览

This episode examines Lorenzo de' Medici behind the later mythology of “the Magnificent,” presenting him as both the glamorous embodiment of Renaissance Florence and a risky young ruler whose taste for display, patronage, and princely status strained the Medici inheritance.

The central narrative is the Pazzi Conspiracy of 1478: a plot involving the Pazzi banking family, papal allies, mercenaries, and priests to murder Lorenzo and his brother Giuliano during high mass in Florence Cathedral.

The discussion builds from Medici family politics and banking power to the violent realities beneath Renaissance culture. Its key conclusion is that Medici Florence was not only art, humanism, and patronage, but also debt, intimidation, factional rivalry, assassination, and public revenge.

## 分段落总结

[00:26] **Assassin's Creed and the Pazzi Conspiracy**

[事实] The episode opens with a dramatic speech attributed in the framing to Antonio Maffei, then explains that the speech is actually from the video game *Assassin's Creed 2*.

[事实] The hosts identify the historical focus as the Pazzi Conspiracy, a plot to murder Lorenzo de' Medici during high mass in Florence Cathedral on 26 April 1478.

[推测] The video-game reference is used to show how Lorenzo and the conspiracy still carry a strong popular afterlife beyond academic history.

[03:36] **Lorenzo's Family and Formation**

[事实] Lorenzo was born in Florence on 1 January 1449, the son of Piero de' Medici and Lucrezia Tornabuoni.

[事实] Lucrezia is described as a wealthy, accomplished, religious woman who wrote poetry, hymns, and verse translations of the Bible.

[事实] Lorenzo received a humanist education with Latin, Aristotle, and Plato, and by his mid-teens was already being sent on diplomatic missions.

[事实] At seventeen, Lorenzo went to Rome to meet Pope Paul II over the Medici concession for alum mines at Tolfa, a resource important to the family business.

[05:57] **Piero the Gouty and Medici Continuity**

[事实] After Cosimo de' Medici died, Lorenzo's father Piero became head of the family network, though Medici dominance was not formally hereditary.

[事实] Piero discovered that the Medici bank was overextended and called in loans, including from major clients and members of the Florentine elite.

[事实] The loan recall contributed to bankruptcies, a local financial crash, and a split in the Medici party.

[事实] In 1466, Piero brought in troops from Milan and used armed pressure around the Signoria elections to defeat opposition and consolidate power.

[推测] Piero's short rule helped normalize Medici family succession inside a republic that officially did not have a prince.

[10:04] **Art, Marriage, and Aristocratic Ambition**

[事实] Piero continued Medici artistic patronage, supported Donatello, promoted Sandro Botticelli, and had Botticelli live in the Palazzo Medici.

[事实] Lorenzo's parents sought a bride for him while he was still young by Florentine standards, indicating that the family wanted to behave like European princely houses.

[事实] Lorenzo married Clarice Orsini, a sixteen-year-old Roman heiress from a noble family with estates near Naples.

[事实] The hosts say the marriage was not a great love affair, though Lorenzo and Clarice had ten children.

[13:29] **Lorenzo's Persona and Public Display**

[事实] Lorenzo is described as physically unconventional, with long dark hair, a long broken-looking nose, a jutting jaw, and a high, nasal voice.

[事实] He loved sport, music, poems, jokes, architectural drawing, parties, and competitions, though he was said to react badly when he lost.

[事实] A lavish tournament in 1469 celebrated his betrothal to Clarice and cost 10,000 ducats.

[事实] His wedding feast included large quantities of wine and elaborate Renaissance dishes, but heavy rain on the wedding morning was treated as a bad omen.

[18:09] **Lorenzo Takes Power**

[事实] Piero died on 2 December 1469 at a country villa above Florence.

[事实] Medici loyalists met before Piero's funeral and agreed that Lorenzo should assume leadership, despite his youth.

[事实] Lorenzo later wrote that he accepted power to protect his friends and possessions, adding that rich men in Florence fared badly if they did not control the state.

[事实] Before fully assuming power, Lorenzo secured support from the Duke of Milan.

[21:29] **The Young Ruler's Weaknesses**

[事实] After the break, the hosts emphasize that Lorenzo was only twenty when he became head of the Medici family and effective master of Florence.

[事实] Lorenzo is described as inexperienced, impulsive, and uninterested in banking.

[事实] The hosts argue that this lack of interest in the family business became a problem for Lorenzo and later Medici generations.

[推测] The episode frames Lorenzo as a classic heir to a business empire: culturally talented and socially brilliant, but less disciplined than the founders who built the fortune.

[23:01] **Extravagance and the Meaning of “Magnificent”**

[事实] Lorenzo staged feasts, dances, horse races, mock battles, and animal spectacles in Florence.

[事实] The hosts explain that “Il Magnifico” was not originally proof of personal greatness, but a standard honorific for rich merchants and heads of families.

[事实] Lorenzo borrowed heavily, including 61,000 florins from a cousin and 10,000 florins from the Duke of Milan.

[事实] Historian Mary Hollingsworth is cited as arguing that Lorenzo's greed and inexperience damaged the Medici brand.

[推测] The hosts present a tension between Lorenzo's financial recklessness and his long-term success in projecting princely status.

[27:00] **Violence Beneath Renaissance Glamour**

[事实] Lorenzo's chief ally, Galeazzo Maria Sforza of Milan, is described as both a patron of the arts and a violent, sadistic ruler.

[事实] In 1472, Lorenzo became involved in a dispute over alum at Volterra, whose cheaper supply threatened Medici-controlled alum interests near Rome.

[事实] Florentine troops and mercenaries attacked Volterra, looted the town, killed hundreds, and committed mass rape.

[事实] Lorenzo later expressed regret, but the hosts say contemporaries may have seen this as disingenuous.

[推测] The Volterra episode is used to undermine a purely polished image of Lorenzo as a benign Renaissance patron.

[29:28] **The Pazzi and Pope Sixtus IV**

[事实] The Pazzi were an older and more prestigious Florentine banking family with offices in Florence, Rome, Avignon, Valencia, and elsewhere.

[事实] Jacopo de' Pazzi is described as both a miser and a gambler, competitive and watchful of Lorenzo.

[事实] Pope Sixtus IV first confirmed the Medici as papal bankers, then fell out with Lorenzo over the purchase of Imola for his nephew Girolamo Riario.

[事实] When Lorenzo refused to finance the purchase, the Pazzi bank supplied the money, and Sixtus later transferred the papal account from the Medici to the Pazzi.

[事实] Sixtus also appointed Francesco Salviati, a Pazzi cousin, as Archbishop of Pisa, which Lorenzo saw as a major insult.

[34:17] **The Conspiracy Forms**

[事实] After Galeazzo Maria Sforza was assassinated on Boxing Day 1476, Lorenzo lost a major ally as Milan entered a succession crisis.

[事实] Three men met secretly in Rome: Girolamo Riario, Archbishop Francesco Salviati, and Francesco de' Pazzi.

[事实] They discussed how to remove Lorenzo de' Medici.

[事实] Francesco de' Pazzi contacted the mercenary commander Montesecco, who insisted on papal approval before acting.

[事实] In a meeting, Pope Sixtus said he did not want killing but strongly wanted political change in Florence, which Montesecco understood as permission.

[36:11] **Jacopo's Reluctant Approval**

[事实] Jacopo de' Pazzi initially warned that the plotters would “break their necks” and did not want to hear more.

[事实] Montesecco told him that the pope supported the plan and that the plotters had money and military force.

[事实] Jacopo then agreed, but insisted that both Lorenzo and Giuliano had to be killed at the same time.

[推测] Jacopo's condition shows that the conspirators understood the Medici network would survive if only one brother died.

[37:46] **The Cathedral Plan**

[事实] The first plan to strike at a banquet failed because Giuliano had an eye infection and might not attend.

[事实] The conspirators then chose high mass at Florence Cathedral on Sunday, 26 April 1478, because both Medici brothers were expected to be present with Cardinal Raffaele Riario.

[事实] Francesco de' Pazzi and Baroncelli were assigned to kill Giuliano.

[事实] Montesecco refused to kill Lorenzo inside the cathedral, so two priests, Bagnoni and Maffei, were assigned to attack him.

[事实] The signal was the sacristy bell at the elevation of the host, when the congregation would bow their heads.

[41:13] **The Attack at High Mass**

[事实] Lorenzo walked from the Medici palace to the cathedral with Cardinal Riario and Archbishop Salviati, after which Salviati excused himself.

[事实] Giuliano initially considered skipping mass, but Francesco de' Pazzi and Baroncelli fetched him and checked that he was not wearing armor.

[事实] When the bell rang, Maffei struck at Lorenzo, but Lorenzo turned, was wounded in the neck, and escaped with help from friends.

[事实] Giuliano was attacked more successfully; Baroncelli and Francesco de' Pazzi stabbed him repeatedly, leaving him dead on the cathedral floor.

[事实] Francesco Norri, a Medici bank partner and friend of Lorenzo, blocked the attackers and was killed, giving Lorenzo time to reach the sacristy doors.

[46:11] **The Failed Coup and Florentine Revenge**

[事实] Lorenzo escaped from the cathedral and appeared at the Medici palace, wounded but alive, calling for resistance against the conspirators.

[事实] Archbishop Salviati tried to seize the Palazzo della Signoria with mercenaries, but Florentine officials and citizens fought back.

[事实] Crowds supporting the Medici overwhelmed the plotters, killed mercenaries, and displayed severed heads in the city.

[事实] Salviati and Francesco de' Pazzi were hanged from windows of the Palazzo della Signoria.

[事实] Around eighty people, including some who were innocent, were lynched before Lorenzo calmed the violence.

[48:23] **Punishment of the Plotters**

[事实] Maffei and the other priest were captured, castrated, and then executed.

[事实] Montesecco was tortured, interrogated, and beheaded; his testimony is presented as a source for details of the conspiracy.

[事实] Baroncelli escaped to Constantinople, was recognized, extradited to Florence, and beheaded.

[事实] Jacopo de' Pazzi fled, was captured, tortured, hanged, and his body was later abused by Florentine crowds.

[事实] The Pazzi family were banished from Florence, their property was confiscated, their dolphin symbol was erased, and Botticelli painted an execution mural.

[50:43] **Papal Retaliation and War**

[事实] Pope Sixtus IV reacted by excommunicating Lorenzo and the senior government of Florence for killing priests and an archbishop.

[事实] At the end of June 1478, Sixtus placed Florence under interdict.

[事实] The Medici bank was expelled from Rome and Naples, its assets were seized, and Alfonso, Duke of Calabria, marched from Naples toward Florence with an army.

[事实] The war damaged the Florentine economy, created food shortages, and led to bread riots.

[推测] The failed assassination did not end the crisis; it transformed a factional conspiracy into a wider Italian political and military confrontation.

[53:31] **Lorenzo's Gamble**

[事实] By late summer 1479, Florence was exhausted and under pressure as the Neapolitan army advanced slowly through central Italy.

[事实] On 5 December 1479, Lorenzo told the Signoria that he would sail to Naples and surrender himself into enemy hands to seek peace.

[事实] Lorenzo presented himself as the chief target of Florence's enemies and said the war had begun with the blood of his brother and himself.

[事实] The episode ends on this cliffhanger, directing listeners to the next installment for the outcome.

[推测] The ending casts Lorenzo's journey to Naples as both a political gamble and a self-fashioned act of sacrifice.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode's main value is that it makes Renaissance Florence feel politically dangerous rather than merely beautiful. It connects art patronage, banking, family honor, papal power, and street violence into one coherent story.

[推测] Its strongest section is the reconstruction of the Pazzi Conspiracy itself, especially the movement from secret planning to the botched cathedral attack and the rapid collapse of the coup.

[事实] The episode does not resolve what happens after Lorenzo goes to Naples, because it ends by setting up the next installment.
