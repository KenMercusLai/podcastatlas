# Dr. Stacy Sims on Female-Specific Training, Nutrition, Hormones, and Longevity

## 概览

This episode centers on how women’s training and nutrition needs differ from men’s across the menstrual cycle, perimenopause, menopause, pregnancy, and aging. Dr. Stacy Sims argues that generic fitness advice often misses female-specific physiology, especially around fasting, cortisol, hormone signaling, protein needs, and recovery timing.

A major thread is that active women generally should avoid fasted training and prolonged morning fasting, because Sims says these can increase cortisol, down-regulate kisspeptin signaling, impair thyroid and luteinizing hormone patterns, and reduce training intensity. Instead, she recommends fueling around training with protein, carbohydrate when needed, and timely post-workout nutrition.

The training message is similarly direct: women, especially in midlife and beyond, should prioritize resistance training, true high-intensity or sprint interval work, jump training for bone, and adequate protein. Sims repeatedly contrasts this with moderate-intensity cardio classes that feel hard but may not provide the stress-response profile needed for strength, metabolic health, body composition, and longevity.

The conversation also covers menstrual-cycle tracking, oral contraceptives, IUDs, menstrual blood diagnostics, PCOS, iron testing, caffeine, adaptogens, cold and heat exposure, sauna timing, supplements, sleep, and pregnancy. The recurring conclusion is that women should understand their own physiology and apply external stressors strategically rather than following one-size-fits-all fitness culture.

[00:00] **Episode scope and guest background**

[事实] Andrew Huberman introduces Dr. Stacy Sims as an exercise physiologist, nutrition scientist, and expert on training and nutrition specifically for women.
[事实] Sims has authored more than 100 peer-reviewed studies on exercise physiology and has worked with Stanford and professional athletic teams.
[事实] The episode is framed around hormones, menstrual cycle, perimenopause, menopause, female-specific nutrition, fasted training, body composition, fitness, and longevity.
[推测] The episode’s core aim is to replace generalized training advice with female-specific protocols grounded in physiology.

[07:50] **Intermittent fasting, time-restricted eating, and fasted training**

[事实] Sims distinguishes intermittent fasting from time-restricted eating and says active women generally do worse with prolonged fasting unless there are specific conditions such as PCOS, obesity, inactivity, or other metabolic issues.
[事实] She says women have more oxidative muscle fibers and are already metabolically flexible, so fasted training adds stress rather than providing the same benefit often claimed for men.
[事实] Sims says fasted exercise can increase cortisol and prevent women from reaching intensities high enough to trigger desired post-exercise growth hormone and testosterone responses.
[事实] She links low-fuel morning stress to kisspeptin neuron down-regulation, thyroid dysregulation, and altered luteinizing hormone pulse.
[推测] Her position is that the timing and context of fuel matter more for active women than pursuing a long fasting window.

[13:15] **Morning cortisol and circadian fueling**

[事实] Sims says cortisol peaks about half an hour after waking, and not eating during that period can raise baseline sympathetic drive in women.
[事实] She says even a small amount of food that raises blood sugar can signal to the hypothalamus that nutrition is available.
[事实] Huberman clarifies that sympathetic activation refers to arousal and stress physiology, not emotional sympathy.
[推测] Breakfast or a small pre-training intake is presented as a way to reduce “tired but wired” physiology in women.

[17:24] **RPE, reps in reserve, and age-specific lifting**

[事实] Huberman and Sims define reps in reserve: an RPE of eight generally means stopping with about two good-form reps still possible.
[事实] Sims says unfueled women may miss roughly 2-5% of top load in resistance training.
[事实] Sims says women in reproductive years can use normal periodization and train to failure, while perimenopausal and older women should emphasize heavier strength and power work with reps in reserve.
[事实] She says estrogen is a key driver for women’s strength and power, and its decline makes external strength stimuli more important.
[推测] The recommendation shifts from “train hard until tired” toward “train heavy enough to trigger nervous-system adaptation.”

[21:07] **Pre-workout and post-workout nutrition**

[事实] Sims gives her own example of espresso with almond milk and protein powder before early training.
[事实] She cites work suggesting about 15 grams of protein before strength training, and about 30 grams of carbohydrate added before cardiovascular work up to an hour.
[事实] Sims says women should eat a real meal after training, ideally within 45 minutes.
[事实] She recommends about 35 grams of high-quality, leucine-oriented protein post-workout for reproductive-age women and about 40-60 grams for perimenopausal women and beyond.
[事实] She says women’s recovery window is tighter than men’s, with women returning toward metabolic baseline within about 60 minutes while men may have up to three hours.
[推测] The protocol is meant to protect lean mass and keep the body in a building state rather than a catabolic state.

[31:52] **Low energy availability and endocrine risk**

[事实] Sims says training fasted may increase percentage of fat used during the workout but does not necessarily improve total body-fat loss.
[事实] She says the endocrine dysfunction tipping point is about 15 calories per kilogram of fat-free mass for men and about 30 for women.
[事实] Sims says women generally have higher carbohydrate and calorie needs because more reproductive and endocrine functions depend on adequate signaling.
[推测] This section reframes under-fueling as a hormonal and neurological problem, not only a performance or calorie-balance problem.

[34:27] **Women, strength training, and rapid strength gains**

[事实] Huberman notes that resistance training is now more common among women than it was in earlier decades.
[事实] Sims says women often gain strength quickly when they begin lifting because the central nervous system rapidly learns to recruit more muscle fibers.
[事实] She says women usually do not put on bulky muscle quickly, even with higher volume.
[事实] Huberman notes that the temporary “pump” during lifting can show what possible hypertrophy might look like.
[推测] The conversation pushes back against the fear that ordinary resistance training will make most women suddenly become overly muscular.

[39:33] **Training structure across age brackets**

[事实] Sims recommends that women in their 20s first learn movement quality, complex movements, and good mechanics.
[事实] She recommends three to four resistance-training sessions per week, with sprint interval training after one session or up to two HIIT sessions on separate days.
[事实] In the 30s, she recommends paying more attention to compound movements, heavier work, and six-week periodized blocks.
[事实] For women in their 40s who have never lifted, she recommends taking two weeks to four months to learn movement well before loading heavily.
[推测] The framework treats technical preparation as a prerequisite for safely increasing intensity later in life.

[45:09] **Perimenopause as a major physiological inflection point**

[事实] Sims says men tend to age more linearly, while women often experience a sharper transition in the late 40s and early 50s.
[事实] She describes perimenopause as involving more anovulatory cycles, lower progesterone, altered estradiol pulses, body-composition changes, sleep disruption, and soft-tissue injury risk.
[事实] She identifies frozen shoulder and plantar fascia issues as common reasons perimenopausal women seek physical therapy.
[事实] Sims says women should enter midlife with heavy lifting and polarized training habits already established when possible.
[推测] Perimenopause is presented as a period where earlier training habits strongly affect resilience.

[48:48] **Cardio, moderate intensity, and polarized training**

[事实] Sims criticizes programs such as Orange Theory and F45 for often putting midlife women into moderate intensity rather than true high intensity.
[事实] She says moderate-intensity work can raise cortisol without producing a strong enough post-exercise hormonal response to reduce it.
[事实] Sims says true high intensity means one to four minutes at 80% or more, or sprint intervals of 30 seconds or less at maximal effort.
[事实] She says long easy activity can be useful for mental health, but resistance training and true high intensity are the “big rocks” for body composition, metabolic control, insulin sensitivity, brain health, and cortisol regulation.
[推测] The critique is less about cardio itself and more about misplaced reliance on hard-feeling but physiologically middle-zone workouts.

[53:35] **Learning resistance training without a personal trainer**

[事实] Sims suggests beginners can start at home with bodyweight movements or a backpack loaded with cans before progressing.
[事实] She mentions mobility work and online programs where people can submit videos for form feedback.
[事实] She says machines can be a useful intermediate step toward compound movements, especially for beginners.
[事实] Huberman emphasizes adjusting machine settings such as seat height and pins rather than only changing the weight.
[推测] The practical message is that cost and gym intimidation can be reduced by using simple progressions and technology.

[59:10] **Menstrual cycle, ovulation, and training readiness**

[事实] Sims says many studies claiming no menstrual-cycle effect use small samples of eumenorrheic university-age women and measure performance at a single time point.
[事实] She says women can often perform at any point in the cycle, but individual tracking is needed because symptoms and readiness vary.
[事实] She identifies the low-hormone phase from day one of bleeding through ovulation as generally better for handling physical and mental stress.
[事实] She says women commonly have four to five anovulatory cycles per year, making cycle-based generalizations difficult.
[推测] Sims favors personalized pattern tracking over rigid universal cycle-phase programming.

[62:21] **Luteal phase fueling and the 10-minute rule**

[事实] Sims says the luteal phase can involve higher inflammation, reduced carbohydrate access, higher sympathetic drive, and higher core temperature.
[事实] She recommends increasing protein and carbohydrate, especially in the week before the next period, to support training intensity.
[事实] She says heart-rate variability may indicate ovulation but is not a reliable indicator of what the body can do that day.
[事实] Sims recommends a 10-minute rule: start the workout, and if intensity still feels unavailable after 10 minutes, change to recovery work.
[推测] This advice balances discipline with self-regulation rather than forcing every planned session regardless of physiology.

[66:49] **High-intensity training, amenorrhea, and fueling**

[事实] Sims says the idea that high-intensity resistance training harms the menstrual cycle is not true.
[事实] She attributes menstrual dysfunction in hard-training women primarily to inadequate food intake and low energy availability, not the training stimulus itself.
[事实] She says amenorrhea was historically normalized in some sports contexts because it made female athletes appear more like male athletes.
[事实] Sims says a small calorie deficit may be better placed away from training, such as at night, so it does not impair recovery.
[推测] The discussion reframes lost periods as a warning sign rather than a badge of serious training.

[72:21] **Oral contraceptives and athletic adaptation**

[事实] Sims explains that the pill’s placebo week creates a withdrawal bleed, not a true menstrual period.
[事实] She says oral contraceptives down-regulate ovarian function and prevent ovulation, creating a different hormone profile than natural cycling.
[事实] She says effects vary by formulation, including estrogen dose and progestin generation, so broad claims are difficult.
[事实] Sims says oral contraceptives can affect inflammation, oxidative responses, hypertrophy, strength, speed, power, mood, and possibly the amygdala, depending on context.
[事实] She cautions that irregular cycles or heavy bleeding may still need investigation rather than simply being masked by oral contraceptives.
[推测] The segment argues for informed contraception decisions, especially for young athletes, rather than treating the pill as physiologically neutral.

[80:04] **IUDs, menstrual blood, and PCOS**

[事实] Sims says copper and progestin-laced IUDs are often used by tactical athletes because they do not have the same systemic effects on adaptation, inflammation, or mood.
[事实] She says menstrual fluid can provide information about endocrine and endometrial status.
[事实] Sims says menstrual blood may be useful for detecting HPV, PCOS-related proteins, and endometriosis-related markers.
[事实] She says apparent PCOS can sometimes appear after stopping oral contraceptives or after coming out of low energy availability, and may not always represent true PCOS.
[事实] Sims says PCOS appears at high incidence in Olympic-level athletes, partly because higher androgens may support recovery and performance.
[推测] Menstrual blood testing is presented as an emerging diagnostic area rather than a fully settled standard.

[84:10] **Training stress, hormones, iron, and blood testing**

[事实] Sims says specific studies on competitive context and androgen shifts in women are lacking.
[事实] She says stress can increase cortisol, and if the body responds well, women may get a testosterone boost.
[事实] For women concerned about low ferritin, she recommends iron every other day starting on the first day of bleeding for 10 days.
[事实] She says active women may be told ferritin of 20-25 is normal, but she prefers seeing levels around 50.
[事实] For one hormone blood test, she recommends five to seven days before the next period; for a second test, she recommends day two of bleeding.
[推测] The practical emphasis is on timing tests to the cycle so results can be interpreted more meaningfully.

[89:33] **Caffeine, blood sugar, and shisandra**

[事实] Sims says caffeine response is more genetic than sex-specific, with fast metabolizers, slow metabolizers, and non-responders.
[事实] She says perimenopausal women can become more sensitive to caffeine-related blood sugar fluctuations and may feel hypoglycemic, dizzy, lightheaded, sick, or nauseous during workouts.
[事实] Her proposed fix is to eat food with caffeine rather than sip more caffeine through the workout.
[事实] Sims describes shisandra as an adaptogen that may support focus by affecting dopamine, serotonin, and cortisol.
[推测] Shisandra is discussed as a possible caffeine alternative, but the transcript does not establish a detailed evidence standard for its use.

[94:24] **Cold exposure and heat exposure for women**

[事实] Sims recommends deliberate cold exposure mainly for open-water swimmers who need to habituate to cold shock or vagal responses.
[事实] She prefers heat exposure for women and says everyone responds to heat with better adaptations.
[事实] She says ice-cold baths can be too cold for women because severe cold causes strong constriction and shutdown.
[事实] Sims suggests around 16 degrees Celsius, or 55-56 degrees Fahrenheit, as cold enough to produce benefits without excessive constriction.
[事实] She says true sauna heat can improve insulin and glucose control, heat-shock protein expression, cardiovascular responses, and hot-flash regulation.
[推测] Her heat-over-cold preference comes partly from her background in environmental exercise physiology and heat research.

[100:01] **Cold exposure, endometriosis, and safety**

[事实] Sims describes a pilot study in which deliberate cold exposure around ovulation and for 10 days over three cycles attenuated endometriosis symptoms.
[事实] She says endometriosis is an inflammatory disease and cold exposure may help by dampening inflammation.
[事实] Huberman warns not to combine cyclic hyperventilation or breath holds with water exposure because drownings have occurred.
[事实] Sims clarifies that their study used cold water exposure, not Wim Hof breathing.
[推测] Cold exposure for endometriosis is presented as promising but preliminary.

[102:19] **Cold and sauna timing around training**

[事实] Huberman says cold immersion after resistance training, especially within about 4-8 hours, may inhibit strength and hypertrophy gains by attenuating inflammation.
[事实] Sims says heat exposure differs because post-training sauna can extend the training stimulus.
[事实] She recommends up to 30 minutes in a true sauna after training, with slow rehydration.
[事实] Sims says passive dehydration and reduced kidney oxygen can stimulate EPO, red cell production, plasma volume, and blood volume expansion.
[事实] She says this can support performance and may resemble some effects of altitude adaptation.
[推测] The sauna protocol is framed as a performance tool, not only a relaxation habit.

[107:29] **Track stack and high-intensity stress**

[事实] Sims describes a “track stack” using 200 milligrams of caffeine, low-dose baby aspirin, and beta-alanine for significant high-intensity work.
[事实] She distinguishes this from older stacks that used ephedrine, which she notes can be dangerous.
[事实] She says the stack may support top-end effects through caffeine, mild blood thinning from aspirin, and beta-alanine’s role in carnosine and muscle contraction.
[事实] She says recovery matters and advises against stacking two consecutive days of very high-intensity work.
[推测] This is a specialized performance tactic rather than a general beginner recommendation.

[109:36] **Sleep across the menstrual cycle and menopause**

[事实] Sims says women and men can have different optimal sleep temperatures.
[事实] She says mid-luteal to premenstrual phases can involve less slow-wave sleep, longer sleep latency, and more light sleep.
[事实] She connects this period with mood issues through estrogen’s interaction with serotonin.
[事实] In perimenopause, she says hot flashes, night sweats, sympathetic load, and reduced parasympathetic access can worsen insomnia.
[事实] She mentions sleep hygiene, L-theanine, apigenin, rhodiola with theanine, cooler room temperature, and non-sleep deep rest or yoga nidra as possible tools.
[推测] The sleep advice emphasizes adapting the environment and recovery tools to hormonal state rather than assuming one fixed sleep setup will work all month.

[112:53] **Supplements for women**

[事实] Sims names creatine as her number-one supplement for women at any age and discusses brain, mood, and gut-health relevance.
[事实] She recommends three to five grams of creatine monohydrate per day and prefers Creapure because of its production process.
[事实] She says there is no evidence that creatine causes hair loss and attributes many midlife hair-loss complaints to progesterone or progestin changes.
[事实] Sims says vitamin D3 is important for cardiovascular, muscle, brain, and iron-related functions, and suggests roughly 2,000-5,000 IU depending on sun exposure and geography.
[事实] She also discusses protein powder, ashwagandha, tulsi, shisandra, lion’s mane, and reishi, while noting that some adaptogens should be cycled.
[推测] The supplement hierarchy favors foundational items first: creatine, vitamin D, and protein support before more targeted adaptogens.

[120:12] **Pregnancy and training**

[事实] Sims says pregnancy reduces anaerobic capacity on purpose while expanding blood volume, so endurance may remain good but high intensity becomes harder.
[事实] She says current guidance has moved away from a strict heart-rate rule and toward staying as active as possible without injury or trying to make gains.
[事实] She says pregnant women should aim to maintain rather than improve in the weight room or cardio.
[事实] She says stopping all activity out of fear can be worse than staying active within what the body allows.
[事实] Sims is cautious about cold exposure during pregnancy, especially for women at high risk of miscarriage in the first 12-20 weeks.
[事实] She says hot yoga has research suggesting it is not detrimental when done moderately, but distinguishes it from extreme heat such as sauna.
[推测] Pregnancy recommendations are necessarily conservative because Sims says pregnant women are difficult to study ethically.

[126:19] **Training after 50 for health span and lifespan**

[事实] Sims says women over 50 should train for independent living, balance, proprioception, bone health, and strength.
[事实] She recommends 10 minutes of jump training three times per week to improve bone mineral density.
[事实] She says women may lose about one third of bone mass at the onset of menopause without intervention.
[事实] Her key training priorities after 50 are jump training, heavy resistance training, and sprint interval training.
[事实] She recommends about 1-1.1 grams of protein per pound of body weight, or about 2-2.3 grams per kilogram per day.
[推测] For older women, the episode prioritizes power, bone loading, and protein over conventional low-intensity fitness habits.

[129:37] **Training from 20 to 40 and the meaning of true high intensity**

[事实] Sims says women in their 20s to 40s should choose physical activities they actually enjoy.
[事实] She says resistance training remains the big rock, but it does not always need to be heavy; it can include periodized blocks, machines, or Olympic lifting depending on comfort and goals.
[事实] She says high-intensity work supports lactate production and may help expose the brain to lactate in ways relevant to cognitive decline and Alzheimer’s plaque development.
[事实] Sims defines true HIIT as one to four minutes of hard work at 80% or more with variable recovery.
[事实] She defines sprint interval training as 30 seconds or less at maximal effort, with enough recovery, possibly two or three minutes, to repeat true maximal output.
[推测] The episode treats “high intensity” as a precise training stimulus, not simply feeling sweaty or exhausted.

[137:08] **Molecular reasons for strength and sprint work**

[事实] Sims says the fitness world often focuses on aesthetics, hypertrophy, and long slow cardio for fat loss.
[事实] She says strong external stress can produce neural, metabolic, and epigenetic changes in muscle.
[事实] She says high stress can increase GLUT4 gates, supporting acute glucose uptake without insulin.
[事实] She says high-intensity work creates an inflammatory response the body learns to overcome, which becomes important as estrogen declines.
[事实] Sims links high-intensity work to better free fatty acid use, glucose handling, inflammation control, body composition, brain health, and independent aging.
[推测] The deeper rationale is adaptation quality: the workout is chosen for the signal it sends, not for calories burned.

[139:30] **Carbohydrates, fats, fiber, and food quality**

[事实] Sims prefers carbohydrate sources such as rice, oatmeal, sourdough bread, fruit, vegetables, sweet potatoes, yams, sprouted bread, quinoa, and amaranth.
[事实] She says ultra-processed carbohydrates may be best limited, though post-training is a context where glycogen uptake is high.
[事实] She emphasizes gut microbiome diversity, fiber, and colorful fruits and vegetables, especially as hormone shifts affect gut-hormone interactions.
[事实] Sims uses an 80-20 framing: 80% “spot on,” 20% life.
[事实] She prefers most fats from whole-food plant sources such as avocados, nuts, seeds, and olive oil, while also saying dairy saturated-fat fearmongering has been disproven and that butter or full-fat yogurt can have a place.
[推测] Her nutrition approach is non-diet, performance-supportive, and built around food quality rather than restriction.

[143:31] **Final message: listen to the body**

[事实] Sims says that if she could change one thing, she would help women understand their intrinsic selves and listen to their bodies.
[事实] She says women have been surrounded by sociocultural rhetoric and external noise that can disconnect them from body signals.
[事实] She says understanding cycles and recognizing perimenopause as normal gives women tools to apply beneficial external stressors.
[事实] Huberman closes by summarizing the episode as covering female-specific cardiovascular training, resistance training, menstrual-cycle protocols, caffeine, supplements, and actionable science.
[推测] The closing message is that self-knowledge is the foundation for using training and nutrition protocols effectively.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its practical specificity. It gives concrete numbers for protein timing, carbohydrate use, creatine, vitamin D, sauna duration, sprint intervals, resistance-training frequency, and blood-test timing, while repeatedly explaining the physiology behind those recommendations.

[推测] The strongest sections are the critiques of fasted training, moderate-intensity cardio, and one-size-fits-all cycle advice. Sims is careful to separate general patterns from individual variation, especially around ovulation, luteal-phase symptoms, oral contraceptives, and perimenopause.

[推测] The main limitation is that several topics are explicitly described as under-researched, including some oral-contraceptive effects, menstrual blood diagnostics, competitive hormone shifts in women, pregnancy interventions, and cold exposure for endometriosis. The transcript also includes some specialized supplement and performance tactics that listeners should interpret cautiously.

[推测] This episode is best suited for active women, coaches, clinicians, and anyone designing training or nutrition around women’s physiology, especially women in their 30s, 40s, and beyond. It is also useful for men who train with or coach women and want to stop assuming that male-derived protocols automatically transfer.
