# The Standard Case

## 概览

This episode examines how people should reason about novel moral problems when inherited principles no longer fit the circumstances. Malcolm Gladwell introduces casuistry, a Jesuit method that begins with concrete cases rather than abstract rules.

The method identifies familiar “standard cases,” compares the new case with them, and builds a taxonomy around morally relevant similarities and differences. Applied to baseball, it reframes Andy Pettitte’s use of human growth hormone by comparing it with Tommy John’s restorative surgery and Barry Bonds’s transformative drug use.

The episode then moves from sports to the Catholic Church’s treatment of LGBT Catholics. Jesuit priest James Martin argues for examining people’s actual lives, applying church expectations consistently, and accompanying marginalized people rather than responding only with doctrine, platitudes, or outrage.

## 分段落总结

[02:03] **Andy Pettitte’s Troubling Apology**

[事实] A Major League Baseball investigation found that pitcher Andy Pettitte had used performance-enhancing drugs, prompting him to apologize publicly to teams, teammates, and fans.

[事实] Pettitte maintained that he used the drugs to recover from injury and return to work, not to gain an advantage over other players.

[事实] Critics characterized his statement as self-protective because it mixed apology with explanation, self-justification, and minimization.

[推测] The ambiguity of Pettitte’s apology introduces the episode’s central question: whether an unfamiliar case is being judged through an overly simple category of wrongdoing.

[04:40] **Novel Problems and the Turn to Casuistry**

[事实] Gladwell presents the episode as the first installment in a three-part series about making sense of novel problems.

[事实] He introduces casuistry, a problem-solving tradition developed by the Jesuits, as a possible alternative to relying immediately on general principles.

[事实] The Jesuits’ work as missionaries, diplomats, and emissaries exposed them to unfamiliar political, commercial, and moral situations far from Rome.

[事实] James Keenan explains that “casuistry” derives from the Latin *casus* and begins with a narrative of something that happened—a case.

[推测] The Jesuits’ practical responsibilities encouraged a form of moral reasoning adapted to uncertainty and local circumstances.

[09:46] **Why Old Principles Can Fail in New Circumstances**

[事实] The episode contrasts Saint Augustine’s absolute prohibition against lying with the predicament of a Catholic priest whose truthful self-identification in Elizabethan England could lead to death.

[事实] Colonial expansion also produced unprecedented questions about sovereignty, jurisdiction, war, and relations with newly encountered peoples.

[事实] The Jesuit conclusion presented in the episode is that principles arise from past experience and may not resolve situations outside the world that produced them.

[事实] In uncharted situations, casuistry proceeds case by case and “descends into the particulars.”

[11:28] **Maritime Insurance as a Standard Case**

[事实] The medieval Catholic Church prohibited usury because interest-bearing loans often enabled wealthy lenders to exploit poor borrowers.

[事实] Expanding maritime trade created a new question: whether an insurance premium protecting valuable cargo should also be classified as usury.

[事实] Casuistry first identifies agreed-upon standard cases and then asks which of them most closely resembles the disputed case.

[事实] Maritime insurance resembled payment to a ship’s captain more than payment to an exploitative lender: both protected the safe transfer of value from one place to another.

[推测] The example shows how analogy can produce agreement by identifying the morally relevant function of an activity rather than forcing it under an inherited label.

[17:12] **The Case Against Andy Pettitte**

[事实] George Mitchell’s investigation reported widespread use of performance-enhancing substances in Major League Baseball and identified Pettitte as a user.

[事实] Trainer Brian McNamee said he injected Pettitte with human growth hormone two to four times while Pettitte was rehabilitating an elbow injury in 2002.

[事实] Pettitte had previously denied the allegation but admitted his use after the Mitchell Report placed baseball’s drug problem under intense scrutiny.

[事实] The public sequence became familiar: denial, exposure, apology, rejection of the apology, and defensiveness.

[推测] Gladwell treats the use of a genetically engineered hormone for injury recovery as sufficiently novel to require comparison with more settled cases.

[19:58] **Tommy John and Restorative Medicine**

[事实] Pitcher Tommy John tore the ulnar collateral ligament in his elbow in 1974, an injury that ordinarily ended pitching careers at the time.

[事实] Surgeon Frank Jobe reconstructed John’s elbow by grafting a tendon from his forearm onto the damaged ligament.

[事实] John returned to Major League Baseball and continued pitching until age 46; the procedure later became widely known as Tommy John surgery.

[事实] Baseball expert Jonah Keri describes the procedure as restorative: it allows a pitcher to use his existing strength and mechanics rather than creating an entirely new level of ability.

[推测] Tommy John surgery supplies a standard case in which advanced medical intervention is broadly accepted because it restores prior function.

[22:30] **Barry Bonds and Transformative Enhancement**

[事实] Barry Bonds is presented as the contrasting standard case: a player believed to have used performance-enhancing drugs late in his career and whose body and performance changed dramatically.

[事实] At age 37, Bonds produced an extraordinary season and hit 73 home runs after typically hitting between 30 and 40 per year.

[事实] George Will argues that Bonds’s physical transformation created pressure for competitors to accept comparable health risks if they wished to compete with him.

[事实] The resulting taxonomy distinguishes restorative interventions, which return an athlete toward an earlier baseline, from transformative interventions that produce materially new capabilities.

[推测] Within this framework, competitive coercion and the scale of transformation matter more than the mere use of medical technology.

[26:45] **Reclassifying Pettitte’s Conduct**

[事实] Pettitte used human growth hormone during a single period on the disabled list to recover from a specific elbow injury.

[事实] His post-treatment performance was described as nearly identical to his performance before treatment.

[事实] Jonah Keri sees no meaningful difference between Pettitte’s intervention and Tommy John’s surgery, while George Will considers Pettitte’s conduct benign if Pettitte’s account is accurate and the treatment did not endanger competitors.

[事实] Gladwell argues that Pettitte’s claim that he sought recovery rather than an advantage was the central moral distinction, not irrelevant hair-splitting.

[推测] Under the episode’s taxonomy, Pettitte is closer to the restorative Tommy John case than to the transformative Barry Bonds case and therefore may have had little to apologize for.

[29:15] **Reframing Bonds and Moving Beyond Principle**

[事实] Gladwell argues that the problem with Bonds was not simply that he used performance-enhancing drugs, but how extensively he used them and what they enabled him to become.

[事实] Jonah Keri suggests that a more modest late-career adaptation might have preserved Bonds’s reputation as one of baseball’s greatest players without provoking the same reaction.

[事实] Philosopher Stephen Toulmin reportedly served on an ethics commission whose members reached agreement while reasoning case by case but began disagreeing when asked to articulate the principles behind their decisions.

[推测] Casuistry may help people cooperate on difficult judgments even when their religious, philosophical, or ideological foundations remain incompatible.

[34:14] **The Pulse Shooting and a Failure to Respond**

[事实] Jesuit priest James Martin recounts his reaction to the Pulse nightclub shooting in Orlando, where 49 people were killed.

[事实] Martin was disturbed that Catholic authorities did not immediately express solidarity as they had after other mass shootings.

[事实] Drawing on James Keenan, Martin describes sin as a failure to bother to love when a person has the capacity to help.

[事实] Martin concluded that the bishops’ silence represented such a failure and responded by examining the Catholic Church’s relationship with gay people in concrete terms.

[推测] The example shifts casuistry from categorizing conduct to identifying responsibilities toward people who are suffering or marginalized.

[37:07] **LGBT Catholics and Consistent Moral Standards**

[事实] Martin emphasizes that LGBT Catholics are not defined solely by sexuality; their lives also concern Jesus, prayer, God, the sacraments, friendship, and service to the poor.

[事实] He argues that LGBT Catholics already belong to the Church through baptism and possess the same claim to it as priests, bishops, or the pope.

[事实] Martin compares violations of Catholic sexual teaching with other conduct contrary to church teaching, including contraception, in vitro fertilization, and failure to help the poor.

[事实] He asks why LGBT employees may be dismissed for one kind of rule violation while people who violate other important teachings remain welcome.

[推测] The comparison suggests that selective enforcement, rather than doctrine alone, is a central source of injustice toward LGBT Catholics.

[39:15] **Accompaniment Instead of Abstract Condemnation**

[事实] Martin says disappointment with part of the Church does not require leaving it, just as disagreement with one’s family or country does not erase membership.

[事实] He maintains that he is not challenging church doctrine and notes that his Jesuit vows and priestly promises commit him to remain.

[事实] Saint Ignatius instructed Jesuits to console people who were suffering or marginalized during their travels.

[事实] Gladwell argues that consolation requires immersion in the particulars of a person’s life; without that knowledge, one can offer only platitudes or outrage.

[事实] Martin describes his approach as encountering LGBT people as they are and accompanying them, following the example he attributes to Jesus.

[推测] The episode ultimately presents casuistry not merely as an analytical technique but as a discipline of attention, consistency, and humane engagement.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest contribution is its clear explanation of casuistry through memorable comparisons. Maritime insurance, Tommy John surgery, Pettitte’s hormone use, and Bonds’s transformation make an unfamiliar method of moral reasoning concrete and accessible.

Its shift from baseball ethics to the treatment of LGBT Catholics demonstrates that case-based reasoning can address both relatively bounded disputes and morally consequential questions involving belonging, suffering, and institutional consistency. The discussion also shows how analogies can expose double standards that remain hidden at the level of abstract doctrine.

[推测] The method’s principal limitation is that the choice of standard cases and morally relevant similarities can itself be contested. The Pettitte analysis also depends on accepting his stated motive, limited pattern of use, and reported performance effects.

[推测] This episode is especially valuable for listeners interested in ethics, law, religion, public policy, medicine, or any field where technological and social change produces situations that inherited rules did not anticipate.
