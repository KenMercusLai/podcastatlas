# Josh Shapiro on Governance, Trump, Iran, Israel, and Democratic Strategy

## 概览

This episode centers on Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro’s governing record and how he presents “getting stuff done” as a centrist Democratic alternative. The discussion starts with permitting reform, tax cuts, pro-business growth, fraud enforcement, public safety, workforce development, housing, and college alternatives.

The conversation then turns into national politics: the Democratic Party after Biden and Harris, Shapiro’s account of the 2024 vice-presidential process, the 2026 midterms, Trump’s tariffs, Medicaid cuts, corruption concerns, pardons, and the role of Congress as a check on executive power.

The final section focuses on antisemitism, Israel, Netanyahu, Gaza, Iran, and whether U.S. policy is being driven by American interests. Shapiro repeatedly separates criticism of Israeli government policy from antisemitism, while arguing that Trump’s Iran war lacked clear objectives and harmed U.S. security and economic interests.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Guest Introduction and Pennsylvania Framing**

[事实] The host introduces Josh Shapiro as governor of Pennsylvania, emphasizing his popularity, moderate image, “GSD” governing style, crime stance, Medicaid fraud enforcement, data center friendliness, and nuanced Israel position.
[事实] The opening includes light banter about Shapiro’s basketball footage and Philadelphia sports before moving into policy.
[推测] The introduction frames Shapiro as a possible counterexample to the stereotype that Democratic leaders are weak on business and execution.

[01:46] **Pennsylvania’s Pro-Growth Record**

[事实] Shapiro says Pennsylvania is a pro-growth state that wants businesses to come and grow.
[事实] He says Pennsylvania has created more jobs than all but two states, cut taxes seven times, improved permitting, invested in workforce development, and kept unemployment below the national rate for 32 months.
[事实] He identifies education, safe streets, and jobs in local communities as core governing priorities.
[推测] Shapiro is using Pennsylvania’s record to argue that pragmatic Democratic governance can appeal to business leaders and working-class voters at the same time.

[04:33] **Permitting Reform and Faster Government**

[事实] Shapiro says Pennsylvania shifted from being bottom five in permitting speed to becoming a top-five or national model.
[事实] He says Pennsylvania uses a money-back guarantee for permits and has issued 40 million permits with only five refunds for lateness.
[事实] He gives the example of barber licensing, saying it went from 20 days to same-day approval, which directly affects a worker’s income.
[推测] The permitting examples are used to make bureaucracy feel concrete rather than abstract, tying administrative speed to wages, business formation, and trust in government.

[08:03] **Government Trust and Getting to Yes**

[事实] Shapiro says government can be a force for good and should try to “get to yes” for businesses and families seeking services.
[事实] He argues that repeated frustrating encounters with government make people more cynical and create openings for extreme voices.
[推测] His argument links service delivery to democratic stability: better bureaucracy may reduce political alienation.

[10:08] **Fraud, Waste, Abuse, and Medicaid Enforcement**

[事实] The host raises Elon Musk’s DOGE effort and asks about waste, fraud, and abuse in government spending.
[事实] Shapiro says people can debate whether a dollar should be spent, but there should be no disagreement about stopping someone from stealing that dollar.
[事实] He says he prosecuted PPP fraud as attorney general and has continued fraud enforcement as governor, including Medicaid fraud referrals and prosecutions.
[推测] Shapiro is positioning anti-fraud enforcement as compatible with public programs rather than as an argument for dismantling them.

[13:13] **Wealth Taxes and Pennsylvania’s Tax Position**

[事实] The host criticizes wealth taxes and taxes on unrealized gains, saying they push entrepreneurs out of states like California.
[事实] Shapiro says Pennsylvania has cut taxes seven times, has one of the lowest income taxes in the country, and does not have the kind of wealth tax described.
[事实] He says people at the top should pay their fair share, while Pennsylvania should maintain a smart tax environment that supports growth.
[推测] Shapiro avoids endorsing wealth taxes while preserving a Democratic fairness argument, which is consistent with a moderate positioning.

[16:17] **Inequality, Working Tax Credits, and Trump’s Budget**

[事实] Shapiro says a national economy cannot work if it only works for the 1%.
[事实] He says Pennsylvania passed its first Working Pennsylvanians Tax Credit, with about 940,000 people qualifying for a little over $800.
[事实] He criticizes Trump’s budget bill, saying it gives tax cuts to people who do not need them while causing healthcare losses for Pennsylvanians.
[事实] Shapiro says 500,000 Pennsylvanians are expected to lose healthcare, 120,000 have already lost it, and 26 rural hospitals are at risk because of Medicaid cuts.
[推测] This section shows Shapiro’s main economic contrast with Trump: growth and lower taxes are acceptable, but not when paid for by lower-income healthcare cuts.

[20:20] **Biden, Harris, and the Democratic Party After 2024**

[事实] The host asks whether Democrats made a mistake by not running a fast primary after Biden dropped out.
[事实] Shapiro says he told Biden directly that seeking another term was probably not in the party’s or country’s best interest.
[事实] He says that once Biden dropped out with roughly 100-plus days remaining, there were limited opportunities for the kind of primary process the host described.
[事实] Shapiro says Democrats should look forward and have a real debate about what they are for and what they are willing to fight for.
[推测] Shapiro declines to dwell on 2024 process blame and instead shifts toward future governing legitimacy.

[23:30] **Vice-Presidential Selection and Shapiro’s Withdrawal**

[事实] The host suggests Harris did not pick Shapiro because she feared running with a Jewish vice president.
[事实] Shapiro pushes back and says he was grateful to Harris for considering him and for their candid dialogue.
[事实] He says that about 48 hours before Harris picked Tim Walz, he called and made clear he was not interested in the vice-presidential job.
[推测] Shapiro’s answer attempts to close off a narrative that his religion cost him the role, while emphasizing his commitment to Pennsylvania.

[25:01] **2026 Midterms and the Case Against Trump**

[事实] Shapiro says no one should look past the midterms and that 2026 should be a national referendum on Washington’s chaos and corruption.
[事实] He argues Democrats need to show “calm, competent governance” as an alternative to Trump.
[事实] He cites Pennsylvania’s 2,000 additional police officers, community violence prevention investments, 12% lower violent crime, and 42% lower fatal gun violence.
[事实] He says Pennsylvania has invested in public education, with scores up, truancy down, and the state ranked around 10th by Consumer Affairs.
[推测] Shapiro’s 2026 pitch is less ideological than managerial: show results, then contrast them with Trump’s chaos.

[28:31] **Tariffs, Prices, and Economic Pain**

[事实] The host says Trump’s tariffs, ICE actions, and Iran war feel chaotic and asks what alternative Democrats should present.
[事实] Shapiro says Trump chose to push the tariff button and that Pennsylvania has seen coffee up 30%, beef up 19%, orange juice up 9%, and fertilizer up 36%.
[事实] He says gas prices are around $4.15 to $4.16 in Pennsylvania and that the Iran war has spiked gas prices.
[推测] Shapiro uses household goods, farm inputs, and gas prices to make Trump’s foreign and trade policy feel materially costly.

[30:09] **Democratic Platform: Education, Safety, Growth, Freedom**

[事实] Shapiro rejects being asked to answer for every Democrat and says the interview should focus on what he says.
[事实] He names public education, safe communities, a pro-growth economy, and freedom as his governing priorities.
[事实] He says Republicans used to be known as the party of freedom but now restrict books, reproductive choices, parenting, and voting rules.
[推测] Shapiro is trying to reclaim “freedom” as Democratic language while keeping business and public safety inside the same platform.

[33:01] **Housing, College, and Workforce Pathways**

[事实] Shapiro says he asked the legislature for a $1 billion fund to build more housing and repair existing housing.
[事实] He says 50% of Pennsylvania housing was built before 1950 and that targeted repairs can keep people in their homes.
[事实] He says his first executive order removed college degree requirements for most state government jobs, and 60% of state hires now do not have a college degree.
[事实] He says Pennsylvania tripled funding for vocational, technical, CTE, and apprenticeship programs.
[推测] Shapiro’s workforce message is designed to appeal to voters who feel college has become too expensive or too narrowly treated as the only path to success.

[36:52] **Socialist Democrats and the Big Tent**

[事实] The host asks whether the socialist Democratic movement worries Shapiro.
[事实] Shapiro says Democrats need a big tent and a real debate around different ideas.
[事实] He says in governing, he focuses on the three or four things he can agree on with Republican lawmakers rather than only on disagreements.
[推测] Shapiro does not directly attack the socialist wing; instead, he reframes party conflict as coalition-building and legislative negotiation.

[38:12] **Congress, War Powers, Tariffs, and Checks and Balances**

[事实] The host asks about Congress, the Senate, tariffs, war powers, executive orders, and the collapse of bipartisan negotiation.
[事实] Shapiro criticizes congressional leadership, especially Speaker Johnson, as a rubber stamp for Trump.
[事实] He says Congress is abandoning constitutional responsibilities on tariffs and declaring war.
[事实] He argues the founders expected honorable officeholders and functioning checks and balances.
[推测] Shapiro’s institutional critique is that Trump’s power depends not only on Trump, but also on weak legislators who surrender their own authority.

[42:00] **Corruption, Pardons, and Family Self-Dealing**

[事实] Shapiro says he sees extraordinary self-dealing, family members doing well, questionable pardons, and Trump accepting a billion-dollar jet from Qatar as examples of corruption concerns.
[事实] The host agrees pardons are troubling and says pardon power appears abused across recent administrations.
[事实] Shapiro says he was publicly critical of Joe Biden pardoning his son but argues Trump’s pardon use is “next level.”
[事实] Shapiro says he takes pardons and clemencies seriously as governor and believes executives must use that power with morality and transparency.
[推测] The conversation suggests that legal rules alone may not be enough when political norms collapse.

[45:39] **Children of Politicians, Investigations, and Norms**

[事实] The host asks how to address politicians’ children enriching themselves, citing Hunter Biden and raising concerns about Trump’s children while noting no court finding or investigation had established wrongdoing.
[事实] Shapiro says legal processes should follow evidence and law, bringing charges when warranted and not bringing them when unwarranted.
[事实] He says the rule of law should not be used as a tool against political opponents.
[事实] He argues a return to ethical norms must be bipartisan and must be demanded by voters.
[推测] Shapiro’s answer favors enforcement and political accountability over a specific new legal ban on family members’ private-market activity.

[47:15] **Antisemitism and Political Consistency**

[事实] The host raises the firebombing of Shapiro’s home, antisemitism, Jewish students, and Democratic divisions over Israel.
[事实] Shapiro says antisemitism and Israel policy should be treated as two conversations.
[事实] He says antisemitism exists on both the left and the right and should be called out universally.
[事实] He praises people on the right, including Ted Cruz, for calling out antisemitism within their own party, and says he tries to do the same in his party.
[推测] Shapiro is drawing a firm line: no nuance on bigotry, but room for nuance on Middle East policy.

[50:01] **Israel, Netanyahu, Two States, and Gaza**

[事实] Shapiro says he views Israel and the Middle East as an American, not primarily as a Jewish American.
[事实] He says America’s interest is peace and stability in the Middle East.
[事实] He says Netanyahu has led Israel down a dangerous and isolated path, fractured bipartisan American support for Israel, and was the leader when October 7 happened.
[事实] Shapiro supports working toward two states living peacefully side by side, while saying a Palestinian state cannot be led by Hamas.
[推测] Shapiro is distinguishing support for Israel’s existence from support for Netanyahu’s government.

[52:48] **Iran War as a War of Choice**

[事实] Shapiro calls the Iran war a war of choice and says Trump never defined the objectives.
[事实] He says he does not know how the United States gets out of the war and that America’s security and economic interests have not been well served.
[事实] The host asks why Trump did it after promising not to launch another Middle East intervention.
[推测] Shapiro’s core criticism is strategic rather than only partisan: undefined goals make exit strategy and military success impossible.

[54:39] **Broken Promises and Undefined Mission**

[事实] Shapiro says the Iran war is another broken Trump promise, alongside farmers hurt by tariffs, Medicaid cuts, and rising prices.
[事实] He says Rubio first suggested action was needed because Netanyahu would move and force America’s hand, then that explanation was walked back.
[事实] He says the administration gave inconsistent explanations around nuclear capability and regime change.
[事实] He says 13 service members did not come home from a mission the president never defined.
[推测] The segment portrays the Iran war as the clearest example of Shapiro’s broader “chaos” critique.

[57:18] **American Interests and Separating Israel Criticism from Antisemitism**

[事实] Shapiro says America should never be led around by any other nation and should act according to U.S. national security interests.
[事实] The host argues that U.S. support for Netanyahu may be contributing to antisemitism among young people who conflate Israel with Judaism.
[事实] Shapiro warns that suggesting Jews are responsible for Israeli policy invokes the old antisemitic trope of dual loyalty.
[事实] He says people should have room to peacefully protest and criticize Israel, Iran policy, or the administration without crossing into antisemitism.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it gives Shapiro extended time to present himself to a business- and politics-focused audience as a Democratic executive who emphasizes growth, permits, public safety, vocational pathways, and institutional norms.

[推测] The strongest parts are the concrete governing examples: same-day barber licenses, permit refunds, tax credits, workforce programs, housing repairs, and crime statistics. These details make the discussion more useful than a generic campaign interview.

[推测] The main limitation is that many claims are presented as assertions inside a political conversation and are not independently verified in the transcript. The host also often frames questions with strong premises, so some sections are more about political positioning than balanced policy analysis.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in centrist Democratic politics, Pennsylvania governance, 2026 and 2028 positioning, and the intersection of U.S. domestic politics with Israel, antisemitism, and the Iran war.
