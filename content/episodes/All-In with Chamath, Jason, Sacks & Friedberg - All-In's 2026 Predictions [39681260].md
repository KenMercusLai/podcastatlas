# All-In’s 2026 Predictions

## 概览

This episode is a prediction round for 2026, framed around politics, business, markets, geopolitics, AI, media, and the future of the All-In podcast itself. The hosts repeatedly return to one central theme: whether the United States is entering a high-growth, high-volatility period shaped by Trump-era policy, AI productivity, deregulation, and backlash against taxes and government waste.

The opening discussion centers on California’s proposed wealth tax and the broader migration of tech wealth out of the state. From there, the hosts move category by category through winners, losers, best assets, worst assets, contrarian calls, big deals, and anticipated trends.

A recurring divide is whether AI will destroy jobs or expand demand, whether tech is politically aligned with MAGA or exposed to populist anger, and whether Trump’s foreign policy is a new doctrine or a return to interventionism. The tone mixes market calls, political argument, inside-baseball tech commentary, and running jokes among the hosts.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Opening, Texas Moves, and California Wealth Tax**

[事实] Jason opens the show with Chamath, Friedberg, and Sacks, and Sacks says he has moved to Texas, closed on a house, gone to the DMV, and signed an Austin office lease.

[事实] The hosts discuss California’s proposed wealth tax, which they call an “asset seizure” or “seizure tax,” and argue it could drive wealthy founders and investors out of the state.

[事实] Chamath says people he knows who have left California represent roughly half a trillion dollars of net worth, which he believes is bad for California’s long-term budget.

[事实] Sacks says the proposed tax could be especially punitive for founders with illiquid or super-voting shares, using Larry Page and Sergey Brin as examples.

[推测] The opening sets California fiscal policy as a symbolic case for the episode’s larger argument about taxes, business formation, and political realignment.

[08:23] **Will California’s Wealth Tax Reach the Ballot?**

[事实] Jason asks the hosts to predict whether the California wealth tax will make the ballot and pass.

[事实] Friedberg says he does not think it will get on the ballot, while the hosts discuss a prediction market showing around 69% odds at that moment.

[事实] Sacks says the measure could fail to reach the ballot if the backing union lacks funds for signature gathering or if California Democratic leadership negotiates it away.

[事实] The hosts say around 850,000 signatures may be needed and that signature gathering could cost roughly $8 million.

[推测] The hosts treat the ballot fight as a potential trigger for more capital flight even before voters decide the measure.

[12:19] **Biggest Political Winners of 2026**

[事实] Friedberg predicts the Democratic Socialists of America will be a big political winner, comparing its rise in the Democratic Party to MAGA’s takeover of the Republican Party.

[事实] Chamath predicts politicians who fight waste, fraud, and abuse at federal, state, and local levels will benefit politically.

[事实] Sacks predicts the “Trump boom” will be the biggest winner, citing inflation, GDP growth, lower trade deficits, job-cut data, stocks, gas prices, mortgage costs, wages, rate cuts, and tax refunds.

[事实] Jason says he gives the edge to the “Mamdani moment,” while also discussing JD Vance as a strong runner-up.

[推测] The hosts broadly expect 2026 politics to be shaped by economic performance and by a leftward pull inside the Democratic Party.

[17:57] **Biggest Political Losers of 2026**

[事实] Sacks predicts Democratic centrism will lose because socialist and progressive energy is rising among younger Democratic voters and because many House districts are not competitive.

[事实] Chamath pushes back on the idea that progressivism broadly succeeded in 2025, arguing that Democratic approval worsened as progressive messaging grew.

[事实] Chamath names the Monroe Doctrine as the biggest political loser, arguing that Trump’s worldview is replacing it with hemispheric dominance, unilateralism, and transactional relationships.

[事实] Friedberg predicts the tech industry will be a political loser because AI and tech wealth are becoming populist targets on both the right and the left.

[推测] The discussion suggests tech may face pressure from both parties unless it repairs trust with conservatives and addresses left-wing hostility to wealth and property rights.

[27:06] **Trump, Venezuela, and the Debate Over Interventionism**

[事实] Jason calls Trump’s foreign policy “neocon” because of military actions and threats involving Venezuela, Iran, Colombia, Greenland, and Cuba.

[事实] Sacks rejects that label, saying neocon policy involved invasion, occupation, and nation building, while Trump’s Venezuela operation had none of those features.

[事实] Sacks describes the Venezuela action as short, successful, and without American deaths, and says the administration is not trying to install Maria Corina Machado with U.S. troops.

[事实] Jason counters that military operations can go wrong and that a failed mission with dead or captured Americans would have created a very different discussion.

[推测] The hosts agree Trump’s approach may need a new label, but they disagree on whether its risks resemble older interventionist policy.

[32:13] **Biggest Business Winners of 2026**

[事实] Friedberg names Huawei and Polymarket as his business winners, citing Huawei’s chip-stack work with SMIC and Polymarket’s move from quirky prediction market to news and market infrastructure.

[事实] Chamath names copper, arguing that demand from data centers, chips, weapons systems, and national-security needs will collide with global supply shortages.

[事实] Sacks predicts IPOs will have a big year and could create trillions of dollars in new public-market capitalization.

[事实] Jason predicts Amazon will have a massive year because of robots, Zoox, same-day delivery, AWS, and what he calls a possible “corporate singularity.”

[推测] Their business winners cluster around hard assets, AI infrastructure, market infrastructure, and reopened capital markets.

[38:35] **Sacks’s Divestment and Public Service**

[事实] The hosts discuss Sacks divesting Groq and xAI-related holdings as part of joining government.

[事实] Chamath says the economic cost to Sacks could exceed a billion dollars by the time he leaves government.

[事实] Sacks says he would not mention the financial cost except that media reporters have claimed the government role is making him money.

[推测] This aside functions as a defense of Sacks’s motives and a criticism of mainstream media coverage.

[40:25] **Biggest Business Losers of 2026**

[事实] Friedberg predicts state governments will be business losers because waste, fraud, abuse, borrowing problems, and pension liabilities will become harder to ignore.

[事实] Chamath predicts the “software industrial complex” will lose as AI agents compress the maintenance and migration revenue that supports large enterprise software businesses.

[事实] Sacks predicts California will lose because the wealth tax, regulations, refinery closures, and business hostility will drive away capital and raise costs.

[事实] Jason predicts young white-collar workers will lose because entry-level work is increasingly automated by AI and companies are removing lower rungs from career ladders.

[推测] This section links AI disruption, fiscal stress, and anti-business policy as the main business risks of 2026.

[45:29] **AI, Gen Z, and Entry-Level Jobs**

[事实] Friedberg shares feedback from a friend who says companies are still hiring junior engineers, but less than before, and that talent quality and cultural issues may also be factors.

[事实] Jason says both AI automation and cultural or motivational issues may be contributing to young workers’ difficulty finding jobs.

[事实] Jason says companies such as Google, Uber, and Coinbase are doing more with less and that young workers who use AI tools will have better job prospects.

[推测] The hosts do not settle whether AI is the main cause of entry-level job pressure, but they agree young workers need stronger skills and adaptability.

[50:21] **Biggest Deals of 2026**

[事实] Sacks predicts coding assistants and tool-use software will become a major category, comparing the current excitement to chatbots at the end of 2022.

[事实] Friedberg predicts the Russia-Ukraine war will settle in 2026 and bring more regional stability.

[事实] Chamath predicts large IP-license deals will replace traditional M&A because antitrust and geopolitical barriers make major acquisitions difficult.

[事实] Jason predicts at least one $50 billion-plus deal involving a Mag 7 company and an AI company such as xAI, Mistral, Perplexity, or Anthropic.

[推测] The hosts expect dealmaking to accelerate, but they disagree on whether it will happen through conventional acquisitions or alternative structures.

[56:17] **Contrarian Beliefs for 2026**

[事实] Friedberg predicts Iran’s ruling ayatollahs may fall, but his contrarian point is that Iran’s change could lead to more conflict among other Gulf and Middle Eastern actors.

[事实] Sacks predicts AI will increase, not decrease, demand for knowledge workers through Jevons Paradox, using code and radiology as examples.

[事实] Chamath predicts SpaceX will not IPO and will instead reverse merge into Tesla.

[事实] Chamath also predicts central banks may seek a new private, secure cryptographic asset because of limits to gold and Bitcoin and future quantum risks.

[事实] Jason predicts the China standoff, including Taiwan-related issues, will be largely resolved under Trump.

[推测] The contrarian round is the episode’s broadest section, moving from Middle East geopolitics to AI labor markets, Elon Musk’s companies, crypto reserves, and U.S.-China relations.

[63:45] **Best Performing Assets of 2026**

[事实] Friedberg picks Polymarket, citing network effects and its potential to replace parts of media and markets.

[事实] Chamath picks a basket of critical metals.

[事实] Sacks picks the expanding tech supercycle and cites a productivity surge and Atlanta Fed GDPNow estimate as evidence of a stronger economy.

[事实] Jason picks gambling and wagering-related assets such as Robinhood, Polymarket, PrizePicks, and possibly Coinbase.

[事实] Sacks predicts California luxury real estate will underperform because of wealth-tax overhang and other California policy risks.

[事实] Chamath predicts hydrocarbons will perform poorly, arguing that electrification and energy storage will keep pressuring oil demand.

[事实] Jason predicts the U.S. dollar will be challenged because debt continues to grow and military spending may rise.

[事实] Friedberg predicts Netflix could underperform if it does not close the Warner Brothers deal, and otherwise says traditional media stocks will remain challenged.

[推测] The bearish calls focus on assets exposed to tax policy, energy transition, debt debasement, and changing media distribution.

[事实] Sacks raises accelerated depreciation as an important asset theme, saying 100% accelerated depreciation now applies to certain capital equipment.

[事实] Chamath says this is not mainly about planes, but about equipment such as Caterpillar, generators, Siemens, and infrastructure assets.

[事实] Chamath says he is still short nuclear because large reactors and SMRs may miss the economic window if solar, storage, coal, and oil drive marginal electricity costs down.

[事实] Friedberg counters that demand may shift upward and that the U.S. may need much more electricity generation to compete with China.

[推测] The nuclear exchange shows a recurring tension in the episode: whether bottlenecks will be solved by new supply, new policy, or cheaper competing technologies.

[事实] Friedberg says his most anticipated trend is Iran becoming an independent democratic state, driven by unrest, young demographics, and economic pain.

[事实] Sacks says he most anticipates audits of government spending at all levels and criticizes efforts to block audits.

[事实] Jason predicts the return of mega IPOs, naming SpaceX, Anduril, Stripe, Anthropic, and OpenAI as companies that could file.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value is in the density of predictions. The hosts cover a wide range of topics and often attach concrete names, assets, policy mechanisms, or market structures to their calls, which makes the episode useful for listeners who want a map of what Silicon Valley investors and operators are watching in 2026.

The strongest sections are the debates where the hosts disagree: AI jobs, Trump foreign policy, nuclear power, California taxation, and M&A structure. Those moments expose the assumptions behind each prediction instead of leaving them as simple hot takes.

[推测] The main limitation is that several claims are made quickly and rhetorically, with little outside evidence provided inside the transcript. Listeners should treat many forecasts as investor-perspective arguments rather than neutral analysis.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in tech, markets, U.S. politics, AI, venture capital, and geopolitical risk, especially those who enjoy opinionated forecasting more than a tightly sourced news briefing.
