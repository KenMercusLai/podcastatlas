# The Peasants' Revolt: Richard II and the Fall of Wat Tyler

## 概览

This episode continues the story of the 1381 Peasants' Revolt, focusing on the rebels' arrival outside London, Richard II's negotiations with them, and the dramatic confrontation at Smithfield.

The discussion argues that the revolt was not simply a peasant uprising. It combined anti-serfdom demands, hostility to corrupt officials, attacks on legal records and monopolies, royalist loyalty to the young king, religious radicalism, xenophobic violence, and London commercial rivalries.

The central turning point is Richard II's handling of Wat Tyler's death at Smithfield. After Tyler is struck down, Richard rides forward, claims leadership of the rebels, and helps defuse a moment that could have become a massacre.

The episode closes by assessing the revolt's mixed legacy: its immediate demands failed, but poll taxes became politically toxic, and the experience shaped Richard II's sense of royal charisma and authority.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **The Mile End Moment**

[事实] The episode opens with a chronicle describing Richard II meeting the rebels at Mile End on 14 June 1381.

[事实] The rebels demand the end of serfdom, fixed rents, freedom from forced labor, and action against supposed traitors.

[事实] The hosts frame this as one of the most famous moments in English history because an anointed king negotiated with commoners.

[02:35] **Why London Matters**

[事实] The rebels are gathered outside London while Richard and the royal government are inside the city.

[事实] London is described as England's largest city, with around 50,000 people, crowded streets, docks, cranes, and major symbols of royal and ecclesiastical power.

[事实] Key targets include the Tower of London, St Paul's, Westminster, and John of Gaunt's Savoy Palace.

[07:09] **Southwark, Prisons, and Lambeth Palace**

[事实] Rebels move along the south bank of the Thames, enter Southwark, open prisons including the Marshalsea and the Clink, and release prisoners.

[事实] Lambeth Palace, the London home of Archbishop Sudbury, is sacked; chancery rolls and legal documents are burned.

[推测] The destruction of documents suggests the rebels were attacking the legal machinery that upheld lordship, debt, and authority.

[09:22] **Royalism Among the Rebels**

[事实] Sir John Newington, whose children are held hostage, is sent to tell Richard that the rebels still regard him as their king.

[事实] The hosts stress that the rebels are strongly royalist and do not resemble French Revolutionaries.

[事实] They believe direct dependence on the king is preferable to dependence on feudal intermediaries such as magnates or abbeys.

[10:42] **Richard's First Test**

[事实] Richard is 14, personally brave, and increasingly aware that the rebels feel personal loyalty to him.

[事实] His advisers arrange for him to meet the rebels by royal barge at Rotherhithe on 13 June.

[事实] When the royal party sees the scale of the crowd, the advisers turn the barge back, angering the rebels.

[12:00] **The Rebels Enter London**

[事实] The rebels advance to London Bridge, and no serious attempt is made to hold it against them.

[事实] The hosts suggest London Bridge may have been spared because it was central to the city's prosperity.

[事实] Rebels from Mile End also force entry through Aldgate, and London quickly falls under rebel control.

[13:02] **Targeted Destruction**

[事实] The rebels attack the Knights Hospitallers' headquarters at Clerkenwell because Robert Hales, the treasurer blamed for the poll tax, is also its prior.

[事实] Charters, court rolls, writings, and records are burned repeatedly during the revolt.

[事实] John of Gaunt's Savoy Palace is destroyed so thoroughly that it cannot realistically be rebuilt.

[事实] The rebels destroy luxury goods rather than stealing them, and looters are punished.

[15:20] **Religion, Justice, and Rivalries**

[事实] The hosts argue that the rebels present themselves as lovers of truth and justice rather than thieves.

[事实] The episode links parts of the movement to religious ideas and possible proto-Protestant currents.

[事实] It also shows that wealthy Londoners used the disorder to settle commercial and political scores.

[16:56] **The Killing of Richard Lyons**

[事实] Sir Richard Lyons, a financier and monopolist associated with John of Gaunt, is dragged from his house and publicly beheaded on Cheapside.

[事实] The hosts argue that rebels from Kent and Essex may not have known where Lyons lived, implying help from London rivals.

[推测] This episode complicates the revolt by showing elite opportunism inside a popular uprising.

[18:42] **Richard and the Royal Government**

[事实] Richard is described as intelligent, brave, conscious of royal dignity, but still untested.

[事实] His advisers first try a general pardon, but the rebels refuse to disperse without abolition of serfdom and the surrender of traitors in the Tower.

[事实] William Walworth wants armed action, but the Earl of Salisbury warns that the government lacks enough men and risks a bloodbath.

[21:04] **Mile End Concessions**

[事实] Richard rides to Mile End and hears complaints about intolerable servitude and heavy oppression.

[事实] He agrees to abolish serfdom and to remove monopolies, tolls, and trading privileges.

[事实] The hosts describe these terms as both radically egalitarian and radically free-market.

[推测] The concessions would have transformed England's social and economic order if they had been upheld.

[23:22] **The Tower Is Entered**

[事实] After Mile End, Richard does not return to the Tower, while many rebels go there and gain entry.

[事实] The hosts say the Tower was considered impregnable, so the rebels probably entered through persuasion or inside assistance.

[事实] Simon Sudbury, Robert Hales, John Legge, a lawyer from Stepney, and John of Gaunt's physician are executed.

[事实] Henry Bolingbroke escapes with help from a rebel from Rochester.

[27:27] **London Remains Uncontrolled**

[事实] Many rebels begin to leave, but enough remain that Richard still lacks control of London.

[事实] The severed heads of Sudbury and Hales are paraded and placed on London Bridge.

[事实] More than 150 foreign merchants are killed, including Flemings dragged from sanctuary in a church.

[29:00] **Smithfield and New Demands**

[事实] On 15 June, Richard prays at Westminster Abbey and rides to Smithfield with Walworth and Sir Robert Knowles.

[事实] Wat Tyler demands abolition of poaching laws, abolition of manorial courts, and a sweeping reduction of church hierarchy and property.

[事实] The hosts connect these religious demands to John Ball and also discuss possible links to John Wycliffe's ideas.

[32:33] **Wat Tyler's Death**

[事实] After Richard agrees to further terms, a confrontation breaks out.

[事实] Tyler is insulted as a rebel and robber, Walworth tries to arrest him, Tyler draws a dagger, and Walworth strikes him.

[事实] Tyler is taken to St Bartholomew's Hospital, then arrested, dragged out, and beheaded.

[34:29] **Richard Takes Command**

[事实] As rebel archers prepare to shoot, Richard rides forward and tells them they shall have no captain but him.

[事实] He leads the rebels toward Clerkenwell Fields.

[事实] City forces are mustered, the rebels are surrounded, and they surrender.

[事实] The Kentish rebels are escorted back through London Bridge, while others return toward Essex and Suffolk.

[36:12] **Promises Reversed**

[事实] The hosts say it is unclear how much of the Smithfield outcome was planned.

[事实] They also argue Richard's own attitude is more ambiguous than that of his advisers.

[事实] Richard does not reverse the Mile End promises until 2 July, more than two weeks after the London revolt is suppressed.

[37:42] **Revolt Beyond London**

[事实] Disturbances continue in East Anglia and elsewhere, including Bury St Edmunds, St Albans, Cambridge, Bridgwater, York, and Beverley.

[事实] At Cambridge, university records from before 1381 are destroyed.

[事实] Marjorie Starr is described dancing around burning university records and denouncing clerical learning.

[40:15] **Suppression and Punishment**

[事实] John of Gaunt, Thomas of Woodstock, and Bishop Henry Despenser help suppress the revolt.

[事实] Wat Tyler is dead, John Ball is captured, tried, and executed.

[事实] Nearly 300 leading rebels are executed.

[事实] Richard eventually issues a broader pardon and confirms there will be no general repression.

[41:38] **Failure and Legacy**

[事实] Serfdom is not formally abolished, officials largely remain in place, monopolies survive, and John of Gaunt remains powerful.

[事实] The rebels' stated aims are not achieved.

[事实] One clear legacy is that poll taxes disappear from English politics for about 600 years.

[事实] The hosts argue that the revolt becomes an institutional warning that the commons cannot be pushed too far.

[43:05] **Ideological Ambiguity**

[事实] The revolt is remembered as a starting point for English radicalism, egalitarianism, and progressive politics.

[事实] The hosts also argue it can be read as royalist, anti-tax, anti-regulation, and friendly to free enterprise.

[事实] They connect its geography and ideas to later Protestant, American, and even Thatcherite themes.

[推测] The episode's larger point is that the revolt resists simple left-right interpretation.

[45:50] **Richard II's Future**

[事实] The hosts end by returning to Richard II, arguing that the revolt was traumatic and foundational for him.

[事实] They say the experience strengthened his belief in his own charisma and centrality.

[事实] The episode points ahead to Richard's later deposition and to future episodes on Geoffrey Chaucer.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode's main value is its layered reading of the Peasants' Revolt. It treats the events not as a simple clash between peasants and nobles, but as a volatile convergence of class grievance, royal legitimacy, religious radicalism, urban politics, commercial resentment, and xenophobia.

[推测] Its strongest moments are the narrative set pieces: the view of London from Blackheath, the destruction of the Savoy Palace, the Tower executions, and Richard's intervention after Wat Tyler is struck down. These scenes make the political stakes vivid without losing the broader historical analysis.

[推测] A limitation is that several key uncertainties remain unresolved, including how the rebels entered the Tower, whether Tyler's death was orchestrated, and how sincere Richard was in his concessions. The hosts flag these uncertainties rather than pretending the evidence is clearer than it is.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in medieval England, political revolt, the origins of English radical traditions, or Richard II's reign. It also works well for listeners who enjoy historical events being interpreted through both immediate drama and long-term political memory.
