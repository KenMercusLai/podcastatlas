# 1922: The Year the Modern Begins

## 概览

This episode asks whether 1922 can be treated as a “year one” of the modern age. The hosts begin with the famous Paris dinner that brought Joyce and Proust into the same room, then use the publication of *Ulysses* and *The Waste Land* to explore why modernism seemed to crystallize that year.

The discussion argues that 1922 was not a clean break from the First World War, but a moment when the war’s aftermath began to harden into a new order. The hosts emphasize cultural fragmentation, political violence, the collapse of empires, the rise of Bolshevism, fascism, and the instability of postwar Germany and Italy.

A central conclusion is that the “Roaring Twenties” image misses much of the period’s morbidity and paranoia. In this account, 1922 is marked less by glamour than by fear: fear of social collapse, communism, inflation, disease, and the failure of old institutions.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **The Dinner of Modern Genius**

[事实] Sidney Schiff hosted a dinner at the Majestic Hotel in Paris on 18 May 1922, inviting figures including Igor Stravinsky, Pablo Picasso, James Joyce, and Marcel Proust.

[事实] Joyce arrived late, drunk, and shabbily dressed, while Proust arrived even later, immaculately dressed.

[事实] The hosts describe Joyce and Proust’s exchange as disappointingly brief, with versions of the story suggesting they spoke only a few words.

[推测] The anecdote is used as a symbolic opening: the great modernist figures were gathered in one place, but the encounter itself was awkward rather than grand.

[02:44] **1922 as a Modern Year One**

[事实] Ezra Pound described the period after *Ulysses* as “post scriptum Ulysses” and treated 1921 or 1922 as a kind of beginning of a new era.

[事实] The hosts compare 1922 to resonant years such as 1968 and 1989.

[事实] They argue that the First World War overshadowed politics and culture, and that fighting continued after 1918 in places such as Russia, Turkey, and Ireland.

[推测] 1922 matters because it appears to be a point when postwar flux began to settle into structures that would shape the rest of the 20th century.

[05:35] **New Political Orders After the War**

[事实] The USSR came into being at the end of 1922.

[事实] The hosts connect the early 1920s to later politics dominated by communism versus capitalism and by the disintegration of European empires.

[事实] They also mention the relationship between the United Kingdom and Ireland, and the configuration of the Middle East, as issues shaped around this period.

[推测] The episode frames 1922 as a hinge between imperial Europe and the ideological conflicts of the later 20th century.

[07:03] **Modernism, Ulysses, and The Waste Land**

[事实] The hosts identify *Ulysses* and *The Waste Land* as major English-language works published in 1922.

[事实] *Ulysses* was published on 2 February 1922, Joyce’s 40th birthday.

[事实] *The Waste Land* appeared later in 1922.

[事实] Both works are presented as difficult, self-consciously modern texts that broke with earlier literary traditions.

[推测] The hosts treat literary modernism as a cultural expression of a wider civilizational rupture after the war.

[08:29] **Joyce’s Dublin and a Vanished World**

[事实] *Ulysses* is set on 16 June 1904, the day Joyce first had a date with Nora Barnacle.

[事实] Joyce spent the First World War in neutral Switzerland and showed limited interest in the Irish Free State or Irish Civil War.

[事实] The hosts note that *Ulysses* depicts a Dublin that had already changed dramatically by 1922.

[事实] Joyce used newspapers and detailed inquiries to reconstruct a specific day in Dublin.

[推测] The novel’s modernity comes partly from combining intense historical specificity with radical literary experimentation.

[11:17] **Difficulty, Fragments, and Tradition**

[事实] The hosts compare Joyce’s literary method with Picasso’s cubism and Eliot’s fragmented structure in *The Waste Land*.

[事实] Eliot argued that modern civilization’s variety and complexity meant poets “must be difficult.”

[事实] The hosts say both Joyce and Eliot break apart older traditions while remaining deeply aware of those traditions.

[推测] Modernist difficulty is presented not as obscurity for its own sake, but as a response to the complexity of modern life.

[13:00] **Modernism Meets Popular Culture**

[事实] Enid Blyton’s first published work appeared in 1910 and was a volume of poetry.

[事实] The hosts contrast Blyton’s wholesome style with the experimentalism of Eliot and Joyce.

[事实] They quote Blyton describing herself as “young and normal” and preferring something more wholesome.

[推测] This contrast shows that avant-garde modernism was not the whole culture of the period; ordinary popular tastes continued alongside it.

[14:00] **Scandal and Reception of Ulysses**

[事实] *Ulysses* received hostile reviews in 1922, with critics calling it disgusting, boring, Bolshevist, and obscene.

[事实] The hosts describe the novel as following Stephen Dedalus, Leopold Bloom, and Molly Bloom through the events of a single day.

[事实] They note that the novel includes bodily functions, masturbation, and sexual fantasy.

[事实] Virginia Woolf was appalled by *Ulysses*, though she would later be influenced by it.

[推测] The scandal around *Ulysses* helped make it famous, especially because many people had heard of it without reading it.

[17:16] **The Waste Land and Postwar Ruin**

[事实] Virginia Woolf was more sympathetic to *The Waste Land* than to *Ulysses*.

[事实] The hosts describe *The Waste Land* as a difficult poem made from fragments, voices, quotations, and languages.

[事实] Pound edited Eliot’s draft and praised it with jealous admiration.

[事实] The poem is connected to the shadow of the Great War, mourning, destroyed certainties, and the memory of mass death.

[推测] The hosts read *The Waste Land* as one of the clearest cultural expressions of postwar disorientation.

[21:47] **Morbidity, Disease, and Conservative Redemption**

[事实] The hosts connect the language of the 1920s to viruses, germs, Bolshevism, doom, decadence, and degeneracy.

[事实] Richard Overy’s book *The Morbid Age* is mentioned as a study of British culture in the 1920s and 1930s.

[事实] Eliot later converted to the Church of England and became more conservative, royalist, and traditionalist.

[推测] Eliot’s later traditionalism is presented as an attempt to reassemble the inheritance that *The Waste Land* had fragmented.

[23:01] **Modernism and the Daily Mail Connection**

[事实] The magazine *The Criterion*, where *The Waste Land* appeared, was funded by Lady Rothermere, wife of Lord Rothermere of the Daily Mail.

[事实] Joseph Conrad had connections with the Daily Mail and struggled with writing to deadline.

[事实] Edward Thomas ghostwrote articles for Conrad.

[事实] Eliot supported the Daily Mail’s strong stance in a murder case involving a woman hanged after her lover killed her husband.

[推测] The hosts use these details to complicate the idea that modernism existed only in elite little magazines detached from mass media.

[26:51] **The Postwar Rubble**

[事实] After the break, the hosts return to 1922 as a year shaped by the civilizational wreckage of the First World War.

[事实] They say they will focus on Germany, Italy, and Russia in this part of the discussion.

[事实] Germany is described as maimed, dismembered, and pauperized after losing its empire.

[推测] The second half shifts from cultural modernism to the political conditions that made the 1920s unstable and dangerous.

[27:54] **Germany and the Weimar Crisis**

[事实] By 1922, the Weimar Republic had survived revolution and fighting between Spartacists and the Freikorps.

[事实] Hitler had joined the Nazi Party, discovered his gift for beer-hall speeches, and become its leader, though he was not yet a national figure.

[事实] Foreign minister Walther Rathenau was assassinated by right-wing paramilitaries.

[事实] German culture in 1922 included works such as *Nosferatu* and Fritz Lang’s first Dr. Mabuse film.

[推测] The hosts connect Weimar politics and culture through a shared sense of instability, violence, and morbidity.

[30:18] **Defeat, Betrayal, and Paramilitary Politics**

[事实] Some Germans accepted the Weimar Republic, while many conservatives rejected its legitimacy.

[事实] Ludendorff promoted the idea that Germany could have fought on and had been betrayed by politicians and fifth columnists.

[事实] Jews were identified as key scapegoats in this “stabbed in the back” narrative.

[事实] The hosts describe German politics as having a paramilitary quality, with communists and the hard right fighting in cities such as Berlin and Munich.

[推测] The normalisation of political violence is presented as one of Weimar Germany’s deepest weaknesses.

[32:03] **Inflation and Its Long Shadow**

[事实] The mark fell from roughly 350 to the dollar at the beginning of 1922 to around 7,400 to the dollar by the end of that year.

[事实] By late 1923, the exchange rate had reached billions of marks to the dollar.

[事实] Inflation destroyed savings and damaged middle-class faith in the system.

[事实] The hosts say Germany’s later fear of inflation influenced its approach to European economic policy.

[推测] They stress that although inflation became the iconic failure of Weimar, unemployment during the Great Depression was more directly responsible for bringing the Nazis to power.

[35:38] **The USSR and Stalin’s Rise**

[事实] The Soviet Union was established at the end of 1922.

[事实] Lenin suffered a stroke in May 1922.

[事实] Stalin became General Secretary of the Communist Party and used control of the party machine and access to Lenin to strengthen his position.

[事实] Stalin was central to the creation of the USSR.

[推测] The hosts treat 1922 as a key moment in the emergence of the Soviet system that would shape global politics for decades.

[36:25] **Italy, War Trauma, and Mussolini**

[事实] Italy entered the First World War to gain territory and suffered around 700,000 deaths.

[事实] The hosts describe the Italian army’s soldiers as heroic but its generals as inept.

[事实] After the war, Italy experienced labour unrest, factory occupations, unemployment, and class hostility.

[事实] Mussolini shifted from socialist journalism toward an alliance with landowners, factory owners, and the right.

[推测] Fascism is presented as a response to both postwar grievance and fear of Bolshevism.

[39:25] **The March on Rome**

[事实] Mussolini launched the March on Rome after a failed general strike in late summer 1922.

[事实] The hosts describe the march as a con, because Mussolini did not personally lead it and waited elsewhere.

[事实] Prime Minister Luigi Facta wanted to declare a state of emergency and crush the fascists.

[事实] King Victor Emmanuel III refused to sign the order and appointed Mussolini prime minister.

[推测] The episode draws a parallel between Italy in 1922 and Germany in 1932-33, where conservative elites underestimated radical authoritarian leaders.

[41:21] **Fascism’s Ambiguous Modernity**

[事实] The hosts discuss fascism as both radical and conservative, modern and anti-modern.

[事实] Fascist parties used radio, cars, and planes while also invoking an idealized past.

[事实] Gabriele D’Annunzio is described as a trailblazer for Mussolini and as a proto-fascist figure.

[推测] Fascism’s appeal is presented as partly coming from its ability to combine revolutionary energy with reactionary aims.

[42:45] **Bolshevism, Civil War, and Famine**

[事实] The hosts argue that the Great War and communism are two keys to understanding the period.

[事实] The Russian Civil War was mostly over by 1920, though fighting continued in Central Asia in 1922.

[事实] Japanese forces left Siberia in 1922, and the Red Army entered Vladivostok on 25 October.

[事实] Russia suffered a major famine in 1922, with around five million deaths.

[推测] The scale of death from war, flu, famine, and civil war made mass catastrophe a defining background condition of the early 1920s.

[45:03] **Lenin, NEP, and the Shape of the USSR**

[事实] Lenin introduced the New Economic Policy in 1921, allowing some small enterprise and peasant sale of surpluses.

[事实] After Lenin’s stroke, Stalin controlled access to him and shared power with figures including Kamenev and Zinoviev.

[事实] The USSR was formally shaped in late December 1922.

[事实] Lenin had wanted a union of Soviet republics of Europe and Asia, while Stalin changed the name to the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics.

[推测] The hosts suggest the USSR began moving toward becoming a communist equivalent of the old czarist empire.

[47:52] **Fear of Exported Revolution**

[事实] The hosts say people outside Russia did not know Bolshevism would move toward Stalin’s “socialism in one country.”

[事实] Many feared communist revolution would be exported through the Comintern.

[事实] This fear helped drive paramilitary politics in Germany and fascism in Italy.

[推测] The episode frames anti-communist fear as energizing rather than paralyzing, pushing societies toward authoritarian and violent responses.

[48:12] **British Paranoia and Bulldog Drummond**

[事实] The hosts argue that the early 1920s were marked by paranoia often missed in the cocktail-glass image of the “Roaring Twenties.”

[事实] They discuss the Bulldog Drummond books by H. C. McNeile, writing as Sapper.

[事实] In the 1922 book, Drummond creates a group called the Black Gang that attacks communists.

[事实] The books were popular among respectable middle-class readers and were later adapted into films.

[推测] Popular fiction is used as evidence that fears of Bolshevism and social infection were widespread even in victorious Britain.

[51:21] **Britain’s Imperial Peak and Fragility**

[事实] The hosts say the British Empire was geographically at its largest extent in 1922.

[事实] Britain had been a victor in the First World War.

[事实] The next episode is said to cover Britain, Ireland, the Levant, America, and Richmal Crompton.

[推测] The closing point is that Britain’s apparent imperial peak concealed deep structural weakness.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it connects literary modernism with political upheaval rather than treating culture and politics separately. Its strongest thread is the argument that *Ulysses*, *The Waste Land*, Weimar instability, Mussolini’s rise, and the USSR all belong to the same postwar world of fragmentation and fear.

The discussion is lively and wide-ranging, moving from elite literary anecdotes to inflation, fascism, famine, and popular fiction. Its breadth is also a limitation: some topics, especially Russia and Italy, are necessarily compressed and treated as part of a larger pattern rather than explored in full detail.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in 20th-century history, modernist literature, and the political consequences of the First World War. It may be less satisfying for listeners looking for a single focused narrative, because the argument depends on connecting many cultural and geopolitical strands.
