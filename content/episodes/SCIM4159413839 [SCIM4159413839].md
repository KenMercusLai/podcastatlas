# Human Mate Selection, Infidelity, Jealousy, and Evolutionary Psychology with Dr. David Buss

## 概览

Andrew Huberman interviews Dr. David Buss about human mating through the lens of evolutionary psychology, with emphasis on long-term mate choice, short-term sexual strategy, deception, infidelity, jealousy, mate value, stalking, intimate partner violence, and unconventional relationship arrangements.

The conversation repeatedly returns to the idea that human mating is shaped by mutual choice, competition, status, reproductive biology, and social cues. Buss distinguishes what men and women tend to prioritize on average, while repeatedly noting overlap, exceptions, cultural variation, and change over time.

A major throughline is that many painful or socially troubling behaviors, including deception, jealousy, infidelity, stalking, and violence, are discussed as mating-related strategies or defenses rather than random acts. The episode also considers practical implications: assessing emotional stability over time, understanding mate value more honestly, and recognizing risky personality traits such as the dark triad.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Guest and Scope**

[事实] Huberman introduces Dr. David Buss as a University of Texas at Austin psychology professor and a major figure in evolutionary psychology.

[事实] The episode covers long-term and short-term mate selection, sex differences in mating strategies, infidelity, deception, jealousy, mate poaching, ovulation, stalking, sexual violence, and relationship health.

[事实] Huberman highlights Buss’s books including *The Evolution of Desire*, *Why Women Have Sex*, and *When Men Behave Badly*.

[05:34] **Sexual Selection as the Framework**

[事实] Buss explains mate choice through Darwin’s theory of sexual selection, separating survival advantages from mating advantages.

[事实] He describes two causal processes: intrasexual competition and preferential mate choice.

[事实] Buss says human mating involves mutual mate choice, meaning both men and women have preferences and both sexes compete for desirable partners.

[10:05] **Long-Term Mate Preferences**

[事实] Buss says long-term pair bonding is rare among mammals and distinguishes humans from many other mammalian and primate species.

[事实] His 37-culture study found universal long-term preferences shared by men and women, including intelligence, kindness, mutual attraction, love, good health, dependability, and emotional stability.

[事实] Buss says women, more than men on average, prioritize earning capacity, status, ambition, slightly older age, and cues of resource acquisition.

[事实] Men, more than women on average, prioritize physical attractiveness and relative youth, which Buss links to cues of fertility and reproductive value.

[15:37] **Status, Attention, and Mate Choice Copying**

[事实] Buss says status is partly inferred through the attention structure: high-status people are those to whom many others pay attention.

[事实] He describes mate choice copying, where a man may be judged more attractive if he is shown with attractive women or appears already selected by women.

[事实] Modern cues such as social media followers are discussed as possible indicators of attention and status.

[推测] The discussion implies that mate evaluation is often socially comparative rather than based only on direct personal preference.

[21:52] **Age Gaps and Cultural Variation**

[事实] Buss says men in his studies preferred women about three to four years younger on average, while women preferred men about three and a half to four and a half years older.

[事实] He says U.S. age gaps tend to increase across first, second, and third marriages, with men generally being older.

[事实] Buss identifies preference for virginity as the most culturally variable trait in his 37-culture study.

[事实] He says the importance of virginity has decreased in modern urban China, while a sex difference emerged in which men value it more than women.

[28:47] **Deception in Dating**

[事实] Buss says both men and women deceive in predictable ways that map onto what the opposite sex tends to prefer.

[事实] He says men tend to exaggerate income, status, and height, while women tend to underreport weight.

[事实] Both sexes may post photos that are not representative, including younger or optimized images.

[事实] Buss recommends meeting in person rather than relying on extensive messaging because face-to-face interaction provides more cues, including smell, voice, and behavior.

[35:22] **Deception About Commitment**

[事实] Buss says deception about long-term interest versus short-term sexual intent is an old and important form of mating deception.

[事实] He says men may exaggerate the depth of their feelings, similarity, shared values, religion, or politics to appear more aligned with a woman.

[事实] Buss argues modern online dating enables forms of deception that were harder in ancestral small-group environments, where reputation and social knowledge were more available.

[38:28] **Short-Term Sexual Partner Choice**

[事实] Buss says physical appearance becomes more important for women in short-term mating than in long-term mating.

[事实] He says women are more attracted in short-term contexts to “bad-boy” qualities such as confidence, arrogance, risk-taking, and defiance of convention.

[事实] Buss says men may lower standards for short-term sex when commitment and perceived risk are low.

[事实] Women’s attraction to men is described as more context-dependent than men’s attraction to women.

[43:00] **Infidelity Rates and Motives**

[事实] Buss says infidelity is hard to measure because people often conceal it.

[事实] He cites Kinsey’s estimate that 26% of married women and about 50% of married men committed sexual infidelity at some point, while noting other studies give lower figures.

[事实] Buss says men who cheat most often cite opportunity and sexual variety, while women who cheat more often cite unhappiness in the primary relationship.

[事实] He clarifies that the 70% figure refers to men who cheat citing opportunity or variety as a key motive, not to all men cheating.

[47:31] **Why Women Have Affairs**

[事实] Buss contrasts the dual mating strategy hypothesis with his preferred mate switching hypothesis.

[事实] The dual mating strategy hypothesis proposes that a woman may seek investment from one man and “good genes” from another.

[事实] Buss says he shifted toward the mate switching hypothesis because many women who have affairs report falling in love or becoming emotionally involved with the affair partner.

[事实] He says modern estimates of genetic cuckoldry are low, around two to three percent.

[52:00] **Ovulation Evidence and Changing Views**

[事实] Buss says early ovulation studies seemed to support the dual mating strategy hypothesis by suggesting women’s preferences shifted toward masculine and symmetrical men during ovulation.

[事实] He says later larger studies weakened or failed to replicate some of those findings.

[事实] Buss now considers mate switching a more likely explanation for why most women have affairs.

[推测] The segment shows Buss revising his position in response to later empirical findings rather than treating early results as settled.

[54:12] **Affair Duration and Detection**

[事实] Buss says women who have affairs tend to have one affair partner and become emotionally involved over time.

[事实] He says men who have affairs tend to have a larger number of affair partners.

[事实] Huberman and Buss discuss how phones, text messages, cloud storage, and cameras can make infidelity easier to detect today.

[55:46] **Emotional and Financial Infidelity**

[事实] Buss defines emotional infidelity as falling in love with someone else, becoming psychologically close to someone else, or sharing intimate information with someone else.

[事实] He describes a study of the show *Cheaters* in which men confronting infidelity tended to ask about sex first, while women tended to ask about love first.

[事实] Buss says financial infidelity includes secret credit cards, secret bank accounts, hidden spending, and concealed diversion of shared resources.

[事实] He says women tend to define infidelity more broadly than men, including emotional and financial forms.

[63:20] **Status and Mate Value**

[事实] Buss says some mate preferences are conscious, while people may not know why they find certain cues attractive.

[事实] He says high status can increase access to a wider pool of potential mates.

[事实] He also says having a desirable mate can itself raise perceived status, especially in examples where an unattractive man is paired with a highly attractive woman.

[推测] The discussion frames status and mate value as mutually reinforcing rather than independent.

[67:24] **Jealousy as an Evolved Emotion**

[事实] Buss says jealousy was historically treated as immaturity, insecurity, neurosis, pathology, or delusion, but he argues it is an evolved emotion.

[事实] He says jealousy helps protect long-term pair bonds and investment.

[事实] Jealousy can be triggered by cues of infidelity, emotional distance, mate poachers, or mate value discrepancies.

[事实] Buss says higher mate value partners are statistically more likely to have affairs or leave for another partner.

[74:24] **Mate Retention, Vigilance, and Violence**

[事实] Buss says jealousy can lead to tactics ranging from vigilance to violence.

[事实] Vigilance can include stalking, following, hacking phones or computers, monitoring mate poachers, and watching eye contact.

[事实] Buss says 28% to 30% of married people in America experience intimate partner violence in their relationship.

[事实] He says verbal denigration, such as insulting a partner’s appearance, can function to lower a partner’s self-perceived mate value.

[76:57] **Intimate Partner Violence and Control**

[事实] Buss says men tend to cause more physical damage when intimate partner violence occurs.

[事实] He says verbal violence predicts physical violence in his studies of married couples.

[事实] He describes isolation from friends and family as a warning sign and as a tactic that reduces exposure to alternative partners.

[事实] Buss says female-to-male violence occurs, sometimes as self-defense and sometimes in response to sexual, emotional, or financial infidelity.

[80:42] **Pregnancy, Paternity Suspicion, and Violence**

[事实] Buss says pregnant women may be more vulnerable to violence when the man suspects he is not the father.

[事实] He says in those cases violence may be directed toward the abdomen.

[事实] Buss labels the hypothesized function as terminating a pregnancy by a rival male.

[推测] This is one of the episode’s darkest examples of Buss interpreting violent behavior through possible evolutionary function rather than moral approval.

[84:05] **The Dark Triad**

[事实] Buss defines the dark triad as narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy.

[事实] Narcissism is described as grandiosity and entitlement; Machiavellianism as exploitative manipulation; psychopathy as lack of empathy.

[事实] Buss says men score higher than women on these traits, especially psychopathy, with clinical psychopathy estimated at about 1% of women and 4% of men.

[事实] He links high dark triad traits to sexual deception, harassment, coercion, intimate partner violence, and serial offending.

[89:50] **Sexual Overperception and Serial Offenders**

[事实] Buss says high dark triad men are more susceptible to male sexual overperception bias, interpreting friendliness or professionalism as sexual interest.

[事实] He says a minority of men commit the majority of sexual violence because perpetrators are often serial offenders.

[事实] He gives examples including Harvey Weinstein, Bill Cosby, and Jeffrey Epstein as high-profile cases involving many victims.

[推测] The discussion suggests prevention should focus heavily on identifying repeat offenders and high-risk traits.

[90:50] **Stalking as a Mating Strategy**

[事实] Buss says stalking has multiple motivations, but one frequent motivation is mating-related.

[事实] He says about 80% of criminal stalkers are men and about 20% are women.

[事实] Male stalkers may try to reestablish a relationship or interfere with a woman’s future mating prospects.

[事实] Buss says stalking works in a minority of cases, around 15%, either temporarily or more permanently.

[96:11] **Children and Mate Value**

[事实] Buss says having children from a previous partner generally decreases mate value for both women and men because children are perceived as costs.

[事实] Costs include time, attention, resources, and investment in children not genetically related to the new partner.

[事实] Buss says the effect often weighs more on women because custody and child investment more often fall to women.

[事实] He notes exceptions, such as divorced parents with children finding compatibility with each other.

[100:04] **Attachment Styles and Relationship Load**

[事实] Huberman asks about childhood attachment and adult partner stability, while Buss says this is not his main area of expertise.

[事实] Buss says secure attachment in both partners is conducive to long-term mating.

[事实] He says avoidant attachment is associated with difficulty in intimacy and a higher probability of infidelity.

[事实] He describes anxious attachment as potentially creating clinginess, dependence, and high “relationship load.”

[103:10] **Polyamory and Unconventional Relationships**

[事实] Buss says polyamory can be understood as an attempt to overcome some evolved features of mating psychology while serving others.

[事实] He says men are more likely on average to initiate polyamorous relationships, often due to desire for sexual variety.

[事实] He says some women may agree to polyamory as a mate retention tactic.

[事实] Buss describes polyamorous couples creating rules around what is acceptable, such as limits by time, location, sex, or emotional involvement.

[108:42] **Pornography and Evolved Desire**

[事实] Buss says internet pornography parasitizes men’s evolved desire for sexual variety by offering psychological access to many sexual partners without actual sex.

[事实] Huberman adds that online pornography offers both high variety and high intensity.

[事实] Huberman says studies are examining whether frequent pornography viewing may wire young people more toward observing sexual acts than participating in them.

[推测] The segment treats pornography as another modern environment that activates old mating circuitry in novel ways.

[110:50] **Self-Assessment and Mate Value**

[事实] Buss says people are generally fairly good at assessing their own mate value.

[事实] He says self-esteem may function as an internal monitor that tracks status, rejection, promotion, and other mate-value-relevant cues.

[事实] He distinguishes consensual mate value from individually specific mate value, such as one person highly valuing knowledge of Russian literature.

[事实] Buss says there is no good scientific measure that fully captures mate value because the components are numerous and complicated.

[117:30] **Rejection and Mating Anxiety**

[事实] Huberman notes that rejection can lower perceived mate value.

[事实] Buss says many men have mating anxiety because approaching someone risks rejection.

[事实] He describes Albert Ellis assigning himself repeated dating approaches to reduce anxiety through exposure to rejection.

[推测] The example suggests that some mating anxiety can be reduced through behavioral desensitization.

[118:47] **Self-Deception and Deception**

[事实] Buss attributes the self-deception hypothesis to Robert Trivers.

[事实] The hypothesis is that successful deception is facilitated by self-deception: if someone truly believes a claim, they may be more convincing to others.

[事实] Buss says confidence can function as a cue to underlying qualities, though it can be overestimated in narcissism.

[事实] He says people high in psychopathy are often very effective deceivers.

[122:40] **Neuroscience and Evolutionary Psychology**

[事实] Huberman says neuroscience and evolutionary psychology appear to be moving toward each other.

[事实] Buss agrees that evolutionary psychology addresses evolved function and ultimate explanation, while neuroscience can examine the machinery underlying those mechanisms.

[事实] They discuss the possibility of future collaboration, such as studying jealousy with brain imaging.

[123:42] **Books and Closing**

[事实] Buss describes *When Men Behave Badly* as a book about sexual conflict, including deception, infidelity, breakups, intimate partner violence, stalking, harassment, and coercion.

[事实] He describes *The Evolution of Desire* as a broader book about human mating strategies, attraction, mate retention, serial mating, and divorce.

[事实] He describes *Evolutionary Psychology: The New Science of the Mind* as his broad textbook covering mating, survival, kinship, friendship, status, warfare, and other topics.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its systematic framing of mating behavior as a set of evolved strategies, preferences, conflicts, and defenses. It is strongest when Buss distinguishes averages from individual exceptions and separates long-term mating, short-term mating, infidelity, jealousy, and mate retention.

[推测] A major strength is that the conversation does not stay at the level of dating advice. It connects everyday experiences, online dating, jealousy, status, and breakups to research concepts such as mate choice copying, mate value discrepancies, the dark triad, and sexual overperception.

[推测] The main limitation is that many claims are discussed through Buss’s interpretation of evolutionary function, and the transcript does not always provide study details, sample sizes, or competing non-evolutionary explanations. Some parts, especially attachment, polyamory, pornography, and neuroscience integration, are explicitly less central to Buss’s direct expertise or are framed more speculatively.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in evolutionary psychology, relationship dynamics, sex differences, dating behavior, and the darker sides of mating conflict. It may be less comfortable for listeners seeking purely therapeutic relationship guidance or a discussion centered on individual variation rather than population-level patterns.
