# Jason, Medea, and the Making of Greek Mythology

## 概览

This episode uses the 1963 film *Jason and the Argonauts* as an entry point into the story of Jason, Medea, the Golden Fleece, and the ancient literary tradition behind them. The hosts argue that the film, despite its camp qualities, is closer to Apollonius of Rhodes’ *Argonautica* than a casual viewer might assume.

The discussion then turns from adventure story to literary history. Apollonius is presented as a Hellenistic poet writing in the shadow of Homer, Euripides, and older Greek myth, while also reshaping those inherited stories for a new age of Alexander’s successors, Alexandria, scholarly libraries, and global horizons.

The broader conclusion is that Greek myth did not simply survive unchanged. In the Hellenistic and Roman worlds, myth increasingly became mythology: a self-conscious, literary, scholarly way of preserving stories that had once belonged to a living religious imagination.

## 分段落总结

[01:41] **The film as the gateway to Jason’s myth**

[事实] The hosts open with the ending of the 1963 film *Jason and the Argonauts*, where Zeus looks down on Jason, Medea, and the Argonauts after the Golden Fleece has been won.
[事实] The film is described as commercially unsuccessful at release but later admired, especially for Ray Harryhausen’s pre-CGI special effects.
[推测] The film’s mixture of kitsch, spectacle, and myth makes it a useful way into the larger question of how Greek stories are adapted.

[05:21] **Monsters, special effects, and mythical material**

[事实] The film includes Talos, harpies, the Clashing Rocks, and skeleton warriors rising from sown monster teeth.
[事实] The dragon’s-teeth motif resembles the story of Cadmus, but the hosts explain that this is not merely Hollywood borrowing at random.
[事实] The film’s core mythical set pieces are said to come from Apollonius of Rhodes’ *Argonautica*.

[08:12] **Apollonius and the Argonautica**

[事实] The *Argonautica* was written around 250 BC by Apollonius of Rhodes, in the Hellenistic period after Alexander the Great.
[事实] In Apollonius’ version, Jason must plough with fire-breathing bulls and sow dragon’s teeth as a challenge set by King Aetes.
[事实] Medea helps Jason with magic ointment and advises him to use Cadmus’ trick of throwing a stone among the warriors who spring from the teeth.
[推测] Apollonius’ explanation that Athena divided the dragon’s teeth between Cadmus and Aetes shows myth being treated like a shared fictional universe.

[10:42] **A blockbuster prequel to Homer**

[事实] The hosts describe the *Argonautica* as a thrilling quest narrative rather than a work mainly about the nature of the gods.
[事实] Apollonius is compared to writers working inside franchises such as Star Wars or Marvel, reusing familiar characters and plot elements.
[事实] Achilles appears as a baby, and the Argonauts encounter figures and places associated with Odysseus, including the Sirens and Circe.
[推测] Part of the poem’s appeal lies in recognizing older mythic material being rearranged into a new adventure.

[14:23] **Callimachus and the charge of scavenging**

[事实] A tradition says that Callimachus, the celebrated poet of the age, criticized Apollonius and treated epic writing as outdated.
[事实] Callimachus’ alleged nickname for Apollonius was the ibis, a bird associated in Greek writing with scavenging and filth.
[推测] The dispute is framed as a high-culture attack on popular, expansive storytelling, similar to criticism of modern blockbusters.

[17:19] **Older sources behind the Golden Fleece**

[事实] Homer mentions Jason, the Argo, and the Clashing Rocks, though not Medea or the Golden Fleece in the same full form.
[事实] Apollonius drew on earlier poets including Hesiod, Pindar, and Euripides.
[事实] Pindar preserves the story of Phrixus and Helle escaping on a flying golden ram, with Helle falling into the Hellespont and Phrixus reaching Colchis.
[事实] The Golden Fleece becomes a source of prosperity for Aetes’ kingdom and is guarded by a dragon in a sacred grove.

[20:52] **Jason’s call to adventure**

[事实] Jason’s father Aeson has been displaced by Pelias, and Jason is raised by Chiron the centaur.
[事实] Pelias has been warned to beware a man with one sandal, and Jason loses a sandal while carrying Hera across a river.
[事实] Pelias sends Jason to Colchis to retrieve the Golden Fleece, promising to give him the throne if he returns.
[事实] Pelias justifies the quest through the ghost of Phrixus, an oracle, and the belief that the fleece will bring prosperity.

[22:51] **Exploration and the geography of wonder**

[事实] The hosts connect the Jason story with Greek exploration and colonisation around the Black Sea in the eighth to sixth centuries BC.
[事实] By Apollonius’ time, the Mediterranean and Black Sea were much better known, making monsters in those regions harder to imagine literally.
[事实] Apollonius sends the Argonauts on a strange return route through areas such as the Danube, the Rhône, and the western Mediterranean.
[推测] The poem shifts wonder toward less familiar spaces so that marvels and monsters can still feel possible.

[24:35] **Medea as helper and threat**

[事实] In Pindar’s outline, Medea is a crucial heroic helper whose magic enables Jason to win the Golden Fleece.
[事实] Apollonius gives Medea a love wound caused by Eros, leading her to betray her father and help Jason.
[事实] Euripides’ *Medea* had already made her famous as a darker figure associated with killing her brother, Pelias, Jason’s new bride, and her own children.
[推测] Apollonius softens Medea because the full Euripidean version would overwhelm the adventure story he wants to tell.

[30:05] **The Hellenistic setting**

[事实] The episode places Apollonius in a world shaped by Macedonian conquest, Alexander’s empire, and the successor kingdoms after Alexander’s death in 323 BC.
[事实] Alexandria is presented as the major cultural metropolis of the period, unlike Athens or Thebes because it lacked ancient local myths.
[事实] Callimachus came from Cyrene, and Apollonius seems to have been born in Alexandria despite being known as “of Rhodes.”
[推测] The argument over epic is also an argument over what Greek myth means in a new, immigrant, imperial city.

[37:25] **Alexander and divine kingship**

[事实] Alexander is described as deeply devoted to Greek myth, especially Achilles, and as someone who came to think of himself as the son of Zeus.
[事实] He looked for traces of Dionysus during his campaigns and hoped to go beyond Dionysus’ achievements.
[事实] After Alexander, rulers such as Demetrius Poliorcetes and the Ptolemies were treated or styled as divine figures and saviors.
[推测] Hellenistic kingship began to occupy some of the imaginative and ritual space once held by the Olympian gods.

[41:49] **Skepticism about the Olympians**

[事实] Euhemerus’ *Sacred Register* is described as claiming that Zeus and the other Olympians had originally been mortal rulers later worshipped as gods.
[事实] The hosts say philosophical skepticism about traditional Olympian stories became more mainstream in the Hellenistic period.
[推测] The decline of confidence in old myths did not remove supernatural thinking; it encouraged new explanations and new forms of belief.

[44:13] **Epicurus, astrology, and Tyche**

[事实] Epicurus believed in gods, but not in gods who intervened in human affairs or controlled destiny.
[事实] His goal in studying nature was ataraxia, a state of tranquility, rather than modern scientific discovery.
[事实] The planets became identified with Olympian gods such as Hermes, Aphrodite, Ares, and Zeus.
[事实] The hosts describe astrology and the worship or fear of Tyche, fortune, as bleaker Hellenistic alternatives to older divine order.

[52:14] **From myth to mythology**

[事实] Traditional rituals and festivals continued even after the Greek city-states lost independence.
[事实] Apollonius and Callimachus were both connected to the Library of Alexandria, and Apollonius was a Homeric scholar who became chief librarian.
[事实] The hosts define mythology as the study and literary retelling of myths with awareness of their inherited background.
[推测] Apollonius preserves myth not by treating it naively, but by reorganizing it as learned literature.

[55:00] **Why Greek myths endured**

[事实] The hosts connect later mythology to Virgil’s *Aeneid*, Ovid’s *Metamorphoses*, Shakespeare, Titian, *Jason and the Argonauts*, and *Percy Jackson*.
[事实] They argue that Greek myths endure because they were stories and poems, not only ritual practices.
[事实] The episode ends by stressing how difficult it is for modern people to recover a Greek mindset in which gods were primary actors in cities and the cosmos.
[推测] The central challenge is to move beyond later “mythology” and imagine the older world of living myth.

[59:27] **Favourite myths and future threads**

[事实] Tom names the *Odyssey* as his favourite Greek myth and describes it as his gateway into the subject.
[事实] Dominic names Theseus and the Minotaur as his own early favourite.
[事实] The hosts briefly discuss possible future episodes on the *Odyssey*, Crete, Enoch Powell, Emma Hamilton, and Nelson.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it turns a familiar adventure story into a case study in how myths are inherited, revised, criticized, and preserved. The strongest thread is the movement from Jason’s cinematic monsters to Apollonius’ scholarly Hellenistic world.

[推测] Its highlight is the combination of literary history and accessible analogies. The comparisons with film franchises, blockbusters, and fan-recognizable shared universes make Apollonius’ method easier to understand without reducing the ancient material to trivia.

[推测] Its limitation is density: Homer, Pindar, Euripides, Callimachus, Alexander, Euhemerus, Epicurus, astrology, Tyche, and Roman mythology all appear in quick succession. Listeners with little background in Greek myth may need to treat it as a broad map rather than a complete guide.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in Greek myth, ancient literature, Alexander’s legacy, and the question of how religious stories become cultural classics.
