# Hitler’s Olympics, Part 2: Pangloss, Polonius, Prufrock

## 概览

This episode of *Revisionist History* profiles Charles Hitchcock Sherrill, an influential but largely forgotten American diplomat, athlete, and International Olympic Committee member who helped ensure that the United States participated in the 1936 Berlin Olympics.

Malcolm Gladwell and Ben Naddaff-Hafrey portray Sherrill as a privileged, supremely self-confident social climber whose life resembled several literary archetypes: Polonius, Pangloss, and Prufrock’s “attendant lord.” His extensive memoirs and scrapbooks preserved both his comic self-importance and his political failures.

The central historical episode is the IOC’s response to Nazi Germany’s exclusion of Jewish athletes. Sherrill obtained a written German promise that Jews would not be barred from Germany’s Olympic team and celebrated it as a victory for equality. The hosts argue that this apparent triumph concealed a deeper failure: Sherrill trusted assurances from a regime already persecuting Jews and subsequently developed an admiration for Nazism.

## 分段落总结

[00:46] **Introducing Charles Hitchcock Sherrill**

[事实] Gladwell and Naddaff-Hafrey describe Sherrill as extraordinarily self-confident despite abundant reasons to question his abilities.

[事实] Sherrill stood at the center of the American debate over participation in the 1936 Berlin Olympics and is presented as one of the people responsible for the Olympic movement’s endurance.

[事实] During his lifetime, Sherrill moved among presidents, diplomats, and elite social circles, crossed the Atlantic 90 times, and regularly appeared in *The New York Times*.

[推测] The hosts’ affectionate mockery establishes Sherrill as both a comic personality and a consequential example of elite political complacency.

[03:07] **Polonius, Pangloss, Prufrock, and “Mr. B”**

[事实] Gladwell compares Sherrill with the cautious, deferential, and ridiculous attendant lord in T. S. Eliot’s “The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock.”

[事实] Sherrill is also compared with Pangloss from *Candide*, whose optimism treats the existing world as necessarily the best possible one.

[事实] Dorothy Thompson’s “Mr. B” archetype describes a well-connected man who has risen beyond his abilities, adopts the values of his class, and follows whatever movement appears likely to succeed.

[推测] Taken together, these comparisons depict Sherrill as a man whose conventional optimism and attraction to success left him vulnerable to fascism.

[06:04] **An Elite Life Preserved in Memoirs**

[事实] Sherrill grew up in Washington, attended Yale, worked as a Wall Street lawyer, and wrote about 20 books, mostly concerning stained glass.

[事实] His unpublished 360-page memoir catalogued anecdotes, praise, witty remarks, and encounters with prominent people.

[事实] In one memoir episode, President Herbert Hoover ate all the cherries at a White House lunch; Sherrill later used the incident to suggest that Hoover might offer him a different position, possibly contributing to his appointment as ambassador to Turkey.

[推测] The cherry story illustrates Sherrill’s tendency to transform minor social episodes into evidence of his courage, tact, and historical importance.

[09:03] **The Champion Sprinter and the Crouching Start**

[事实] Sherrill was a champion Yale sprinter and briefly shared the world record for the 100-yard dash.

[事实] He repeatedly claimed to have invented the crouching start, while the hosts state that this claim was untrue.

[事实] Sherrill’s relationships with presidents included Theodore Roosevelt, who questioned him at length about the crouching start after meeting Booker T. Washington.

[推测] The episode contrasts the seriousness of racial injustice with the elite fascination surrounding Sherrill’s athletic legend.

[13:13] **The International Olympic Committee as an Aristocratic Club**

[事实] The IOC controlled the rules, events, schedule, invitations, and host locations of the Olympic Games from its base in Lausanne.

[事实] Founder Pierre de Coubertin surrounded himself with aristocratic “trusted men,” and subsequent IOC leaders and members continued this socially exclusive culture.

[事实] The hosts cite IOC figures including Henri de Baillet-Latour, Juan Antonio Samaranch, and Lord Burghley to illustrate the organization’s privilege and detachment.

[事实] Sherrill joined the IOC in 1922, with press coverage praising his athletic expertise and repeating his claim to have originated the crouching start.

[推测] Sherrill’s social connections, athletic reputation, and self-assurance made him an ideal member of an institution operating like an international gentlemen’s club.

[18:18] **Nazism, Antisemitism, and the Berlin Games**

[事实] Hitler’s failed 1923 coup was followed by imprisonment, the writing of *Mein Kampf*, and the Nazi Party’s rapid political ascent.

[事实] After Hitler came to power in 1933, Jewish businesses were boycotted, Jews were assaulted, and Jewish people were excluded from professional bodies and sports clubs.

[事实] Jewish tennis player Daniel Prenn was removed from Germany’s Davis Cup team, while Berlin organizing official Theodor Lewald lost his position because of Jewish ancestry in his family.

[事实] The American Jewish Congress warned that no self-respecting Jew could participate in Germany under those conditions.

[推测] Nazi racial policy made the proposed Berlin Olympics fundamentally incompatible with the Games’ professed ideals of fair competition.

[20:40] **The IOC’s Vienna Dilemma**

[事实] At its June 1933 meeting in Vienna, the IOC considered the conflict between Nazi discrimination and its own definition of sportsmanship.

[事实] The meeting program asked whether a true sportsman would prefer losing to doing anything unfair.

[事实] Sherrill argued that the IOC did not intervene in countries’ internal athletic affairs but insisted that race, religion, and color must not influence Olympic team selection.

[事实] The IOC could relocate or cancel the Games, allow a boycott, or persuade Nazi Germany to change its treatment of Jewish athletes.

[推测] By separating domestic persecution from Olympic team selection, the committee created a narrow procedural solution to a much broader moral crisis.

[24:01] **Sherrill’s Negotiated Guarantee**

[事实] Sherrill told the American Jewish Congress that he would defend the American principle of equality before the law.

[事实] As a leading member of the IOC executive committee, he pressed the German representatives to declare explicitly that German Jews would not be excluded from the national Olympic team.

[事实] The German Interior Ministry supplied a written statement containing that principle, and Sherrill said he personally drafted its crucial paragraph.

[事实] Sherrill later presented the agreement as proof that he had done his best for Jewish people.

[推测] Sherrill regarded personal diplomacy and formal assurances as sufficient safeguards despite the Nazi government’s already visible persecution of Jews.

[26:24] **A Celebrated Victory That Solved Nothing**

[事实] Reporters and newspapers initially treated the Vienna agreement as a major victory, and *The New York Times* praised Sherrill as a hero of racial freedom in sports.

[事实] Sherrill’s answer to the contradiction of holding the Olympics in Nazi Germany was not to stay away but to make the host country conform to Olympic standards.

[事实] The controversy temporarily faded, and plans for the Berlin Games continued.

[事实] The episode states that the apparent solution marked the beginning, rather than the end, of the problem.

[推测] The Vienna declaration allowed Olympic officials and prospective participants to claim a moral victory without confronting the nature of the Nazi regime.

[27:55] **The Scrapbooks and an Unedited Fascist Record**

[事实] Sherrill preserved his life in 44 carefully assembled scrapbooks, later donated to the New-York Historical Society by his wife.

[事实] His wife was an heir to the Berkshire Cotton Manufacturing Company, which later became part of Berkshire Hathaway.

[事实] Because Sherrill died in 1936, he could not revise or sanitize his papers after the crimes of Nazi Germany became fully known.

[事实] The hosts say that Scrapbook 35 displays the full extent of Sherrill’s admiration for the Nazi Party and preview it as the subject of the following episode.

[推测] The archive is unusually valuable because it preserves, without retrospective self-defense, the process by which a prominent American elite embraced Nazism.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s principal strength is its use of humor, literary analogy, and archival detail to make an obscure institutional figure memorable. Sherrill’s cherries, stained-glass books, athletic stories, and immaculate scrapbooks reveal how vanity and social prestige can coexist with genuine historical influence.

Its deeper value lies in connecting personality to political failure. [推测] Sherrill is not presented merely as an overt ideologue; he represents a more ordinary danger—the confident insider who believes elite negotiation, formal promises, and personal charm can neutralize an authoritarian movement.

[推测] The comic framing occasionally risks making Sherrill’s behavior seem less serious than its consequences. However, the shift from amusing self-importance to preserved evidence of Nazi admiration gives the episode its moral force.

The episode is especially suited to listeners interested in Olympic history, American responses to fascism, institutional hypocrisy, and the ways privileged social networks shape political judgment.
