# Metta 青少年安全和解引发第一修正案争议

## 概览

This episode of Marketplace Tech examines Metta’s recent settlement in a landmark teen social media addiction case, focusing less on the child-safety measures themselves and more on their broader implications for online speech and identity verification.

Host Megan McCarty Carino speaks with Will Creeley, legal director at the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression, who argues that the settlement could pressure major platforms toward age or identity checks that courts might not allow if imposed directly by law.

The discussion weighs public support for stricter limits on children’s social media use against concerns that broad verification requirements could weaken anonymous speech, make online dissent harder, and shift decisions about protected expression from families and users toward platforms or government pressure.

## 分段落总结

[00:21] **Metta settlement and proposed teen restrictions**

[事实] Metta agreed to a settlement last month in a landmark case over teen social media addiction.

[事实] The company promised restrictions for young users, including time limits, overnight blackouts, and hidden likes.

[事实] Metta also offered to pay up to $17 billion, partly contingent on TikTok and YouTube adopting similar reforms.

[事实] Surveys cited in the episode show a strong majority of Americans support stricter social media limits for children.

[00:54] **Identity verification and anonymous speech concerns**

[事实] Will Creeley argues that separating children and teenagers from adults would likely require everyone to show identification before accessing major platforms.

[事实] He says this would affect anonymous speech rights on major platforms for expression.

[事实] Creeley argues that Metta’s settlement structure pressures YouTube and TikTok to adopt similar steps.

[推测] His concern is that a settlement by one major company could become an industry-wide norm without going through the same constitutional scrutiny as legislation.

[01:57] **Court challenges to age verification laws**

[事实] The host notes that many reforms like broad age verification have been challenged in court when enacted as state laws.

[事实] Creeley says courts have been consistent that teenagers and children have First Amendment rights.

[事实] He says courts have also protected adults from being required to prove identity before accessing protected speech.

[事实] Creeley cites Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton as a recent Supreme Court case allowing Texas to require identity verification for adult content that would be obscene for minors.

[事实] He emphasizes that the ruling concerned pornography or other adult content, not general access to platforms like Facebook.

[03:39] **Why Creeley calls the settlement an end run around the First Amendment**

[事实] Creeley says that if a state law required Metta to make these changes, Metta would be in a strong position to challenge it on First Amendment grounds.

[事实] He argues that a private platform has its own First Amendment right to curate speech and make editorial decisions.

[事实] He compares platform curation to a newspaper deciding how to write op-eds or choose front-page images.

[事实] Creeley says Metta’s voluntary agreement to settle weakens the First Amendment argument it might otherwise have made.

[推测] The phrase “voluntary” is contested in his view because the agreement was made in the context of litigation pressure.

[05:09] **Public support and the loneliness of the civil-liberties critique**

[事实] The host notes that FIRE, the Electronic Frontier Foundation, and some speech-oriented academics and journalists have criticized the settlement.

[事实] Creeley says many people are cheering because Metta and Big Tech are convenient villains.

[事实] He acknowledges the dangers of excessive screen time and says he has two children.

[事实] He argues that the government should not control choices made by parents, teenagers, and citizens about what speech they access.

[推测] Creeley expects more concern once users understand that ordinary social media access may require showing identification.

[06:45] **Possible alternatives for protecting children online**

[事实] The host asks whether platforms can be made safer for children without creating First Amendment problems.

[事实] Creeley says it is hard to find a one-size-fits-all solution that protects all children.

[事实] He argues there is no substitute for platforms empowering parents and children to make choices about what they see online.

[事实] He says neither Big Tech nor the government can do the job of a parent.

[推测] His preferred approach is more user and family control rather than broad platform mandates or government-backed restrictions.

[07:52] **Risk to broader speech protections**

[事实] Creeley says he worries that concern over social media harms could sour Americans on basic speech protections.

[事实] He says the First Amendment protects the right to say things the government does not want people to hear or that are unpopular with most Americans.

[事实] He argues that making it harder to get online can empower the government to crack down on dissenting speech.

[事实] He also says such restrictions can make it harder for people to connect across difference.

[推测] The episode frames child safety and free expression as a policy conflict where popular protective measures may create broader civil-liberties costs.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] This episode’s main value is that it complicates a broadly popular child-safety proposal by asking what infrastructure is required to enforce it and who else would be affected.

[事实] The strongest throughline is Creeley’s argument that age verification for minors can become identity verification for everyone, with consequences for anonymous speech and dissent.

[推测] The episode is most useful for listeners interested in platform regulation, online child safety, civil liberties, and First Amendment debates. Its limitation is that the discussion centers on one critic’s legal perspective and does not include a direct response from Metta, plaintiffs, parents, or child-safety advocates.
