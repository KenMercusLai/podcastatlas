# The Anschluss: Austria, Hitler, and the First Victim Myth

## 概览

This episode examines the Anschluss of 1938: Germany’s takeover and absorption of Austria. It begins with *The Sound of Music* as a familiar Anglophone myth of Austrian victimhood, then tests that image against the political enthusiasm, coercion, opportunism, and violence described in the transcript.

The hosts argue that Austria’s post-war claim to have been Hitler’s “first victim” is partly true but incomplete. Austria was plundered, repressed, and erased as a state, yet many Austrians welcomed Hitler, and Austrian Nazis and ordinary citizens participated in violence against Jews.

The discussion follows Hitler’s broader road to war: his plans for expansion, the removal of army leaders who might have restrained him, the pressure campaign against Chancellor Schuschnigg, the failed referendum gambit, the German invasion, and the immediate persecution that followed.

## 分段落总结

[00:11] **The Sound of Music and Austria’s victim story**

[事实] The episode opens with “Edelweiss” from *The Sound of Music* and uses the von Trapp family story as a way into the Anschluss.

[事实] The hosts describe the film as one of the most celebrated portrayals of the Anschluss in the Anglophone world.

[事实] They frame the episode around whether Austria’s later self-image as Hitler’s first victim is myth, history, or both.

[03:56] **Hitler’s expansion timetable**

[事实] The hosts return to Hitler’s November 1937 meeting with his generals, where he laid out the need for Lebensraum and future expansion.

[事实] Hitler identified Austria and Czechoslovakia as early targets before further expansion eastward.

[事实] The generals were alarmed less by moral objections than by the risk of war with Britain and France before Germany was ready.

[07:40] **The Blomberg-Fritsch crisis clears the army leadership**

[事实] Field Marshal Blomberg was forced out after scandal surrounding his new wife’s past.

[事实] Colonel General Fritsch was also removed after accusations connected to blackmail, though the hosts say the charge against him was not substantiated.

[事实] Hitler used the moment to remove other generals and promote more compliant figures such as Keitel and Brauchitsch.

[推测] The episode presents this purge as a crucial precondition for the Austrian crisis because it weakened institutional resistance to Hitler’s next move.

[13:02] **Austria, pan-Germanism, and the post-1918 settlement**

[事实] Hitler had long supported the union of German-speaking Austria with Germany.

[事实] After the Habsburg Empire collapsed, Austria was left as a small German-speaking state of about seven million people.

[事实] The Allies blocked Austria from joining Germany and even prevented it from calling itself “German Austria.”

[事实] Austria was politically divided between socialist Vienna, Catholic conservatives, and a smaller Austrian Nazi movement.

[16:11] **Dollfuss, Schuschnigg, and the failed Nazi coup**

[事实] By 1933 Austria had become a Catholic authoritarian state under Engelbert Dollfuss.

[事实] Austrian Nazis, encouraged by Hitler, attempted a coup in 1934 and killed Dollfuss.

[事实] Mussolini opposed German intervention at that time and moved forces toward the Brenner Pass, forcing Hitler to back down.

[事实] Kurt von Schuschnigg succeeded Dollfuss and continued as Austria’s conservative Catholic leader.

[18:37] **Strategic motives and Mussolini’s shift**

[事实] Germany wanted Austrian resources, especially iron ore, for rearmament and the Four Year Plan.

[事实] Goring was eager to gain access to Austrian raw materials and showed Mussolini maps depicting Austria inside the German Reich.

[事实] Mussolini’s position shifted after his break with Britain and France over Abyssinia and his desire for Hitler’s support for Italian ambitions.

[推测] The transcript presents Mussolini’s retreat from defending Austria as a key diplomatic opening for Hitler.

[21:02] **The Berghof meeting intimidates Schuschnigg**

[事实] Franz von Papen arranged a meeting between Schuschnigg and Hitler at the Berghof on 12 February 1938.

[事实] Schuschnigg expected a summit, but Hitler used generals and an ultimatum to intimidate him.

[事实] Hitler demanded fewer restrictions on Austrian Nazis, the appointment of Seyss-Inquart as interior minister, and closer economic integration with Germany.

[事实] Schuschnigg eventually accepted most of the demands under threat of invasion.

[26:30] **Schuschnigg’s referendum gamble**

[事实] On 9 March, Schuschnigg announced a referendum intended to preserve Austrian independence.

[事实] The proposed question favored a “free” and independent Austria, and voting was restricted to those over 24, limiting younger Nazi support.

[事实] Hitler reacted furiously and planned to force a rewritten referendum or use Austrian Nazi unrest as a pretext for invasion.

[推测] The hosts treat Schuschnigg’s referendum as an attempt to use Nazi-style political tactics against the Nazis, but one that badly misjudged Hitler’s response.

[29:24] **Ultimatum, isolation, and Austrian collapse**

[事实] Hitler demanded that Schuschnigg resign and be replaced by Seyss-Inquart.

[事实] Britain, through Lord Halifax, told Austria that it could not guarantee protection.

[事实] Schuschnigg resigned, and Austrian President Wilhelm Miklas eventually accepted Seyss-Inquart.

[事实] Nazi mobs in Vienna smashed Jewish shop windows and occupied government buildings even before the German army fully arrived.

[33:49] **German troops enter Austria**

[事实] The German army crossed the Austrian border early on Saturday, 12 March, after Austria ordered no resistance.

[事实] German troops were met by large crowds throwing flowers and cheering.

[事实] The hosts discuss whether the public enthusiasm reflected Nazi commitment, fear, excitement, or a mixture of these forces.

[推测] The episode suggests many Austrians believed they were joining Germany as partners, not realizing Austria would be fully subordinated.

[35:03] **Hitler’s emotional homecoming**

[事实] Hitler crossed into Austria at Braunau am Inn, his birthplace, and later went to Linz, where crowds greeted him with intense enthusiasm.

[事实] The hosts describe Hitler crying on the balcony in Linz and presenting the moment as fulfillment of his mission.

[事实] The emotional reception changed Hitler’s thinking from some form of union toward complete absorption of Austria into Germany.

[推测] The hosts interpret this as a psychologically powerful moment for Hitler because he returned triumphant to places associated with earlier failure and resentment.

[38:10] **Austria is legally absorbed**

[事实] Lawyers and civil servants drafted the laws incorporating Austria into the Reich in a hotel.

[事实] Austria’s distinct nationhood and institutions were swept away, and the Austrian army swore loyalty to Hitler.

[事实] Hitler visited his parents’ grave and childhood places while the legal absorption was being arranged.

[39:49] **Vienna’s reception and the plebiscite**

[事实] Vienna greeted Hitler with huge crowds, including about a quarter of a million people in the streets.

[事实] The hosts note that some crowd attendance was organized through closed workplaces, but they also cite Kershaw’s view that the enthusiasm was genuine.

[事实] Catholic churches in Vienna flew swastikas on the order of the local cardinal.

[事实] Later plebiscites in Germany and Austria produced official results above 99 percent in favor, amid intimidation.

[41:47] **Plunder and the end of Austria**

[事实] Austria became the Ostmark and later was divided into Reich regions, disappearing from the map as a state.

[事实] Austrian Nazis were often displaced by officials sent from Berlin.

[事实] Austrian institutions, railways, banks, postal services, the army, gold reserves, foreign currency, art, and iron ore were taken over by Germany.

[推测] The hosts present the plunder of Austria as a prototype for later Nazi occupation practices across Europe.

[43:50] **Mass repression and anti-Jewish violence**

[事实] The Nazis took control of Austrian police records and detained 21,000 people in one evening.

[事实] Schuschnigg was imprisoned and later survived the war, eventually becoming a professor in St. Louis and a US citizen.

[事实] The worst repression fell on Austria’s Jews, especially in Vienna, where there were about 200,000 Jews in Austria, mostly living in the capital.

[事实] Jewish-owned businesses were seized, Jewish business leaders were killed, and Jews were forced to scrub political slogans from the streets while crowds abused them.

[47:03] **Austrian participation in persecution**

[事实] The hosts emphasize that many perpetrators of street violence were Austrian Nazis and ordinary Austrians.

[事实] They describe accounts of neighbors and local policemen participating in attacks on Jews.

[事实] Many Jews tried to flee toward the Czech border, where some were robbed, beaten, turned back, or trapped.

[事实] The suicide rate among Jews rose sharply as the scale of the danger became clear.

[48:21] **Eichmann and the machinery of forced emigration**

[事实] The Nazis created a central agency for Jewish emigration after absorbing Austria’s Jewish population.

[事实] Adolf Eichmann was appointed to run this apparatus.

[事实] The hosts describe Eichmann as fanatical, methodical, and bureaucratic.

[推测] The episode treats Eichmann’s work in Austria as an ominous preview of later Nazi administrative persecution during the Second World War.

[49:16] **Hitler’s triumph and the next target**

[事实] Hitler returned to Germany more popular than ever after the Anschluss.

[事实] The hosts say he had fulfilled many goals of German nationalists without provoking war.

[事实] Hitler quickly turned his attention to Czechoslovakia.

[推测] The episode’s central warning is that Hitler’s success in Austria strengthened his belief that further aggression would also succeed.

[50:29] **The von Trapps and the limits of the film myth**

[事实] The hosts return to *The Sound of Music* and say the von Trapps did not escape over the Alps into Switzerland.

[事实] Georg von Trapp was not Jewish and had no travel restrictions, so the family left by train to Italy, then went to London and the United States.

[事实] The hosts contrast their escape with the far more dangerous position faced by Jews trying to leave Austria.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its refusal to give a single simple answer to Austria’s “first victim” narrative. It shows both real victimization by Nazi Germany and real Austrian enthusiasm and participation.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the way it connects high politics, Hitler’s psychology, military leadership, diplomacy, and street-level violence into one sequence. The opening use of *The Sound of Music* gives listeners an accessible cultural reference before the episode moves into much darker material.

[推测] A limitation is that the conversational style sometimes moves quickly through jokes and names, which may make the chronology harder to follow for listeners without prior knowledge of 1930s Austria and Germany.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in the origins of the Second World War, Nazi expansion, Austrian memory, and the early escalation of anti-Jewish persecution.
