# Adam Grant on Procrastination, Motivation, Creativity, and Hidden Potential

## 概览

Andrew Huberman speaks with organizational psychologist Adam Grant about procrastination, creativity, motivation, feedback, blind spots, authenticity, growth mindset, and hidden potential. The conversation emphasizes practical tools grounded in peer-reviewed psychology and neuroscience-adjacent reasoning.

A central thread is that performance improves less from raw talent than from motivation, opportunity, feedback, discomfort, and context. Grant repeatedly argues that people underestimate potential when they judge themselves or others by starting ability.

The episode also frames several counterintuitive tools: moderate procrastination can support creativity, advice can be more useful than feedback, asking others for advice can build their confidence, and being “authentic” is not the same as sharing every thought or impulse.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode Setup**

[事实] Huberman introduces Adam Grant as a professor of organizational psychology at Wharton and author of *Hidden Potential*.

[事实] The episode is framed around tools for goals, procrastination, blind spots, motivation, creativity, confidence, performance, and growth mindset.

[推测] The opening positions Grant as both a researcher and a practical translator of behavioral science.

[02:32] **Procrastination and Deadlines**

[事实] Huberman says deadlines help him focus and create a useful level of arousal.

[事实] Grant distinguishes procrastination from deliberate delay, defining procrastination as delay despite an expected cost.

[事实] Grant says he procrastinates mainly on boring administrative tasks, grading, and calendar responses.

[推测] The conversation reframes procrastination as emotion avoidance rather than simple laziness.

[10:03] **Moderate Procrastination and Creativity**

[事实] Grant describes research with Jihae Shin showing an inverted-U relationship between procrastination and creativity.

[事实] In experimental work, moderate temptation to procrastinate produced more creative ideas than either immediate work or extreme procrastination.

[事实] Grant explains that pre-crastinators may lock onto first ideas too quickly, while chronic procrastinators run out of time.

[推测] Moderate delay appears useful when it allows incubation without eliminating execution time.

[15:01] **Intrinsic Motivation and Incubation**

[事实] Grant says moderate procrastination supports creativity only when the person is intrinsically motivated by the task.

[事实] If a person is bored by the topic, they are less likely to keep it active in the background of the mind.

[事实] Huberman describes finding small hooks in disliked subjects until genuine interest developed.

[推测] Knowing the task early, then delaying commitment, may seed subconscious processing.

[20:02] **Self-Persuasion and Curiosity**

[事实] Grant cites cognitive dissonance research showing that persuading someone else can shift one’s own interest.

[事实] He recommends finding a mystery, puzzle, or curiosity gap inside an unwanted task.

[事实] Huberman defines curiosity as wanting to know without being attached to a specific outcome, and Grant agrees with that framing.

[推测] Curiosity functions as a bridge from external obligation toward intrinsic motivation.

[25:02] **Purpose for Boring Tasks**

[事实] Grant says not every task can be made intrinsically motivating.

[事实] When the process is not interesting, he recommends connecting the task to a meaningful outcome.

[事实] He uses motivational interviewing by asking why motivation is not lower, prompting the person to articulate their own reasons.

[推测] Purpose can substitute for enjoyment when a task itself remains boring.

[27:00] **Extrinsic Rewards**

[事实] Grant says meta-analyses show extrinsic incentives generally increase productivity, especially quantity more than quality.

[事实] He warns that rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation when they make people feel controlled.

[事实] Rewards work better when paired with autonomy and framed as appreciation rather than manipulation.

[推测] Extrinsic rewards are most useful as entry points or signals of value, not as the whole reason for action.

[30:02] **Flow and Attention**

[事实] Grant says intrinsic motivation supports focus, flow, persistence, and clearer thinking.

[事实] Huberman raises concern that social media can pull people out of intrinsically rewarding experiences.

[事实] Grant cites average pre-COVID email checking at 72 times per day and describes this fragmentation as “time confetti.”

[推测] Constant communication can erode both productivity and enjoyment by breaking attention into tiny fragments.

[39:05] **Quiet Time and Chronotypes**

[事实] Grant describes a quiet-time intervention with no meetings or interruptions on certain mornings that improved productivity.

[事实] He suggests deep work may be best protected at the beginning and end of the day, with meetings clustered in the middle.

[事实] Huberman explains that, for many typical schedules, the first eight hours after waking support alertness, attention, and analytic work.

[推测] Work timing should account for chronotype, arousal, and the kind of thinking a task requires.

[45:01] **Creative States and Body-Mind Strategies**

[事实] Huberman describes creative strategies where some people keep the body still and mind active, while others move the body and allow freer thought.

[事实] Grant suggests different people may need to stabilize or activate different parts of the system depending on their default tendencies.

[事实] They discuss the possibility of studying these states with brain imaging and comparing idea output.

[推测] Movement may increase idea volume, while stillness may support filtering and judgment.

[51:02] **Evaluating Ideas**

[事实] Grant asks five to eight trusted people to score early creative work from zero to ten.

[事实] He says multiple reviewers help separate idiosyncratic taste from repeated quality issues.

[事实] Huberman shares a story where negative early feedback on a manuscript did not predict journal rejection.

[推测] Good idea evaluation requires enough independent input to avoid overreacting to one person’s opinion.

[57:03] **Advice Instead of Feedback**

[事实] Grant cites feedback research showing that feedback is most useful when it focuses on the task rather than the self.

[事实] He says asking for “feedback” often produces cheerleading or criticism.

[事实] He recommends asking for advice for next time, because it directs people toward future improvement.

[推测] Advice lowers defensiveness because it frames critique as coaching rather than evaluation.

[60:01] **The Second Score**

[事实] Grant describes Sheila Heen’s “second score,” which means rating how well one receives criticism.

[事实] He shares a story about receiving harsh feedback from Air Force generals and colonels after a motivation class.

[事实] He improved the next session by leading with humility and acknowledging the audience’s skepticism.

[推测] The second score turns criticism from a fixed judgment into a process of recovery and learning.

[66:02] **Growth Mindset and Context**

[事实] Grant says growth mindset cannot be reliably installed through one workshop or one podcast episode.

[事实] He argues that classroom or workplace culture must also support the belief that people can learn and grow.

[事实] He introduces scaffolding as temporary support that helps people reach a new level, then gradually gets removed.

[推测] Mindset matters most when paired with environmental support and concrete learning structures.

[72:06] **Dual Mindset and Job Crafting**

[事实] Grant describes research teaching people that both their skills and their jobs are malleable.

[事实] Participants learned to break jobs into tasks they could accentuate, subtract, or swap.

[事实] The intervention produced a happiness boost lasting at least six months without reducing performance.

[推测] People may need a growth mindset about their environment, not only about themselves.

[75:05] **The Dark Side of Intrinsic Motivation**

[事实] Grant says strong passion for one task can make a subsequent boring task feel worse and hurt performance.

[事实] He changed his own task sequencing by starting with moderately interesting work before moving to highly engaging work.

[事实] Huberman shares an example of a musician doing menial work after performances to make returning home easier.

[推测] Mundane tasks can reset expectations after highly rewarding experiences.

[81:19] **Contrast, Spillover, and Emotional Carryover**

[事实] Grant distinguishes contrast effects from positive spillover between domains.

[事实] He says extreme enjoyment can make later tasks suffer by comparison, while moderate positive experiences may lift later work.

[事实] He cites research suggesting morning workouts can increase confidence at work by creating an early win.

[推测] Small accomplishments may carry emotional momentum into unrelated tasks.

[87:21] **Recovering From Bad Performance**

[事实] Grant says distraction and reframing are two effective emotion regulation strategies.

[事实] His diving coach asked whether he made himself better or made someone else better that day.

[事实] This reframed bad practice from “I failed” to “some improvement or contribution still happened.”

[推测] Reframing performance around progress can reduce negative spirals.

[90:01] **Phone Boundaries and Capturing Ideas**

[事实] Grant keeps a “to-don’t list,” including not scrolling on social media and not picking up his phone after 9 p.m.

[事实] Huberman and Grant discuss notebooks, pens, and the risk of losing ideas if they are not captured.

[事实] Grant mentions research showing better memory for handwritten notes than keyboard notes.

[推测] Low-friction analog capture can preserve ideas without pulling attention into phone distractions.

[93:02] **Blind Spots and Hidden Strengths**

[事实] Grant identifies the bias blind spot as the belief that one is less biased than others.

[事实] He says people with high cognitive ability can be especially vulnerable because they have been reinforced for being good thinkers.

[事实] He describes the reflected best-self exercise, where people ask 10 to 20 others for stories of when they were at their best.

[推测] Other people can reveal both weaknesses and strengths that remain invisible from the inside.

[102:04] **Preacher, Prosecutor, Politician, Scientist**

[事实] Grant says people often think like preachers, prosecutors, or politicians.

[事实] Preachers defend their views, prosecutors attack others’ views, and politicians seek approval from those who already agree.

[事实] He recommends scientist mode, where opinions are hypotheses and decisions are experiments.

[推测] Scientist mode helps people shift from proving they are right to finding out what is true.

[111:03] **Disagreement and Social Media**

[事实] Grant recommends following people one disagrees with when their reasoning process has integrity.

[事实] Huberman argues that follows are not endorsements and says he follows accounts he disagrees with to learn.

[事实] They discuss how social media strips context from scientific interpretation and public statements.

[推测] Thoughtful disagreement can reduce blind spots, but platform signals can easily be misread.

[120:02] **Authenticity and Etiquette**

[事实] Grant says authenticity should not excuse disrespectful behavior.

[事实] He argues that authenticity without boundaries is careless, and authenticity without empathy is selfish.

[事实] He distinguishes authenticity from sincerity, describing sincerity as trying to become the person one claims to be.

[推测] Values may be a better guide than momentary thoughts or emotions when deciding what to say.

[123:02] **Online Judgment and Self-Representation**

[事实] Huberman discusses how a single public statement or post can damage careers or opportunities.

[事实] Grant recommends asking whether one would be proud of a post if it were the only post someone saw.

[事实] Huberman applies classroom rules to online comments, allowing criticism but not attacks on other commenters.

[推测] Digital communication benefits from a pause between emotional impulse and public expression.

[126:05] **Hidden Potential**

[事实] Grant says people underestimate potential by judging starting ability too heavily.

[事实] He argues that motivation and opportunity matter more than raw ability for growth.

[事实] He illustrates this with his diving experience, where he began late and lacked many natural traits but improved substantially with coaching and effort.

[推测] Potential is better measured by rate of growth through obstacles than by early talent.

[135:01] **Character Skills and Imperfectionism**

[事实] Grant names three character skills for realizing potential: embracing discomfort, being a sponge, and being an imperfectionist.

[事实] He says perfectionism is linked to burnout, depression, and anxiety.

[事实] He recommends calibrating standards by task importance, aiming for higher quality where it matters most and accepting “good enough” elsewhere.

[推测] Imperfectionism protects energy for the work that actually deserves excellence.

[141:03] **Success, Risk, and Failure Budget**

[事实] Grant says early success can build confidence but can also create complacency and competency traps.

[事实] He dislikes the phrase “best practices” because it can imply that improvement is finished.

[事实] He created a goal of starting at least one project each year that does not succeed.

[推测] A failure budget encourages experimentation without making failure the goal.

[147:05] **Tag-Team Debate and Difficult Topics**

[事实] Grant describes an idea for a podcast format inspired by professional wrestling tag-team matches.

[事实] The format would bring multiple people into debates on complex issues rather than framing disagreement as one person versus another.

[事实] He names policies for trans athletes in sports and rewriting the constitution as examples of difficult topics.

[推测] Grant is interested less in winning arguments than in creating structures that improve collective thinking.

[153:01] **Art, Magic, and Science Communication**

[事实] Grant discusses research suggesting Nobel Prize-winning scientists are more likely than peers to have artistic hobbies.

[事实] He says magic influenced his science communication because surprise can make people more interested.

[事实] Huberman frames good science communication as rigorous, interesting, clear, actionable, and sometimes surprising.

[推测] Surprise is useful when it invites learning rather than defensiveness.

[162:03] **Self-Relevance and Naming Experience**

[事实] Huberman says people are interested in content that helps them understand themselves and others.

[事实] They discuss emotional granularity and the value of having precise words for specific feelings.

[事实] Grant says affect labeling, or naming feelings, is an effective emotion regulation strategy.

[推测] Language can reduce suffering by making private experiences feel recognizable and shared.

[165:01] **Languishing and Mental Health Language**

[事实] Grant describes languishing as a middle state between depression or burnout and flourishing.

[事实] He says people strongly resonated with the term because it named a feeling of stagnation, emptiness, and lack of purpose.

[事实] Grant argues that scientists give people valuable language for talking about experience.

[推测] Languishing may sometimes signal that change, unlearning, or reorientation is needed.

[168:02] **Neuroscience and Psychology**

[事实] Grant says Huberman changed his view of cognitive neuroscience by showing that mechanisms can help people believe and understand psychological processes.

[事实] Huberman says neuroscience now includes neuroanatomy, neurophysiology, psychology, computational neuroscience, and cognitive neuroscience.

[推测] The exchange presents psychology and neuroscience as complementary ways to study behavior and experience.

[174:02] **Parenting and the Coach Effect**

[事实] Grant says children need to feel that they matter, and mattering includes feeling that they can make a difference.

[事实] He describes asking his daughter for public-speaking advice before a TED Talk, then later helping her reuse her own advice before a school play.

[事实] Grant says research shows people who give advice can become more motivated and confident than people who receive advice.

[推测] Parents can build confidence by letting children contribute guidance instead of only receiving instruction.

[180:03] **Closing Reflections**

[事实] Huberman says the conversation covered procrastination, creativity, motivation, blind spots, authenticity, and potential.

[事实] Grant says he cannot imagine an unsolvable problem.

[事实] The episode ends with mutual appreciation and standard Huberman Lab closing notes.

[推测] The final exchange reinforces the episode’s core theme that growth often comes from reaching beyond one’s current habits, assumptions, and identity.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] This episode is especially valuable for listeners interested in evidence-based self-improvement, learning, creative work, leadership, parenting, and professional development. Its strongest feature is the way it turns abstract psychology concepts into practical questions and behavioral tools.

[事实] The discussion repeatedly ties tools to research examples, including procrastination studies, feedback meta-analysis, growth mindset work, reflected best-self exercises, motivational interviewing, and advice-giving research.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode covers many large topics in one conversation, so some areas receive broad treatment rather than step-by-step implementation detail. Listeners looking for one narrow protocol may need to extract and test the tools selectively.

[推测] The episode is best suited for people who enjoy long-form, research-informed conversations and want to improve how they work, learn, give and receive criticism, handle blind spots, and think about potential.
