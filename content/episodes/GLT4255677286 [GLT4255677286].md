# Boxing Day: Good King Wenceslas and Gilbert & Sullivan’s *Thespis*

## 概览

This episode is the second installment in *The Rest Is History*’s extended “12 Days of Christmas” run, which the hosts jokingly stretch to 13 days because the 12 days can be counted from either Christmas Day or Boxing Day.

The discussion focuses on two Boxing Day-linked, musical subjects. First, the hosts examine the carol “Good King Wenceslas,” its 19th-century lyrics, medieval tune, and the historical Wenceslas of Bohemia, who was remembered as a saintly ruler despite being a duke rather than a king.

The second half turns to Gilbert and Sullivan’s first collaboration, *Thespis, or The Gods Grown Old*, which opened on Boxing Day 1871. The hosts summarize its plot, reception, lost music, and later relevance to Gilbert and Sullivan’s much larger cultural legacy.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **The Christmas Series Setup**

[事实] The hosts introduce this as number two in their Christmas sequence and explain that they are doing 13 episodes rather than 12.
[事实] They say the 12 days of Christmas can be counted from Christmas Day or from Boxing Day, so they are covering both possibilities.
[事实] The previous episode covered Charlemagne’s coronation and Mikhail Gorbachev’s resignation.

[01:06] **“Good King Wenceslas” and Its Origins**

[事实] Tom introduces “Good King Wenceslas” as the first Boxing Day-related topic.
[事实] The lyrics were written in 1853 by John Mason Neale, an Anglo-Catholic Church of England figure interested in saints and Catholic inheritance.
[事实] The tune came from a 13th-century hymn, “Tempus adest floridum,” which the hosts say was associated with Easter rather than Christmas.

[02:31] **The Historical Wenceslas**

[事实] Wenceslas was a 10th-century Bohemian saint and was actually a duke, not a king.
[事实] The hosts place him around the same period as Athelstan, with dates given as 921 to 935.
[事实] He was posthumously treated as a king by Otto the Great after being remembered as stately, holy, and martyred.

[03:43] **Wenceslas as Charitable Saint**

[事实] The hosts describe Wenceslas as a ruler famous for giving aid to widows, orphans, prisoners, and people in hardship.
[事实] They connect this charitable reputation to the carol’s story of Wenceslas helping a poor man gathering winter fuel.
[事实] The miracle in the carol is that the page can follow Wenceslas’s footsteps through the cold and suffer less from the winter.

[05:10] **Boleslaus the Cruel and Wenceslas’s Death**

[事实] Wenceslas had a brother called Boleslaus the Cruel.
[事实] The hosts say Boleslaus was four years younger and that Wenceslas was killed in a coup, possibly by Boleslaus himself with a spear.
[事实] They also mention a tradition that Boleslaus’s son was born at the moment Wenceslas was murdered.

[06:35] **Wenceslas in Czech Memory**

[事实] Boleslaus the Cruel was succeeded by Boleslaus the Pious, creating what the hosts call a dramatic run of Bohemian names.
[事实] Wenceslas is described as a King Arthur-like figure for Bohemians and Czechs.
[事实] The hosts mention legends that Wenceslas sleeps under a mountain or that his statue in Prague’s Wenceslas Square will come alive when needed.
[推测] The hosts use the legend humorously to point out that 20th-century Czech history offered many moments when such a returning hero might have been expected.

[08:32] **Bohemia, Czechia, and Historical Names**

[事实] The hosts discuss the modern use of “Czechia” rather than “the Czech Republic.”
[事实] They say Bohemia cannot simply name the whole country because it also includes Moravia and parts of Silesia.
[推测] The hosts express nostalgia for historical names like Bohemia and Persia because they feel more romantic.

[09:43] **Introducing Gilbert and Sullivan**

[事实] After the break, the hosts introduce a second Boxing Day topic: Gilbert and Sullivan.
[事实] Dominic describes Gilbert and Sullivan as major Victorian light opera figures who became popular in Britain and abroad, especially in America.
[事实] Their first collaboration opened on Boxing Day 1871 at the Gaiety Theatre.

[10:36] **The Creation of *Thespis***

[事实] The first Gilbert and Sullivan collaboration was called *Thespis, or The Gods Grown Old*.
[事实] Gilbert wrote the words, while Sullivan composed the music.
[事实] Sullivan is described as someone who saw himself as a serious composer and sometimes wanted to escape the comic association of Gilbert and Sullivan.

[11:34] **The Plot of *Thespis***

[事实] *Thespis* was advertised as “an entirely original grotesque opera in two acts.”
[事实] The plot involves the gods of Olympus growing old and bored, then swapping places with Thespis and his actors.
[事实] The actors try to rule the universe and do it badly, creating comic disorder.

[12:37] **Comic Role Reversals Among the Gods**

[事实] The substitute for Mars is a pacifist.
[事实] The substitute for Hymen does not want to marry anyone.
[事实] The substitute for Pluto does not want anyone to die or enter the underworld.
[事实] The gods eventually return to Olympus, expel the actors, and send them back to earth as eminent tragedians whom no one goes to see.

[13:44] **Reception and Lost Music**

[事实] *Thespis* ran for 63 performances, which the hosts describe as a success.
[事实] The public found it funny, but critics were much less impressed.
[事实] Reviews complained that the premiere was too long and that the plot and music were weak.
[事实] The hosts say the music is lost, which is why Dominic cannot sing it.

[15:00] **Gilbert and Sullivan’s Later Reputation**

[事实] The hosts mention later Gilbert and Sullivan works including *Trial by Jury*, *The Pirates of Penzance*, and *The Mikado*.
[事实] Dominic recommends Mike Leigh’s film *Topsy-Turvy* as a strong recreation of Victorian England and the Gilbert and Sullivan world.
[事实] They describe Gilbert and Sullivan as gentle satire of institutions and note that phrases from their work entered the language.

[16:45] **The Mikado and Cultural Debate**

[事实] The hosts discuss *The Mikado* and say it is now sometimes viewed through the lens of cultural appropriation because of its Japanese setting.
[事实] Dominic argues that *The Mikado* is not really about Japan but about Britain, using Japan as a satirical disguise.
[推测] The discussion frames Gilbert and Sullivan’s exotic settings as comic devices that can now be read differently by modern audiences.

[18:12] **Closing Recommendation**

[事实] Dominic recommends *Topsy-Turvy* as a good Boxing Day film.
[事实] The hosts close by saying they will return the next day with two events from December 27.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode works as a light festive history piece: it links Boxing Day to music, sainthood, Victorian entertainment, and national memory without becoming too formal or dense.

Its main strength is the contrast between the two subjects. “Good King Wenceslas” leads into medieval Bohemia, martyrdom, and Czech legend, while *Thespis* opens onto Victorian theatre, comic opera, and the later Gilbert and Sullivan phenomenon.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners who enjoy historical anecdotes, cultural afterlives, and comic presenter chemistry more than a tightly argued historical thesis. Its limitation is that some claims are presented conversationally and briefly rather than explored in depth.
