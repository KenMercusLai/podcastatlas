# CZ's Untold Story: The Rise, Fall, and Redemption of Binance's Founder

## 概览

This episode traces CZ’s life from childhood in China and immigration to Canada through McDonald’s, McGill, Tokyo trading systems, Bloomberg, Shanghai entrepreneurship, Bitcoin, Binance, U.S. enforcement, prison, pardon, and his post-Binance projects.

The core arc is that Binance did not appear from a single “genius founder” moment. CZ describes it as a long accumulation of technical work, low-latency trading infrastructure, failed and pivoted startups, crypto community credibility, and a 2017 timing window around ICOs and exchange demand.

The later discussion centers on regulation, law enforcement, FTX, the DOJ case, prison, and CZ’s view that crypto was treated harshly by the Biden administration. The closing sections shift toward Giggle Academy, AI agents using crypto payments, privacy in crypto, his book, parenting, and a broader philosophy that money is only one part of a good life.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Family Migration and Early Canada Years**

[事实] CZ says his father went to Canada in 1984 through an academic exchange before the family later received passports and visas around 1989.
[事实] He says getting a Chinese passport took years, and after Tiananmen, visas became easier while new passports became harder.
[事实] After moving to Vancouver, his father had a low university stipend and his mother, formerly a teacher in China, worked in a sewing factory.
[推测] The opening frames CZ’s story as an immigrant adaptation story rather than a conventional tech-founder origin story.

[04:00] **Work, School, and Self-Sufficiency**

[事实] CZ got his first job at McDonald’s around age 14 or 15 and describes himself as technical but not a “genius coder.”
[事实] He chose McGill after considering Waterloo and UBC, initially studied biology, then switched to computer science after one semester.
[事实] He worked summers and part-time during school, took some family help early, and says he did not take student debt.
[事实] He did not graduate from McGill; later he obtained a degree from an online program to support work visa applications.

[09:00] **Tokyo and Trading Infrastructure**

[事实] CZ’s Tokyo internship involved order execution systems for brokers on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.
[事实] He says this became the same style of software that later mattered for Binance: fast, efficient, low-latency trading systems.
[事实] The discussion covers optimization through in-memory processing, fewer database lookups, simplified risk checks, FPGAs, and colocation.
[推测] This segment establishes the technical foundation that later differentiated Binance’s exchange performance.

[14:00] **M&A Lessons and Bloomberg**

[事实] CZ says the Tokyo company was sold for about $52 million, but the merged culture clashed and a follow-on company failed within a year.
[事实] After 9/11, he moved to New York to work at Bloomberg, joining Tradebook Futures as a senior developer.
[事实] He was promoted several times, moved into management, and led a team that grew to roughly 80 people.
[推测] His early career gave him both engineering depth and a cautionary view of how company culture can break after success.

[17:00] **Shanghai Startup and First Entrepreneurship**

[事实] In 2005, CZ moved to Shanghai with partners to bring Wall Street trading technology to China.
[事实] They discovered that Chinese financial institutions could not work with their wholly foreign-owned enterprise structure.
[事实] The company pivoted into broader IT services and served clients including major auto companies and later financial institutions.
[事实] CZ says the company grew to around 200 people and remained operating after he left in 2013.

[22:00] **Discovering Bitcoin**

[事实] CZ says friends introduced him to Bitcoin in 2013, including Rong Cao and Bobby Lee.
[事实] Bobby Lee argued for putting 10% of net worth into Bitcoin, and CZ spent about six months reading, talking with the community, and trying to understand it.
[事实] CZ attended a small Las Vegas Bitcoin conference in December 2013 where figures like Vitalik Buterin and Charlie Lee were present.
[推测] The contrast between negative media narratives and the “geek” community helped make Bitcoin feel more credible to him.

[30:00] **Going All-In on Crypto**

[事实] CZ says he viewed Bitcoin as one of the fundamental technologies of his lifetime, alongside the internet and later AI.
[事实] He quit his Shanghai company path, sold his Shanghai apartment for just under $1 million, and bought Bitcoin in tranches at an average around $600.
[事实] He joined Blockchain.info as VP of engineering, learned from its remote Bitcoin-paid culture, and saw how grassroots forum marketing could grow a major product.
[事实] He later joined OKCoin as CTO after competing equity offers from BTC China and OKCoin.

[36:00] **From Exchange Idea to Exchange Software**

[事实] After leaving OKCoin, CZ and two developers first considered building a Bitcoin exchange in Japan.
[事实] They created a quick demo using open-source exchange software and market data, while CZ answered investors’ technical questions in depth.
[事实] Investors suggested selling the technology to exchanges instead, and CZ says they quickly signed a contract worth $360,000 with a $180,000 down payment.
[事实] The company pivoted into exchange-as-a-service software and later had around 30 exchange clients.

[40:00] **Entrepreneurship as Iteration**

[事实] Chamath and CZ discuss that Binance’s origin was not a single magical startup moment but a sequence of work, pivots, and accumulated skill.
[事实] CZ says most successful businesses are not like Facebook, Microsoft, or Google’s simplified garage or campus origin stories.
[事实] The exchange software business became a steady SaaS-like model with recurring monthly revenue.
[推测] This section is one of the episode’s main founder lessons: resilience and compounding experience matter more than a clean origin myth.

[42:00] **The Binance Founding Moment**

[事实] In March 2017, the Chinese government shut down many of CZ’s securities-exchange clients, forcing the business to pivot.
[事实] By May 2017, the team decided to build its own crypto-to-crypto exchange using the exchange system they already had.
[事实] After seeing a China ICO raise $15 million with only a document and website, CZ decided Binance should run an ICO instead of relying on VC funding.
[事实] On June 14, 2017, he told the team to write a white paper and launch the ICO process.

[44:00] **BNB ICO and Token Design**

[事实] CZ says about 20,000 people bought into the Binance ICO, and he estimates 80-90% were Chinese buyers.
[事实] Binance sold 60% of the BNB token supply for roughly $15 million, without selling equity.
[事实] The main immediate utility was a 50% trading-fee discount for BNB holders.
[事实] The white paper also described longer-term plans for BNB, including its own chain and decentralized ecosystem.

[47:00] **China Ban and Move to Tokyo**

[事实] On September 4, 2017, Chinese government departments issued a memo banning crypto exchanges in China.
[事实] CZ says Binance still had about 70% of users outside China, so the company could survive by cutting off Chinese users.
[事实] Binance moved the team to Tokyo, and he says nearly everyone moved because most employees were young and had few family constraints.
[事实] Binance’s token price initially fell below ICO price, then recovered after several weeks.

[50:00] **Product Speed, Revenue, and Sudden Wealth**

[事实] CZ says Binance’s order placement was visibly faster than competing platforms like Poloniex and Bittrex.
[事实] He describes surreal moments when revenue numbers in Bitcoin looked too high to be believable.
[事实] Forbes put him on a crypto cover in early 2018, after which people began calling him a billionaire.
[事实] CZ says sudden wealth did not change his habits much because he was older, function-driven, and not interested in luxury signaling.

[54:00] **Money, Personality, and Work Addiction**

[事实] CZ describes money as useful for food, shelter, and comfort, but not as the whole of life.
[事实] He says he lives functionally rather than luxuriously and is not strongly moved by style or status objects.
[事实] He describes himself as calm, emotionally stable, and self-aware about weaknesses.
[事实] He says he was not addicted to growth but was addicted to the work and its sense of fulfillment.

[58:00] **Daily Active Users and Law Enforcement**

[事实] CZ says Binance’s main north-star metric was daily active users, not trading volume or revenue.
[事实] He argues that if more people use a product, the product must be giving them value.
[事实] Around New Year’s 2018, a U.S. Homeland Security contact asked Binance for help tracking funds related to the EtherDelta hack.
[事实] CZ says this made him realize Binance needed people experienced with law enforcement.

[61:00] **Regulatory Pressure and Binance.US**

[事实] CZ says he has legal limits on what he can say about the plea and related issues.
[事实] He characterizes the Biden administration as hostile to crypto and says the new administration changed direction.
[事实] Binance.US was created in 2019 after CZ saw U.S. authorities focusing on crypto companies such as BitMEX and Bitfinex.
[事实] CZ says Binance.US had a separate entity, deployment, matching engine, and equity structure.

[64:00] **FTX, SBF, and Binance’s Exit**

[事实] CZ says Binance invested in FTX for about 20% equity and exited about a year later.
[事实] He first met Sam Bankman-Fried around January 2019, when Alameda was a large Binance trader.
[事实] CZ says he heard SBF was criticizing Binance in Washington and that FTX hired a Binance VIP account manager at much higher pay.
[事实] Binance exited in July 2021, well before FTX’s later collapse, and CZ says he was a passive investor who did not review FTX financials.

[68:00] **FTX Aftermath and U.S. Case Pressure**

[事实] CZ says he does not fully understand the FTX bankruptcy process and is limited in what he can say because of an ongoing clawback lawsuit.
[事实] He says U.S. government information requests to Binance began around 2021-2022 and became more hostile by late 2022.
[事实] By early 2023, he says it became clear that Binance either had to reach a deal or face indictment.
[推测] This section connects industry-wide scandals and enforcement pressure into the period when Binance’s legal exposure became personal for CZ.

[70:00] **DOJ Negotiations and Stress**

[事实] CZ says the legal process involved daily calls with many lawyers for more than a year.
[事实] He found organizing lawyers difficult because they had different specialties, opinions, and incentives.
[事实] He evaluated best-case and worst-case scenarios, including fines, a deferred prosecution agreement, jail, indictment, and restricted travel.
[事实] He says staying in the UAE and not resolving the case would have limited his travel and created pressure on the UAE government.

[76:00] **Charges, KYC/AML, and Rejected Enhancements**

[事实] CZ says his layman understanding was that the first charge was a Bank Secrecy Act registration failure tied to servicing U.S. users without proper registration.
[事实] He distinguishes that from inadequate KYC/AML procedures and from knowingly facilitating bad transactions.
[事实] CZ says Binance agreed there were registration and KYC/AML weaknesses, but he denies personally handling or knowingly facilitating bad transactions.
[事实] He says the court rejected two additional government-requested enhancements.

[80:00] **Plea, Bail, and Sentencing**

[事实] CZ stayed in Seattle for the plea process, with his sister, mother, and older children present.
[事实] A magistrate judge initially allowed him to return to the UAE while awaiting sentencing, but the government appealed and he was kept in the U.S.
[事实] The government later requested a three-month extension, keeping him in the U.S. longer.
[事实] Before sentencing on April 30, 2024, the government requested 36 months, while his side requested probation.

[84:00] **Four-Month Sentence and Prison Risk**

[事实] CZ says the judge said positive things about him, then sentenced him to four months.
[事实] His main fear was not the length of the sentence but personal safety after media coverage framed him as the richest person to enter U.S. prison.
[事实] He consulted people who knew the prison system, including former prison staff and former inmates.
[事实] He says the U.S. prison system is vast and that each prison has its own rules and social order.

[88:00] **Inside Prison**

[事实] CZ says the judge did not require supervision, which later meant no probation or parole.
[事实] The DOJ requested he be remanded immediately, but the judge declined because he was not considered a flight risk or danger to society.
[事实] CZ describes prison intake as involving strip search, assignment to a unit, and entry into a large group of inmates.
[事实] He says prisoners were organized by ethnicity, and he was grouped with a small Asian-related group that included Pacific Islanders and Native Americans.

[92:00] **Release, Return, and Leaving Binance**

[事实] CZ says prison had limited fruit, protein, vegetables, and privacy, and that a normal shower and fruit felt luxurious after release.
[事实] He says he left prison and was on an airplane out within about 26 minutes.
[事实] Emotionally, he says he mainly wanted the matter to be over.
[事实] He says stepping down from Binance was very hard and that he cried, but he later became happy to have more time for other meaningful work.

[96:00] **Pardon**

[事实] CZ describes the pardon process as unclear and says lawyers prepared a petition arguing why he should be pardoned.
[事实] He says his understanding is that the Constitution gives the president broad pardon authority, without much procedural detail.
[事实] He says he did little personally to obtain the pardon.
[事实] CZ speculates that a pro-crypto president who had his own conflicts with the Biden DOJ may have sympathized with his case.

[100:00] **Giggle Academy and Post-Binance Work**

[事实] CZ says he now works on Giggle Academy, consults governments on crypto policy, invests through CZ Labs in blockchain, AI, and biotech, and mentors founders.
[事实] Giggle Academy is described as a free education platform aimed at people without access to schooling or literacy.
[事实] He says the product is intended as an app using gaming, psychology, and AI to deliver education content for free.
[事实] He says he wants to resist issuing a Giggle token because speculation could distort the platform’s educational purpose.

[104:00] **AI Agents and Crypto Payments**

[事实] CZ says AI is the third fundamental technology wave in his life, after the internet and Bitcoin.
[事实] He expects people to have many agents transacting, paying, booking, and moving money in the background.
[事实] He argues banks and card systems will not fit agent commerce because agents cannot meaningfully do traditional KYC, AML, or card workflows.
[事实] He declines to name a specific crypto project for agent payments, saying the area is early and he does not want to move token prices.

[108:00] **Privacy and Crypto**

[事实] CZ agrees that Bitcoin and most cryptocurrencies lack sufficient privacy.
[事实] He says Bitcoin is only pseudo-anonymous because transactions are traceable, especially when centralized exchanges with KYC are involved.
[事实] He argues privacy has real safety uses, such as preventing people from identifying where someone is staying through a payment address.
[事实] He says the industry needs to evolve privacy features, though current privacy-focused coins remain relatively small.

[110:00] **Book, Children, and Legacy**

[事实] CZ says he began drafting a book in prison because he was bored and wanted to stay busy.
[事实] The book is about 95,000 words, roughly 300 pages, and he is editing English and Chinese versions.
[事实] He says the book aims to tell his story, address misconceptions about him, Binance, crypto, and parts of the political context.
[事实] He wants his children to understand the story in more detail and says he wants them to live healthy, happy lives however they define that.

[114:00] **Success, Luck, and Life Beyond Money**

[事实] CZ says he is a normal person and that success does not require being super smart.
[事实] He emphasizes principles, values, emotional quotient, luck, and improving oneself a little each day over decades.
[事实] He says being a billionaire is not everything because money is only one dimension alongside health, family, time, values, contribution, and mental health.
[事实] He says he is grateful not to run Binance anymore because he now has more time, even though running it was enjoyable.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is the unusually long-form founder narrative. It gives listeners a chronological view of how CZ’s immigrant background, low-latency trading work, early Bitcoin conviction, exchange software business, and 2017 ICO window all compounded into Binance.

The strongest parts are the operational details: why Binance’s speed mattered, how BNB was positioned at launch, why the team moved after China’s exchange ban, how CZ thought about DAU, and how he distinguishes registration failures, weak KYC/AML, and intentional facilitation in the legal discussion.

The limitations are clear: many legal, political, prison, FTX, and pardon details are presented from CZ’s perspective, and he repeatedly says there are things he cannot comment on. [推测] Listeners looking for a fully adversarial legal or regulatory account would need additional sources beyond this transcript.

[推测] The episode is best suited for listeners interested in founder psychology, crypto market history, exchange infrastructure, regulatory conflict, and post-founder reinvention rather than a purely technical explanation of blockchain.
