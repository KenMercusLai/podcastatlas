# Cortés, Alvarado, and the Massacre in Tenochtitlan

## 概览

This episode continues the series on the fall of the Aztecs, picking up after Hernán Cortés has left Tenochtitlan to confront Pánfilo de Narváez on the coast. The hosts frame Cortés as a ruthless but undeniably bold risk-taker, while also noting that modern historians sharply disagree about whether he deserves credit for events or merely exploited circumstances.

The central drama is the collapse of Spanish control inside Tenochtitlan while Cortés is away. Pedro de Alvarado, left in command, panics amid rumors of an Aztec plot and orders a massacre during the festival of Toxcatl, turning a tense occupation into open war.

The episode ends with Moctezuma’s death. The hosts stress that the sources are contradictory and politically compromised: Spanish accounts present him as killed by his own people, while some Indigenous-linked accounts suggest the Spaniards murdered him after he ceased to be useful.

## 分段落总结

[00:33] **Opening With The Temple Massacre**

[事实] The episode opens with a dramatic description of dancers and drummers in the temple square of Tenochtitlan before Spaniards close the exits and attack.
[事实] The scene is set in 1520 in the center of Tenochtitlan, the city that would later become Mexico City.
[推测] The opening passage is used to foreshadow the episode’s central event: Alvarado’s massacre during a major Mexica festival.

[01:34] **Recap Of Cortés’s Campaign So Far**

[事实] Cortés was born around 1485 in Medellín, Spain, and sought fortune in the Caribbean before leading an expedition toward Mexico.
[事实] He disobeyed orders from Diego Velázquez, moved inland, allied with Tlaxcala, entered Tenochtitlan, and eventually took Moctezuma hostage.
[事实] Cortés then divided his force, taking about 80 men and Malinche to the coast while leaving around 120 men in Tenochtitlan under Pedro de Alvarado.
[推测] The hosts present this decision as an enormous gamble that reveals Cortés’s appetite for risk.

[05:17] **Debating Cortés’s Character**

[事实] The hosts discuss Matthew Restall’s interpretation of Cortés as neither heroic nor satanic, but mediocre, manipulative, and lucky.
[事实] They disagree with the idea that Cortés was merely a mediocrity, arguing that his gambles and courage are hard to dismiss.
[推测] The episode treats Cortés as morally appalling but strategically formidable, leaving a fuller judgment of his character for later in the series.

[07:06] **Narváez Arrives On The Coast**

[事实] Pánfilo de Narváez arrives on the coast on behalf of Diego Velázquez, who is furious that Cortés has exceeded his orders and sent gold toward Spain.
[事实] Velázquez cannot go himself because Cuba is suffering from a smallpox epidemic.
[事实] Narváez brings around a thousand men and establishes himself at Zempoala after Gonzalo de Sandoval holds Veracruz for Cortés.
[推测] Narváez’s numerical advantage makes Cortés’s decision to confront him especially audacious.

[11:52] **Cortés Defeats Narváez**

[事实] Cortés sends messages, gold, and jewels to men in Narváez’s camp, weakening their loyalty.
[事实] He attacks by night, reducing Narváez’s numerical advantage, and wins while losing only two men.
[事实] Narváez is captured after being wounded in the eye, and his men largely join Cortés.
[推测] The victory transforms Cortés’s position by giving him more than a thousand Spaniards, including gunners and crossbowmen.

[16:57] **Bad News From Tenochtitlan**

[事实] Messengers from Tenochtitlan tell Cortés that the capital has erupted, some Spaniards are dead, and the rest are besieged in the palace.
[事实] Cortés returns toward Tenochtitlan over about three weeks.
[事实] Bernal Díaz later recalled that the Spaniards sensed growing hostility on the road back, with none of the previous marks of respect.
[推测] The silence and hostility suggest that Spanish authority has collapsed even before Cortés reaches the city.

[18:32] **Cortés Re-enters The City**

[事实] Cortés and his men cross the causeway and reach the palace without being attacked.
[事实] The hosts note that this is mysterious and admit they do not know why the Mexica did not ambush him.
[推测] Possible explanations include intimidation, broken command structures after Moctezuma’s capture, or a deliberate decision to let Cortés enter and trap him inside.

[21:07] **Alvarado’s Personality And Misjudgment**

[事实] Pedro de Alvarado is described as brave, flamboyant, charming, unreliable, and a direct contemporary of Cortés.
[事实] The Mexica call him Tonatiuh, “the sun,” because of his blond hair and demeanor.
[事实] Cortés had left Alvarado in command during a period of extreme uncertainty, with Moctezuma’s status unclear.
[推测] The hosts imply that Alvarado was badly suited to command such a tense hostage situation.

[24:01] **Fear Inside The Spanish Position**

[事实] The Spaniards become increasingly anxious as servants stop arriving, food supplies become uncertain, and they fear attack if they go to the market.
[事实] Rumors circulate that the Mexica may kill the Spaniards during the festival or rescue Moctezuma from the palace.
[事实] Malinche is away with Cortés, so Alvarado and his men lack their key translator.
[推测] Tlaxcalan informants may have intensified Spanish fear, either sincerely or to keep the Spaniards hostile to the Mexica.

[26:31] **The Festival Of Toxcatl**

[事实] Tenochtitlan is preparing for the feast of Toxcatl, involving warriors dancing before an effigy of Huitzilopochtli.
[事实] The festival was supposed to culminate in the sacrifice of a victim who would break a flute before being killed.
[事实] Alvarado concludes that the Spaniards must strike first, comparing the situation to earlier violence at Cholula.
[推测] Alvarado appears to act from fear, but his decision turns fear into catastrophe.

[28:42] **The Massacre In The Temple Courtyard**

[事实] An Indigenous account in the Florentine Codex describes Spaniards closing the exits, entering the courtyard, and killing unarmed dancers.
[事实] The account says the Spaniards used metal swords and lances, severed a drummer’s hands, and killed people as they tried to flee.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that hundreds of men, women, and children were killed.
[推测] The massacre is presented as grimly ironic because Spaniards horrified by sacrifice spill far more blood than the festival itself would have required.

[31:19] **The City Turns On The Spaniards**

[事实] People outside the square call for Mexica warriors to arm themselves, and the gates eventually give way.
[事实] Armed Mexica attack, forcing Alvarado and his men to retreat back to the palace.
[事实] Some additional prisoners, apparently nobles or members of Moctezuma’s circle, are killed inside the palace.
[推测] The massacre destroys any remaining possibility of a stable accommodation between the Spaniards and the city.

[33:03] **Moctezuma As Hostage**

[事实] Moctezuma is still alive after the massacre but is now clearly a Spanish hostage.
[事实] The Spaniards bring Moctezuma and Itzquauhtzin onto the roof to urge the crowd to put down its weapons.
[事实] The hosts stress that every source for these events has an agenda, whether Spanish, Franciscan-mediated Indigenous, pro-Moctezuma, or anti-Mexica.
[推测] Moctezuma’s authority has likely been badly damaged by his captivity and his perceived accommodation with the Spaniards.

[36:44] **Cortés Returns Into A Trap**

[事实] Cortés hears Alvarado’s account and finds himself trapped in the same palace.
[事实] Cortés appears to lose confidence in Moctezuma, believing he has been communicating with Narváez.
[事实] The Mexica begin breaking or blocking the causeways to prevent the Spaniards from escaping.
[推测] Releasing Moctezuma’s brother Cuitláhuac to seek supplies backfires because he instead encourages resistance.

[38:20] **The Spaniards Besieged**

[事实] The Spaniards have little food or fresh water and are crammed inside the palace with many more men than before.
[事实] Bernal Díaz later claimed that the Spaniards feared sacrifice, dismemberment, and cannibalism if captured.
[事实] Stories circulate of omens and terrifying visions, including headless figures.
[推测] These accounts reveal Spanish fear and projection more reliably than they reveal actual Mexica plans.

[40:23] **Moctezuma’s Final Appearance**

[事实] Cortés has Moctezuma brought onto the roof again to address the crowd.
[事实] The crowd initially falls silent when it sees the emperor, then resumes throwing missiles.
[事实] Spanish accounts say Moctezuma was struck by a stone during this scene.
[推测] It is plausible that some in the crowd saw Moctezuma as having betrayed them to the strangers from the sea.

[41:31] **Competing Accounts Of Moctezuma’s Death**

[事实] Matthew Restall is cited as listing several possible explanations: accidental projectile injury, deliberate killing by the crowd, suicide-like exposure, involvement by Cuauhtémoc, or murder by the Spaniards.
[事实] Bernal Díaz claims Moctezuma died after refusing treatment and that Cortés and the Spaniards mourned him.
[事实] Diego Durán’s account, based on Nahuatl sources, says the Mexica later found Moctezuma chained, with dagger wounds in his chest.
[推测] The hosts find the Spanish mourning narrative improbable and regard Spanish murder as plausible because Moctezuma had ceased to be useful as a hostage.

[45:20] **The Road To The Noche Triste**

[事实] The episode ends by arguing that once Moctezuma is dead, Cortés has little choice but to escape Tenochtitlan.
[事实] The next episode is set up as the story of the Noche Triste, the “sad night.”
[推测] Killing or losing Moctezuma marks a decisive break: negotiation has failed, and survival now depends on flight.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strength lies in its combination of narrative momentum and source criticism. It tells the massacre and Moctezuma’s death as gripping events while repeatedly reminding listeners that the surviving accounts are partial, self-serving, and sometimes contradictory.

The most valuable theme is the collapse of interpretation on both sides: Spaniards misunderstand ritual, rumor, and political signals, while the Mexica court is fractured by the unprecedented captivity of its ruler. The result is not portrayed as inevitable, but as a chain of fear, opportunism, violence, and failed judgment.

Its limitation is also built into the material: the hosts often have to say that the precise sequence of events cannot be known. That uncertainty is handled openly, but listeners wanting a single definitive account of Moctezuma’s death will not get one.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in conquest narratives, colonial violence, and the problem of reconstructing history from compromised sources.
