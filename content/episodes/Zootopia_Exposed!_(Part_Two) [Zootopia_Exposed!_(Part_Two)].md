# Zootopia Exposed! (Part Two): The Perfect Literary Crime

## 概览

The episode investigates whether *Zootopia 2* deliberately comments on Gary Goldman’s failed claim that Disney appropriated his original idea for *Zootopia*. Malcolm Gladwell and his guests point to the sequel’s plot: a snake named Gary seeks proof that his family invented Zootopia’s defining technology, only for wealthy lynxes to steal the credit.

The discussion identifies apparent Disney references embedded throughout the film, including Lynxley Manor presented like the Disney castle, a *Ratatouille* visual gag, bootleg Disney movies, and a weather reporter named Bob Tiger. These details lead the speakers to interpret the sequel as an unusually public acknowledgment of Goldman’s grievance rather than a collection of coincidences.

The episode then traces screenwriter Jared Bush’s family history. His father, Lester Bush Jr., challenged the Mormon Church’s historical justification for excluding Black members from the priesthood; other relatives also fought institutional exclusion. The podcast proposes that *Zootopia 2* continues this family tradition by restoring reptiles to the animal community and symbolically recognizing Goldman.

## 分段落总结

[01:56] **A Professional Close Reading of Zootopia 2**

[事实] Film scholar Ben Nadler-Hafer attended an afternoon screening of *Zootopia 2*, took notes, and approached the movie as an object of close analysis.

[事实] He enjoyed the sequel, although he preferred the first *Zootopia*.

[事实] After watching the film, he contacted Gladwell with what he believed was a visual clue to its underlying meaning.

[04:37] **The Gary Goldman Controversy**

[事实] Gladwell recaps the first episode’s account: Gary Goldman said he pitched Disney a story about an animal kingdom called Zootopia, Disney passed, and the company later released a film with the same name and a similar aspirational premise.

[事实] Goldman sued Disney but lost after a seven-year legal battle.

[事实] In *Zootopia 2*, Gary De’Snake says his family created the weather-wall technology that made Zootopia possible, while the wealthy Lynxley family stole their patents.

[推测] Gladwell interprets the lynxes as “corporate fat cats” and the plot as a conspicuous parallel to Goldman’s dispute with Disney.

[06:34] **Lynxley Manor as the Disney Castle**

[事实] An aerial view of Lynxley Manor shows fireworks over the building in a composition that Ben and Gladwell compare to the Disney castle logo shown before Disney films.

[事实] Lynxley Manor belongs to the family accused within the story of stealing Gary De’Snake’s family invention.

[推测] The speakers read the visual quotation as identifying Disney itself with the powerful Lynxleys.

[推测] They argue that presenting the villains’ home through Disney’s iconic visual language is strikingly out of character for a corporation protective of its wholesome image.

[08:40] **A Trail of Disney Self-References**

[事实] During an escape through Lynxley Manor’s kitchen, the film includes a visual reference to *Ratatouille*.

[事实] A Zootopia weather reporter is named Bob Tiger and is voiced by Disney CEO Bob Iger.

[事实] Another scene features a weasel selling bootleg versions of Disney films while joking about sequels, prequels, and the state of the film industry.

[推测] Taken together, these details reinforce the speakers’ belief that the film is intentionally commenting on Disney rather than merely using isolated inside jokes.

[12:24] **Why the References Are Treated as Deliberate**

[事实] Gladwell acknowledges that obsessive critics can discover meanings filmmakers never intended, using interpretations of an Apollo 11 sweater in *The Shining* as an example.

[事实] *Zootopia 2* also contains a maze sequence resembling the climax of *The Shining*.

[事实] Gladwell argues that animation requires years of planned work and that every frame must be deliberately created and executed.

[推测] He concludes that the repeated references to Disney, *Ratatouille*, Bob Iger, and the Disney castle are too coordinated to be accidental.

[14:25] **“It Matters to Him”**

[事实] Near the film’s climax, Paubert Lynxley tells Gary De’Snake that nothing he does matters; Judy Hopps replies that it matters to Gary.

[事实] The scene occurs after the Lynxleys have been exposed and brought to justice.

[推测] Ben and Gladwell interpret Judy’s response as the screenwriter addressing Gary Goldman: the legal outcome will not change, but his experience and contribution can still be acknowledged.

[推测] They describe the moment as an explicit act of recognition embedded in the film, even though it cannot provide Goldman with formal legal vindication.

[16:22] **Public Acknowledgment, Not a Hidden Message**

[事实] Filmmakers Brit Marling and Zal Batmanglij join the discussion after watching *Zootopia 2*.

[事实] They argue that the film’s apparent message is not hidden between the lines but expressed directly through its plot and dialogue.

[事实] The episode notes that Disney’s legal team would normally review a script for rights issues, defamation, and similarities to real people.

[推测] The guests suggest that a creative group inside Disney managed to pass an anti-Disney argument through the company’s own approval process.

[推测] They characterize the movie as an acknowledgment addressed not only to Goldman but to the wider creative community.

[19:21] **The “Perfect Literary Crime” Theory**

[事实] The speakers collectively call their interpretation the “perfect literary crime.”

[推测] Their theory is that Disney creatives regarded the treatment of Goldman as a moral wrong and encoded an act of revenge or restitution in *Zootopia 2*.

[推测] The “crime” would be a violation of the creators’ implied loyalty to Disney, while its commercial and artistic success would protect them from punishment.

[推测] Under this reading, the film uses Disney’s resources to deliver a message about authorship, creative ownership, and respect for originators.

[21:43] **Lester Bush Jr. Challenges Mormon History**

[事实] Lester Bush Jr., the father of Jared Bush, was a physician, a former CIA medical director, and a Mormon troubled by his church’s exclusion of Black members from the priesthood.

[事实] After eight years of archival research, Lester found documented cases of Black men ordained during Joseph Smith’s leadership.

[事实] His research concluded that the ban originated under Brigham Young rather than as a founding revelation from Joseph Smith.

[事实] Lester published his findings in 1973, reframing the restriction as a historically contingent policy influenced by American racism rather than immutable doctrine.

[24:41] **Institutional Change and Its Personal Cost**

[事实] The Mormon Church rescinded its restriction on Black priesthood ordination five years after Lester’s article appeared.

[事实] The episode says his research provided justification for that change.

[事实] Some church leaders attempted to block his essay, and Lester was subsequently shunned; he and his children eventually withdrew from active participation in the church.

[事实] Late in his life, the church recognized his contribution through an honorary lecture and a private expression of gratitude from a senior official.

[推测] His experience illustrates how institutions may adopt a dissenter’s conclusions while delaying recognition of the person who made change possible.

[27:13] **A Family Tradition of Defending Outsiders**

[事实] Lester Bush Sr. worked to establish credit unions for Black Americans excluded from the financial system in the American South.

[事实] Lester Jr.’s brother Larry was expelled from Brigham Young University for being gay and later became a gay activist in San Francisco.

[事实] Across three family stories, members of the Bush family confronted institutions that excluded people on racial or sexual grounds.

[推测] Gladwell presents these stories as a family inheritance of moral resistance, redemptive gestures, and bringing outsiders back into the community.

[28:39] **Jared Bush and the Reptiles’ Return**

[事实] Jared Bush, Lester Bush Jr.’s eldest son, leads animation at Disney and wrote *Zootopia 2*, as well as working on *Zootopia*, *Moana*, and *Encanto*.

[事实] *Zootopia 2* centers on reptiles who have been excluded from the animal world and their restoration to Zootopia.

[事实] Greg Prince says related concerns appear in *Encanto*, including its emphasis on extended family and a protagonist who wears glasses.

[推测] Gladwell sees a parallel between Lester’s effort to bring an excluded group fully into the Mormon Church and Jared’s story about restoring an excluded group to the animal kingdom.

[31:08] **Social Justice as a Personal Theme**

[事实] Gladwell cites a pre-release article reporting that Jared Bush became emotional while discussing the social-justice subtext of *Zootopia 2*.

[推测] Gladwell believes the emotional connection makes greater sense in light of the Bush family’s history of confronting exclusion.

[推测] He suggests that Gary De’Snake embodies both the restoration of a marginalized community and recognition of an allegedly wronged creator.

[31:50] **Gary Goldman Finds a Measure of Peace**

[事实] Goldman’s wife, Judy, researched Jared Bush’s family history and connected it to the themes of *Zootopia 2*.

[事实] Goldman says this discovery helped him understand the origin of Gary De’Snake and gave him peace.

[事实] Goldman sent Jared Bush a handwritten letter but received no response.

[推测] Goldman interprets the film as the Bush family standing up for the Goldman family and imagines Jared Bush’s actions as courageous.

[推测] Gladwell closes by praising Bush for creating a film that appears to offer Goldman symbolic recognition, even without changing the legal outcome.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest feature is its layered close reading. It connects visual composition, character names, dialogue, corporate references, and family history into a coherent argument about authorship and institutional accountability.

[推测] Its larger value lies in showing how commercial entertainment can carry moral and autobiographical themes. The history of Lester Bush Jr. gives the film analysis emotional weight and expands the discussion from a copyright dispute into a broader examination of exclusion, recognition, and institutional change.

[推测] The central claim remains circumstantial. The transcript provides no direct confirmation from Jared Bush that Gary De’Snake represents Gary Goldman or that the sequel was conceived as an internal critique of Disney. The speakers acknowledge the danger of overinterpretation, but their confidence sometimes exceeds the evidence presented.

[推测] The episode is especially suited to listeners interested in film criticism, intellectual-property disputes, corporate culture, and the ways family history may shape creative work.
