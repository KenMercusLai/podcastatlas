# Andy Stumpf on Influence, Discipline, Risk, and Mental Health

## 概览

Andrew Huberman speaks with retired Navy SEAL Andy Stumpf about tools from Stumpf’s book *Drownproof*, especially the distinction between what occupies attention and what a person can actually influence. The episode repeatedly returns to one core idea: agency is built through small, repeated choices that are slightly harder than the default option.

The conversation moves from social media, alcohol, cold exposure, wingsuiting, and jiu-jitsu into more personal territory: divorce, parenting, pain, suicide, trauma, and life after elite military service. Stumpf emphasizes that extreme professional backgrounds do not make people immune to ordinary human suffering.

The practical conclusion is not a rigid protocol but a pattern: sort concern from influence, do small disciplined acts when nobody sees them, talk openly about pain, maintain connection with others, and define “enough” before success extracts too high a price.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode Setup and Main Themes**

[事实] Huberman introduces Andy Stumpf as a retired Navy SEAL, former Red Bull high-performance wingsuiter, world-record-setting wingsuit pilot, and author of *Drownproof*.

[事实] Huberman says the discussion was prompted less by Stumpf’s military or wingsuiting career than by the tools and life lessons in the book.

[事实] The episode is framed around concern versus influence, taking the slightly harder road, personal hardship, suicide, and mental health.

[04:30] **Concern Versus Influence**

[事实] Stumpf describes a paper exercise: draw a line down the middle, list concerns on one side and areas of influence on the other.

[事实] He says the concern side is usually much larger, while direct influence ultimately comes back to oneself: thoughts, speech, planning, time management, and responses.

[事实] Huberman says he has been using the exercise weekly and found it improved his sense of agency.

[推测] The tool works by converting diffuse worry into a concrete sorting process that directs attention back toward action.

[10:00] **Social Media and the Expansion of Concern**

[事实] Huberman argues that social media makes people constantly aware of past classmates, distant acquaintances, and global problems, expanding the “concern” column.

[事实] Stumpf describes a screen-time challenge with Chad Wright in which he reduced phone use dramatically for a period by moving social media use to a laptop.

[事实] Stumpf says his mental health improved during that low-phone-use period, but he later returned to thumb-scrolling.

[推测] The discussion presents social platforms as unusually sticky because users can recognize the behavior is unhealthy while still continuing it.

[20:00] **Social Media as a Low-Resolution Addiction**

[事实] Huberman proposes that social media may be a “perfect addiction” because it does not fully intoxicate the user; people can half-monitor themselves while continuing to scroll.

[事实] Stumpf says two of his children have developed more cautious relationships with social media, while his daughter remains heavily attached to it.

[事实] Huberman compares youth resistance to social media manipulation with anti-smoking campaigns that became effective when they framed tobacco companies as manipulative.

[推测] The conversation suggests that recognizing manipulation may activate young people’s desire for autonomy.

[27:00] **Alcohol, Cannabis, and Formative Risk**

[事实] Stumpf says his children’s generation appears to be pushing back against alcohol consumption compared with the culture he grew up in.

[事实] Huberman asks whether avoiding alcohol entirely might cause young people to miss some social or formative experiences.

[事实] Huberman expresses concern about cannabis, especially in young males with vulnerability to psychosis or bipolar disorder.

[推测] Both speakers treat risk not as something to eliminate entirely, but as something to understand honestly before choosing.

[32:00] **Sleep, Cold Exposure, and Stress Training**

[事实] Huberman says there can be value in learning what it feels like to perform when under-rested or emotionally strained, even though sleep optimization is important.

[事实] He describes cold exposure as a reliable way to create an adrenaline spike and learn how to work with a highly activated state.

[事实] Stumpf jokes that his preferred cold plunge temperature would be around 80 degrees Fahrenheit.

[推测] Cold exposure is presented as a safer analogue for practicing composure under stress than high-consequence risk activities.

[36:30] **Wingsuiting and Base Jumping Mechanics**

[事实] Stumpf explains that a wingsuit turns the human body into a nylon wing with ram-air inflation, arm wings, and a leg wing.

[事实] He distinguishes skydiving, which uses a main and reserve parachute, from base jumping, which usually has only one parachute because of low altitude.

[事实] He says wingsuit base jumping is especially dangerous during the first few seconds after a zero-airspeed exit from a fixed object.

[推测] The technical explanation makes clear that the sport’s danger comes from narrow margins, not just from thrill-seeking.

[46:00] **Why Wingsuiting Felt Like a Mental Reset**

[事实] Stumpf says wingsuiting and base jumping gave him a mental reset similar to the narrowed focus he experienced before military operations.

[事实] He describes the most powerful headspace as focusing only on the next few seconds of life.

[事实] He says multi-day jumping trips could leave him calmer, clearer, and more patient for months afterward.

[推测] The “reset” appears tied to deep focus and time perception more than to simply chasing adrenaline.

[56:30] **Risk, Dunning-Kruger, and Getting Away With It**

[事实] Stumpf says people in high-risk sports can confuse survival with competence: “Did you nail it or did you get away with it?”

[事实] He links some fatalities to the Dunning-Kruger phase where a person believes they have mastered risks they do not fully understand.

[事实] He says he would not stop his children from pursuing wingsuiting as adults, but would confront them with the reality of the risks and required preparation.

[推测] The lesson extends beyond wingsuiting: early success can hide unrecognized danger in many domains.

[62:45] **Divorce as the Hardest Experience**

[事实] Stumpf says a nearly two-year contentious divorce was the hardest thing he has ever gone through, harder than military selection or operations.

[事实] He avoids detailed criticism of his ex-wife because he has a public platform and she does not.

[事实] He says he used every tool in the book during that period, including concern versus influence, shortening time frames, and managing self-talk.

[推测] The discussion challenges the assumption that elite military hardship is automatically harder than personal and relational suffering.

[66:45] **Vulnerability and Not Being Alone**

[事实] Stumpf says he included personal struggles because people mistakenly assume former SEALs are not normal people with normal problems.

[事实] He says many listeners write to him saying they feel alone or do not know how to start toward their goals.

[事实] He rejects selling a perfect image or a simplistic paid program as the answer.

[推测] His credibility in the conversation comes partly from refusing to separate toughness from vulnerability.

[70:15] **Parenting, Estrangement, and Reconnection**

[事实] Stumpf says he lost contact with his oldest son for 18 months after initiating the end of his first marriage.

[事实] He says that experience made him question whether he was good enough to keep going in a way military experiences had not.

[事实] He says their relationship is now closer than ever, and he treats his children asking for advice as something worth stopping for.

[推测] Staying available without forcing the relationship is implied as part of how reconnection became possible.

[79:45] **Flow, Time Perception, and Post-Flow Clarity**

[事实] Stumpf says wingsuiting did not leave him chronically adrenalized; it left him feeling settled and able to think clearly.

[事实] Huberman connects this to flow states and asks whether intense focus recalibrates time perception.

[事实] Huberman mentions research suggesting stress can impair the ability to make insightful links between memories.

[推测] The speakers are exploring a possible “post-flow” benefit without claiming to know the mechanism.

[91:00] **Toilet Paper and Everyday Discipline**

[事实] Stumpf uses his children’s habit of stacking new toilet paper rolls beside empty ones as an example of shortcuts that create more work later.

[事实] He says “it always takes longer to do it wrong,” applying the point to laundry, dishes, and small household tasks.

[事实] He connects the idea to making the bed and doing small acts of discipline early in the day.

[推测] The toilet paper example functions as a mundane but memorable symbol for how small neglected tasks accumulate.

[94:45] **Choosing the Slightly Harder Option**

[事实] Stumpf says every decision has a slightly easier and a slightly harder choice.

[事实] He recommends choosing the slightly harder option more often, even when the act is microscopic.

[事实] Huberman connects this idea to small daily actions that may compound into major behavioral change.

[推测] The episode’s central behavioral prescription is incremental discipline rather than dramatic transformation.

[102:30] **Anterior Mid-Cingulate Cortex and Tenacity**

[事实] Huberman discusses the anterior mid-cingulate cortex and says it grows when people do things they do not want to do, such as difficult dieting or disliked exercise.

[事实] He says the effect depends on doing something aversive in the moment, not merely doing a hard thing one already enjoys.

[事实] He links the region to perseverance, successful dieting, and “superagers.”

[推测] Huberman presents this brain area as a possible biological basis for the value of small acts of will.

[106:45] **Jiu-Jitsu and the Value of the Unmasterable**

[事实] Stumpf says what he loves about jiu-jitsu is that it cannot be mastered.

[事实] He says highly experienced black belts often become more aware of how much they do not know.

[事实] He links ongoing learning to staying mentally young.

[推测] Jiu-jitsu serves in Stumpf’s life as a lower-consequence way to access focus, humility, and continuous growth.

[110:00] **Pain, Openness, and Emergency Surgery**

[事实] Stumpf recounts ignoring severe abdominal pain, rolling jiu-jitsu, and later being taken to a hospital by his wife.

[事实] He says he had an intestinal blockage that required emergency surgery.

[事实] He describes learning that being more open about pain helped him receive better care.

[推测] The story argues against mistaking silence about pain for strength.

[122:00] **Suicide, Isolation, and the Case of Dave**

[事实] Huberman and Stumpf discuss rising suicide and the difficulty of understanding why highly accomplished people take their own lives.

[事实] Stumpf says the Green Beret community has lost more people to suicide than to combat operations since 2001.

[事实] He describes Dave as a top performer whose internal self-view appeared far worse than how others saw him.

[事实] Stumpf says alcohol, isolation, and an inability to live up to his own standard were part of Dave’s situation as he understands it.

[推测] The conversation suggests that status and competence do not protect people from shame, isolation, or distorted self-perception.

[137:00] **Pre-Military Trauma and Checking In**

[事实] Stumpf says many men he served with carried significant trauma before joining the military.

[事实] He notes that identity loss, isolation, unhealthy alcohol use, and moving away from a peer group can compound after leaving service.

[事实] Huberman describes the value of regular check-ins among friends and says small morning messages can matter.

[推测] The speakers imply that suicide prevention may require sustained connection, not only crisis intervention.

[149:55] **A Practical Daily Program Without Hacks**

[事实] Stumpf resists a rigid prescription but suggests starting the day with a disciplined act such as making the bed or drinking water before coffee.

[事实] He recommends preparing small things in advance, moving physical exertion earlier when possible, and sweating or getting close to sweating once a day.

[事实] He says people generally know which option is harder and which is easier; the work is choosing the harder one more often.

[推测] The practical advice is deliberately flexible so it can fit different lives and constraints.

[155:30] **Life After the Teams and Saying No**

[事实] Stumpf says he now owns a Black Rifle Coffee shop in Montana, hosts a podcast, practices jiu-jitsu, travels with his wife, and is now an author.

[事实] He says none of his current roles would have been on his list when he left the SEAL teams.

[事实] He says he is most excited by not knowing what comes next and trusting that he has tools to handle it.

[推测] His post-military story frames uncertainty as less threatening once a person has built adaptable processes.

[161:30] **The Price of Success and Defining Enough**

[事实] Stumpf says people may need some experience before they can understand the price of pursuing a major goal.

[事实] He would rather see someone fall slightly short of a massive lifetime goal while remaining fulfilled than achieve it after sacrificing family, relationships, and life experiences.

[事实] Huberman and Stumpf discuss money as something that can buffer certain stresses but cannot buy real connection or solve every form of helplessness.

[推测] The episode closes with a warning that “more” can become self-defeating when it is never translated into enough.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strongest value is its unusual range: practical self-management, extreme-risk psychology, family hardship, and suicide are treated as connected parts of one conversation about agency and attention. Stumpf’s examples make abstract ideas concrete without turning them into a rigid self-help formula.

The highlight is the repeated return to small actions: concern versus influence, replacing toilet paper, making the bed, drinking water before coffee, putting the cup in the dishwasher, and choosing the slightly harder option. These examples make the episode useful even for listeners who have no interest in military life or extreme sports.

[推测] Its main limitation is that some of the deepest topics, especially suicide and the post-flow state, remain exploratory rather than conclusive. The speakers acknowledge this uncertainty, which makes the conversation more honest but less prescriptive.

[推测] The episode is best suited for listeners interested in discipline, resilience, parenting, elite performance, mental health, and practical behavior change, especially those who prefer grounded stories over polished motivational slogans.
