# Vikings in the East: Rus, Silver, and the Road to Baghdad

## 概览

This episode examines the Vikings who travelled east from Scandinavia into the river systems of what became Russia and Ukraine. The hosts frame the topic through Vladimir Putin’s 2021 essay on ancient Rus’, noting that the origins of Rus’ remain politically charged because Russia, Ukraine and Belarus all claim parts of that inheritance.

The main argument is that the eastern Viking story was less like coastal raiding in Britain and more like a river-based commercial system built around trade, forts, portage, furs, silver and slavery. The hosts stress that these routes connected Scandinavia to the wealth of the Abbasid Caliphate and Byzantium.

A large part of the episode focuses on evidence from archaeology, linguistics and written sources, especially Arabic accounts such as Ibn Fadlan’s description of Rus’ traders and a violent pagan funeral on the Volga. The episode ends by setting up a second part on Kiev, Constantinople and the Christianisation of the Kievan Rus’.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Putin, Rus’ and the Politics of Origin**

[事实] The episode opens in early February 2022, amid escalating tensions around Russia and Ukraine.

[事实] The hosts discuss Vladimir Putin’s essay, which traced Russians, Ukrainians and Belarusians back to ancient Rus’ and emphasized language, economic ties, the Rurik dynasty and Orthodox Christianity.

[事实] Tom notes that Putin’s account does not foreground Scandinavian origins, despite the hosts’ view that Vikings were central to the early Rus’ story.

[推测] The omission is presented as politically meaningful because Scandinavian origins complicate modern nationalist claims over Rus’.

[02:10] **Why Scandinavian Influence Became Controversial**

[事实] Dominic explains that Viking involvement in the origins of Rus’ has long been controversial, especially in Soviet historiography.

[事实] The hosts say some Soviet-era arguments minimized Scandinavian influence and emphasized Slavic traditions instead.

[事实] They connect this resistance partly to earlier Russian tensions with Sweden and later to Nazi-era Germanic racial claims about Slavs.

[推测] The controversy shows how medieval history can become a proxy for modern arguments about national legitimacy and cultural hierarchy.

[04:28] **The East as the Other Viking Frontier**

[事实] The hosts contrast the familiar western Viking story of Lindisfarne, England, France, Iceland and Greenland with the less familiar eastern story.

[事实] Dominic says some historians argue Viking activity in the West copied patterns that had already begun in the East.

[事实] The eastern route is described as more lucrative, with access to Baghdad, Constantinople and vast supplies of silver.

[推测] The episode presents the eastern Viking world as essential to understanding both early Rus’ and wider Viking expansion.

[06:21] **Harald Hardrada and the Eastern Career Path**

[事实] Harald Hardrada is used as an example of how eastern service shaped Viking careers.

[事实] The hosts say he went to Kiev, then Constantinople, and commanded the imperial bodyguard.

[事实] They add that he may also have gone to Sicily and the Holy Land.

[推测] His life is treated as a compact illustration of the mobility and ambition made possible by the eastern routes.

[08:02] **Why Vikings Went East**

[事实] A listener asks whether eastern Viking movement was expedition or conquest.

[事实] The hosts explain that geography mattered: Swedes looked east across the Baltic, while Norwegians were more naturally oriented toward the North Sea.

[事实] Gotland is described as a staging post rich in hoards of Arabic dirhams.

[事实] The main commodities identified are furs and slaves.

[推测] The hosts suggest that profit, not territorial conquest, was the main initial driver.

[10:41] **Birka, Ladoga and the River Network**

[事实] Birka, near modern Stockholm, is described as one of the closest things to a town in early ninth-century Scandinavia.

[事实] From Swedish centers, Vikings could move through the Gulf of Finland toward river systems leading deep into Eurasia.

[事实] Staraya Ladoga is described as an early settlement founded around the mid-eighth century.

[推测] The hosts compare the atmosphere to frontier trading posts, suggesting a rough, mobile and commercially driven world.

[12:37] **Rus’, Rowing and Portage**

[事实] A listener notes that Finnish Ruotsi, Rus’, Roslagen and related terms may derive from a root associated with rowing.

[事实] The hosts describe Viking ships as shallow and light enough to navigate rivers and be carried between waterways.

[事实] Portage is emphasized as central to eastern travel, with slaves sometimes carrying ships or cargo.

[推测] The “business model” is described as brutally efficient because enslaved people were both cargo and labor.

[14:28] **Forts, Novgorod and Trade Stations**

[事实] Forts emerged at key points along rivers, lakes and portage routes.

[事实] Novgorod is described as meaning “new fort,” and the Norse name Holmgard is mentioned.

[事实] The hosts argue that the eastern Viking presence was not mainly about occupying land, but about controlling routes and making money.

[事实] These trading stations already involved both Scandinavians and Slavs.

[推测] This mixed environment is presented as one of the ingredients from which Rus’ society developed.

[16:47] **Routes to Constantinople and Baghdad**

[事实] Novgorod is described as a meeting point linking Scandinavia, the Dnieper and the Volga.

[事实] The Dnieper led toward the Black Sea and Constantinople, while the Volga led toward the Caspian and Baghdad.

[事实] Smolensk and Gnezdovo are discussed as important points on the western route.

[事实] The hosts mention that Vikings even attacked Azerbaijan at one point.

[18:00] **Constantine VII and the Dnieper Rapids**

[事实] The Byzantine emperor Constantine VII Porphyrogenitus wrote a foreign policy guide for his son that describes the Rus’ routes.

[事实] He describes Rus’ boats and flotillas travelling through what is now Ukraine.

[事实] The Dnieper was navigable only in summer, and travellers had to pass dangerous rapids with names such as “the Drinker” and “the Yeller.”

[推测] Byzantine interest in these routes shows that Rus’ activity mattered to imperial diplomacy and security.

[19:20] **Pechenegs and Khazars**

[事实] The hosts identify the Pechenegs as nomadic groups who posed a threat along the southern routes.

[事实] The Khazars are described as a Turkic people acting as a buffer between Slavs and the Caliphate.

[事实] The Khazar elite converted to Judaism, which the hosts present as historically remarkable.

[推测] Their choice of Judaism is interpreted as a third path between Christian Byzantium and the Muslim Caliphate.

[21:33] **Silver, Slaves and the Caliphate**

[事实] The hosts say the early Rus’ were initially more focused on the Caliphate than Byzantium because of silver.

[事实] Large quantities of Arabic silver coins found in Scandinavia are explained through eastern trade.

[事实] Baghdad’s demand for slaves is described as a major part of the trade, with Slavs especially central to the origin of the word “slave.”

[事实] The hosts cite the argument that early medieval Europe’s main export to the richer Islamic world was enslaved people.

[24:45] **Eastern Silver and Western Viking Raids**

[事实] The hosts argue that Viking activity in the East and West should be understood together.

[事实] They say silver mines in Central Asia expanded eastern opportunities in the late ninth and early tenth centuries.

[事实] When eastern silver declined after about 965, Viking attention shifted more strongly back toward England.

[推测] This suggests that the timing of Viking attacks in England was partly shaped by economic conditions far to the east.

[26:23] **Interlude on Charles I and the Members’ Podcast**

[事实] After the break, the hosts discuss strong listener reactions to recent episodes on the trial and execution of Charles I.

[事实] They mention criticism from Cappell Loft and say he would appear on a members’ episode to debate their treatment of Charles I.

[推测] This section is a promotional and conversational interlude rather than part of the main historical argument.

[30:09] **Vikings and Islam**

[事实] A listener asks whether Viking-Arab contact was a clash of civilizations or trade-based contact, and whether Islam influenced the decline of Norse polytheism.

[事实] Dominic says the contacts were more about trade, fascination and exchange than a clash of civilizations.

[事实] Tom notes finds in Sweden including a woman’s ring with an invocation of Allah and textiles with Quranic phrases.

[推测] The hosts do not claim these finds prove conversion to Islam; they treat them as evidence of contact, prestige goods or possible movement of people.

[32:27] **Ibn Fadlan Meets the Rus’**

[事实] Ibn Fadlan was sent by the Abbasid caliph al-Muqtadir to the Volga Bulgars as an envoy and missionary.

[事实] He encountered Vikings on the Volga and described them as tall, blonde and ruddy, but also filthy by his standards.

[事实] The hosts say Ibn Fadlan viewed them as impressive but barbarous.

[推测] His account is valuable because it gives written context to archaeological evidence that otherwise only preserves material remains.

[34:15] **A Pagan Ship Funeral on the Volga**

[事实] Ibn Fadlan witnessed the funeral of a Rus’ chief, which the hosts describe as openly pagan and recognisably Scandinavian.

[事实] The funeral took ten days and was overseen by a woman called the Angel of Death.

[事实] The dead man’s wealth was divided between alcohol, funeral equipment and heirs.

[事实] A female slave was selected to accompany the dead chief into the afterlife.

[推测] The ritual is presented as evidence that eastern Rus’ traders could remain culturally Scandinavian even far from Scandinavia.

[37:20] **The Violence of the Funeral Ritual**

[事实] The hosts describe the selected slave girl being given status, clothes, jewels, drink and drugs before the final ritual.

[事实] Animals including a dog and chickens were killed and placed in or near the ship.

[事实] The girl was eventually forced into the ship’s cabin, where men restrained and strangled her while the Angel of Death stabbed her.

[推测] The hosts imply that Ibn Fadlan’s horror makes the ritual feel morally alien to both Islamic and modern Christian-influenced sensibilities.

[40:06] **The Darker Side of Norse Religion**

[事实] Dominic says modern children’s versions of Norse mythology often present a much more cheerful version than the historical evidence suggests.

[事实] Tom argues that Norse gods and rituals could be terrifying, violent and sexually charged.

[事实] The hosts connect Ibn Fadlan’s account to archaeological finds of dismembered animals and human remains in burials.

[推测] The episode uses the funeral to challenge romantic or sanitized versions of Viking culture.

[41:38] **Conversion and the Next Episode**

[事实] Tom argues that conversion to Christianity moved Vikings out of a world that now seems extremely strange and remote.

[事实] The hosts connect this to Putin’s opening reference to the baptism of Rus’.

[事实] They say the next episode will cover the founding of Kiev, Rus’ relations with Constantinople and the Christianisation of Kievan Rus’.

[推测] The ending frames conversion as a central turning point in the history of Rus’ identity.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strongest value lies in connecting a medieval trade and migration story to present-day political arguments over Rus’, Russia and Ukraine. It avoids treating the Vikings simply as raiders and instead emphasizes logistics, economics, river geography and the slave trade.

A major highlight is the use of different kinds of evidence: coin hoards, place names, Byzantine policy writing, Arabic travel accounts and archaeology. Ibn Fadlan’s account gives the episode a vivid centerpiece, though the funeral section is intentionally disturbing.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in early medieval Europe, Viking history, Russian and Ukrainian origins, or the relationship between historical memory and modern politics. Its limitation is that it is the first half of a larger discussion, so Kiev, Constantinople and Christianisation are mostly deferred to the next episode.
