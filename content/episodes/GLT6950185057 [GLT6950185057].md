# Captain Cook and the Prime Directive: Tahiti, New Zealand and Australia

## 概览

This episode follows Captain James Cook’s first voyage after the Endeavour leaves Tierra del Fuego, reaches Tahiti, observes the transit of Venus, and then moves on to New Zealand and Australia under sealed Admiralty instructions.

The hosts frame Cook through a Star Trek comparison: the “prime directive” of non-interference resembles the Royal Society’s instructions to observe without causing harm, but the voyage repeatedly shows how impossible neutrality becomes once trade, sex, disease, theft, violence, and imperial claims enter the encounter.

The central argument is that Cook is neither presented as a simple colonial villain nor as an innocent explorer. The discussion stresses his discipline, scientific curiosity, and growing respect for Pacific peoples, while also emphasizing the violence, condescension, British claims of possession, and long-term colonial consequences of the voyage.

## 分段落总结

[01:33] **Star Trek as a frame for Cook**

[事实] The hosts begin with Star Trek because Gene Roddenberry is said to have admired Captain Cook, with Captain Kirk modeled on Cook, the Enterprise on the Endeavour, and Spock on Joseph Banks.

[事实] They compare Star Trek’s “prime directive” against interference with alien civilizations to the moral and practical dilemmas facing Cook’s crew.

[推测] The opening uses science fiction to make Cook’s voyage feel like a first-contact story, where curiosity and power are inseparable.

[03:29] **Mission to Tahiti and secret imperial instructions**

[事实] Cook’s official mission is to establish an astronomical post in Tahiti to observe the transit of Venus.

[事实] He also carries sealed instructions to claim the fabled Terra Australis for Great Britain if he finds it.

[事实] The hosts say British urgency is shaped by fear that European rivals such as France or Spain might get there first.

[事实] After leaving Tierra del Fuego, the Endeavour spends months at sea in cramped and sometimes stormy conditions.

[05:07] **Tahiti: Arcadia, trade, sex, and disease**

[事实] The Endeavour reaches Tahiti on 11 April 1769, and some aboard initially wonder whether it might be part of the Great Southern Continent.

[事实] Joseph Banks describes Tahiti in Arcadian terms, but the hosts stress that Tahiti already has its own history and had recently been visited by other European ships.

[事实] Samuel Wallis had warned Cook about trade, especially Tahitian demand for iron and nails, and about the risk of quarrels and theft.

[事实] The hosts discuss the sexual economy around sailors, Tahitian women, and iron, and say Cook is horrified by the risk of Europeans introducing venereal disease.

[推测] Cook’s refusal to take a Tahitian lover may reflect marital loyalty, fear of disease transmission, captainly self-discipline, or some combination of those motives.

[08:56] **Cook’s discipline and the impossibility of neutral observation**

[事实] Cook is described as a man for whom self-control, discipline, and the authority of captaincy matter enormously.

[事实] The Royal Society’s instructions tell him not to cause trouble or interfere, but the hosts argue that simply arriving and observing already changes societies.

[事实] Early interactions include stolen items from Solander and Monkhouse, and later a Tahitian attempt to steal a musket ends with Marines shooting the culprit dead.

[事实] Banks and Sydney Parkinson both express regret about the violence, while the hosts note European condescension and the recurring use of superior firepower.

[12:20] **Banks in Tahiti: loss, surfing, tattoos, and interpretation**

[事实] Banks’s artist Alexander Buchan dies after an epileptic fit, and Banks mourns the loss partly because it prevents him from recording scenes for friends in England.

[事实] Banks observes surfing in Tahiti and is described as transfixed by it.

[事实] A double-canoe encounter leads Banks to interpret a woman’s display as sexual, while Nicholas Thomas is cited for a different interpretation: she may have been showing body markings as credentials for trade.

[事实] The Tahitian word “tatao” is transcribed by Banks as “tattoo,” helping introduce the word into English and later European usage.

[推测] The episode uses Banks to show both genuine curiosity and a tendency to misread Pacific customs through European assumptions.

[16:58] **Transit of Venus and the problem of theft**

[事实] On 3 June, the transit of Venus is observed successfully, and the measurements are treated as a scientific success.

[事实] Cook then discovers that his own men have broken into ship stores and stolen 120 pounds of nails to trade with Tahitians.

[事实] Cook publicly flogs a butcher who threatens a Tahitian woman and also threatens to burn canoes when items such as a rake and water cask are stolen.

[事实] Banks worries about Cook’s hard line toward Tahitians, while the hosts say Cook is balancing discipline, diplomacy, and the risk of escalation.

[19:10] **Tupia and the ambiguity of first contact**

[事实] The hosts emphasize that encounters between Europeans and Tahitians are not only damaging or traumatic, but also invigorating for curious people on both sides.

[事实] Banks studies rituals, traditions, language, and Tahitian-English vocabulary.

[事实] On 12 July, Banks records that Tupia wants to travel with the Endeavour, while Banks says he might keep him “as a curiosity.”

[推测] The hosts treat Banks’s remark as troubling, but also stress that Tupia has agency and may see the Endeavour itself as the curiosity.

[23:50] **From Tahiti to New Zealand**

[事实] After the break, the hosts say Cook has completed the Tahiti phase and opens his sealed Admiralty instructions.

[事实] The instructions tell him to search for Terra Australis Incognita and, if he fails, to locate and explore New Zealand’s coast.

[事实] Abel Tasman had already encountered New Zealand in 1642, but had been discouraged from landing because of concern about Māori.

[事实] Banks marks the anniversary of departure from England with Cheshire cheese and porter on 25 August.

[26:08] **First sight and first violence in New Zealand**

[事实] The Endeavour sights New Zealand on 7 October 1769, and the land is named Young Nick’s Head after Nicholas Young.

[事实] They land at a place they call Poverty Bay and encounter Māori, who initially run away.

[事实] A confrontation near the boats leads to the killing of Tay Mauro, described as the first Māori to die at European hands.

[事实] Cook is said to be upset by the killings and anxious to repair relations.

[推测] Cook’s admiration for Māori independence and heroism becomes part of the hosts’ broader argument that his thinking develops beyond simple European superiority.

[28:36] **Māori encounters, Tupia’s translation, and Polynesian navigation**

[事实] The next day, about 100 Māori perform a weapon-brandishing dance and chant that the hosts compare to the All Blacks’ pre-match ritual.

[事实] Tupia serves as a translator, which helps the Europeans communicate with Māori.

[事实] The linguistic link between Tahiti and New Zealand reveals to Cook that Polynesians had crossed immense ocean distances before Europeans.

[事实] Cook tells Māori through Tupia that the British are friends and want water and trade, but also warns that they could easily kill them if insulted.

[推测] The translation episodes sharpen Cook’s respect for Polynesian seafaring and show the contradiction between friendship and coercive threat.

[30:36] **Failed diplomacy and remorse**

[事实] A Māori man steals a sword, the British fire to disperse the group, and another Māori is killed.

[事实] Cook’s men later try to capture a Māori canoe to prove friendly intentions, but they kill four people in the attempt.

[事实] Banks calls this the most disagreeable day of his life.

[事实] As the Endeavour charts the New Zealand coast, the crew encounters evidence of cannibalism, and Banks buys a dried human head.

[事实] Cook writes that, despite cannibalism, Māori are naturally of good disposition and have humanity.

[34:42] **Claiming and charting New Zealand**

[事实] Cook takes possession of New Zealand for the Crown near Queen Charlotte Sound by building a cairn, hoisting the Union flag, and drinking Queen Charlotte’s health.

[事实] An old Māori man is the only local witness to the ceremony and is given the empty bottle.

[事实] The Endeavour circumnavigates both the North Island and South Island by March 1770.

[事实] The crew then prepares to sail west toward New Holland.

[36:16] **First sight of Australia and Botany Bay**

[事实] The Endeavour leaves New Zealand on 31 March 1770, after about two years away from England.

[事实] On 19 April 1770, Zachary Hicks spots the eastern coastline of New Holland.

[事实] Cook later calls the area New South Wales, and the hosts say it is unclear why “South Wales” was chosen.

[事实] At Botany Bay on 28 April 1770, the crew sees Aboriginal people fishing and living on the coast, but they show little interest in the ship.

[事实] Cook and Banks are bewildered by Aboriginal Australians appearing unmoved by the Endeavour.

[40:14] **Attempts to communicate in Australia**

[事实] Isaac Smith, Cook’s wife’s nephew, is allowed to be the first man ashore.

[事实] The British try to communicate by offering beads and nails, but Aboriginal men throw rocks and spears.

[事实] Cook responds with muskets or small shot, and the British collect spears and artifacts.

[事实] Banks becomes interested in local fauna and initially finds tracks that are probably from a dingo.

[41:06] **Great Barrier Reef, kangaroo, and Aboriginal life**

[事实] The Endeavour runs into the Great Barrier Reef, tears a hole in the ship, and comes close to disaster before repairs.

[事实] During repairs, relations with Aboriginal peoples improve, and the hosts say this may be the first recorded European learning of an Aboriginal person’s name, Iaparico, if understood correctly.

[事实] Cook and Banks ask about a strange animal, record the answer as “kangaroo,” and Nicholas Thomas is cited to support the accuracy of the word.

[事实] Banks also sees a dingo and a flying fox.

[事实] Cook describes Aboriginal Australians with curiosity and admiration, saying they may be happier than Europeans because they are not troubled by European wants or inequality.

[45:02] **Return to Britain and the honored goat**

[事实] The Endeavour leaves the northern tip of Australia around 20-22 August, then goes to Batavia/Jakarta, where malaria is rife, and later Cape Town, where dysentery strikes.

[事实] The hosts say about 30 or 40 men die of disease late in the voyage, despite Cook having looked after his men well.

[事实] The ship spots Land’s End on 10 July 1771 and anchors off Deal on 13 July.

[事实] Cook is received with praise, dines with Benjamin Franklin and James Boswell, and is presented to the king by Lord Sandwich.

[事实] The goat that had circumnavigated the world twice receives a pension, Greenwich Hospital privileges, and a silver collar; its name is unknown.

[48:37] **Cook’s legacy and the colonial question**

[事实] The hosts describe Cook’s first voyage as legendary because it marks the first English glimpse of, and landing on, Australia and New Zealand.

[事实] They say the voyage has incalculable long-term demographic and geopolitical consequences and explains why Cook is seen by some as an embodiment of colonialism.

[事实] Cook raises the British flag and claims Australia and New Zealand for the Crown, but the hosts argue he is not like Cortés, not greedy or rapacious, and does not plant colonies or lead armed expeditions inland.

[事实] The hosts say Joseph Banks, not Cook, later proposes Botany Bay as a convict settlement and advises British colonization policy in Australia.

[推测] Their final portrait is of Cook as a disciplined Enlightenment figure whose experience of Pacific peoples begins to challenge the limits of European systems of thought.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its refusal to make Cook either a flat hero or a flat villain. It keeps the violence, imperial claims, and later colonial consequences in view while also taking seriously Cook’s curiosity, restraint, and changing perception of other societies.

[推测] Its strongest moments come from pairing vivid anecdotes with larger historical questions: Star Trek as a first-contact metaphor, Banks’s Tahitian misunderstandings, Tupia’s agency, Māori resistance, Aboriginal indifference, and the strange afterlife of the kangaroo and the goat.

[推测] The limitation is that the conversation depends heavily on European journals and later European interpretation; Aboriginal, Māori, and Tahitian perspectives appear mostly through what Cook, Banks, and the hosts can reconstruct.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in exploration history, empire, Enlightenment science, Pacific encounters, and the moral ambiguity of historical figures whose actions were both personally complex and historically consequential.
