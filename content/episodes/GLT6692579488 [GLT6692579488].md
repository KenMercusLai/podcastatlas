# Captain Cook: Exploration, Enlightenment and the Road to Hawaii

## 概览

James Cook’s death in Hawaii in 1779 became a European sensation because of the extraordinary scale of his achievements. Across two completed Pacific voyages, he mapped coastlines with unprecedented precision, crossed the Antarctic Circle, disproved the existence of the imagined southern continent Terra Australis and transformed European understanding of the Pacific.

The episode presents Cook as a complicated figure rather than simply a humane scientist or an agent of colonial violence. His orders stressed friendship, restraint and recognition of Indigenous ownership, and he often criticised his own crew’s conduct. Yet his expeditions also claimed territory, used kidnapping and coercion, and caused deadly encounters with Māori and other Pacific peoples.

A sharp change emerged during Cook’s third voyage. Exhausted, ill and under pressure after years at sea, he became increasingly impulsive and punitive. Severe floggings, mutilations and the destruction of homes and canoes replaced much of his earlier restraint. The episode ends in January 1778 with his unexpected arrival in Hawaii, setting up the circumstances of his eventual death.

## 分段落总结

[01:50] **The death and global reputation of Captain Cook**

[事实] Surgeon David Samwell’s account describes Cook being clubbed and stabbed to death by Hawaiians at Kealakekua Bay on 14 February 1779.

[事实] Cook’s death generated poems, paintings, plays and theatrical reenactments across Europe; George III reportedly wept when he heard the news.

[事实] Cook was celebrated as one of history’s greatest sailors because his voyages fundamentally reshaped European maps of the Pacific.

[推测] The immense public reaction reflected not only the violence of his death but the sense that Europe had lost the leading explorer of its age.

[06:06] **Cook’s achievements and contested colonial legacy**

[事实] Cook circumnavigated the world twice, mapped coastlines including Newfoundland and Alaska, charted New Zealand, crossed the Antarctic Circle and became the first known European captain to reach eastern Australia and Hawaii.

[事实] For centuries he was presented as a humane and respectable face of British expansion, but his monuments have more recently become targets of protest.

[事实] The hosts argue that Cook saw himself primarily as a scientific explorer and received explicit Admiralty instructions to avoid attacking Indigenous peoples.

[推测] Cook cannot be separated from Britain’s imperial project, but treating him as equivalent to conquistadors such as Cortés or Pizarro obscures significant differences in purpose, policy and conduct.

[09:44] **From Yorkshire apprentice to scientific navigator**

[事实] Cook was born in North Yorkshire in 1728, became a merchant-seaman apprentice and voluntarily joined the Royal Navy in 1755.

[事实] During the Seven Years’ War, he developed exceptional skills in navigation and surveying, producing accurate charts of the St Lawrence River, Halifax Harbour, Newfoundland and southern Labrador.

[事实] His growing reputation led the Royal Society to select him in 1768 to command an expedition observing the transit of Venus from the Pacific.

[事实] Few records reveal Cook’s private thoughts because his wife Elizabeth later destroyed their personal correspondence.

[13:13] **The scientific and imperial objectives of the first voyage**

[事实] Observations of the transit of Venus were intended to improve calculations of astronomical distances and navigation.

[事实] Cook also received secret instructions to search for Terra Australis Incognita, chart any major landmass and, with Indigenous consent, secure useful locations for Britain.

[事实] The expedition sailed aboard the Endeavour with about one hundred people, including botanist Joseph Banks.

[推测] The voyage combined Enlightenment science, strategic rivalry with France and the early expansion of British influence in ways that could not be cleanly separated.

[18:45] **Tahiti: restraint, violence and coercion**

[事实] Before landing in Tahiti, Cook ordered his crew to cultivate friendship with its inhabitants and treat them with “all imaginable humanity.”

[事实] Despite those instructions, marines killed a Tahitian man after a musket was stolen, while sexual contact between sailors and local women contributed to disease and disciplinary problems.

[事实] Cook punished a ship’s butcher for threatening a Tahitian woman and resisted demands from some crew members to shoot islanders over thefts.

[事实] When two sailors deserted, Cook kidnapped Queen Oberea and three chiefs to force their return.

[推测] Tahiti established a recurring contradiction: Cook tried to restrain violence but readily adopted coercive methods when naval discipline or expedition property was threatened.

[25:39] **Deadly encounters in New Zealand and Australia**

[事实] Cook’s party attempted to seize a Māori canoe during an effort to establish contact, and four Māori were shot; across the first voyage, Cook and his men shot about eleven Māori in separate incidents.

[事实] Cook nevertheless admired Māori seamanship and recognised through linguistic similarities that Māori and Tahitians belonged to related ocean-going cultures.

[事实] In 1770 the expedition reached Australia’s eastern coast, entered Botany Bay, claimed New South Wales for George III and recorded unfamiliar wildlife including the kangaroo.

[事实] Disease later killed both Tahitian interpreters and 34 members of Cook’s roughly one-hundred-person crew before the Endeavour returned in 1771.

[30:59] **The search for the imagined southern continent**

[事实] Cook returned from his first voyage after 1,074 days but began planning a second expedition within a month.

[事实] The second voyage aimed to settle whether a vast southern continent existed and to prevent France from gaining a strategic advantage in the Pacific.

[事实] Cook doubted that Terra Australis existed but believed a single systematic voyage could resolve the question.

[事实] Joseph Banks withdrew after the Navy removed modifications that had made HMS Resolution dangerously top-heavy; Johann Reinhold Forster and his son Georg joined as naturalists instead.

[39:23] **The second voyage and renewed contact with Māori**

[事实] Resolution and Adventure departed in July 1772 with a marine chronometer whose successful performance helped demonstrate a reliable method of calculating longitude.

[事实] After a punishing journey through Antarctic fog and ice, Cook reached New Zealand in March 1773 and warned his crew to avoid repeating the earlier violence.

[事实] At Duck Cove, Cook approached a terrified Māori man with gifts, greeted him by touching noses and established peaceful contact with his family.

[推测] This encounter illustrates how patience, ritual adaptation and gift exchange could produce a markedly different result from the armed confusion of Cook’s first visit.

[47:27] **Sex, disease and Cook’s critique of European contact**

[事实] Cook wrote that Māori men sometimes pressured wives and daughters into sexual exchanges for coveted metal goods such as nails.

[事实] He placed still greater blame on European sailors and rejected the idea that the behaviour observed around ports represented the character of all Indigenous women.

[事实] Cook warned that Europeans disrupted Pacific societies by introducing new desires, diseases and moral corruption, and questioned what Indigenous Americans had gained from European commerce.

[推测] His observations reveal an unusually strong contemporary critique of colonial contact, even though he continued to participate in the system producing those harms.

[50:37] **Discipline deteriorates across the Pacific**

[事实] During the expedition’s return to Tahiti, islanders stole equipment while Cook’s increasingly undisciplined sailors drank, fought and behaved improperly toward local women.

[事实] Cook apologised to local chiefs for his crew’s conduct and continued to interpret unfamiliar customs, including cannibalism, with relative restraint.

[事实] In December 1773, ten men from Adventure were killed and consumed by Māori near Queen Charlotte Sound.

[事实] Captain Tobias Furneaux rejected demands for reprisals, reasoning that revenge would not restore the dead, and returned home without attacking Māori communities.

[推测] Furneaux’s response supports the episode’s broader argument that Enlightenment ideas of restraint influenced at least some British naval commanders in this period.

[57:26] **Antarctic exploration and the end of Terra Australis**

[事实] Cook sailed farther south than any previous mariner in that part of the Antarctic and systematically crossed unexplored areas to determine whether land existed there.

[事实] The absence of land and birds convinced him that the traditional vast, habitable Terra Australis did not exist.

[事实] Cook nevertheless inferred the existence of a frozen polar landmass surrounded by dangerous seas, closely anticipating what became known as Antarctica.

[事实] The voyage also brought Easter Island’s monumental statues and the cultural richness of Polynesia to wider European attention.

[推测] The most important achievement of the second voyage was not discovering a rich new continent but eliminating a centuries-old geographical misconception.

[62:49] **Cook’s celebrity and the Northwest Passage mission**

[事实] After returning in 1775, Cook joined the Royal Society and published work on sailors’ health and the prevention of scurvy.

[事实] The Polynesian traveller Omai became a celebrity in Britain, meeting George III and being painted by Joshua Reynolds.

[事实] The Admiralty planned a third expedition to seek the Northwest Passage from the Pacific, initially expecting the exhausted Cook to remain home as an adviser.

[事实] Cook unexpectedly volunteered to lead the voyage himself, while Charles Clerke was assigned the second ship and the young William Bligh served as Resolution’s master.

[推测] Cook’s decision to return to sea after spending six of the previous seven years on expedition was the pivotal error leading toward the final tragedy.

[69:23] **A crowded and troubled third voyage**

[事实] The expedition publicly presented its mission as returning Omai home, while the search for the Northwest Passage remained secret.

[事实] Omai brought furniture, weapons, armour, a globe and other goods, while George III supplied livestock intended to introduce European farming to Polynesia.

[事实] Cook was nearly 47, suffered health problems and appeared visibly exhausted, impatient and more hot-tempered than before.

[事实] Delays, an overcrowded and leaking ship, animal supplies and difficult weather put the expedition behind schedule almost immediately.

[74:53] **Cook’s judgment begins to deteriorate**

[事实] After stops at Desolation Island and Tasmania, Cook abruptly changed plans and hurried onward to New Zealand.

[事实] By 1777 the expedition had missed its intended Arctic window, food and water were short, and Cook responded to missing provisions by cutting everyone’s meat ration.

[事实] In Tonga, he imposed punishments far exceeding normal limits, including a 72-lash flogging, mutilation and violence against people escaping through the water.

[事实] Midshipman George Gilbert described this behaviour as unbecoming of a European and markedly different from Cook’s conduct on earlier voyages.

[推测] Physical exhaustion, illness, frustration and prolonged command pressure may have contributed to Cook’s increasingly erratic cruelty, although the transcript cannot establish a single medical or psychological cause.

[79:45] **Escalating reprisals and the approach to Hawaii**

[事实] Cook returned Omai to the Society Islands, where his crew built him a house; later visitors reported that Omai died roughly three years afterward.

[事实] When two goats were stolen on Mo‘orea, Cook ordered the destruction of eighteen canoes, more than one hundred paddles and twenty houses.

[事实] His own officers carried out these orders reluctantly and noted how sharply they contradicted his earlier conduct.

[事实] On 18 January 1778, after leaving the Society Islands many months late, the expedition unexpectedly sighted the previously unmapped Hawaiian Islands.

[推测] By the time Cook reached Hawaii, the restraint that had distinguished much of his earlier leadership had been badly eroded, making confrontation increasingly likely.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s chief strength is its refusal to reduce Cook to either heroic explorer or uncomplicated colonial villain. It places his scientific achievements, humane principles, territorial claims, coercion and violence in the same narrative, using contemporary journals to show that many moral objections were already understood by Cook and his crew.

Its chronological reconstruction also makes the transformation of Cook’s leadership unusually vivid. Peaceful exchanges, lethal misunderstandings and mounting disciplinary failures gradually establish why the exhausted commander of the third voyage behaved so differently from the meticulous navigator of the first two.

[推测] The discussion sometimes leans toward defending Cook through comparisons with more violent conquistadors, and the perspectives of Pacific peoples are largely mediated through European records. Listeners should therefore treat it as a nuanced account of Cook and his crews rather than a complete Indigenous history of these encounters.

The episode is especially valuable for listeners interested in maritime exploration, Enlightenment science, colonial history and the moral ambiguities of cross-cultural contact. It also functions as a detailed prelude to the subsequent episode about Cook’s arrival and death in Hawaii.
