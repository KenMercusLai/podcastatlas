# How to Become a Better Communicator with Matt Abrahams

## 概览

Andrew Huberman interviews Matt Abrahams of Stanford Graduate School of Business about communication across public speaking, one-on-one conversation, spontaneous exchanges, meetings, and high-stakes professional settings.

The core message is that effective communication is not mainly about memorizing words or displaying credentials. Abrahams repeatedly emphasizes audience relevance, structure, authenticity, presence, practice, feedback, and the ability to adapt in the moment.

The discussion moves from stage fright and self-judgment into practical tools: asking better questions, using “tell me more,” structuring ideas with “what, so what, now what,” recording yourself, practicing out loud, managing interruptions, reducing filler words, and building an anxiety-management plan.

[事实] Abrahams says fear of public speaking may have an evolutionary basis because public performance can feel like a threat to status.
[事实] He says both content and delivery matter: confusing content harms the message, while confidence, posture, and voice also shape reception.
[推测] The episode treats public speaking anxiety as a normal status-threat response rather than as a personal defect.

[06:58] **Connection Before Credentials**

[事实] Abrahams argues that presenters should not begin by listing credentials; they should first engage the audience.
[事实] He recommends hooks such as provocative statements, questions, interesting statistics, or showing relevance to the audience.
[事实] He distinguishes resume-style credibility from “Costco credibility,” where speakers demonstrate value through samples of useful thinking.

[07:59] **Authenticity and Self-Monitoring**

[事实] Abrahams defines authenticity as understanding what matters to you, coming from that place, and translating it clearly for the audience.
[事实] Huberman and Abrahams agree that constantly monitoring how the audience sees you interferes with effective communication.
[事实] Abrahams links self-judgment and memorization to wasted cognitive bandwidth.

[09:05] **Improv and Spontaneous Speaking**

[事实] Abrahams describes an improv exercise where students point at objects and call them something they are not.
[事实] The exercise reveals how much people judge themselves and how quickly they fall into habitual patterns.
[事实] Abrahams says spontaneous speaking can be prepared for through drills, much like athletes train for live performance.

[15:41] **Rhythm, Prosody, and Attention**

[事实] Huberman says he sometimes uses crowd interaction to break up the cadence of public lectures.
[事实] Abrahams discusses Lego manuals as wordless narratives whose rhythm affects motivation and emotion.
[事实] They discuss how visual information may need frequent updating, while long-form audio can remain engaging over time.

[21:46] **Audience Needs and Fidelity**

[事实] Huberman says visual teaching works best when diagrams contain enough detail to be meaningful but not so much that they overwhelm.
[事实] Abrahams says successful communication is not merely getting information out; it is helping the audience understand and use it.
[事实] He calls fidelity the key issue in communication: accurate and clear transmission of ideas.

[24:07] **Heuristics and Presence**

[事实] Abrahams defines heuristics as mental shortcuts people use to reduce uncertainty.
[事实] He warns that heuristics can mislead communicators, such as assuming someone wants feedback when they actually want support.
[事实] He says communicators need to stay present enough to notice audience reactions and adjust.

[30:00] **Structure and Feedback**

[事实] Abrahams says lists are hard to remember, while structures such as problem-solution-benefit or “what, so what, now what” help comprehension.
[事实] He recommends building feedback into talks through polls, paired discussion, reactions, or other real-time audience signals.
[事实] He has students watch recordings three ways: sound only, video only, and both together.

[35:45] **Deliberate Improvement**

[事实] Abrahams says improvement requires repetition, reflection, and feedback.
[事实] He describes a daily practice of writing down what went well and poorly in his communication, then reviewing the week on Sunday.
[事实] He and Huberman both discuss learning from communicators in other fields rather than simply copying them.

[39:00] **Movement and Physical Energy**

[事实] Huberman says movement, pacing, and gesture can help discharge high bodily arousal before or during speaking.
[事实] Abrahams says movement should be purposeful and can signal transitions to the audience.
[事实] He notes that stand-up comedians often avoid moving during punchlines so the important point lands clearly.

[46:07] **Social Media and Generational Expectations**

[事实] Huberman asks whether social media has changed audience expectations for engagement speed.
[事实] Abrahams says younger generations may expect communication to be faster and more transactional, while relationship-building still takes time.
[推测] The discussion suggests modern communicators need to manage both speed and depth.

[48:46] **Curiosity and Conversation**

[事实] Abrahams says friendliness helps, but his preferred frame is curiosity.
[事实] He describes conversation as turn-taking, with supportive turns that build on the other person and switching turns that introduce a new direction.
[事实] Huberman gives examples of asking Uber drivers where they are from and learning through those conversations.

[51:16] **Linearity, Tour Guides, and Different Speakers**

[事实] Abrahams says linear scaffolding is often best for teaching, though side paths can be engaging if the larger route remains clear.
[事实] He compares good speakers to tour guides who set expectations, guide transitions, and leave the audience with something useful.
[事实] He says some anxious speakers can reduce spotlight pressure by telling stories, facilitating discussion, or using media.

[58:00] **Practice Out Loud**

[事实] Abrahams says vocalizing is the most important part of practice because thoughts often seem clearer in the mind than when spoken.
[事实] He recommends practicing high-stakes conversations, anticipating responses, and role-playing.
[事实] He discusses VR presentation tools that simulate audiences and can help people rehearse under pressure.

[62:03] **Improv as Agility Training**

[事实] Abrahams says improvisation is not about being funny; it is about being present and responding to what is needed.
[事实] He suggests drills such as speaking about a random object or word.
[推测] These drills are framed as communication agility training rather than performance games.

[64:03] **Memorable Speeches and Noise**

[事实] Abrahams says speeches by figures such as Steve Jobs, Martin Luther King Jr., and JFK were effective because of strong writing, timing, context, and rhetorical devices.
[事实] He says modern communication is noisier, making it harder for positive speeches to become widely remembered.
[事实] He later says interpersonal and stage communication overlap in some skills, but presence does not always transfer directly.

[72:02] **Neurodiversity and Communication Range**

[事实] Abrahams says there is no single correct way to communicate.
[事实] He says introverted people and neurodiverse people can communicate effectively by leaning into their strengths.
[事实] He notes that extroversion is often rewarded because it is visible, but it is not always the best communication mode.

[74:59] **Writing and Building the Message**

[事实] Abrahams says writing can improve speaking because it forces thought, word choice, structure, and editing.
[事实] Huberman says building his own slides helped him prepare scientific talks because the slides embodied his thought process.
[事实] Abrahams criticizes “Franken-decks,” where unrelated slides are pasted together without a unifying story.

[77:18] **Calculated Risk and Authentic Style**

[事实] Huberman describes a Stanford talk where a speaker used a movie and song about oil droplets before explaining sound-based molecular work.
[事实] Abrahams says the talk worked because it violated expectations while supporting the speaker’s goal.
[事实] He recommends testing risky choices, such as unusual openings or humor, before using them publicly.

[83:23] **Blanking Out and Recovery**

[事实] Abrahams says memorization increases the risk of blanking out.
[事实] He recommends rationalizing the fear by asking how likely blanking out really is and what the worst realistic outcome would be.
[事实] If a speaker blanks, he recommends repeating the last thing said, asking a question, or reframing the pause without announcing panic.

[88:33] **Contingency Planning**

[事实] Huberman tells a story about Mark Zilka calmly recovering after spilling water on his laptop during a job talk.
[事实] Abrahams says contingency planning should be part of communication preparation.
[事实] He says silence under pressure can communicate confidence.

[91:44] **Voice and Breath**

[事实] Huberman says nervous speakers often raise their voice and speak higher than their natural tone.
[事实] Abrahams says breath work can help anxiety and allow a more natural voice to emerge.
[事实] Huberman says exhale-emphasized breathing, including longer exhales, can slow heart rate and calm the body.

[93:16] **Spontaneity, Sleep, and Caffeine**

[事实] Abrahams says most communication is spontaneous because people cannot know exactly how questions, feedback, or mistakes will unfold.
[事实] He discourages beta blockers as a first-line tool for speaking anxiety, while noting he takes them for a heart condition.
[事实] He recommends communication hygiene: sleeping, eating, exercising, and keeping caffeine close to one’s normal routine before important communication.

[事实] Huberman recommends non-sleep deep rest or yoga nidra for sleep recovery and learning how to be relaxed while alert.
[事实] Huberman describes eye movements with closed eyes as a tool that may help falling or returning to sleep.
[事实] Abrahams recommends having ordinary conversation before a major talk to warm up speaking and become present.

[105:00] **Introductions and Small Social Bridges**

[事实] Abrahams dislikes going around the room for introductions and prefers pairing people or having someone introduce another person.
[事实] He recommends listening to others and thinking of questions so anxiety does not build while waiting to speak.
[事实] He suggests starting an introduction with something one cares about before saying one’s name.

[108:00] **Everyday Conversation Practice**

[事实] Abrahams says simple observations can start conversation, illustrated by his story about noticing many people wearing blue.
[事实] He suggests phones can be used to invite interaction by showing someone something appropriate or interesting.
[事实] Huberman and Abrahams say customer service jobs, camp counseling, and similar roles teach communication through repeated interaction.

[114:00] **Modeling Communication and Pandemic Effects**

[事实] Abrahams recommends talking openly with younger people and colleagues about how one prepares for difficult conversations.
[事实] He says pandemic-era screen communication likely caused some young people to miss important opportunities for social testing and development.
[事实] Huberman introduces the idea of people who are “quiet but not shy,” and Abrahams agrees that listening can be powerful communication.

[120:24] **Physical Training and Presence**

[事实] Abrahams says decades of martial arts have helped his communication by building presence, responsiveness, and confidence.
[事实] He describes martial arts as a form of communication involving observation, connection, and anticipation.
[事实] He compares different martial arts formats to different communication formats: spontaneous exchange, scripted forms, and mixed prepared responses.

[124:34] **Apologies, Culture, and Interruptions**

[事实] Abrahams says apologizing before sharing an opinion can backfire by putting the speaker one-down at the start.
[事实] He says speakers should not pre-apologize for being tired, jet-lagged, or nervous because it primes the audience to notice those flaws.
[事实] For cross-cultural and accented communication, he says the goal is getting the point across, not sounding like a native speaker.
[事实] For interruptions, he recommends setting expectations upfront and using paraphrasing to acknowledge the interrupter while taking the floor back.

[129:29] **Preparing Speeches and Handling Disagreement**

[事实] Abrahams says preparation starts with audience, then goal, then structure, then practice.
[事实] He defines a communication goal as what the audience should know, feel, and do.
[事实] For arguments, he recommends listening first and having each person state how important the issue is to them.

[133:20] **Filler Words, Stories, Raises, and Anxiety Plans**

[事实] Abrahams says filler words are not inherently bad; the goal is to make them non-distracting.
[事实] He recommends “landing phrases,” ending phrases out of breath so the next move is an inhale rather than a filler word.
[事实] For storytelling, he recommends being concise, putting the bottom line up front, and entering the story quickly.
[事实] For asking for a raise, he recommends choosing context carefully, framing value from the boss’s perspective, and role-playing.
[事实] For pre-talk anxiety, he recommends managing both symptoms and sources, including cooling the body, using breathing, becoming present-oriented, and creating an anxiety-management plan.
