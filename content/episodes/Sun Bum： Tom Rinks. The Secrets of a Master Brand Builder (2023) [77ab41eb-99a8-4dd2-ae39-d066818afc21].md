# Sun Bum: Tom Rinks and the Secrets of a Master Brand Builder

## 概览

This episode traces Tom Rinks’s path from art student and commission furniture salesman to the brand builder behind Sun Bum, the sunscreen brand known for its woodgrain packaging and yellow ape mascot. The core question is how design, sales instincts, timing, and emotional positioning can turn ordinary consumer products into distinctive brands.

The conversation moves through Tom’s earlier ventures: Surf Michigan t-shirts, University of Michigan basketball apparel, Psycho Chihuahua, a long legal battle with Taco Bell, Christian video work, Rinks Canetti, and DeLeón Tequila. Each project becomes a lesson in making people feel something before asking them to buy.

Sun Bum emerges as the culmination of those lessons: a market gap in sunscreen, a carefully designed visual system, a mascot built for recognition, and a retail strategy that made the company appear bigger than it was. The brand grew from $100,000 in year-one sales to a reported $400 million sale to SC Johnson in 2019.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Archive Introduction And Episode Hook**

[事实] The host introduces the episode as a revisited archive favorite, originally aired in March 2023.

[事实] Tom Rinks is introduced as the founder of Sun Bum, described as a suntan lotion brand with a surprising story.

[事实] The opening clip highlights the early Cocoa Beach identity, where calls were routed to Grand Rapids while the company still presented itself through a Florida beach image.

[推测] The cold-weather Grand Rapids call-center anecdote previews the episode’s larger theme: brand perception was managed with humor, place, and careful language.

[01:17] **Design As The Central Thesis**

[事实] Guy Raz frames the episode around the power of product design, comparing Sun Bum to Method’s use of packaging to stand out.

[事实] The host cites a 2013 Psychological Science study saying brand design can matter more than price when consumers choose between similar products.

[事实] Sun Bum entered a sunscreen market dominated by legacy brands such as Coppertone, Neutrogena, and Hawaiian Tropic.

[事实] The Sun Bum bottle is described as a woodgrain-patterned package with a yellow gorilla or ape face, drawing from surf culture, mid-century furniture, Northern European typography, and Japanese streetwear.

[推测] The episode presents Sun Bum less as a formula breakthrough and more as a brand-design breakthrough.

[04:13] **Upbringing And Furniture Sales**

[事实] Tom grew up in Long Beach, California, with a teacher mother and a father he describes as a Christian fundamentalist minister.

[事实] His strict religious upbringing pushed him away from organized religion and toward drawing and painting.

[事实] After studying art at Hope College near Grand Rapids, he worked on full commission at Art Van Furniture.

[事实] He says the furniture job taught him how to sell, including how to match language and salespeople to what different customers wanted to feel.

[推测] The furniture floor became Tom’s practical training in customer psychology before he had formal business discipline.

[07:45] **Surf Michigan And The First Break**

[事实] Tom saw surfers at Holland State Park in Michigan and made a joke t-shirt: “Surf Michigan, waiting for the big one.”

[事实] He called Meijer, presented himself as a surfwear rep, arrived with one t-shirt, then admitted to the buyer that he actually worked at Art Van Furniture.

[事实] Buyer Vivian Dreyer ordered 1,800 shirts after seeing the shirt and sketches.

[事实] Tom had the shirts printed at Perron Screen Printing and delivered them himself while continuing his furniture job.

[推测] This first success established a recurring pattern: spot a cultural oddity, turn it into a visual identity, and sell before everything is fully built.

[12:04] **Michigan Basketball Apparel**

[事实] During the Fab Five era, Tom noticed that University of Michigan basketball shirts did not feel cool or connected to the team’s cultural energy.

[事实] He walked into the University of Michigan athletic department and obtained a license structured around royalties.

[事实] He created the “Slam Dance” shirt with a large Michigan M and a player swinging from the rim.

[事实] Stores in Ann Arbor sold 10,000 of those shirts that season.

[事实] Tom later expanded to many schools and says he eventually had more than 400 licenses, but poor margin and inventory management limited profitability.

[推测] He was strong at cultural timing and product feel, but still weak at financial operations.

[16:09] **Joe Shields And Psycho Chihuahua**

[事实] Tom met cartoonist Joe Shields through Perron Screen Printing and admired his character work.

[事实] Their early Mosh and Caveman shirt lines did not sell.

[事实] After seeing Madonna photographed with her Chihuahua Chiquita, Tom proposed a new line called Psycho Chihuahua.

[事实] The Psycho Chihuahua shirts used a fierce small dog with phrases such as “I don’t roll over,” “Bite me,” and “Big dogs suck.”

[事实] Reps reported strong demand, and the line drew heavy attention at a major apparel trade show.

[推测] Psycho Chihuahua worked because it mixed cute scale with rebellious attitude, a combination Tom would reuse in later mascot thinking.

[21:01] **Licensing Show And Taco Bell Interest**

[事实] Tom and Joe took Psycho Chihuahua to a licensing show at the Javits Center in New York.

[事实] Taco Bell representatives visited their booth while looking for a mascot or licensable idea.

[事实] Tom and Joe hired licensing agent Arlene Scanlon, flew to Irvine, and worked with Taco Bell for about 18 months.

[事实] They say they developed tray liners, uniforms, hats, posters, toys, commercials, and storyboards.

[事实] They moved the idea from a cartoon Chihuahua to a real dog because they believed television required it.

[推测] By Tom’s account, the work had moved far beyond a casual pitch into a developed campaign concept.

[25:33] **Taco Bell Ghosting And The Ad Shock**

[事实] Around 1997, Taco Bell stopped returning Tom and Joe’s calls.

[事实] Tom says they assumed Taco Bell had chosen another idea and moved on.

[事实] About three months later, Taco Bell released a Chihuahua ad campaign using the phrase “yo quiero Taco Bell.”

[事实] Tom says the first three commercials were the commercials he had written and storyboarded.

[推测] The episode presents this moment as both a creative betrayal and a major professional shock for Tom.

[27:52] **Five-Year Legal Fight**

[事实] Tom and Joe sued Taco Bell, citing breach of contract or implied contract.

[事实] In 1999, a U.S. district court in Michigan granted summary judgment to Taco Bell, but Tom and Joe appealed.

[事实] The legal fight consumed about five years before a final decision and strained Tom’s work and personal life.

[事实] Tom says Psycho Chihuahua shirt sales dropped because consumers now wanted the Taco Bell Chihuahua.

[事实] Tom says he would have accepted $200,000 earlier just to be paid for the work, but Taco Bell offered nothing at that stage.

[推测] The lawsuit became about recognition and fair treatment as much as money.

[31:48] **Christian Video Nonprofit**

[事实] Around 2001, Tom started working on Christian-oriented inspirational videos.

[事实] He was drawn back into church life partly through Rob Bell, whose sermons felt fresh, relevant, and culturally familiar to him.

[事实] Tom made the video project a nonprofit.

[事实] He says he wanted to improve the “brand” of Christianity, which he felt had become publicly ugly and negative.

[推测] Even this faith-based project was approached through brand positioning: creating an accessible on-ramp for people alienated by existing religious messaging.

[34:38] **Trial Verdict And Financial Outcome**

[事实] The federal trial lasted three and a half weeks, and Tom says he was on the stand for about three days.

[事实] Taco Bell offered a few million dollars to settle just before the trial started, but Tom and Joe declined.

[事实] The jury found in their favor on all counts and awarded $30.1 million plus $12 million in interest.

[事实] Taco Bell appealed, then settled a few months later.

[事实] After lawyer fees, agent fees, splitting the remaining money with Joe, and taxes, Tom estimates his share was about $5 million to $6 million.

[推测] The public headline number overstated Tom’s personal financial outcome, but the win still changed his family’s financial position.

[38:44] **René Kennedy And Rinks Canetti**

[事实] Tom met René Kennedy, a designer from Copenhagen, through a design agency near his nonprofit office.

[事实] René accepted lower pay to join Tom’s project because he said there had been no spiritual on-ramp for someone like him.

[事实] In 2007, Tom and René formed the branding agency Rinks Canetti.

[事实] Their model was to charge branding fees while also taking a piece of the companies they helped create.

[推测] The partnership combined Tom’s strategy and selling instincts with René’s stronger design execution.

[41:38] **DeLeón Tequila And Emotional Branding**

[事实] Tom’s childhood friend Brent Hocking wanted to enter the spirits business with a high-end tequila brand.

[事实] Tom says people buy brands because of how those brands make them feel about themselves.

[事实] The tequila became DeLeón, with a luxury bottle and a sterling silver cork featuring skulls and snakes.

[事实] Bottles ranged from $125 to $850.

[事实] Rinks Canetti received equity as part of the brand-building work.

[推测] DeLeón shows Tom translating a product category into an identity: ordering straight tequila was framed as a “badass” feeling.

[45:10] **The Sunscreen Opportunity**

[事实] Around 2009, a friend connected to Tom’s children’s school asked him to evaluate several failing sunscreen concepts.

[事实] The concepts included Raceface for NASCAR, Gameface for colleges, and an early Sun Bum design with a carabiner and hammock-style clip art.

[事实] Tom told them the concepts and design were bad and would not work.

[事实] He saw a large market gap because sunscreen was dominated by big companies and lacked an authentic brand his kids would want in their car or on their towel.

[推测] Sun Bum began as a rescue and rebuild of a weak sunscreen idea rather than as a fully formed startup from day one.

[47:53] **Three-Month Design Process**

[事实] Tom and René used a three-month process: one month of research, one month of ideation, and one month of design work.

[事实] An Eames lounge chair inspired the woodgrain and curved design direction.

[事实] Tom’s son’s yellow-and-brown Billabong thrift-store shirt influenced the color palette.

[事实] Tom initially disliked the name Sun Bum, but taglines such as “Trust the Bum” and “Protect Your Lazy Ass” helped the name make sense.

[事实] Tom liked that the word “Sun” directly connected the brand to the sunscreen category.

[推测] The name survived because it was category-linked, casual, and flexible enough to support a playful voice.

[50:06] **Mascot Strategy**

[事实] Tom believed durable brand characters, such as the Budweiser frogs, M&M characters, Energizer Bunny, and Mickey Mouse, can outlast human founders or celebrities.

[事实] Early concepts moved from surf or hippie figures to animals and eventually to an ape.

[事实] Tom says a global brand needed broader appeal than a narrow surf-only reference.

[推测] Sunny was designed as an ownable symbol that could work across youth culture, beach culture, family retail, and travel.

[52:21] **Creative Boards And The Elvis Principle**

[事实] Their inspiration boards included surf counterculture, Scandinavian mid-century furniture, Northern European typography, Japanese and New York streetwear, collectible vinyl figures, and graffiti.

[事实] Tom says René’s Copenhagen design background shaped the clean typography, spacing, and layout.

[事实] Tom describes the “Elvis principle” as combining edge, sex, goodness, kindness, and humility.

[推测] Sun Bum’s personality was deliberately built to feel rebellious enough to be cool but friendly enough to be widely accepted.

[55:34] **Sunny Logo And Wordless Recognition**

[事实] René presented the final ape logo to Tom, and Tom says it was essentially the same mark seen today.

[事实] Tom believed the ape logo was powerful enough to sit beside major marks such as Nike, Adidas, Disney, Ford, and Apple.

[事实] They used the ape without the Sun Bum name on stickers, footballs, frisbees, paddleball sets, towels, hats, ads, and billboards.

[事实] They stickered surf towns in California to spread the mark.

[推测] The strategy aimed to make the ape trigger sunscreen awareness even before consumers knew the brand name.

[57:50] **Surf Expo Validation**

[事实] Sun Bum debuted at the January 2010 Surf Expo in Orlando.

[事实] Skaters took Sunny stickers and placed them on ramps, and kids came back asking for more stickers.

[事实] Boutique owners from places such as Nantucket and Martha’s Vineyard, along with high-end hotels, tried, smelled, and bought the product.

[事实] Tom says he knew on the first day that the brand had unusually broad demographic appeal.

[事实] He also says the sunscreen itself had to be safe, clean, and good enough for his family and surf friends.

[推测] The trade show validated both the visual system and the product’s ability to cross customer segments.

[59:52] **Closing The Agency And Cocoa Beach Positioning**

[事实] After the trade show, the owner asked Tom to take over Sun Bum, and Tom and René closed Rinks Canetti.

[事实] The early Sun Bum team worked from Grand Rapids with only a few people.

[事实] The bottle used Cocoa Beach, Florida, because Tom thought it sounded like the right place for a sunscreen company.

[事实] Calls were first routed from a Cocoa Beach P.O. box to Grand Rapids, and the company later created a real Cocoa Beach office with staff.

[推测] Cocoa Beach shows how Tom treated place as part of the brand experience, even while operations were still catching up.

[63:00] **Sales Reps, Stickers, And Retail Displays**

[事实] Tom says independent sales rep Michael LaSara was crucial and that Sun Bum would not have made it without him.

[事实] LaSara tested the product in the water and then promoted it aggressively, including by placing stickers on off-ramps, signs, and gas nozzles.

[事实] Radio stations received calls from people asking about the ape they kept seeing.

[事实] Sun Bum pushed stores to buy full displays instead of cherry-picking a few bottles.

[事实] The team modified Ikea bookcases into branded display units with sunscreen, shirts, footballs, frisbees, and an ape on top.

[推测] The display strategy made the young brand look larger and more established than it was.

[65:29] **Early Revenue And Prestige Accounts**

[事实] Sun Bum did about $100,000 in sales in year one and $1.1 million in year two.

[事实] Tom says the early growth came from being in the right stores, including strong surf shops and high-end hotels.

[事实] Richard Branson and Joan bought Sun Bum as the exclusive sunscreen for Necker Island near the end of the first year.

[事实] Other hospitality accounts such as Four Seasons and The Standard followed.

[推测] Prestige placement gave Sun Bum credibility beyond what its early revenue alone would suggest.

[66:13] **CEO Handoff And SC Johnson Sale**

[事实] A few years into Sun Bum, the original owners sold it to Tom and his group.

[事实] The company moved to California in 2014.

[事实] SC Johnson purchased Sun Bum in 2019, reportedly for $400 million.

[事实] Tom says bringing in Adam Francis as CEO was necessary because Tom lacked skills in margins, scalability, production, and financial management.

[事实] Tom says the company sold because international sunscreen regulation required a partner with manufacturing, offices, and regulatory capability in many countries.

[推测] One of Tom’s important founder decisions was recognizing when someone else should run the company’s next stage.

[70:08] **Staying Out Of The Public Eye**

[事实] Tom says this was one of his first intensive interviews and that he barely existed online.

[事实] He connects his low profile to insecurity, not wanting to brag, and not feeling like he personally embodied the brands he created.

[事实] He says the Taco Bell lawsuit made him known as “the guy that sued Taco Bell,” which could distract from other brands.

[事实] He preferred to let other people represent brands while he focused on the work.

[推测] His invisibility was both personal and strategic: it protected brand authenticity by keeping the founder out of the frame.

[72:28] **Luck, Practice, And New Work**

[事实] Tom says his success came from both lucky breaks and hard work.

[事实] He names Vivian Dreyer’s 1,800-shirt order and buyers taking a chance on him as examples of luck.

[事实] He says brand-building is like his art and an extension of painting and drawing.

[事实] The episode closes by noting his work on Made by Dentists, a brand founded by two dentist sisters from Ireland.

[事实] Tom redesigned the brand for the U.S. and created kids’ toothpaste flavors such as Alien Blood and Eyeball Juice.

[推测] The closing reinforces that Tom sees brands as continuing creative projects, not just exits.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is practical: it shows brand-building as a repeatable discipline involving cultural observation, emotional positioning, visual distinctiveness, sales pressure, and distribution strategy.

[推测] Its standout moments are Tom’s candor about selling, legal stress, failed concepts, and giving up the CEO role. The story is useful because it does not present creativity as separate from commerce.

[推测] Its main limitation is that many details come from Tom’s own account, especially around the Taco Bell relationship and product quality claims. The transcript does not independently verify the legal record, sunscreen formulation, or full SC Johnson deal terms.

[推测] This episode is best for founders, designers, marketers, and consumer-brand operators who want to understand how ordinary categories can be made emotionally specific. It is less useful for listeners seeking technical sunscreen science or a detailed acquisition analysis.
