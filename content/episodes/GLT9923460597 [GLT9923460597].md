# The Habsburg Way: Habsburg History and Seven Rules for Turbulent Times

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* features Eduard Habsburg, a member of the Habsburg-Lothringen family and Hungary’s ambassador to the Holy See, discussing the long arc of Habsburg history and the themes of his book *The Habsburg Way: Seven Rules for Turbulent Times*.

The conversation moves from the family’s medieval origins and rise through marriage diplomacy to the Spanish and Austrian branches, the extinction of the Spanish Habsburgs, Maria Theresa’s restoration of dynastic strength, the end of the Holy Roman Empire, and the collapse of Austria-Hungary in 1918.

In the second half, the hosts and guest use the book’s seven rules to explore family, Catholicism, empire, subsidiarity, law, dynastic identity, battlefield courage, and attitudes toward death. The tone mixes historical explanation with comic deference to Habsburg titles and recurring jokes about the Order of the Golden Fleece.

[01:30] **Franz Josef’s Titles and Habsburg Nostalgia**

[事实] The episode begins by reading the long list of Franz Josef I’s imperial and royal titles.
[事实] The hosts frame the Habsburgs as a dynasty with an unusually rich collection of titles and a strong appeal for people interested in Central European history.
[事实] Dominic mentions his fondness for Vienna, Habsburg culture, and Franz Ferdinand’s castle at Konopiště.
[推测] The humorous opening establishes the episode as both a serious historical discussion and an affectionate performance of Habsburg grandeur.

[04:47] **Introducing Eduard Habsburg**

[事实] Eduard Karl Josef Michael Marcus Antonius Koloman Volkard Maria Habsburg-Lothringen is introduced as a Habsburg and Hungary’s ambassador to the Holy See.
[事实] Eduard explains that every Habsburg is technically an Archduke or Archduchess, rather than there being one single “the Archduke.”
[事实] He says using the title Archduke in Austria can lead to a fine, though outside Austria it is treated as a historical title attached to the family.
[推测] The discussion shows the tension between the dynasty’s historical identity and modern republican legal realities.

[06:47] **The Origins of the Habsburgs**

[事实] Eduard says the family came from the region between Germany, Switzerland, and France, around the Bodensee area.
[事实] In 1273, Rudolph I was elected King of the Holy Roman Empire, bringing the family onto the wider stage of European history.
[事实] Eduard explains that the Habsburg name comes from a small castle near Zurich in the Swiss canton of Aargau.
[事实] After losing their Swiss base, the Habsburgs established themselves in Austria and later used the title Casa de Austria.

[10:15] **Maximilian, Burgundy, and Marriage Diplomacy**

[事实] Around 1500, the Habsburgs expanded dramatically through a series of marriages, especially under Maximilian.
[事实] Maximilian married Mary of Burgundy, daughter of Charles the Bold, after Charles was killed and Burgundy became vulnerable to French pressure.
[事实] Their descendants connected the Habsburgs to Burgundy, Spain, Hungary, Bohemia, and eventually a global empire.
[事实] Eduard describes Maximilian as romantic, ambitious, connected to artists such as Dürer, and often short of money.
[推测] The episode treats marriage diplomacy as the core mechanism by which the Habsburgs became a world power.

[14:24] **The Spanish and Austrian Branches**

[事实] Philip, son of Maximilian and Mary of Burgundy, married the heiress of Castile, bringing Spain into the Habsburg orbit.
[事实] Charles V inherited a vast empire including Spain, Burgundy, Central Europe, and territories in the Americas and Pacific.
[事实] Eduard explains that the dynasty split into Spanish and Austrian branches, with Spain handling the world empire and Austria focusing on Austria, Hungary, Bohemia, and the Holy Roman imperial title.
[事实] The hosts describe this as the first properly global empire.

[15:52] **Inbreeding, the Spanish Habsburgs, and 1700**

[事实] Eduard says the Spanish Habsburg branch died out in 1700.
[事实] He acknowledges that repeated marriages between the Austrian and Spanish branches contributed to genetic diseases and the inability of the last Spanish Habsburg to have children.
[事实] The death of the last Spanish Habsburg led to the War of the Spanish Succession.
[事实] Eduard rejects the idea that a surviving Spanish Habsburg branch remains in any meaningful dynastic sense.

[17:20] **Maria Theresa and Dynastic Survival**

[事实] Charles VI had no sons and negotiated the Pragmatic Sanction so that his daughter Maria Theresa could inherit and continue the family line.
[事实] Maria Theresa had 16 children and reestablished Habsburg strength from 1740 onward.
[事实] Eduard describes her as dynamic, Catholic, socially energetic, and politically determined.
[事实] She appealed to the Hungarians for support against Frederick of Prussia after her father’s death.
[推测] Maria Theresa is presented as one of the dynasty’s most important figures because she converted a succession crisis into a renewed Habsburg future.

[20:23] **1806 and the End of the Holy Roman Empire**

[事实] In 1806, Francis II dissolved the Holy Roman Empire under pressure from Napoleon.
[事实] Eduard says this narrowed Habsburg horizons in one sense, but by then the Holy Roman Empire was no longer the world-spanning structure it had once been.
[事实] He argues that principles used by Habsburg Holy Roman emperors carried into the Austrian Empire and later Austria-Hungary.
[事实] Eduard suggests the European Union could learn from aspects of the Austro-Hungarian Empire.
[推测] His comments imply that he sees Habsburg political practice as relevant to current debates about European governance.

[21:47] **1918 and the Fall of the Monarchy**

[事实] The final key date discussed is 12 November 1918, when Emperor Karl was removed from power after the First World War.
[事实] The hosts note that Karl did not abdicate and later tried to return to Hungary.
[事实] Eduard says he does not currently see monarchy returning in Austria or Hungary, but adds that stranger things have happened in history.
[推测] Eduard’s diplomatic answer keeps dynastic restoration as a joke and a possibility without presenting it as an active political program.

[24:53] **Rule One: Get Married and Have Lots of Children**

[事实] Eduard’s first rule is to get married and have many children.
[事实] He says he has been married for 28 years and has six children.
[事实] He argues that large families teach virtues needed for society, such as care for younger siblings.
[事实] In Habsburg history, daughters were especially useful for marriage diplomacy because they could unite countries.
[推测] The rule links private family structure to the dynasty’s political strategy and to Eduard’s broader social conservatism.

[28:02] **The Habsburg Jaw and Dynastic Marriage**

[事实] Eduard confirms that repeated marriages between the Austrian and Spanish Habsburgs caused genetic problems in the Spanish branch.
[事实] He argues that the famous Habsburg jaw predated the Spanish branch and did not originate from later inbreeding.
[事实] He says the jaw may have become more prominent through inbreeding, but came earlier through another family line.
[事实] Eduard also claims many intra-Habsburg marriages were happy because the spouses shared faith, values, culture, and expectations.

[31:25] **Family Cohesion and Catholic Identity**

[事实] The hosts contrast the Habsburgs with other dynasties famous for internal violence and note that the Habsburgs had relatively few palace coups and murderous family feuds.
[事实] Eduard says one reason was that the dynasty governed complex territories and had to be diplomatic.
[事实] His second rule is to be Catholic.
[事实] He argues that Catholic faith shaped Habsburg decisions and made rulers conscious of accountability before God.
[推测] The discussion presents Catholicism as a moral and institutional glue for the dynasty.

[34:00] **Catholicism, the Reformation, and Ferdinand II**

[事实] The hosts raise Rudolph II and his father as examples of Habsburgs whose Catholic orthodoxy was questionable.
[事实] Eduard calls this a dark moment in family history and says he prays for them.
[事实] The conversation turns to Ferdinand II, Counter-Reformation Catholicism, and the Thirty Years’ War.
[事实] Eduard says his Catholic side sees Ferdinand II as firm and justified, while his diplomat side understands the argument that compromise might have avoided problems.
[推测] The exchange highlights a recurring tension in the episode between conviction and political flexibility.

[36:51] **Rule Three: Empire and Subsidiarity**

[事实] Eduard’s third rule is to believe in empire and subsidiarity.
[事实] He contrasts the Habsburg idea of empire with fictional empires in *Star Wars* and *Dune*, which he describes as ruthless and centralized.
[事实] He says the Habsburg Empire worked best when it respected lower levels of identity, including nations, peoples, legal structures, and languages.
[事实] He connects this idea to Article 5 of the European Union and to Hungary’s current feeling of being disrespected by Brussels.
[推测] Eduard uses Habsburg history to argue for a less centralized and more locally respectful model of European integration.

[40:07] **Nationalism and the Austro-Hungarian Compromise**

[事实] Dominic raises the argument that 19th-century nationalism corroded dynastic loyalty in Hungary, Croatia, Transylvania, and the Czech lands.
[事实] Eduard points to 1848 as a moment when concessions and repression both became central to imperial survival.
[事实] The 1867 Ausgleich divided the monarchy into Austrian and Hungarian halves, giving Hungary significant rights and autonomy.
[事实] Eduard notes that Slavic peoples felt left out by this arrangement.
[推测] The conversation suggests that subsidiarity helped stabilize parts of the empire but did not fully solve the nationalities problem.

[42:52] **Rule Four: Law, Justice, and Service to Subjects**

[事实] Eduard’s fourth rule is to stand for law, justice, and one’s subjects.
[事实] He uses Archduke Joseph, Palatine of Hungary, as an example.
[事实] Joseph arrived in Hungary in the 1790s, embraced the country and language, founded institutions such as the Academy of Sciences and early fire departments, and mediated between Hungarian leaders and the emperor.
[事实] Eduard says many Habsburgs, especially after the Enlightenment, saw themselves as servants of their subjects.
[推测] This section presents the Habsburg ideal as paternalistic public service rather than mere dynastic self-interest.

[44:05] **Counterexamples: America and the Netherlands**

[事实] Tom raises the conquest of America and the Dutch revolt as counterexamples to Habsburg claims about justice and service.
[事实] Eduard admits these examples barely appear in his book.

[44:59] **Rule Five: Know Who You Are**

[事实] Eduard’s fifth rule is to know who you are.
[事实] The discussion focuses on documents allegedly linking Habsburg titles to ancient Roman authority, including Caesar and Nero.
[事实] Eduard acknowledges that the Latin in these documents sounds implausible and that the titles are wrong, but says they were accepted by an emperor and therefore had legal value.
[事实] The family also developed genealogies reaching back to biblical figures such as Noah.
[推测] The hosts frame this as a lesson in dynastic mythmaking and control of narrative.

[47:13] **Rule Six: Be Brave in Battle**

[事实] Eduard’s sixth rule is to be brave in battle.
[事实] He cites Archduke Charles at the Battle of Aspern in 1809, where Napoleon suffered a rare land defeat.
[事实] Eduard says the battle sent a message across Europe that Napoleon could be beaten.
[事实] The hosts describe Charles leading a countercharge on a white horse with a flag, a moment later memorialized by a statue in Vienna’s Heldenplatz.
[事实] Eduard acknowledges Napoleon won the next battle, but insists Aspern’s significance should not be minimized.

[49:24] **Rule Seven: Die Well**

[事实] Eduard’s final rule is to die well.
[事实] He says thinking about death gives life perspective and, as a Catholic, he sees death as spiritually decisive.
[事实] He describes the Habsburg knocking ritual at the Capuchin crypt in Vienna, where titles and achievements are rejected before the deceased is admitted as a poor sinner.
[事实] The ritual was performed at the funerals of Empress Zita and Otto von Habsburg.
[推测] The ritual captures the episode’s central contrast between immense worldly rank and religious humility.

[51:11] **Maximilian and Marie Antoinette as Examples of Dying Well**

[事实] Eduard says Emperor Maximilian ordered his body to be treated with signs of humility, including removing teeth, cutting hair, whipping the body, and covering it with lime and ash.
[事实] Maximilian was buried under the altar steps at Wiener Neustadt with only his name marked.
[事实] Eduard then discusses Marie Antoinette, Maria Theresa’s youngest daughter, and says she went to execution peacefully after receiving absolution from a hidden bishop.
[事实] He says many Americans on Twitter do not realize Marie Antoinette was a Habsburg.
[推测] These examples reinforce Eduard’s view that Habsburg identity was inseparable from Catholic preparation for death.

[54:06] **Closing Reflections and the Double-Headed Eagle**

[事实] The hosts ask whether the seven rules might guide their own lives and return to jokes about the Order of the Golden Fleece.
[事实] Eduard’s book is named as *The Habsburg Way: Seven Rules for Turbulent Times*.
[事实] Asked whether Europe might need the return of the double-headed eagle, Eduard replies that if the Habsburgs are ever needed, they are around; meanwhile, they are on Twitter.
[推测] The ending keeps the episode’s restorationist humor playful rather than programmatic.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is that it gives a lively, personality-driven tour of Habsburg history through someone who is both a diplomat and a member of the family. Eduard Habsburg’s perspective is openly sympathetic to the dynasty, which makes the conversation unusually vivid when discussing marriage diplomacy, Catholic identity, dynastic myth, and imperial governance.

The strongest sections are those connecting specific historical episodes to broader political principles: Maximilian and marriage diplomacy, Maria Theresa and dynastic survival, the Ausgleich and subsidiarity, Archduke Charles at Aspern, and the Capuchin crypt ritual. The hosts’ jokes about titles and the Golden Fleece keep the discussion accessible without losing the historical thread.

The main limitation is that the episode is intentionally Habsburg-friendly. Counterexamples such as the Americas and the Netherlands are raised, but not explored in depth. [推测] Listeners looking for a critical account of Habsburg imperial rule, colonial violence, or nationalism may find the treatment partial.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in European dynastic history, Catholic monarchy, the Holy Roman Empire, Austria-Hungary, or the ways historical memory can be used to comment on modern Europe.
