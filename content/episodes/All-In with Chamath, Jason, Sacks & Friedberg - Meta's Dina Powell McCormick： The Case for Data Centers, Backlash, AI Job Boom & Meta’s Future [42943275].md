# Meta's Data Center Bet: Dina Powell McCormick on Backlash, Jobs, and the Next Three Years

## 概览

This episode of All-In features Dina Powell McCormick, Meta's president and vice chairwoman and a former presidential advisor, in a conversation that moves from the ground-level economics of data centers to Meta's product, workforce, and AI strategy. The framing question, carried over from a prior conversation with Satya Nadella, is why data centers poll so badly nationally even as the communities hosting them report concrete gains.

The strongest part of the discussion is the Louisiana case study. Local officials from Richland Parish describe tax revenue flowing into teacher bonuses, a site whose water use is lower than the farmland it replaced, and a utility arrangement in which Meta pays for its own generation, grid upgrades, and storm costs. Powell McCormick uses that case to argue that the industry has simply failed at communication, and that communities should be allowed to opt in.

The last third turns adversarial. Jason Calacanis presses on Meta's child-safety record, the multi-billion-dollar settlement, and whether newer AI companion products repeat the same addiction dynamics; Powell McCormick disputes the premise and points to new under-18 defaults agreed with 52 bipartisan attorneys general. The panel then discusses Meta's glasses as a real product category, the "whack-a-mole" problem of parental control, and Meta's stated belief that distributing AI widely is stabilizing rather than destabilizing.

Threaded throughout are Meta's own numbers claims: America's Workforce Academy's 40,000 applicants and 250 graduates, hundreds of thousands of open skilled-trades jobs, and a first American open-source model released weeks before the taping.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Guest Introduction and Framing**

[事实] Dina Powell McCormick is introduced as a former presidential advisor to Donald Trump who joined Meta Platforms as president and vice chairwoman, helping lead the company's AI expansion across data centers, investments, and global partnerships.

[事实] The opening montage frames the stakes with the claims that "there is a transformation in humanity coming," that 3.5 billion people are on Meta every day, and that the next three years "could be the whole game."

[推测] The montage's emphasis on scale and speed signals the episode's intended tone: a defense of Meta's buildout rather than a debate about whether the buildout should happen.

[01:57] **The Richland Parish Video**

[事实] A produced video profiles Richland Parish, Louisiana, described as a place people left for work, and presents the Meta data center as the arrival of an industry the parish had never had.

[事实] In the video, a local caterer says the business started because Meta was coming and went from catering for 40 people to 400 a week, while a school superintendent describes teachers receiving bonuses so large he called them "jaw-dropping."

[事实] The video also features residents saying people are moving back home and that the investment gives students opportunities they had not expected.

[推测] The video functions as the episode's evidence base, so its selection of small-business and school impacts is a deliberate counter to national polling on data centers rather than a neutral survey of local effects.

[04:03] **Louisiana Officials on the Deal**

[事实] Sheldon, the local superintendent, and Susan, Louisiana's commerce and economic development secretary, join the panel; Powell McCormick notes Louisiana is Meta's largest data center investment.

[事实] Sheldon describes seeing sales tax collections rise from a normal 5–10 percent growth to 60–70 percent, then peak at about 260 percent, as data center construction workers arrived, and says one- and half-cent sales taxes dedicated to employee salaries translated into larger checks for teachers.

[事实] He states that a December 2025 half-cent sales tax check doubled from roughly $4,000 to about $8,000, and that a later certified-employee check reached about $50,000, for a net of about $45,000 for teachers across the two increases.

[事实] Susan says the state was late to the data center game, that Meta's investment was Louisiana's first, and that the deal was approached as a partnership rather than a negotiation.

[09:29] **A Community Starved for Opportunity**

[事实] Susan says average wages in Richland Parish were roughly $36,000–$38,000 a year before the investment and that the parish had lost population.

[事实] Sheldon says the community's initial posture was anxiety driven by the unknown rather than opposition, and that Meta did a good job informing and educating residents.

[事实] Sheldon argues that communication must target "the least informed person in the community," not the most informed one, and that Meta's messaging effort has to continue.

[推测] The panel treats the absence of a prior large investment as the reason the backlash was muted locally: with no reference case, the community had less material for organized opposition.

[11:15] **State Framework and Incentives**

[事实] Susan says the governor signed an executive order creating a framework for engaging large load users, and that Louisiana offers a sales tax exemption considered critical to landing investments of this scale.

[事实] She says the state can now be selective about partners, that Meta was the prototype that set a high standard, and that Louisiana is a state "saying yes and" rather than saying no.

[推测] The officials' willingness to be "choosy" presupposes that demand for data center siting exceeds the state's willingness to host, which is a claim about market conditions rather than a verified fact in the transcript.

[11:54] **Answering Valid Community Concerns**

[事实] Chamath Palihapitiya lists the concerns communities raise: pollution, increased electricity cost, water use and closed-loop versus non-closed-loop cooling, noise, and aesthetics.

[事实] Sheldon responds that the site was agricultural land and that Meta chose a more expensive but more efficient system that uses less water than farming had used on that land.

[事实] On energy, Susan says Louisiana has about 32,000 miles of natural gas pipelines and that from day one Meta paid for its own generation, grid resilience, grid upgrades, and storm costs—including in a state where hurricanes periodically trigger storm assessments.

[事实] Susan's conclusion is that these issues were addressed in the original negotiated deal, which is why the arrangement became the state's prototype for future projects.

[推测] The "less water than the farmland" comparison only addresses the water concern relative to the land's prior use, not relative to regional water stress, which the transcript does not examine.

[14:45] **Misinformation, Adversaries, and Meta's Compact**

[事实] Asked why the anti-data-center narrative is winning nationally, Powell McCormick says the questions raised are legitimate, that communities should get to decide whether to host, but that the industry has done a poor job communicating.

[事实] She describes Meta's "compact" as paying for its own electricity and working to drive costs down, adopting water-saving technology, and funding teachers and first responders, and says Meta applies this everywhere it goes.

[事实] She says she was with Chairman Cotton and Vice Chairman Warner of the Intelligence Committees, who believe they have evidence that adversaries are amplifying the backlash; she says she cannot comment on that but argues that stopping US data center construction is exactly what an adversary would want.

[事实] She says Texas and Ohio are also seeing positive impacts, and a clip of her responding to a widely circulated clip involving a woman with a bottle of dirty water is referenced approvingly by the hosts.

[推测] The adversary framing is presented as consistent with, but not proof of, foreign involvement in the backlash; the transcript provides no evidence beyond her account of the committee members' belief.

[19:59] **Workforce Development and America's Workforce Academy**

[事实] Powell McCormick says Meta could not find enough fiber technicians, so it built a curriculum with partners including CBRE, then expanded it into a five-week fast-track program covering safety training and JobSiteReady certification, with Meta guaranteeing a job at one of its data center sites for graduates.

[事实] She says the program was launched about four months earlier under the name America's Workforce Academy, that 40,000 Americans applied, that 250 people have graduated, and that retention stands at 90 percent.

[事实] She cites a statement by David Sacks that there are 500,000 open jobs for these roles over the next couple of years, and identifies the barrier as the inability of workers living paycheck to paycheck to take unpaid time off.

[事实] She says welders in Pittsburgh make $100,000–$120,000 a year, and that the program pays trainees what they would earn on the job rather than an apprentice-level fee.

[推测] Guaranteeing employment at graduation is what makes the program different from existing training pipelines, since the transcript identifies the fear of training without a job as a primary deterrent.

[23:55] **Scaling Through an Industry Alliance**

[事实] Powell McCormick says Meta deliberately kept its name out of the program because it wants other companies recruiting these workers involved, and that it partners with unions including the building trades led by Sean McGarvey.

[事实] She says union leadership has positioned itself against the anti-data-center backlash and has said it will support only candidates who support data centers, and that Google's Ruth Porat is convening an alliance that Meta and BlackRock's Larry Fink will join.

[事实] She says Mary Barra of GM has asked for 1,000 trained employees, and that the curriculum can be delivered in community colleges and VFW halls, with the Association of Builders and Contractors providing thousands of locations.

[事实] Chamath presses on why certifications like JobSiteReady and OSHA awareness are expensive outside of Meta's backing, and Powell McCormick's answer focuses on paying trainees full wages rather than explaining the certification cost itself.

[推测] The cross-company alliance suggests Meta sees labor scarcity, not model competition, as the binding constraint on the buildout—a reading consistent with the panel's framing but not stated as a conclusion in the transcript.

[26:57] **The Hard Question: Settlement, Children, and Culture**

[事实] Jason Calacanis questions Meta's impact on children, calls the products addictive, cites a $17 billion settlement he compares to the tobacco and BP cases, and asks how internal culture has changed as Meta builds AI companion products.

[事实] Powell McCormick says she disagrees with essentially every premise, notes she has been at the company eight months and on the board for a year, and says the team is focused on empowering and supporting parents.

[事实] She says Meta reached an agreement with 52 bipartisan attorneys general under which, in signing states, users under 18 are limited to two hours a day with the app shutting off afterward and at night, and no notifications are sent during the school day.

[事实] She says Meta agreed to add $5 billion and reduce the limit to one hour of use if YouTube, TikTok, and Snap join, and that she is a mother of six daughters, which makes the issue personal to her.

[推测] The under-18 defaults are described as conditional on other platforms joining only in the one-hour case, and the transcript does not establish whether the two-hour limit is already in force in all signing states.

[30:41] **Parental Control and Platform Defaults**

[事实] David Sacks argues the new defaults are an improvement, defends Meta against what he calls a "pile on" by adults suing over a voluntary product, and says parental control, not government control, should determine what kids use and for how long.

[事实] Chamath says content is syndicated across platforms, making parental limits a "whack-a-mole" problem that is a full-time job, and argues company-level labeling and governance decisions are therefore deeply helpful.

[事实] Powell McCormick cites a story about Instagram limits affecting college football recruiting as evidence that the app genuinely shuts off and requires parental override, and notes the feature is opt-out rather than advisory.

[推测] The panel's convergence on parental override rather than a hard legal ban suggests the agreement's practical effect depends on how many parents leave the defaults in place.

[34:22] **Gamification, Addiction, and Recursive Self-Improvement**

[事实] Chamath says the industry became addicted to gamification and to designing products to increase time spent, that increasing time on app was an internal Facebook focus, and that Facebook and YouTube's algorithm were simply too good at addicting users.

[事实] He predicts the term "recursive self-improvement," or RSI, will be used constantly over the next 18 months, and argues that with time as the reward function, every app—down to Microsoft Word—will proliferate indefinitely absent explicit thresholds.

[事实] Jason asks how this informs product decisions for new products like Muse and Meta's AI bots.

[推测] Chamath's RSI framing implies that the child-safety defaults are a necessary external constraint on product design rather than something the optimization process will produce on its own.

[35:31] **Meta Glasses and the Wearables Bet**

[事实] Chamath questions whether any face-worn device has durable repeat use, noting Google Glass and Apple's VR efforts tapered off, and asks what the repeated use case is.

[事实] Powell McCormick says engineers have worked on the glasses for ten years, that style and weight drove Mark Zuckerberg's attention, and highlights listening to music and calls, conversation focus that isolates a speaker's voice, and translation of speech and signage.

[事实] She says Meta has an exclusive partnership with Ray-Ban and Oakley versions for extreme sports, and shows a video about blind veterans in Austin whose feedback with Meta engineers shaped features like reading aloud and placing calls.

[事实] Chamath says the battery life and control now work where earlier devices failed, and argues glasses are less intrusive than holding a phone, bringing users into the real world rather than pulling them out of it.

[推测] The veterans' use case is presented as evidence that the form factor has a functional, non-novelty purpose, which the transcript offers as the answer to the stickiness question rather than measured retention data.

[41:35] **AI Safety, Regulation, and Open Source**

[事实] Asked about Dario Amodei's essay and an unverified report about Zuckerberg wanting to move as fast as possible, Powell McCormick agrees with Satya Nadella that safety is paramount and says Mark Zuckerberg, Alex Wang, and Nat Friedman treat model safety as a core concern.

[事实] She says Zuckerberg's view is rooted in 20 years of a business model of connecting people, that 3.6 billion people use Meta's platforms daily and 200 million small businesses operate on them, and that Zuckerberg believes democratizing AI will produce a more stable society.

[事实] She cites Zuckerberg's essay "The Future is for Everyone" and says Meta was proud to release the first American open-source model a few weeks earlier.

[推测] Meta's position as described treats broad distribution as the safety strategy itself, which is a direct contrast to a caution-first regulatory posture and explains the panel's framing of Zuckerberg as holding a "very different view."

## 播客点评/总结

The episode's main value is the Louisiana case study. Rather than arguing data center economics in the abstract, it puts a superintendent and a state commerce secretary on stage with specific numbers—sales tax growth in the hundreds of percent, teacher checks in the tens of thousands of dollars, water use below prior agricultural use, and a utility arrangement where the operator funds generation, upgrades, and storm costs. For anyone trying to understand why local outcomes and national polling diverge so sharply, that section is the most useful part of the conversation.

The middle section on workforce development is the second highlight. The fiber technician shortage, the paycheck-to-paycheck barrier to unpaid training, the five-week program with a job guarantee, and the cross-company alliance with Google and BlackRock give a concrete account of what an AI buildout's labor bottleneck actually looks like. The $100,000–$120,000 welder wages and GM's request for 1,000 trained workers make the argument tangible.

The limitations are structural. This is an executive making her case on a friendly stage: the numbers are self-reported, there is no independent verification, no critic of the Louisiana deal is present, and the panel accepts the framing quickly. The child-safety exchange is the one genuinely adversarial moment, and it is compressed—Jason Calacanis makes a serious allegation, Powell McCormick disputes the premise and pivots to the agreement's terms, and there is no follow-up on the underlying research or on how the new defaults interact with the AI companion products that prompted the question. Meta's own AI investments, engagement economics, and open-source release are largely taken at face value. [推测] The economic impact figures would be strongest if cross-checked against independent parish budget data.

Suitable audiences include investors and operators evaluating data center siting and local political risk, workforce and community-college leaders interested in the training pipeline, and policy watchers tracking how AI companies are repositioning themselves ahead of regulation. Listeners seeking adversarial scrutiny of Meta, or a full treatment of AI safety, should treat this as one side's case rather than a balanced debate. [推测] Given the episode's structure, the summary value is highest for the Louisiana and workforce segments and lowest for the settlement and safety discussion.
