# Allende, Pinochet, and the Chilean Coup of 1973

## 概览

This episode examines how Augusto Pinochet’s image remains contested: Margaret Thatcher praised him in 1999 for defeating communism, backing Britain in the Falklands, and leaving behind a free-market economy, while the hosts contrast that defense with the coup, killings, torture, and repression that followed.

The discussion traces Chile’s crisis in 1973: Allende’s socialist reforms had energized poor and rural supporters but alarmed middle-class and conservative groups amid inflation, shortages, constitutional conflict, and U.S. Cold War interference. The hosts emphasize that the United States helped create the climate for a coup, but they argue the Chilean military also had its own agency.

The narrative follows the failed Tanquetazo, General Prats’s fall, Pinochet’s unexpected rise, the September 11 coup, Allende’s last speech and suicide, and the brutal post-coup repression. The conclusion rejects simple rehabilitation of Pinochet while also warning against treating the story as a flat morality play with no Chilean social divisions.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Thatcher’s Case for Pinochet**

[事实] The episode opens with Margaret Thatcher’s 1999 House of Lords defense of Pinochet, crediting him with defeating communism, building a free-enterprise economy, and creating a route back to democracy.  
[事实] The hosts recall Pinochet’s arrest in Britain on a Spanish extradition request over human rights charges and his eventual return to Chile on health grounds.  
[推测] The opening frames the episode around why a figure widely associated with dictatorship could still command loyalty among some conservatives and anti-communists.

[02:35] **Chile’s Polarized Memory**

[事实] The hosts say Gabriel Boric admired Allende, while Jose Antonio Kast is described as a right-wing populist with family and student-era links to Pinochet’s political world.  
[事实] They identify three reasons for Thatcher’s sympathy: Cold War anti-communism, perceived overlap with free-market economics, and Pinochet’s support for Britain during the Falklands War.  
[推测] The discussion suggests that Chile’s 1973 crisis still functions as a live political memory rather than a settled historical episode.

[05:00] **Allende’s Reforms and the Social Breakdown**

[事实] Allende’s government redistributed land, nationalized copper and banks, expanded welfare spending, and saw inflation soar.  
[事实] Poor and rural Chileans could see Allende as someone who gave them dignity, while middle-class professionals faced vanished savings, empty shelves, black markets, and fear of more radical left-wing rule.  
[事实] The hosts explicitly describe the conflict as class-based.  
[推测] The contrast explains why Allende could be both a democratic hope for some and an existential threat for others.

[06:43] **American Interference and the Chilean Army**

[事实] After the 1973 congressional elections, the Americans had largely given up on stopping Allende by democratic means before his term ended.  
[事实] The hosts argue U.S. policymakers had few qualms about interference because they saw the Cold War as an unclean fight against Soviet methods.  
[事实] By 1973, U.S. intelligence had links with Chilean officers, whom the hosts describe as a closed, poorly paid military caste.  
[推测] The episode treats Kissinger as an emblem of U.S. foreign policy while cautioning that responsibility was broader than one person.

[09:24] **The Failed Tanquetazo**

[事实] At the end of June 1973, Roberto Souper led a small tank-based coup attempt in Santiago, with about 16 vehicles and 80 soldiers.  
[事实] The mutineers fired on the presidential palace and Defense Ministry, but General Carlos Prats, with help from his chief of staff Augusto Pinochet, helped shut the revolt down.  
[事实] Allende addressed a large crowd from La Moneda after the failure and told supporters to trust the government.  
[推测] The hosts present the failure as a rehearsal that taught anti-Allende officers that a better-organized coup could succeed.

[12:08] **The Fall of General Prats**

[事实] The CIA saw Prats as a major obstacle because he supported constitutional obedience.  
[事实] In Las Condes, Prats fired at a car after being insulted, then discovered the driver was Alejandrina Cox; the incident damaged him despite later apologies.  
[事实] Officers’ wives demonstrated outside Prats’s house, and El Mercurio, described as funded by ITT and the CIA, helped build antipathy toward him.  
[事实] Prats resigned, and Pinochet succeeded him as army commander on August 22, 1973.

[15:51] **Pinochet the Underestimated Officer**

[事实] Pinochet is described as the son of a customs official, a career soldier, a Freemason, and someone Allende considered loyal and reliable.  
[事实] U.S. reports characterized him as quiet, mild, honest, hard-working, modest, and too conventional to lead a coup, though likely to follow one.  
[事实] The hosts mention his large private library of 55,000 books, while joking that it contained no fiction or poetry.  
[推测] The central irony is that the man dismissed as dull and secondary became the decisive beneficiary of the crisis.

[18:49] **Chile on the Eve of the Coup**

[事实] August 1973 brought another truckers’ strike and a worsening constitutional fight between Allende’s government and Congress/Supreme Court.  
[事实] Congress passed a resolution accusing Allende of breaching the constitution, while Allende accused his opponents of undermining democracy.  
[事实] The hosts say some people claim Allende planned a September 11 referendum speech, but they say this cannot be known.  
[事实] Ariel Dorfman’s recollection is used to show that people around the palace knew the army might act but still trusted Chile’s non-intervention tradition and Pinochet’s loyalty.

[22:55] **Misconceptions About the Coup**

[事实] After the break, the hosts say the 1973 coup is often treated as an archetypal Latin American tragedy.  
[事实] They argue that Pinochet did not plan the coup from the beginning and only became seriously involved late.  
[事实] They also argue the CIA did not mastermind the coup from Washington, though it knew about the plotting, welcomed it, and helped create the climate.  
[推测] The discussion shifts responsibility from a single puppet-master story toward a mix of U.S. pressure, Chilean military agency, and domestic polarization.

[25:25] **September 11: The Coup Begins**

[事实] The coup began at dawn on September 11, 1973, after being postponed from September 10, with the Navy capturing Valparaiso.  
[事实] Allende went to La Moneda with bodyguards and ministers, while communications failed and he was unable to reach Pinochet.  
[事实] By about 8:30 the coup leaders claimed control, and by about 9:00 only the presidential palace area remained outside their control.  
[事实] Allende refused both escape advice and the military’s offer of exile, saying he was the democratically elected president and would stand by the constitution.  
[推测] His continued trust in “poor old Pinochet” underscores how fully Pinochet’s betrayal surprised Allende’s circle.

[28:05] **Pinochet’s Ruthlessness and Allende’s Last Speech**

[事实] The hosts say intercepted military communications recorded Pinochet joking that Allende’s exile plane would never land and speaking about killing him and his family.  
[事实] Pinochet called for aircraft and helicopter gunships to strike the presidential palace.  
[事实] During the remaining hours, Allende gave a final radio speech expressing faith in Chile and saying future free people would build a better society.  
[推测] The hosts read the speech through Roman analogies, especially Cato’s refusal to submit to an overambitious general.

[30:24] **Allende’s Death and Its Myth**

[事实] As jets attacked La Moneda and smoke and tear gas filled the palace, Allende and roughly 30 others began moving toward surrender.  
[事实] According to Dr. Patricio Guijon’s account, Allende slipped away to Independence Hall and killed himself with an AK-47 given to him by Fidel Castro.  
[事实] Fidel Castro later told a Havana crowd that Allende died fighting Pinochet’s troops, and that version became popular among people who wanted a more martial ending.  
[事实] The hosts say a 2011 forensic examination found little doubt that Allende took his own life.  
[推测] The episode presents suicide rather than surrender as still fitting a martyr tradition.

[32:23] **Mass Arrests and Stadium Killings**

[事实] By the afternoon of September 11, the coup was effectively complete; some Allende ministers were killed and others taken to concentration camps.  
[事实] The hosts say the CIA estimated about 1,500 early deaths by late October, while the military initially rounded up roughly 13,000 to 14,000 people.  
[事实] The Chilean National Stadium held thousands of prisoners, with interrogations and torture carried out in the velodrome.  
[事实] Victor Jara, described as a folk singer and leading cultural figure of the Chilean left, was killed in Estadio Chile five days after the coup.

[34:34] **American Victims and Official Lies**

[事实] Charlie Horman, a Harvard graduate in Chile, was detained, taken to the national stadium, tortured, and killed after the coup.  
[事实] Frank Teruggi, who worked for a pro-Allende newspaper, was also tortured with electrodes and killed in the national stadium.  
[事实] The hosts say U.S. officials knew Horman was dead but told his father Ed and wife Joyce stories suggesting he had escaped.  
[事实] The State Department later asked Ed Horman to pay $900 to ship his son’s body back to the United States.  
[推测] The account implies U.S. officials were more concerned with containing embarrassment than confronting the new regime.

[36:46] **The Caravan of Death and DINA**

[事实] About a month after the coup, Pinochet set in motion the Caravan of Death, in which one of his associates and other officers toured provinces identifying leftists, trade unionists, and cultural figures.  
[事实] Those who surrendered were imprisoned, tortured, bayoneted, and shot.  
[事实] The hosts say this anticipated DINA, the national intelligence body associated with arresting, torturing, and disappearing people.  
[推测] The segment marks the transition from coup violence to organized state terror.

[37:30] **Pinochet Consolidates Personal Power**

[事实] The junta was expected to rotate leadership among the army, air force, and navy, with Pinochet going first because he was oldest and the army was senior.  
[事实] Pinochet did not hand over power; he pressured the other services into accepting him as “supreme chief of the nation” and built a personality cult.  
[事实] Supporters portrayed him as chosen by God and specially approved by the Virgin Mary to destroy communism.  
[事实] By 1974, he held a level of supreme power the speakers say no one had predicted.  
[推测] The hosts connect his dark glasses, captain-general title, and larger hat to vanity and self-mythologizing.

[39:19] **The Case Against Rehabilitation**

[事实] The hosts say Pinochet’s regime killed probably 3,000 to 4,000 people and tortured about 20,000 to 30,000.  
[事实] Thatcher’s defense is linked to gratitude for Falklands War support and admiration for an economic revolution she saw as related to her own politics.  
[事实] Dominic says some listeners might argue Pinochet saved Chile from Cuban-style socialism, but he frames that as a possible argument rather than his own position.  
[事实] He says Western governments often supported authoritarian anti-communist regimes during the Cold War, including in Argentina, Brazil, and Uruguay.  
[推测] The discussion leaves Thatcher’s economic admiration politically understandable but morally inadequate against the record of violence.

[41:56] **Referendum, Memory, and Historical Judgment**

[事实] Dominic says Thatcher was wrong to portray Pinochet as simply choosing to lay down power; he says Pinochet was pressured into the late-1980s referendum by younger officers and political actors.  
[事实] The hosts stress that Chile had deep democratic traditions and that support for Pinochet still exists among people who believe he saved Chile from Marxism.  
[事实] Dominic argues historians should try to set aside their own political prejudices when judging such events.  
[推测] The episode’s method is to acknowledge the fears driving the right without excusing the destruction of democracy and repression.

[44:07] **Choice, Corruption, and Final Comparison**

[事实] Dominic contrasts Pinochet with Costa Rica’s Don Pepe and argues individuals in power have choices.  
[事实] He says Pinochet did not have to continue killing, maintain the Caravan of Death, or preserve the machinery of repression.  
[事实] He says Pinochet’s regime was corrupt and that Pinochet and his family amassed an enormous fortune.  
[事实] The hosts compare Pinochet to Franco more than Thatcher, while noting Thatcher herself rejected Argentine-style repression when it was described to her.  
[推测] The final judgment is that Cold War context helps explain the coup but does not rehabilitate Pinochet’s brutality.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s value lies in refusing a single caricature: it explains why Allende inspired loyalty, why opponents panicked, why U.S. interference mattered, and why Chilean actors still made independent choices. Its strongest material is the narrative buildup from Prats’s fall to Allende’s last hours and the immediate violence after the coup.

[推测] Its main limitation is that, because this is a narrative discussion rather than an archival debate, some claims are framed through the hosts’ interpretation, especially around hypothetical Pinochet defenders, CIA foreknowledge, and Allende’s possible referendum speech. The hosts themselves mark some points as unknowable or guessed.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who want a historically grounded account of the Chilean coup that takes Cold War politics seriously without using that context to excuse dictatorship, torture, or the destruction of Chile’s democratic order.
