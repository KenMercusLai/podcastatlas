# School of shock: teenagers get dimmer

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* opens with a discussion of falling academic performance among 15-year-olds across rich countries, using the OECD’s PISA tests as the main evidence. The central concern is that weaker test scores are not just an education story: they are linked in the discussion to earnings, longevity and national growth.

The show then turns to Miami’s boom. The segment explains how tax advantages, business-friendly politics, wealthy migrants, foreign capital and Donald Trump’s South Florida orbit have helped transform the city’s economy and status, while also raising questions about sustainability and climate exposure.

The final segment examines BBC Radio 2’s *Jeremy Vine Show* as a window into everyday British concerns. It argues that radio remains powerful in Britain because it is simple, collective and responsive to the public mood, moving easily between serious politics and lighter cultural topics.

## 分段落总结

[01:57] **Why Exam Results Matter**

[事实] The host says good grades matter because test scores can predict higher earnings and longer lives.

[事实] The discussion also links a “brainier country” with stronger economic growth.

[事实] Across the rich world, exam results are described as being in long-term decline.

[02:25] **New PISA Data**

[事实] The OECD’s PISA tests assess 15-year-olds every few years in maths, science and reading.

[事实] The latest data show student abilities in these areas have fallen sharply over the past three years.

[事实] The transcript says 15-year-olds today are scoring in maths and reading at levels associated with 14-year-olds ten years ago.

[03:13] **What PISA Measures**

[事实] The tests are described as assessing not only knowledge, but also the ability to apply it to real-world problems.

[事实] China, taking part for the first time since 2018, scored extremely well across the disciplines.

[事实] Singapore, Japan and Taiwan are also named as strong performers.

[04:11] **Long-Term Decline and Pandemic Effects**

[事实] PISA results have been carried out since 2000, and scores began flattening and falling around 2012.

[事实] Scores took a major dive after the pandemic, and the latest OECD average has fallen further by about the same amount as during the pandemic period.

[事实] The students taking the latest tests were 10 when COVID hit, so they lost school days.

[事实] The transcript also points to higher absence rates, grade inflation, weaker discipline and teachers spending more time keeping order.

[05:35] **Technology, AI and Screens**

[事实] The speaker says much classroom technology has been rolled out haphazardly, with growing doubt about whether it helps learning.

[事实] AI tools have only been widely available to schools for a couple of years, so their effects may not yet be fully visible in PISA results.

[事实] Survey evidence in the latest PISA round suggests weaker students are making the most use of AI.

[推测] The discussion frames AI as potentially helpful when used for learning, but harmful when used mainly as a shortcut to answers.

[07:23] **Lessons from Asian School Systems**

[事实] The speaker says Asian countries deserve continued study, even though some believe their education culture cannot be replicated in the West.

[事实] Asian systems are described as placing strong social emphasis on education and having parents willing to pay for extra lessons.

[事实] The speaker says these systems often prefer fewer, better-paid and more intensively trained teachers with larger classes, rather than focusing on smaller class sizes.

[08:21] **England as a Positive Example**

[事实] England is described as a strong European example in the latest PISA round.

[事实] Its science scores appear to have risen, while maths and literacy stayed roughly level as many other countries declined.

[事实] The segment credits England’s more knowledge-rich curriculum, tougher inspections and return to more tried-and-tested teaching methods.

[推测] The speaker suggests England’s approach is controversial but worth another look in light of the results.

[11:07] **Miami’s Boom**

[事实] Miami is described as attracting investment from technology, property and politics.

[事实] Central Miami’s economy was roughly one quarter the size of Manhattan’s 15 years ago and is now about half the size.

[事实] Jeff Bezos and Mark Zuckerberg are mentioned as examples of wealthy people connected to the city’s rise.

[12:02] **Domestic Pull Factors**

[事实] People are moving to Miami from places such as San Francisco and New York, where living costs, office rents and taxes are high.

[事实] Florida has no state income tax, and property levies on the priciest homes are described as less than 1%.

[事实] Former mayor Francis Suarez is described as having aggressively courted investors, including Ken Griffin.

[13:04] **Foreign and Latin American Capital**

[事实] Miami hotels are described as full of investors, hedge fund managers and consultants.

[事实] Saudi Arabia has opened an office in the city, and Gulf governments own assets including hotels and crypto exchanges based in Miami.

[事实] A second wave of capital from Latin America is described, with investors choosing Miami assets, especially luxury condos.

[14:04] **Trump and South Florida Politics**

[事实] Donald Trump’s Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach and another hotel in East Miami are presented as part of South Florida’s political pull.

[事实] The transcript says Trump has filled his inner circle with Floridians, including Marco Rubio, Susie Wiles and Todd Blanch.

[事实] Palm Beach is described as having many lobbyists, with Ballard named as a major lobbying firm based there.

[15:10] **Economic Growth and Winners**

[事实] Between 2020 and 2024, Miami metro area GDP grew by almost 25%.

[事实] Property now makes up about a fifth of the economy, and construction jobs are soaring.

[事实] Corporate services such as law and private family offices are booming, along with services demanded by wealthy families.

[15:49] **Sustainability Questions**

[事实] The segment says Miami’s boom predates Trump’s second term but may face a challenge when Trump leaves office in 2029.

[事实] Florida is America’s fourth-largest state economy, but the state spent less on research, development and productivity than ten other states last year.

[推测] The speaker questions whether a boom based heavily on wealthy people meeting and doing business can last if capital can leave as easily as it arrived.

[17:08] **Climate and Insurance Risk**

[事实] In 2025, Miami had 17 house sales above $50 million, almost half of such sales in America.

[事实] These homes are described as effectively uninsurable because many are built on flat, sea-level areas such as South Beach and Coconut Grove.

[事实] Miami is described as highly vulnerable to hurricanes, sea-level rise and difficult to defend with sea walls because of flat land and porous rock.

[推测] The segment concludes that Miami’s boom may be short-sighted and could reverse quickly if conditions deteriorate.

[19:15] **Jeremy Vine and Britain’s Subconscious**

[事实] The episode’s final segment asks what Britons care about beyond headline topics such as immigration and the economy.

[事实] BBC Radio 2’s *Jeremy Vine Show* is presented as a guide to everyday British concerns.

[事实] The show’s topics have included social housing, vertical drinking, pornography and garden centres.

[20:04] **Radio 2’s Popularity**

[事实] Britain listens to about one billion hours of radio every week, roughly as much as 20 years ago.

[事实] Radio 2 is described as by far the most popular station.

[事实] Its appeal is linked to simplicity and light entertainment that can draw audiences toward higher-brow programming.

[20:55] **Seriousness and Silliness**

[事实] Radio 2 is described as cheerfully unpretentious, mixing classics like The Beatles with newer chart-toppers such as Dua Lipa’s “Houdini.”

[事实] The *Jeremy Vine Show* switches between serious analysis, such as discussion of war in Iran, and lighter topics such as ten-pin bowling.

[推测] The segment presents this mix as part of the programme’s broad appeal.

[22:04] **Radio as Public Mood**

[事实] The transcript contrasts the online world, described as atomised and lonely, with radio as a collective experience.

[事实] Phone-in radio shows are described as responsive to the public mood.

[事实] Radio 2 discussed mass fly-tipping before the House of Lords began an inquiry, and parents phoned in about online harms before the Online Safety Act became law.

[推测] The segment suggests policymakers and observers could learn from listening to such programmes.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value lies in connecting data-heavy subjects with everyday consequences. The education segment makes PISA scores feel politically and economically important, while the Miami segment uses concrete examples of taxes, property, lobbying and climate risk to explain a city’s boom.

A strength of the episode is its range: it moves from schooling and global test scores to urban economics and then to British radio culture without losing a clear explanatory thread. The recurring theme is how institutions, incentives and public habits shape outcomes over time.

[推测] Its limitation is that each topic is necessarily compressed. The education discussion does not deeply test competing explanations for falling scores, and the Miami segment raises large climate and sustainability risks without exploring possible policy responses in detail.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in education policy, urban economics, American politics, British media culture and concise current-affairs analysis.
