# Elbows up, again: a US-Canada trade flare-up

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* leads with the latest breakdown in US-Canada trade talks. The discussion focuses on why negotiations between Donald Trump’s administration and Mark Carney’s government collapsed, what new tariffs mean for Canada, and why Carney faces a difficult paradox: Canada needs to reduce dependence on America, but to do that it first needs enough stability with America to attract investment.

The second segment examines Japan’s “employment ice age generation”, people who entered the labour market after the country’s early-1990s bubble burst. Many were pushed into insecure or low-paid work, and even those who later found stable jobs often still carry long-term earnings scars.

The final segment reflects on famous sieges in military history, prompted by Christopher Nolan’s adaptation of *The Odyssey*. It distinguishes between sieges with symbolic civilisational importance and those with vast strategic or material consequences.

## 分段落总结

[01:18] **Episode Introduction**

[事实] Host Jason Palmer introduces the episode as covering Canada-US trade tensions, Gen X workers in Japan’s labour market, and the historical importance of sieges.

[01:54] **US-Canada Trade Talks Break Down**

[事实] Trade talks between America and Canada appeared close to agreement before the weekend, but Prime Minister Mark Carney called off negotiations at the last minute.

[事实] Without a deal, new trade barriers came into force, and Carney said Canada would match Washington’s tariffs dollar for dollar.

[事实] Hal Hodson says Canada needs to diversify trade and reorient its economy, but doing so requires investment that is harder to attract during a major trade war with Donald Trump.

[推测] The episode frames Carney’s central dilemma as needing peace with Trump in order eventually to become less dependent on America.

[03:31] **Canada’s Vulnerability to Trump’s Tariffs**

[事实] Canada and Mexico were the first countries Trump targeted with tariffs, and Trump had been talking about Canada as a “51st state” since before taking office.

[事实] Hodson describes Canada’s economy as highly integrated with America’s, making retaliation costly.

[事实] Carney’s decision to walk out of talks is described as his closest move toward defiance so far, though Hodson expects him to return to negotiations.

[推测] Canadian public opinion may reward visible resistance to Trump, even if Canada’s economic constraints limit how confrontational Carney can truly be.

[04:57] **Why the Deal Failed**

[事实] Hodson says three major American demands helped derail the talks.

[事实] The first was excluding medium and heavy-duty trucks from tariff reductions, which would hit a key part of Canada’s auto industry.

[事实] The second was asking Canada to curb its ability to strike trade deals with other countries.

[事实] The third was attempting to weaken protections for French language and culture in Canada, including rules affecting sales into Quebec.

[推测] The French-language demand was especially politically dangerous for Carney because Quebec has provincial elections coming up and separatists could use such a concession against him.

[06:39] **Canadian Provincial Politics Complicate Retaliation**

[事实] The Canadian response is not presented as fully unified because different provinces have different interests.

[事实] Quebec faces high effective tariff exposure, while Alberta has almost zero tariff exposure on exports to America.

[事实] Alberta supplies more than half of the oil America imports from outside its borders.

[事实] Cutting off Canadian energy supplies to America has been discussed as retaliation, but Carney is reluctant because it would anger Alberta.

[推测] Alberta’s pending referendum on whether to remain in Canada makes energy retaliation politically risky for the federal government.

[08:03] **Economic Effects of the New Tariffs**

[事实] New tariffs came into effect at one minute past midnight on Saturday morning on about $20 billion of Canadian exports.

[事实] These tariffs come on top of existing tariffs on aluminium, wood, cars and vehicles.

[事实] Canada’s economy shrank at the beginning of 2025, was flat in the second half of the year, and had only just started to improve.

[事实] Hodson says the new tariffs could push the economy back toward stagnation or contraction, raise inflation, and threaten about 90,000 Canadian jobs.

[09:11] **Carney’s Waiting Game**

[事实] Carney said he would wait two weeks and then, on September 8th, match American tariffs dollar for dollar.

[事实] Hodson says Carney’s main option appears to be waiting, especially for the American midterms.

[事实] Tariffs are already raising prices for some Americans, including people in Maine buying Canadian goods.

[推测] The hope is that domestic US political pressure might eventually make Trump return to talks in a less aggressive mood.

[11:40] **Japan’s Employment Ice Age Generation**

[事实] Moeka Iida interviews Torigoe Atsushi, a 50-year-old man in Tokyo whose career has mostly been spent in stock rooms at a luxury fashion firm.

[事实] Torigoe says he graduated during bad economic conditions and could only find low-paying jobs.

[事实] His income has barely increased over the years and recently fell after his employer cut bonuses and revised its payment scheme.

[事实] He later saw recruitment ads from the same firm offering higher base salaries to younger recruits.

[12:29] **What the Employment Ice Age Means**

[事实] Japan’s “employment ice age generation” refers to people who graduated and entered the workforce roughly between 1993 and 2004.

[事实] Their experience was shaped by the bursting of Japan’s economic bubble in the early 1990s.

[事实] Many struggled with precarious, insecure work and low pay.

[事实] Their hardship is associated in Japanese public discussion with social problems such as hikikomori.

[13:24] **New Wage Growth Leaves Older Workers Behind**

[事实] Japan is emerging from decades of deflation, with prices rising and firms increasingly able to pass costs to consumers and raise wages.

[事实] These pay rises are concentrated among younger workers.

[事实] The ice age generation is described as being left behind again, after missing the better employment conditions before and after their entry into the labour market.

[推测] The labour-market recovery may be worsening the generation’s sense of unfairness because younger recruits are gaining from conditions that older workers never had.

[14:05] **How the Bubble’s Collapse Shaped Careers**

[事实] During Japan’s bubble era in the 1970s and 1980s, companies hired in large numbers and actively courted students.

[事实] After the bubble burst in the 1990s, Japan’s rigid employment system made firms reluctant to shed existing workers, so they froze hiring instead.

[事实] The ratio of job openings to college applicants fell from nearly three to less than one by 2000.

[事实] Many younger people were pushed into irregular work.

[15:04] **Irregular Work and Long-Term Earnings Damage**

[事实] Japan’s labour market is described as polarised between regular workers with strong protections and seniority-based pay, and irregular workers with lower pay and more precarious contracts.

[事实] Many ice age workers entered irregular work, and terms such as “freeter” and “parasite singles” became common at the time.

[事实] Analysts told Iida that the share of ice age workers in regular employment has caught up with older cohorts.

[事实] Even so, their earnings remain much lower than older generations because early career instability damaged long-term pay trajectories.

[17:01] **Retirement, Pensions and Housing Risks**

[事实] Japan’s pension scheme is tied to lifetime earnings, creating future risks for ice age workers with low career earnings.

[事实] One possible solution is for people to work longer, as many older people in Japan already do.

[事实] An expert warned that many could end up on welfare, while the welfare system is not designed to support elderly people for long periods.

[事实] Ice age workers are less likely than older generations to own homes, and Japan’s rental market is hostile toward older tenants.

[推测] The generation’s problems may become a fiscal and housing challenge as they age.

[18:21] **Policy Responses in Japan**

[事实] The Japanese government has discussed the ice age generation for a long time.

[事实] Many programmes have focused on helping them find stable and regular jobs.

[事实] The government recently produced a three-year plan mentioning asset building and housing.

[事实] Iida says more serious thinking will be needed.

[19:03] **Sieges as Historical Turning Points**

[事实] Richard Gawket says Christopher Nolan’s nearly three-hour adaptation of *The Odyssey* made him think about real sieges from military history.

[事实] He says sieges are often hinge points of conflict, with campaigns and even civilisations depending on them.

[事实] Military historian Jeremy Black identifies two categories: totemic sieges and utilitarian sieges.

[20:03] **Totemic Sieges**

[事实] Totemic sieges are described as having civilisational significance or wider cultural importance.

[事实] The 1683 Ottoman siege of Vienna is given as an example; Vienna, then capital of the Holy Roman Empire, withstood two months of assault.

[事实] The Ottoman failure to take Vienna ended their ambitions to expand into Christian Europe.

[事实] The 1836 Alamo siege is also cited, where 200 Texans resisted a much larger Mexican force led by Antonio Lopez de Santa Anna.

[推测] The Alamo’s lasting importance came less from military success than from its later use as a rallying symbol.

[21:24] **Utilitarian Sieges**

[事实] Utilitarian sieges are described as strategically important and often involving vast numbers of men and material.

[事实] Alexander the Great’s siege of Tyre in 332 BC required building a giant causeway to reach the island fortress.

[事实] The siege of Leningrad lasted more than two years during the Second World War, flattened the city, and killed up to 1.5 million people.

[事实] The great siege of Malta in 1565 involved 500 Knights Hospitaller and their foot soldiers holding out for four months against at least 40,000 Ottoman attackers.

[事实] The episode ends with the host saying *The Intelligence* will return tomorrow.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest section is the opening discussion of Canada’s trade dilemma. It clearly connects immediate tariff politics with longer-term questions about investment, diversification and Canada’s internal provincial tensions.

The Japan segment is valuable because it avoids a simple generational stereotype. It notes that many ice age workers did eventually enter regular employment, while still showing how early labour-market conditions can permanently depress earnings and retirement security.

The siege segment is shorter and more essay-like. It offers vivid historical examples, but it is less analytical than the trade and labour-market discussions.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in international political economy, demographic labour-market problems, and compact historical context rather than a single-topic deep dive.
