# A Good Walk Spoiled

## 概览

Malcolm Gladwell examines why Los Angeles reserves enormous tracts of scarce urban green space for private golf clubs while ordinary residents are confined to narrow paths outside their fences. His central argument is that golf courses combine inefficient land use, exclusivity, and unusually generous public subsidies.

The episode traces those privileges through California tax law. A 1960 constitutional amendment exempted golf courses from taxation based on their land’s “highest and best use,” while Proposition 13 later froze their already reduced assessments near 1978 levels.

Gladwell then explores whether member-owned clubs have effectively changed ownership as generations of members have been replaced. Using the Ship of Theseus paradox, he argues that officials adopted a convenient theory of institutional continuity that preserves benefits for clubs despite wholesale turnover in their membership.

The episode concludes that subsidized private courses function like aristocratic institutions. Gladwell proposes opening them to the public during evenings and weekends, citing courses in Scotland and Canada that combine private golf with public access.

## 分段落总结

[02:15] **A private park behind a fence**

[事实] Gladwell describes the numerous large, beautiful private country clubs occupying valuable land across western Los Angeles.

[事实] Runners outside Brentwood Country Club must use a narrow, rocky track because the course is enclosed and offers no public sidewalk.

[事实] Gladwell frames the episode around why residents are excluded from what appears to be a magnificent urban park.

[04:34] **What the land could become**

[事实] Landscape architect Dai Zock observes mature trees and substantial open space inside Brentwood Country Club, features that are uncommon in Los Angeles.

[事实] Zock says that converting the course into a welcoming park would begin with removing its barbed wire.

[事实] Gladwell states that a typical golf course occupies roughly 200 acres but may accommodate only about 72 golfers at once.

[事实] Maintaining such a course requires extensive mowing, pesticides, landscaping, and large quantities of bunker sand.

[06:58] **Golf as an executive obsession**

[事实] Economist Lee Biggerstaff cross-referenced golf scores in a USGA database with the chief executives of 1,500 large publicly traded companies.

[事实] Of those executives, 363 appeared in the database; the average was about 15 rounds per year, while the top 10 percent played at least 37.

[事实] Gladwell calculates that 37 rounds represent more than 160 hours on the course, excluding travel, preparation, practice, and unrecorded play.

[事实] Biggerstaff’s research found that heavier CEO golfing was associated with poorer company performance and a greater likelihood of dismissal.

[事实] One executive in the sample recorded approximately 148 rounds in a single year.

[13:19] **Bob Hope and the 1960 tax exemption**

[事实] California property taxation ordinarily considers a property’s “highest and best use,” which would make centrally located golf-course land extremely valuable.

[事实] In 1960, California country clubs promoted a constitutional amendment exempting their land from that standard.

[事实] At the time, many Los Angeles clubs excluded Jewish and Black members, yet sought tax treatment resembling that of a public amenity.

[事实] Bob Hope, a hugely popular entertainer and enthusiastic golfer with an everyman persona, became the public face of the campaign.

[事实] The measure passed and remains part of Article 13 of the California Constitution.

[19:32] **Proposition 13 compounds the advantage**

[事实] Proposition 13, passed in 1978, set California property tax at one percent of assessed value and generally limited reassessment to ownership changes or new construction.

[事实] Property values were effectively anchored near pre-1978 levels until a qualifying transfer occurred.

[事实] Golf clubs therefore received a second benefit: their already reduced assessments were frozen so long as the clubs were not deemed to have changed ownership.

[事实] Gladwell criticizes this system for favoring long-held property regardless of an owner’s financial need.

[23:24] **Has a member-owned club changed hands?**

[事实] Most private Los Angeles country clubs use equity ownership, meaning members receive shares that pass to replacements when they leave or die.

[事实] A 2010 newspaper article asked whether cumulative membership turnover should constitute a change of ownership under Proposition 13.

[事实] Los Angeles County concluded that it did not, reasoning that no single transfer involved more than 50 percent ownership.

[事实] A Bel-Air Country Club member estimated that only about 10 percent of its then-current members had belonged in 1978.

[25:44] **The Ship of Theseus and institutional identity**

[事实] Gladwell compares gradual membership replacement to the Ship of Theseus: if every plank is replaced one at a time, philosophers dispute whether it remains the same ship.

[事实] The “mereological” view treats identity as the sum of component parts, while the spatiotemporal-continuity view preserves identity through gradual change.

[事实] Philosopher Mark Cohen explains that counterexamples prevent the paradox from having a simple, universally compelling resolution.

[推测] Gladwell presents the county’s choice of spatiotemporal continuity as a policy decision favoring the clubs, rather than an unavoidable philosophical conclusion.

[30:20] **The public cost of private privilege**

[事实] Gladwell says Los Angeles ranks near the bottom of major US metropolitan areas for public park space, while its large golf courses remain both publicly subsidized and closed to most residents.

[事实] He reports an informal estimate valuing Los Angeles Country Club’s land at $9 billion, which would imply roughly $90 million in annual property tax under ordinary conditions.

[事实] The club reportedly paid about $200,000, creating an estimated annual difference of $89.8 million.

[事实] Gladwell and Cohen discuss how institutional continuity can preserve inherited rights and privileges despite changes in circumstances and beneficiaries.

[推测] Their comparison with aristocracy suggests that neutral-sounding rules of identity can perpetuate inequality when attached to valuable legal privileges.

[34:34] **Opening subsidized courses to the public**

[事实] Gladwell argues that clubs benefiting from public subsidies should remove their barriers and give residents access during evenings and weekends.

[事实] St Andrews in Scotland is open to the public on Sundays.

[事实] Toronto’s Rosedale Golf Club is private during its golf season but available at other times for walking, Frisbee, and cross-country skiing.

[事实] At Brentwood on a Saturday afternoon, Gladwell and Zock observe more people using the narrow exterior running track than the golf course itself.

[推测] The contrast reinforces the episode’s case that these courses distribute scarce urban space according to privilege rather than public demand.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s greatest strength is its ability to connect an ordinary streetscape—a crowded dirt path beside an empty green expanse—to larger questions about taxation, ownership, and inherited privilege. Its combination of reporting, economic research, legal history, and philosophy makes an abstract policy problem vivid.

[推测] The Ship of Theseus section is especially effective because it shows how a seemingly technical definition of ownership can determine who receives millions of dollars in public benefits. The humorous delivery keeps a dense discussion accessible without obscuring its central argument.

[推测] Its principal limitation is its openly adversarial framing: Gladwell announces that he hates golf and concentrates on evidence supporting reform of private clubs. The episode offers relatively little from club representatives or defenders of the existing tax treatment, and its headline valuation is described as an informal estimate.

[推测] The episode is particularly suitable for listeners interested in urban planning, taxation, inequality, public space, and the ways historical legal privileges persist inside modern institutions.
