# Even More Maximum Pressure: Iran Sanctions, AI Offshoring, and Wage Premiums

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* first examines America’s new economic offensive against Iran, introduced by Treasury Secretary Scott Besant as “Operation Economic Outcast.” The discussion argues that the rhetoric was sweeping, but the concrete measures announced were more piecemeal than many expected.

The second segment looks at the Philippines’ offshoring industry, where AI has not caused the predicted collapse in jobs. Instead, workers and firms are using AI to handle more work and more complex tasks, while basic roles and workplace monitoring are becoming more vulnerable.

The final segment explains why moving cities can raise wages. It distinguishes between high wages caused by skilled people clustering in a city and a “place premium” created by the city itself, then considers how easier mobility could improve wages and worker-firm matching.

## 分段落总结

[01:49] **America’s New Iran Sanctions Push**

[事实] The episode opens its main discussion with America’s Treasury Secretary announcing “Operation Economic Outcast,” described as an unprecedented campaign against Iran and its enablers.

[事实] Scott Besant framed the effort as “Economic D-Day” and a major financial offensive aimed partly at Iran’s trading partners.

[事实] The correspondent Fraser McIlwraith says the rhetoric sounds hollow and argues Iran is likely to shrug off the threats.

[推测] The segment presents the sanctions push as more about coercive signalling than an immediately decisive economic blow.

[03:06] **Maximum Pressure Plus**

[事实] Iran has faced American sanctions since the Islamic Revolution in 1979 and has long experience circumventing them.

[事实] The discussion compares the new effort with President Trump’s first-term “maximum pressure” strategy, which sought concessions on Iran’s nuclear programme.

[事实] The new plan includes enforcing an American naval blockade against Iranian oil exports and increasing pressure on trading partners and financial institutions that help Iran move money.

[事实] The actual announcement mostly involved smaller designations and renewed threats, rather than immediate major sanctions on Chinese banks or other large institutions.

[04:38] **Iran’s Economy Under Strain**

[事实] Besant said Iran’s economy is under immense pressure, and the correspondent agreed with that assessment.

[事实] The Iranian rial had lost nearly half its value against the dollar since the start of the year and hit an all-time low ahead of the announcement.

[事实] The transcript says Iran’s economy was already weak before the war because of decades of sanctions, and that fighting since February had destroyed parts of Iranian industry.

[事实] America’s blockade since April was said to have caused Iran’s oil exports to collapse, with Iran’s central-bank head reportedly saying oil income was at zero.

[推测] The episode suggests economic pain is real, but not necessarily enough to make soldiers abandon the regime or trigger collapse.

[05:53] **China and the UAE as Key Targets**

[事实] Besant avoided naming countries directly, but the discussion identifies China and the UAE as important trading partners that America would be expected to pressure.

[事实] China was described as by far the biggest importer of Iranian oil, importing about 90% of Iran’s oil last year despite sanctions.

[事实] Those oil purchases reportedly provided Iran with around half of its annual budget last year.

[事实] The UAE was described as both a hawkish American ally in the Gulf and a country with close commercial ties to Iran, providing around a third of Iran’s imports last year.

[事实] The UAE was also described as a hub for exchange houses, shadow banks and other channels used by Iran to evade sanctions.

[07:00] **Compliance and China Risks**

[事实] The UAE had already announced on August 18th that it would stop all trade and financial transactions with Iran.

[事实] The transcript says this followed Iranian attacks on Emirati tankers and accusations that Iran fired ballistic missiles at Emirati territory.

[事实] China was described as much harder for America to pressure because it was already objecting to new sanctions threats.

[事实] The correspondent says America may be reluctant to sanction large Chinese financial institutions because President Trump has been trying to reset relations with China.

[推测] The sanctions plan appears constrained by America’s broader strategic need to avoid another major trade dispute with China.

[08:22] **Iran’s Defiance and Escalation Risk**

[事实] Iran’s regime was described as defiant, while also aware of serious economic problems.

[事实] The speaker of Iran’s parliament reportedly warned that Iran would not survive without economic growth, regardless of military strength.

[事实] Iranian leaders had urged America to return to a June memorandum of understanding that would have allowed Iran to re-enter the global economy in exchange for ending fighting and foreswearing a nuclear bomb.

[事实] The correspondent says Iran may see America’s pivot to sanctions as evidence that Washington has lost appetite for a military campaign.

[事实] Possible Iranian responses discussed include retaliatory attacks on cooperating countries, renewed attacks on military bases or energy facilities, and pressure in the Strait of Hormuz.

[11:21] **Philippine Offshoring and AI**

[事实] The second main segment introduces Tresa, a worker in the Philippines’ offshore industry who works mostly for an American chiropractic firm.

[事实] Her work includes calling personal injury lawyers, using an American accent she has developed over many years.

[事实] Tresa starts work at 8pm in Manila and works long hours, but says AI has made parts of her job easier, especially email work.

[事实] Josh Spencer says the industry includes call centres, IT work and other tasks performed for companies, especially in America and Europe.

[13:03] **Why AI Has Not Wiped Out the Industry**

[事实] The episode says AI can theoretically do many traditional offshoring tasks, such as medical transcription or resolving simple customer queries.

[事实] Around 1.9 million Filipinos worked in the industry at the end of 2025, up 4% from the previous year and 20% from 2022.

[事实] The predicted mass destruction of the sector has not occurred.

[事实] The main explanation offered is that many customer complaints and support tasks are complex, messy and dependent on local context.

[事实] Tresa says some automated jobs will probably be replaced, but technical support and similar work still need people.

[15:15] **AI as Productivity Tool**

[事实] Companies may be offering more work to workers like Tresa because AI allows them to resolve more complaints per hour.

[事实] AI is helping some workers do more complex jobs that they might not previously have been able to learn.

[事实] Some workers have been able to raise rates or bill clients by resolved incidents rather than by the hour.

[事实] Josh Spencer says AI is already eating into some basic “tier one” work, such as data entry and simple order-status questions.

[推测] The segment frames AI as unevenly disruptive: it can upgrade some roles while eroding the lowest-rung entry points.

[16:56] **Stress, Entry-Level Jobs, and AI Management**

[事实] The industry body predicted employment in offshoring would grow by only 3% this year, slower than growth in 2024 and 2025.

[事实] The transcript says the industry has reduced projections for future employment.

[事实] A major concern is how young Filipinos can enter the industry if basic entry-level jobs disappear.

[事实] At a union event, workers discussed feeling forced to adapt to the speed of machines while doing more work without extra pay or time off.

[事实] Kyle, a call-centre worker in Manila since 2018, described AI tools that evaluate call quality and may contribute to termination decisions.

[19:40] **Moving Cities for Higher Wages**

[事实] The final segment says people move cities for many reasons, but often for the fundamental goal of increasing wages.

[事实] Vishnu Padmanabhan explains that city wages depend on two factors: the people in the city and the place-based effect of the city itself.

[事实] Cities with more skilled or higher-earning people can have higher average wages without necessarily benefiting every worker.

[事实] The place-based effect comes from agglomeration, including dense business activity and good transport infrastructure.

[20:27] **People Effects Versus Place Premiums**

[事实] The discussion explains that average city wages combine the characteristics of workers with the wage boost created by the place itself.

[事实] A place premium can raise wages more broadly when a city’s structure and economy make workers more productive.

[推测] For someone deciding where to move, looking only at average wages can be misleading if those wages mostly reflect who already lives there.

[21:13] **LinkedIn Evidence on City Premiums**

[事实] Research by Gaurav Karna of the University of California, San Diego, used job histories of 500 million LinkedIn users to separate worker talent from city effects.

[事实] The method tracks the same people as they move between cities, then estimates the wage change attributable to the place.

[事实] Within a country, 45% to 73% of city wage gaps were attributed to place.

[事实] Across borders, more than 90% of the wage gap between cities in different countries was attributed to differences in place.

[事实] The transcript gives the example that moving from Delhi to London would raise salary by nearly 300% because of differences between the two cities.

[22:46] **What Workers and Firms Can Learn**

[事实] Vaduz, the capital of Liechtenstein, had the biggest place premium in the database discussed.

[事实] Among larger countries, American cities tended to have the highest premiums, with San Francisco and New York named as examples.

[事实] In rich countries, cities with the highest wages also tend to have the highest location premiums.

[事实] In poorer countries, the relationship can break down; Bangalore is cited as a city with high average wages but not as high a place premium as some nearby smaller towns.

[事实] The study also finds that weaker matching between productive workers and productive firms can depress wages.

[24:19] **Policy Implications for Mobility**

[事实] The discussion says global free movement would raise wages in an ideal world, but border restrictions make that impossible.

[事实] Governments can still make movement within countries easier, especially in poorer countries where moving between cities is often difficult.

[事实] Suggested policies include better transport infrastructure and more affordable housing.

[事实] The closing point is that raising wages in one city can benefit the whole economy.

[推测] The segment implies that domestic mobility policy can be a practical substitute for the politically harder goal of cross-border labour mobility.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in testing familiar assumptions: that sanctions can easily break Iran, that AI will quickly destroy offshore service jobs, and that high-wage cities are always the best destinations for workers.

[推测] Its strongest moments come from combining concrete examples with broader analysis, especially the contrast between Tresa’s practical use of AI and Kyle’s concerns about AI-based monitoring.

[推测] The limitation is that each topic is treated briefly, so listeners get a sharp analytical overview rather than a deep policy history or detailed labour-market case study.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in geopolitics, AI and work, global services, migration, and urban economics.
