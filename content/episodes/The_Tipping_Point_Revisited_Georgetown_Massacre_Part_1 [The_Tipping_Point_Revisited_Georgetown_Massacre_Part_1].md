# The Tipping Point Revisited: Georgetown Massacre, Part 1

## 概览

Malcolm Gladwell revisits the fifty-seventh and final case of the Varsity Blues investigation: *United States v. Khoury*. Unlike the other defendants, wealthy businessman Amon Khoury refused to plead guilty, insisting that the payment connected to his daughter’s Georgetown admission was not a bribe.

The prosecution initially presented an apparently overwhelming case. A former Brown tennis teammate testified that Khoury paid $200,000 for a Georgetown recruiting slot even though his daughter was not qualified to compete at that level and did not intend to join the team.

The trial changed direction when the defense questioned Georgetown fundraiser Brenda Smith. Internal emails showed coaches and development staff openly discussing prospective recruits’ family wealth and donation potential, undermining Georgetown’s claim that fundraising and admissions operated independently.

The episode ultimately asks what separates an illegal admissions bribe from an institutionally accepted preference for wealthy applicants. Part one ends as the defense prepares to call a mystery witness whose testimony promises to create even greater problems for the prosecution.

## 分段落总结

[00:43] **The Forgotten Fifty-Seventh Varsity Blues Case**

[事实] The 2019 Varsity Blues investigation produced indictments against more than fifty people accused of paying to secure admission to elite universities for their children.

[事实] Gladwell identifies *United States v. Khoury* as the investigation’s fifty-seventh and final case, one the Justice Department declined to discuss with him.

[事实] The episode expands on material referenced only briefly in Gladwell’s book *Revenge of the Tipping Point*.

[02:45] **Why Gladwell Calls It the “Georgetown Massacre”**

[事实] Gladwell describes the Khoury case as riveting, unintentionally funny, heartbreaking, and built around a philosophical puzzle.

[事实] He says the case exposes the absurdities of elite universities and contains an exceptionally damaging cross-examination.

[事实] Because the full story would not fit into his book, he uses this two-part podcast story to present what he calls the “Georgetown massacre.”

[05:18] **Amon Khoury, Gordon Ernst, and the Alleged Deal**

[事实] Amon Khoury was a former Brown University varsity tennis player who wanted his daughter Katherine to play college tennis.

[事实] At a 2014 Brown reunion dinner, Khoury met former teammate Gordon “Gordy” Ernst, then Georgetown University’s tennis coach.

[事实] Prosecutors later described Ernst as a prolific participant in the admissions scheme who took roughly $3.5 million in bribes and sold nearly two dozen Georgetown recruiting slots.

[事实] The government alleged that one of those slots was sold to Khoury for his daughter.

[08:05] **Khoury Chooses a Trial**

[事实] Khoury hired defense attorneys Roy Black and Howard Srebnik and rejected a plea deal because he did not believe he had committed a crime.

[事实] He maintained that his payment was an act of generosity made after the relevant events, not a bribe offered beforehand.

[事实] All fifty-six other Varsity Blues defendants had pleaded guilty, making Khoury’s decision exceptionally risky.

[事实] Black initially believed the defense faced poor odds, while Srebnik said Khoury even wanted to testify before his lawyers persuaded him not to.

[12:34] **The Philosophical Problem of Bribery**

[事实] Law professor Leo Katz distinguishes acts considered inherently immoral, or *malum in se*, from conduct treated as criminal only because a law prohibits it, or *malum prohibitum*.

[事实] Katz places bribery in the first category, where fewer moral gray areas are normally expected.

[推测] Khoury’s defense therefore needed to challenge not only the prosecution’s evidence but also the seemingly self-evident assumption that his conduct constituted an inherently corrupt exchange.

[16:16] **The Prosecution’s Apparently Overwhelming Evidence**

[事实] Timothy Donovan testified that he acted as intermediary in an agreement under which Khoury would pay $200,000 for a Georgetown tennis recruiting slot.

[事实] Donovan said he collected $180,000 in cash from Khoury, retained $20,000, and delivered the remaining money to Ernst’s wife.

[事实] Donovan testified that Katherine Khoury was not qualified for Georgetown-level tennis and had no intention of playing there.

[事实] Her academic results were also described as substantially below those of typical Georgetown tennis recruits.

[事实] After several days of similar testimony, Gladwell says the case appeared disastrous for the defense.

[19:46] **Brenda Smith Takes the Stand**

[事实] On the fifth day, the defense called Brenda Smith, a senior Georgetown athletics fundraiser who appeared under subpoena.

[事实] Smith initially denied communicating with admissions officers, lobbying admissions, or sharing information about prospective recruits’ family wealth.

[事实] The defense possessed her employment emails and began confronting her with messages discussing property values, donor potential, and wealthy families with Georgetown coaches.

[事实] In one exchange, Smith acknowledged telling a soccer coach that a prospective player’s family owned a $5.6 million home.

[23:43] **Emails Expose Georgetown’s Interest in Wealth**

[事实] Additional emails referred to recruiting “really rich kids who can play,” families with six- or seven-figure resources, a $500,000 donor, and a prospect connected to wealth exceeding $500 million.

[事实] Smith and Georgetown witnesses continued to maintain that development work was separate from admissions.

[事实] The defense team believed Smith lost credibility by denying an influence of wealth that the emails appeared to document directly.

[事实] In another exchange with Ernst, Smith wrote that a prospective recruit “has no money at all” and “sounds dreadful.”

[推测] The cross-examination weakened the moral contrast between Khoury’s conduct and Georgetown’s own admissions-related cultivation of wealthy families.

[31:04] **Where Institutional Preference Ends and Bribery Begins**

[事实] Gladwell asks what meaningfully distinguishes Khoury and Ernst’s cash arrangement from Georgetown’s routine efforts to connect wealthy families, athletic recruitment, and donations.

[事实] Katz proposes that Ernst might legally have charged wealthy applicants large sums to attend a tennis camp and then preferentially recruited participants from that camp.

[事实] Ernst actually operated a summer tennis camp on Georgetown property, retained all its proceeds, and exercised broad discretion over both camp participants and tennis recruits.

[事实] An email described a potential recruit from a “well-positioned family,” to which Ernst replied that another mediocre player was in his “strike zone.”

[推测] The episode suggests that the alleged crime may have differed from accepted university practices chiefly in its directness and lack of institutional euphemism.

[37:05] **The Government’s Case Begins to Crack**

[事实] Gladwell identifies Smith’s cross-examination as the first major rupture in the prosecution’s case.

[事实] The defense next called an unidentified witness who, according to the episode, had a major surprise for federal prosecutors.

[事实] Part one ends before revealing that witness or the outcome of the trial.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its transformation of an apparently simple bribery prosecution into a difficult question about institutional legitimacy. Trial testimony and Georgetown’s own emails provide a concrete basis for examining how elite admissions systems treat wealth.

The recreated courtroom exchanges make a complicated legal case accessible and dramatic. Gladwell also openly acknowledges his admiration for Khoury’s defense attorneys, giving listeners useful notice that the narrative is strongly shaped by their perspective.

Its main limitation is that part one ends before the defense’s mystery witness, the verdict, or the prosecution’s full response can be assessed. The episode also focuses more heavily on Georgetown’s hypocrisy than on whether that hypocrisy legally excuses Khoury’s alleged conduct.

[推测] This episode will be especially valuable to listeners interested in elite education, legal strategy, institutional double standards, and the boundary between formally prohibited corruption and socially accepted influence.
