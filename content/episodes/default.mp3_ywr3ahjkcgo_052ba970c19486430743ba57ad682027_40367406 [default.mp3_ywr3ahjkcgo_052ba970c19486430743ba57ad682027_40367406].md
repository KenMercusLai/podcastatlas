# Planet Money Summer School World Tour: Nigeria and Kenya’s Development Experiments

## 概览

This episode of Planet Money Summer School visits Nigeria and Kenya to ask how countries can create more jobs, healthier populations, and stronger long-term growth. The show frames Africa not only as a region with major economic challenges, but also as a place generating practical economic ideas.

The first case study looks at Nigeria’s YouWin competition, a government program that gave large grants to small-business owners through a partly randomized process. The episode argues that direct capital, when paired with screening and training, helped some tiny firms grow and create jobs.

The second case study turns to Busia, Kenya, where randomized controlled trials became a major tool in development economics. Through studies on textbooks, deworming, HIV-risk education, and other interventions, the episode explores how economists test what actually improves education, health, and poverty outcomes.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Summer School arrives in Africa**

[事实] Planet Money introduces this as part of its Summer School World Tour, focused on lessons from other countries that might work elsewhere.
[事实] The episode’s main destination is Kenya, with a stopover in Nigeria.
[事实] Tavneet Suri, a professor at MIT Sloan and a Kenyan economist, joins as the episode’s guide.

[02:16] **Africa as an economic case-study hub**

[事实] Suri describes African cities as full of visible commerce, street activity, and entrepreneurship.
[事实] The episode notes that Nigeria’s population is growing rapidly and may exceed the United States by 2050.
[事实] Suri says Africa will have a very large share of the world’s youth population by 2050.
[推测] The episode presents Africa’s demographics as both a challenge for job creation and a potential source of future global economic growth.

[05:01] **Nigeria’s small-business financing problem**

[事实] The first case study asks how a small business with good ideas can get enough money to expand.
[事实] The episode introduces Nigeria’s government program as a “mashup of Shark Tank and the lottery.”
[事实] The central question is whether giving money directly to entrepreneurs can create lasting businesses and jobs.

[05:58] **Lariat Alhassan’s paint business**

[事实] In 2011, Lariat Alhassan ran Larklux Paint in Abuja, selling different kinds of house and industrial paint.
[事实] Her company was extremely small: she did production, marketing, delivery, and nearly everything else herself.
[事实] She lost potential clients because she had no office or showroom, even though customers liked her paint.
[推测] Her story illustrates a “stuck” growth problem where a firm needs credibility to win clients but needs clients to afford that credibility.

[07:28] **The YouWin opportunity**

[事实] Lariat heard a radio ad for the Youth Enterprise with Innovation in Nigeria competition, known as YouWin.
[事实] The program promised money for Nigerians trying to start or grow businesses, with no loan repayment described.
[事实] Lariat initially thought the offer was a scam.
[事实] The program was led by Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala, Nigeria’s finance minister at the time.

[08:14] **Why the Nigerian government tried grants**

[事实] Okonjo-Iweala was responding to high youth unemployment, with eight million young Nigerians needing jobs.
[事实] The episode says many young Nigerians wanted to create or expand businesses but lacked money.
[事实] Banks struggled to lend to very small firms because it was hard to distinguish promising one-person companies from weak ones.
[推测] The program treated lack of capital as a bottleneck that conventional lending institutions were not solving.

[09:23] **Designing a fair business-plan contest**

[事实] The government proposed a nationwide contest where applicants submitted business plans with ideas, budgets, and projections.
[事实] Nigerian president Goodluck Jonathan supported the idea and told Okonjo-Iweala to figure it out.
[事实] The organizers faced logistical concerns about evaluating tens of thousands of applications.
[事实] They also faced concerns that corruption or personal connections could distort who received money.

[10:42] **Random selection as both policy and experiment**

[事实] The program used outside judges, including judges outside Nigeria, and removed names from applications.
[事实] World Bank economist David McKenzie suggested choosing many winners randomly after screening out very weak proposals and identifying top ones.
[事实] Random selection helped the program evaluate winners against similar non-winners.
[推测] Randomization served two purposes: reducing discretion in selection and making the program easier to study rigorously.

[12:03] **Lariat applies and wins**

[事实] Lariat heard repeated YouWin ads, eventually applied online, and joined about 24,000 applicants.
[事实] She made the first cut and joined about 6,000 semi-finalists in business training.
[事实] She prepared a full business plan with a budget and sales projections.
[事实] She won 10 million naira, described as about $65,000 and more than ten times her yearly earnings.

[14:01] **Grant money changes the business**

[事实] Lariat used the money to hire sales and marketing staff and rent a proper showroom.
[事实] Having an office allowed her to invite clients to a professional location.
[事实] The episode asks whether the important question is not the immediate effect, but how the business and other winners performed over time.

[14:48] **YouWin’s measured results**

[事实] Because some winners were chosen randomly, the World Bank could compare recipients with non-recipients like an experiment.
[事实] The study looked at Lariat and about 1,200 other winners.
[事实] The program reportedly created 7,000 jobs that lasted for years.
[事实] The total cost was $60 million, or about $8,500 per job created.
[推测] The results challenged the idea that successful entrepreneurship is extremely rare among small firms.

[16:37] **The missing middle**

[事实] The YouWin contest was eventually shut down after Nigeria changed presidents.
[事实] The program was copied in Kenya and Senegal.
[事实] Lariat’s paint business was still running, though she still struggled to find financing to grow further.
[事实] Suri explains the “missing middle” as a shortage of mid-sized firms between many one-person businesses and large corporations.

[17:29] **Skills, capital, and institutions**

[事实] Suri says entrepreneurs need both skills and capital to grow businesses.
[事实] She explains that banks often use cash-flow lending, but many African businesses lack electronic records, spreadsheets, tax filings, or formal documentation.
[事实] The episode links lending constraints to institutions such as property rights, accountants, and trusted legal systems.
[推测] The discussion suggests that grants can work around financing gaps, but long-term growth also depends on stronger financial and legal infrastructure.

[21:36] **Busia becomes an economics laboratory**

[事实] The second case study begins in Busia, a small town in western Kenya near the Uganda border.
[事实] Carol Nkesa grew up there and later returned for a computer-related job entering survey data for economists.
[事实] She was puzzled by questions about goats, televisions, and bicycles.
[事实] Those questions were part of research designed to compare households and measure economic conditions.

[23:51] **Michael Kremer and randomized trials**

[事实] Michael Kremer had taught high school near Busia and later studied development economics.
[事实] He advised a nonprofit working on school projects and proposed using experiments to measure whether interventions helped.
[事实] For a textbook project involving 100 schools, Kremer designed a randomized controlled trial.
[事实] Randomized trials were already standard in medicine, but were not yet common in economics in the early 1990s.

[25:44] **The textbook surprise**

[事实] Schools were randomly assigned to groups, and textbooks were distributed to different groups over four years.
[事实] Researchers surveyed families to check whether groups were comparable.
[事实] Kremer expected test scores to rise when students received textbooks.
[事实] Average test scores did not improve, though high-scoring students benefited significantly.
[推测] The result showed that an intuitive education intervention can fail for many students if deeper barriers are ignored.

[26:55] **From textbooks to remedial education**

[事实] The textbook study led to the insight that governments and aid groups had been spending heavily on textbooks with limited average effect.
[事实] Education reformers began focusing more on students who were not doing well.
[事实] The episode says this encouraged more emphasis on remedial education.
[推测] The study’s value came partly from showing what not to fund, not only from identifying successful interventions.

[27:13] **Busia’s research ecosystem grows**

[事实] Kremer wanted to do more randomized trials that addressed real problems.
[事实] Other economists, including his students and research assistants, began developing projects in Busia.
[事实] Carol observed the growth of economist activity in town, including multiple houses rented for visiting experts.
[事实] By 2010, studies in Busia were helping reshape development economics.

[28:17] **Deworming and long-term outcomes**

[事实] Ted Miguel evaluated free medicine for parasitic worms among schoolchildren.
[事实] The study showed that treated children were more likely to attend school.
[事实] A follow-up study found links to better jobs and higher incomes later in life.
[事实] Deworming treatment became standard across Kenya.

[28:48] **Carol Nkesa’s role in research**

[事实] Some Kenyan staffers who helped run studies realized research could become a career.
[事实] Carol began contributing suggestions to researchers after years of working on randomized trials.
[事实] She later earned a master’s degree at Harvard.
[事实] She returned to Busia and founded a research organization to support randomized trials in Kenya.

[29:19] **HIV-risk education study**

[事实] Carol told an economist about girls having sex with older men in exchange for money, increasing HIV risk.
[事实] Pascaline Dupas developed a curriculum teaching girls that older men had higher HIV rates.
[事实] The curriculum was tested through a randomized trial across dozens of schools.
[事实] The study found fewer pregnancies in schools receiving the teaching, suggesting less unprotected sex.

[31:15] **Nobel recognition for randomized development economics**

[事实] Michael Kremer later won the Nobel Prize in economics alongside Abhijit Banerjee and Esther Duflo.
[事实] The prize recognized the use of randomized controlled trials to help alleviate global poverty.
[推测] The episode treats Busia as a key site in the rise of evidence-based development economics.

[32:07] **Ethics and benefits of experimentation**

[事实] Suri says some people may feel proud that knowledge was generated in Kenya, while others may feel uneasy about being part of experiments.
[事实] She says people ask whether research changes policy and benefits everyone.
[事实] Suri argues that the direct effect on Kenyan policy may not always be large, but the effect on global knowledge is substantial.
[推测] The episode acknowledges a tension between producing knowledge and ensuring local communities benefit from it.

[34:01] **Human capital and growth**

[事实] Suri defines human capital as the education and health people bring to the economy.
[事实] She says policies that reduce disease, increase education, and reduce poverty can contribute to a richer country.
[事实] She cites research on returns to education and health investments.
[事实] The deworming study is used as an example where health investment also improved education.

[35:19] **Positive externalities and policy uptake**

[事实] Suri defines a positive externality as a case where one person’s investment benefits others.
[事实] She gives education as an example: educated people can make people around them more productive.
[事实] She says economists can be frustrated when governments do not use research in decision-making.
[事实] She says academics try to build policy relationships, and Nobel recognition can help persuade governments to look at data.

[36:47] **What Kenya could teach the United States**

[事实] When asked what the United States could learn from Kenya, Suri points to civic and economic participation.
[事实] She says voter turnout in Kenya is extremely high and that people stand in line for hours to vote.
[事实] She connects that civic enthusiasm to the entrepreneurial spirit discussed earlier.
[推测] Her answer suggests that participation itself is an economic resource, but institutions must deliver on people’s desire to contribute.

[38:07] **Final exam concepts**

[事实] The episode closes by defining three key concepts: randomized controlled trials, human capital, and the missing middle.
[事实] Suri defines randomized controlled trials as randomly splitting a study population so one group receives an intervention and another does not.
[事实] She defines human capital as education and health embodied in people.
[事实] She defines the missing middle as the lack of businesses with roughly five to twenty employees between very small firms and large firms.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its concrete storytelling: it connects abstract development-economics concepts to Lariat’s paint business, Carol Nkesa’s career, and real policy experiments in Nigeria and Kenya. That makes concepts like capital constraints, randomized trials, human capital, and externalities easier to understand.

[推测] A major strength is that the episode does not present evidence-based policy as simple or automatic. The Nigerian grant program produced impressive job-creation results, but still faced continuity and financing problems; the Busia research improved global knowledge, but raised questions about whether local communities always benefit.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode moves quickly across major topics, so listeners looking for deeper methodological detail on the studies may need outside reading. It is best suited for listeners interested in development economics, public policy, entrepreneurship, education, health, and how economists test whether interventions actually work.
