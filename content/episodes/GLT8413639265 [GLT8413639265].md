# The Rights of Man, the Church, and the Fracturing of the French Revolution

## 概览

This episode centers on the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, approved by the National Assembly on 26 August 1789, and asks why a revolutionary body facing hunger, bankruptcy, panic, and disorder devoted itself to abstract principles of liberty, equality, sovereignty, and rights.

The discussion argues that the Declaration was both a decisive rupture with the Ancien Régime and a document shaped by older inheritances. It drew on the American Revolution and Enlightenment thought, but the hosts also stress its deeper tensions: universal rights versus national sovereignty, secular anti-clericalism versus Christian moral assumptions, and revolutionary idealism versus revolutionary violence.

The second half focuses on the Church. The abolition of tithes, nationalization of Church property, dissolution of monasteries and convents, and Civil Constitution of the Clergy turned reform into a religious and political fracture. The hosts present the clergy’s oath as a major turning point that split the revolution, the Church, and the French population.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **The Declaration and the Problem of Duties**

[事实] The episode opens with the preamble to the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, including its claim that ignorance, forgetfulness, or contempt for rights caused public misfortunes and corrupt governments.
[事实] The hosts note that the preamble mentions duties, despite an earlier discussion of Margaret Thatcher’s criticism that the French Revolution forgot duties.
[事实] The Declaration was approved by the National Assembly on 26 August 1789 after about a month of debate.
[推测] The opening frames the episode as a reassessment of whether the French Revolution should be understood mainly through violence, ideals, or the relationship between the two.

[01:38] **Violence, Idealism, and Revolutionary Faith**

[事实] Tom argues that the French Revolution cannot be understood by emphasizing violence alone, because ideals, principles, and abstract nouns were central to how revolutionaries understood their actions.
[事实] Dominic agrees that much of the violence was driven by belief in an ideology rather than violence for its own sake.
[事实] The Declaration is described as a manifesto with seismic implications and a lasting place in France’s constitutional and political memory.
[推测] The hosts suggest that revolutionary idealism could function with something like religious force, giving moral certainty to political action.

[03:48] **Universal Values and Global Influence**

[事实] The episode says the Declaration’s values were specifically French but also presented as universal.
[事实] It is described as influential on republican constitutions around the world and on the UN’s Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
[事实] Jonathan Israel is quoted as presenting the Declaration as a foundational moment for democratic modernity.
[推测] The episode treats the Declaration’s universalism as both its source of power and one of its deepest ambiguities.

[05:59] **France’s Crisis After the Bastille**

[事实] The National Assembly had emerged from the three estates and claimed to embody the will and sovereignty of the people.
[事实] The Bastille had fallen, some émigrés had fled, and Louis XVI was attempting to reinvent himself as a constitutional monarch.
[事实] The financial crisis and high bread prices that helped trigger the revolution had not been solved.
[推测] The Assembly’s focus on a declaration of rights is presented as an attempt to build new political foundations before solving practical crises.

[07:52] **The Great Fear in the Countryside**

[事实] The episode describes the summer of 1789 as the time of la grande peur, the Great Fear, when panic spread through the French countryside.
[事实] Arthur Young’s account of a poor peasant woman in Lorraine is used to show the crushing effect of taxes and feudal dues.
[事实] Peasants attacked chateaux, abbeys, and monasteries, especially targeting registers and charters associated with feudal obligations.
[事实] Rumors spread that aristocrats, foreigners, or brigands were plotting to starve, poison, or attack ordinary people.

[12:43] **The Night of 4 August**

[事实] On the evening of 4 August 1789, the National Assembly held a thinly attended session in which radical action was proposed to calm the country.
[事实] The Vicomte de Noailles argued that France was poised between social destruction and a government admired across Europe.
[事实] He proposed abolishing feudal obligations and requiring everyone in the kingdom to pay taxes.
[事实] The discussion describes this night as a sweeping rejection not only of feudalism but of privilege itself.

[15:48] **Lafayette, Jefferson, and the Declaration’s Articles**

[事实] Lafayette proposed a declaration modeled partly on the American example, with Thomas Jefferson assisting while serving as American ambassador in Paris.
[事实] The Declaration proclaimed 17 rights, beginning with the statement that men are born and remain free and equal in rights.
[事实] Article 2 listed liberty, property, safety, and resistance to oppression as natural and imprescriptible rights.
[事实] Article 3 located sovereignty in the nation rather than in the king, the Church, or any corporate body.

[17:24] **Anti-Clerical Implications**

[事实] Article 9 rejected arbitrary punishment by emphasizing the presumption of innocence.
[事实] Article 10 protected opinions, including religious opinions, as long as they did not disturb public order.
[事实] Article 11 affirmed free communication of ideas and opinions, undermining censorship by Church or crown.
[事实] Clerical opposition to religious liberty delayed final agreement on the Declaration for about a week.

[19:19] **Rupture, Continuity, and Nationalism**

[事实] Tom calls the Declaration a decisive moment because it openly broke with much of the old order in Europe’s intellectual capital.
[事实] The hosts also question whether the Declaration’s rupture was complete, since it claimed to proclaim natural, unalienable, and sacred rights that should have existed before 1789.
[事实] Dominic identifies a tension between universalism and nationalism, saying France invents modern nationalism while also claiming universal principles.
[推测] The Declaration is presented as both a universal document and a document tied to French national sovereignty.

[20:43] **Destroying the Ancien Régime**

[事实] The Declaration is compared to the demolition of the Bastille, because its articles helped give legal form to the destruction of absolutism.
[事实] Hereditary titles were abolished on 19 June 1790, along with symbols such as coats of arms, liveries, and privileged church pews.
[事实] The nobility kept much of its land because property was guaranteed, but the Church suffered heavily from the abolition of tithes.
[事实] The episode argues that freedom of worship and thought was especially revolutionary in France because of the Church’s antiquity and power.

[22:14] **The Church, the Monarchy, and the Weight of the Past**

[事实] Louis XVI’s 1775 coronation at Reims is described as deeply rooted in Catholic tradition and royal sacred history.
[事实] His coronation oath included a commitment to oppose heretics condemned by the Church.
[事实] The hosts argue that Louis’s later concessions against Church privilege would have had a major psychological effect on him.
[事实] The tricolor cockade is discussed as a symbol of a new order that still carried older religious associations through the colors of Parisian saints.

[26:49] **America, Enlightenment, and French Ambition**

[事实] After the break, the hosts cite the Elysée Palace website’s claim that the Declaration was inspired by the American Declaration of Independence and the Enlightenment.
[事实] They argue that the French Declaration was more ambitious than the American Declaration because it listed rights and claimed to speak for all mankind.
[事实] The American Declaration is treated as more focused on specific grievances against George III.
[推测] The French Declaration’s universal tone is portrayed as both inspiring and potentially arrogant.

[28:30] **Condorcet and the Radical Enlightenment**

[事实] Jonathan Israel’s idea of the radical Enlightenment is described as hostile to religion and often hostile to monarchy.
[事实] Condorcet is presented as a philosopher, mathematician, economist, and enthusiastic supporter of the revolution.
[事实] Condorcet viewed the revolution as a triumph of progress over superstition and saw Christianity as a force that had held back reason.
[推测] Condorcet represents the strand of revolutionary thought that wanted to cleanse public life through reason, evidence, and anti-clerical reform.

[31:15] **Voltaire and the Campaign Against Religious Intolerance**

[事实] Voltaire is described as the most famous French philosophe of the 18th century and a major influence on revolutionary anti-clericalism.
[事实] His campaign over Jean Calas, a Protestant wrongly convicted and broken on the wheel, is used as an example of his activism against religious persecution.
[事实] Louis XV received Calas’s family and issued a posthumous exoneration after Voltaire’s campaign.
[事实] In 1791, Voltaire’s remains were reinterred in the Pantheon, reconsecrated as a temple to liberty.

[34:32] **Popular Catholic Unease**

[事实] The hosts stress that most French people were rural, Catholic, and attached to parish life rather than readers of Voltaire.
[事实] Some devout Catholics came to see the revolution as a Protestant plot because Protestants played visible roles in revolutionary politics.
[事实] A Protestant pastor was elected president of the National Assembly in March 1790.
[事实] Anti-clerical pamphlets, images, processions, and theatrical parodies spread after censorship weakened.

[37:20] **Church Property and Financial Crisis**

[事实] France remained close to bankruptcy after the revolution began.
[事实] The Church owned about 10 percent of the nation’s wealth, making its property an obvious target for fiscal reform.
[事实] Talleyrand proposed taking Church land and using the proceeds both to support national finances and to pay clergy as state-supported functionaries.
[事实] The National Assembly voted the proposal through, while many clergy opposed the implication that they would become state employees.

[40:22] **Dissolving Monastic Life**

[事实] On 13 February, monasteries and convents were dissolved except those devoted to charitable work.
[事实] Taking new monastic vows was forbidden.
[事实] Monks and nuns had long been targets of anti-clerical and pornographic literature in 18th-century France.
[事实] By summer 1790, alienation between the Church and the revolution had deepened.

[42:15] **The Civil Constitution of the Clergy**

[事实] Pope Pius VI secretly condemned the Declaration of the Rights of Man and revolutionary Church policies.
[事实] The National Assembly responded by pushing forward rather than seeking accommodation with the papacy.
[事实] In July 1790, it drew up the Civil Constitution of the Clergy, under which priests and bishops would be elected.
[事实] Clergy were required to swear loyalty to the nation and its laws, creating a painful choice between the revolution and the authority of the Pope and Church.

[44:11] **The Oath That Fractured the Revolution**

[事实] Historians Simon Schama, William Doyle, and Peter McPhee are cited as treating the Civil Constitution and clerical oath as a major turning point.
[事实] About 54 percent of priests took the oath, while 46 percent did not.
[事实] Oath-taking was high in Paris, the region around Paris, and Provence, but lower in the countryside and especially in the west.
[事实] The episode describes this as the emergence of a counterrevolutionary bloc, with some people defining themselves as citizens and others as Christian communities of souls.

[47:25] **Stone Tablets, Moses, and Revolutionary Revelation**

[事实] Jean-Jacques François Le Barbier painted a visual representation of the Declaration after 1789.
[事实] The painting included revolutionary symbols such as broken chains, a Liberty cap, and an eye looking down.
[事实] The Declaration appeared as if carved on stone tablets, echoing Moses and divine revelation.
[推测] The image raises the question of whether the revolution synthesized biblical inheritance and Enlightenment values or appropriated religious forms for a new revolutionary order.

[49:00] **Christian Roots of Enlightenment Morality**

[事实] Tom argues that many values assumed by the Enlightenment to be universal were bred from Christian assumptions.
[事实] Voltaire’s horror at the torture of Jean Calas is presented as possibly reflecting a deeper Christian moral inheritance.
[事实] David Bien is cited to argue that later revolutionary polarization between clerical and anti-clerical camps should not be read too far back into pre-revolutionary France.
[事实] The Archbishop of Paris preached from Paul at funerals for those killed in the storming of the Bastille, using the text “you my brothers were called to be free.”

[51:10] **Rival Revolutionary Orders**

[事实] Tom explicitly agrees that the French Revolution’s ideological struggle can be understood as a struggle between different kinds of Christians.
[事实] He argues that the revolution attempted to appropriate much of its inheritance from the Christian past.
[事实] He traces the idea of universal human rights back to Latin Christendom and the 12th-century cultural ferment in Italy.
[推测] The hostility between the revolution and the Catholic Church is framed less as simple secularism versus religion and more as a clash between rival universal authorities.

[53:47] **The Monarchy as the Unresolved Problem**

[事实] Louis XVI responded to the Declaration in September by accepting its spirit while rejecting many specifics.
[事实] Dominic argues that the constitution had a central problem: the king.
[事实] The hosts doubt that Louis could psychologically adapt to a British-style constitutional monarchy, especially once the Church came under attack.
[事实] The episode ends by pointing ahead to the Women’s March on Versailles and the revolution’s need to decide what to do about the monarchy.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it refuses to treat the Declaration of the Rights of Man as a simple triumphal milestone. It shows how the language of rights, liberty, and equality emerged amid fear, hunger, violence, rumor, and institutional collapse.

[推测] Its strongest thread is the connection between political abstraction and practical consequence. The Declaration is not presented as mere rhetoric: its ideas lead into the abolition of privilege, the weakening of monarchy, the assault on Church power, and the split created by the Civil Constitution of the Clergy.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for listeners interested in the intellectual and religious dimensions of the French Revolution. It may be less satisfying for listeners seeking a simple chronological narrative, because the discussion often moves between 1789 events, Enlightenment thought, Reformation parallels, and long-term Christian inheritance.

[推测] The closing argument is deliberately provocative: the revolution’s struggle with the Church is not just a battle between secular modernity and religion, but a conflict between rival claims to universal truth, moral authority, and social rebirth.
