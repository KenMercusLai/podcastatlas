# Oliver Cromwell, the Protectorate, and the British Republic

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* examines Oliver Cromwell and the short-lived British Republic after the execution of Charles I. Dominic Sandbrook begins from open admiration for Cromwell, while Paul Lay argues that Cromwell is neither simple hero nor villain but an elusive figure whose gaps in the record have made him unusually vulnerable to myth.

The discussion traces the path from the English Civil War to the Commonwealth and Protectorate, emphasizing that the post-regicide regime never had a settled constitutional answer. Cromwell emerged from military prominence into political supremacy, but the system built around him depended heavily on the army and could not survive his death.

A major part of the episode deals with Cromwell’s violence in Ireland. Paul Lay argues that some atrocity stories have been mythologized, but he also describes Cromwell’s actions at Drogheda and Wexford, especially the killing of Catholic clergy, as barbaric and indefensible.

The episode closes by exploring Cromwell’s afterlife in British and Irish memory: Victorian liberal admiration, the controversy over his statue at Westminster, his recurring use in Brexit-era rhetoric, and the difficulty of separating the historical Cromwell from later political projections.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Milton’s Praise and Cromwell’s Divisive Reputation**

[事实] The episode opens with John Milton’s 1652 sonnet praising Oliver Cromwell, written after Charles I’s execution and before Cromwell became Lord Protector.
[事实] Dominic Sandbrook says he is “definitely a Cromwellian” and calls Cromwell his favourite British political leader.
[事实] Tom Holland notes that royalists and many Irish listeners would strongly reject such admiration.
[推测] The opening frames Cromwell as a figure whose reputation still depends heavily on political, national, and moral starting points.

[03:00] **Paul Lay’s Cromwell: Neither Hero Nor Villain**

[事实] Paul Lay rejects the simple choice of Cromwell as either hero or zero.
[事实] He describes Cromwell as fascinating because he is elusive: for the first two thirds of his life he barely appears in the records, then suddenly becomes a major cavalry commander and head of state.
[事实] Lay argues that Cromwell helped create a lasting shift in which monarchy never returned to its pre-civil-war form and Parliament remained more ascendant.
[推测] Lay’s interpretation presents Cromwell less as an isolated ruler than as a force in a longer constitutional transformation.

[05:15] **Why Both Left and Right Can Claim Cromwell**

[事实] Dominic suggests that Cromwell can be claimed by different sides of English politics.
[事实] Lay says some on the left admire Cromwell as a republican, while some on the right see him as an English nationalist.
[事实] Lay also describes Cromwell as a defender of a relatively broad religious settlement by mid-17th-century standards, while noting that Catholics would challenge that claim.
[事实] Cromwell is also credited with an important role in the development of the British army and fleet.

[06:18] **Civil War Background**

[事实] Dominic summarizes the crisis of Charles I’s rule: personal rule without Parliament, conflict with Scotland over a prayer book, rebellion in Ireland, and the king’s need to recall Parliament for money.
[事实] Civil war breaks out between king and Parliament, and Charles loses the first civil war.
[事实] Parliament initially seeks a deal with Charles, but after he makes a secret agreement with the Scots and the second civil war follows, he is judged untrustworthy.
[事实] Charles is tried and executed, leaving Parliament and the army to decide what should replace monarchy.

[09:14] **After Regicide: Commonwealth Without a Clear Plan**

[事实] Lay says the trial and execution of Charles I were carried out by a relatively small group making a bold decision without knowing what to do next.
[事实] England wanted peace and stability after civil war, and many people were willing to find accommodation with the new regime.
[事实] The monarchy and House of Lords were abolished, Parliament was in the ascendant, and England came closest at this point to being a republic or Commonwealth.
[事实] Cromwell had great military and political prestige, but he was not yet the settled supreme political figure he would later become.

[11:34] **Cromwell’s Personality and Religion**

[事实] Lay says Cromwell does not fit a simple image of an austere Puritan: he drank, smoked, enjoyed music, and played sports such as cricket and football as a young man.
[事实] Religion was central to Cromwell, especially the belief that he must discern and perform God’s will.
[事实] Lay links Cromwell’s outlook to predestination and says Cromwell was not a malicious enemy of pleasure.
[事实] Several stories about Cromwell, including anecdotes from childhood or family life, are treated cautiously because the record around him is full of mythology.

[15:39] **From the King’s Execution to Barebone’s Parliament**

[事实] Lay says Cromwell and others became convinced during the second civil war that Charles was a “man of blood” whose duplicity made settlement impossible.
[事实] After the king’s execution, the regime had to deal with Scotland and Ireland and then seek a domestic settlement.
[事实] Parliament was eventually pushed aside, and the nominated assembly, known as Barebone’s Parliament, emerged as an experiment shaped by religious radicals.
[事实] Lay describes Thomas Harrison and the Fifth Monarchists as holding extreme religious beliefs about England’s role in bringing about Christ’s return.
[事实] Barebone’s Parliament passed some legal reforms but failed partly because it did not provide enough money for the army.

[20:07] **The Instrument of Government and the Protectorate**

[事实] John Lambert emerges as a major figure and helps produce the Instrument of Government, described as the world’s first written constitution.
[事实] The Instrument of Government replaced the old structure of king, Lords, and Commons with Protector, Council of State, and Parliament.
[事实] Cromwell accepts the role of Lord Protector, beginning the Protectorate.
[事实] Lay rejects the idea that Cromwell was a modern-style military dictator, arguing that he repeatedly sought “healing and settling” and that a 17th-century state lacked the machinery for modern dictatorship.
[推测] The Protectorate is presented as authoritarian and army-backed, but not as a dictatorship in the 20th-century sense.

[22:55] **Why the Protectorate Could Not Settle the Succession**

[事实] Lay says the Protectorate was not a republic but a quasi-monarchy.
[事实] He argues that many people still associated the “ancient constitution” with monarchy, Commons, and House of Lords.
[事实] Cromwell was decisive as a military commander but uncertain as a political architect.
[事实] By the end of his life, options included hereditary kingship, elective kingship, hereditary protectorship, or elective protectorship.
[事实] On his deathbed in 1658, Cromwell leaves the protectorship to his son Richard, but Richard lacks the army’s support and the regime collapses toward Restoration.

[28:14] **The Levellers and the Missed Republican Opportunity**

[事实] After the break, Dominic asks whether left-wing admirers of the Levellers are wrong to see the period as a missed republican opportunity.
[事实] The Levellers are described as New Model Army soldiers and radicals who wanted a more democratic republican settlement.
[事实] At Burford, Cromwell suppresses a Leveller mutiny and three soldiers are shot against the church wall.
[事实] Lay notes that the Levellers’ language of the “freeborn Englishman” has appealed both to left-wing figures such as Tony Benn and to conservative Brexiteers such as Daniel Hannan.
[推测] The Levellers serve in the episode as a contrast between radical republican possibility and Cromwell’s need to preserve army discipline and state authority.

[31:53] **Ireland, Conquest, and English Election**

[事实] The Levellers objected to the crossing to Ireland and criticized conquerors such as Julius Caesar, Alexander, and William of Normandy as lawless thieves.
[事实] Lay says the Cromwellian conquest of Ireland is most comparable to the Norman Conquest rather than to some modern analogies.
[事实] He describes the campaign as part of an attempt to Anglicize Britain and Ireland.
[事实] Lay argues that Cromwell and others saw England as an elect nation, analogous to Israel in the Old Testament.
[推测] This religious and national ideology helps explain why the Irish campaign could be imagined by its supporters as both military necessity and providential mission.

[34:16] **Cromwell’s Irish Reputation and the Question of Atrocity**

[事实] The hosts raise listener questions about Cromwell’s brutality in Ireland and why he holds such a dark place in Irish memory.
[事实] Lay says Cromwell spent about nine months in Ireland, and that his actions were decisive and sometimes barbaric, especially toward Catholic priests.
[事实] He also says the facts have often been mythologized, as Cromwell himself has been.
[事实] Lay argues that Cromwell is not simply celebrated in England; he remains deeply controversial there too.
[事实] He links the ruthlessness in Ireland partly to revenge for the 1641 Irish Rebellion, which was real but greatly exaggerated in English propaganda.

[37:32] **Drogheda, Wexford, and New Ross**

[事实] Lay explains that James Butler, Earl of Ormond, built an Irish and royalist coalition intended to use Ireland as a base for restoring royalism in England.
[事实] Michael Jones’s victory at Rathmines allowed the New Model Army to enter Dublin with 12,000 experienced soldiers and substantial cash from Parliament.
[事实] Cromwell instructed that soldiers caught looting should be hanged, showing an intended discipline within the army.
[事实] At Drogheda, after Arthur Aston refused surrender terms, Cromwell’s forces took the town; Lay identifies the burning of St Peter’s Church and the execution of Catholic priests as atrocities.
[事实] At Wexford, Lay says Cromwell lost control of his troops and roughly 2,000 died, while at New Ross the garrison surrendered on generous terms.

[43:47] **How Much Blame Belongs to Cromwell?**

[事实] Lay says the Elizabethan and Jacobean campaigns in Ireland were also brutal and, in some respects, worse.
[事实] He argues that Cromwell was part of a longer continuum of English violence in Ireland, not the sole author of that history.
[事实] Lay still holds Cromwell responsible for specific actions such as the treatment of priests and events at Drogheda.
[推测] Cromwell’s name has become a symbol that carries more than his own nine-month campaign, condensing centuries of English-Irish conflict into one figure.

[45:28] **Victorian Memory and the Westminster Statue**

[事实] Dominic asks about the 1899 statue of Cromwell by Hamo Thornycroft outside the Houses of Parliament.
[事实] Lay says modern heroic memory of Cromwell begins in part with Thomas Carlyle’s “great man” interpretation.
[事实] Victorian liberals embraced a secularized Cromwell as part of a liberal dissenting tradition.
[事实] Lord Rosebery paid for the statue, and it was controversial amid debates over Irish home rule; Tories and Irish critics opposed it.
[推测] The Victorian Cromwell discussed here reflects 19th-century political needs as much as 17th-century realities.

[47:30] **Brexit, Myth, and Misremembered Cromwell**

[事实] Tom says Cromwell’s name resurfaced during the Brexit debate, especially in arguments about parliamentary supremacy and Anglo-Irish tensions.
[事实] Dominic notes that Brexiteers invoked the famous phrase “in the name of God, go” against Parliament under Theresa May.
[事实] Lay says Cromwell probably never said those words, and that they derive from a later forged version of his speeches.
[事实] Lay argues that much of what people believe about Cromwell is surrounded by myth, error, and ignorance.
[推测] The episode treats Cromwell as a recurring political reference point whenever British democracy or Anglo-Irish relations come under strain.

[49:50] **The Thatcher Analogy and Cromwell’s Final Disappointment**

[事实] Dominic highlights Lay’s comparison between Cromwell and Margaret Thatcher: both East Anglian, nonconformist, decisive, divisive, and associated with difficulty over Ireland.
[事实] Lay adds Cromwell’s role in readmitting Jews to England, while stressing that Cromwell’s motive was religious conversion rather than humanitarianism.
[事实] Lay says Cromwell wanted a godly nation but came to see the English people as spiritually unready.
[事实] Cromwell is succeeded briefly by his son Richard, but ultimately by Charles II.
[推测] The Thatcher comparison is offered mischievously, but it helps underline a shared problem of trying to reshape the nation according to a moral vision that the nation itself does not fully share.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its refusal to flatten Cromwell into either a heroic republican founder or a uniquely monstrous tyrant. It keeps the tension between admiration, atrocity, constitutional experiment, and later myth in view.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the explanations of the Protectorate’s instability and the careful discussion of Ireland, where Lay distinguishes between mythologized claims, documented atrocities, and Cromwell’s place in a longer history of English violence.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners who already enjoy political and constitutional history, but it also gives enough context for non-specialists through summaries of the Civil War, the Levellers, and the Restoration.

[推测] Its limitation is that the pace is broad and conversational, so some complex subjects, especially Ireland and radical republicanism, are necessarily compressed rather than exhaustively examined.
