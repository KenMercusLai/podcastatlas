# Weird Wars

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* is a countdown-style discussion of ten unusual, obscure, or strangely remembered wars, with Dominic Sandbrook and Tom Holland each choosing five examples.

The hosts repeatedly stress that these conflicts involved real suffering, even when the stories sound bizarre, comic, or mythic. Their choices range from ancient Greek sacred warfare and early Islamic North Africa to imperial expeditions, colonial trade wars, South American catastrophes, the Emu War, and the Russian Civil War.

A recurring theme is that “weirdness” often reflects distance: wars seem strange when they fall outside familiar national histories, or when later narratives turn them into symbols, jokes, myths, or political lessons.

## 分段落总结

[00:25] **Introducing “Weird Wars”**

[事实] The hosts define the episode as a selection of obscure, colorful, little-known, or strange wars.
[事实] They acknowledge at the start that every war involves suffering and that their perspective is shaped by discussing these conflicts from comfortable modern Britain.
[推测] The format lets the episode balance entertainment with historical caution, while also inviting listeners to dispute omissions and favorites.

[02:15] **The First Sacred War**

[事实] Tom’s first choice is the First Sacred War, dated to around 590 BC, involving Greek cities and the port city of Kirra near Delphi.
[事实] The anti-Kirra coalition claimed Apollo’s sanction and besieged the city, cutting off its water supply before poisoning it with hellebore.
[事实] Tom links the war to Delphi’s independence and to later stories about the cursed barrenness of Kirra’s surrounding lands.
[推测] The war is presented as “weird” because it combines sacred authority, extreme collective punishment, and an early example of poisoning as warfare.

[06:01] **The War of Canudos**

[事实] Dominic chooses the War of Canudos in Brazil, fought in 1896-1897 after the end of slavery and the fall of Emperor Dom Pedro II.
[事实] Antonio Conselheiro founded a religious community at Canudos in Bahia, attracting thousands of poor, formerly enslaved, mixed-race, and marginalized people.
[事实] The Brazilian military viewed the community as backward and monarchist, attacked it repeatedly, destroyed the settlement, and killed about 25,000 people.
[推测] Dominic frames the conflict as a tragic clash between self-described progress and a dispossessed religious community.

[09:45] **The Umayyad-Berber War and Al-Kahina**

[事实] Tom’s second war is the Umayyad-Berber conflict of the late 690s to 702 AD, during the Arab conquest of North Africa.
[事实] The Berbers were led by Dia, better known as Al-Kahina, a prophetess-like queen about whom later traditions make fantastical claims.
[事实] Al-Kahina defeated Arab forces in 696 but later lost after reportedly devastating land to deny supplies to the returning Arabs.
[推测] Her later use by both French colonial writers and Algerian resistance narratives shows how flexible her symbolic meaning became.

[13:41] **The British Expedition to Abyssinia**

[事实] Dominic describes the 1867-1868 British expedition to Abyssinia, triggered after Emperor Tewodros II’s letters to European powers, including Queen Victoria, went unanswered.
[事实] Tewodros responded by taking missionaries, traders, and other British-linked people hostage.
[事实] A British-Indian expedition under Sir Robert Napier marched about 400 miles inland to Magdala, defeated Tewodros, and freed the prisoners.
[事实] Tewodros killed himself with a dueling pistol that had been a gift from Queen Victoria.
[推测] The episode treats the war as both absurd and sad, since a major imperial campaign grew from diplomatic contempt and unanswered correspondence.

[17:27] **The German-Hungarian War of 955**

[事实] Tom discusses the German-Hungarian War of 955, centered on the Battle of the Lechfeld and the siege of Augsburg.
[事实] The Hungarians are described as highly mobile raiders feared by Christian Europe, while the Saxon forces under Otto are linked to heavy cavalry and Christian imperial imagery.
[事实] Bishop Ulrich helped defend Augsburg until Otto arrived and defeated the Hungarians.
[事实] Otto was hailed as imperator after the victory and was crowned emperor in Rome six years later.
[推测] Tom presents the story in deliberately epic terms, comparing its atmosphere to the return of a king and the restoration of imperial authority.

[21:29] **The War of Jenkins’s Ear**

[事实] Dominic’s next choice is the War of Jenkins’s Ear, fought from 1739 to 1748 after earlier tensions between Britain and Spain.
[事实] The war’s famous origin story concerns Welsh merchant Robert Jenkins, whose ear was cut off by the Spanish in 1731 after his ship was captured in the Caribbean.
[事实] Dominic says the story that Jenkins showed his pickled ear to the House of Commons is untrue.
[事实] The war was driven by trade tensions, colonial rivalry, and pressure on Robert Walpole, and later became absorbed into the War of the Austrian Succession.
[推测] Its remembered name makes a messy imperial trade conflict seem more comic and personal than it really was.

[25:08] **The Dutch-Portuguese War**

[事实] Tom chooses the Dutch-Portuguese War, fought from 1602 to 1663, and argues that it could be considered the first world war.
[事实] The conflict spanned South America, Africa, India, and the Pacific, as Dutch commercial companies challenged Portugal’s global empire.
[事实] The Dutch failed to take Brazil and Angola but seized the Cape of Good Hope and displaced Portuguese power in parts of the spice trade.
[事实] Tom says both Portugal and the Dutch were left weakened, allowing the English to move in later.
[推测] The war’s “weirdness” lies less in eccentric detail than in its huge global scale being relatively unfamiliar in popular memory.

[28:39] **The War of the Triple Alliance**

[事实] Dominic discusses the War of the Triple Alliance, also known as the Paraguayan War.
[事实] He describes Paraguay as a landlocked and historically isolationist South American state, earlier shaped by Dr. Francia’s Rousseau-influenced rule.
[事实] Paraguay fought Argentina and Brazil, and the war lasted six years in the 1860s.
[事实] Dominic says around half a million people died, and estimates of Paraguay’s population losses range from about 60 percent to 90 percent.
[推测] The war is presented as one of the episode’s starkest examples of an obscure conflict with catastrophic human consequences.

[31:55] **The Emu War**

[事实] Tom’s next choice is the Emu War of 1932 in Western Australia.
[事实] After World War I, returning Australian soldiers were given farmland, which was later affected by roughly 20,000 emus damaging crops.
[事实] Australian military personnel used machine guns against the emus, but the birds proved difficult targets and the campaign was mocked as ineffective.
[事实] The hosts say no humans died, though some emus did.
[推测] Tom treats the episode as a comic parody of colonial warfare and a metaphor for the limits of heavily armed forces against mobile opponents.

[35:17] **The Russian Civil War**

[事实] Dominic’s final choice is the Russian Civil War, which he describes as a massive, bloody, and often underappreciated conflict after the Russian Revolution.
[事实] The Bolsheviks faced White Russian armies, Allied intervention, Baltic states, Poland, Ukraine, Germans, and other short-lived or regional forces.
[事实] Dominic highlights the Czech Legion, former Austro-Hungarian prisoners of war who seized parts of the Trans-Siberian Railway while trying to travel east and eventually return home.
[事实] He says the Bolsheviks won partly because they controlled industrial centers and Moscow, and because they offered the peasantry a more positive message than the Whites.
[推测] The war is framed as “weird” because of its vast geography, chaotic alliances, and board-game-like movement of armies across Russia.

[39:20] **Closing Reflections on War**

[事实] The hosts return to the opening caveat that, apart from the Emu War, the conflicts discussed involved terrible suffering.
[事实] Dominic mentions Margaret MacMillan’s argument that war can drive social progress and inventions.
[事实] Tom answers with the line “war, what is it good for?” and the hosts suggest they may discuss the question in a future episode.
[推测] The ending deliberately undercuts the episode’s comic tone by returning to the moral difficulty of treating war as entertainment.

## 播客点评/总结

[事实] The episode’s strongest feature is its range: it moves from ancient Greece and medieval Europe to Brazil, Paraguay, Ethiopia, North Africa, global maritime empires, Australia, and revolutionary Russia.

[事实] The hosts make obscure history accessible through memorable hooks: a poisoned water supply, an unanswered royal letter, a severed ear, a prophetess-queen, armed trains, and a failed campaign against emus.

[推测] Its limitation is that each conflict receives only a short treatment, so the episode favors striking interpretation and narrative color over sustained analysis of causes, historiography, or local perspectives.

[推测] It is best suited to listeners who enjoy historical oddities, imperial and military history, and big comparative narratives, especially when serious subjects are handled with a mixture of caution and dry humor.
