# The History of Wine

## 概览

This episode tells the history of wine as a long story of taste, trade, religion, technology, empire, and marketing. The hosts frame wine not just as a drink, but as a cultural system: people have grown it, traded it, ritualized it, moralized against it, and used it to mark status for thousands of years.

The discussion moves through seven major turning points: the earliest evidence for wine in Georgia and Armenia, Canaanite and Phoenician export networks, Greek and Roman wine culture, wine’s survival and transformation after Rome, Islamic debates over prohibition and intoxication, English innovations in bottles and corkscrews, French classification and terroir, and finally the 1976 Judgment of Paris.

A recurring conclusion is that wine history is never only about flavor. It is also about who controls production, who defines quality, who can afford prestige, and how old traditions are sometimes preserved, sometimes reinvented, and sometimes successfully marketed to the rest of the world.

## 分段落总结

[01:39] **Pliny, Wine Snobbery, and the Episode’s Premise**

[事实] The episode opens its main discussion with Pliny the Elder’s writing on wine, including his attention to regions, grape varieties, storage, age, soil, and quality.
[事实] The hosts joke about wine snobbery and Tom’s questionable performance in wine tastings before framing the episode as a history of wine.
[事实] They present wine as a subject that has belonged to human culture for millennia, including attempts to enjoy it, classify it, misunderstand it, and ban it.

[05:28] **Wine as a Global Historical Thread**

[事实] The hosts connect modern wine regions such as Napa, Sonoma, Barossa, South Africa, New Zealand, and Australia to much older historical processes.
[事实] They explain that Australian wine began with vine cuttings brought by the First Fleet in 1788 from Rio and the Cape.
[事实] They also connect the German settlement of the Barossa Valley to viticultural traditions originally introduced to Germany by the Romans.

[07:11] **Deep Origins and Seven Key Moments**

[事实] The episode briefly reaches back into prehistory, saying vines may have benefited from post-asteroid ecological conditions after the extinction of the dinosaurs.
[事实] The hosts state that the history of wine spans at least 8,000 years.
[事实] They structure the episode around seven key moments that trace wine’s emergence, spread, and transformation into a roughly $500 billion global industry.

[08:54] **Noah, Georgia, and Domesticated Grapes**

[事实] The hosts use the Book of Genesis story of Noah planting a vineyard, drinking wine, and becoming drunk as an early cultural reference point.
[事实] They locate the key development of wine around the region south of the Black Sea, including the Caucasus, Taurus, and Zagros mountain zones.
[事实] They describe the domestication of the vinifera grape and quote Kathleen Burke on the emergence of hermaphroditic vines capable of producing fruit by themselves.
[事实] Archaeological evidence near Tbilisi includes pottery shards with chemical traces interpreted as wine, dated to around 6000 BC.

[11:15] **Armenia and the First Known Winery**

[事实] The hosts identify Areni in Armenia as the site of the oldest known winery, dated about 2,000 years after the Georgian evidence.
[事实] The Areni site included a large wine press in a cave, along with traces of grapevines, skins, and seeds.
[推测] The size of the Areni wine press suggests wine was already being produced on a scale that could support distribution beyond an individual household.

[12:41] **Canaanites, Phoenicians, and Amphorae**

[事实] The Canaanites are presented as the first mass exporters of wine, with the Beqaa Valley in Lebanon described as a major early center.
[事实] An Egyptian story from around 2000 BC describes Canaan as a place where wine was more plentiful than water.
[事实] The Canaanites are credited with inventing the amphora, a two-handled vessel with a pointed base and narrow sealable neck.
[事实] Phoenician merchants later expanded wine exports across the Mediterranean and beyond, including to Iberia.

[16:05] **Greek Wine Culture and the West**

[事实] The Greeks are described as major rivals of the Phoenicians in spreading wine westward.
[事实] The Odyssey is used to illustrate Greek wine culture through Odysseus, Polyphemus, dilution, and the contrast between civilized drinking and barbarian excess.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that Greeks exported not only alcohol but also a culture of sociability, rules, and symposia.
[事实] Greek settlement at Massilia, later Marseille, helped bring wine culture into Gaul.

[18:00] **Rome and the First International Wine Culture**

[事实] The Roman Empire is described as the first properly international wine culture because it created a large integrated marketplace.
[事实] Romans initially treated Greek wines as superior and expensive, while also learning from Carthaginian agronomic writing such as Mago’s work.
[事实] Wine became a marker of Roman civilization as Roman power expanded beyond the Mediterranean.
[事实] Roman merchants used wine to create demand among Gauls, and the hosts argue that this trade helped destabilize Gaul before Caesar’s conquest.
[事实] By Pliny’s time, Italy had become the heartland of wine production, with Falernian cited as a famous prestigious Roman wine.

[23:32] **After Rome: Survival Through Rivers and Ritual**

[事实] The collapse of Roman power in the West damaged vineyards and disrupted the single market that had supported long-distance wine trade.
[事实] Beer became more prominent among northern barbarian elites, partly because it was easier to produce in colder climates.
[事实] Wine culture survived through remaining trade routes, especially the Rhine, and through Christianity’s need for wine in the Eucharist.
[事实] Abbeys, particularly along the Rhine, became important vineyard owners and helped maintain wine production.
[事实] Over time, France recovered more quickly than Italy and replaced it as a central wine region.

[27:03] **Islamic Prohibition and Legal Sanctions**

[事实] The episode presents Islam as a major turning point because wine had a very different place in Islamic culture than in Christian culture.
[事实] The hosts state that the mainstream Islamic legal position is that wine should not be drunk.
[事实] They cite Quranic condemnation of wine and hadith material that curses those involved in drinking, selling, making, or transporting it.
[事实] They describe Sunni legal schools prescribing punishments for drinking wine, commonly 80 lashes for a free person and 40 for a slave.

[29:29] **Ambivalence, Evasion, and Islamic Wine Culture**

[事实] The hosts also stress that Islamic attitudes to wine were not culturally simple or uniform.
[事实] They discuss Quranic passages that appear more ambivalent, including heavenly rivers of wine and earlier verses later treated as abrogated.
[事实] They describe Hanafi arguments that distinguished grape wine from date wine and focused on drunkenness rather than the act of drinking itself.
[事实] In medieval Baghdad, taverns served wine and were often run by Christians, while poets such as Abu Nuwas wrote transgressive wine poetry.
[事实] The contrast between Ibn Taymiyyah’s hardline opposition and Rumi’s mystical use of wine as a metaphor shows wine’s continuing cultural force even where drinking was forbidden.

[37:20] **England and the Modern Wine Bottle**

[事实] After the break, the hosts turn to England and argue that English glassmakers developed the first modern wine bottle.
[事实] Earlier glass wine bottles existed in the ancient world, but they were too fragile for transport and were mainly decorative or status objects.
[事实] In the early 17th century, English glassmakers used coal-fired furnaces, wind tunnels, and changes in glass recipe to make stronger bottles.
[事实] Kenelm Digby is named as the figure credited in a 1662 parliamentary inquiry, though the hosts suggest others were also involved.
[事实] Dark glass became associated with quality because early English bottle glass was darkened by coal fumes.

[42:22] **Sparkling Wine, Cork, Port, and Corkscrews**

[事实] Strong English bottles made it safer to contain sparkling wine, whose pressure had previously made bottles prone to exploding.
[事实] The hosts explain that bottle shape evolved from pear-shaped to onion-shaped and then toward the modern form by the 1740s.
[事实] Cork from Portugal became increasingly important for stoppering bottles.
[事实] The 1703 Methuen Treaty encouraged English access to Portuguese wine and helped develop English enthusiasm for port.
[事实] The English are also credited with inventing the corkscrew, with Samuel Henshall receiving a patent in 1795.

[47:02] **French Wine Language Takes Over**

[事实] The hosts then move to France as the source of the modern language of wine.
[事实] Bordeaux had supplied claret to English tables during the Middle Ages, though English control of Bordeaux ended after the Hundred Years War.
[事实] Dutch commercial expansion, English-Portuguese trade, and repeated Anglo-French wars weakened France’s economic centrality in the early modern wine trade.
[事实] Even as France’s economic role faced competition, its cultural authority over wine terminology and prestige expanded.

[50:04] **Haut-Brion and Estate Branding**

[事实] Samuel Pepys’s 1663 diary entry is used as evidence for the early London reputation of Haut-Brion.
[事实] Haut-Brion is presented as the first Bordeaux wine sold in London under the name of the producing estate rather than just as claret, Bordeaux, or a merchant’s product.
[事实] Arnaud de Pontac is described as a marketing innovator who used estate branding and opened Pontac’s Head in London.
[事实] Haut-Brion could sell for seven shillings when ordinary wine cost about two shillings.
[事实] Pontac’s use of terms such as First Growth and Second Growth helped establish a long-lasting model for ranking wine.

[52:50] **Grand Cru, 1855 Classification, and English Taste**

[事实] By the 1720s, Lafite, Latour, and Margaux had joined Haut-Brion as elite Bordeaux names.
[事实] Napoleon III’s 1855 Paris exposition led brokers to classify France’s best wines, especially Bordeaux reds, into ranked growths.
[事实] Haut-Brion, Lafite, Latour, and Margaux occupied the top class, with Château Mouton Rothschild added later in 1973.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that the 1855 rankings were based heavily on what English consumers were prepared to pay.
[推测] This makes French wine prestige partly a French achievement and partly a product of foreign elite demand.

[56:28] **Terroir, Tradition, and Marketing**

[事实] John Locke’s 1677 Bordeaux wine tour is used to show early attention to the relationship between soil and wine quality.
[事实] The hosts describe terroir as an untranslatable French concept linking soil, place, tradition, and wine character.
[事实] They argue that terroir gained cultural and political significance in industrializing France as a symbol of rooted national identity.
[事实] Burgundy is presented as a region that marketed small vineyards through claims of ancient local tradition, some of which the hosts describe as 1920s inventions.
[事实] Terroir also became a way for French producers to differentiate themselves from New World competition.

[61:04] **The Judgment of Paris**

[事实] The final key moment is the 1976 Judgment of Paris, a blind tasting organized by English wine merchant Stephen Spurrier.
[事实] Spurrier wanted to promote New World wines to French drinkers who generally regarded Californian wines as inferior.
[事实] The judges included senior French wine authorities, not merely journalists or casual critics.
[事实] The tasting compared French and Californian whites and reds.

[62:17] **California’s Reputation Before 1976**

[事实] The hosts explain French skepticism toward Californian wine through examples such as Leland Stanford’s failed large-scale vineyard project.
[事实] Stanford’s fourth vintage in 1890 produced about two million gallons of wine that had to be distilled into brandy because the quality was poor.
[事实] Prohibition from 1920 to 1933 severely damaged the Californian wine industry.
[事实] During Prohibition, Americans used workarounds such as wine bricks, fake religious permits, and registration as rabbis or clergy.
[事实] The hosts say French critics also associated American wine with excessive sweetness, novelty flavoring, and commercialism.

[66:12] **California Defeats Gaul**

[事实] In the white wine tasting, a Californian Chardonnay came first, ahead of Burgundy and other Californian Chardonnays.
[事实] In the red wine tasting, Château Mouton Rothschild came second, while the top place went to a Californian wine from Stag’s Leap.
[事实] The Stag’s Leap vineyard had only been planted in 1970.
[事实] Time magazine reported the event as California defeating Gaul.
[事实] The hosts describe the result as having a major impact on the prestige of Californian and New World wines.

[68:49] **France After the Shock**

[事实] The hosts argue that the Judgment of Paris did not destroy French prestige.
[事实] Californian wineries continued to use French prestige markers such as château-style naming and French-sounding brands.
[事实] Americans, Britons, and more recently Chinese investors are described as having invested in Bordeaux.
[事实] The concept of terroir has been transplanted internationally, including to New Zealand, where it is linked in the episode to a Māori idea of belonging to place.
[推测] The modern wine world is therefore shaped by both competition with France and continued dependence on French cultural vocabulary.

[70:40] **Closing Reflections**

[事实] The hosts end by debating whether wine history is more about competition or partnership.
[事实] They conclude with the idea that for people who enjoy wine without chauvinism, the historical rivalry matters less than the pleasure of wine itself.
[事实] They close with poetic references, including Omar Khayyam, and point listeners toward a bonus episode on wine in the New World.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is its ability to treat wine as a serious historical subject without losing the comic rhythm of conversation. It connects archaeology, scripture, empire, Islamic law, medieval Christianity, early modern technology, French branding, and modern global competition into one coherent story.

[推测] A major highlight is the repeated insistence that wine quality is never purely objective. The episode shows how price, classification, language, ritual, national pride, and elite taste all help determine what counts as “great” wine.

[推测] The limitation is that the episode moves quickly across a vast subject, so some regions and periods are necessarily compressed. Listeners looking for a technical history of winemaking methods may find the cultural and political emphasis stronger than the oenological detail.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in cultural history, food and drink history, ancient trade, European prestige systems, and the way modern markets inherit old ideas of status and place.
