# Young Napoleon: Corsica, Revolution, and the Making of an Outsider

## Overview

This episode examines Napoleon Bonaparte’s origins, especially the importance of his Corsican identity before he became the central French figure of the nineteenth century. The hosts argue that his early anti-French feeling, outsider status, and attachment to Corsica are crucial to understanding the young Napoleon.

The discussion places Napoleon inside the political turbulence of eighteenth-century Corsica: Genoese rule, French takeover, Pasquale Paoli’s nationalist movement, and the Bonaparte family’s uneasy accommodation with French power. Napoleon emerges as ambitious, prickly, studious, emotionally intense, and deeply shaped by both Corsican nationalism and Enlightenment revolutionary ideas.

By the end, the episode presents Napoleon as a young man caught between causes: Corsican patriotism, service in the French army, and the accelerating French Revolution. His horror at revolutionary disorder does not make him royalist; instead, the hosts describe him as a radical republican with an authoritarian instinct for order.

## Section-by-section summary

[00:25] **A teenage anti-French Napoleon**

[事实] The episode opens with a 1786 passage by Napoleone Buonaparte, later known as Napoleon Bonaparte, attacking “Frenchmen” for robbing and corrupting his country.

[事实] The quoted passage frames France as an oppressor and Corsica as Napoleon’s true “mother country.”

[推测] The opening is used to challenge the familiar image of Napoleon as simply French and to foreground his Corsican identity.

[01:10] **Napoleon as a giant of history**

[事实] The hosts describe Napoleon as a titanic figure in French and Western history, culturally central to the nineteenth century.

[事实] They mention that the podcast has previously covered his Egypt expedition and Trafalgar but has not focused much on Napoleon himself.

[事实] This episode begins a series on young Napoleon and his origins.

[04:01] **Corsica as setting and identity**

[事实] The hosts stress that Napoleon’s Corsican background is “colossally important” and note that Corsican hostility to France persisted long after his lifetime.

[事实] Eighteenth-century Corsica is described as mountainous, poor, proud, rugged, and only loosely controlled from the coast by Genoa.

[事实] Most Corsicans are said to have spoken a Ligurian dialect rather than French.

[05:49] **Boswell, Paoli, and Corsican liberty**

[事实] James Boswell presented Corsicans to Europe as a brave and free people and became associated with Corsican dress and politics.

[事实] Pasquale Paoli is described as a nationalist leader who wanted a modern Corsican state with a constitution, university, and public institutions.

[事实] Paoli admired the British constitution, unlike the later French revolutionary and Napoleonic hostility to the British model.

[推测] Paoli’s mix of nationalism, Enlightenment reform, and “father of the nation” leadership is presented as an important model for Napoleon.

[08:17] **The Bonaparte family and French takeover**

[事实] Napoleon’s father, Carlo Maria Buonaparte, admired Paoli and married Letizia Ramolino, whom Napoleon later credited as a major formative influence.

[事实] Genoa effectively handed Corsica to France in 1768, after which Paoli resisted and was forced into exile in England.

[事实] Napoleon was born on 15 August 1769 amid the turmoil of French conquest.

[事实] Carlo later accommodated himself to French rule and obtained a noble title that helped his sons access elite education.

[10:49] **Childhood in occupied Corsica**

[事实] Napoleon grew up in Ajaccio as part of a large, aspirational but not especially wealthy family.

[事实] The hosts note that accounts of his childhood contain many anecdotes with mythic qualities, including stories whose reliability is uncertain.

[事实] His childhood environment is described as violent and marked by French repression of Corsican rebels.

[事实] Accounts of Napoleon as a boy emphasize aggression, awkwardness, and a strong appetite for reading.

[13:13] **Education in France and permanent outsider status**

[事实] In 1779, when Napoleon was nine, Carlo arranged for him to go to France for education and eventual military training.

[事实] Napoleon first had to learn French, which he barely spoke, and he never became especially fluent or polished in it.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that his Corsican accent, poor grammar, and outsider status are often underplayed in accounts of Napoleon.

[推测] Being educated in France intensified rather than erased his sense of being Corsican.

[15:05] **Military schooling, reading, and mythmaking**

[事实] Napoleon’s military schools were austere and partly run on monastic lines, with a curriculum including ancient history and mathematics.

[事实] His strength in mathematics helped steer him toward artillery.

[事实] He read classical authors including Caesar, Cicero, Suetonius, Tacitus, Erasmus, and especially Plutarch.

[事实] The hosts also discuss the famous snowball-fight story as likely untrue but important to later Napoleonic mythmaking.

[18:10] **A difficult, ambitious adolescent**

[事实] Napoleon is described as lonely, bullied, short, dark-skinned compared with classmates, and marked by his Corsican accent.

[事实] Rather than abandoning his Corsican background, he embraced it.

[事实] In 1785, he placed 42nd out of 58 candidates in the artillery exam, a result the hosts frame as respectable given his shorter preparation and foreignness.

[事实] He became a second lieutenant in a prestigious regiment stationed at Valence.

[20:31] **Paoli, Corsican writings, and gothic moods**

[事实] Napoleon worshipped Pasquale Paoli and turned him into an imagined father figure after his own father’s death.

[事实] His surviving early writings include a history of Corsica, essays about Corsica, and gothic fiction.

[事实] The hosts describe him as moody, intellectually arrogant, humorless, and not yet visibly a man of destiny.

[推测] His literary and political writings reveal adolescent intensity more clearly than mature strategic genius.

[23:40] **French crisis and Napoleon’s double loyalty**

[事实] In the late 1780s, France’s financial crisis and the summoning of the Estates General opened a revolutionary moment.

[事实] Napoleon welcomed constitutional liberalization because he thought it might give Corsica more autonomy.

[事实] In 1789 he wrote to Paoli asking to write a history of Corsica and still described the French as oppressors.

[事实] At the same time, he supported the revolutionary movement when many other artillery officers fled.

[推测] The key tension was whether he could be both a French revolutionary and a Corsican nationalist.

[27:33] **Awkwardness, sexuality, and Rousseau-like sentiment**

[事实] After the break, the hosts discuss Napoleon’s awkwardness with women and his unattractive appearance in social settings.

[事实] In 1787, he met a prostitute in Paris after seeing a play and later wrote about the encounter in a strange, reflective style.

[事实] The account emphasizes his nerves, inhibition, and high-minded sentimental language rather than sexual confidence.

[推测] The hosts treat the episode as another example of Napoleon masking insecurity with abstract Enlightenment language.

[31:03] **Return to chaotic Corsica**

[事实] Napoleon returned to Ajaccio at the end of September 1789 and found Corsica in disorder.

[事实] Corsican politics involved demands for self-rule, calls for greater integration with France, and clan or factional conflict beneath the surface.

[事实] Joseph Bonaparte helped set up a patriotic committee, and Napoleon handed out tricolor cockades.

[推测] Napoleon’s revolutionary symbols and Corsican motives show that his politics were still emotionally driven and unsettled.

[33:20] **Contradictory revolutionary and anti-French writings**

[事实] Napoleon continued writing a Corsican history claiming Corsicans were heirs of the Romans.

[事实] He also wrote a violent short story in which a Corsican man takes revenge on Frenchmen after his mother is raped and murdered by French soldiers.

[事实] Around 1790, Napoleon composed a letter read in the French National Assembly asking for Corsica to be fully integrated into the French nation.

[推测] These simultaneous positions show the confusion of his politics during the revolutionary crisis.

[35:06] **Paoli returns and Corsican politics radicalize**

[事实] Pasquale Paoli returned triumphantly to Corsica in the summer of 1790, and Napoleon made sure he was present to welcome him.

[事实] Napoleon, Joseph, and their circle joined patriotic clubs in Ajaccio, gave speeches, and took part in street fights.

[事实] Napoleon wrote against Matteo Buttafuoco, the representative of the nobility, as class warfare from Paris spread into Corsica.

[推测] Revolutionary ideology added a new motive to already intense Corsican factional conflict.

[36:01] **1791-1792: danger on both fronts**

[事实] Napoleon spent 1791 and 1792 moving between France and Corsica while still serving in the French army.

[事实] Staying away too long risked making him a deserter, and the outbreak of war raised the possibility that he could be treated as a traitor.

[事实] The king’s failed flight to Varennes in June 1791 intensified revolutionary paranoia and violence.

[事实] In April 1792, Napoleon was involved in street fighting in Ajaccio, and Paoli began distancing himself from the Bonapartes.

[38:20] **A denunciation lost in Parisian chaos**

[事实] A French representative in Corsica wrote to the War Ministry in Paris accusing the Bonaparte family of treachery and other vices.

[事实] Napoleon returned to Paris in summer 1792 worried about the consequences.

[事实] He found the War Ministry in chaos because France was at war, and the denunciation appears not to have harmed him.

[推测] Administrative chaos unexpectedly protected Napoleon at a moment when his career could have collapsed.

[39:09] **The Tuileries and Napoleon’s hatred of disorder**

[事实] On 20 June 1792, Napoleon saw a crowd force Louis XVI to wear the red cap of liberty.

[事实] Napoleon despised the king’s weakness and reportedly commented in Italian that he was a fool.

[事实] Nearly two months later, Napoleon witnessed the aftermath of the attack on the Tuileries, including bodies of Swiss Guards and nobles in the palace gardens.

[事实] The hosts argue that Napoleon was horrified less by violence itself than by disorder and mob rule.

[推测] This moment helps define him as a radical Jacobin with authoritarian instincts rather than a royalist sympathizer.

[41:53] **From Corsican nationalism toward revolutionary opportunity**

[事实] The hosts say Napoleon’s Corsican identity never vanished but became less dominant as he found a new cause in the French Revolution.

[事实] They note that the departure of many aristocratic officers opened space for rapid promotion in the French army.

[事实] The episode ends by portraying young Napoleon as driven, ambitious, prickly, spiky, humorless, and still writing about Corsica.

[推测] The Revolution offered Napoleon a path that Corsican nationalism alone could no longer provide.

## Podcast Review/Summary

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it reframes Napoleon before fame: not as the inevitable emperor, but as a marginal Corsican outsider shaped by conquest, humiliation, reading, ambition, and revolutionary confusion.

[事实] A major strength is the way the hosts connect personal biography with larger political forces: Genoese Corsica, French annexation, Paoli’s nationalism, Enlightenment ideas, the French Revolution, and the breakdown of royal authority.

[推测] The limitation is that some childhood material depends on anecdotes the hosts themselves describe as uncertain or mythologized, so listeners should treat the most vivid early stories cautiously.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in Napoleon’s formation, Corsican identity, and the psychological and political roots of his later authoritarian revolutionary career.
