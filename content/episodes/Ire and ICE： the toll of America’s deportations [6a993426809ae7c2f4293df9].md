# Ire and ICE: the toll of America’s deportations

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* examines three stories: the political costs of Donald Trump’s intensified deportation campaign, the threat AI poses to Reddit’s business model, and the Bayeux Tapestry’s first exhibition outside France.

The lead segment argues that Trump’s second-term deportation drive has become quieter after public backlash in Minneapolis, but also more systematic and technologically assisted. The discussion highlights uncertainty around official deportation figures, crowded immigration courts, and signs that public support may be weakening, especially among Hispanic voters.

The second segment explores Reddit’s dependence on human-generated discussion as both an advertising asset and AI-training resource. The final cultural segment frames the Bayeux Tapestry as a rare medieval object whose artistic immediacy, missing ending, and survival history continue to invite interpretation.

## 分段落总结

[00:42] **Episode Introduction**

[事实] The hosts introduce *The Intelligence* from *The Economist* and preview stories on Reddit in the age of AI and the Bayeux Tapestry in Britain.
[事实] The first major story turns to Donald Trump’s deportation policy and whether it is creating political costs.

[01:18] **Trump’s Deportation Campaign**

[事实] The segment says Trump promised the largest deportation operation in American history and has followed through in both visible and quieter ways.
[事实] Aaron Braun contrasts Trump’s approach with Barack Obama’s 2013 removals, saying Obama focused on recent border crossers and people in jails or prisons.
[事实] Because the border is described as quiet, Trump’s administration is focusing more on arrests inside the country, including on streets and in homes.
[推测] This shift makes deportation enforcement more visible and personal to many Americans than border-focused enforcement.

[02:38] **Unclear Numbers and Rising Arrests**

[事实] Aaron says the administration has not published official removal statistics since Trump took office again in January 2025.
[事实] A Department of Homeland Security spokesman claimed one million people had been deported and about 2.2 million more had left voluntarily.
[事实] ICE arrested 50,000 people in July, described as a record for the agency.
[事实] Immigration judges are issuing removal orders at a rapid pace.
[推测] Even without official statistics, the available claims and arrest figures suggest the campaign is expanding.

[03:30] **Long-Term Residents and Legal Status**

[事实] Aaron says the administration is not necessarily focusing specifically on long-term residents, but interior arrests mean people who have lived in the United States for decades are being caught up.
[事实] He describes a legal strategy aimed at stripping people in America legally of their status.
[事实] Haitians in Ohio with temporary protected status are cited as people now vulnerable to deportation and fitted with ankle monitors.
[事实] People admitted on humanitarian parole during the Biden administration are also described as newly vulnerable.
[推测] The policy broadens deportation risk beyond recent border crossers and undocumented migrants with criminal records.

[04:39] **After Minneapolis**

[事实] The hosts refer to extraordinary scenes in Minneapolis, thousands of federal agents on the streets, protests, and two deaths.
[事实] Aaron says Americans were horrified by what they saw and that the administration was put on the back foot.
[事实] Kristi Noem was sacked as Homeland Security Secretary, Tom Homan was sent in, and Markwayne Mullin came in to lead DHS.
[事实] Mullin said during confirmation that he did not want to be in the news and wanted to do a quiet, effective job.
[推测] The administration appears to have adjusted presentation and tactics after backlash, while maintaining the policy’s intensity.

[05:34] **Quieter but More Effective Enforcement**

[事实] Congress appropriated $70 billion more for immigration enforcement.
[事实] Aaron says the deportation campaign is now less visible but more effective and still aggressive.
[事实] Court testimony described an ICE agent using a Palantir-created app that shows potential immigrant targets in an area, compared to Google Maps.
[事实] The app is described as helping agents make target lists.
[推测] Technology and funding are allowing enforcement to continue without relying as heavily on theatrical public raids.

[06:28] **Immigration Courts Under Pressure**

[事实] After arrest, immigrants go through immigration courts run by the Department of Justice.
[事实] Aaron says these courts are not independent.
[事实] In Los Angeles, he observed overcrowded proceedings and “mega master hearings” with 50 to 100 cases in a three-hour setting.
[事实] He describes immigrants and families clogging hallways and a judge trying to get through 96 cases in one morning.
[推测] The pace and volume of hearings may weaken people’s practical ability to argue their cases.

[07:27] **Notice, Lawyers, and Removal Orders**

[事实] Hearings are being changed around, and immigrants may receive less notice about when they need to appear.
[事实] Some people arrive unprepared, without a lawyer, or do not appear at all.
[事实] If migrants do not show up, they can be ordered removed in absentia.
[事实] Aaron says this helps the administration continue increasing removal orders.
[推测] Procedural pressure is functioning as part of the broader deportation strategy.

[07:52] **Public Opinion and Electoral Risk**

[事实] Aaron says Trump’s immigration approval rating is underwater.
[事实] He says Americans broadly are unhappy with the level of force ICE has used.
[事实] Roughly half of Americans say they support abolishing ICE.
[事实] Hispanic voters helped Trump win in 2024, but Aaron says they dislike what is happening with ICE more than other ethnic groups.
[推测] The policy could affect swing House seats in South Texas and California during the midterms.

[08:57] **Promise Versus Delivery**

[事实] Aaron says Americans were promised a deportation campaign targeting criminals and gang members.
[事实] He says that is not what is being delivered.
[事实] The hosts note that the topic is also discussed on *Checks and Balance* and *Insider*.
[推测] The political danger lies in a gap between campaign messaging and the broader reality of enforcement.

[10:15] **Reddit’s AI Problem**

[事实] The episode describes Reddit as a self-governing internet forum with a subreddit for almost any interest.
[事实] Reddit calls itself the heart of the internet, but shareholders are worried about human-generated content in the AI era.
[事实] Shira Ovide says Reddit’s share price has dropped by about a third this year.
[事实] Investors worry that Google’s AI-generated search box may reduce traffic to Reddit links.
[推测] AI search threatens Reddit by using the value of Reddit-like information while reducing visits to Reddit itself.

[11:24] **Reddit’s Hybrid Business Model**

[事实] Reddit executives say the platform is neither quite social media nor entirely something else.
[事实] Its business resembles Instagram through advertising based on relevant user targeting.
[事实] It also resembles Wikipedia because it depends heavily on volunteer moderators policing subreddits.
[事实] In 2023, many volunteer moderators went on strike after Reddit announced charges for API access.
[推测] Reddit’s dependence on unpaid community labor creates tension when the company tries to monetize access more aggressively.

[12:17] **Data Deals With AI Companies**

[事实] Reddit kept its API charging policy and later announced deals with Google and OpenAI.
[事实] Google and OpenAI reportedly agreed to pay $60 million and $70 million a year to use Reddit data, in part to train AI chatbots.
[事实] A company-commissioned study found Reddit was the most cited source among many leading American chatbots.
[事实] Reddit says about 40% of conversations on the site relate to commercial activity in some form.
[推测] Product reviews and shopping discussions make Reddit especially valuable for AI systems that want to recommend products.

[14:00] **Google Traffic and Investor Anxiety**

[事实] Google historically sent substantial search traffic to Reddit, including through users appending “Reddit” to search queries.
[事实] Google’s AI overviews may steer users away from other sites and toward Google’s own summaries.
[事实] Reddit’s stock had its largest single-day plunge in July after the company said choppy search referrals may have hurt.
[事实] Independent data suggests average time per user on Reddit’s app has fallen in recent months.
[推测] Reddit’s relationship with Google has become less balanced because Google can both license Reddit data and reduce referrals to Reddit.

[15:06] **Reddit’s Strategic Options**

[事实] Shira says Reddit can bargain for better deals with Google and other companies now that it knows the value of its data.
[事实] Reddit has sued several AI companies it says used its data illegally to train chatbots.
[事实] Winning those lawsuits could give Reddit leverage in future negotiations.
[事实] Reddit executives prefer attracting users directly to the site and app rather than becoming mainly a data mine.
[推测] Reddit’s long-term resilience depends on turning casual search visitors into logged-in app users.

[17:20] **The Bayeux Tapestry Arrives in Britain**

[事实] Alexandra Suich Bass describes going to the British Museum at night to witness the arrival of the Bayeux Tapestry.
[事实] The tapestry arrived by truck with a police escort from an undisclosed location in France.
[事实] Curators began carefully unfurling the object inside the museum.
[事实] The exhibition at the British Museum is described as the tapestry’s first outside France.
[推测] The segment presents the arrival as both a logistical event and a symbolic homecoming.

[18:23] **What the Tapestry Shows**

[事实] The Bayeux Tapestry is described as stretching the length of a Boeing 747 and as the only surviving medieval artwork of its size.
[事实] Though likely stitched in England, it is named after the French town where it lived for close to a millennium.
[事实] It depicts William the Conqueror’s invasion of England in 1066.
[事实] The story includes Edward sending Harold to Normandy, Harold apparently swearing that William should have the crown, Harold later taking the throne, and Halley’s Comet appearing as an omen.
[推测] The tapestry functions as both historical narrative and political storytelling.

[19:29] **A Graphic Medieval Narrative**

[事实] The Normans are identified by long hair in front and shaved backs of the head, while the English have moustaches.
[事实] Curator Michael Lewis says the tapestry’s three-dimensional, cartoon-like characters feel immediately legible.
[事实] He compares its style to cartoon books or graphic novels.
[事实] The battle scenes include stripped corpses, felled horses, flying arrows, Harold’s death, English troops fleeing, and William’s victory.
[推测] Its visual clarity helps explain why a medieval object remains accessible to modern viewers.

[20:32] **The Missing Ending**

[事实] The end of the tapestry is missing.
[事实] It is theorized that the missing section may once have shown William’s coronation, but no one knows for sure.
[事实] One expert said the missing part could have been a few centimeters or a few meters long.
[事实] Nick Cullinan says the gaps invite people to think about why it was made, what is missing, and what it means.
[推测] The uncertainty around the ending adds to the tapestry’s appeal rather than diminishing it.

[21:25] **Unknown Makers and Intimate Labor**

[事实] Much about the tapestry’s creation remains unknown, including who commissioned it, who made it, and how it was done.
[事实] Nick Cullinan says it was probably embroidered by women, probably nuns.
[事实] He says it was probably made outside because of the light.
[事实] He describes the contrast between the monumental scale of the object and the intimacy of its making.
[推测] The segment emphasizes the human labor behind an artwork often discussed mainly as a historical artifact.

[22:00] **Details, Survival, and Timeless Themes**

[事实] The embroidered linen includes 737 animals and 627 people.
[事实] It contains Latin phrases, Aesop’s fables in the margins, and prominent genitalia.
[事实] The tapestry survived threats including being nearly ripped up for wagon covers during the French Revolution, Napoleon’s interest in it, and Nazi efforts to steal it.
[事实] The segment says it evokes themes of promises, power, suffering, and salvation.
[推测] Its endurance and violent subject matter make it feel relevant across centuries.

[22:53] **Art After Violence**

[事实] Michael Lewis says the women who embroidered the tapestry may have reflected on scenes of violence as they stitched them.
[事实] The tapestry includes women experiencing violence, houses being plundered, and men being killed.
[事实] The narrator describes it as a tragic and timeless tableau of a world ravaged by violence.
[事实] The episode ends by saying survivors seek solace and understanding in art.
[推测] The segment frames the tapestry not just as a record of conquest, but as a way people process upheaval.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is strongest when it connects policy, business, and culture to visible human consequences: immigrants moving through crowded courts, Reddit communities producing value for AI systems, and medieval makers stitching scenes of violence into a monumental artwork.

The deportation segment is the most politically urgent. Its value lies in distinguishing official uncertainty from reported claims, and in showing how a quieter enforcement strategy can still be more expansive and aggressive.

The Reddit segment gives a concise account of a modern platform dilemma: the same human discussion that makes Reddit valuable may also help AI products reduce Reddit’s own traffic. The Bayeux Tapestry segment is more reflective, using vivid reporting to make an old object feel immediate.

[推测] The episode is well suited to listeners who want a fast, broad scan of current affairs with one deeper cultural essay at the end. Its limitation is that each topic is necessarily compressed, so listeners wanting legal detail on immigration courts, financial detail on Reddit, or art-historical debate on the tapestry would need follow-up sources.
