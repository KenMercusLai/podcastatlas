# Fredegund and Brunhilde: The Dark Queens of the Franks

## 概览

This episode tells the story of the feud between Fredegund and Brunhilde, two sixth-century Frankish queens whose rivalry shaped Merovingian politics for decades. The hosts frame them through Gregory of Tours, a key source who is openly hostile to Fredegund and far more sympathetic to Brunhilde.

The discussion moves from post-Roman Gaul and the fractured Frankish kingdoms into a chain of marriages, murders, child deaths, succession crises, and regencies. Fredegund rises from likely servile origins to become queen and regent of Neustria; Brunhilde, a Visigothic princess, becomes queen and later regent in Austrasia and Burgundy.

The central conclusion is that both women wielded extraordinary power in a violent and unstable world, but that power depended on husbands, sons, dynastic legitimacy, aristocratic backing, and survival. Their feud ends after Fredegund’s death, when her son Clothar captures and brutally executes Brunhilde.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Gregory of Tours and the Rival Queens**

[事实] The episode opens with Gregory of Tours describing Fredegund sending a household cleric to infiltrate Brunhilde’s court and assassinate her.

[事实] Gregory’s account presents Fredegund as a figure linked to assassination, cruelty, and witchcraft, while Brunhilde receives far more favorable treatment.

[事实] The hosts compare the Fredegund-Brunhilde feud to that of Elizabeth I and Mary, Queen of Scots, while stressing that this Frankish rivalry was bloodier.

[推测] The opening signals that the story must be read through hostile sources as well as through the dramatic political violence they preserve.

[05:03] **Post-Roman Gaul and Fredegund’s Origins**

[事实] The setting is the former western Roman Empire, where Frankish rulers under Clovis and his successors have taken power in Gaul.

[事实] Fredegund’s birth date is uncertain, probably in the 530s or early 540s, and the hosts say she seems to have begun life as a slave.

[事实] The mid-sixth century is described as a disastrous period marked by volcanic cooling, plague, repeated dysentery outbreaks, war, and social instability.

[推测] Fredegund’s later ascent is presented as especially striking because she starts from a position with almost no formal security.

[07:58] **Frankish Kingdoms and Chilperic’s Resentment**

[事实] The hosts argue that Frankish kings inherited Roman military traditions as much as “barbarian” ones.

[事实] Clothar divides his kingdom among four sons, creating unstable territories and rival claims.

[事实] Chilperic receives the small western kingdom of Neustria and bitterly resents his brother Sigebert, ruler of the larger eastern kingdom of Austrasia.

[推测] The partition of the kingdom makes family rivalry almost structural, giving personal grudges a territorial and military form.

[12:40] **Fredegund Enters Chilperic’s Bed and Politics**

[事实] Fredegund is described as a slave in Chilperic’s household who is intelligent, daring, and able to read people and gather information.

[事实] A later story says she rose from kitchen service to serve Chilperic’s queen, then helped displace that queen.

[事实] Fredegund becomes Chilperic’s chief royal concubine, but this position remains insecure because it depends on his favor.

[推测] The hosts frame her early skills in observation, manipulation, and secrecy as the foundations of her later political survival.

[15:26] **Galswintha’s Marriage and Murder**

[事实] Chilperic seeks a Visigothic princess after his territory expands toward the Pyrenees, making him more relevant to the Visigothic kingdom in Spain.

[事实] Galswintha arrives in 568 with a large dowry, and the marriage negotiations require Chilperic to give up Fredegund.

[事实] After Galswintha finds Chilperic in bed with Fredegund and threatens to return to Spain, she is found strangled in bed.

[事实] A few days later, Chilperic marries Fredegund, and Gregory of Tours says Chilperic ordered Galswintha’s killing.

[推测] The speed of Fredegund’s marriage to Chilperic makes suspicion around her politically unavoidable, even though the transcript does not prove she ordered the murder.

[20:54] **Brunhilde Enters the Feud**

[事实] Brunhilde is Galswintha’s younger sister and the wife of Sigebert of Austrasia.

[事实] She is described as beautiful, fashionable, politically gifted, and successful at building alliances at Sigebert’s court.

[事实] Brunhilde urges Sigebert to seek justice for Galswintha’s murder, and Chilperic is tried in absentia after refusing to answer Guntram’s summons.

[事实] Sigebert invades Neustria in 575, takes Paris, and appears close to victory as Neustrian nobles defect to him.

[推测] Brunhilde’s personal grief becomes a legally and religiously framed political campaign against Chilperic and Fredegund.

[25:56] **Sigebert’s Assassination Reverses Everything**

[事实] Two small slave boys approach Sigebert with a supposed message and stab him with poisoned knives.

[事实] Sigebert dies shortly afterward, and the boys are immediately killed.

[事实] The hosts say Fredegund is assumed to be the person with the motive, nerve, and skill to organize such an assassination.

[推测] The killing saves Fredegund and Chilperic at the moment of defeat, while deepening Fredegund’s reputation for underhand violence.

[27:40] **The Precarious Power of Queens**

[事实] Janet L. Nelson is cited to explain that a Merovingian queen’s position depended heavily on her husband’s favor and sexual attachment.

[事实] The hosts stress that both Fredegund the former slave and Brunhilde the foreign princess faced severe constraints as women at Frankish courts.

[事实] Their rivalry is described as lasting decades and as being remarkable for both women’s endurance and political force.

[推测] The feud is not only a personal vendetta but also a struggle over how women could exercise power when formal kingship belonged to men.

[29:21] **Fredegund Restored, Brunhilde Isolated**

[事实] After Sigebert’s death, his young son Childebert is not seen as an immediate threat, and many nobles return to Chilperic.

[事实] Chilperic treats Fredegund with unusual respect and devotion after this point.

[事实] Brunhilde is left stranded in Paris, but her son Childebert is smuggled back to Austrasia and crowned on Christmas Day 575.

[事实] Chilperic sends Brunhilde to a convent in Rouen, effectively imprisoning her.

[推测] Fredegund’s suspected role in Sigebert’s death remains unproven, but the political result strongly benefits her.

[34:00] **Brunhilde’s Marriage to Merovich**

[事实] Merovich, Chilperic’s eldest son by his first wife, disobeys orders and goes to Rouen, where Brunhilde is held.

[事实] The hosts say Brunhilde likely encouraged him to marry her as part of an alliance against Fredegund.

[事实] Bishop Praetextatus performs the marriage despite its problematic kinship implications.

[事实] Chilperic captures Merovich, has his royal hair shaved, sends him to a monastery, and Merovich later escapes before having a servant kill him when cornered.

[推测] The marriage is presented as a bold but failed attempt by Brunhilde to regain agency and threaten Fredegund’s dynastic plans.

[38:47] **Dysentery, Witchcraft, and Succession Violence**

[事实] In 580, a dysentery epidemic kills Fredegund’s two sons, undermining her future security as queen mother.

[事实] Gregory of Tours says Fredegund accuses Clovis, Chilperic’s surviving son by his first wife, of using witchcraft with a servant to kill her children.

[事实] Clovis is handed over to Fredegund and dies in custody; the alleged witch and her mother are tortured and executed.

[事实] Fredegund also has Chilperic’s first wife murdered, and after another son dies in 582 she punishes suspected witches and burns the dead child’s possessions.

[推测] The episode presents Fredegund’s violence here as driven by a mixture of grief, succession panic, and belief in witchcraft.

[43:02] **The Secret Heir and Chilperic’s Murder**

[事实] Fredegund gives birth to another son in secret because she and Chilperic fear witchcraft.

[事实] In September 584, Chilperic is stabbed to death by a servant after returning from hunting.

[事实] The hosts say Fredegund is accused by some, but they argue her involvement would make little sense because she needed Chilperic alive until her infant son matured.

[事实] Fredegund writes to Guntram, reveals the baby, and asks him to take charge of his brother’s kingdom while she presents herself as his humble servant.

[推测] Fredegund survives this crisis by making vulnerability itself a political tactic.

[45:25] **Regencies, Treaties, and Fredegund’s Rule**

[事实] Guntram protects Fredegund and her son Clothar, allowing Fredegund to rule Neustria as regent.

[事实] Brunhilde signs the Treaty of Andelot with Guntram on 28 November 587, confirming that her son Childebert will inherit Burgundy.

[事实] Fredegund continues covert action against Brunhilde, including inciting revolt, while Bishop Praetextatus is murdered in 586.

[事实] A story is told of Fredegund ending a family blood feud by inviting three faction leaders to a banquet and having them executed.

[推测] Her authority rests on both fear and a visible ability to impose order.

[48:38] **The Feud Restarts After Guntram’s Death**

[事实] After Guntram dies in 592, Brunhilde moves against Neustria and orders forces to capture Soissons.

[事实] Fredegund personally leads a smaller Neustrian army and uses surprise tactics at Doisy, including bells on horses and branches as disguise.

[事实] The Austrasian force is surprised and massacred, and Fredegund returns to Soissons with booty.

[推测] This battle is presented as Fredegund’s last major confrontation with Brunhilde and as proof that she could act as a war leader, not only as a court intriguer.

[50:50] **Dual Female Rule**

[事实] In March 595, Brunhilde’s son Childebert dies of dysentery at age 25.

[事实] Brunhilde becomes regent for her grandsons Theudebert and Theuderic.

[事实] Shelley Puhak is cited to emphasize that Fredegund and Brunhilde ruled as regents at the same time over territories covering much of modern France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg, western and southern Germany, and parts of Switzerland.

[事实] The hosts say the two women were implacable enemies but never met.

[推测] By this stage, the episode portrays them as mirror images: rival queens using motherhood, regency, violence, and political intelligence to hold power.

[51:57] **Fredegund’s Death and Brunhilde’s Fall**

[事实] Fredegund dies in 597 in her bed, and her son Clothar inherits the feud with Brunhilde.

[事实] Brunhilde’s grandsons Theudebert and Theuderic fall out; Theuderic kills his elder brother and later dies of dysentery.

[事实] At around age 70, Brunhilde again becomes regent for underage heirs, but Austrasian nobles led by Pippin and Bishop Arnulf of Metz hand her and her great-grandchildren over to Clothar.

[事实] Clothar has two boys beheaded, sends the girl to a convent, spares the youngest boy, tries Brunhilde for murdering ten kings, and has her tortured, humiliated, dragged by horses, and burned.

[推测] The charges against Brunhilde function as a political rewriting of the feud, shifting blame away from Fredegund and onto her enemy.

[56:00] **A Carolingian Shadow**

[事实] The episode ends by noting that Pippin and Arnulf’s descendants will later overshadow the Merovingian line.

[事实] The hosts then preview the next episode on the Battle of Tours.

[推测] Brunhilde’s destruction is used as a bridge from Merovingian family conflict toward the later rise of new Frankish power brokers.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strength is its combination of narrative drive and source awareness. It tells a dramatic story of murder, revenge, dynastic collapse, and battlefield cunning, while repeatedly reminding listeners that Gregory of Tours is not a neutral witness.

[推测] Its main limitation is that some claims depend on hostile or uncertain sources, especially accusations of assassination and witchcraft. The hosts often flag uncertainty, but the story’s pace can still make suspicion feel close to proof.

[推测] This episode is especially suitable for listeners interested in early medieval politics, powerful royal women, post-Roman Europe, and history told through vivid narrative. It is less suited to listeners looking for a dry institutional account of Merovingian administration.
