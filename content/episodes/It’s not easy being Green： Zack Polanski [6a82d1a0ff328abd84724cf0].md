# It’s not easy being Green: Zack Polanski

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* profiles Zack Polanski, the British Green Party leader who has turned a once-staid environmental party into a vehicle for younger, left-populist energy. The report follows him from a Green Party rave in London to a sit-down interview, using his personal history, theatrical instincts, controversies and policy platform to explain his appeal.

The central conclusion is that Polanski’s rise is not just a story about one politician. It reflects a wider movement of voters away from centrist parties toward populist alternatives, driven by economic stagnation, housing pressure, political disillusionment and a desire for leaders who speak directly to grievance.

The episode then turns to two shorter segments: synthetic “spud cells” made at the University of Minnesota, which challenge the boundary between living and non-living matter; and the surprising persistence of human travel agents in an age of AI planning tools.

## 分段落总结

[01:19] **Episode Setup**

[事实] Rosie Blore introduces *The Intelligence* and says the episode will cover cells with no biological ancestors and why people still use human travel agents.
[事实] The first major report is Andrew Miller’s profile of Zack Polanski and the British Green Party.

[01:53] **A Green Party Rave**

[事实] Andrew Miller describes reporting from an outdoor rave in London’s Docklands where around a thousand Gen Z clubbers had gathered in solidarity with the Green Party.
[事实] The crowd had come largely because of Zack Polanski, whose leadership had made the Greens more visible among people in their late teens and early 20s.
[推测] The rave is used as a symbol of how Polanski has made Green politics feel culturally energetic rather than merely earnest or environmentalist.

[03:17] **Polanski’s Transformation of the Greens**

[事实] The segment says Polanski has more than tripled Green Party membership and boosted its polling.
[事实] Critics describe him as dangerous or even a con artist, but Miller argues his rise is more interesting than those critiques suggest.
[事实] The report links Polanski’s appeal to stagnating economies, frustration with established parties and votes moving from the centre to populist fringes.
[推测] The episode frames Polanski less as an isolated novelty than as part of a broader Western political realignment.

[04:25] **Personal Background and Identity**

[事实] Miller meets Polanski in a courtyard cafe near Euston Station, where they are interrupted by admirers.
[事实] Polanski describes unhappy younger years connected to his parents’ divorce and coming to terms with his sexuality.
[事实] He grew up on the edge of Manchester, is gay, was bullied, and says he was attacked on Canal Street when he was about 15 or 16.
[事实] Polanski is Jewish and was involved in Habonim Dror, a Zionist socialist youth movement.

[06:27] **Gaza, Jewish Identity and Controversy**

[事实] The report says many British Jews now see Polanski as having betrayed his origins because of his embrace of anti-Israel sentiment on the new left.
[事实] Under his leadership, Gaza has become a Green Party touchstone, even in local election campaigns.
[事实] The episode says Polanski caused controversy in April by questioning whether British Jews were experiencing actual unsafety or only a perception of unsafety.
[推测] This segment presents one of the sharpest tensions in Polanski’s public identity: his Jewish background sits uneasily beside positions that many British Jews find hostile.

[07:04] **Acting, Odd Jobs and Hypnotherapy**

[事实] Polanski studied drama and sociology at Aberystwyth University and spent a year in America.
[事实] He later moved to London to pursue acting and worked in nightclubs, as a waiter and as a hypnotherapist.
[事实] In 2013, he told a tabloid journalist that hypnotherapy could enlarge women’s breasts; he says he did not charge for that work.
[事实] The report also mentions resurfaced embarrassments, including a misleading claim to have been a Red Cross spokesperson.
[推测] Miller suggests Polanski’s performative background may help him connect with voters and manage attention, even as it gives critics material to question his trustworthiness.

[09:07] **Precarity as Political Asset**

[事实] The episode says graduate jobs in Britain have become scarcer and more than two-thirds of 23-year-olds live with their parents amid high rents.
[事实] Miller argues Polanski’s firsthand experience of precarious work makes him a timely voice for many people in the gig economy.
[推测] Polanski’s irregular career path may strengthen his credibility with voters who feel shut out of stable professional life.

[09:27] **From the Lib Dems to the Greens**

[事实] Polanski joined the centrist Liberal Democrats in 2015, despite having had little interest in political parties in his youth.
[事实] Former Lib Dem colleagues remembered him singing at a party conference and trying to secure a parliamentary by-election candidacy in 2016.
[事实] Polanski switched to the Greens in 2017 and was elected to the London Assembly four years later.
[推测] The report implies that Polanski’s move through a weakened party and then into the Greens shows both ambition and a sense for political opportunity.

[10:54] **Eco-Populism and Farage Comparisons**

[事实] Under Polanski, the Greens are described as a vehicle for eco-populism or eco-socialism.
[事实] The party remains interested in the environment, but the report says economic grievances and “squeezing the rich” have become more prominent.
[事实] Polanski rejects comparison with Nigel Farage, arguing Farage represents the elite rather than “the people.”
[推测] The episode presents Polanski and Farage as ideological opposites who nevertheless benefit from being cast against each other.

[12:10] **Burnham, 2029 and the Green Platform**

[事实] Polanski says Andy Burnham’s current positioning would not exist if the Green Party were not doing so well.
[事实] A British general election is due by 2029, and the episode says current polling could make the Greens coalition partners for Labour.
[事实] The Greens’ platform includes nationalised water, rent controls, higher top income and capital-gains taxes, legalising and regulating all drugs, ditching the monarchy and nuclear weapons, and seeking an alternative to NATO.
[事实] The report identifies the Greens’ liberal migration policy, including opposition to detention camps for migrants or failed asylum seekers, as a major vulnerability.
[推测] The episode suggests Polanski could either gain real power, damage Labour’s chances, or lose support once voters focus on the radicalism of the platform.

[14:15] **Decline, Symbolism and Political Promise**

[事实] Miller reflects that the rave’s brutalist venue had once been a hub for industrial firms.
[事实] He says the building’s transformation could symbolize decline for those who think Britain has fallen from “muscular enterprise” into revolutionary cosplay.
[事实] Miller says Polanski is not a declineist and instead offers “the dream of a magically improved future.”
[推测] The closing of the Polanski segment treats his appeal as emotional as much as programmatic.

[15:05] **What Counts as Life**

[事实] Oliver Morton explains that at the simplest level, life can be understood as a cell: a membrane-bound unit of molecules that can grow, reproduce and evolve.
[事实] He says all ordinary cells descend from earlier cells in a chain going back to the ancestors of all living things.
[推测] The segment sets up synthetic cells as a challenge to the idea that every cell belongs to an unbroken biological lineage.

[16:23] **Synthetic Spud Cells**

[事实] Morton says Kate Adamalla’s lab at the University of Minnesota has created synthetic cells from scratch using chemicals.
[事实] These cells have no biological ancestors and are described as the first such cells that can reproduce themselves.
[事实] The “spud cell” line can reproduce for a small but real number of generations.
[推测] The name and modest capabilities underline that this is a major scientific step, not a fully natural-like organism.

[17:26] **How Spud Cells Reproduce**

[事实] The cells are made by squeezing out bits of membrane that contain internal material.
[事实] They include seven small DNA molecules acting as chromosomes.
[事实] Feeder blobs add to their membranes, and once the membranes grow large enough, the genes cause the cells to split in two.
[推测] The design is highly simplified, but it demonstrates that some cell-like behaviours can be engineered from the bottom up.

[18:00] **Alive or Not Alive**

[事实] Morton says life and death are not always binary concepts in the biology lab.
[事实] He describes a spectrum between living and non-living things.
[事实] Spud cells are further toward living than anything else made in a lab, but they cannot reproduce perfectly and have almost no metabolism.
[推测] The episode treats spud cells as scientifically important because they expose how hard it is to define life precisely.

[19:03] **Why Build Synthetic Cells**

[事实] Morton says the project is closer to “pure engineering” than pure science: scientists build the cells to understand how to build such systems.
[事实] Because the cells are designed from the bottom up, researchers know why each component behaves as it does.
[事实] Possible future uses include streamlined cells built for specific tasks, though Morton says they are mostly research tools for now.
[推测] Their value lies more in clarifying life’s basic mechanisms than in immediate commercial or medical applications.

[21:21] **Human Travel Agents Persist**

[事实] Caitlin Talbot says that despite AI itineraries and online travel reviews, people still turn to human travel agents.
[事实] ABTA estimates that about a third of British travellers booked holidays through a human last year.
[事实] The American Society of Travel Advisers has more than 310,000 agent members, up from nearly 200,000 in 2024.
[推测] The segment argues that travel agents are adapting rather than disappearing.

[22:38] **What Human Agents Offer**

[事实] Talbot says travel agents provide taste, judgment, curation, destination knowledge, hotel networks and VIP perks.
[事实] Some agents specialize in niches such as food allergies, solo travel or travel with pets.
[事实] Human advisers provide support when things go wrong, such as a wrong room or missed transfer.
[事实] People are more likely to use agents during geopolitical turmoil or extreme weather, when travel feels riskier.
[推测] The human advantage is strongest when travel involves uncertainty, complexity or emotional stakes.

[23:44] **Travel Agents and AI**

[事实] Some travel advisers criticize AI’s shortcomings, but many have begun using it in their work.
[事实] Some agencies treat AI as a team member that handles boring administrative tasks.
[事实] Fora is described as a modern travel agency that built a billion-dollar business by giving advisers an AI-powered platform.
[推测] AI is presented less as a direct replacement for advisers than as infrastructure that can make their work faster.

[24:12] **A Personal Booking Example**

[事实] Talbot says she booked her honeymoon with a travel agent because she wanted a luxury safari, visa advice and less stress.
[事实] She says the booking experience was thoroughly enjoyable.
[事实] She expects people may use human advisers less as bots improve, but top-end services will probably endure.
[推测] The segment’s outlook is that AI will absorb routine planning, while human agents remain valuable for special trips and high-touch service.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest section is the Polanski profile, because it connects biography, performance, policy and voter mood rather than treating his rise as a simple personality story.

[推测] Its limitation is that the profile is filtered through a skeptical Economist lens, especially in its language around populism, radicalism and “magically improved” futures; listeners looking for a sympathetic Green Party case may find the framing pointed.

[推测] The science and travel-agent segments broaden the episode effectively: one asks what life is, the other asks what human judgment is still worth when AI is available. The episode is best suited to listeners interested in contemporary politics, applied science and social changes behind everyday decisions.
