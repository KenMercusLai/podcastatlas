# Pompeii, Herculaneum and Vesuvius

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* discusses Pompeii, Herculaneum, Vesuvius, and the archaeological challenges of interpreting a city preserved by catastrophe. Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook are joined by archaeologist Sophie Hay, who has worked in Pompeii and surrounding sites for 25 years.

The conversation moves between the public image of Pompeii as a tourist destination and the professional reality of excavation, conservation, and interpretation. Hay stresses that Pompeii is not simply a “time capsule,” but a layered city with centuries of development before the eruption.

Key themes include the mechanics and dating of the eruption, recent discoveries such as a newly excavated bar, the ethics of displaying plaster casts, the role of graffiti and inscriptions in reconstructing lives, Roman food and sanitation, and the difficulty of proving the presence of Christians or Jews in Pompeii.

## 分段落总结

[00:14] **Introducing Pompeii and Sophie Hay**

[事实] The episode focuses on Pompeii, Herculaneum, Vesuvius, and the archaeological work connected to them.

[事实] Sophie Hay is introduced as an archaeologist who has worked at Pompeii and adjoining areas for 25 years and runs Pompeii’s social media accounts.

[事实] Hay is preparing a book about women archaeologists who worked at Pompeii.

[01:34] **Pompeii as workplace and tourist attraction**

[事实] Dominic describes Pompeii as both an archaeological site and a heavily touristed attraction.

[事实] Hay says she often worked in areas closed to tourists, but when she worked in the Forum, visitors constantly photographed her and asked questions.

[事实] She says the attention can slow work, but she values public fascination because it shows that the work matters to many people.

[03:05] **What tourists see and what remains unexcavated**

[事实] Hay says many visitors see only the Forum, the brothel, and one house before leaving.

[事实] She explains that access to buildings changes frequently because some are opened while others are closed for conservation.

[事实] About one third of Pompeii remains unexcavated.

[05:04] **The eruption and preservation of Pompeii**

[事实] Hay says the eruption lasted about 24 hours and began with a column of smoke, ash, and gases rising from Vesuvius.

[事实] Pliny the Younger provides an eyewitness account of the eruption.

[事实] The ash column rose to about 30 kilometers before collapsing and sending pumice down over the city.

[事实] Hay says many people may have stayed because the disaster unfolded gradually and they hoped they would be all right.

[06:28] **Ancient awareness of Vesuvius and earthquakes**

[事实] Hay says the previous major eruption was nearly 2,000 years earlier, so Pompeians did not have a clear model for what was happening.

[事实] They were familiar with earthquakes and knew they lived in an unstable area.

[事实] Pliny the Elder did not clearly recognize Vesuvius as a volcano.

[推测] Myths about Hercules, giants, and local place names may reflect some cultural memory of dangerous forces beneath the mountain.

[08:54] **Pompeii before the eruption**

[事实] Hay describes Pompeii as more of a working port town, while Herculaneum was more like a resort.

[事实] She rejects the idea that Pompeii was merely an ordinary Roman town, emphasizing its craftsmanship and organization.

[事实] Pompeii predates Roman control and had earlier Oscan and Greek connections before becoming a Roman colony after Sulla.

[事实] Sulla’s siege left marks on Pompeii’s walls.

[10:59] **Against the “time capsule” cliché**

[事实] Hay objects to calling Pompeii a frozen time capsule because the city had a long history back to the sixth century BC.

[事实] She describes Pompeii as a palimpsest, with older and newer elements layered together.

[推测] The site preserves a moment of destruction, but not a simple, static snapshot of “Roman life.”

[12:24] **Everyday lives and overlooked people**

[事实] Hay says Pompeii allows historians to move away from emperors and civic buildings toward everyday life.

[事实] The site helps illuminate bar owners, laundry workers, and enslaved people who rarely appear in written histories.

[事实] Hay stresses that the city could only function with enslaved labor behind it.

[14:24] **Plaster casts and the ethics of display**

[事实] Dominic raises the question of whether displaying plaster casts of people who died in agony crosses an ethical line.

[事实] Hay says she is conflicted: the casts feel voyeuristic, but they give Pompeii a powerful sense of human suffering and population.

[事实] She says the casts help visitors and archaeologists connect emotionally with the disaster.

[16:03] **Projecting stories onto bodies**

[事实] The speakers discuss the temptation to impose narratives onto plaster casts and bodies.

[事实] Hay gives examples where assumed identities, such as a pregnant woman or a person called “the Moor,” were later questioned or overturned by scans and analysis.

[事实] Hay says people naturally create stories to relate emotionally to the dead, even though evidence often complicates those stories.

[17:52] **Reconstructing lives through inscriptions**

[事实] Hay discusses Gaius Alleius Nigidius Maius as an example of how fragmentary evidence can build a biography.

[事实] His name appeared in a rental notice found in 1810, and later evidence linked him to organizing amphitheatre games.

[事实] A tomb inscription found around 2018 probably belongs to him and describes a banquet for nearly 7,000 people and his role during Nero’s ban on games.

[事实] Hay says reconstructing one person’s life took about 200 years and evidence from different places in the city.

[21:08] **Erotic art and Roman difference**

[事实] Dominic asks whether Pompeii’s erotic art has been exaggerated or whether it reflects a Roman moral world unlike the modern one.

[事实] Hay says erotic imagery and phallic symbols were widespread across Pompeian society.

[事实] Early excavators were horrified and removed such material to restricted collections, including what became the Secret Museum in Naples.

[事实] The Secret Museum opened to the public in 2000.

[23:43] **Public sexuality, private space, and slavery**

[事实] Hay says erotic images appeared in contexts such as brothels and private houses, while phallic symbols also appeared publicly as good-luck objects.

[事实] Tom argues that Pompeii’s sexual culture must be understood alongside slavery and the sexual exploitation of enslaved people.

[事实] He says the idea that Romans were “just like us” breaks down when looking closely at the realities of ownership and abuse.

[27:08] **Recent excavations and the Pompeii bar**

[事实] Hay says recent excavations were part of a conservation project funded to stabilize excavation edges, not simply digging for new finds.

[事实] She identifies a newly excavated bar as one of the most important recent discoveries.

[事实] The bar contained food remains in pots, including duck bone, and evidence suggesting hot food was served.

[事实] Hay says this changes the earlier picture that such bars mainly served dried food.

[29:51] **Herculaneum and the Villa of the Papyri**

[事实] The speakers note that modern buildings stand over parts of Herculaneum.

[事实] Hay recalls working near a house where a mafia boss under house arrest was watching excavation work.

[事实] The Villa of the Papyri is mentioned as a famous site with tantalizing possibilities.

[31:03] **The disputed date of the eruption**

[事实] Hay says the traditional date of 24 August comes from medieval copies of Pliny the Younger’s letters.

[事实] Other manuscript traditions give dates in October, November, or December.

[事实] Archaeological evidence such as ripened pomegranates and grape-pressing debris suggests the eruption happened later in autumn.

[事实] Hay says many scholars would like to say the autumn date is now preferred, though 24 August is still widely repeated.

[33:14] **Charcoal graffiti, coins, and uncertain evidence**

[事实] A charcoal graffito from new excavations gives a date of 17 October, which would conflict with an August eruption.

[事实] Hay notes that the graffito does not include a year, so it cannot conclusively date the eruption.

[事实] A coin once thought to prove a later date was later questioned because the relevant inscription was too worn to read securely.

[推测] The discussion highlights how ancient-history evidence can be suggestive without being decisive.

[34:43] **Rediscovery and memory of Pompeii**

[事实] Hay says Bourbon excavators wanted to present Pompeii’s rediscovery in 1748 as sudden and dramatic.

[事实] She argues that the site was effectively known earlier: Pompeii appears on a late Roman map, and the area was later called La Civita.

[事实] In 1594, canal diggers hit walls while tunneling through the mound, but they continued because the finds were not treated as important.

[37:05] **Politics, collecting, and excavation history**

[事实] Hay says Pompeii has repeatedly been used politically, including by the Bourbons, Napoleon, Mussolini, and during Italian unification.

[事实] She argues that without a political or collecting motive, earlier generations had less reason to excavate the site systematically.

[事实] Herculaneum began attracting attention in the early eighteenth century after marble was pulled from the theater area.

[39:18] **Diet in Pompeii and Herculaneum**

[事实] Hay cites Andrew Wallace-Hadrill’s sewer excavations at Herculaneum, which showed food remains from houses and shops of different social levels.

[事实] Evidence included fresh fish, shellfish, meat, and vegetables.

[事实] Hay says diet appears to be a great unifier across social groups in these towns.

[事实] Archaeological remains include figs, grain, dates, and loaves of bread.

[41:56] **Sanitation, smells, and street cleaning**

[事实] Hay says Pompeii had few sewers and mainly used soakaways for human waste.

[事实] She says Pompeii would at times have smelled bad.

[事实] Laws required property owners to keep areas outside their property clean, but recent excavations found evidence of waste accumulation in streets.

[事实] Rainwater running downhill through streets helped flush the roads, explaining the usefulness of stepping stones.

[44:04] **Future discoveries and Hay’s dream find**

[事实] Hay says there is still plenty to find because one third of Pompeii remains unexcavated.

[事实] She says future value lies less in spectacular monuments than in small details such as graffiti, political notices, and food evidence.

[事实] Her dream discovery would be something like a planning office, because it would reveal how people changed and extended their houses.

[46:16] **Christians, Jews, and uncertain religious evidence**

[事实] Tom discusses a disputed inscription found in 1862 that may mention Christians, though its transcription and interpretation are uncertain.

[事实] A supposed crucifix at Herculaneum was later likely explained as a shelf fixture.

[事实] Hay says evidence for Jews in Pompeii is also limited, though Jews are known in Rome.

[事实] The speakers return to the Indian goddess figure as one clearer sign of wider cultural connections.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it treats Pompeii not as a simple spectacle of disaster, but as a complicated urban, social, and archaeological problem. Sophie Hay’s expertise gives the discussion weight, especially when she pushes back against familiar clichés like “frozen in time.”

A major strength is the balance between vivid public-interest subjects, such as bodies, brothels, food, and eruption dates, and the slower reality of archaeological interpretation. The episode repeatedly shows that Pompeii’s evidence is powerful but fragmentary.

Its main limitation is that some topics are necessarily brief, especially religion, the Villa of the Papyri, and the social history of enslaved people. Those sections point toward deeper questions rather than resolving them.

[推测] The episode is especially suitable for listeners who know Pompeii mainly as a tourist destination and want a more nuanced account of what the site can and cannot tell us.
