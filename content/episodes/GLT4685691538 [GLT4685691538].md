# The Princes in the Tower: Richard III, Usurpation, and a Murder Mystery

## 概览

This episode revisits the final stage of the princes in the Tower story: Edward IV’s death, Edward V’s brief and precarious kingship, Richard of Gloucester’s seizure of control, and the disappearance of Edward V and Richard of Shrewsbury.

The hosts argue that Richard probably did not begin with a long-planned bid for the crown. Instead, they present him as improvising under pressure, first trying to secure access to the young king and then escalating through arrests, executions, delegitimisation, and usurpation.

Their conclusion is that the princes were most probably murdered on Richard III’s orders, while acknowledging that definitive proof is impossible. They also explore alternative suspects, later pretenders, discovered bones, and modern theories that the princes may have survived.

## 分段落总结

[00:09] **Ricardian Romance and the Mystery’s Frame**

[事实] The episode opens with Josephine Tey’s *The Daughter of Time*, described as a major source of modern sympathy for Richard III and fascination with the princes in the Tower.
[事实] The hosts connect that sympathetic image to Philippa Langley’s reaction to Richard III’s portrait and later reconstruction.
[推测] The opening frames the episode as a clash between the romantic image of Richard and the harsher political evidence.

[02:12] **Edward IV’s Death and the Succession Problem**

[事实] Edward IV dies on 9 April 1483, leaving Edward V, aged 12, and his younger brother Richard.
[事实] Edward V is at Ludlow with Anthony Woodville, while Richard of Gloucester is in the North and Elizabeth Woodville is in London.
[事实] The council initially plans Edward V’s coronation for 4 May.
[推测] The core crisis is not the legal succession itself, but who will control the young king and the machinery of government.

[03:30] **Did Richard Plan to Take the Crown?**

[事实] The hosts say there is no evidence that Richard intended to become king before Edward IV died.
[事实] Richard is described as improvising and seeking short-term solutions rather than executing a long-term plan.
[事实] The role of protector is presented as dangerous, with Humphrey Duke of Gloucester under Henry VI used as a warning from history.
[推测] Richard’s early actions are interpreted more as defensive power politics than as a premeditated coup.

[07:53] **Woodvilles, Hastings, and Fragile Compromise**

[事实] Elizabeth Woodville and Hastings try to reach an accommodation in London, including limiting the size of armed retinues.
[事实] Hastings is described as a loyal supporter of Edward IV, despite tensions with the Woodvilles.
[事实] The hosts say there is chronicle evidence for Edward IV trying to reconcile factions on his deathbed.
[推测] The court is shown as a system where compromise exists, but fear of betrayal makes pre-emptive violence plausible.

[10:50] **Northampton, Buckingham, and the Seizure of Edward V**

[事实] Richard, Anthony Woodville, Buckingham, and Edward V’s party converge around Northampton and Stony Stratford.
[事实] After Richard and Buckingham talk late into the night, Anthony Woodville and Richard Grey are arrested the next morning.
[事实] Richard and Buckingham tell Edward V that Rivers is a traitor and morally disreputable, but Edward defends Rivers.
[推测] Edward V’s age and loyalty to Rivers make him much harder for Richard to manage than a younger child would have been.

[16:34] **Sanctuary, the Council, and the Tower**

[事实] Elizabeth Woodville takes sanctuary at Westminster with Richard of York after the arrests.
[事实] Richard asks the council to condemn Rivers as a traitor, but the council asks for evidence while still largely going along with him.
[事实] Edward V’s coronation is postponed, and he is lodged in the Tower of London.
[推测] The Tower is not inherently presented as a murder site at this stage, but Edward is isolated under Richard’s control.

[19:48] **The Fall of Hastings**

[事实] On 10 June, Richard summons a large northern force, breaking the earlier understanding about limiting armed retinues.
[事实] The hosts suggest Richard probably sounded out Hastings about the possibility of becoming king.
[事实] Hastings remains loyal to Edward V and is executed after Richard accuses Elizabeth Woodville of witchcraft.
[推测] Hastings’s execution marks a decisive escalation from controlling the succession to removing obstacles to usurpation.

[22:35] **Richard of York and the Illegitimacy Campaign**

[事实] Elizabeth Woodville hands over young Richard of Shrewsbury from sanctuary to join his brother in the Tower.
[事实] Richard then delays the coronation again, and Ralph Shaw preaches that Edward IV was illegitimate and that his marriage to Elizabeth Woodville was invalid.
[事实] Buckingham promotes support for Richard, and on 25 June the princes are declared illegitimate.
[推测] The hosts treat the arguments against Edward IV’s legitimacy and marriage as politically convenient and morally damaging to Richard.

[28:40] **Richard III Crowned and the Princes Vanish**

[事实] Rivers and Richard Grey are executed at Pontefract.
[事实] Richard III is crowned on 6 July, but Edward V is absent despite preparations for him to attend.
[事实] The princes are later seen in the Tower gardens and then disappear from public view after their servants are dismissed.
[推测] Edward V’s absence from the coronation weakens the idea that the old heir visibly accepted Richard’s new regime.

[30:59] **Who Murdered the Princes?**

[事实] The hosts conclude that the overwhelming probability is that the princes were murdered on Richard’s orders, while admitting certainty is impossible.
[事实] Buckingham is discussed as the main alternative suspect, partly because some Ricardian writers and Josephine Tey’s novel point to him.
[事实] The hosts argue Buckingham probably could not have acted inside the Tower without Richard’s knowledge.
[推测] Richard’s control of access, political motive, and failure to produce the boys make him the strongest suspect in their analysis.

[33:08] **Rebellion, Buckingham, and Other Suspects**

[事实] A late July revolt aims to rescue the boys and stage a pro-Woodville countercoup.
[事实] Buckingham later rebels in support of Henry Tudor, is captured, and is executed.
[事实] Richard does not blame Buckingham for the princes’ deaths, despite having a convenient opportunity to do so.
[推测] The shift of anti-Ricardian hopes toward Henry Tudor suggests many people already assumed the princes were dead.

[37:49] **Rumours, Henry VII, and Pretenders**

[事实] Later stories picked up by Thomas More and Shakespeare include claims that Brackenbury refused to kill the princes and that Sir James Tyrrell carried out the murder.
[事实] Henry VII does not make the princes central after Bosworth because their status complicates his own claim.
[事实] Lambert Simnel and Perkin Warbeck later emerge as Yorkist pretenders connected to disputed identities around the missing princes and other heirs.
[推测] The pretenders show that uncertainty over the princes remained politically useful after Richard’s death.

[42:15] **Bones and Later Discoveries**

[事实] In 1674, child bones found in the Tower are identified as the princes and buried in Westminster Abbey by order of Charles II.
[事实] A 1933 re-examination finds incomplete skeletons and animal bones.
[事实] The hosts also mention child bodies found near Edward IV and Elizabeth Woodville’s burial place at St George’s Chapel, Windsor.
[推测] The physical evidence remains intriguing but inconclusive.

[44:10] **The Devon Survival Theory**

[事实] The hosts discuss a Telegraph-reported theory linked to the Missing Princes Project that Edward V survived as “John Evans” in Devon.
[事实] The theory involves hidden clues and symbols in a church.
[事实] The hosts note Philippa Langley’s involvement and acknowledge she previously helped find Richard III’s body.
[推测] They are highly sceptical because the theory requires both Richard III and Henry VII to spare and conceal a dangerous rival.

[47:22] **Why the Mystery Endures**

[事实] The hosts say the mystery survives because no conclusive answer is possible and the evidence allows multiple narratives.
[事实] They connect continued interest to kings, princes, the last Plantagenets, Ricardian romance, Shakespeare, and resistance to received wisdom.
[事实] Both hosts ultimately think Richard probably killed the princes.
[推测] Their final portrait of Richard is tragic rather than cartoonishly evil: a ruthless political actor whose choices destroyed his regime and family.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its balance between narrative drama and historical reasoning. It does not simply repeat the Shakespearean villain portrait, but it also resists the romantic impulse to exonerate Richard without strong evidence.

[推测] Its strongest moments come from explaining how each political step narrowed Richard’s options: seizing Edward V, alienating the council, killing Hastings, delegitimising the princes, and then facing rebellion.

[推测] The limitation is that the central murder cannot be proven from the transcript’s evidence, and the hosts themselves acknowledge that no definitive solution is available. The episode is best suited to listeners interested in late medieval English politics, dynastic crisis, and historical mysteries where probability matters more than certainty.
