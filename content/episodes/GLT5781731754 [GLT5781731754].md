# Paul McCartney on Liverpool, The Beatles, and the Uses of Memory

## 概览

Tom Holland presents a *Rest Is History* special centered on an interview with Paul McCartney, framed around the transcript’s stated new album, *The Boys of Dungeon Lane*, and its songs about McCartney’s early years in Liverpool.

The core question is whether Liverpool itself helped make McCartney and the Beatles possible. McCartney links the city’s Irish influence, port culture, wartime experience, music, humor and postwar resilience to the Beatles’ wit, confidence and creative habits.

The conversation moves from family memory to musical formation: his parents, bombsites, scarce American records, informal chord-sharing, John Lennon, literary influences, and the development of character-driven songs. It closes by treating memory itself as a historical problem.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Special Setup and Framing**

[事实] Tom introduces the episode as a *Rest Is History* special built around an interview with Paul McCartney.
[事实] He says many songs on McCartney’s new album look back to his childhood and teenage years in Liverpool.
[事实] He says the song “Salesman’s Saint” will feature in the interview.

[02:27] **Introducing McCartney as History**

[事实] Tom says the show has discussed many world-historical figures, but this is the first time he will speak directly to one.
[事实] He presents McCartney as central to the Beatles and to one of the most transformative decades in modern history.
[推测] The interview is framed as cultural history as much as music promotion.

[03:24] **Liverpool’s Character**

[事实] Tom asks whether McCartney or the Beatles could have emerged from a city other than Liverpool.
[事实] McCartney says Liverpool had a strong character shaped by Irish influence, wartime hardship, music at home and a habit of joking through difficulty.
[事实] He links that humor to the Beatles’ confidence when facing the New York press in America.

[05:01] **War and Postwar Resilience**

[事实] McCartney says all the Beatles were born during the war, but most were too young to remember the bombing directly.
[事实] He says adults around him had to carry on despite bombs falling on the city.
[事实] He remembers playing football on bombsites called “bommies,” only later realizing their danger and meaning.
[推测] The episode treats postwar resilience as one root of McCartney’s optimism.

[08:28] **Parents, Work and Aspiration**

[事实] “Salesman’s Saint” is discussed as a song about McCartney’s parents: his father was a salesman and his mother was a midwife.
[事实] McCartney says his family moved often, partly because his mother’s work provided houses that felt like upgrades.
[事实] He describes his mother as aspirational and recalls her encouraging her children away from a Liverpool accent.
[事实] He remembers her cycling through deep snow in uniform to attend a birth.

[12:05] **Irish Roots and Port-City Identity**

[事实] Tom raises McCartney’s Irish family origins and Liverpool’s sense of being not quite like the rest of England.
[事实] McCartney says Liverpudlians thought of themselves as special and saw the city as grand and historic.
[事实] He says he only later learned about Liverpool’s connection to the slave trade, while as a child he mainly noticed local Caribbean people.

[13:51] **American Records and Musical Scarcity**

[事实] McCartney says merchant sailors brought back records from America, including from New Orleans and the American South.
[事实] He describes records being borrowed, passed around, copied and learned within a small music culture.
[事实] Tom and McCartney stress that access to music was much harder than in the streaming era.
[推测] Scarcity made musical knowledge a badge of belonging among young musicians.

[15:01] **Learning by Ear**

[事实] McCartney says musicians learned chords, riffs and ideas from one another rather than from notation.
[事实] He says the Beatles and many 1960s groups did not learn to read or write music.
[事实] He compares this direct transfer of ideas to an oral or bardic tradition.
[事实] He describes Beatles sessions where John and Paul brought in a song and the band quickly found parts before recording.

[17:48] **Meeting John Lennon**

[事实] McCartney says he first met John Lennon at the Woolton fête through his school friend Ivan Vaughan.
[事实] He says John initially played banjo-style chords on guitar because his mother had taught him that way.
[事实] Their early exchange involved showing each other practical ways to play songs and chords.

[19:02] **Ambition and a Wider Audience**

[事实] McCartney says the Beatles hoped for a wider market and believed that if other artists had done it, they could too.
[事实] He says they had learned many tricks from the artists they admired.
[事实] In London, they met people who knew the same American music, including the Isley Brothers’ “Twist and Shout.”
[推测] McCartney presents ambition as a mix of confidence, imitation, shared taste and persistence.

[20:34] **Key Musical Influences**

[事实] McCartney says John’s stepfather, Dyckins, had records by artists such as Carl Perkins.
[事实] He says they learned songs by copying words, working out chords and learning riffs.
[事实] Buddy Holly mattered because he sang, played guitar and stood out front.
[事实] McCartney says Holly’s glasses helped make glasses feel acceptable for John Lennon.

[22:16] **The Guitar as Therapist**

[事实] Tom asks about McCartney’s description of taking a guitar away privately, almost like visiting a psychiatrist.
[事实] McCartney says he wrote his first song, “I Lost My Little Girl,” when he was 14.
[事实] He notes that someone later connected the song to his mother’s recent death.
[推测] Songwriting appears here as a way of processing feeling before it was fully conscious.

[24:01] **Lennon-McCartney Maturity**

[事实] McCartney says he and John always thought their songs were good because they were cocky.
[事实] He remembers “From Me to You” feeling sophisticated because it used a chord they had not used before.
[事实] He says one of them often brought an idea and the two finished it together, giving “Norwegian Wood” as an example.
[事实] The conversation marks a move from simple love songs toward more complex, almost novelistic writing.

[26:02] **Literary Influences**

[事实] McCartney says three Beatles were grammar school boys and had been exposed to Lewis Carroll, Dickens, Thomas Hardy and Shakespeare.
[事实] He says that material found its way into songwriting even though they disliked some of it at school.
[事实] He connects Shakespearean rhyming couplets to his later awareness of the ending of “The End.”
[事实] Tom notes that “Golden Slumbers” draws from Elizabethan poetry.

[28:13] **Characters and Compassion**

[事实] Tom asks whether “Eleanor Rigby” begins a line of McCartney songs written about characters in a novelist-like or filmic way.
[事实] McCartney says he likes that filmic approach and links “Eleanor Rigby” to older women he knew on his housing estate.
[事实] He recalls shopping for pensioners and hearing their stories, including memories of the war.
[推测] The discussion presents McCartney’s character songs as rooted in observation of ordinary people.

[30:00] **Hardship and Joy**

[事实] Tom describes “Life Can Be Hard,” written during COVID, as light and joyous rather than dark.
[事实] McCartney says lockdown could create enforced family time for people who loved their families.
[事实] He says he did not want the song to go down a dark route, but toward getting things together again.
[事实] He connects this to a wider theme in his writing: hard things must be carried on through.

[31:19] **Trauma Without Defeat**

[事实] McCartney says his uncles did not sit around saying life was terrible; they told jokes.
[事实] He recalls seeing images of Hitler’s bombers and Bergen-Belsen prisoners, and says Holocaust denial is insane.
[事实] He says his generation inherited a rich moment after the war, when people wanted to make something of their lives.
[推测] The episode links McCartney’s optimism to proximity to catastrophe, not ignorance of it.

[32:53] **Memory and History**

[事实] Tom asks about a hitchhiking trip with George Harrison on an electric milk float.
[事实] McCartney says George sat on the battery, a jeans pocket zip connected the terminals, and George received an electric shock.
[事实] He says Olivia Harrison later retold the story as if the accident had happened to Paul.
[事实] McCartney uses this to reflect that memory can morph and that accurate history is difficult.

[36:04] **Host Reflection**

[事实] After the interview, Tom says interviewing McCartney was one of the greatest things *The Rest Is History* has brought him.
[事实] He describes McCartney as generous and personable.
[事实] Tom says the interview flowed in unexpected ways, especially around “Norwegian Wood” and the unknowability of the past.
[事实] He ends by connecting McCartney’s reflection on memory to historical evidence and the Battle of Hastings.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is that it makes McCartney’s biography feel historical rather than merely celebratory. Liverpool’s war damage, port culture, Irish inheritance, class aspiration, informal music networks and schooling all become part of the explanation for the Beatles.

[推测] Its best moments come when familiar Beatles mythology is grounded in specific lived details: a mother cycling through snow, records passed from hand to hand, chords learned in bedrooms, and a milk-float anecdote turning into a lesson about historical uncertainty.

[推测] The limitation is that the interview is affectionate and compressed, so some topics are touched quickly rather than debated deeply. It will suit listeners interested in the Beatles, Liverpool, postwar Britain, songwriting, memory and the relationship between popular music and history.
