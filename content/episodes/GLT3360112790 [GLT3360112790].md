# England Without a Crown: The Restless Republic

## 概览

This episode examines what happened after the execution of Charles I in 1649, when England abolished kingship but had not yet settled what would replace it. The discussion frames the 1650s not simply as “Cromwell and banning Christmas,” but as a serious constitutional, religious, social, and imperial experiment.

Anna Keay argues that the English Republic was improvised under pressure: the monarchy was gone, the Lords were abolished, and power rested uneasily between Parliament and the army. The decade produced radical experiments, pragmatic compromises, opportunistic careers, harsh colonial policies in Ireland, and repeated attempts to find a workable settlement.

The episode concludes that the Republic failed constitutionally, ending in the Restoration of Charles II, but was not a historical dead end. Ideas and institutions associated with religious toleration, parliamentary sovereignty, a larger state, and an England-Scotland union continued to matter after 1660.

## 分段落总结

[01:01] **The Monarchy Is Abolished**

[事实] The episode opens with the 1649 language declaring kingship unnecessary, burdensome, and dangerous to the liberty and safety of the people.

[事实] Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook frame Charles I’s execution and the abolition of monarchy as one of the most extraordinary moments in English history.

[推测] The opening deliberately contrasts the drama of republican abolition with modern British familiarity with monarchy.

[02:16] **Why the 1650s Matter**

[事实] The hosts say the decade of the Commonwealth has largely dropped out of the British imagination.

[事实] They note that popular memory often reduces the period to Oliver Cromwell and the banning of Christmas.

[推测] The episode treats the English Republic as both a failed experiment and a source of later political ideas.

[04:03] **Anna Keay and The Restless Republic**

[事实] The guest is Anna Keay, author of *The Restless Republic: Britain Without a Crown*.

[事实] Dominic Sandbrook had named the book his history book of the year.

[事实] The hosts connect the book to a broader desire for a social and cultural history of the 1650s, not just a political narrative.

[05:42] **No One Had Decided What Came Next**

[事实] Anna Keay says that when Charles I was executed, there was no clarity about whether this meant punishing one king, removing the Stuart dynasty, or ending monarchy itself.

[事实] The execution had been rushed through after an army coup, while the Civil War had originally been about the form of monarchy and church government.

[事实] The new settlement took weeks to formulate and eventually became a Commonwealth, with the House of Lords abolished.

[07:07] **Cromwell Before Dominance**

[事实] Oliver Cromwell voted for retaining the House of Lords.

[事实] At this point, Cromwell was a senior army figure but not yet the dominant leader of the regime.

[事实] He was not the leading force behind the trial of Charles I and was away when the decision was made to purge Parliament.

[08:04] **Army, Parliament, and Radicalism**

[事实] The new regime depended on parliamentary leaders in alliance with the army.

[事实] The army was more religiously and politically radical than the purged House of Commons.

[事实] Pride’s Purge had removed about half the MPs, though some returned during 1649, making Parliament somewhat more moderate.

[推测] This unresolved tension between military radicalism and parliamentary authority shaped the instability of the whole decade.

[09:15] **The Diggers and Gerard Winstanley**

[事实] Gerard Winstanley led the Diggers, a small radical group that believed land ownership should dissolve and people should cultivate land together.

[事实] About 50 people from Cobham went to St George’s Hill and planted crops such as beans and peas.

[事实] Winstanley’s writings helped make the group more widely known despite its small size.

[事实] The Republican regime, including Thomas Fairfax, saw them as mostly harmless, but local neighbors forced them out.

[13:25] **Royalist Resistance and the Countess of Derby**

[事实] Charlotte, Countess of Derby, held the Isle of Man as one of the last royalist strongholds.

[事实] Her husband, the Earl of Derby, joined Charles II’s 1651 attempt to retake the kingdom and was executed after Charles was defeated at Worcester.

[事实] Cromwell was among those who argued that the Earl of Derby should receive clemency.

[推测] The treatment of royalists shows that the regime often sought control and money more than mass revenge.

[16:42] **Fines, Estates, and Financial Retribution**

[事实] The Republic was financially strained and used defeated royalists’ estates to raise money.

[事实] Royalists were granted back a fifth of the value of their estates and then forced to pay large fines to recover the rest.

[事实] The Derbys eventually regained their estates by the end of the 1650s, though at major financial cost.

[推测] The regime’s administrative competence was one reason it could extract resources without fully destroying the old landowning class.

[18:13] **What Ordinary People Wanted**

[事实] Anna Keay says most people were not clamoring for a republic and many did not know what republicanism meant.

[事实] When Charles II entered England in 1651, almost nobody joined him.

[事实] Keay argues that many people chiefly wanted peace and normality after years of war.

[事实] For most people, local worship, county courts, and justices of the peace mattered more than Westminster constitutional debates.

[22:00] **Marchamont Needham and Political Adaptation**

[事实] Marchamont Needham was a newspaper editor who moved between parliamentarian, royalist, and republican positions.

[事实] He was imprisoned by the Republican regime after writing pro-royalist attacks on Cromwell.

[事实] The regime later hired him to run *Mercurius Politicus* as a pro-republican newspaper.

[推测] Needham’s career illustrates how many people adapted to whichever regime held power rather than acting from fixed ideology.

[27:20] **Anna Trapnel’s Visions**

[事实] Anna Trapnel was the daughter of a shipwright from London’s East End and belonged to the Fifth Monarchists.

[事实] She became known for religious trances and visions that seemed to predict major political events.

[事实] She reportedly foresaw Cromwell expelling MPs from the House of Commons shortly before he did so.

[事实] Later visions portrayed Cromwell as a sinister figure, and his opponents used her as a voice against him.

[30:21] **From Prophet to Cromwell Skeptic**

[事实] Anti-Cromwell MPs took Trapnel to Cornwall to speak about her visions.

[事实] Local authorities saw her as disruptive, and she eventually ended up imprisoned in Bridewell.

[事实] After her imprisonment, she became more explicitly convinced that Cromwell’s regime was corrupt and doomed.

[推测] Her treatment turned a visionary religious figure into a more political symbol of opposition.

[32:15] **Cromwell Compared with Charles I**

[事实] Anna Keay says Cromwell exceeded Charles I in dismissing disagreeable MPs, expanding his powers, controlling worship, and taxing heavily.

[事实] Dominic Sandbrook notes Cromwell was in a difficult position while trying to create efficient government.

[推测] The discussion presents Cromwell as both a sincere reformer and a ruler whose methods resembled the authoritarianism his side had opposed.

[33:13] **Cromwell and Ireland**

[事实] Anna Keay says English and Scottish attitudes toward Ireland were shockingly denigratory.

[事实] She links this to both Protestant anti-Catholicism and the relationship between colonizer and colonized.

[事实] Cromwell was sent to Ireland in 1649 to reconquer it for the Republican regime.

[事实] Keay says atrocities were committed, including killings of surrendered people and civilians.

[推测] The episode treats Ireland as essential to judging the English Republic, not as a side issue.

[36:24] **William Petty, Science, and Dispossession**

[事实] William Petty surveyed Ireland and was connected to the scientific and intellectual circles of the period.

[事实] The discussion links Petty to early modern evidence-based thinking, mapping, economics, infrastructure, and natural resources.

[事实] The Irish land survey enabled redistribution after mass dispossession.

[事实] Keay says English policy in Ireland amounted to ethnic cleansing on a massive scale.

[推测] Petty embodies the disturbing overlap between scientific rationalization and colonial violence.

[41:25] **Cromwell’s Character and Failures**

[事实] Keay gives Cromwell credit for not being primarily driven by self-aggrandizement.

[事实] She also says he was not a good politician because he constantly looked for signs of God’s will.

[事实] The Major Generals regime followed a failed Caribbean expedition against the Spanish, which Cromwell interpreted as a sign of divine disapproval.

[推测] Cromwell’s sincerity may have made him more dangerous politically because compromise could be overridden by providential interpretation.

[44:22] **Succession and Collapse**

[事实] Cromwell died without achieving a stable constitutional settlement.

[事实] He named his son Richard Cromwell as successor shortly before death, and Richard was quickly pushed aside.

[事实] The resulting chaos was shaped by the continuing power of the army.

[推测] The succession exposed how fragile the Protectorate was when personal authority was removed.

[46:20] **George Monk and the Return of Parliament**

[事实] George Monk, the general commanding the army in Scotland, decided to march on London.

[事实] His stated aim was to restore Parliament, not initially to restore Charles II.

[事实] Monk refused to open messages from Charles II and believed the old royal settlement was gone.

[事实] The immediate problem was that the army had expelled Parliament and left an army junta in control.

[48:12] **Anne Monk’s Influence**

[事实] Anne Monk came from a humble background and had worked as a laundry woman at the Tower of London when George Monk was imprisoned there.

[事实] She and George Monk became a close political and personal team.

[事实] Keay says Anne urged Monk first to intervene for Parliament and later to consider restoring the king.

[推测] Anne Monk’s practical judgment helped move events from parliamentary restoration toward monarchical restoration.

[50:10] **Was the Restoration Inevitable?**

[事实] Keay says the return of Charles II was not inevitable.

[事实] She suggests that if Cromwell had named his more capable son Henry Cromwell rather than Richard, the Protectorate might have taken root.

[事实] A hereditary Lord Protectorship would have filled a monarchy-shaped role without restoring the old monarchy on its previous terms.

[推测] The Republic failed through contingency and poor settlement-making, not simply because England was naturally unable to live without a king.

[51:25] **The Republic’s Longer Legacy**

[事实] Keay rejects seeing the 1650s as merely a cul-de-sac because the constitutional regimes failed.

[事实] She identifies religious toleration, an England-Scotland union, a larger state, military and overseas capacity, and parliamentary sovereignty as major developments of the decade.

[事实] Although monarchy returned in 1660, those developments remained part of Britain’s political inheritance.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it makes the English Republic feel like a lived decade rather than a constitutional footnote. It moves between high politics, radical religion, journalism, local society, colonial violence, and family influence.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the character studies: Winstanley, Needham, Trapnel, Petty, Cromwell, and the Monks each show a different way people responded to revolutionary instability.

[推测] The limitation is that the discussion is broad and compressed, so some major constitutional details are necessarily sketched rather than fully explained.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in the English Civil War, Cromwell, republicanism, Restoration politics, and the social consequences of revolution.
