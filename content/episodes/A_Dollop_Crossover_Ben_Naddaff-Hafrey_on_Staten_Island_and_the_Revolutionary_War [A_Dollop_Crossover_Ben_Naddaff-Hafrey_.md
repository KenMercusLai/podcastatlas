# Staten Island, the American Revolution, and Colonel Christopher Billopp

## Overview

This crossover episode between *Revisionist History* and *The Dollop* revisits Staten Island’s unusually strong Loyalist position during the American Revolution. Ben Naddaff-Hafrey joins Dave Anthony and Gareth Reynolds to connect the island’s revolutionary past with its enduring identity as New York City’s “forgotten borough.”

The story centers on Colonel Christopher Billopp, a wealthy Staten Island landowner and militia commander who opposed the revolutionary movement, supported the British Crown, and repeatedly became a target for Patriot kidnappers. His experiences reveal how commerce, geography, personal loyalty, and local self-interest complicated the familiar Patriot-versus-Loyalist narrative.

Staten Islanders initially welcomed British forces, but years of military occupation brought theft, inflation, destroyed farms and churches, and the collapse of civil government. By the end of the war, many residents who had once supported the Crown were refusing militia service, spying for the Americans, or attacking British patrols.

## 分段落总结

[01:29] **The crossover and Staten Island’s outsider identity**

[事实] Ben Naddaff-Hafrey explains that this story grew out of reporting for *The Staten Island Problem*, his *Revisionist History* series about Staten Island’s attempt to secede from New York City.

[事实] A section about Staten Island’s opposition to the American Revolution was cut from that series, prompting *The Dollop* to explore the subject in greater depth.

[事实] The hosts describe Staten Island as New York’s “forgotten borough,” geographically and culturally separated from the rest of the city.

[推测] The episode treats the island’s Revolutionary-era Loyalism as an early expression of its recurring resistance to outside authority and uneasy relationship with New York City.

[07:26] **Christopher Billopp and Bentley Manor**

[事实] Christopher Billopp was born on Staten Island in 1738 into a prominent military family and became known locally as “the Colonel.”

[事实] His family possessed Bentley Manor, a 932-acre estate on the island’s southern tip that included a large stone house and several rental properties.

[事实] By the 1770s, Billopp commanded the island’s militia.

[事实] Staten Island was then largely agricultural, producing wheat, livestock, fruit, oysters, and clams for New York City and the Royal Navy.

[13:00] **An island economically tied to the Crown**

[事实] The episode credits historian Phil Pappas and his book *That Ever Loyal Island* as a major source for Staten Island’s Revolutionary history.

[事实] Royal Navy ships purchased local food and water with silver, making British trade economically valuable to island residents.

[事实] British ships replenished their water at a northeastern Staten Island spring known as the Watering Place.

[推测] Staten Island’s Loyalism was sustained not only by political allegiance but also by a profitable commercial relationship with British forces.

[17:25] **Resistance to the colonial boycott**

[事实] After the Continental Congress organized a boycott of British goods, Richmond County refused to participate.

[事实] Billopp used his position in the New York Assembly to oppose measures supporting the Continental Congress and defeated a local effort to select delegates for Philadelphia.

[事实] Following the fighting at Lexington in 1775, Billopp asked the British commander not to send troops into New York because he hoped the conflict would subside.

[事实] Staten Islanders continued supplying the Royal Navy despite the boycott and later created an enforcement committee that largely declined to enforce it.

[事实] In February 1776, the Continental Congress formally condemned Richmond County for opposing measures intended to preserve American liberties.

[23:26] **Blockades, bribery, and British arrival**

[事实] Continental soldiers occupied the Watering Place to prevent the Royal Navy from obtaining supplies, but islanders continued rowing goods out to British ships.

[事实] George Washington demanded an investigation into reports that his own riflemen were accepting bribes to help islanders reach the ships.

[事实] Washington described Staten Islanders as “inveterate enemies.”

[事实] On July 3, 1776, approximately 9,000 British troops began landing on Staten Island under General William Howe.

[27:03] **Staten Island openly joins the Loyalist cause**

[事实] Two days after the Declaration of Independence, approximately 500 Staten Island men gathered at Richmond Town and entered the king’s service.

[事实] The island’s total population was only 2,847, including children and enslaved people, making the Loyalist mobilization a substantial share of its inhabitants.

[事实] Residents burned Patriot leaders in effigy, while British soldiers wrote home about the enthusiastic welcome they received.

[事实] Billopp’s militia guarded the New Jersey-facing shoreline, built defensive works, transported equipment, and supplied British forces with food, firewood, horses, and wagons.

[31:56] **Nathaniel Fitz Randolph targets Billopp**

[事实] Patriot captain Nathaniel “Fitz” Randolph repeatedly crossed from New Jersey to Staten Island at night to capture Loyalist soldiers and prominent residents.

[事实] Even Loyalist accounts acknowledged that Randolph treated captives decently and discouraged his men from looting homes.

[事实] Billopp placed a £100 bounty on Randolph, who responded with a matching bounty and promised to capture the colonel himself.

[事实] After learning that Billopp would attend a ball, Randolph entered the gathering to identify him and later seized him on his journey home.

[推测] Billopp was probably released in a prisoner exchange, although the transcript says the surviving record does not establish exactly how he returned to Staten Island.

[38:47] **Capture, retaliation, and prisoner exchanges**

[事实] In February 1779, Loyalist troops crossed into New Jersey at night, surrounded Randolph’s residence, and captured him along with twelve American soldiers.

[事实] Randolph was imprisoned in New York and placed in irons, prompting repeated Patriot attempts to recapture Billopp for an exchange.

[事实] On June 23, 1779, Patriots watched Billopp’s house from a New Jersey church steeple, crossed the water after seeing him return, entered his home, and abducted him from bed.

[事实] Billopp initially remained free in New Jersey after promising not to escape or resume military activity.

[事实] New York later convicted him of loyalty to the Crown, banished him, and confiscated his estate.

[49:20] **Billopp’s imprisonment**

[事实] New Jersey placed Billopp in irons and chained him to a jail floor on bread and water in retaliation for the treatment of Randolph and another American prisoner.

[事实] Officials told Billopp that he could end the harsh treatment by arranging better conditions for the American captives.

[事实] George Washington offered to let Billopp return home with his property protected if he resigned command of the Loyalist militia.

[事实] Billopp refused the offer, but the two sides nevertheless arranged an exchange in December 1779.

[53:14] **The frozen invasion of Staten Island**

[事实] The winter of 1779–1780 was so severe that waterways around New York froze strongly enough to carry troops, horses, and artillery.

[事实] On January 14, 1780, Washington sent approximately 2,500 soldiers across the ice to Staten Island using 300 sleds borrowed from New Jersey farmers.

[事实] Billopp asked his militia to defend Richmond, warning that the Americans might hang Loyalists and confiscate their property.

[事实] Not one militia member followed him into the garrison; the men instead went home or gathered in public houses.

[推测] Their refusal demonstrated that practical grievances had overtaken the ideological and economic loyalties that had originally tied them to Britain.

[55:50] **British occupation alienates its supporters**

[事实] British soldiers initially paid for supplies but later stole extensively after returning from operations in New Jersey.

[事实] They dismantled farm fences for firewood, allowing livestock to damage crops, while local prices more than doubled.

[事实] Soldiers gradually consumed or destroyed several Staten Island churches, occupied private homes, damaged furniture, and removed congregational records.

[事实] Complaints to British officers often worsened civilians’ treatment, while courts closed and the courthouse was burned.

[事实] After years of abuse, Staten Island’s Loyalist militia was no longer willing to risk its lives for the Crown.

[61:55] **Patriot looting and the costs of war**

[事实] The alerted British garrison prevented Washington’s army from taking the island, and the soldiers withdrew after standing in a blizzard for roughly a day and a half.

[事实] American civilians followed the army across the ice and looted stores, livestock, carriages, bedding, furniture, and boats.

[事实] Pompey Prowl later recalled that Americans abducted Black residents and sold them into slavery.

[事实] Billopp’s estate was looted, including an enslaved Black man, livestock, vehicles, bedding, and household property valued at £944.

[推测] The episode complicates any morally simple account of the Revolution by showing civilians exploited by both the occupying British and the Patriot side.

[64:04] **The failed Staten Island peace conference**

[事实] Ben recounts that Billopp’s house was probably the site of a September 1776 peace conference following the British victory around New York.

[事实] British representatives offered to restore the previous relationship if the colonies renewed their allegiance to the king.

[事实] Benjamin Franklin and John Adams were among the American representatives identified in the discussion, and the proposal was rejected.

[推测] The house therefore stood at the intersection of both local Loyalist history and a potentially decisive moment in the wider Revolution.

[65:15] **Billopp’s fear and Staten Island’s changing allegiance**

[事实] After his imprisonment, Billopp stopped sleeping regularly at Bentley Manor and stayed at his father-in-law’s home under overnight guard because he feared another abduction.

[事实] Staten Islanders began refusing Loyalist militia service, providing information about British troop strength to the Americans, ambushing patrols, and shooting at British soldiers.

[事实] British forces evacuated Staten Island on December 3, 1783.

[事实] Residents celebrated the departure by cheering, waving hats, firing muskets, and making impolite gestures toward the departing fleet.

[事实] Staten Island had endured British occupation for seven years and five months, longer than any other American community during the Revolution.

[68:52] **Billopp’s losses and life in British North America**

[事实] Billopp left Staten Island and submitted a compensation claim in London for lost property, wartime damage, separation from his family, and the forced sale of his land.

[事实] Although his recognized property claim exceeded £5,000, he received £400 and an annual allowance of £80.

[事实] The allowance was set to end if he moved to Nova Scotia, which he nevertheless did.

[事实] Billopp later served in the first New Brunswick Assembly and on the provincial council under royal appointment.

[事实] He died in Saint John in 1827 at age 90, reportedly worth at least $75,000.

[事实] His gravestone framed his departure from Staten Island as the consequence of uncompromising loyalty and claimed he had abandoned his hereditary estate without compensation.

[73:45] **What Staten Island’s story changes about the Revolution**

[事实] The hosts emphasize that Staten Island’s prolonged Loyalism exposes divisions usually obscured by simplified accounts of American independence.

[事实] Ben argues that the island’s original preference was understandable because it benefited from access to British imperial trade and feared the disruption rebellion would bring.

[事实] The occupation ultimately produced exactly the disorder, property loss, and violence residents had hoped to avoid.

[推测] The episode presents Staten Island’s reversal as a warning that support for a powerful ruler can collapse when that ruler’s forces begin treating their own allies as expendable.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its use of a sharply defined local story to complicate the conventional national narrative of the American Revolution. Billopp’s repeated kidnappings, Staten Island’s commercial motives, and the behavior of both armies make the conflict feel contingent, divided, and materially costly rather than inevitable.

Its comedic digressions make an obscure historical subject accessible, while surviving letters, government records, compensation claims, and Phil Pappas’s research provide a substantial factual foundation. The contrast between official language and chaotic events is especially effective.

[推测] The loose, joke-heavy format occasionally interrupts the chronology, and some details—especially Billopp’s first release and parts of the peace-conference discussion—remain uncertain or are introduced conversationally rather than documented in depth.

[推测] This episode is particularly well suited to listeners interested in Revolutionary history, Loyalist experiences, New York City’s outer boroughs, and historical stories that challenge clean patriotic myths.
