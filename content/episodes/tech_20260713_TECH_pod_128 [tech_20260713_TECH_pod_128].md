# The Climate Project the Trump Administration Supports

## 概览

This episode of Marketplace Tech looks at direct air capture, a carbon removal approach that aims to pull existing carbon dioxide from the atmosphere rather than only reduce future emissions.

The main example is Heirloom Carbon Technologies, which uses limestone in a looping process to capture CO2. The company has a small operating facility in California and plans a much larger project in Louisiana, though the timeline is uncertain.

The episode’s central tension is political and economic: while the Trump administration has cut many climate initiatives, some carbon removal projects still have bipartisan support. Critics remain skeptical because of fossil fuel industry involvement, high energy use, and concerns that carbon removal could justify continued drilling.

[01:45] **How Heirloom’s Direct Air Capture Works**

[事实] Reporter Amy Scott visits Heirloom Carbon Technologies in Brisbane, California, where Christian Toya demonstrates direct air capture using limestone.

[事实] Heirloom heats crushed limestone in a kiln, separating it into carbon dioxide and calcium oxide powder.

[事实] The carbon dioxide is removed and permanently stored in concrete or underground.

[事实] The remaining powder captures more CO2 from the air and turns back into limestone, allowing the process to repeat.

[02:42] **Scale and Cost Challenges**

[事实] Heirloom’s first direct air capture facility in Tracy, California, can remove 1,000 tons of carbon dioxide per year.

[事实] The current cost is in the high hundreds of dollars to capture one ton of CO2.

[事实] Christian Toya says the industry needs to reach about $100 per ton for the economics to work.

[事实] Heirloom plans a larger Louisiana project with capacity to remove up to 200,000 tons initially, but the timeline was uncertain when the reporter visited.

[03:28] **Federal Funding and Political Support**

[事实] The timing and rollout of the protected projects remain unclear.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable as a concise explainer of direct air capture’s promise, limits, and political durability. It gives listeners a concrete technical example through Heirloom’s limestone process rather than discussing carbon removal only in abstract policy terms.

Its strongest point is the tension it identifies: carbon removal can attract bipartisan and corporate support, but that support also fuels skepticism about whether the technology is a genuine climate tool or a way to delay reductions in fossil fuel use.

The episode is brief, so it does not deeply examine technical efficiency, lifecycle emissions, storage verification, or the full economics of scaling direct air capture. [推测] It is best suited for listeners who want a quick overview before exploring the longer How We Survive series.
