# The Suez Crisis: Britain’s Post-War Humiliation

## 概览

The episode traces the final phase of the 1956 Suez Crisis, beginning with Britain’s secret agreement with France and Israel to manufacture a pretext for invading Egypt. Prime Minister Anthony Eden, convinced that Gamal Abdel Nasser threatened British interests, pressed ahead despite legal, diplomatic, and economic warnings.

Although the military operation initially overwhelmed Egyptian forces, it produced fierce international opposition. Egypt appealed to the United Nations and the United States, while President Dwight Eisenhower—angered by the deception of his allies—used American financial power to force Britain and France to accept a ceasefire.

The discussion argues that Suez did not itself end the British Empire or permanently traumatize the British public. Instead, it exposed an imperial decline already underway and taught British leaders that they could no longer conduct major foreign policy against American wishes.

## 分段落总结

[02:55] **Eden Defends the Decision to Invade**

[事实] Anthony Eden wrote to Eisenhower on 4 November 1956, describing Nasser as a potential “Muslim Mussolini” and presenting military intervention as necessary to prevent a wider Middle Eastern conflict.

[事实] The message followed a cabinet decision authorizing the ground invasion of Egypt and preceded the attack scheduled for the next morning.

[事实] The hosts characterize the episode as both a major Anglo-American crisis and one of Britain’s greatest post-war humiliations.

[05:22] **The Secret Franco-Israeli Proposal**

[事实] After Nasser nationalized the Suez Canal, French representatives proposed that Israel attack Egypt, allowing Britain and France to intervene ostensibly to protect the canal.

[事实] Eden did not immediately approve the proposal, but junior minister Anthony Nutting recalled that he could barely conceal his excitement.

[事实] Foreign Secretary Selwyn Lloyd initially warned that the scheme was risky, underhand, potentially illegal, and possibly ineffective.

[08:37] **Selwyn Lloyd Chooses Loyalty**

[事实] Lloyd faced a choice between supporting Eden and resigning, but the hosts conclude that his temperament and loyalty made resignation highly unlikely.

[事实] On 22 October, Lloyd secretly flew to France and travelled to Sèvres for negotiations with French and Israeli leaders.

[事实] The Israeli delegation included David Ben-Gurion, Moshe Dayan, and Shimon Peres, while the French side included Guy Mollet and Christian Pineau.

[推测] Lloyd’s attendance committed him politically even though he remained doubtful about the plan.

[12:58] **The Protocol of Sèvres**

[事实] The conspirators agreed that Israel would attack across Sinai, after which Britain and France would order both sides to withdraw from the canal zone.

[事实] Israel would accept the ultimatum, Egypt was expected to reject it, and that refusal would provide the pretext for Anglo-French air strikes and invasion.

[事实] A written agreement scheduled the Israeli attack for 29 October, the ultimatum for 30 October, and air strikes for 31 October.

[事实] Ben-Gurion, Pineau, and British official Patrick Dean signed the agreement, after which the participants shared an awkward champagne toast.

[14:55] **Cabinet Approval and Macmillan’s Miscalculation**

[事实] Eden formally presented the plan to the British cabinet and received crucial support from Lloyd and Chancellor Harold Macmillan.

[事实] Macmillan assured ministers that Eisenhower would ultimately tolerate the operation because he believed he understood the Americans.

[事实] Treasury officials had already warned that Britain’s weak currency reserves made American support essential.

[事实] Macmillan nevertheless argued that a rapid victory would restore international confidence in sterling.

[推测] The episode presents Macmillan’s confidence as a decisive strategic error because Britain lacked the financial capacity to withstand American opposition.

[20:20] **Israel Launches Operation Kadesh**

[事实] On 29 October, Israeli aircraft attacked Egyptian positions and approximately 400 paratroopers landed at the Mitla Pass.

[事实] Israeli ground forces advanced across Sinai, while Nasser initially suspected that the operation might be a diversion rather than a full invasion.

[事实] By the following morning, Israeli forces were entrenched about 40 miles from the canal.

[事实] Britain and France then issued their planned ultimatum, demanding an end to fighting and Egyptian acceptance of Anglo-French forces in the canal zone.

[22:47] **Egypt Appeals to Washington and the UN**

[事实] Nasser rejected the ultimatum and appealed to the United Nations, arguing that Egypt could not reasonably be ordered to disarm while its sovereign territory was under attack.

[事实] Rather than requesting Soviet intervention, Nasser asked the United States for support and rejected an offer of Soviet volunteers.

[事实] Britain’s currency reserves were already falling as the crisis weakened rather than strengthened confidence in sterling.

[推测] Nasser’s appeal to Washington made it harder to portray Egypt as merely a Soviet-aligned threat.

[25:42] **Military Success and Egyptian Resistance**

[事实] British and French aircraft rapidly established control of the skies and destroyed most of the Egyptian air force, including many Soviet-made aircraft on the ground.

[事实] Israel achieved its main immediate military objectives as Egyptian forces retreated from Sinai.

[事实] Despite military panic, the Egyptian population did not rise against Nasser as Britain and France had hoped.

[事实] Nasser used radio broadcasts and a public appearance at Cairo’s Al-Azhar Mosque to promote national resistance and declare that Egypt would not surrender.

[推测] The invasion’s overwhelming military force strengthened Nasser’s political position by allowing him to embody Egyptian defiance.

[29:29] **A Divided Britain and an Angry Arab World**

[事实] British opinion was initially divided, with roughly 40 percent supporting the operation, 40 percent opposing it, and 20 percent expressing no strong view.

[事实] Most Conservatives supported the war, while churches, students, unions, and many Labour figures opposed it.

[事实] Demonstrations spread across the Arab world, and Saudi Arabia imposed an oil embargo on Britain and France.

[事实] Britain subsequently introduced petrol rationing, limiting motorists through a coupon system from December 1956 to May 1957.

[32:55] **Commonwealth Opposition and the Hungarian Crisis**

[事实] Australia and New Zealand publicly supported Britain but privately objected to having been misled.

[事实] Canadian Prime Minister Louis St. Laurent condemned the operation for undermining the United Nations.

[事实] The invasion coincided with the Soviet suppression of the Hungarian Revolution, weakening Western criticism of Soviet actions in Budapest.

[推测] By attacking Egypt at the same moment, Britain and France compromised the West’s claim to defend international law against Soviet aggression.

[34:07] **Eisenhower Turns Against His Allies**

[事实] Eisenhower was furious that Britain, France, and Israel had acted without informing him and resolved to oppose the operation through sanctions and the United Nations.

[事实] He faced a conflict between supporting key NATO allies and avoiding the alienation of the Arab world during an American presidential election.

[事实] Eisenhower later said that a rapid fait accompli might have been accepted, but the slow Anglo-French timetable allowed opposition to organize.

[事实] Britain and France vetoed a US-backed Security Council ceasefire resolution and were then overwhelmingly isolated in the General Assembly.

[37:48] **Political Conflict at Home**

[事实] Labour leader Hugh Gaitskell accused Eden’s government of collusion and of damaging both the Commonwealth and the American alliance.

[事实] The Commons debate became so disorderly that the sitting had to be suspended.

[事实] Eden’s televised defense emphasized his lifelong commitment to peace while insisting that the intervention was justified.

[事实] The Observer subsequently accused the British and French governments of behaving like gangsters and demanded Eden’s resignation.

[40:55] **The UN Peacekeeping Plan**

[事实] Canada proposed a United Nations peacekeeping force, the United States supported it, and the resolution passed.

[事实] This placed Eden in a difficult position because he had publicly claimed that Britain would welcome UN action while expecting it to arrive too late.

[事实] Several cabinet ministers urged delay or cancellation, but Israel’s refusal to accept a ceasefire gave Eden’s supporters a renewed pretext to continue.

[事实] The cabinet authorized the ground invasion scheduled for 5 November.

[45:24] **Britain and France Invade Egypt**

[事实] British paratroopers landed near Port Said on 5 November, while French paratroopers seized strategic roads, bridges, and infrastructure to the south.

[事实] By nightfall, British and French losses stood at 32 men, while Egyptian losses were estimated at about 2,000.

[事实] On 6 November, Royal Marines entered Port Said in amphibious vehicles and conducted what the hosts describe as the first major helicopter assault.

[事实] British forces secured key parts of the city and advanced south along the canal with limited Egyptian resistance.

[47:02] **The Risk of Superpower Escalation**

[事实] Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev suggested joint Soviet-American intervention and threatened to become involved in the crisis.

[事实] Eisenhower placed the US Sixth Fleet on full alert and ordered preparations for emergency war plans.

[事实] Eisenhower’s comments to his aides indicated that he regarded a direct Soviet move as potentially leading to nuclear conflict.

[推测] The Suez operation had therefore expanded from a colonial intervention into a crisis with possible superpower consequences.

[49:36] **Financial Pressure Forces a Ceasefire**

[事实] Saudi oil restrictions, the disruption of a Syrian pipeline, and pressure on sterling rapidly depleted British reserves.

[事实] Britain needed American support for short-term credit and an International Monetary Fund loan.

[事实] Washington linked financial assistance to an immediate ceasefire, while the United States also considered oil sanctions against Britain and France.

[事实] Macmillan, who had previously championed the operation, now warned that continuing could force sterling’s devaluation and severely damage the economy.

[事实] The cabinet accepted a ceasefire, halting British forces only about 23 miles south of Port Said.

[52:22] **Withdrawal and Humiliation**

[事实] Eden publicly described the operation as a success, but the hosts argue that its abandonment was plainly a political defeat.

[事实] Eisenhower refused to allow British troops to remain as part of the UN force and threatened to withhold the IMF loan and sell American sterling reserves.

[事实] Britain ultimately announced the withdrawal of all its forces, which Labour politician Aneurin Bevan used to ridicule the government in Parliament.

[推测] Britain’s dependence on American financial cooperation revealed the limits of its ability to act as an autonomous great power.

[54:29] **Eden’s Health and Jamaican Retreat**

[事实] The hosts reject claims that Eden was directing events while heavily dependent on amphetamines and painkillers, citing his biographer’s examination of the medical records.

[事实] Eden was nevertheless exhausted, feverish, depressed, and unable to sleep, and his doctors warned that he needed immediate rest.

[事实] He temporarily transferred responsibility to Macmillan and Rab Butler before staying at Ian Fleming’s GoldenEye estate in Jamaica.

[事实] The holiday generated damaging publicity because British troops were still withdrawing from Egypt.

[56:58] **The Final Parliamentary Lie and Resignation**

[事实] After returning to Britain, Eden denied in the Commons that there had been prior plans or foreknowledge of Israel’s attack.

[事实] The hosts describe this denial as an outright lie and note that it became Eden’s final statement in Parliament.

[事实] His doctors later warned that remaining prime minister endangered his life, and he resigned on 9 January.

[事实] Eden subsequently withdrew almost completely from politics and lived until 1977.

[推测] His resignation is presented as primarily medical rather than the result of a direct American conspiracy to remove him.

[60:47] **Assessing Eden’s Responsibility**

[事实] Eden came to be widely regarded as one of Britain’s worst twentieth-century prime ministers because of Suez.

[事实] The hosts argue that his decisions were shaped by imperial decline, poor health, his anti-appeasement identity, and wartime assumptions about the use of force.

[事实] Churchill and Clement Attlee later suggested that, once the operation had begun, Britain should have continued.

[推测] The hosts reject that counterfactual because continuing would likely have devastated Britain’s economy and American alliance for the limited prize of occupying the canal.

[63:46] **The Missing Endgame**

[事实] The discussion questions what Britain and France intended to do after seizing the canal.

[事实] Removing Nasser would probably have required occupying Cairo and subduing Egypt at a time when France was already struggling in Algeria after losing in Vietnam.

[推测] The plan appears to have depended on “imperialist muscle memory”—an assumption that earlier colonial methods could still work despite radically changed political conditions.

[66:27] **The Winners and Losers**

[事实] Harold Macmillan succeeded Eden despite having misread both Eisenhower and Britain’s financial position.

[事实] Nasser emerged as a hero of pan-Arab nationalism, while Eisenhower won reelection and expanded American involvement through the Eisenhower Doctrine.

[事实] Israel destroyed much of Egypt’s military capacity and gained roughly a decade before its next major war with Egypt in 1967.

[事实] France concluded that it needed greater military independence and a closer partnership with West Germany because Britain would prioritize the United States.

[68:47] **Did Suez End the British Empire?**

[事实] The hosts dispute the common claim that Suez ended the British Empire.

[事实] They argue that British imperial power had already been declining for decades, with India independent and decolonization underway in Africa.

[事实] Suez demonstrated Britain’s weakness dramatically but did not create that weakness.

[推测] The crisis is better understood as a conspicuous symptom of imperial retreat than its principal cause.

[70:11] **Was Suez a National Trauma?**

[事实] The episode also challenges the idea that Suez permanently shattered popular trust in the British government.

[事实] The hosts argue that most people soon returned their attention to improving living standards, consumer goods, and popular culture.

[事实] In the 1959 election, Suez was not a decisive issue, and Macmillan’s Conservatives won a majority of about 100 seats.

[推测] The crisis mattered far more to political elites and foreign-policy institutions than to the everyday lives of most British voters.

[72:14] **The Lasting Anglo-American Lesson**

[事实] A Foreign Office review concluded that American action had defeated Britain and that such a confrontation with the United States must never recur.

[事实] Subsequent British governments made closeness to Washington a central principle of foreign policy.

[事实] Macmillan acknowledged that Britain’s pursuit of a special relationship with the United States obstructed deeper integration with Europe.

[推测] Suez institutionalized Britain’s subordinate reliance on American power while complicating its long-term relationship with continental Europe.

[74:14] **De Gaulle’s Verdict and the Hungarian Revolution**

[事实] Charles de Gaulle privately predicted that Britain would compromise, the United States would not intervene militarily, and France would bear the cost.

[事实] The episode closes by emphasizing that the invasion of Egypt coincided with the Soviet assault on the Hungarian Revolution.

[事实] On 4 November, the same day Eden’s cabinet approved the full invasion, Soviet tanks launched their major attack on Budapest.

[推测] The parallel crises expose the strategic and moral cost of Suez: Western divisions over Egypt unfolded while Soviet forces crushed an uprising in Eastern Europe.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest feature is its integration of military events, cabinet politics, diplomacy, and economic pressure. It shows that Britain and France were not defeated on the battlefield; their operation collapsed because political isolation and dependence on American financial support made continuation impossible.

The lively portraits of Eden, Lloyd, Macmillan, Nasser, and Eisenhower make the decision-making vivid without losing sight of structural forces. The discussion is especially valuable in distinguishing immediate military success from strategic failure and in explaining why Nasser emerged politically strengthened despite Egypt’s battlefield losses.

[推测] The humorous digressions and modern political comparisons make the history accessible but occasionally blur the boundary between evidence and analogy. Some counterfactual claims—particularly about the likely economic and diplomatic consequences of continuing the invasion—are persuasive interpretations rather than demonstrable facts.

This episode is well suited to listeners interested in post-war Britain, decolonization, Cold War diplomacy, Middle Eastern history, and the origins of Britain’s modern relationship with the United States.
