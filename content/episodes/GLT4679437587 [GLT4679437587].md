# Peter the Great, Charles XII, and the Battle of Poltava

## 概览

This episode presents the Battle of Poltava in 1709 as one of the decisive turning points in European history: the collapse of Swedish imperial dominance and the confirmation of Russia as a great power.

The narrative follows Charles XII’s exhausted Swedish army as it attacks Peter the Great’s fortified Russian position, loses coordination, and is overwhelmed by a reformed Russian army. The battle is framed not only as a military disaster for Sweden, but as a geopolitical hinge for the Baltic, Poland, Ukraine, and Russia’s future.

The second half traces the aftermath: Charles’s flight into Ottoman territory, Sweden’s refusal to concede defeat, further devastation in Finland and Sweden, Charles’s eventual death in Norway, and the Treaty of Nystad in 1721. The episode ends with Peter taking the title “Emperor of all Russia” and setting up the succession problem around his son Alexei.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Poltava’s Historical Weight**

[事实] The episode opens with Byron’s poem *Mazeppa*, using it to show how the Great Northern War still resonated in European memory a century later.
[事实] The hosts describe Poltava as the death knell of the Swedish Empire and the birth of the Russian Empire.
[推测] The episode frames the battle as a foundation for later Russian power in Eastern and Northern Europe.

[03:19] **How Charles Reached Disaster**

[事实] The Great Northern War had been raging since 1700, with Sweden facing Denmark, the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, and Russia.
[事实] Charles XII invaded Russia in 1707, lost his supply column, diverted into Ukraine, endured a catastrophic winter, and arrived near Poltava badly outnumbered.
[事实] Charles had a severe foot wound but still chose an all-or-nothing attack.

[04:55] **The Battlefield and Swedish Plan**

[事实] The Swedes had about 30,000 men, while Peter’s Russians had about 80,000 and a fortified camp protected by redoubts.
[事实] Charles trusted Swedish discipline and fighting spirit so much that he told Ivan Mazepa’s Cossacks to stay out of the battle.
[事实] Because Charles was on a stretcher, command depended heavily on Rehnskiöld and Lewenhaupt, two capable officers who despised each other.
[推测] The Swedish plan relied on speed, morale, and elite infantry performance compensating for poor supplies, no artillery, and serious command friction.

[10:20] **The Night Before the Attack**

[事实] The Swedish watchword was “With God’s help,” reflecting the army’s Lutheran sense of divine mission.
[事实] Rehnskiöld and Lewenhaupt argued before the battle, and Swedish scouts discovered that the Russians had built additional redoubts overnight.
[事实] Once surprise was lost, the Swedes revised the plan and split their force into several divisions moving around or against the redoubts.

[12:54] **The Advance Breaks Apart**

[事实] At dawn, the Swedes advanced across the Ukrainian grasslands while Russian cannon began firing from the redoubts.
[事实] Major General Roos’s battalions became bogged down attacking the redoubts and were separated from the main army.
[事实] Lewenhaupt drifted far to the right, nearly attacked Peter’s camp with only about 2,500 men, then had to rejoin the main force.
[推测] Swedish coordination failed before the main battle had properly begun.

[17:03] **Roos’s Division Is Destroyed**

[事实] Roos lost half his men, withdrew into a nearby wood, and was then hit by Menshikov’s dragoons.
[事实] Around 2,000 of Roos’s men were killed or captured, effectively wiping out about a third of the Swedish infantry.
[事实] Rehnskiöld waited for troops that never arrived and eventually ordered a retreat.
[事实] At that moment, Peter opened his fortified camp and sent the Russian army out to fight.

[20:00] **The Main Battle**

[事实] Roughly 5,000 Swedish infantry without artillery faced about 24,000 Russian infantry supported by 70 cannons.
[事实] The Swedish line advanced under heavy cannon and musket fire without firing back, then smashed into the Russian line at close range.
[事实] Peter was said to have narrowly escaped Swedish shots that struck his hat, saddle, and a cross around his neck.
[事实] Russian reserves widened the gaps in the Swedish line, surrounded parts of it, and broke Swedish morale.

[24:03] **Swedish Collapse**

[事实] Charles, still on his litter, tried and failed to rally the retreating Swedes.
[事实] In about half an hour, the Swedish infantry was almost destroyed and the cavalry was badly hit by Russian cannon fire.
[事实] The Swedes lost about 10,000 men killed, wounded, or captured; the Russians lost about 1,500 killed and 3,000 wounded.
[事实] Peter toasted the captured Swedish officers as his “teachers in the art of war.”

[26:17] **Charles Escapes South**

[事实] Charles regrouped with the Cossacks, the baggage train, and the remnants of the Swedish army after the battle.
[事实] The only escape route led farther south, away from Sweden, toward the Dnieper and then the Ottoman frontier.
[事实] With too few boats for about 14,000 men, Charles was persuaded to cross the Dnieper rather than risk capture.
[推测] Charles’s escape preserved a symbolic Swedish cause but left most of his army abandoned.

[29:15] **Poltava’s Consequences**

[事实] Peter described Poltava as the final stone in the foundation of St Petersburg.
[事实] The hosts argue it was also the final stone in Russia’s emergence as a great power.
[事实] They discuss a counterfactual in which a Swedish victory might have meant a march on Moscow, a client ruler, Russian civil war, and the loss of the Baltic or Ukraine.
[推测] The battle is treated as a moment when Russia’s westernization and imperial trajectory could still have failed.

[32:51] **Europe Recalibrates Around Russia**

[事实] Peter celebrated Poltava with a Roman-style triumph in St Petersburg.
[事实] Captured Swedish generals were treated courteously, while many common soldiers were sent to mines or dockyards.
[事实] Of around 40,000 men who had marched into Ukraine with Charles, only about 5,000 eventually returned home.
[事实] Denmark, Augustus the Strong, and others moved against Sweden after Poltava, while Poland became increasingly subordinate to Russian power.

[36:54] **Charles in Ottoman Exile**

[事实] Sultan Ahmed III welcomed Charles and his men as refugees and housed them near Bender in modern Moldova.
[事实] Ivan Mazepa died there, described as effectively dying of heartbreak or depression.
[事实] Charles stayed in Ottoman territory for three years, hoping to persuade the Ottomans to enter the war against Russia.
[事实] The Ottomans did eventually go to war after Peter demanded that they expel the Swedes.

[39:24] **Peter’s Ottoman Setback**

[事实] Peter planned to march into Moldavia and Wallachia and presented himself as a liberator of Orthodox peoples in the Balkans.
[事实] The hosts connect this to Russia’s later claim to protect Orthodox Slavs and to the long-term road toward 1914.
[事实] Peter was cornered by a large Ottoman army but escaped after the Grand Vizier accepted limited concessions.
[事实] Peter gave up Azov, scrapped his southern fleet, and abandoned his Black Sea ambitions for the time being.

[42:29] **Sweden Refuses to Quit**

[事实] By this stage Sweden had lost territory, suffered famine and plague, and faced Russia, Saxony, Poland, Denmark, Hanover, and soon Prussia.
[事实] Charles still refused to surrender any Swedish ground.
[事实] The war shifted to northern Germany, while Peter campaigned in the west and visited places such as Carlsbad and Wittenberg.
[推测] Charles’s determination is portrayed as both heroic and strategically ruinous.

[45:30] **The Bender Chaos and Charles’s Return**

[事实] In January 1713, the Sultan and the Khan of Crimea tried to remove Charles from his camp, leading to a violent clash with Ottoman and Tatar forces.
[事实] The incident became known as “Kalabalik,” a term that entered Swedish and Finnish with the meaning of chaos or ruckus.
[事实] Charles eventually left Ottoman territory, travelled in disguise as Captain Peter Frisk, and reached Stralsund in November 1714.
[事实] He rode 1,296 miles at more than 100 miles a day and then chose to join the siege of Stralsund rather than return directly to Stockholm.

[49:47] **A Ravaged Sweden and Finland**

[事实] Charles returned to Swedish soil on Christmas Eve 1715 after 15 years away.
[事实] Sweden was devastated by Russian raids, destroyed farms, conscription flight, and the loss of Finland.
[事实] Finland suffered famine, plague, public floggings, rape, murder, enslavement, and severe demographic collapse according to some Finnish historians.
[事实] Charles imposed new taxes and planned to defeat Denmark, Hanover, Prussia, and then Russia again.

[52:25] **The Death of Charles XII**

[事实] In October 1718, Charles marched into Norway and besieged Frederiksten.
[事实] On the evening of 30 November 1718, he was shot in the temple by a Norwegian musket ball and died instantly.
[事实] Peter reportedly wept at the news and ordered a week of mourning at the Russian court.
[事实] The war still continued for three more years.

[54:40] **Nystad and the End of the Swedish Empire**

[事实] Britain joined Sweden’s side because George I was concerned about the Baltic balance of power.
[事实] The South Sea Bubble helped bring Robert Walpole to power, and Walpole refused to fund the war.
[事实] In September 1721, the Treaty of Nystad dismantled Sweden’s Baltic empire.
[事实] Sweden kept its integrity, but Karelia, Estonia, Latvia, and Ingria went to Russia, while Swedish footholds in Germany were lost.

[56:13] **Peter Becomes Emperor**

[事实] St Petersburg celebrated the peace with wine, beer, fireworks, and masked balls.
[事实] The Senate and Holy Synod offered Peter the titles Peter the Great, Father of the Fatherland, and Emperor of all Russia.
[事实] Peter’s speech linked Russia’s victory to God, Roman imperial imagery, and the need never to become weak in arms.
[推测] The hosts suggest this language still resonates with modern Kremlin ideas of Russian strength.

[57:57] **The Succession Problem**

[事实] The episode ends by turning from Peter’s victory to the question of whether his son Alexei can inherit his militarized, reforming vision.
[事实] The hosts say the final episode will focus on Peter’s relationship with Alexei and what Peter does if his heir proves unsuitable.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strength is its combination of battlefield narration and geopolitical consequence. The account of Poltava is vivid, but it is not treated as spectacle alone: the hosts continually return to what the battle meant for Sweden, Russia, Poland, the Baltic, and later European history.

Its liveliest material comes from the contrasts between Charles and Peter. Charles appears as a fearless but increasingly self-destructive warrior king, while Peter emerges as a ruler whose reformed army, imperial symbolism, and postwar diplomacy reshape Europe.

[推测] The main limitation is that the episode is heavily focused on rulers, commanders, battles, and diplomatic outcomes; social experience appears most clearly when discussing Swedish soldiers and the devastation of Finland. It is best suited to listeners interested in military history, empire, and the long roots of Russian power.
