# Cruel and Unusual: Kenny Smith’s Failed Execution

## Overview

This episode examines Alabama’s first attempt to execute Kenneth “Kenny” Smith, one of the men convicted of murdering Elizabeth Sennett. Although Smith’s jury recommended life imprisonment without parole by an 11–1 vote, the trial judge overrode that decision and sentenced him to death.

The episode explains how lethal injection creates an outward appearance of medical calm while relying on personnel whose identities, training, and experience Alabama does not disclose. Difficulty establishing intravenous access had already produced disturbing results in the executions of Joe Nathan James and Alan Miller.

On November 17, 2022, Smith was strapped to a gurney for several hours while an unidentified execution team repeatedly attempted to access veins in his arms and chest. The attempt ended only when his death warrant was about to expire.

Alabama subsequently paused executions and reviewed its procedures, but the principal change described in the episode was an extension of the time available to carry them out. The episode argues that institutional indifference, professional inadequacy, and political defensiveness transformed a supposedly humane procedure into cruelty.

## 分段落总结

[01:46] **Lethal Injection and the Meaning of “Humane”**

[事实] The episode recalls evidence that lethal injection can burn a prisoner’s lungs while paralysis prevents the prisoner from communicating the suffering.

[事实] Alabama Attorney General Steve Marshall characterized the events of November 17, 2022, as a “travesty,” but rejected the interpretation advanced by death-penalty opponents.

[事实] Host Malcolm Gladwell introduces the episode as the story of Kenny Smith and what was done to him in the name of justice.

[03:42] **Kenny Smith Before Death Row**

[事实] Smith was 22 at the time of Elizabeth Sennett’s murder and had a girlfriend, two young children, and a factory job.

[事实] His mother described him as frequently drunk or high but generally cheerful.

[事实] The day after the attack, Smith had a swollen, bandaged hand and repeatedly told his best friend that he had “messed up,” without explaining what he meant.

[事实] Before his arrest, Smith appeared distant around his mother, as though he wanted to tell her something but never did.

[06:22] **A Jury’s Sentence Overridden**

[事实] Smith was convicted of murder, and the jury recommended life imprisonment without parole by an 11–1 vote.

[事实] The judge overrode the jury and sentenced Smith to death.

[事实] Smith spent decades at Holman Prison appealing his sentence before receiving an execution date of November 17, 2022.

[事实] Marshall later minimized Smith’s experience as complaints about the “prodding and poking” required to place a small IV line.

[07:45] **The Medical Appearance of Lethal Injection**

[事实] Alabama’s 41-page execution protocol specifies matters including prisoner transfers, final meals, witnesses, and the organization of the execution team.

[事实] The execution team consists of roughly a dozen people and is expected to rehearse during the week before an execution.

[事实] Intensive-care specialist Joel Zivot says lethal injection gained acceptance partly because it appears bloodless and peaceful to witnesses.

[事实] Medical terminology, equipment, and personnel give lethal injection the appearance of a legitimate medical procedure.

[推测] The episode presents this medical appearance as a way of shielding witnesses and institutions from the physical reality of an execution.

[10:59] **Why Establishing an IV Can Fail**

[事实] Alabama’s protocol calls for ordinary intravenous access first and a central line into a large vein if that fails.

[事实] Zivot explains that dehydration, illness, terror, prolonged imprisonment, and lack of cooperation can make venous access more difficult.

[事实] Skilled anesthesiologists are unlikely to participate because professional medical ethics prohibit using their expertise to harm or kill.

[事实] Alabama does not disclose the execution team’s identities, qualifications, training, or experience.

[事实] IV preparation occurs before witnesses enter Alabama’s execution chamber, leaving outsiders unable to observe what is happening during delays.

[14:43] **The Autopsy of Joe Nathan James**

[事实] Joe Nathan James’s execution in the summer of 2022 ran significantly late, although Alabama maintained that the procedure went according to plan.

[事实] During a second autopsy, Zivot found evidence of repeated IV attempts and bruising along both arms.

[事实] The autopsy also revealed a surgical “cut down,” in which the skin was opened with a knife to expose a vein.

[事实] Blood along the incision indicated that James was alive when the cut down occurred.

[事实] The protocol in force at the time did not authorize that procedure.

[16:55] **Alan Miller and the Death-Warrant Deadline**

[事实] Alabama death warrants specified the time and place of execution, and an execution then had to be completed before midnight on the designated date.

[事实] Prison officials generally began at 6 p.m., creating a six-hour operational window.

[事实] During the attempted execution of Alan Miller, officials failed to establish access before the deadline and abandoned the attempt.

[事实] The state intended to seek another opportunity to execute Miller.

[20:06] **A Changed Man and an Unlikely Friendship**

[事实] Attorney Robert Grass began representing Smith in 2005 as part of his pro bono death-penalty work.

[事实] Grass repeatedly traveled from New York to Alabama over nearly two decades and developed a friendship with Smith.

[事实] Grass described Smith as religious, gracious, decent, and a constructive influence inside prison.

[事实] Grass said he had represented other death-row prisoners without developing the same bond.

[23:20] **The Final Legal Battle**

[事实] After the James and Miller cases, Smith’s lawyers argued that Alabama’s administration of lethal injection constituted cruel and unusual punishment.

[事实] The lawsuit was dismissed, and the Eleventh Circuit heard Smith’s appeal on November 16, 2022.

[事实] On the evening of November 17, the Eleventh Circuit ruled in Smith’s favor, temporarily stopping the execution.

[事实] Alabama appealed, and shortly after 10 p.m. the U.S. Supreme Court allowed the execution to proceed.

[事实] Smith’s death warrant was still scheduled to expire at midnight.

[26:16] **Inside Smith’s Execution Chamber**

[事实] Smith was removed from his cell, strapped to a gurney, and left without clear information about whether or when the execution would proceed.

[事实] Three unidentified IV-team members repeatedly attempted to find a vein.

[事实] After the arm attempts failed, a person in surgical clothing attempted a central line beneath Smith’s collarbone without first explaining the procedure.

[事实] According to a report commissioned by his legal team, Smith saw a long needle moved inside his chest and feared he was being given a substance that would prevent him from speaking.

[事实] Smith reported searing pain, difficulty breathing, panic, and approximately five attempts to place the large needle beneath his collarbone.

[29:47] **The Attempt Is Abandoned**

[事实] Smith remained strapped to the gurney for approximately three and a half to four hours.

[事实] Afterward, he could not stand, walk, change clothes, or unbutton his shirt without assistance.

[事实] When Smith requested a wheelchair, officials refused, and guards eventually carried him to the infirmary by his arms.

[事实] The execution was abandoned as the midnight deadline approached.

[推测] Gladwell imagines the execution team recognizing that it lacked the competence to complete a task presented as clean, professional, and humane.

[33:38] **Alabama’s Response to Failure**

[事实] Four days after the failed execution, Governor Kay Ivey paused pending executions and ordered a comprehensive review of Alabama’s capital-punishment protocol.

[事实] Attorney General Marshall blamed delays by defense lawyers and resistance from condemned prisoners for making executions more difficult.

[事实] Marshall argued that the existing six-hour window was inadequate.

[事实] The review produced a rule allowing execution personnel until the following morning to complete an execution.

[推测] The episode interprets this change as granting officials more time to continue invasive procedures without resolving the underlying problems of competence, transparency, and suffering.

[36:52] **From Indifference to Cruelty**

[事实] Gladwell states that, as a “moral failure cascade” gains momentum, indifference becomes cruelty.

[事实] After surviving the failed attempt, Smith returned to his cell while his lawyers considered whether Alabama could legally try to execute him again.

[事实] His legal team sought an assessment from trauma specialist Kate Porterfield to understand his condition after the experience.

[事实] The episode ends by previewing Smith’s inability to manage ordinary interactions after the attempt and testimony that he continued to approach others from a place of love.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its reconstruction of the institutional process surrounding Smith’s failed execution. By combining Alabama’s written protocol with medical analysis, legal chronology, autopsy evidence, and Smith’s reported experience, it exposes the distance between lethal injection’s orderly appearance and its physical reality.

Its focus is not simply whether capital punishment is justified. It asks whether a state can describe an execution as humane when the people carrying it out lack transparent qualifications, repeatedly fail at basic procedures, and continue invasive attempts against a terrified and restrained person.

[推测] The narrative is openly critical of Alabama officials and gives limited space to defenses of the state beyond Marshall’s press conference. Listeners seeking a neutral debate over capital punishment may therefore find the framing one-sided, while listeners interested in medical ethics, constitutional law, institutional accountability, and the human consequences of execution procedures will find it especially valuable.

[推测] The episode’s broader conclusion is that extending an execution deadline does not correct the moral or practical failures of the process; it may merely give the institution more time to inflict them.
