# Senators Fetterman and McCormick on Bipartisanship, AI, Datacenters, and Money in DC

## 概览

This episode centers on Senators John Fetterman and Dave McCormick, Pennsylvania’s Democratic and Republican senators, using their working relationship as a case study in bipartisan politics during a highly polarized moment.

The discussion moves from Senate procedure and shutdowns to broader economic anxieties: inflation, debt, wealth concentration, capitalism, AI-driven disruption, and whether government can still solve hard problems without falling into tribal reflexes.

A major throughline is Pennsylvania’s role in the AI and energy race. Both senators argue that datacenters, energy infrastructure, and manufacturing investment could become a blue-collar growth engine, while warning that misinformation and political extremism may slow the United States against China.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Pennsylvania as a Bipartisan Test Case**

[事实] The opening frames Pennsylvania as one of the most important states in the presidential race.
[事实] Fetterman and McCormick both voted for a bill to keep the government open and said shutting down the government is wrong for Pennsylvania and the country.
[事实] Both senators describe their cooperation as “country over party” and say they can disagree while still trusting each other.

[00:40] **Opening Statements on Cooperation**

[事实] Fetterman says he is proud to call McCormick a friend and rejects political extremism.
[事实] McCormick says Pennsylvania voters expect their senators to find ways to work together.
[事实] McCormick argues AI is part of a broader moment of change affecting life sciences, defense, energy, and society.
[推测] The senators present bipartisanship less as ideological moderation than as a practical requirement for a divided swing state.

[02:52] **Flipped Seats, AI, and Datacenter Moratoriums**

[事实] Fetterman says he and McCormick were the only people in their respective cycles to flip Senate seats.
[事实] Fetterman says a datacenter moratorium is a “China first” policy and rejects describing AI infrastructure as a scourge.
[事实] Fetterman praises McCormick’s Western Pennsylvania symposium connecting AI and energy.
[推测] Fetterman is positioning AI infrastructure as an economic and national-security issue, even when that puts him at odds with parts of his party.

[03:58] **Pennsylvania’s Political Coalition**

[事实] McCormick describes Pennsylvania as a microcosm of the country, with Democratic urban centers and Republican rural areas.
[事实] He says some voters supported Fetterman, McCormick, and Trump across different elections.
[事实] He identifies working families, rank-and-file union members, Latino voters, and African-American voters as central to Pennsylvania’s future coalition.
[推测] The discussion suggests that the party able to win this working-family coalition may shape national politics in 2028 and beyond.

[06:37] **Inflation, Iran, and Anti-Trump Reflexes**

[事实] The hosts challenge the senators on low institutional approval, debt, inflation, and frustration among ordinary Americans.
[事实] Fetterman says recent inflation is largely tied to energy prices and supports holding Iran accountable to prevent it from acquiring a nuclear weapon.
[事实] Fetterman says his party is too often defined by opposition to whatever Trump supports.
[事实] He says Democrats should focus less on symbolic distractions and more on higher-stakes issues.

[10:03] **Fetterman Reverses on the Filibuster**

[事实] Fetterman says he and the Democratic Party were wrong to support eliminating the filibuster in 2022.
[事实] He says Senators Manchin and Sinema were vindicated for defending it.
[事实] He now says he would defend the filibuster because it protects minority rights and forces cross-party work.
[推测] This is one of the clearest examples in the episode of Fetterman publicly breaking with an earlier party-line position.

[11:39] **Institutional Trust and Senate Productivity**

[事实] McCormick says trust in institutions is low across Congress, churches, business, and public life.
[事实] He says he votes as a conservative but still looks for bipartisan legislation.
[事实] He argues that the filibuster is frustrating but creates pressure to work together.
[事实] He cites energy progress, possible permitting reform, border closure, and a claimed decline in fentanyl deaths, while saying debt and deficits remain unresolved.

[13:53] **Wealth Concentration and Economic Anxiety**

[事实] McCormick calls wealth concentration a huge and growing problem.
[事实] He says Pennsylvania’s median income is $52,000 and compares AI anxiety to earlier globalization-driven job losses in his hometown.
[事实] He says capitalism must address opportunity gaps or risk losing legitimacy.
[事实] He points to Invest America accounts and a school-choice tax credit as ways to expand opportunity outside direct government control.

[16:05] **Government Spending, Shutdowns, and Capitalism**

[事实] Chamath argues that more government spending and intervention can reduce affordability and economic mobility.
[事实] Fetterman responds that repeated government shutdowns show failure to perform basic responsibilities.
[事实] Fetterman says he is proud to be a capitalist and criticizes socialism and communism in his party.
[事实] He says AI creates real risks, including hacking and weaponization, but should be led by America rather than China.

[19:54] **Graham Platner and Extremist Candidates**

[事实] The hosts ask why Graham Platner is polling well despite allegations including a Nazi tattoo and other controversies discussed in the transcript.
[事实] Fetterman says someone with a Nazi tattoo could reasonably be called a Nazi sympathizer.
[事实] Fetterman criticizes Platner for alleged comments about an American soldier and the U.S. Army.
[事实] McCormick says the left has seen a migration toward socialism, Marxism, antisemitism, and hatred.
[推测] Both senators use Platner as an example of how partisan backlash can make candidates viable despite conduct they see as disqualifying.

[23:47] **Israel, Antisemitism, and Party Identity**

[事实] Fetterman says his party’s stance on Israel has become a profound betrayal.
[事实] He says some successful candidates on the left have anti-Israel or borderline antisemitic views.
[事实] The hosts briefly debate whether Trump’s comments about John McCain helped normalize harsher rhetoric.
[推测] The exchange shows that even within an anti-extremism discussion, the hosts and senators disagree sharply about who bears responsibility for political rhetoric.

[25:03] **Fetterman on Primary Risk**

[事实] Fetterman says a Senate seat is not worth keeping if he has to lie about what he believes.
[事实] He says he could be primaried by Democrats angry over his support for Israel, opposition to shutdowns, or refusal to call Trump a Nazi or fascist.
[事实] He says he votes with Democrats 93% of the time and will not change parties.
[事实] He criticizes Democratic campaigns built around Trump outrage and says Pennsylvania keeps politicians honest.

[28:15] **Constituent Pressure and Datacenter Opposition**

[事实] McCormick says he receives around 100,000 constituent outreaches in an average week.
[事实] He says datacenter calls have increased and that activists appear to mobilize around certain issues.
[事实] He says his Pennsylvania energy innovation summit produced $92 billion in committed investment.
[事实] He describes Homer City as a project converting a coal plant to natural gas, with power allocated to both datacenters and the grid.

[31:45] **Energy as National Security**

[事实] McCormick says opposition to datacenters includes misinformation from China and other outside forces, plus legitimate lack of understanding.
[事实] He says communities need clear commitments on energy, water, tax base, schools, and infrastructure.
[事实] Fetterman says energy security is national security and compares today’s datacenter debate to earlier fights over fracking.
[事实] Fetterman says he refuses for Democrats to become a party of Luddites.

[34:43] **Shale, AI, and Foreign Competition**

[事实] McCormick says Pennsylvania would rank fourth globally in natural gas reserves if it were a country.
[事实] He says Pennsylvania is the second-largest energy producer in the United States.
[事实] McCormick says fracking faced years of misinformation before gaining broad support.
[事实] Fetterman says a Democratic push for datacenter moratoriums would benefit China in the AI race.

[36:53] **AI Infrastructure as a Blue-Collar Boom**

[事实] Sacks says McCormick’s summit included software, hardware, manufacturing, robotics, energy, nuclear, fracking, and trade labor.
[事实] He says the AI buildout has created construction jobs and wage increases for construction workers.
[事实] McCormick says recent Pennsylvania investments include energy projects, datacenters, U.S. Steel, Eli Lilly, and Hanwha’s Philadelphia Shipyard.
[事实] McCormick says welders and electricians are in high demand and can earn more than $100,000 at these sites.

[39:45] **Far-Left and Far-Right Resistance to Datacenters**

[事实] Sacks says broad AI concerns are being conflated with the narrower issue of datacenters.
[事实] He says opposition is not only Democratic, because the far left and parts of the America First right converge against datacenters.
[事实] Jason Calacanis says job displacement is occurring and new jobs need to be created.
[推测] The exchange exposes a tension between optimism about AI infrastructure jobs and concern that AI may still displace other workers.

[41:08] **Money in Politics and Primaries**

[事实] McCormick says getting money out of politics is conceptually desirable but not among his top five fixes for America.
[事实] He says his race cost about $500 million, with $200 million on his side and $300 million on the opposition side.
[事实] Fetterman says his 2022 race cost $330 million and expects future cycles to cost even more.
[事实] Fetterman says primaries elevate extreme views and that hundreds of millions were spent to destroy candidates’ reputations.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it puts two senators from opposing parties in a relatively direct, unscripted conversation about where they actually agree: government funding, energy, AI infrastructure, Israel, and the need to resist performative extremism.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the discussions of Pennsylvania’s political economy and the datacenter buildout, because the senators connect national AI competition to concrete local issues: energy supply, trade jobs, tax base, water concerns, and community consent.

[推测] The limitation is that many empirical claims are asserted rather than interrogated in depth, especially claims about China-driven misinformation, job creation, fentanyl declines, and AI’s long-term labor effects.

[推测] This episode is most useful for listeners interested in U.S. politics, AI infrastructure, energy policy, swing-state coalitions, and the internal tensions inside both major parties.
