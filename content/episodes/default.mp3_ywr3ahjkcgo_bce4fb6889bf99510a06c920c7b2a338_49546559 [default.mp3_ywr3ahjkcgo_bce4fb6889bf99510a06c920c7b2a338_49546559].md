# Grief, Resilience, and the Choices That Help Us Survive Loss

## 概览

This episode challenges the popular five-stage model of grief. Although denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance offer a simple framework, the model can become a prescriptive script that fails to reflect how individual, nonlinear, and untidy grief actually is.

Public health researcher Lucy Hone recounts losing her 12-year-old daughter, Abby, in a car crash. Drawing on resilience psychology and close observation of her own bereavement, she distinguishes involuntary grief reactions from the choices people can make in responding to grief.

Her practical approach includes limiting counterfactual “what if” thoughts, asking whether an action is helping or harming, alternating between confronting loss and taking restorative breaks, and deliberately noticing what remains good. The goal is not to erase grief but to regain some agency while carrying it.

## 分段落总结

[00:04] **How the Five Stages Became a Cultural Script**

[事实] Elisabeth Kübler-Ross developed the five stages—denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance—while studying terminally ill patients.

[事实] A descriptive framework entered popular culture and became prescriptive, encouraging people to expect a fixed sequence of emotions.

[事实] The episode argues that this familiar model overshadowed later findings based on more rigorous grief research.

[03:01] **Studying Resilience After the Christchurch Earthquakes**

[事实] Lucy Hone, a researcher at the University of Canterbury, was preparing to study resilience when the Christchurch earthquakes and thousands of aftershocks struck.

[事实] Residents experienced prolonged hypervigilance because further danger remained real and unpredictable.

[事实] Lucy applied her resilience training to help affected residents before later needing those same ideas herself.

[06:36] **The Sudden Death of Lucy’s Daughter**

[事实] During a family trip in 2014, Lucy’s 12-year-old daughter Abby travelled with Lucy’s friend Sally and Sally’s daughter Ella.

[事实] A driver went through a stop sign and crashed into their vehicle, killing Abby, Sally, and Ella.

[事实] Lucy describes shock, physical distress, sleeplessness, numbness, and the realization that Abby’s death would remain part of the family’s life story.

[13:33] **When Well-Meaning Advice Removes Hope**

[事实] Grief counselors told Lucy that she might be unable to function for five years and that her family faced elevated risks of divorce, estrangement, and mental illness.

[事实] Instead of helping, these warnings made Lucy feel worse and suggested that she had no ability to influence what came next.

[事实] Lucy began questioning whether grief had to be an entirely passive experience.

[16:20] **Exhaustion, Collective Grief, and Emotional Ambushes**

[事实] Lucy reached a point where daily life felt like repeatedly climbing a mountain without reaching the top, and she wondered whether she could continue.

[事实] She describes grief as physically exhausting and says sleep provided both restoration and a temporary pause from relentless thoughts.

[事实] Her family and community grieved differently, making support complicated because the people she would normally rely on were also suffering.

[事实] Unexpected reminders—such as seeing Abby’s favorite snacks in a supermarket—could trigger overwhelming “grief ambushes.”

[21:36] **Rejecting a Passive Model of Bereavement**

[事实] Lucy did not experience the five stages as expected: she was not in denial and did not feel anger toward the driver, whom she believed had made a terrible but unintentional mistake.

[事实] She found the model too passive because it described what she might feel without explaining what she could do to help her family adapt.

[事实] Lucy began treating her own experience as a research question, testing whether tools from resilience psychology could be useful in bereavement.

[27:30] **Grief Reaction Versus Grief Response**

[事实] Lucy distinguishes the largely uncontrollable grief reaction—physical pain, sweating, and intense emotional swings—from the grief response.

[事实] The grief response consists of thoughts, actions, and small daily choices that can either help or harm adaptation.

[事实] Research discussed in the episode indicates that most people regain healthy psychological functioning without medication or clinical intervention, although a minority remain stuck in grief.

[28:30] **Oscillating Between Loss and Restoration**

[事实] The episode presents Margaret Stroebe and Henk Schut’s oscillation model as an alternative to fixed stages.

[事实] The model describes movement between confronting grief and taking breaks from it, as well as between focusing on the loss and rebuilding everyday life.

[事实] Positive experiences and temporary relief are portrayed as healthy parts of grieving rather than evidence of denial or avoidance.

[31:23] **Choosing Where to Place Attention**

[事实] Lucy limited herself to two “what if” questions before deliberately shifting her attention to another demanding activity.

[事实] She adopted a recurring question from cognitive behavioral approaches: “Is this helping me or harming me?”

[事实] Lucy and her husband decided not to attend the driver’s trial because they believed it would not help them; they instead focused on checking in with their sons’ school.

[事实] She used the same question when viewing Abby’s photographs, using social media late at night, or encountering Abby’s friends.

[事实] Lucy emphasizes that these choices are difficult and do not require suppressing grief; they are intended to prevent uncontrollable counterfactual thinking from consuming the bereaved person.

[34:06] **Grief Is Individual, Messy, and Nonlinear**

[事实] Lucy describes grief as being as individual as a fingerprint.

[事实] She says there is little evidence that bereaved people actually progress through the five stages in a uniform sequence.

[事实] The tidy model persists partly because it offers practitioners and families a simple structure for an experience that is inherently disorderly.

[38:04] **Hunting for What Is Still Good**

[事实] Resilience training discussed in the episode encourages people to examine whether their thoughts and actions are working for or against them.

[事实] The phrase “hunt the good stuff” captures the practice of deliberately noticing what remains good despite adversity.

[事实] Lucy preferred the phrase “accept the good” to conventional language such as “count your blessings,” showing that supportive language needs to fit the individual.

[事实] Writing about her experience gave Lucy perspective and comfort and eventually became her book, *Resilient Grieving*.

[40:34] **Suffering as a Universal Part of Life**

[事实] Lucy argues that adversity does not discriminate and that suffering is part of the universal human experience.

[事实] Recognizing this can reduce feelings of being singled out and can also prevent people from treating ordinary mistakes as proof that they are worthless.

[事实] The discussion connects these ideas with Buddhism, Stoicism, and the principle of focusing on what matters and what can be controlled.

[43:59] **Making Room for Positive Emotion**

[事实] Lucy sought awe in New Zealand’s mountain landscapes, pride at her sons’ rugby matches, and hope in stories about other people’s lives.

[事实] Bereaved people may feel guilty or judged for laughing, seeing friends, watching a movie, or otherwise experiencing pleasure.

[事实] The episode rejects the assumption that prolonged misery is the only legitimate expression of grief.

[46:09] **Agency Without Blame or Pressure**

[事实] Lucy rejects the idea that bereaved people should simply pull themselves up by their own efforts.

[事实] Her work is intended for people who want to participate actively in adapting to loss, not to impose another mandatory grieving method.

[事实] She encourages people to test scientifically supported strategies, observe what works for them, and develop an individual path through grief.

[47:54] **Life Grows Around Grief**

[事实] Lucy rejects the idea that grief necessarily shrinks with time.

[事实] She describes grief as remaining present while a person’s life expands around it through new experiences, relationships, and responsibilities.

[事实] Abby remains part of the family’s hearts and story, while life continues to grow around her absence.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its combination of personal testimony and psychological research. Lucy’s experience gives emotional weight to concepts such as oscillation, attentional choice, and the distinction between grief reactions and grief responses.

Its practical value lies in offering flexible questions rather than another rigid formula: Is this helping or harming me? Am I focusing on something I can control? Can I confront the loss and also permit myself a restorative break?

[推测] The episode will be especially useful to bereaved people who feel constrained by conventional expectations, as well as to relatives, counselors, and clinicians who want to support grief without dictating its timetable or emotional sequence.

[推测] Its main limitation is that one person’s experience and a relatively brief account of the research cannot address every form of bereavement or every need for professional care. Still, the episode carefully frames agency as an available source of hope rather than an obligation or a judgment.
