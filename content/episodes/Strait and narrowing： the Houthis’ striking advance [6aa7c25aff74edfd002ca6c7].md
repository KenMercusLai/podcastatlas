# The Intelligence: Houthis Advance, Trump’s Swing Voters, and Hollywood Nepotism

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* covers three main stories: the Houthis’ advance along Yemen’s western coast, signs that Donald Trump’s 2024 voter coalition is fragmenting before the midterms, and the persistence of “nepo babies” in Hollywood.

The first segment argues that the Houthis’ capture of key coastal positions has changed Yemen’s war and added pressure to Saudi Arabia, oil markets, and the wider America-Iran conflict. The discussion stresses that the advance reflects not only Houthi strength but also the weakness and disunity of their opponents.

The second segment uses polling and modelling to show that some voters who helped Trump win in 2024, especially young voters and ethnic minorities, are now drifting away from Republicans. Inflation, tariffs, and the Iran war are presented as major causes of dissatisfaction.

The final segment examines research showing that family connections are increasingly common among young actors and key creative figures in Hollywood. It weighs criticism of inherited advantage against the industry’s argument that access may open doors, but talent is still needed to remain inside.

## 分段落总结

[01:25] **Episode Introduction**

[事实] The host, Jason Palmer, introduces three topics: Houthi advances in Yemen, swing voters moving away from Donald Trump, and nepotism in Hollywood.
[事实] The first substantive story focuses on a renewed Middle Eastern flashpoint involving the Houthis.

[01:58] **Houthis Advance Along Yemen’s Coast**

[事实] The Houthis, described as an Iran-backed Shia militia, captured the port city of Moqa and Perim Island in Bab al-Mandab.
[事实] Bab al-Mandab is described as a choke point into the Red Sea and an alternate oil route since the blocking of the Strait of Hormuz.
[事实] Drones also hit a major oil pipeline in Saudi Arabia.
[推测] The advance matters because it gives the Houthis greater leverage over maritime traffic and regional energy routes.

[02:41] **Strategic Impact For Saudi Arabia And Iran**

[事实] Greg Carlstrom says this is the biggest shift in Yemen’s war for several years.
[事实] He describes the development as an embarrassment for Saudi Arabia, which had been discussing a new offensive against the Houthis.
[事实] He says the advance gives Iran new leverage in its war with America and adds pressure on oil prices.
[推测] Saudi Arabia is likely to face a difficult choice between tolerating Houthi coastal gains and attempting a costly counteroffensive.

[03:09] **How Yemen Reached This Point**

[事实] Yemen’s civil war has been going on since 2014, when the Houthis pushed out from northern strongholds and seized much of the country, including the capital.
[事实] A Saudi-led coalition invaded Yemen in 2015 to push the Houthis back, but the war became a stalemate.
[事实] Since a 2022 ceasefire, front lines had been largely frozen while Saudi-Houthi talks failed to produce a lasting end to the war.
[事实] The situation changed after America and Israel went to war with Iran, which then pressured its Yemeni allies to join the conflict.

[04:48] **Anti-Houthi Forces In Disarray**

[事实] Carlstrom says the Saudi-backed anti-Houthi coalition is diverse and divided, including religious fundamentalists, southern separatists, and tribal groups.
[事实] Divisions widened after Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates fell out, and the UAE withdrew troops from Yemen late last year.
[事实] During the Houthi offensive, some factions defending the coast retreated, and rival commanders accused one another of betrayal.
[推测] The Houthis’ success appears to have depended heavily on the fragmentation of their opponents, not only on Iranian support or Houthi military improvements.

[06:20] **Saudi Arabia Seeks Outside Support**

[事实] Saudi Arabia wants foreign support, but Turkey and Pakistan have not provided help despite the Mecca agreement, a mutual defence pact announced in August.
[事实] Mohammed bin Salman asked Donald Trump to conduct airstrikes against the Houthis, but Trump has so far refused.
[事实] American personnel are helping Saudi Arabia select targets in Yemen, but the United States does not appear interested in launching its own airstrikes.
[推测] Sustained American airstrikes would be difficult because US military resources in the region are already stretched by the conflict with Iran.

[07:58] **Oil Prices Face More Pressure**

[事实] Houthi control over Bab al-Mandab and the attack on Saudi Arabia’s east-west pipeline are both described as sources of pressure on oil prices.
[事实] Saudi exports through the Red Sea fell from about four million barrels a day in June to 200,000 barrels a day by August.
[事实] The damaged pipeline can probably be repaired, but repeated attacks would create continuing risk.
[推测] The market concern is less the immediate damage than the possibility that key Saudi export routes remain vulnerable.

[09:07] **Where The Yemen Escalation Might Lead**

[事实] Carlstrom says the Yemen war remains far from over and warns against overinterpreting one battle.
[事实] He notes that previous Houthi advances have sometimes been reversed after the group became overextended.
[事实] Planned talks between Iran and Gulf states over Strait of Hormuz arrangements were cancelled partly because Saudi Arabia was angry at Iran over Yemen.
[推测] The Houthi advance complicates regional diplomacy but does not clearly decide the wider balance of power between America and Iran.

[13:38] **Trump’s 2024 Coalition Starts To Fragment**

[事实] The second segment turns to America’s midterm elections and how voters will decide control of Congress.
[事实] Owen Winter says The Economist built a statistical model using polling with YouGov and a database of 22 million voter profiles.
[事实] The model suggests that Trump’s 2024 coalition is fragmenting and many voters who backed him are considering leaving Republicans.
[推测] Because Trump’s 2024 victory margin was narrow, relatively small voter shifts could matter significantly in the midterms.

[14:47] **The Fragile 2024 Trump Coalition**

[事实] Trump won in 2024 by about 1.5 percentage points over Kamala Harris.
[事实] His victory was driven partly by gains among young voters and ethnic minority voters, groups Republicans had traditionally struggled with.
[事实] Some Republicans initially interpreted this as a broad political realignment.
[推测] The episode presents that realignment argument as premature because some of those gains are already reversing.

[15:39] **Tariffs, Inflation, And Voter Backlash**

[事实] Winter says Trump’s approval began falling early in his second term, as often happens to presidents.
[事实] He identifies Trump’s embrace of tariffs, including “Liberation Day” reciprocal tariffs in April 2025, as a key cause of voter dissatisfaction.
[事实] The polling says 72% of Americans believe tariffs increased prices, and 64% disapprove of Trump’s handling of tariffs.
[事实] Disapproval is even higher among young voters and ethnic minorities who helped Trump in 2024.

[16:36] **The Iran War Deepens Dissatisfaction**

[事实] Winter says the Iran war angered many Trump voters from 2024, especially because Trump had promised there would be no new wars.
[事实] The war is described as having a knock-on effect on prices, again touching voters’ top concern.
[事实] YouGov polling found that 57% of voters think the Iran war was a mistake, while 25% think it was the right decision.
[事实] Trump’s approval rating fell to around negative 26 after strikes in Iran began, the lowest recorded in The Economist’s YouGov polling since 2009.

[18:10] **Which Voters May Shift**

[事实] The groups of Trump voters most likely to say they will now vote for Democrats are young voters and ethnic minority voters.
[事实] The analysis estimates that around 10% of Trump’s Black voters, 7% of his Hispanic voters, and 5% of his white voters under 30 now say they will vote Democratic.
[事实] Middle-aged white voters are less likely to switch directly to Democrats, but many say they may not vote or are undecided.
[推测] Democratic gains may come both from persuasion and from reduced Republican turnout.

[19:07] **The Senate As A Coin Toss**

[事实] The Economist’s congressional forecast describes the Senate contest as essentially a coin toss.
[事实] Key Senate races are concentrated in about 12 states, many of them Republican-leaning.
[事实] Winter says Democratic chances depend on candidate quality in states such as Alaska, Iowa, and Ohio.
[推测] Democrats need to convince wavering Trump voters that Democratic candidates are preferable to a Republican-controlled Senate, even where the Democratic brand is unpopular.

[20:36] **The Origins And Rise Of Nepotism**

[事实] The final segment begins with Gregorio Leti’s 1667 criticism of “nepotismo,” referring to popes giving powerful church roles to nephews.
[事实] The segment connects that historical complaint to modern Hollywood and the rise of “nepo babies.”
[事实] Rachel Lloyd says recent research shows nepotism is a real trend in Hollywood.

[21:26] **Research On Hollywood Nepo Babies**

[事实] One study of young actors in American films over the past 25 years found that 28% had a parent who already worked in entertainment.
[事实] The share of roles going to nepo babies more than doubled over that period.
[事实] Another study found that 70% of major films over the past 25 years had a nepo baby in a key creative role.
[推测] The trend extends beyond acting into writing and directing, making it an industry-wide pattern rather than a casting-only issue.

[22:01] **Why Hollywood Keeps It In The Family**

[事实] Lloyd says lower barriers to entry in the digital age mean gatekeepers receive auditions from everywhere, making it harder to decide whom to back.
[事实] Gatekeepers may therefore rely more on pre-existing networks.
[事实] There is also a marketing advantage in casting the child of a famous person.
[事实] Michael Gandolfini playing a young version of his father James Gandolfini in a *Sopranos* prequel is given as an example.

[22:39] **Talent, Access, And Criticism**

[事实] Lloyd says being the child of a good actor does not automatically make someone a good actor.
[事实] One producer described nepo babies as a symptom of an industry lacking originality.
[事实] Others in the industry argue that people who grew up on sets understand the business better.
[推测] The debate turns on whether family connections merely provide useful familiarity or unfairly displace outsiders with fewer opportunities.

[23:31] **Why Hollywood Nepotism Feels Different**

[事实] The host notes that few people object if a mechanic’s child becomes a mechanic.
[事实] Lloyd says Hollywood is different because celebrities play an outsized role in popular culture, rewards are huge, and opportunities are scarce.
[事实] In a field where auditions and callbacks are hard to get, family help can feel insulting to outsiders.
[推测] Public frustration is intensified because Hollywood visibility makes inherited advantage especially conspicuous.

[24:02] **Hollywood Dynasties Are Likely To Continue**

[事实] Lloyd says Hollywood dynasties are not new and have existed since the beginning of the industry.
[事实] She says access will always matter in a business like Hollywood.
[事实] For a lucky few, part of their inheritance is a “little black book” of contacts.
[推测] The segment suggests nepo babies will remain common as long as access, reputation, and networks carry high value in entertainment.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strongest value is its range: it moves from hard geopolitics to electoral modelling to cultural economics, while keeping each segment anchored in reported facts and expert explanation. The Yemen section is especially useful for linking battlefield movements to oil routes, Saudi strategy, and the wider America-Iran conflict.

The midterms segment is data-driven and clear about uncertainty. It avoids presenting voter movement as a guaranteed Democratic wave, instead framing the Senate as a close contest shaped by turnout, candidate quality, and voter dissatisfaction.

The Hollywood segment is lighter but still analytical, using research rather than celebrity gossip alone. Its main limitation is that it sketches causes and tensions quickly, without spending much time on possible reforms or comparisons with other creative industries.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners who want concise briefings across global affairs, US politics, and culture, rather than a deep dive into only one subject.
