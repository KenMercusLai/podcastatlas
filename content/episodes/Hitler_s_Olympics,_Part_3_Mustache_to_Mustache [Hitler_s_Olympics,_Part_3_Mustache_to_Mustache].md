# Hitler’s Olympics, Part 3: Mustache to Mustache

## Overview

This episode examines Charles Hitchcock Sherrill, an American sports official and member of the International Olympic Committee who helped ensure U.S. participation in the 1936 Berlin Olympics. Sherrill believed he had defused boycott demands by persuading Nazi Germany to include the partly Jewish fencer Helene Mayer on its Olympic team.

The episode argues that Sherrill’s conduct grew out of his position within a narrow, privileged social elite. He practiced a restrained but entrenched form of prejudice, admired powerful men such as Mussolini and Hitler, and treated Nazi persecution principally as an inconvenience threatening the Games.

Sherrill is contrasted with journalist Dorothy Thompson, whose experience in the suffrage movement and among European outsiders helped her recognize fascism’s danger. Where Thompson saw Hitler as an insecure and vicious demagogue, Sherrill saw an impressive leader worthy of admiration.

His apparent diplomatic victory amounted to symbolic inclusion for one athlete while leaving the Nazi regime’s antisemitic system untouched. After the United States committed to Berlin, Sherrill dismissed further objections and called for “peace,” helping the Olympics proceed under the appearance of normal international cooperation.

## 分段落总结

[00:47] **Sherrill Presents Himself as the Savior of the Games**

[事实] At an October 1935 luncheon of the Advertising Club of New York, Charles Hitchcock Sherrill is introduced as a distinguished athlete, lawyer, diplomat, military officer, and longtime Olympic official.

[事实] Sherrill recounts how he believes he rescued the 1936 Olympics twice from boycott advocates and other critics.

[事实] The episode recalls his earlier Vienna agreement, under which German representatives had promised not to exclude Jewish athletes.

[04:56] **Life at the Top of the Social Pyramid**

[事实] The narrator describes Sherrill as belonging to the tiny elite of wealthy white American men, with his Yale background, appearance, and social connections strengthening his position.

[事实] Sherrill understood that members of this elite were expected to flatter one another and avoid publicly criticizing people within their circle.

[事实] Those outside the circle—including Black servants and other subordinated groups—could remain present in Sherrill’s life without being treated as social equals.

[推测] The “pyramid” metaphor explains how privilege could make injustice nearly invisible to someone who experienced the existing order as personally rewarding.

[06:26] **Patrician Antisemitism**

[事实] Historian Nick Lemann explains that upper-class institutions of Sherrill’s era—including clubs, neighborhoods, and workplaces—often formally or informally excluded Jews.

[事实] Members of this class commonly regarded Jews as vulgar, overly concerned with money, and unsuitable for elite society, even when they did not share Hitler’s exterminatory obsession.

[事实] IOC president Henri de Baillet-Latour expressed dislike for Jews but still maintained that they could not be barred from Olympic competition.

[推测] The episode presents this genteel discrimination as different in intensity from Nazi antisemitism but still useful to a system that marginalized Jews.

[10:28] **Optimism as an Ideology of Privilege**

[事实] Sherrill repeatedly described himself as having lived an exceptionally happy life.

[事实] The narrator argues that his happiness was more than temperament: it supported his conviction that the social order was fundamentally sound.

[事实] From Sherrill’s perspective, radicals, communists, suffragists, and Jews appeared to be complaining about a world that worked well for him.

[推测] His optimism functioned as a defense of the status quo and made criticism from less powerful groups seem unreasonable.

[11:20] **Admiration for Strongmen**

[事实] Sherrill wrote a historical work comparing Otto von Bismarck with Benito Mussolini, whom he deeply admired.

[事实] He described Mussolini’s appearance, manner, and handshake in highly flattering terms.

[事实] After receiving Sherrill’s book, Mussolini awarded him the Knight Grand Cross of the Order of the Crown of Italy.

[事实] Sherrill celebrated the honor at a large anniversary dinner attended by diplomats and other prominent guests.

[推测] Recognition from a powerful ruler mattered more to Sherrill than negative scholarly judgment of his work.

[15:00] **Dorothy Thompson’s Opposing View of Power**

[事实] Foreign correspondent Dorothy Thompson came from a less privileged background and entered public life through the women’s suffrage movement.

[事实] She spent years in Europe studying fascism, married a Hungarian Jewish writer, and moved among artists, radicals, and other outsiders.

[事实] Historian Sarah Churchwell says these experiences gave Thompson an unusually nuanced American understanding of European fascism.

[事实] Thompson saw Hitler as an angry, vicious demagogue rather than deferring to him as Germany’s rising leader.

[推测] Her identification with oppressed groups helped her recognize dangers that Sherrill’s elite worldview concealed from him.

[19:10] **The Olympic Agreement Begins to Collapse**

[事实] By the summer of 1935, Nazi antisemitism was intensifying and international calls to boycott the Berlin Games had returned.

[事实] Sherrill feared that the agreement he had negotiated in Vienna two years earlier would fail.

[事实] Concluding that he had previously dealt only with subordinates, he sought a direct meeting with Hitler.

[事实] Sherrill’s surviving account describes the Nazi leader as “undeniably” great and records their August 24, 1935 meeting at Hitler’s Munich residence.

[20:39] **Sherrill’s Admiring Portrait of Hitler**

[事实] Sherrill brought Hitler books he had written about German stained glass and about Bismarck and Mussolini.

[事实] He was impressed that Hitler sat facing a brightly lit window and praised the strength of his head, expressive eyes, engaging manner, and distinctive mustache.

[事实] Thompson had interpreted the same man’s appearance as formless, insecure, and suggestive of hysteria.

[推测] The sharply different portraits reveal how each observer’s relationship to power shaped what they believed they saw.

[23:09] **A Proposal for Token Inclusion**

[事实] Sherrill proposed that Germany place a single Jewish athlete on its Olympic team to calm controversy in the United States.

[事实] His preferred candidate was Helene Mayer, an elite German fencer living in Los Angeles whose father was Jewish and whose mother was considered “Aryan.”

[事实] Sherrill believed Mayer’s blond hair, blue eyes, and partial Jewish ancestry would make her acceptable to Hitler.

[事实] Hitler initially rejected the argument, declared German sport exclusively Aryan, and claimed no knowledge of the Vienna agreement.

[推测] Sherrill sought a symbolic concession capable of protecting the Olympics, not a reversal of Nazi discrimination.

[24:35] **“Mustache to Mustache”**

[事实] Sherrill invoked Bismarck and asked Hitler what the celebrated German statesman would do in the same situation.

[事实] Hitler remained polite but gave no indication that the appeal had succeeded.

[事实] Sherrill later portrayed the encounter as an act of personal courage, noting that Hitler could not “eat” him because he was a private citizen.

[事实] Afterward, Sherrill warned Baillet-Latour that the Vienna agreement might have failed and urged him to intervene before a destructive crisis.

[推测] Sherrill framed the exchange as a contest between important men, elevating his own diplomatic performance above the people threatened by Nazi policy.

[26:31] **An Enthusiastic Guest at Nuremberg**

[事实] Sherrill accepted an invitation to the September 1935 Nuremberg Rally and traveled on Hitler’s train.

[事实] His scrapbook preserved extensive Nazi propaganda, swastikas, press coverage, photographs, and signatures apparently collected from prominent party members.

[事实] At this rally Hitler announced the Nuremberg Laws, which prohibited marriage between Jews and “Aryans” and stripped German Jews of citizenship.

[事实] While many Americans saw the laws as a reason to reconsider participation in Berlin, Sherrill continued collecting souvenirs and autographs.

[推测] His fascination with ceremony, status, and organized power prevented him from grasping—or prioritizing—the moral significance of what he witnessed.

[29:55] **Mission Accomplished**

[事实] A senior Nazi official informed Sherrill that Helene Mayer would be invited to join the German Olympic team.

[事实] Returning to the United States, Sherrill declared himself more convinced than ever that America should participate in Berlin.

[事实] He also called himself the best friend American Jews had, while warning that Jewish protests against German persecution could provoke further antisemitism.

[事实] The narrator says Sherrill covered the menace of the Nazi regime with a thin symbolic concession and hoped no one would look beneath it.

[推测] Mayer’s inclusion gave Olympic officials political cover while leaving the underlying system of persecution intact.

[31:32] **Success as Sherrill’s Measure of Value**

[事实] The episode applies Dorothy Thompson’s description of “Mr. B” from her essay “Who Goes Nazi?” to Sherrill.

[事实] Thompson described this personality as following the code of his class and adapting easily to whichever movement appeared likely to succeed.

[事实] She argued that such a person would not be attracted to Nazism while it remained marginal but would accept it once it approached power.

[推测] Sherrill’s attraction was less to a specific Nazi doctrine than to hierarchy, force, prestige, and visible success.

[33:04] **Sherrill’s Final Defense**

[事实] Sherrill died of a heart attack in Paris in June 1936, only weeks before the Berlin Olympics opened.

[事实] In his Advertising Club speech, he complained that Jewish correspondents had sent him abusive or threatening messages after his return from Germany.

[事实] He insisted that he was not anti-Jewish but announced that the criticism had caused him to end his supposedly friendly efforts on behalf of Jews.

[事实] He declared that the authorized American Olympic organization had already decided to attend Berlin and urged critics to accept the decision in the name of peace.

[事实] The audience responded with loud approval and praised the Olympics as an international democracy.

[推测] His appeal for peace converted an elite institutional decision into a demand that those endangered by Nazism stop disrupting social harmony.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s central strength is its psychological and social portrait of complicity. Instead of depicting Sherrill as an ideological Nazi, it shows how privilege, casual prejudice, admiration for power, and devotion to institutional success could produce cooperation with a brutal regime.

The contrast with Dorothy Thompson is especially effective. Their radically different readings of Hitler demonstrate that access to the same people and events does not guarantee equal understanding; experience, social position, and sympathy for the powerless affect what an observer is prepared to recognize.

The narration uses irony and humor to expose Sherrill’s vanity, particularly through his scrapbooks, decorations, fascination with mustaches, and self-dramatizing account of confronting Hitler. [推测] That comic tone makes the story engaging, although it may occasionally soften the gravity of the persecution occurring behind Sherrill’s diplomatic performance.

[推测] The episode is particularly valuable for listeners interested in Olympic history, fascism, elite institutions, and the mechanisms of respectable complicity. Its broader lesson is that token concessions and procedural legitimacy can preserve the appearance of fairness while allowing profound injustice to continue.
