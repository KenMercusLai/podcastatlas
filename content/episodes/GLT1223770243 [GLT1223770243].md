# Charles XII's March to Poltava

## 概览

The episode follows Charles XII of Sweden after his decision to push east against Peter the Great in the Great Northern War. What began as a confident march on Moscow becomes a story of distance, scorched-earth warfare, weather, supply failure, and strategic stubbornness.

Peter initially appears anxious and reactive, but he steadily learns how to fight Charles: avoid decisive battle, burn or strip the countryside, break Swedish logistics, and exploit gaps between Swedish forces. The destruction of Count Lewenhaupt's supply column is presented as a major turning point.

The second half shifts to Ukraine, where Ivan Mazepa's defection briefly seems to offer Charles food, shelter, and Cossack support. Peter's rapid retaliation at Baturin destroys that prospect, and by the end Charles is starving, wounded, abandoned by expected allies, and still choosing battle at Poltava.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Frostbite Frames the Campaign**

[事实] The episode opens with Ensign Gustav Piper, whose heels and toes had been lost to frostbite, speaking with Charles XII in April 1709.
[事实] Charles responds to the injury with extreme stoicism, calling it "a trifle" and suggesting men could still walk after losing part of a foot.
[推测] The opening sets up the episode's larger contrast between Charles's heroic self-image and the physical destruction suffered by his army.

[02:03] **The Great Northern War and the Moscow Plan**

[事实] The hosts place the story in the Great Northern War, the struggle between Peter the Great of Russia and Charles XII of Sweden for power in northern and eastern Europe.
[事实] Charles had already moved through Poland and Saxony, deposed Augustus the Strong as king of Poland, and in August 1707 ordered a thousand-mile march on Moscow.
[事实] The stated aim was to depose Peter and redraw the map of Europe.

[03:35] **Russia Uses Scorched Earth**

[事实] As the Swedes entered Poland, Russian Cossack and Kalmyk forces had burned towns, smashed bridges, poisoned wells, and tried to turn the route into a desert.
[事实] Peter refused to fight in flat Poland and ordered a withdrawal east toward what is now Belarus.
[事实] Foreign diplomats in Moscow reported fear of Swedish violence, Russian instability, and unrest caused by Peter's heavy taxation.

[06:02] **Peter's Anxiety and the Swedish Advance**

[事实] Peter is described as ill, anxious, feverish, and emotionally dependent on Catherine, whom he married in November 1707.
[事实] In January 1708 he learned that the Swedes had crossed the Vistula faster than expected and were moving toward Lithuania.
[推测] The episode presents Peter not as naturally unflappable, but as a ruler under extreme pressure who adapts through crisis.

[09:45] **Grodno and Swedish Shock Tactics**

[事实] At Grodno on the Niemen, Charles personally led about 700 Swedish cavalry against a much larger Russian force.
[事实] The Swedes crossed the frozen river, attacked the bridge, and caused Russian troops in the town to panic and retreat.
[事实] Peter himself was in Grodno and narrowly avoided possible capture.

[11:35] **Winter Quarters and Total Destruction**

[事实] After reaching the Lithuanian frontier, Charles put his army into winter quarters and remained confident about marching on Moscow.
[事实] Peter ordered a "belt of total destruction" roughly 100 miles wide along routes from the Swedish camp, with villages burned and food removed.
[事实] In June 1708, Charles resumed the march with about 35,000 men, expecting to meet a second Swedish army from Riga with supplies.

[13:40] **Holowczyn and the Manpower Problem**

[事实] The Swedes crossed the Berezina and moved toward Smolensk through rain, mud, and food shortages.
[事实] At Holowczyn, Charles won another victory with a frontal assault, but the Russians retreated in good order.
[事实] The Russians lost more men than the Swedes, but Russia could replace losses while Sweden's manpower reserves were much weaker.

[17:13] **Lewenhaupt's Supply Column Stalls**

[事实] Count Lewenhaupt left Riga late with about 12,000 men and roughly 2,000 supply wagons.
[事实] Rain, mud, marshes, and bad roads slowed the column badly, forcing men to build makeshift timber roads.
[事实] Charles waited at Mogilev on the Dnieper for nearly two months before deciding to move on without him.

[19:18] **Charles Turns South**

[事实] Near Smolensk, Charles found the countryside burning and his army short of food.
[事实] Instead of returning to the Dnieper rendezvous, he turned south toward Severia and Ukraine, where the fields had not been burned.
[事实] The hosts describe this decision as the single most disastrous decision of Charles's life.

[20:32] **Lesnaya Destroys the Relief Column**

[事实] Lewenhaupt reached the rendezvous point after Charles had left and was ordered to follow south as quickly as possible.
[事实] Peter saw the gap between the Swedish armies and attacked Lewenhaupt near Lesnaya on 28 September 1708.
[事实] After a bloody battle and night chaos, the Swedes destroyed or lost the supply train, including clothing, gunpowder, muskets, medicines, and cannons.
[事实] About 6,000 of Lewenhaupt's 12,000 men were lost before the remainder reached Charles.

[25:23] **Peter Gains Momentum, Then Mazepa Defects**

[事实] Peter celebrated Lesnaya with a triumph in Smolensk and publicized the battle in Russian and Dutch.
[事实] A third Swedish force that had aimed toward St Petersburg also retreated after running out of food.
[事实] On 27 October 1708 Peter learned that Ivan Mazepa, Hetman of the Zaporozhian Host and leader of the Ukrainian Cossacks, had abandoned Russia and joined Charles.

[27:31] **Ukraine and the Cossack Background**

[事实] The hosts summarize early modern Ukraine as formerly part of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, with Polish landowners, Ruthenian peasants, Jewish town populations, and Cossack frontier communities.
[事实] They describe the Cossacks as fugitives, adventurers, outlaws, and semi-nomadic steppe fighters with a distinct identity.
[事实] The Khmelnitsky rebellion of 1648 led to a Cossack military state, the Zaporozhian Host or Hetmanate, which became a vassal of the Russian tsar.

[30:45] **Mazepa's Life and Reputation**

[事实] Mazepa was born in 1645 in Podolia to an Orthodox Ruthenian family, educated in Catholic and Jesuit settings, and learned Polish, Russian, and Latin.
[事实] He worked at the Polish court and later became a diplomat for the Cossacks, traveling to Russia and the Ottoman Empire.
[事实] The famous story of Mazepa being tied naked to a horse after seducing a landowner's wife is described as a later legend that did not happen.

[33:22] **Mazepa Balances Moscow and Cossack Autonomy**

[事实] In 1687 the Russians selected Mazepa as Cossack Hetman.
[事实] The hosts say he is remembered in Russia as a great traitor, while Ukrainian nationalists see him more positively.
[事实] Mazepa had to satisfy Moscow while also showing Cossacks that he was not merely a Russian puppet.
[推测] His defection is presented less as simple villainy than as a high-risk attempt to preserve or expand Cossack autonomy.

[40:00] **Baturin and Peter's Retaliation**

[事实] As Charles moved toward Baturin, Mazepa left the Cossack stronghold with about 2,000 loyal retainers to join the Swedish camp.
[事实] Peter ordered Menshikov to block other Cossacks from joining Mazepa and publicly denounced Mazepa as a new Judas.
[事实] Menshikov's dragoons stormed Baturin, killed 6,000 men, women, and children, destroyed supplies, burned the fortress, and staged a public ritual stripping Mazepa of his title.
[事实] Mazepa's excommunication was read in Ukrainian churches every year until 1869.

[45:20] **The Ukrainian Option Collapses**

[事实] Charles had expected Baturin to provide food, gunpowder, shelter, and support.
[事实] By the time the Swedes arrived, the fortress and its supplies had been destroyed.
[事实] The Russians blocked roads north, leaving Charles deep in Ukraine, away from Moscow and Sweden, with few practical gains from Mazepa's defection.

[46:02] **The Worst Winter in Europe for 500 Years**

[事实] The winter of 1708 to 1709 is described as Europe's worst in 500 years, freezing the Baltic, the Seine, the Thames, Venice's canals, and Atlantic harbors.
[事实] In Ukraine, Swedish troops suffered extreme frostbite, with reports of frozen riders and men losing hands, feet, fingers, faces, ears, and noses.
[事实] The hosts say perhaps 3,000 Swedes froze to death.

[48:27] **Charles Persists Despite Collapse**

[事实] In January, Charles ordered an attack on a nearby Cossack village and lost 400 killed and 800 wounded.
[事实] Field Marshal Reinskjold was wounded by shrapnel and never fully recovered.
[事实] By April, the Swedish army was half-starved, its boots rotted, uniforms ragged, and gunpowder wet.
[事实] Charles still claimed to Stanislaw Leszczynski that his men were in good condition and close to winning.

[51:22] **Poltava Becomes the Lure**

[事实] Charles chose to move toward Poltava, a small fortified town on the Vorskla with a Russian garrison of about 5,000 men and cannons.
[事实] The Swedes besieged Poltava for six weeks, although Swedish officers were unsure why such an insignificant fort mattered.
[推测] The siege is presented as a lure intended to draw Peter into the pitched battle Charles believed he could win.

[53:40] **Peter Chooses Battle**

[事实] Peter assembled about 80,000 men near Poltava, while the Swedes had about 30,000.
[事实] Peter planned to cross the Vorskla north of the town and use superior artillery and numbers against the Swedes.
[事实] The Swedish plan was to let the Russian vanguard cross, then destroy it before the rest of the army could join.

[55:28] **Charles Is Wounded**

[事实] On Charles's 27th birthday, a Russian musket ball entered his left heel, passed through his foot, and exited near his big toe.
[事实] Charles continued inspecting the defenses for three hours before collapsing back in camp.
[事实] His foot was badly shattered and later infected; surgeons considered amputation, and by 21 June they believed he might die within hours.

[59:44] **A Missed Swedish Opportunity**

[事实] While Charles lay near death, Reinskjold and other officers did not carry out the planned attack on the Russians crossing the river.
[事实] The Russian army crossed to the western bank without facing serious Swedish resistance.
[推测] The king's injury removed the Swedes' best chance to strike Peter before the Russian army was fully positioned.

[60:40] **Allies Fail to Arrive**

[事实] On 22 June, Charles's fever broke and he appeared alive before his troops on a stretcher.
[事实] Messengers then reported that the Crimean Tatars would not come because their Ottoman overlords had refused permission.
[事实] Stanislaw Leszczynski also refused to send the Polish army, saying his own rule was too insecure.

[61:12] **Charles Rejects Retreat**

[事实] Count Piper urged Charles to retreat to the Dnieper, cross into Poland, and seek a truce if necessary.
[事实] Charles rejected retreat and any compromise with Peter.
[事实] On 27 June, Charles told his generals that Swedish experience, tactics, surprise, and God's support could still defeat the larger Russian army.
[事实] The episode ends with the Battle of Poltava about to begin.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode's main value is its clear explanation of how logistics, geography, weather, and political alliances can matter as much as battlefield courage. It makes Charles's predicament understandable without reducing it to a simple morality tale about invading Russia.

[推测] Its strongest moments are the vivid personal scenes: Piper's frostbite, Peter's anxiety, Lewenhaupt's ruined column, Mazepa's desperate timing, Baturin's destruction, and Charles cutting into his own wounded foot. These details keep the strategic story grounded in human experience.

[推测] The limitation is that the episode is built as a cliffhanger before Poltava, so it does not yet resolve the military consequences of the choices it describes. It is best suited to listeners interested in early modern war, Russia and Ukraine, Swedish imperial history, and leadership under extreme pressure.
