# The French Revolution: The Trial and Execution of Louis XVI

## 概览

This episode follows the trial, sentencing, execution, and immediate political afterlife of Louis XVI in late 1792 and January 1793. The hosts frame the trial as more than a legal proceeding against one man: it is presented as the Republic’s attempt to destroy the sacred idea of kingship itself.

The discussion moves from the defence plea for mercy, through the Convention’s votes on guilt, referendum, and punishment, to Louis’s final hours with his priest and family. The key conclusion is that Louis’s death served as a founding ritual for the French Republic, even though the official line insisted he was being treated like any other condemned criminal.

The episode ends by stressing the instability created by the execution. Royalists and Republicans immediately began competing over the meaning of Louis’s death, and the hosts suggest that these irreconcilable narratives pointed toward deeper internal conflict in France.

## 分段落总结

[00:15] **A plea for mercy before the Convention**

[事实] The episode opens with the closing words of Louis XVI’s defence, delivered on December 26, 1792, by a Bordeaux lawyer celebrated for his eloquence.

[事实] The defence appeals to French humanity, hospitality, and compassion, arguing that a deposed king had already suffered enough.

[事实] The hosts present the French Revolution as a major rhetorical event, contrasting this plea for mercy with Saint-Just’s earlier insistence that a king could not reign innocently.

[推测] The opening establishes the central moral tension of the episode: whether revolutionary justice leaves room for mercy.

[03:10] **The sacred status of kingship**

[事实] Public opinion in Britain and other European monarchies was largely appalled by the trial, even though England had executed Charles I.

[事实] The hosts say that royalism remained strong in France and that the king’s sacred inviolability had deep roots in French political culture.

[事实] The 1791 constitution had declared the king’s person inviolable and sacred, except in very specific circumstances.

[推测] The trial is presented as an attack not only on Louis’s actions, but on a millennium-old political theology of monarchy.

[05:21] **Louis as a symbol of monarchy itself**

[事实] The hosts connect the trial to French military advances into Switzerland, Nice, and the Austrian Netherlands.

[事实] Revolutionary leaders increasingly saw their cause as the overthrow of tyranny and monarchy beyond France.

[事实] Louis is described as being tried both as an individual accused of treason and as a symbol of worldwide despotism.

[事实] The hosts argue that the Republic lacked a single formal founding proclamation, so Louis’s death could become the ritual act through which it was born.

[09:04] **Indictment and the name “Louis Capet”**

[事实] On December 10, the formal indictment charged Louis with conspiring with enemies of the French nation against the Republic.

[事实] The hosts say the indictment recast his actions since 1789 in the worst possible light, although the most damaging correspondence with foreign monarchs had been burned.

[事实] On December 11, Louis was summoned from the Temple and addressed as “Louis Capet,” a name he rejected because he identified as a Bourbon.

[推测] Calling him “Capet” let the Republic put the whole line of French kings symbolically on trial.

[12:03] **A king stripped of royal ritual**

[事实] Louis entered the Convention dressed as a private citizen, while deputies remained seated and kept their hats on.

[事实] Observers noted the reversal of courtly hierarchy: Louis had only a humble chair and could sit only when permitted.

[事实] The hosts compare the scene to the trial of Charles I, including the deliberate refusal of royal deference.

[事实] Louis calmly denied the charges, though he showed indignation when accused of shedding French blood.

[14:21] **Legal representation and defenders**

[事实] After Louis’s first appearance, the Convention allowed him legal representation.

[事实] His first preferred lawyer refused, citing political danger and also claiming he was physically unable to stand through the proceedings.

[事实] Olympe de Gouges publicly argued that Louis should be spared and offered to defend him, but Louis did not choose her.

[事实] Malesherbes volunteered, recruited two other lawyers, and the defence team had two weeks to prepare.

[18:37] **The defence strategy Louis rejected**

[事实] Malesherbes wanted Louis to challenge the legitimacy of the court by relying on the 1791 constitution’s claim that the king was inviolable.

[事实] Louis rejected that approach and chose instead to answer the charges point by point.

[事实] The hosts argue this was a poor strategy because Louis had in fact fled to Varennes, contacted foreign powers, and plotted against the Revolution.

[推测] Louis’s choice suggests that he still believed deeply in his own innocence and legitimacy.

[23:02] **The Girondin referendum proposal**

[事实] The hosts say the defence probably did not persuade deputies of Louis’s innocence, but may have influenced some on whether he should be executed.

[事实] The Girondins proposed that the Convention’s verdict should be put to a referendum.

[事实] The Montagnards and Parisian sans-culottes treated this as a suspicious attempt to delay or soften punishment.

[推测] The referendum proposal damaged the Girondins because it made them look too close to royalism, even though the hosts say they were not royalists.

[24:23] **Street pressure and political suspicion**

[事实] On December 30, delegates from Paris sections petitioned the Convention for the tyrant to be put to death immediately.

[事实] The petitioners used emotional symbols, including bloody clothing and wounded survivors from August 10.

[事实] In early January, letters emerged showing that three leading Girondins had corresponded with the king before the attack on the Tuileries.

[推测] These revelations fed a broader revolutionary fear that treason was hidden behind patriotic language.

[26:25] **The Convention votes**

[事实] On January 15, the Convention voted publicly on Louis’s guilt, a procedure pushed by Marat.

[事实] No deputy voted against guilt, although there were 26 abstentions.

[事实] The proposed referendum was defeated by 425 votes to 286.

[事实] The sentence debate lasted more than 24 hours, with proposals including death, exile, imprisonment, transportation, and the galleys.

[31:11] **Death by a narrow margin**

[事实] The final sentencing vote concluded on January 17, and the hosts say Louis was sentenced to death by one vote.

[事实] A further Girondin attempt to secure a reprieve was rejected.

[事实] The final verdict was delivered at about three in the morning on January 18.

[事实] Malesherbes broke down in tears when trying to announce the verdict to Louis.

[35:29] **Louis’s final requests**

[事实] Three days after the trial, a Convention delegation formally read the sentence to Louis in the Temple.

[事实] Louis asked for a three-day delay to prepare himself before God, but this was rejected because he was to be executed the next morning, January 21.

[事实] He asked for freedom for his family; this was not granted, though he was given some hope that their fate would be considered.

[事实] He was allowed to see his family one last time and to confess to a non-juring priest.

[38:03] **Confession and farewell**

[事实] The priest Henry Essex Edgeworth, known in France as the abbé de Firmont, arrived at the Temple on the evening before the execution.

[事实] Marie Antoinette, the dauphin, Madame Royale, and Madame Elisabeth were brought to Louis for a final private meeting.

[事实] Cléry, Louis’s valet, later described the family’s grief and said Louis told them they could see him again in the morning, though this was not true.

[推测] Louis’s promise of another meeting seems designed to make the separation bearable for his family.

[41:20] **The executioner and the guillotine**

[事实] Henri Sanson, the executioner, had served as royal executioner and now worked as a state functionary under the Revolution.

[事实] The hosts describe the guillotine as rational, mechanical, modern, and Republican.

[事实] Louis was to be executed like any other criminal, despite the symbolic weight of killing a former king.

[推测] The guillotine’s ordinary procedure was meant to deny Louis any remaining royal exception.

[42:45] **A stage for regicide**

[事实] The execution site was the Place de la Révolution, formerly the Place Louis XV and now the Place de la Concorde.

[事实] The square’s symbolism mattered because it had once honored monarchy and stood near the Tuileries.

[事实] About 80,000 armed men and artillery were present, while crowds were controlled and royalist sympathy was forbidden.

[事实] Women were ordered to remain indoors, and houses were shuttered along the route.

[46:43] **The last morning and journey**

[事实] Louis was awakened at 5 a.m., confessed at 6 a.m., and was dressed by Cléry at 7 a.m.

[事实] He removed his wedding ring for Marie Antoinette and a royal seal for his son.

[事实] He was denied permission for Cléry to cut his hair, because he was to be treated as a common criminal.

[事实] He travelled by coach for security reasons, praying silently as the procession took about two hours to reach the square.

[50:06] **The execution at 10:22**

[事实] Louis initially resisted being handled by the executioners, then submitted after looking to his confessor.

[事实] His wrists were bound, his hair was cut, and he tried to address the crowd from the scaffold.

[事实] Drums drowned out his final speech, in which accounts say he declared his innocence and forgave those responsible for his death.

[事实] Sanson released the blade at 10:22 a.m., then displayed Louis’s head to cries of “Vive la Nation” and “Vive la République.”

[52:13] **The Republic’s sacrificial moment**

[事实] Marie Antoinette heard the gunfire marking the execution and knelt before her son, who royalists now regarded as Louis XVII.

[事实] Marat later described the public mood as one of “serene joy” and compared it to a religious festival.

[事实] Marat wrote that only after the tyrant’s head had fallen did he truly believe in the Republic.

[推测] The hosts present the execution as a sacrifice that gave the Republic symbolic existence.

[54:20] **Burial and competing memories**

[事实] Louis was not buried with the royal dead but in the ordinary burial place for executed criminals in that section of Paris.

[事实] His body and head were placed in a ditch with quicklime and no marker.

[事实] Royalists soon cast Louis as a martyr, while Republican accounts falsely claimed he panicked, struggled, ate greedily, or was botched by the guillotine.

[推测] The struggle over Louis’s death shows that the execution did not settle France’s political conflict.

[58:44] **A warning of civil war**

[事实] The hosts conclude that royalists and Republicans could not agree on what Louis’s execution meant.

[事实] They suggest that internal French conflict may prove as serious as foreign threats.

[事实] The episode closes the third French Revolution series and points toward future episodes on Marie Antoinette, Robespierre, Danton, and Marat.

[推测] The execution marks an end point for one phase of the Revolution and the opening of a more violent one.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest value is its interpretation of Louis XVI’s execution as a political and religious ritual, not merely as the legal punishment of a failed king. The hosts make clear why the trial mattered to France, Europe, royalists, Republicans, and the Revolution’s own self-image.

Its most vivid sections are the courtroom inversion of royal ceremony, the defence team’s failed strategy, Louis’s final meeting with his family, and the heavily staged execution at the Place de la Révolution. These details give the episode narrative force while also supporting the larger argument about legitimacy and symbolic power.

[推测] The main limitation is that the episode is built as a dramatic narrative rather than a neutral legal analysis. The hosts make interpretive judgments about Louis’s guilt, strategy, and character, and listeners looking for a strictly documentary reconstruction may want additional sources.

[推测] This episode is especially suited to listeners interested in the French Revolution, political violence, the fall of monarchy, and the making of republican symbolism. It is less suited to someone who only wants a brief timeline, because much of its value lies in the atmosphere, rhetoric, and political meaning of the event.
