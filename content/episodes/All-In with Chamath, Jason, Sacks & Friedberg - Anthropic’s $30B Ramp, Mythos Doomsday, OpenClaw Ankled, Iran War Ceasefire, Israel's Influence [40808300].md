# All-In: Anthropic’s $30B Ramp, Mythos Doomsday, OpenClaw Ankled, Iran War Ceasefire, Israel’s Influence

## 概览

This episode centers on whether frontier AI companies can manage dangerous capabilities without government-led regulation. The panel debates Anthropic’s decision to withhold broad access to Mythos after the model reportedly found serious vulnerabilities across major software systems.

The AI discussion then shifts to market structure: OpenClaw access, coding agents, Anthropic’s possible dominance in coding tokens, open-source competitors, antitrust risk, and whether coding becomes the gateway to the broader agent market.

The back half covers Anthropic’s reported $30B revenue run-rate, AI profitability, OpenAI’s competitive position, and where value may accrue across chips, hyperscalers, models, and applications. The episode closes with Iran ceasefire politics, Israel’s influence in U.S. foreign policy, and X’s auto-translation feature.

## 分段落总结

[00:45] **Opening and Guest Setup**

[事实] David Friedberg is absent, and Brian Gerstner joins as the “fifth bestie” alongside Jason, Sacks, and Chamath.
[事实] The hosts open with banter about moderation, show dynamics, and an internet meme about not ruminating and just building.
[推测] The opening sets the tone for an episode that mixes serious AI and geopolitical debates with informal in-group humor.

[04:24] **Anthropic Withholds Mythos**

[事实] Jason says Anthropic is withholding its newest model, Mythos, because the company claims it is too dangerous for broad public access.
[事实] The transcript says Mythos found vulnerabilities in major operating systems and browsers, including old bugs in OpenBSD, FFmpeg, and the Linux kernel.
[事实] A Dario clip says strong coding ability also produces cyber capability, including chaining multiple vulnerabilities into more sophisticated exploits.
[推测] The discussion frames frontier coding models as dual-use tools that can improve defense while also enabling offensive cyberattacks.

[06:00] **Voluntary AI Safety and Project Glasswing**

[事实] Brad argues Anthropic deserves credit for not immediately releasing Mythos and for organizing Project Glasswing with major companies including Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and JP Morgan.
[事实] Brad says the goal is to spend 100 days using advanced AI to find, fix, and harden software vulnerabilities before hackers exploit them.
[事实] He argues this shows industry and market forces can coordinate with government without relying on top-down regulation.
[推测] Brad treats Anthropic’s move as a model for voluntary governance at the frontier of AI capability.

[09:51] **Sacks on Fear Marketing and Real Cyber Risk**

[事实] Sacks says Anthropic has a pattern of pairing model releases with scary studies, including a previous blackmail study he calls reverse-engineered.
[事实] He says the cyber case is more legitimate because better coding models logically become better at finding bugs and vulnerabilities.
[事实] Sacks argues companies may have a window of a few months to use advanced models to find dormant vulnerabilities and patch them.
[推测] His position is that Anthropic may be dramatizing the risk, but the industry still has to treat the risk as real.

[14:59] **Chamath Questions the Theater**

[事实] Chamath calls much of the Mythos rollout “theater” and compares it to OpenAI’s cautious GPT-2 release in 2019.
[事实] He argues that sophisticated hackers may already be able to do similar work with existing models such as Opus.
[事实] Chamath says fully patching the internet would take years, not 100 days.
[推测] The disagreement is less about whether cyber vulnerabilities exist and more about whether Anthropic’s 100-day plan can meaningfully change systemic risk.

[19:03] **Hardening the Internet Before Release**

[事实] Brad says the Frontier Model Forum is already coordinating on adversarial distillation and related safety work.
[事实] Sacks says he expects many patches over the next few months and gives Anthropic the benefit of the doubt on this specific cyber issue.
[事实] Jason says the amount of code being pushed with AI tools is rising sharply and security should be embedded directly into coding tools.
[事实] Chamath says if the risk is truly severe, the effort should include energy companies, nuclear operators, and aircraft companies.

[24:02] **OpenClaw Access and Anthropic Pricing**

[事实] Sacks asks about OpenClaw founder Peter Steinberger saying Anthropic cut off OpenClaw’s access.
[事实] Jason and Brad explain that OpenClaw users were using $200 subscriptions while consuming far more tokens than average subscribers.
[事实] Anthropic’s response, as described in the discussion, was to require OpenClaw usage to move to API-based pricing.
[推测] The issue combines legitimate cost control with concern about whether a model provider can disadvantage third-party tools.

[27:19] **Bundling and Antitrust Concerns**

[事实] Sacks asks whether Anthropic’s own first-party agent product will remain bundled under flat-rate pricing while third-party tools must pay metered API prices.
[事实] Brad says a bundling or anti-competitive argument could exist if that pricing difference happens, but enterprises may still prefer Anthropic’s product for security integration.
[事实] The panel debates whether Anthropic has dominant market share in AI coding.
[推测] The antitrust risk depends on market definition, pricing details, and whether coding agents become a durable control point.

[29:05] **Agent Competition and Open Source**

[事实] Jason lists competitors to OpenClaw, including Perplexity Computer, Anthropic managed agents, Hermes Agent, Alibaba’s Qwen-based work, xAI’s Grok Computer, and assistant updates from Amazon and Apple.
[事实] Jason argues frontier AI companies want to kill or contain OpenClaw because open source could become highly disruptive.
[事实] Brad responds that agents are an obvious product direction because consumers and enterprises want AI systems that can do tasks, not just answer questions.
[推测] The agent layer is presented as strategically important because it could hold a user’s business context, personal data, and workflow.

[32:05] **Coding Market Share and Enterprise Tech Debt**

[事实] Sacks says that if Anthropic provides more than half of coding tokens, it has dominant share in that market.
[事实] Chamath estimates Anthropic may have 50-60% share in AI coding tokens but says AI-enabled coding is still only about 5% of the broader coding market.
[事实] Chamath argues current models still struggle with long-horizon enterprise-grade software and decades of legacy tech debt.
[推测] Early leadership in AI coding tokens may not translate directly into durable enterprise control.

[36:29] **Ridges, BitTensor, and Distributed AI**

[事实] Jason discusses Ridges AI on BitTensor subnet 62 and says it reached 80% of Claude 4 capability in 45 days using Tau-based rewards.
[事实] Chamath says open-source training and orchestration could be disruptive if capital markets cannot keep funding huge model-training rounds.
[事实] Chamath also says real companies will not outsource production-code reengineering to an open-source project.
[事实] Jason compares open-source AI tooling to enterprise adoption of Linux, Kubernetes, Apache, Postgres, and Terraform.

[39:55] **Coding as the Gateway to Agents**

[事实] Sacks says AI-generated code may rise from about 5% to 95% over the next few years.
[事实] He argues an early leader in coding could build a flywheel from developer usage, code access, training tokens, and data-scale effects.
[事实] Sacks says agents will depend heavily on code-writing ability and urges AI companies to avoid discriminatory or anti-competitive behavior.
[推测] He frames coding as a gateway market that could shape who wins the broader agent platform.

[42:21] **Anthropic’s $30B Revenue Run-Rate**

[事实] Jason says Anthropic’s revenue run-rate topped $30B after starting paid API access in early 2023.
[事实] He describes the ramp as $1B at the end of 2024, $4B in mid-2025, $9B at the end of 2025, and $30B in April.
[事实] Jason says Dario announced more than 1,000 enterprise customers paying over $1M annually.
[事实] Brad says Anthropic hit its planned year-end run-rate by the end of March.
[推测] The panel treats the ramp as evidence that enterprise AI demand has crossed from experimentation into urgent adoption.

[44:20] **Intelligence TAM and Compute Constraints**

[事实] Brad says model and product capability crossed a threshold where customers see AI as labor augmentation and labor replacement, not just IT budget.
[事实] He argues the TAM for intelligence is much larger than traditional software markets.
[事实] Brad says Anthropic is compute constrained with roughly 1.5 to 2 gigawatts of compute and expects major compute additions.
[事实] He says some large companies already consume 65-70% of tokens through open-source models.
[推测] The episode presents frontier AI and open source as expanding together rather than as mutually exclusive categories.

[49:18] **Profitability, Gross Margins, and CapEx**

[事实] Chamath says the market discussion is still around gross versus net revenue rather than mature free cash flow or EBITDA.
[事实] Brad says compute is the main cost input and that gross margins have moved from meaningfully negative to very positive.
[事实] Brad says Anthropic has only about 2,500 people and may become accidentally profitable because it cannot stand up compute fast enough.
[事实] Chamath argues incumbents such as Meta and Google may want AI to become a capital and compute problem because they have fortress balance sheets.
[推测] Profitability remains hard to evaluate from the transcript because audited financials and exact gross-margin figures are not provided.

[55:29] **AI Bubble Debate**

[事实] Sacks says AI bubble skeptics argued that massive data-center capex lacked revenue justification.
[事实] He says Anthropic’s coding revenue ramp is early proof that AI revenue can justify at least part of that investment.
[事实] Sacks says the top four hyperscalers are expected to spend about $350B in capex this year, and Jensen has discussed $1T by 2030.
[推测] Sacks uses the Anthropic example to argue that Silicon Valley’s willingness to fund massive buildouts can pay off when the technology direction is right.

[推测] The limitation is that many numbers are discussed as insider perspective, market chatter, or host assertions rather than independently verified evidence inside the transcript. Listeners should treat claims about revenue, gross margins, model capability, and IPO odds as discussion points rather than settled facts.

[推测] This episode is most useful for listeners interested in AI strategy, venture markets, model-company competition, and U.S. foreign-policy debates. It is less useful for anyone looking for a neutral or tightly sourced policy briefing, because the format is opinion-driven and conversational.
