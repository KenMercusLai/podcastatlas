# Alice Weidel on the AfD’s Vision for Germany

## 概览

The Economist’s Zanny Minton Beddoes interviews Alice Weidel, co-leader of Alternative for Germany (AfD), about what her party would do if it gained national power. The conversation tests the substance and internal consistency of AfD policies on the economy, the European Union, public finances, defence, Ukraine, migration and social issues.

Weidel portrays Germany as facing a systemic crisis caused by high energy costs, nuclear shutdowns, bureaucracy, taxation, debt and migration. She advocates restoring nuclear power, reducing state spending and taxes, reforming the EU into a looser association of sovereign states, strengthening Germany’s own defence and ending German support for Ukraine.

The sharpest exchanges concern whether AfD’s fiscal promises add up, whether withdrawing support from Ukraine would reward Russian aggression, and who would be affected by “remigration.” The interview also examines tensions between Weidel’s self-described libertarianism and AfD positions on education, family, gender and national identity.

The final section confronts accusations of far-right or neo-Nazi tendencies within AfD and the political “firewall” that prevents other German parties from cooperating with it. Weidel rejects the accusations, argues that AfD represents common-sense policies and says its core positions are not open to compromise.

## 分段落总结

[00:09] **Introducing Alice Weidel and the AfD**

[事实] The programme introduces Weidel as AfD’s co-leader, a former banker and self-described libertarian conservative who lives part-time in Switzerland with her Sri Lankan wife.

[事实] The host characterises AfD as increasingly hardline, strongly pro-Russia, opposed to support for Ukraine and supportive of “remigration.”

[事实] The stated purpose of the interview is to clarify what Weidel believes and what AfD would attempt to do in national government.

[02:20] **Germany’s economic and energy crisis**

[事实] Weidel says Germany’s economy requires comprehensive reconstruction rather than minor repairs.

[事实] She attributes the crisis principally to the Energiewende, the closure of nuclear plants, dependence on intermittent wind and solar power, high-priced energy imports and excessive bureaucracy.

[事实] She argues that high energy costs are making German companies uncompetitive, contributing to bankruptcies and encouraging firms to relocate abroad.

[事实] The host agrees that energy costs and nuclear closures are legitimate concerns and notes that AfD proposes reducing regulation.

[06:14] **The euro and reform of the European Union**

[事实] Challenged over AfD’s manifesto position on leaving the euro, Weidel denies that the party simply wants Germany to leave either the euro or the EU.

[事实] She says AfD first wants to reform the EU into a community of sovereign states centred on free trade, with more decisions made at national or local level.

[事实] Weidel criticises the European Commission as overly bureaucratic and insufficiently accountable, and argues that the euro’s institutions should obey stricter monetary rules.

[事实] When repeatedly asked whether Germany should ultimately leave the euro, she does not give a direct yes-or-no answer.

[推测] Her answers leave withdrawal available as leverage or a possible fallback if the reforms sought by AfD cannot be achieved.

[11:51] **European debt risks and Germany’s fiscal programme**

[事实] Weidel contrasts her preference for monetary and fiscal restraint with Marine Le Pen’s support for greater European Central Bank financing.

[事实] She predicts a European bond-market crisis involving highly indebted countries and says Germany’s interest bill could rise sharply under the government’s borrowing plans.

[事实] To finance tax reductions and pension reform, she proposes cutting welfare payments to foreigners, development aid and subsidies associated with climate and energy policy.

[事实] She describes a three-pillar pension model based on the public system, company pensions and tax-favoured private savings.

[事实] The host argues that AfD has not convincingly demonstrated how tax cuts and its pension promises would produce lower debt and deficits.

[19:19] **Defence spending and German support for Ukraine**

[事实] Weidel accepts that Germany should spend more on defence after failing to meet previous NATO commitments, but says the money should strengthen Germany’s own capabilities.

[事实] She opposes Germany’s financial and military support for Ukraine and calls for immediate peace talks.

[事实] Asked whether Russia threatens NATO countries beyond Ukraine, she redirects the issue toward whether German foreign policy serves Germany’s national interests.

[事实] She supports renewed access to Russian energy and argues that sanctions and aid to Ukraine impose excessive economic and security costs on Germany.

[推测] Her concept of defence prioritises territorial capacity and national economic interest over continued military assistance to partners outside NATO.

[23:54] **Russian aggression and the terms of peace**

[事实] The host cites Russia’s invasion and continued bombardment of Ukraine as reasons for Western support, while Weidel acknowledges that Russia attacked a sovereign country.

[事实] Weidel nevertheless describes the war as already lost for Ukraine and says Ukraine cannot defeat Russia.

[事实] She argues that any settlement must address Russian as well as Ukrainian security concerns, including Russia’s objections to NATO expansion.

[事实] The host argues that ending Western support could make Ukraine’s survival impossible and notes that Putin has shown no willingness to recognise Ukraine fully or accept peace on Ukrainian terms.

[事实] Weidel calls Ukrainian attacks inside Russia dangerous provocations and says pressure should be placed on President Zelensky to end the war.

[推测] The exchange exposes a fundamental disagreement: Weidel treats continued resistance as escalation, whereas the host treats it as necessary defence against aggression.

[34:26] **“Remigration” and the return of refugees**

[事实] Weidel initially defines remigration as enforcing existing law against illegal entrants, rejected asylum applicants and foreign criminals.

[事实] When pressed further, she says Syrians should return because the basis for their protection has ended, Afghans should also be returned, and Ukrainians should leave after the war.

[事实] She estimates that these groups together amount to roughly four to five million people.

[事实] She connects migration since 2015 with crime, welfare costs and integration problems, while also saying skilled, law-abiding foreigners who work and contribute are welcome.

[推测] Her expanded description of remigration is substantially broader than the initial figure of approximately 260,000 people legally obliged to leave.

[40:48] **Skilled migration, integration and demographic needs**

[事实] The host challenges the practicality of returning Syrians when Germany faces labour shortages and relies on foreign-born professionals, including doctors.

[事实] Weidel distinguishes asylum seekers from qualified migrants holding work visas and acknowledges that Germany needs foreign professionals because of its demographic disadvantages.

[事实] She argues that high taxes, bureaucracy and public-safety concerns deter skilled migrants and contribute to the emigration of qualified Germans.

[事实] Asked about AfD language opposing “culturally alien” skilled workers, she says integration should require language proficiency, professional cooperation and equal treatment of women.

[事实] She rejects racial quotas and says merit, conduct and integration—not skin colour—should determine acceptance.

[48:09] **Libertarianism, national identity and family policy**

[事实] The host cites an AfD state manifesto advocating separate schooling for some children, stronger national-history education, more emphasis on Bismarck and less on Hitler, and penalties for offending the German state.

[事实] Weidel endorses greater attention to German history and says AfD seeks secure borders, affordable family life, safe streets and orderly schools.

[事实] She rejects the description of these goals as far-right and presents them as “common sense.”

[事实] Although AfD’s manifesto identifies a mother-father household as the best model for children, Weidel says the party tolerates her same-sex family and would protect families like hers.

[事实] She supports personal freedom in lifestyles but opposes queer and gender policies, particularly rules permitting people to change their registered gender.

[推测] The discussion highlights unresolved tension between Weidel’s personal libertarianism and her party’s willingness to use state policy to promote traditional social norms.

[54:16] **Extremism accusations and Germany’s historical memory**

[事实] The host raises reports of neo-Nazi-linked figures around AfD and notes Björn Höcke’s convictions for using a prohibited Nazi slogan.

[事实] Weidel categorically denies that AfD contains extremist or proto-fascist tendencies and says she does not regard “Everything for Germany” as a Nazi slogan.

[事实] She argues that comparisons between AfD and the Third Reich are politically motivated attempts to damage the party’s reputation.

[事实] The host responds that fear of such historical echoes remains one of the central public concerns about AfD.

[推测] Weidel’s categorical denial avoids addressing whether specific rhetoric and associations within the party warrant internal scrutiny, even apart from judgments about AfD as a whole.

[57:12] **The political firewall and prospects for governing**

[事实] Weidel says claims that AfD is extremist no longer persuade many voters and cites the party’s polling strength as evidence.

[事实] She describes the refusal of other parties—especially the CDU—to cooperate with AfD as undemocratic and harmful to both the CDU and Germany.

[事实] She argues that the firewall strengthens left-wing parties despite voters’ support for AfD and CDU.

[事实] Asked whether AfD would compromise to enter government, Weidel says there can be no compromise on the central policies outlined in the interview and insists that the CDU must move first.

[推测] This position may make coalition-building difficult even if the formal firewall weakens, because AfD’s electoral growth alone would not necessarily provide a governing majority.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s chief value lies in sustained, specific questioning. Rather than discussing AfD only through labels, the host tests how its programme would work in practice—especially whether its tax and pension plans are fiscally credible, who “remigration” would affect and what ending support for Ukraine would mean.

A notable strength is the interview’s exposure of ambiguities and tensions in Weidel’s platform. Her initial formulations are repeatedly tested against concrete cases, revealing gaps between a narrow description of policy and its potentially much broader application.

Its limitation is that many empirical claims—on energy prices, migration, crime, wartime spending, debt and polling—are asserted or disputed without being independently verified within the conversation. Listeners should therefore distinguish between the participants’ stated positions and established evidence.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for listeners seeking a direct account of AfD’s governing ambitions and for those interested in Germany’s debates over economic stagnation, Ukraine, migration and the boundaries of the democratic right.
