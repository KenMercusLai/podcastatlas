# Cynicism, Trust, and Hopeful Skepticism with Dr. Jamil Zaki

## 概览

Andrew Huberman speaks with Stanford psychology professor Dr. Jamil Zaki about cynicism, trust, skepticism, empathy-adjacent social perception, and how people can update their beliefs about others.

The central argument is that cynicism is not the same as clear-eyed skepticism. Zaki defines cynicism as a fixed theory that people are fundamentally selfish, greedy, and dishonest, while skepticism is a scientific willingness to test assumptions against evidence.

The episode repeatedly returns to the same pattern: people often underestimate others, avoid social risks, and then lose the chance to learn that their fears were exaggerated. Zaki argues that better data, social experimentation, and “hopeful skepticism” can help reverse that cycle.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode Framing**

[事实] Huberman introduces Dr. Jamil Zaki as a Stanford psychology professor and director of the Social Neuroscience Laboratory.

[事实] The episode focuses on cynicism, trust, empathy, learning, conflict resolution, relationships, and workplace collaboration.

[事实] Huberman says Zaki’s book is titled *Hope for Cynics: The Surprising Science of Human Goodness*.

[07:39] **What Cynicism Is**

[事实] Zaki defines cynicism as a theory that people are generally selfish, greedy, and dishonest at their core.

[事实] He says cynics do not deny that people can act kindly, but they tend to interpret kindness as a surface layer over self-interest.

[推测] The discussion frames cynicism less as a mood and more as a worldview that filters social evidence.

[08:46] **Self-Fulfilling Social Predictions**

[事实] Zaki uses Kurt Vonnegut’s line about becoming who we pretend to be to discuss self-fulfilling prophecies.

[事实] He says cynics often keep their guard up even with friends, romantic partners, and family.

[事实] In a speech-stress study, social support buffered blood pressure increases for most people, but not for cynical people.

[事实] Zaki says cynical behavior such as monitoring, spying, or threatening others can bring out more selfish responses from them.

[13:39] **Developmental Roots of Mistrust**

[事实] Zaki says classical cynicism is hard to measure very early in life because it usually requires self-report.

[事实] He connects later mistrust with insecure attachment, often studied through the “strange situation” with infants and caregivers.

[事实] He says about two-thirds of children are securely attached, while roughly one-third are insecurely attached.

[事实] A looking-time study suggested insecurely attached children found loving, stable caregiver-infant interactions more surprising.

[17:29] **Cynicism Versus Skepticism**

[事实] Zaki distinguishes cynicism from skepticism: cynicism is a locked-in negative theory, while skepticism seeks evidence and remains open to updating.

[事实] He compares cynics to lawyers looking for confirming evidence and skeptics to scientists testing claims.

[事实] He says cynicism can resemble gullibility because it mistrusts people in an unthinking way.

[事实] He describes cynicism as a “wicked learning environment” because mistrust prevents people from gathering evidence that might disconfirm it.

[23:45] **Trust Across Cultures and Time**

[事实] Zaki says cynicism data is more local to the U.S., while generalized trust has more international data.

[事实] He says more trusting cultures tend to be more economically equal.

[事实] In the U.S., the share of Americans who believed most people can be trusted fell from about half in 1972 to about one-third by 2018.

[事实] Zaki links inequality with zero-sum thinking, where another person’s gain is felt as one’s own loss.

[27:45] **Cynicism, Happiness, and Health**

[事实] Zaki says large prospective studies associate cynicism with lower happiness, lower life satisfaction, more depression, and more loneliness.

[事实] He says cynics also show higher cellular inflammation, more heart disease, and higher all-cause mortality in the studies he cites.

[事实] He cautions that these studies are correlational, though he describes them as rigorous.

[事实] He argues that social connection protects health, while cynicism blocks vulnerability and connection.

[32:08] **The Cynical Genius Illusion**

[事实] Zaki says culture often glamorizes cynicism by associating negativity with intelligence and social wisdom.

[事实] In survey examples, 70% of respondents picked a cynical person for difficult intellectual tasks, and 85% thought cynics were socially wiser.

[事实] Zaki cites Olga Stavrova’s work on the “cynical genius illusion,” saying cynics perform worse on cognitive and mathematical tests.

[事实] He also says cynics do worse than non-cynics at detecting liars.

[40:01] **Whether Cynicism Is Domain-Specific**

[事实] Zaki says cynicism tends to be relatively stable over time and often domain-general.

[事实] He says cynical people tend to trust strangers, romantic partners, friends, and colleagues less.

[事实] He notes that cynicism tends to decline in older adulthood, contrary to the stereotype of older people becoming more cynical.

[41:48] **Environments Shape Trust**

[事实] Zaki describes two fishing villages in southeastern Brazil: one ocean-based and collaborative, one lake-based and more competitive.

[事实] At the start of their careers, fishermen in both villages were similarly trusting and trustworthy.

[事实] Over time, the collaborative environment made people more trusting and trustworthy, while the competitive environment made people less trusting and less trustworthy.

[推测] The example suggests that social environments can train people toward either trust or mistrust.

[46:13] **Competition and Conflict**

[事实] Zaki says he is not judging cynics and notes that he has struggled with cynicism himself.

[事实] He says competition can be healthy and beautiful, using sports as an example.

[事实] He distinguishes task conflict, which can be useful, from personal conflict, which becomes destructive when it turns into suspicion and blanket judgments.

[事实] He says athletes can compete intensely while still respecting one another outside the task.

[51:27] **Cynicism as Simplification**

[事实] Huberman suggests cynics apply physics-like rules to social interaction, with strict categories and rigid predictions.

[事实] Zaki agrees that cynicism simplifies a complex world but says that simplification loses detail and wonder.

[事实] He says cynicism can block goals such as strong relationships, psychological flourishing, and accurate learning.

[事实] Zaki connects this to awe and says Dacher Keltner’s work identifies “moral beauty” in everyday kindness as a major source of awe.

[59:48] **Workplace Creativity and Group Intelligence**

[事实] Zaki discusses stack ranking as a workplace practice that forces managers to identify top and bottom performers.

[事实] He says stack-ranked workplaces make people less likely to take creative risks and less willing to share knowledge.

[事实] He says collaborative creativity often comes from dialogue and the space between people.

[事实] He cites Anita Woolley’s work on collective intelligence, including interpersonal sensitivity and willingness to share the conversation.

[64:26] **Measuring Cynicism and Trust**

[事实] Zaki says researchers can measure cynicism with the Cook-Medley hostility scale and later shorter adaptations.

[事实] He describes the trust game, where an investor can send money to a trustee, the money is multiplied, and the trustee can return some or none.

[事实] Huberman guesses that about 55% of trustees return the money, while Zaki says one study found about 80% made the trustworthy decision.

[事实] Zaki says people often underestimate how friendly, trustworthy, compassionate, and open-minded others are.

[71:17] **Social Media and Mean World Perceptions**

[事实] Zaki says social media coincided with rising cynicism but cautions that historical coincidence does not prove causation.

[事实] He says the average person scrolls about 300 feet of social media feed per day, roughly the height of the Statue of Liberty.

[事实] He cites work suggesting outrage and moral outrage are algorithmically amplified and can reinforce more outrage in future posts.

[事实] He says a small group of highly active users produces most political tweets, which can make fringe views look average.

[76:29] **Media, Fear, and the Exhausted Majority**

[事实] Zaki connects social media and legacy media to “Mean World Syndrome,” where media exposure can make people perceive more danger than is actually present.

[事实] He gives the example of stranger-kidnapping fears, saying people greatly overestimated the number of children kidnapped by strangers.

[事实] He says many people feel alone because they do not share the intense outrage they see online.

[事实] He uses Amanda Ripley’s term “conflict entrepreneurs” for people who intentionally stoke conflict.

[80:03] **Negativity Bias**

[事实] Zaki says humans show negativity bias: negative events and threats loom larger in attention, memory, decision-making, and impressions.

[事实] In a lab study, people shared information about selfish actors three times more often than generous actors.

[事实] When later participants saw that negatively skewed gossip, they underestimated the group’s generosity.

[事实] Zaki says ingrained biases are not destiny and compares counteracting cynicism to counteracting other cognitive biases.

[84:38] **Social Media and Safety**

[事实] Zaki says it is possible, in his view, to live a safe and informed life without social media.

[事实] He says some threats learned through social media are phantom threats or exaggerated depictions of groups.

[事实] He argues that avoiding social chances can create long-term danger through isolation and disconnection.

[87:45] **Hopeful Skepticism**

[事实] Zaki defines hopeful skepticism as a scientific, curious mindset that starts from the awareness that human defaults are often too negative.

[事实] He imagines a more accurate social media feed that shows representative human behavior, including ordinary kindness and cooperation.

[事实] He says hope is stereotyped as naive, but his reading of the data is that people are already seeing the world through overly negative filters.

[90:12] **Stanford Student Misperceptions**

[事实] Zaki’s lab surveyed Stanford undergraduates about themselves and the average Stanford student.

[事实] Students described themselves as more compassionate, friendly, and socially interested than they imagined the average student to be.

[事实] Students who underestimated peers were less willing to start conversations or confide in friends and became more isolated.

[事实] An intervention showing real peer data in dorms and a first-year class made students more willing to take social risks and, six months later, more socially integrated.

[93:06] **AI and Debiasing Information**

[事实] Huberman asks whether AI could be tuned to reduce cynicism and produce more hopeful skepticism.

[事实] Zaki says AI models reflect the data they are fed, and if the internet overweights negative content, models may reflect that.

[推测] Zaki sees potential for AI systems to correct for negativity bias and give people a less distorted digest of social information.

[99:03] **Tools for Becoming Less Cynical**

[事实] Zaki recommends “being skeptical of my cynicism,” meaning asking what evidence supports a suspicious inference.

[事实] He recommends a reciprocity mindset: understanding that trust can make others more trustworthy because people often reciprocate.

[事实] He recommends social savoring, or noticing and sharing positive things about other people.

[事实] He says social savoring changed what he noticed because the habit of sharing positive examples made him look for them.

[106:31] **Taking Social Risks**

[事实] Zaki says people can treat life like an experiment and take calculated leaps of faith with others.

[事实] He clarifies that this does not mean taking unsafe or reckless risks.

[事实] He cites research on under-sociality showing people expect conversations with strangers, gratitude, helping, vulnerability, and disagreement to be more awkward or harmful than they actually are.

[事实] He says people’s actual social experiences are often much more positive than their forecasts.

[109:49] **Encounter Counting**

[事实] Zaki recommends documenting social encounters that contradict negative expectations.

[事实] He gives an example of having a meaningful political conversation with a relative despite not reaching agreement.

[事实] He says writing down such experiences can help lock in social learning so pleasant surprises become less surprising.

[112:43] **Testing and Sharing Core Beliefs**

[事实] Huberman connects Zaki’s practice to Byron Katie-style questioning of core beliefs.

[事实] Zaki says this resembles cognitive behavioral therapy, where people test the evidence for painful or anxious beliefs.

[事实] Zaki says people should also share core beliefs because they often underestimate how much others share their desire for peace, compromise, cooperation, and compassion.

[事实] He says surveys in his lab found more than 80% of people across the political spectrum wanted more compromise, more peace, and less conflict.

[118:11] **Perceived Political Polarization**

[事实] Zaki says perceived polarization is often greater than real polarization.

[事实] He says Democrats overestimate how many Republicans earn more than $250,000, and Republicans overestimate how many Democrats are LGBTQ.

[事实] He describes research showing people misperceive the other side’s views on issues such as immigration as more extreme than they are.

[事实] He says people imagine rivals hate them twice as much as they really do, overestimate anti-democratic attitudes by about two times, and overestimate support for political violence by about 400%.

[124:41] **Correcting Political Misperceptions**

[事实] Zaki says showing people more accurate data about the other side can reduce perceived threat and escalatory impulses.

[事实] He does not claim hopeful skepticism will solve political division, but says it can open a door toward compromise and peace.

[事实] He emphasizes that real disagreements and real extremists exist, but they are not the average.

[126:10] **Dialogue Across Disagreement**

[事实] Zaki describes a 2016 Thanksgiving study using geotracking to infer whether people crossed partisan county lines for dinner.

[事实] People who crossed into perceived “enemy territory” had dinners about 50 minutes shorter than those dining with politically similar people.

[事实] In Zaki’s lab, people who disagreed on gun control, immigration, and climate change had Zoom conversations that they expected to go poorly.

[事实] After the conversations, the most common rating was 100 out of 100, and participants reported less negative emotion toward the other side and more intellectual humility.

[131:01] **Closing Reflections**

[事实] Huberman summarizes Zaki’s message as using internal and external reality testing to move away from polarization.

[事实] Huberman thanks Zaki for data-supported tools and says he hopes to have him return to discuss empathy.

[事实] Zaki thanks Huberman for science communication and describes the conversation as exceeding his forecast.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value is its sharp distinction between cynicism and skepticism. The strongest idea is that cynicism can feel sophisticated while actually reducing learning, creativity, connection, and accuracy.

A major strength is the repeated use of concrete studies: attachment research, the trust game, fishing villages, social media research, Stanford student interventions, and political dialogue experiments. These examples make the episode practical rather than merely philosophical.

[推测] The main limitation is that the episode emphasizes the costs of cynicism more than the boundary conditions for justified distrust, though Zaki repeatedly clarifies that real threats exist and that trust should be calculated.

[推测] This episode is especially useful for listeners interested in relationships, leadership, organizational culture, political polarization, social media, and practical ways to become less reflexively mistrustful without becoming naive.
