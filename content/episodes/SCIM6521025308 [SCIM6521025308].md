# Cortisol: How to Control It to Avoid and Overcome Burnout

## 概览

This episode reframes cortisol as more than a “stress hormone.” The central argument is that cortisol’s main role is to mobilize energy, especially glucose for the brain, and that health, mood, focus, sleep, and burnout are strongly influenced by when cortisol rises and falls.

The key pattern is simple: cortisol should be high shortly after waking, then gradually decline through the day, and be low in the hours before sleep and the first hours of sleep. The episode explains the HPA axis, the SCN circadian clock, negative feedback, and why mistimed cortisol spikes can disrupt the next day.

The practical discussion focuses on timing-based tools: morning bright light, hydration, caffeine timing, exercise consistency, occasional cold exposure, grapefruit, licorice, evening light control, breathing, starchy carbohydrates, late-day exercise recovery, and supplements such as ashwagandha and apigenin.

Burnout is presented as two main timing patterns: morning overactivation followed by afternoon exhaustion, or morning exhaustion followed by nighttime stress. The proposed solution is not simply “lower cortisol,” but restore the right cortisol rhythm for the person’s schedule.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode Premise**

[事实] Andrew Huberman introduces the episode as a discussion of cortisol, how to control it, how to avoid burnout, and how to overcome burnout.
[事实] He says cortisol affects mood, sleep, immune function, wellbeing, and long-term health.
[事实] The episode’s core claim is that controlling cortisol at different times of day and night is a major lever for health and performance.

[01:31] **Cortisol Is Not Just a Stress Hormone**

[事实] Cortisol is released in response to stress, but the episode argues that calling it only a stress hormone is incomplete.
[事实] Huberman describes cortisol as a hormone that deploys energy, especially glucose, to tissues that need it, particularly the brain.
[事实] Cortisol is made in the adrenal glands and acts more slowly than adrenaline because it must be synthesized and released.
[事实] Cortisol can cross the blood-brain barrier and has many receptors in the hippocampus, a brain area involved in memory.

[11:39] **The 24-Hour Cortisol Rhythm**

[事实] Cortisol follows a circadian rhythm rather than staying constant across the day.
[事实] The first phase is a low-cortisol period beginning about four hours before sleep and lasting until about two hours after lights out.
[事实] Cortisol rises slightly in the third to fifth hours of sleep, then rises more quickly in the later sleep hours when REM sleep is more abundant.
[事实] The first hour after waking is described as a special window when cortisol can be further amplified.

[18:05] **HPA Axis and Negative Feedback**

[事实] Cortisol release is controlled through the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis: CRH from the hypothalamus stimulates ACTH from the pituitary, which stimulates cortisol release from the adrenals.
[事实] Cortisol regulates its own production through negative feedback: when cortisol gets high enough, upstream signals are suppressed.
[事实] This feedback explains why cortisol should rise in the morning, then decline later in the day.

[25:02] **Stress, SCN, and Parallel Cortisol Control**

[事实] Acute stress rapidly increases adrenaline and norepinephrine, while cortisol comes on more slowly and can last longer.
[事实] The suprachiasmatic nucleus, or SCN, is described as the brain’s central circadian pacemaker.
[事实] The SCN helps control the HPA axis and also has a parallel pathway that can boost cortisol after waking.
[事实] Chronically elevated cortisol at the wrong times is linked in the episode to immune suppression, metabolic dysregulation, midsection fat accumulation, “moon face,” and hyperpigmentation.

[34:28] **Morning Bright Light**

[事实] The recommended protocol is to get bright light into the eyes within the first hour after waking, ideally within the first 30 minutes.
[事实] Sunlight is preferred; if sunlight is unavailable, a 10,000 lux artificial light is suggested.
[事实] Morning bright light can quickly increase cortisol by up to 50%, according to the episode.
[事实] Huberman argues that a higher morning cortisol peak helps set up lower cortisol in the evening and better sleep at night.

[41:50] **Hydration and Caffeine Timing**

[事实] Hydration first thing in the morning is recommended because even mild dehydration can impair cognitive, physical, cellular, and organ function.
[事实] Proper hydration is said to increase alertness partly through effects on adrenaline and cortisol.
[事实] For habitual caffeine users, caffeine may not greatly increase cortisol, but it can prolong cortisol’s effects.
[事实] Delaying caffeine by 60 to 90 minutes after waking is recommended for people who crash in the late morning or afternoon.

[49:35] **Exercise and Cortisol Entrainment**

[事实] Exercise increases cortisol, but the size and duration of the increase depend on whether the exercise type, intensity, and timing are familiar.
[事实] Novel or unusually intense exercise can produce a larger and longer cortisol increase.
[事实] Exercising in the same two- to three-hour window several days per week acts as an entrainment cue for the SCN.
[事实] Huberman says consistent exercise timing can create anticipatory energy before workouts.

[57:56] **Cold Exposure**

[事实] Deliberate cold exposure increases epinephrine, norepinephrine, and a longer arc of dopamine release.
[事实] Cold exposure can raise cortisol when it is novel or uncomfortable.
[事实] If cold exposure is done regularly, the cortisol increase becomes less significant.
[事实] Huberman suggests occasional cold exposure, probably no more than twice per week, if the goal is specifically to boost cortisol.

[62:56] **Grapefruit and Licorice**

[事实] Grapefruit is described as extending cortisol’s life in the bloodstream by inhibiting enzymes such as CYP3A4.
[事实] The episode says eating grapefruit can extend cortisol duration by about 25% to 50%.
[事实] Black licorice, through glycyrrhizin, can potently increase circulating cortisol.
[事实] Huberman warns that people with hypertension and people who are pregnant or breastfeeding should avoid black licorice.

[71:41] **Afternoon Light and the Evening Sensitivity Window**

[事实] Bright light in the afternoon does not boost cortisol the same way morning light does because this pathway is described as gated off during the circadian “dead zone.”
[事实] Afternoon sunlight is still described as potentially beneficial for mood and wellbeing.
[事实] About two hours after sundown, the system becomes sensitive again, and bright artificial light can trigger large cortisol increases.
[推测] The practical implication is that light timing matters more than light exposure alone.

[75:27] **Evening Light Control**

[事实] In the evening, the goal is low and declining cortisol.
[事实] Huberman recommends dimming indoor lights, especially overhead lights.
[事实] Table lamps are described as better than overhead lights, and floor-level lighting as better than table lamps.
[事实] Short-wavelength light from white LEDs, blue or green light, phones, and computers is described as especially problematic at night.

[80:21] **Night Reactivity and Caffeine Cutoff**

[事实] The episode says it is easy to create cortisol spikes at night because baseline cortisol should already be low.
[事实] Evening anxiety from online content, texts, or thoughts is attributed to sharp cortisol inflections from a low baseline.
[事实] Huberman recommends exploring caffeine cutoff timing if sleep is disrupted, with about 2 p.m. as his general suggestion and 2:30 to 3 p.m. as a latest range for many people.
[事实] Caffeine late in the day may extend cortisol’s duration even if it does not sharply raise cortisol in habitual users.

[82:37] **Physiological Sigh for Real-Time Stress Control**

[事实] Exhale-emphasized breathing is presented as a fast way to reduce stress and slow heart rate.
[事实] The physiological sigh is described as a deep inhale through the nose, a second sharp inhale through the nose, and a long mouth exhale until the lungs are empty.
[事实] Huberman recommends one to three physiological sighs during evening or nighttime stress.
[事实] He says this shifts the autonomic nervous system toward parasympathetic “rest and digest” mode.

[87:04] **Starchy Carbohydrates and Evening Cortisol**

[事实] Starchy carbohydrates in the evening or last meal are recommended if they fit the listener’s nutrition plan and the goal is lower cortisol.
[事实] The episode explains this through blood glucose: when blood glucose is elevated, cortisol is less readily released.
[事实] Low-carbohydrate diets may raise cortisol during the first three weeks of transition, then normalize.
[事实] Huberman suggests that people on low-carbohydrate diets assess sleep quality and consider evening starches if sleep is poor.

[95:34] **Late-Day Exercise**

[事实] The ideal cortisol pattern favors exercise earlier in the day, but Huberman says people should train when they can.
[事实] Afternoon training is generally framed as less disruptive than late evening training.
[事实] Moderate to high intensity exercise late in the day can triple or quadruple cortisol.
[事实] After late workouts, he recommends starchy carbohydrates, protein, long-exhale breathing, dim lights, hot showers, and avoiding bright screens before bed.

[104:38] **Supplements for Lowering Evening Cortisol**

[事实] Ashwagandha is described as capable of reducing cortisol by about 11% to 29%.
[事实] Huberman recommends using ashwagandha, if used, in the late afternoon, evening, or night rather than early in the day.
[事实] Apigenin, found in chamomile, is described as acting through GABA and chloride-channel pathways and having a smaller cortisol-lowering effect than higher-dose ashwagandha.
[事实] He stresses that behavioral tools should come before supplementation.

[111:02] **Two Patterns of Burnout**

[事实] Burnout is described as real, but usually not the same as adrenal failure.
[事实] Cushing’s and Addison’s are discussed as medical conditions that require medical care, but Huberman says most burnout is not those conditions.
[事实] The first burnout pattern is morning stress or anxiety followed by afternoon and evening exhaustion.
[事实] The second burnout pattern is morning exhaustion and brain fog followed by nighttime stress and difficulty sleeping.

[116:23] **Morning-Stress Burnout**

[事实] For people waking too early or too stressed, Huberman recommends non-sleep deep rest, also called yoga nidra.
[事实] NSDR is described as involving long-exhale breathing, deliberate body relaxation, and parasympathetic activation.
[事实] Regular NSDR sessions of about 11 to 30 minutes are said to significantly reduce cortisol.
[事实] After waking, he recommends hydration and bright light, while delaying caffeine 60 to 90 minutes to avoid making the morning peak too high and too early.

[121:40] **Night-Stress Burnout**

[事实] For people who wake exhausted but become stressed at night, Huberman focuses on the six hours before desired sleep and the first two hours after sleep onset.
[事实] He recommends avoiding caffeine, reducing stress, dimming indoor lights, and using physiological sigh breathing for three to five minutes.
[事实] Late-day sunlight or a sunset walk is presented as acceptable and potentially helpful, provided the sun is still out.
[事实] He says recovery from this burnout pattern depends heavily on getting nighttime cortisol low so morning cortisol can rise properly again.

[127:13] **Final Integration: Rhythm, Aging, and Cognition**

[事实] Huberman says the early day, middle day, evening, and night are linked by cortisol rhythms.
[事实] He recommends focusing first on the time of day where the listener’s main “pain point” occurs.
[事实] Aging, menopause, and chronic illness are discussed in relation to a flatter afternoon cortisol curve.
[事实] Elevated cortisol at the wrong times is linked to poorer cognition and hippocampal vulnerability.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is its timing-based framework: it avoids treating cortisol as simply “good” or “bad” and instead shows why the same tool can help or hurt depending on the hour.

[事实] The discussion gives many practical levers, including light, hydration, caffeine, exercise, breathing, carbohydrates, and supplements. It also repeatedly emphasizes behavior before supplementation.

[推测] The main limitation is density: listeners who want only a short burnout protocol may find the biology, mechanisms, and repeated caveats demanding. The episode is best suited for people willing to adjust daily routines and think in terms of rhythms rather than isolated hacks.

[推测] It is especially useful for people with morning anxiety, afternoon crashes, late-night wired-but-tired states, or disrupted sleep, while people who suspect Cushing’s, Addison’s, hypertension-related issues, pregnancy, or breastfeeding need medical guidance rather than self-experimentation.
