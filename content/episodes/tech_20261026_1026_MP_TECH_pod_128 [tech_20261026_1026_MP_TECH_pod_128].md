# Asking AI to Write Your Obituary: Reflection, Memory and the Limits of Digital Intimacy

## Overview

After the death of her brother and around her 80th birthday, author and educator Donna Guthrie asked ChatGPT to write her obituary. The result was surprisingly accurate, drawing on personal details accumulated through two years of interactions with the chatbot.

Guthrie sees the AI-generated obituary as a useful starting template, despite the unsettling experience of reading about her own death. Technologist and artist Michelle Wong frames AI as a mirror that reflects and amplifies the material users give it.

The episode explores the potential therapeutic value of personalized AI while stressing that chatbots should supplement, not replace, human relationships. Unlike AI systems that tend to echo their users, loved ones provide care, disagreement and criticism that can strengthen real relationships.

## Podcast Commentary/Summary

The episode uses one intimate, easily understood experiment to examine a broader question: what happens when AI retains enough personal information to narrate a human life? Guthrie’s experience captures both sides of that possibility—the comfort of feeling accurately represented and the unease of discovering how much a chatbot appears to know.

Wong’s “AI as a mirror” framing is the episode’s strongest insight. It explains why personalized systems can support reflection while also warning that their responses are shaped by users’ existing patterns and the information supplied to them.

The discussion offers a clear boundary rather than a simple endorsement or rejection of AI: personalized chatbots may assist reflection, but they cannot reproduce the disagreement, accountability and mutual care found in human relationships. The episode is especially relevant to listeners interested in AI companionship, digital memory, privacy and technology’s emerging role in emotionally sensitive experiences.
