# Understanding PTSD, Stress, Resilience, and Cue-Centered Therapy with Dr. Victor Carrion

## 概览

This episode centers on PTSD, especially in children and adolescents, through a conversation with Dr. Victor Carrion, a Stanford psychiatrist whose work links trauma symptoms, brain development, cortisol rhythms, and treatment design.

A core theme is that stress is not inherently bad: moderate stress can build coping and performance, but excessive or threatening stress can become traumatic. Carrion repeatedly frames PTSD as a condition that worsens with avoidance and improves when people can identify cues, understand their responses, and build personalized tools.

The discussion moves from biology to practice: cortisol, the hippocampus, amygdala, prefrontal cortex, dissociation, ADHD misdiagnosis, cue-centered therapy, mindfulness, yoga, sleep, school-based prevention, resilience, and organoid research.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode Setup**

[事实] Andrew Huberman introduces Dr. Victor Carrion as a Stanford professor and vice chair of psychiatry and behavioral sciences.

[事实] Carrion is presented as an expert on PTSD, particularly treatment of PTSD in children and adolescents, while the episode also covers adult PTSD.

[事实] The conversation is framed around PTSD biology, cue-centered therapy, mindfulness, CBT, stress, anxiety, trauma, and practical tools.

[06:24] **Stress as a Spectrum**

[事实] Carrion describes stress as an inverted U-shaped curve: some stress improves performance, health, and problem-solving, while too much stress produces physiological cost.

[事实] He distinguishes homeostasis from allostasis, saying stress becomes costly when the body must adapt beyond its normal coping range.

[事实] Traumatic stress is described as stress that threatens physical integrity and can lead to PTSD symptoms.

[事实] Carrion says some children show impairing post-traumatic symptoms without meeting the full PTSD diagnosis.

[11:47] **Avoidance, Rumination, and Accumulated Stressors**

[事实] Carrion says “PTSD feeds on avoidance,” meaning pretending the trauma did not happen or avoiding treatment can worsen outcomes.

[事实] He says untreated or avoided PTSD can become complicated by substance abuse and self-injurious behaviors.

[事实] In children, trauma can turn play into repetitive, non-joyful traumatic play as an attempt to make sense of events.

[事实] Carrion argues PTSD often reflects accumulated stressors rather than a single event alone, using children in Haiti after an earthquake as an example.

[14:10] **Children, Vulnerability, and Plasticity**

[事实] Carrion rejects the simple phrase “children are resilient,” saying children are more vulnerable but can become resilient with help and tools.

[事实] He says children’s neuroplasticity can make them vulnerable to trauma, but it can also support recovery through positive environments and psychotherapy.

[事实] He frames PTSD as evidence that environment can affect biology.

[16:16] **Transgenerational Trauma**

[事实] Carrion says the evidence is not settled on whether stress-induced genomic changes can be passed between generations.

[事实] He says genetic vulnerabilities can be passed from one generation to another.

[事实] He describes seeing children with PTSD-like symptoms where the apparent trauma was in the parent’s history, and the child had learned patterns such as avoidance, hypervigilance, lack of trust, or re-experiencing.

[事实] Carrion says nature and nurture interact in vulnerability.

[20:49] **PTSD, Autonomic Arousal, and Dissociation**

[事实] Carrion notes that some people call PTSD “post-traumatic stress injury” to emphasize injury and dysregulation rather than disorder.

[事实] He explains that young children may not be able to fight or flee because of size and dependence, so they freeze.

[事实] Freezing is linked to dissociation, which he describes as a developmentally useful defense that can become pathological if it becomes the only response.

[事实] He says PTSD in children is often misunderstood because hypervigilance can look like hyperactivity, and dissociation can look like inattention.

[23:20] **Cortisol and Night Symptoms**

[事实] Carrion studied cortisol in children with PTSD symptoms because the arousal system appeared central to their condition.

[事实] His studies found normal daily cortisol rhythm overall, but pre-bedtime cortisol stayed higher than in healthy controls.

[事实] He connects elevated nighttime arousal with symptoms such as bedwetting, nightmares, shallow or short sleep, and nighttime fears.

[事实] He became concerned about glucocorticoid effects on brain regions such as the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex.

[33:18] **PTSD Versus ADHD**

[事实] Carrion says children overall can be overmedicated, but children with true ADHD can also be under-medicated because of access and identification problems.

[事实] He says stimulant treatment is first-line for true ADHD, while PTSD treatment in children is primarily psychosocial.

[事实] Giving stimulants to a child with PTSD but not ADHD may increase existing hyperarousal.

[事实] Carrion says ADHD hyperactivity tends to be more persistent, while PTSD hyperarousal tends to appear on and off in response to cues.

[38:30] **Hypervigilance and Context**

[事实] Carrion says hypervigilance is not always the problem; the problem is not knowing when to turn it on and off.

[事实] He gives an example of a child exposed to domestic violence who learned to run to a room when household noise signaled danger.

[事实] The same noise level in a classroom can later trigger running out of class, even when the original danger is absent.

[事实] Carrion says treatment should help children develop new competitive responses rather than simply demand that they stop the old response.

[44:08] **Cues and Triggers**

[事实] Carrion says triggers are often personal, sensory, and neutral, such as a color, rain, or a voice quality.

[事实] He distinguishes a cue from a threat: a gun is a threat, while a red car nearby during a trauma might later become a cue.

[事实] Identifying cues helps people understand that symptoms are not random and that they are not “crazy,” bad, or the problem.

[事实] Carrion says people may not identify every cue, but learning even a few can increase self-understanding and self-forgiveness.

[53:22] **Brain Imaging and Treatment Targets**

[事实] Carrion’s work with school-age children examined cortisol, hippocampal volume, and brain function using MRI.

[事实] He reports that a longitudinal sample showed a relationship between higher pre-bedtime cortisol and smaller hippocampal volume.

[事实] Functional imaging showed children with post-traumatic stress symptoms activated fewer hippocampal voxels during a memory task than healthy children.

[事实] Carrion says findings point to problems in frontolimbic pathways, including amygdala activation and prefrontal regulation.

[58:04] **Cue-Centered Therapy**

[事实] Carrion developed cue-centered therapy because existing trauma treatments often focused on one event and frequently required parent involvement.

[事实] He wanted a multimodal treatment including CBT, self-efficacy, empowerment, and insight-oriented work.

[事实] Trials of cue-centered therapy reduced symptoms of PTSD, anxiety, and depression, with improvements reported by children and observers.

[事实] Carrion says parents’ anxiety also decreased in one trial even when parents were not participating in treatment.

[67:11] **Practicing Positive Thoughts**

[事实] Carrion says positive thoughts can help regulate stress responses, but unlike negative threat-based thoughts, they are not automatic.

[事实] He tells children that positive thoughts must be practiced like learning an instrument.

[事实] His team uses the slogan “practice positive thoughts.”

[事实] He encourages practicing positive thoughts even when the child is not currently in a stress response.

[68:58] **The Toolbox Approach**

[事实] Cue-centered therapy uses an empty toolbox metaphor that children fill with tools such as positive thoughts, deep breathing, mindfulness, and muscle relaxation.

[事实] Children decide what their cues are and which tools belong in their toolbox.

[事实] Carrion says self-created tools often work especially well; he gives an example of a child using orange juice as a calming tool.

[推测] The mechanism in that example is likely less about orange juice itself and more about agency, self-care, and a sense of control.

[75:27] **Narrative After Preparation**

[事实] Carrion says trauma narrative work is important, but it should come after education and after the child has learned tools.

[事实] He says narrative work can assess memory gaps, cues, and emotions.

[事实] He says the narrative should include negative, neutral, and positive events rather than only trauma.

[事实] He argues that tools help children tolerate charged material when narrative work begins.

[78:18] **Practical Tools for Children**

[事实] Carrion asks children what already helps them feel better when they are agitated or anxious.

[事实] Children often mention music, sports, teammates, coaches, friends, and sleepovers.

[事实] Clinicians may teach breathing exercises, muscle relaxation, positive thinking, mindfulness, and simple yoga poses.

[事实] Carrion emphasizes that children learn tools, then decide whether those tools work for them.

[86:43] **The Four-Corner Square**

[事实] Carrion describes a square with four corners: what the child is thinking, what they are feeling emotionally, what they are feeling physically, and what they are doing.

[事实] He adds the physical or somatic corner because many children express distress as headaches, stomachaches, racing heart, or other body sensations.

[事实] The therapist can start with whichever corner is most accessible to the child.

[事实] Changing one corner can change the other corners and create a new response.

[96:01] **Emotional Thermometer and Timing**

[事实] Carrion uses an emotional thermometer from 0 or 1 to 10 to assess the child’s stress level.

[事实] If a child is at a 9 or 10, Carrion says it is usually better to use a toolbox skill before attempting the square exercise.

[事实] Once the child is closer to a lower stress level, they can better engage cognitively with the square.

[事实] He hopes children internalize these exercises after cue-centered therapy.

[99:31] **Social Media, Space, and Boundaries**

[事实] Carrion compares social media overstimulation to being in a highly stimulating public environment and needing to retreat for rest.

[事实] He says technology was helpful during the pandemic for school and social connection, but it can also be harmful.

[事实] He compares social media to tools such as hammers or knives: useful when used properly, dangerous without rules.

[事实] He recommends family-level boundaries, such as putting phones in a basket during dinner, while emphasizing that parents must model the behavior.

[107:11] **School-Based Prevention**

[事实] Carrion became interested in yoga and mindfulness partly to support his own team against vicarious trauma.

[事实] His group brought yoga and mindfulness practices into East Palo Alto classrooms while also doing school counseling work.

[事实] After about three months, a principal asked what was happening because children from those classrooms had not been sent to her office.

[事实] Carrion’s team later partnered with Pure Power to develop and test a school yoga and mindfulness curriculum.

[115:18] **Yoga, Mindfulness, and Sleep**

[事实] The school curriculum was delivered roughly two to three times per week for 15 to 50 minutes.

[事实] Students used mats and stayed in their regular school clothes.

[事实] Carrion says the intervention improved mood and increased sleep by 73 minutes.

[事实] He says the intervention increased REM sleep, total sleep, and deep sleep, and preliminary unpublished data suggested reduced amygdala activity.

[121:23] **Scaling Mental Health Interventions**

[事实] Carrion says scaling these programs requires prioritizing education and mental health.

[事实] He says teachers are overworked and under-resourced, and need more time, space, and support.

[事实] He says districts and the Department of Education should make these efforts a priority.

[事实] Huberman adds that parents and citizens can contact schools and elected officials to advocate for programs.

[125:00] **Puerto Rico Project**

[事实] Carrion describes a Puerto Rico project in which teachers are trained in the yoga and mindfulness curriculum and counselors are trained in cue-centered therapy.

[事实] Children are assessed at baseline, after the yoga and mindfulness curriculum, and again after treatment for those with impairing post-traumatic stress symptoms.

[事实] Carrion says Puerto Rico is useful for this work because the whole island functions as one large school district.

[事实] The goal is to test whether combining prevention and treatment can be feasible, sustainable, and scalable to larger districts such as New York or Los Angeles.

[128:22] **Redefining Success and Belonging**

[事实] Carrion says society needs to redefine success beyond extreme performance, fame, or elite achievement.

[事实] He says a broad sense of belonging across roles can be protective.

[事实] He warns that if someone’s identity depends on only one domain, even perceived failure in that domain can destabilize them.

[事实] He says citizenship, care for others, and participation in multiple parts of life should be included in success.

[130:34] **Resilience and Adaptation**

[事实] Carrion defines resilience as bouncing back, but says he prefers adaptation because it means returning to a better place after difficulty.

[事实] He says known resilience-related factors include humor, perseverance, and the presence of a supportive adult in a child’s life.

[事实] He raises the question of whether some children may have traits that help them seek and maintain supportive relationships.

[事实] He says more work is needed on the biology of resilience.

[132:33] **Organoids and the Biology of Stress**

[事实] Carrion describes collaboration using organoids, or “mini-brains,” made from stem cells that become neurons.

[事实] The team exposed organoids to different cortisol levels, including trauma-mimetic levels and lower cue-like levels.

[事实] The organoids had active glucocorticoid receptors.

[事实] Epigenetic analysis found genes previously linked to PTSD and a novel set related to collagen formation.

[136:16] **Stress, Aging, and Epigenetic Follow-Up**

[事实] Carrion connects stress with accelerated aging and mentions links between stress and atherosclerosis.

[事实] He says people with severe chronic mental illness die about 25 years younger than the rest of the population.

[事实] In the Puerto Rico project, children provide epithelial cell samples for epigenetic analysis.

[事实] The research aims to examine resilience and treatment response to yoga, mindfulness, and cue-centered therapy.

[142:18] **Final Message**

[事实] Carrion says the most important message is to listen to children and adults about their experiences.

[事实] He emphasizes creating space where people do not feel isolated and can feel supported.

[事实] He wants people to identify their own strengths and capacities for getting better.

[事实] He says the best psychiatrists he knows say very little and listen carefully.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it connects PTSD across levels: lived symptoms, child development, family learning, autonomic physiology, brain imaging, psychotherapy, school systems, and molecular research.

[推测] Its strongest practical contribution is the cue-centered framework: identify cues, build a personalized toolbox, practice positive thoughts, and use the four-corner square when arousal is low enough for reflection.

[推测] A limitation is that many interventions discussed require trained clinicians, supportive schools, or organized implementation; listeners without access to those systems may get principles more than a complete treatment path.

[推测] This episode is especially useful for clinicians, educators, parents, trauma researchers, and adults trying to understand how stress responses become conditioned and how agency, support, and structured tools may help recovery.
