# Titanic: Third Class and the Iceberg Warnings

## 概览

This episode focuses on the Titanic’s third-class passengers, arguing that steerage on Titanic was unusually comfortable by contemporary standards and not the grim, cramped space often imagined.

The hosts use third class as a window into early twentieth-century migration: Scandinavian farmers, Lebanese Maronite Christians, Armenians, Croats, Irish and British families were crossing the Atlantic for work, safety, family networks, or a new start.

The second half follows the ship from Queenstown into the Atlantic, showing how calm weather, Marconi message backlogs, scattered ice warnings, accepted maritime practice, and split-second decisions culminated in the collision at 11:40 p.m. on Sunday, April 14.

## 分段落总结

[00:40] **Titanic, Jack Dawson, and Steerage**

[事实] The episode opens with Jack Dawson’s speech from James Cameron’s 1997 film *Titanic* and uses it to introduce third class on the ship.

[事实] The hosts say the film captures something true about steerage: it could be lively, communal, and for many passengers possibly the best experience of comfort they had ever known before the disaster.

[推测] The cinematic reference helps frame third class not as background scenery, but as the emotional and social center of this episode.

[03:10] **Third-Class Comfort and White Star’s Selling Point**

[事实] Third class on Titanic is described as the best third-class accommodation yet provided on a liner, with two- or four-berth cabins, washbasins, ventilation, baths, showers, smoking rooms, bars, and a large dining saloon.

[事实] Single women and families were housed separately from couples or single men, and the hosts contrast Titanic favorably with rival ships that packed more people into smaller spaces.

[事实] Third class was not full: the transcript gives 712 third-class passengers, with most embarking at Southampton and about 100 each joining in France and Ireland.

[推测] The hosts are challenging the idea that Titanic’s class system can be reduced to simple neglect of third-class passengers before the collision.

[06:23] **Who the Third-Class Passengers Were**

[事实] The largest third-class groups included 118 British passengers, 113 Irish passengers, and a larger combined Scandinavian presence: 104 Swedes, 55 Finns, 25 Norwegians, and seven Danes.

[事实] Other groups included 79 Lebanese passengers, 33 Bulgarians, 22 Belgians, 12 Armenians, and eight Chinese passengers.

[事实] The hosts connect this passenger mix to the wider immigration boom into the United States, saying about a million people a year were arriving at around this time, with 1907 reaching 1.125 million.

[事实] White Star agents promoted the United States abroad through advertisements and other publicity about its opportunities.

[08:41] **Migration, Prejudice, and Passenger Networks**

[事实] Many Scandinavian passengers are described as poor rural people heading to places such as Minnesota and the Midwest to farm.

[事实] The hosts say there were relatively few Eastern European Jewish passengers because White Star discouraged Eastern Europeans from traveling from Southampton, while still providing kosher food.

[事实] Croatian passengers are discussed through Nikola Lulic, who had been to America, knew English, and acted as a chaperone or interpreter.

[推测] The ship’s passenger list reveals both the promise of migration and the prejudices built into the travel industry that served it.

[10:24] **Lebanese and Armenian Passengers**

[事实] The Lebanese passengers are linked to Christians leaving the Ottoman Empire amid inter-communal violence and economic hardship.

[事实] Some came from the village of Hardin, where silk-related work had become less viable because silk prices had fallen.

[事实] They were heading to Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania, a coal-mining area nicknamed Diamond City.

[事实] The Armenian passengers are described as coming from the Keki district, a poor mountainous region affected by violence and pressure within the Ottoman Empire.

[推测] The hosts treat these passengers’ stories as especially tragic because the ship seemed to offer escape from danger and poverty, yet became another catastrophe.

[15:04] **Family Stories and the Goldsmiths**

[事实] The hosts discuss migrant send-offs, remittances, temporary migration, and employment networks such as padrones who helped new arrivals find work.

[事实] They then focus on the Goldsmith family, Methodists from Rochester heading to Detroit after the death of their youngest son from diphtheria in 1911.

[事实] The family traveled with Alfred Rush, who turned 16 on April 15 and received long trousers as a sign of adulthood.

[事实] The hosts say this birthday gift effectively killed him, because it marked him as no longer a child.

[事实] Frankie Goldsmith survived and later left memories of his excitement aboard Titanic.

[19:04] **Third Class as an Unexpected Holiday**

[事实] Many third-class passengers had never been on an ocean liner and may never previously have had a week’s holiday.

[事实] They included miners, farm laborers, stonemasons, dressmakers, servants, and salesmen.

[事实] Life aboard included cards, skipping, accordions, mouth organs, and regular meals such as porridge, toast, marmalade, herrings, and boiled potatoes.

[推测] For many passengers, Titanic’s third class combined emigration with a rare experience of leisure, abundance, and modern comfort.

[22:40] **Titanic Leaves Queenstown**

[事实] After the break, the episode resumes with Titanic leaving Queenstown near Cork on April 12 and steaming into the Atlantic on the standard route.

[事实] The ship had 1,320 passengers and, with crew included, 2,235 people aboard.

[事实] Cargo mentioned includes a Joseph Conrad short story, ostrich plumes, a Renault car, diamonds from Antwerp, and a rare copy of the *Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam*.

[事实] J. P. Morgan was not aboard; the hosts say he was supervising the transfer of his art collection to France because of concerns about taxation.

[24:25] **Calm Seas and Sunday Routine**

[事实] Titanic made 500 miles on April 12 and 546 miles on April 13 in very calm conditions.

[事实] On Sunday, April 14, Captain Smith led a Christian service at 10:30 in the first-class dining saloon.

[事实] A lifeboat drill was expected on Sunday morning but was cancelled, which the hosts say was common when conditions made it inconvenient.

[事实] The weather grew colder after lunch, and passengers moved indoors to read, play cards, use the library, or visit the gym.

[27:08] **Marconi Messages and Communication Overload**

[事实] First-class passengers had telephones, and Titanic also had a Marconi wireless system used to send passenger messages and receive news.

[事实] The ship printed the *Atlantic Daily Bulletin* from received wireless reports.

[事实] The Marconi operators Jack Phillips and Harold Bride worked for Marconi, not directly for White Star.

[事实] After the apparatus broke down on Friday night and Saturday morning, a large backlog of passenger messages built up.

[推测] The hosts imply that fatigue and commercial message traffic contributed to the handling of later ice warnings.

[29:25] **Ice Warnings and Missed Signals**

[事实] The ship received ice warnings from La Touraine on Friday and later from ships including Caronia, Noordam, Californian, and Mesaba.

[事实] The hosts say the warnings arrived piecemeal, mixed among other messages, and were not collated into a single clear picture.

[事实] Captain Smith saw at least some warnings, and one message was handed to Bruce Ismay, who read it, folded it, put it in his pocket, and showed it to some first-class passengers.

[事实] Smith later wanted the message back, but the hosts say it is unclear that it was shown properly to the crew.

[32:02] **Speed, Practice, and the False Ismay Story**

[事实] The hosts reject the film’s suggestion that Ismay pressured Smith to race to New York for publicity.

[事实] They say Titanic was not traveling at full speed and did not have all boilers on.

[事实] They explain that standard practice was not necessarily to reduce speed in an ice zone, because ships were thought able to survive striking an iceberg head-on.

[事实] A German ship, Kronprinz Wilhelm, is given as an example of a vessel that had rammed an iceberg in 1907 and still reached port.

[推测] The episode presents the disaster less as theatrical arrogance and more as a failure produced by normal procedures that proved disastrously inadequate.

[34:07] **The Dangerous Calm**

[事实] On the night of April 14, the sea was extremely flat, the sky was clear, and conditions were very cold.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that a glassy sea made icebergs harder to see because there was no swell breaking at their base.

[事实] In second class, Reverend Ernest Carton led a hymn-singing service, while other passengers read or played cards.

[事实] Jack Thayer later remembered the night as one that made him feel glad to be alive.

[37:03] **The Watch and the Collision**

[事实] William Murdoch was the officer on watch, replacing Charles Lightoller, while Captain Smith was dining with the Wideners.

[事实] Murdoch had no authority from Smith to reduce speed, and the ship continued at about 22 knots.

[事实] Lookouts included Lee and Frederick Fleet in the crow’s nest; Fleet saw a dark shape at 11:40 p.m., rang the bell three times, and telephoned the bridge with “iceberg right ahead.”

[事实] Murdoch ordered the ship to turn and the engines to reverse, trying to swing the bow away from the iceberg.

[事实] The hosts say this became fatal because the ship’s side scraped along the iceberg, opening damage along the hull.

[40:02] **The Moment After Impact**

[事实] Many aboard did not immediately understand the seriousness of the collision and thought the ship had merely brushed past the iceberg.

[事实] Steward James Witter, clearing the second-class smoking room before midnight, remembered a slight jar and then apparent normality.

[事实] Around 40 men were in that room playing cards, and the hosts say that within four hours, nine out of ten of them would be dead.

[推测] The understated physical sensation of the collision makes the coming disaster feel more disturbing, because the passengers’ immediate experience did not match the scale of what had happened.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strongest value is its shift of attention from famous first-class figures to third-class migrants. The hosts make Titanic’s lower decks feel historically specific, showing comfort, aspiration, prejudice, religious identity, labor migration, family grief, and global instability all moving through the same ship.

The discussion is also useful because it separates later myth from what the hosts present as contemporary practice. In particular, it challenges the idea that Ismay simply forced the ship to speed recklessly, while still showing how ignored or fragmented warnings mattered.

A limitation is that the episode depends heavily on host narration and selected stories rather than presenting every passenger group equally. [推测] Listeners wanting a systematic maritime inquiry may need additional sources, especially for technical details of the collision and wireless procedures.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in social history, migration, and how large disasters emerge from ordinary systems, assumptions, and routines rather than from one simple villain.
