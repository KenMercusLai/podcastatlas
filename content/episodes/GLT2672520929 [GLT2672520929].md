# The History of France in Ten Films

## 概览

This episode uses ten films as a route through French history, with the hosts and a guest film critic moving from Joan of Arc to the Dreyfus affair, the two world wars, the Occupation, postwar comedy, and the radical politics of the 1960s.

A central theme is that French historical cinema does not simply illustrate the past. The discussion repeatedly asks how films confirm, revise, mythologize, or evade national memory, especially around contested subjects such as Joan of Arc, Vichy collaboration, anti-Semitism, and May 1968.

The episode also treats cinema itself as part of French identity: both popular entertainment and serious art. The strongest praise goes to films that recreate a historical mindset rather than merely dressing modern characters in period costume.

## 分段落总结

[01:15] **Setting Up France Through Film**

[事实] The hosts introduce the episode as a history of France in ten films, after joking through references to Master and Commander, Napoleon, and Inspector Clouseau.

[事实] The guest argues that France takes cinema seriously as both mass entertainment and an art form.

[推测] The comic opening frames the episode as affectionate but irreverent toward French national myths.

[03:19] **Cinema, History, and National Narrative**

[事实] The discussion asks whether people learn history from film, and the guest says films often reinforce national narratives taught elsewhere.

[事实] The guest says younger French viewers may encounter some historical events first through film or television because school coverage changes over time.

[推测] The episode treats film less as a neutral teaching tool than as a way nations remember themselves.

[05:17] **Ancient Gaul and the Problem of Spectacle**

[事实] Asterix is rejected as the first choice because the books are said to be better than the film adaptations.

[事实] The guest says early Gallic history is difficult for French cinema because it is obscure and hard to make visually competitive with Roman spectacle.

[事实] A low-budget Christophe Lambert film about a Gallic chieftain is cited as an example of how not to make such a historical epic.

[推测] Budget constraints become one reason the list moves quickly past early French history.

[07:27] **Joan of Arc as a Foundational Figure**

[事实] The first chosen film is Jacques Rivette’s Joan the Maid, starring Sandrine Bonnaire and divided into The Battles and The Prisons.

[事实] The guest says Joan of Arc is the first figure in the list to crystallize French national identity in a way that remains important.

[事实] The film is praised for austerity, natural light, natural settings, movement, and minimal battle scenes.

[推测] Rivette’s restrained style is presented as a practical and artistic solution to filming the Middle Ages without Hollywood-scale resources.

[10:28] **Faith, Visions, and Joan’s Political Afterlife**

[事实] Rivette does not show Joan’s visions directly; instead, the film internalizes them through Bonnaire’s performance.

[事实] Joan is described as having been claimed at different times by republicans, monarchists, Vichy, communists, and the National Front.

[事实] The guest says Rivette, broadly a man of the left, seems to reclaim Joan as a heroine for everyone.

[推测] The film’s refusal to over-explain the visions lets viewers decide how to understand Joan’s spiritual authority.

[15:03] **La Reine Margot and the Wars of Religion**

[事实] The second film is La Reine Margot, an early 1990s film based on Alexandre Dumas’s novel.

[事实] The film is described as more an adaptation of Dumas than a serious work of historical research.

[事实] It is compared to Game of Thrones because of its sex, violence, gore, nudity, and court intrigue.

[事实] The discussion identifies the St Bartholomew’s Day massacre and the killing of Huguenots as central historical context.

[20:04] **Ridicule and the Court of Versailles**

[事实] The third film is Patrice Leconte’s Ridicule, set at Versailles near the end of the Ancien Régime under Louis XVI.

[事实] Its protagonist is a provincial nobleman who wants royal support to drain marshland and improve the lives of his peasants.

[事实] At Versailles, he learns that access to power depends on wit, social performance, and verbal cruelty.

[推测] The film is valued because it turns an unfamiliar social code into the engine of historical drama.

[24:27] **Ridicule as a Model Historical Film**

[事实] One host calls Ridicule the best historical film ever made.

[事实] The reason given is that it dramatizes a past worldview on its own terms rather than simply imposing modern assumptions.

[事实] The film is also linked to the coming Revolution through its portrayal of aristocratic ritual, exclusion, and exile.

[推测] The praise suggests that historical accuracy here means emotional and social accuracy as much as factual detail.

[25:22] **Abel Gance’s Napoleon**

[事实] The fourth film is Abel Gance’s silent Napoleon from the 1920s.

[事实] Gance intended a much larger six-part life of Napoleon, but the surviving film is a five-and-a-half-hour fragment focused on young Bonaparte.

[事实] Kevin Brownlow is credited with reassembling surviving fragments of the film.

[事实] The film’s triptych finale is described as groundbreaking and almost religious in its sense of destiny.

[30:01] **Les Enfants du Paradis and Occupied France**

[事实] The fifth film is Marcel Carné’s Les Enfants du Paradis, made under the German occupation and released around the Liberation.

[事实] It is set in the Paris of the 1830s, around the Boulevard du Crime and the world of theatres, pantomime, actors, and popular audiences.

[事实] The title refers to poorer spectators seated high in the theatre, equivalent to “the gods” in English theatre language.

[事实] The film centers on interconnected characters, especially Garance, and on impossible love.

[34:12] **Poetic Realism, Escape, and the Myth of Paris**

[事实] The guest says Vichy-era conditions encouraged non-contemporary films because they seemed safer and offered escape from the present.

[事实] Les Enfants du Paradis is linked to poetic realism, with fatalism, melancholy, ordinary people, and doomed love.

[事实] The film is described as recreating a vanished Paris of culture, underworld life, aristocratic salons, performance, and romance.

[推测] Its historical setting functions both as escapism and as a coded assertion that Parisian culture survived occupation.

[37:23] **Prévert and French Cinema’s Cultural Range**

[事实] Jacques Prévert wrote the script, and the guest praises his dialogue as crafted, direct, and unpretentious.

[事实] The conversation says French cinema often tries to create work that can be enjoyed across high, middle, and popular culture.

[事实] The guest also acknowledges that French cinema can be pretentious and over-engineered.

[推测] The episode presents French cinema’s prestige as rooted in its refusal to separate art completely from popular entertainment.

[40:35] **The Dreyfus Affair on Screen**

[事实] The sixth film discussed is Roman Polanski’s Dreyfus film, adapted from Robert Harris’s novel An Officer and a Spy.

[事实] The Dreyfus affair is introduced as the 1890s case of Alfred Dreyfus, a Jewish French artillery officer from Alsace accused of treason.

[事实] Dreyfus is described as degraded in public, sent to Devil’s Island, and protesting his innocence.

[事实] The film’s narrative focus is Georges Picquart rather than Dreyfus or Émile Zola.

[43:22] **Picquart, Duty, and Anti-Semitism**

[事实] Picquart discovers that Dreyfus was innocent, evidence had been fabricated, and the real culprit was Major Esterhazy.

[事实] The guest stresses that Picquart is not transformed into a liberal hero; he remains personally anti-Semitic.

[事实] Picquart’s actions are driven by loyalty to the army and a sense of duty.

[推测] This makes the story morally sharper because justice emerges from institutional duty rather than personal warmth.

[45:47] **Polanski, MeToo, and French Controversy**

[事实] The discussion notes that Polanski’s own criminal and sexual history affected the film’s reception.

[事实] The guest says the film’s release coincided with France’s delayed MeToo moment.

[事实] Interviews were cancelled and some journalists refused to discuss the film.

[推测] The Dreyfus subject made the controversy more charged because the film itself concerns an accused man, public judgment, and injustice.

[50:22] **La Grande Illusion and the First World War**

[事实] The seventh film is Jean Renoir’s La Grande Illusion, made in 1937 and looking back to the First World War.

[事实] It follows French prisoners escaping from German captivity.

[事实] The main prisoners include an aristocrat, a working-class engineer played by Jean Gabin, and a wealthy Jewish bourgeois character.

[事实] The guest calls it a humanist anti-war film about friendship across class and social divisions.

[52:20] **Class, Germans, and Wartime Memory**

[事实] The film includes a noble German officer and a French aristocrat who recognize each other across national lines through class culture.

[事实] The guest says the “good German” element was less controversial in 1937 but became harder to accept when the film was re-released in 1946.

[事实] The discussion compares this figure to similar noble German characters in British war stories.

[推测] La Grande Illusion is treated as a film about class solidarity as much as national conflict.

[56:08] **Le Corbeau and Poison-Pen France**

[事实] The eighth film is Henri-Georges Clouzot’s Le Corbeau, made during the Occupation in 1943.

[事实] It concerns a small town where anonymous poison-pen letters expose secrets such as adultery, abortion, illegitimacy, and other scandals.

[事实] “Corbeau” is explained as meaning both raven and blackmailer.

[事实] The film is based on a real earlier case of poison-pen letters in Tulle.

[59:00] **Délation and Occupation Allegory**

[事实] The guest links the film to délation, the practice of informing on neighbors during the Occupation.

[事实] The film contains no direct Gestapo or occupation setting, but its paranoia and community surveillance are described as internalized occupation behavior.

[事实] Informing is said to have been encouraged by both the Germans and Vichy.

[推测] The film’s power comes from making collaboration feel like a social disease rather than only a political act.

[61:00] **Why Le Corbeau Offended Everyone**

[事实] Vichy disliked the film because it attacked family values, Catholic morality, and idealized womanhood.

[事实] After Liberation, resistance and Free French voices criticized it as demoralizing or even collaborative because it portrayed France darkly.

[事实] The guest notes that a sexually transgressive female character is treated sympathetically, while a Vichy-style ideal woman is revealed as dangerous.

[推测] The film’s refusal to flatter any side explains why it remained controversial across changing political contexts.

[62:12] **La Grande Vadrouille as Occupation Comedy**

[事实] The ninth film is Gérard Oury’s La Grande Vadrouille, made in 1966.

[事实] Its story follows British airmen shot down over Paris and helped by two ordinary Frenchmen.

[事实] Terry-Thomas plays the chief British pilot, and Bourvil and Louis de Funès play the unlikely French helpers.

[事实] The Germans are described as comic, obtuse, and largely non-threatening.

[67:07] **Comedy, Healing, and Franco-British Tension**

[事实] The film is described as a relief after darker treatments of occupation and collaboration.

[事实] The discussion says it uses national stereotypes and resembles British memories of Allo Allo.

[事实] The guest connects the film to French gratitude toward, and resentment of, Britain and America for liberation.

[推测] Its comedy helps domesticate painful wartime memory by turning danger into farce.

[70:26] **La Chinoise and the Shadow of May 1968**

[事实] The tenth film is Jean-Luc Godard’s La Chinoise, released in 1967.

[事实] It follows mostly bourgeois French students in a Paris apartment forming a Maoist commune.

[事实] The film is described as prophetic of May 1968.

[事实] The guest connects it to the fragmentation of the French left after the Second World War and the Algerian War.

[72:00] **The Algerian War as an Oblique Absence**

[事实] The guest says the Algerian War remains a lightly treated subject in French cinema.

[事实] The Battle of Algiers is mentioned but not counted because the director was Italian.

[事实] Other films are noted as addressing Algeria obliquely rather than directly.

[推测] The list’s gap around Algeria reflects a broader difficulty in French cultural memory.

[73:00] **Godard, Maoism, and Dostoevsky**

[事实] La Chinoise is linked to Dostoevsky’s The Devils.

[事实] The film includes Mao’s Little Red Book, primary colors, slogans, and debates about revolution.

[事实] The guest says Godard is both supportive of and critical toward the student radicals.

[事实] A proletarian character, Yvonne, is shown repeatedly doing work while others debate revolutionary theory.

[推测] The film exposes the contradiction between bourgeois radical language and actual class relations inside the commune.

[78:01] **French Cinema After the Classic List**

[事实] The final discussion asks whether French cinema remains independent or is threatened by Hollywood and streaming.

[事实] The guest says everyone is threatened by US streaming services, but France has adapted by making French-language content for streaming.

[事实] Le Bureau des Légendes is cited as a strong French television example involving the secret service and international political settings.

[推测] The episode implies that French historical storytelling is shifting from cinema alone into serialized screen culture.

[79:18] **The One Film to Watch**

[事实] When asked to choose one film, the guest selects Les Enfants du Paradis.

[事实] She recommends it partly because people should watch more black-and-white films.

[事实] One host argues that Les Enfants du Paradis may be the best film on the list, while Ridicule may be the best film about history.

[推测] The final exchange positions Les Enfants du Paradis and Ridicule as the episode’s two strongest recommendations.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in how it turns a film list into a map of French memory. The films are not treated as illustrations but as arguments about nationhood, class, religion, war, collaboration, and political fantasy.

[推测] Its main strength is the balance between expert film criticism and comic conversation. The discussion remains accessible while still making serious claims about how cinema handles history.

[推测] The limits are also clear: the list leaps over large areas, especially early France and the Algerian War, and several choices require patience because they are long, old, or formally demanding.

[推测] It is best suited to listeners interested in French history, film history, and the politics of national memory, especially those looking for a curated watchlist rather than a conventional chronological lecture.
