# Hidden London: Newgate, Smithfield, and the History Underfoot

## 概览

This episode is a walking tour through a compact but heavily layered part of London around Newgate, Smithfield, St Bartholomew’s, the River Fleet, and Saffron Hill. The hosts focus on places where little visible evidence remains, but where prisons, executions, markets, monasteries, hospitals, fires, ghosts, and literature have left dense historical traces.

The core argument is that London’s past often survives less as grand visible monuments than as names, plaques, odd corners, rebuilt churches, hidden rivers, and remembered stories. The route repeatedly contrasts the banality of modern streets with the violence, faith, commerce, spectacle, and fiction associated with them.

The main walk ends by linking the hidden histories of real places to Dickens’s imagined London. A later promotional excerpt shifts briefly to the Spanish Civil War and Hemingway’s *For Whom the Bell Tolls*, but that is separate from the main London episode.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Introducing Hidden London**

[事实] The hosts begin near the Old Bailey and Newgate, describing the episode as a tour of “Hidden London” and the secrets of the city.
[事实] Tom explains that London is deeply historic, but many of its most important historic corners look ordinary or even banal today.
[事实] The walk grows out of lockdown-era themed walks through Roman, medieval, Tudor, ghostly, and Dickensian London.
[推测] The episode’s method is psychogeographical: ordinary streets become meaningful once their buried associations are recovered.

[02:18] **Newgate and the Roman Edge of the City**

[事实] The hosts identify the area as the site of Newgate, one of the gates in the Roman wall.
[事实] John Stow thought Newgate was built under Henry I or Stephen, but Tom says it was actually Roman.
[事实] Watling Street ran from this gate toward the west and the Cotswolds.
[事实] The area just outside the city wall is presented as especially rich in history.

[03:18] **Newgate Prison and Public Execution**

[事实] A prison stood at Newgate from the late 12th century until 1902.
[事实] The Old Bailey’s location preserves the connection between arrest, imprisonment, trial, and punishment.
[事实] The Viaduct Tavern is said to preserve former debtor cells in its basement, and a condemned man’s cell is mentioned nearby.
[事实] Before 1783, many executions took place at Tyburn; after that, executions moved to the Newgate area.
[推测] The hosts present Newgate as a place where legal authority, spectacle, fear, and urban memory overlap.

[05:17] **St Sepulchre and the Execution Bell**

[事实] St Sepulchre without Newgate stood outside the city wall and beside the execution site.
[事实] Dickens and Thackeray are mentioned as visitors to executions.
[事实] Inside the church there was an execution bell endowed in 1604, rung when an execution was due.
[事实] The bell was associated with verses urging condemned prisoners to repent before death.

[06:48] **A Victorian Drinking Fountain beside the Gallows**

[事实] The hosts stop at London’s oldest water fountain, described as the first Metropolitan public drinking fountain and later moved when the viaduct was built.
[事实] The fountain still has cups, but no water.
[事实] Tom links the fountain to Victorian public-service engineering and the temperance movement.
[推测] The juxtaposition of gallows and drinking fountain lets the hosts show Victorian London as both moralizing and reform-minded.

[08:03] **Giltspur Street, Smithfield, and the Dissolution**

[事实] The hosts walk north along Giltspur Street toward Smithfield.
[事实] Smithfield was a major open space where cattle were brought from across the country, and it remains a meat market.
[事实] St Bartholomew’s Priory stood nearby and was closed during the Reformation.
[事实] About ten monks who refused the oath of allegiance to Henry VIII were sent to Newgate and left to starve.
[事实] The Great Fire of 1666 destroyed much medieval fabric inside the city, making surviving monuments beyond its reach especially important.

[09:31] **The Golden Boy of Pye Corner and Body Snatchers**

[事实] The Golden Boy of Pye Corner marks the furthest point reached by the Great Fire.
[事实] Its inscription connected the fire to the sin of gluttony, in contrast to anti-Catholic explanations elsewhere.
[事实] The statue was once built into the Fortune of War pub, which was later demolished.
[事实] The Fortune of War is described as a meeting place for resurrectionists, where bodies were kept for surgeons at St Bartholomew’s.

[11:27] **Cock Lane Ghost**

[事实] Cock Lane is introduced as a famous ghost-haunted street.
[事实] In 1762, a poltergeist case there became one of London’s most notorious hauntings.
[事实] The supposed ghost claimed to be a murdered woman, and Dr Johnson investigated the case.
[事实] The haunting was eventually treated as a fraud.
[推测] The hosts use Cock Lane to underline the gap between London’s romantic memory and its drab present-day appearance.

[13:25] **Rahere, St Bartholomew’s Hospital, and the Priory**

[事实] St Bartholomew’s Priory was founded by Rahere, a courtier of Henry I who was sometimes said to have been the king’s jester.
[事实] Rahere became ill on pilgrimage to Rome, prayed, saw a vision of St Bartholomew, recovered, and vowed to found a priory and hospital.
[事实] Henry I gave Rahere land at Smithfield.
[事实] After the Reformation, Londoners petitioned Henry VIII to refound the hospital because they feared losing medical services provided by the monks.
[事实] The hospital still functions and bears St Bartholomew’s name.

[15:20] **Henry VIII, Sherlock Holmes, and the Hospital Entrance**

[事实] The hospital entrance was covered in scaffolding, but behind it stood what the hosts call London’s only statue of Henry VIII.
[事实] St Bartholomew’s is linked to William Harvey’s work on blood and to the first meeting of Sherlock Holmes and Dr Watson.
[事实] The Benedict Cumberbatch adaptation is mentioned as using St Bartholomew’s for Holmes’s apparent fall.
[推测] The hospital functions in the episode as both real institution and literary landmark.

[17:15] **St Bartholomew the Great**

[事实] The hosts pass through a Tudor gatehouse with a 13th-century arch beneath it.
[事实] St Bartholomew the Great preserves part of the medieval priory and is described as the oldest surviving parish church in London.
[事实] Inside, the hosts emphasize the contrast between Romanesque arches and early Gothic forms.
[事实] The church is said to be 900 years old in 2023.
[事实] The church appears in *Four Weddings and a Funeral* as the scene of the final wedding.

[20:21] **Hogarth and the Virgin Mary Apparition**

[事实] A font dating from 1404 is identified as the place where William Hogarth was baptized.
[事实] Hogarth is linked back to the Cock Lane Ghost because he was skeptical about it.
[事实] Tom says the church contains the only known London site where the Virgin Mary appeared.
[事实] The apparition is said to have happened around 1180, when Mary told a canon named Hubert that the monks were performing the liturgy incorrectly.
[推测] The story lets the hosts connect local space with medieval devotion and later skepticism.

[22:39] **The Lady Chapel, Benjamin Franklin, and Continuity**

[事实] The Lady Chapel was built in honor of the Virgin Mary’s appearance.
[事实] After the Reformation, the chapel was sold off and used as private accommodation and workshops.
[事实] In the 1720s it became a printer’s, and Benjamin Franklin worked there in 1724.
[事实] The same space also housed a dancing school.
[推测] Tom sees the chapel’s history as evidence of continuity between medieval Christianity and the Enlightenment rather than a clean break.

[24:51] **Rahere’s Tomb and the Church’s Screen Life**

[事实] The hosts point out an oriel window from which the prior could look down on the monks.
[事实] Rahere’s tomb is inside the church.
[事实] A ghost story claims that workmen opened the tomb in the 19th century and one stole Rahere’s sandal.
[事实] The hosts note that this type of stolen-relic ghost story appears in many versions.
[事实] The church is listed as appearing in films including *Shakespeare in Love*, *Elizabeth: The Golden Age*, *The Muppets Most Wanted*, *Avengers: Age of Ultron*, and *Transformers: The Last Knight*.

[29:52] **Smithfield and William Wallace**

[事实] After the break, the hosts stand by a memorial to William Wallace at Smithfield.
[事实] Smithfield meant “smooth field” and was a large open space beside medieval London.
[事实] Wallace was hanged, drawn, and quartered there.
[事实] Tom argues that Wallace’s patriotic cult was shaped by Scots confident in British identity but interested in preserving Scottish heroic memory.
[事实] The Wallace memorial was placed there in 1956 by Scots and friends at home and abroad.

[33:05] **Smithfield as Market, Fair, and Stage**

[事实] Smithfield’s slaughter and butchery created a powerful medieval stench, with waste swept toward the River Fleet.
[事实] Dickens places Mr Jaggers’s office from *Great Expectations* near Little Britain, close to the hospital and church.
[事实] St Bartholomew’s Fair was established by the priory in 1123.
[事实] Smithfield also hosted tournaments, including one under Richard II that Chaucer helped organize.

[34:52] **The Peasants’ Revolt and Radical Memory**

[事实] During the Peasants’ Revolt, rebels gathered at Smithfield.
[事实] Wat Tyler was struck down there by the Lord Mayor of London during the confrontation with Richard II.
[事实] A modern plaque commemorates the peasants who took part in the revolt.
[事实] The plaque includes a Thomas Paine quotation comparing Tyler’s claim to commemoration with the barons at Runnymede.
[推测] The hosts suggest that later radical memory matters historically even when it simplifies or reshapes the original event.

[36:42] **Protestant Martyrs, Bloody Mary, and Charterhouse**

[事实] A Protestant Alliance memorial from 1870 commemorates Protestants burned at Smithfield under Mary I.
[事实] The hosts say these burnings contributed to Mary’s nickname “Bloody Mary” and shaped Protestant imagination through works such as Foxe’s *Book of Martyrs*.
[事实] They also note that Mary’s reputation might have been different if Catholicism had been restored permanently.
[事实] In 1849, workers digging a sewer reportedly found blackened stones, ashes, and human bones that an antiquary linked to the Protestant martyrs.
[事实] Nearby Charterhouse Green is described as an original plague pit from the Black Death, later associated with a Carthusian priory and Charterhouse School.

[40:18] **Modern Quiet and the Lost River Fleet**

[事实] The hosts contrast the quiet, office-heavy modern district with its historically crowded and violent past.
[事实] They walk downhill toward the valley of the River Fleet, one of London’s most famous lost rivers.
[事实] The Fleet gave its name to Fleet Street and was gradually covered over from the 17th to 19th centuries.
[事实] The meat market’s location is linked to the ability to sweep animal waste into the river.
[事实] Jonathan Swift is cited as describing the Fleet as full of butchers’ waste, mud, dead animals, and refuse.

[43:43] **Saffron Hill and Dickens’s Oliver Twist**

[事实] The hosts cross the buried Fleet and head to Saffron Hill.
[事实] Saffron Hill is connected to the Bishop of Ely’s land, where saffron once grew.
[事实] Tom identifies the area as the location of Fagin’s lair in *Oliver Twist*.
[事实] Dominic reads Dickens’s description of a narrow, muddy, foul-smelling street full of poverty, disorder, and public houses.
[事实] The pub now called the One Tun is identified as the Three Cripples, associated with Fagin and Bill Sikes.

[48:04] **The Closing Lesson of the Walk**

[事实] The hosts say there is little obvious to see at Saffron Hill unless one knows the history.
[事实] Tom argues that knowing what happened in a place makes the past seem to rise from tarmac and brickwork.
[事实] The episode ends as rain begins and the hosts leave for a drink.
[推测] The closing point is that hidden urban history depends on interpretation as much as preservation.

[49:43] **Bonus Clip on the Spanish Civil War**

[事实] Dominic introduces a clip from *The Book Club* about Hemingway’s *For Whom the Bell Tolls* and the Spanish Civil War.
[事实] He says plans are being made for *The Rest Is History* to cover the Spanish Civil War in 2027.
[事实] The clip describes Spain before the war as politically turbulent, socially unequal, regionally divided, and polarized between left and right.
[事实] In 1936, a Popular Front government came to power, and right-wing army elements launched a coup in July.
[事实] The coup did not fully succeed, leaving Nationalists and Republicans divided across the country, with foreign backing from Italy, Germany, the Soviet Union, and volunteers.
[事实] The war is described as bitter, bloody, and marked by massacres and mass graves.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is its ability to make unremarkable streets feel historically charged. The hosts move quickly, but the repeated links among law, religion, violence, markets, literature, and urban infrastructure give the walk a strong narrative shape.

[推测] Its main limitation is that some claims are presented as entertaining stories rather than fully tested historical arguments, especially ghost stories, relic tales, and local antiquarian discoveries. The hosts often acknowledge this uncertainty through humor and skepticism.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who enjoy London history, Dickens, medieval and early modern religious conflict, and the way cities preserve memory in street names, plaques, and overlooked spaces.
