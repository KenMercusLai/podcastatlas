# Everything Comes from Persia? Iran’s Underestimated Civilizational Influence

## 概览

This episode argues that Persia, or Iran, has played a far larger role in world history than many Western audiences recognize. Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook frame Persia as an “underground spring” feeding many later civilizations, while Ali Ansari explains why Iran’s image is often narrowed by the 1979 Islamic Revolution.

The discussion first clarifies that “Iran” and “Persia” refer to the same continuous civilizational tradition, though the terms come from different historical perspectives. Ansari then outlines the Achaemenid, Parthian, and Sasanian empires as major pre-Islamic Iranian powers.

The second half becomes a playful but historically grounded list of Persian contributions: empire, historical consciousness, religious ideas, paradise gardens, equestrian culture, backgammon, fashion, bureaucracy, and possibly even spaghetti and algebra. Several claims are presented humorously or cautiously rather than as settled facts.

## 分段落总结

[00:33] **Persia as a Hidden Source of Modern Civilization**

[事实] The episode opens with Herodotus’s claim that Persian boys were taught riding, archery, and truth-telling.

[事实] Tom says the episode’s thesis is that Persia taught and contributed far more than this, and that many things taken for granted in the modern West ultimately derive from Persia.

[事实] Dominic connects modern perceptions of Iran to the 1979 Iranian Revolution and the rise of the Ayatollahs.

[推测] The hosts frame the episode as a corrective to a Western habit of foregrounding Greece, Rome, Egypt, Russia, and China while overlooking Iran.

[03:01] **Ali Ansari and the Underrated Iranian Contribution**

[事实] Ali Ansari is introduced as a professor of Iranian studies at St Andrews University and author of a short introduction to Iran.

[事实] Ansari says Iranian or Persian contributions to human civilization have been underrated and largely ignored.

[事实] He agrees that the Islamic Revolution of 1979 has strongly colored Western understanding of Iran.

[推测] Ansari’s role is to move the conversation from modern stereotypes of Iran toward a longer civilizational history.

[04:00] **Iran and Persia as the Same Historical Continuum**

[事实] Ansari argues that Iran and Persia are the same thing in the Western historical record, though the terms have different origins.

[事实] He says “Iran” is the indigenous name for the country, territory, or culture, while “Persia” is a Greek and Roman rendering tied to a particular Iranian people or tribe.

[事实] Ansari cites an attribution to Darius the Great: Achaemenid by family, Persian by tribe, and Iranian by people.

[事实] He rejects the idea that Iran was invented in the twentieth century and says Western travelers from the seventeenth century onward recognized that Persians called themselves Iranians.

[06:00] **Language, Geography, and Distinction from the Arab Middle East**

[事实] The speakers emphasize that Persia is distinct from the Arab Middle East in architecture and language.

[事实] Ansari says Persian is an Indo-European language and gives examples linking Persian words for mother, father, and brother to European languages.

[事实] Iran is described as a great upland plateau, with the Zagros Mountains separating it from the Mesopotamian flatlands.

[推测] The discussion pushes back against treating the Middle East as a single undifferentiated cultural zone.

[08:00] **Three Pre-Islamic Persian Empires**

[事实] Ansari identifies three major pre-Islamic Iranian empires: the Achaemenid Persian Empire, the Parthians, and the Sasanians.

[事实] The Achaemenid Empire is described as stretching from Greece to the Indus and down to Egypt, and as the empire known through Cyrus, Darius, Xerxes, the Bible, and classical sources.

[事实] The Parthians are described as a long-lasting, decentralized dynasty that contested power with the Roman Republic and Roman Empire.

[事实] Ansari says the Sasanians were probably the most influential pre-Islamic Persian empire for Iran, compiling historical records and giving the name Iran to the political territory.

[10:35] **Iran as an Early Territorial Political Unit**

[事实] Ansari says there is a case that Iran is one of the first territorially defined political units with continuity from antiquity to the present.

[事实] He says Iranian nationalists often promote the idea of Iran as the first kingdom or first dominion of man over nature.

[事实] He mentions an early twentieth-century nationalist historiographer who calculated Iran’s kingdom through the Book of Kings as going back 10 billion years.

[推测] The hosts use the exaggerated chronology humorously to show both the power and absurdity of some nationalist origin stories.

[12:50] **Rupture, Islam, and Persian Cultural Absorption**

[事实] Dominic asks whether the Arab conquest and introduction of Islam created a radical rupture or continuity in Iranian culture.

[事实] Ansari says Iranian history includes major ruptures from Alexander, the Arab Muslim conquest, and the Mongol invasions.

[事实] He argues that in each case Persian cultural confidence reasserted itself.

[事实] Ansari says Iranians helped turn Islam from an Arab religion into a global universal religion in which non-Arabs were equally valid.

[事实] He also says Iran absorbed the Mongols into Persian culture, unlike China, which expelled them.

[15:01] **Persia as “Midwife to the West”**

[事实] Ansari says Persia is the “midwife to the West” and that there would be no West without the Persians.

[事实] He begins a list of major Persian contributions by citing Hegel’s claim that history begins with the Persians.

[事实] Ansari connects Persia with the idea of the first kingdom, the first dominion of man over nature, and a territorially delimited state.

[推测] His argument is not just that Persia influenced later civilizations, but that it helped define the historical contrast through which the West imagined itself.

[17:25] **Cyrus, Universal Empire, and Historical Memory**

[事实] Ansari says Cyrus the Great is popularly understood as the father of the nation who bound the Medes and Persians into the first Iranian empire.

[事实] Tom adds that Persian imperial rule was formulated as universal and moral, reflecting the pattern of the universe.

[事实] Tom notes that Cyrus is praised by conquered peoples, including Greeks and Jews, and is called the anointed one by Isaiah.

[事实] Ansari says Persian historical narratives are often mythological or folkloric rather than historical in the modern disciplinary sense.

[20:30] **Zoroastrianism and Religious Ideas**

[事实] Ansari identifies Zoroastrianism as the pre-Islamic religion of Persia or Iran.

[事实] He says Zoroastrianism contributed ideas such as the end of times and a spiritual redeemer.

[事实] The discussion links Zoroastrian themes to later Jewish and Christian texts, especially Daniel and Revelation.

[事实] Tom highlights the Persian emphasis on truth and its opposite, the lie, along with a worldview divided between good and evil, light and darkness.

[推测] The episode treats Persian religious influence as significant but stops short of saying Judaism, Christianity, or Islam were simply Persian creations.

[25:03] **Paradise, Gardens, and the Natural World**

[事实] After the break, Ansari says the Persians invented paradise, specifically the idea of a planned walled garden.

[事实] He associates this with Cyrus the Great and a walled garden at Pasargadae in southern Iran.

[事实] Ansari says Persians showed a deep affection for the natural world, gardens, order, and beauty.

[事实] The speakers discuss Persian gardens as cool refuges shaped by irrigation, water features, architecture, and vegetation.

[事实] Ansari confirms that the word “paradise” comes from Persian through Greek.

[30:51] **Leisure, Horses, and Courtly Culture**

[事实] Ansari jokes that he would like to claim cricket for Persia but says he has no evidence.

[事实] The discussion mentions polo and the importance of horses in Persian culture.

[事实] Ansari says the Sasanians and Parthians developed heavily armored mounted warriors, or cataphracts.

[事实] He says there is an argument that the stirrup came from Central Asia into Iran and that Iranians operationalized it, though he treats the issue as controversial.

[事实] Ansari links Persian epic poetry with knightly and courtly themes.

[33:03] **Backgammon, Chess, and Spaghetti**

[事实] Ansari says Iranians can claim backgammon.

[事实] He says chess came from India, though Iranians probably refined the rules and made it workable.

[事实] Ansari makes a humorous claim for spaghetti, arguing that noodles may have come through the Mongol Empire into Iran before being taken to northern Italy by Marco Polo.

[推测] The spaghetti claim is presented as a playful, debatable bid rather than a fully established historical conclusion.

[34:41] **Alexander, Political Collapse, and Decadence**

[事实] Dominic asks why the Persians lost to Alexander if they were so formidable.

[事实] Ansari answers that the heavily armored knights developed after Alexander and that Iranian states often collapsed for political rather than military reasons.

[事实] He cites divisions of command and internal backstabbing as reasons for collapse.

[事实] Ansari and the hosts discuss decadence, including Gibbon’s idea that Persians became over-civilized.

[推测] The segment uses humor to balance admiration for Persian civilization with the problem of explaining its defeats.

[36:24] **High Heels, Coats, and Trousers**

[事实] Ansari says high heels are connected to Persian cavalry culture because they helped riders sit in the saddle with stirrups.

[事实] He says seventeenth-century European travelers to Iran returned with fashion influences, and Charles II is mentioned as wearing extravagant high heels.

[事实] Ansari traces the Persian coat to John Evelyn’s diaries and says it was absorbed by Louis XIV’s court and other European courts.

[事实] The speakers describe the modern suit jacket and trousers as having Persian connections.

[推测] The discussion frames everyday Western formal clothing as another example of unacknowledged Persian influence.

[38:52] **British India and Persian Bureaucratic Influence**

[事实] Tom suggests that the British Raj was highly Persian, especially through Lord Curzon’s admiration for Persia and Persepolis.

[事实] Ansari says the British took over administration from the Mughal Empire, whose administration was based on Persian models and used Persian as its language.

[事实] He says Indian Civil Service officials had to learn Persian at least until 1857.

[事实] Ansari argues that the modern civil service, through the Indian Civil Service, borrowed ideas and attitudes from traditional Persian administration.

[事实] He explains that “purdah” comes from a Persian word meaning curtain or veil and connects it to the civil service withdrawing during elections.

[42:16] **Science, Algebra, and the Closing Joke**

[事实] Near the end, the speakers joke that time is also a Persian idea.

[事实] Ansari says he did not have time to discuss science and astronomy, though he considers them important.

[事实] He adds algebra to the list and says the relevant figure may have written in Arabic but was really Persian.

[事实] Dominic says the algebra claim does not sound immensely convincing to him, but lets it go.

[推测] The ending keeps the episode’s central joke intact: almost everything can be rhetorically claimed as Persian, even when some claims are stronger than others.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value lies in making Iranian history feel central rather than peripheral. It uses humor and exaggeration, but the underlying point is serious: Persia has shaped empire, religion, language, administration, gardens, fashion, and historical imagination in ways often overlooked.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the explanations of Iran/Persia terminology, the pre-Islamic empires, Zoroastrian influence, and Persianate administration in British India. These claims are grounded in the transcript with clearer historical framing than the more playful claims about spaghetti or algebra.

[推测] The limitation is that the “top 10” format encourages quick leaps across very different topics, so some arguments are asserted rather than fully demonstrated. Listeners looking for detailed evidence on each claim may find the episode more suggestive than conclusive.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in ancient history, Iran, empire, religion, and the hidden pathways by which cultures influence one another.
