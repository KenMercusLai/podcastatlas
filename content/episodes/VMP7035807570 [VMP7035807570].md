# How Good Culture Made Cottage Cheese Cool Again

## 概览

Jesse Merrill’s path to founding Good Culture began far from the dairy aisle. After early work in film production, he moved into experiential marketing and learned brand building through Teany, Honest Tea, and Activate Drinks. Those roles taught him how to create emotional connections with consumers, win retail distribution, and translate a product’s benefits into a compelling story.

Good Culture emerged from an overlooked opportunity: cottage cheese offered high protein and low sugar, yet the category had declined because of limited innovation, dated branding, and products often perceived as watery or overly processed. Merrill and co-founder Anders Eisner reframed it as a clean-label, nutrient-dense food aligned with major consumer trends.

The company survived its early years through careful retail expansion, intensive in-store sampling, packaging changes, and relentless attention to sales velocity. Years of influencer outreach then positioned Good Culture to benefit disproportionately when a cottage-cheese ice cream recipe went viral on TikTok, introducing the category to Gen Z and accelerating demand beyond available manufacturing capacity.

By the end of 2025, Good Culture had surpassed $200 million in annual revenue. A majority investment from L Catterton gave the company an experienced partner for its next stage, while Merrill remained focused on supply, innovation, financial discipline, and the ambition to build a billion-dollar household brand.

## 分段落总结

[04:34] **From Film Production to Experiential Marketing**

[事实] Merrill initially hoped to become a filmmaker and worked as an office production assistant for director Jonathan Demme before moving to Los Angeles to work around Spike Jonze and the production of *Jackass*.

[事实] Because production-assistant work paid very little, he began taking brand-ambassador jobs, distributing samples and helping execute experiential campaigns for products including KY and Amstel Light.

[事实] He advanced from sampler to manager and became increasingly interested in how creative marketing could build an emotional connection between consumers and brands.

[推测] These early jobs gave Merrill a practical, street-level education in consumer behavior that later shaped Good Culture’s sampling-heavy launch strategy.

[10:09] **Learning Brand Building with Moby’s Teany**

[事实] A connection made during a Nokia campaign introduced Merrill to musician Moby, who was developing a lightly sweetened bottled tea called Teany.

[事实] Merrill was hired to spread the brand across metropolitan New York through unconventional distribution and lifestyle-oriented marketing.

[事实] Because Teany had a small team, he worked not only in marketing but also in sales, operations, supply chain, procurement, and finance.

[事实] Merrill came to see brand building as analogous to filmmaking: both require a strong story that resonates emotionally with a broad audience.

[16:30] **Scaling Honest Tea**

[事实] After Teany was sold, Honest Tea recruited Merrill to expand its presence in New York, where the two brands had previously competed for shelf space.

[事实] He joined after meeting co-founder Seth Goldman, whose mission-led approach, responsible sourcing, and personal authenticity strongly appealed to him.

[事实] Merrill used grassroots sampling at art galleries, marathons, music events, and other lifestyle destinations to attract younger consumers.

[事实] He later became director of marketing, helped launch Honest Kids, and participated in growing Honest Tea to roughly $75–80 million in revenue before its 2011 sale to Coca-Cola.

[22:07] **Activate Drinks and the Cost of Product Friction**

[事实] Merrill next joined Activate Drinks, an enhanced-water startup whose proprietary cap released powdered vitamins into water when twisted.

[事实] The package required consumers to twist the cap, shake the bottle, and twist it again, forcing the company to spend heavily on usage education.

[事实] Activate reached roughly $5 million in revenue but did not achieve the rapid growth expected by its investors; after Tata declined to invest in another round, the company was sold.

[事实] Merrill believed a slower approach—with fewer hires, stronger fundamentals, and more disciplined spending—might have given the product more time to find its rhythm.

[推测] The experience reinforced that strong marketing cannot fully compensate for product complexity or weak product-market fit.

[27:07] **A Personal Health Crisis Changes His View of Food**

[事实] While Good Culture was being developed, Merrill experienced severe symptoms and was diagnosed with ulcerative colitis in 2015.

[事实] He briefly took prescribed medication but disliked its effects, then worked with a functional doctor and adopted an autoimmune paleo diet that eliminated numerous foods.

[事实] Merrill said his symptoms began improving within roughly a week to a week and a half, and that he now primarily manages his condition through food while keeping medication available for a serious flare.

[推测] The experience deepened his personal commitment to simple ingredients, although the interview explicitly notes that it was not the original catalyst for choosing cottage cheese.

[36:02] **Finding an Overlooked Opportunity in Cottage Cheese**

[事实] The initial idea arose while Merrill’s pregnant wife was eating cottage cheese and he noticed that it contained more protein and less sugar than Greek yogurt.

[事实] His category research showed that cottage cheese had once been larger than yogurt but declined after decades of limited innovation.

[事实] Merrill and Eisner saw that cottage cheese already aligned with trends including high protein, low sugar, gut health, simple ingredients, and mission-led brands.

[事实] Despite the category’s decline and aging customer base, they believed the food could be reframed and presented in a way that appealed to contemporary consumers.

[推测] Their core insight was not to invent a new nutritional proposition, but to modernize an existing food whose benefits had been poorly communicated.

[39:40] **Developing a Thicker, Cleaner Product**

[事实] Merrill bought competing cottage cheeses, strained their curds, and experimented in his kitchen to create a thicker, creamier texture that ate more like yogurt.

[事实] He tested fruit and savory flavors, including strawberry açaí chia, blueberry açaí chia, Kalamata olive, and sun-dried tomato.

[事实] Finding a manufacturer was difficult because cottage cheese was declining, established plants wanted large minimum orders, and many lacked organic, clean-label, single-serve, or fruit-on-the-bottom capabilities.

[事实] A Wisconsin co-packer agreed to collaborate, but successful laboratory samples initially turned soupy when processed on full-scale equipment.

[事实] For Good Culture, “clean label” meant a short recipe of milk, cream, salt, and cultures rather than gums, starches, or chemical preservatives.

[45:43] **Structuring the Partnership and Defining the Brand**

[事实] Eisner supplied the initial capital and became co-founder and board chair, while Merrill became the active CEO responsible for day-to-day operations.

[事实] The name Good Culture was designed as a double meaning: good-tasting products made responsibly, and the live cultures within the fermented food.

[事实] Merrill said the company also wanted to build a healthy culture for employees, farmers, partners, people, animals, and the planet.

[事实] At Expo West in March 2015, the founders treated retailer reaction as a decisive market test and were prepared to pivot if buyers showed little interest.

[事实] Whole Foods, Sprouts, and other retailers responded positively, validating the idea that cottage cheese was ready for innovation.

[49:32] **Winning Shelf Space One Store at a Time**

[事实] Good Culture initially entered 55 Whole Foods stores and all Sprouts locations rather than attempting an immediate nationwide Whole Foods rollout.

[事实] Early packaging looked so much like yogurt that some shoppers failed to recognize the product as cottage cheese, leading the company to enlarge the category name and add an image of a cottage-cheese bowl.

[事实] In-store sampling became essential because many shoppers did not normally buy cottage cheese, but often responded positively after tasting Good Culture.

[事实] Whole Foods required sales of roughly eight to ten units per store each week, and unsold products during the initial reclamation period had to be bought back by the company.

[事实] With a shelf life of about 45 days—and often only 30 days remaining upon store arrival—Merrill closely monitored expiration dates and dispatched brand ambassadors to stores where inventory was moving too slowly.

[推测] The deliberately narrow rollout allowed the company to apply labor-intensive tactics that would have been impossible at national scale.

[57:37] **From $2 Million to $70 Million**

[事实] Good Culture generated about $2 million in revenue in 2016 and received authorization to expand nationally across Whole Foods.

[事实] By 2021, revenue had reached approximately $70 million, supported by broader distribution through retailers including Kroger and Walmart.

[事实] Growth came from a combination of social media, public relations, coupons, shopper marketing, and relationships with micro- and macro-influencers.

[事实] The company prioritized influencers who already consumed the product authentically, supplying VIP kits and occasionally paying for partnerships.

[推测] Its marketing system joined long-term brand awareness with immediate retail conversion instead of relying on a single breakthrough campaign.

[61:50] **The TikTok Moment That Transformed the Category**

[事实] After years of influencer seeding, health influencer Lainey Cate posted a recipe for high-protein ice cream made with Good Culture cottage cheese, honey, peanut butter, and chocolate chips.

[事实] Other creators replicated and extended the idea into flatbreads, bagels, smoothies, eggs, and pasta dishes, causing the trend to snowball rather than disappear.

[事实] The viral content introduced Good Culture to Gen Z, who encountered cottage cheese as an aspirational social-media food rather than an outdated category requiring rehabilitation.

[事实] Good Culture appeared in the original video and in a disproportionate share of the content that followed.

[推测] The virality was partly fortunate, but years of influencer relationships, distribution growth, and brand positioning left Good Culture unusually prepared to capture it.

[65:02] **Explosive Demand Meets Limited Manufacturing Capacity**

[事实] Cottage-cheese demand accelerated so quickly that producers lacked sufficient manufacturing capacity, prompting several companies to invest in additional facilities.

[事实] Merrill said the US category had grown to approximately $2.4 billion, while per-capita consumption had risen from roughly 1.4–1.5 pounds when Good Culture launched to about 2.4–2.5 pounds.

[事实] Good Culture surpassed $200 million in annual revenue by the end of 2025, but Merrill said the figure reflected available capacity rather than total demand.

[事实] At the time of the interview, the company was servicing less than 50% of demand and was ramping dedicated facilities while adding an experienced chief operating officer.

[事实] Competitors also benefited from the category’s revival, while new competitive interest emerged after years in which established players had largely ignored Good Culture.

[68:50] **Choosing an Experienced Growth Partner**

[事实] In 2025, Good Culture explored a strategic partnership and ultimately selected L Catterton, which acquired a majority stake in a transaction that closed in 2026.

[事实] Merrill said the profitable company did not primarily need private-equity funding and could have used debt or other financing for its supply investments.

[事实] The main objective was to gain a partner with experience, operational rigor, and a track record of scaling businesses through similar challenges.

[事实] Merrill remained CEO and continued to focus on unit economics, profitability, brand, innovation, and supply chain, while gaining more time for commercial priorities.

[74:14] **The Billion-Dollar Ambition and the Role of Grit**

[事实] Merrill’s stated goal is to turn Good Culture into a billion-dollar-plus business and a household name.

[事实] He attributed the company’s success to both fortune and hard work, but placed greater emphasis on perseverance after years of steady rather than spectacular growth.

[事实] Before the viral breakthrough, the business posted strong double-digit annual growth but still faced repeated existential concerns.

[事实] Merrill described virality as “the right message meeting the right moment,” arguing that repeated effort positioned the company to benefit when that moment arrived.

[推测] The story presents persistence and preparation as the controllable foundations of success, with virality acting as an accelerator rather than a substitute for them.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode is most valuable as a practical case study in reviving a neglected category. It shows how product reformulation, positioning, retail execution, packaging clarity, and consumer education can work together to change perceptions of a familiar food.

[推测] Its strongest material concerns the unglamorous mechanics of consumer-goods growth: minimum manufacturing volumes, short shelf life, retailer velocity requirements, sampling costs, supply constraints, and the danger of expanding faster than operations can support.

[推测] The discussion of Merrill’s ulcerative colitis is a personal account rather than broadly applicable medical guidance, and the interview offers limited independent scrutiny of the health, sustainability, or “clean label” claims surrounding the brand.

[推测] The episode is especially relevant to founders, marketers, and operators in food or consumer packaged goods, as well as listeners interested in how patient brand building can create the conditions for an apparently sudden viral breakthrough.
