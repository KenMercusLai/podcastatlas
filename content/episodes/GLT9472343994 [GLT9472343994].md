# The Fall of the Soviet Union, 1987-1991

## 概览

This episode follows the final phase of the Soviet Union’s collapse, beginning around the 70th anniversary of the Russian Revolution in 1987 and ending with Gorbachev’s resignation on 25 December 1991.

The central thread is the rivalry between Mikhail Gorbachev and Boris Yeltsin. The hosts present their personal conflict as a major force in the Soviet endgame, alongside economic breakdown, nationalist movements, failed reforms, and the weakening authority of the Communist Party.

The discussion argues that the USSR did not simply fall because of mass street revolution. Instead, its breakup is framed as the result of elite rivalries, republican leaders protecting their own power, institutional paralysis, and a failed hardline coup that handed Yeltsin his defining political moment.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **1987 and the mood inside the USSR**

[事实] The episode opens with the Soviet Union in 1987, the 70th anniversary of the Russian Revolution.

[事实] Gorbachev is described as caught between reformers who want faster change and hardliners who resist it.

[事实] The hosts say things on the ground are not going well, with reform creating political and economic strain.

[01:22] **Yeltsin enters the story**

[事实] Boris Yeltsin is introduced as a very different figure from Gorbachev: populist, impulsive, forceful, and good with crowds.

[事实] Gorbachev had brought Yeltsin from Sverdlovsk, now Yekaterinburg, into national politics.

[推测] The episode frames the Gorbachev-Yeltsin relationship as unusually important because their personal hostility shaped state-level outcomes.

[03:01] **Yeltsin’s humiliation and revenge motive**

[事实] Yeltsin became the first person to resign from the Politburo.

[事实] After being attacked by hardliners, he attempted suicide by stabbing himself in the chest with scissors, according to the transcript.

[事实] Gorbachev forced Yeltsin to attend a Moscow party meeting where he was publicly humiliated.

[推测] The hosts treat this humiliation as a turning point that made Yeltsin determined to defeat Gorbachev.

[04:11] **Failed reform and Gorbachev’s halfway house**

[事实] The economic reforms are described as failing, with longer queues, shortages in shops, and unrest in the republics.

[事实] The hosts say Gorbachev ended up between systems, without the benefits of either full reform or firm control.

[事实] Gorbachev is described as reluctant to use violence, although troops were used in Georgia and Lithuania.

[推测] His unwillingness to rely on repression weakened the Soviet state’s ability to hold itself together, but also distinguished him from more ruthless Soviet leaders.

[05:39] **The Congress of People’s Deputies**

[事实] Gorbachev launched plans in 1988 for the Congress of People’s Deputies, with about 2,250 members.

[事实] The Congress would choose a Supreme Soviet, and Gorbachev would be personally elected president rather than only serving as general secretary.

[事实] The hosts describe this as a way to bypass the Communist Party while introducing a real democratic element.

[事实] Figures such as Andrei Sakharov, Baltic nationalists, and Yeltsin entered this new political space.

[06:42] **Politics on television and public confusion**

[事实] The Congress was broadcast on television, exposing Soviet citizens to open political argument in a way they had not seen before.

[事实] Deputies publicly discussed shortages, failures, and disagreements over policy.

[事实] Some people welcomed the openness, while others were confused by it.

[推测] Televised political argument made the Soviet system look less stable because it revealed conflict that had previously been hidden.

[07:41] **Shortages and historical truth**

[事实] By 1989, much of Russia had meat rationing, no sugar, and failing supply lines between producers and urban shops.

[事实] At the same time, works such as Solzhenitsyn’s *The Gulag Archipelago* became readable in the Soviet Union.

[事实] The hosts say many citizens cared more about shortages than new access to dissident literature.

[推测] Glasnost created moral and historical upheaval, but daily scarcity made many people judge reform through material failure.

[08:32] **Stalin, memory, and backlash**

[事实] The episode discusses Nina Andreeva’s 1988 article defending Stalin and criticizing attacks on Soviet history.

[事实] Gorbachev’s preferred historical line is described as returning to Lenin while treating Stalin as the figure who distorted the revolution.

[事实] The hosts say many people were upset by being told that much of what they had believed was false.

[推测] The reform of historical memory destabilized Soviet identity as much as formal political reform did.

[09:37] **Eastern Europe and Putin in Dresden**

[事实] The hosts say there was limited Soviet reaction when the Berlin Wall fell and Eastern Europe left the Soviet orbit.

[事实] Reasons given include Gorbachev’s reluctance to use force, his idealism about socialism, and the scale of domestic Soviet problems.

[事实] Putin is described as being in Dresden, burning files and experiencing Moscow’s silence during the crisis.

[事实] The episode says Putin later regarded Nicholas II and Gorbachev as weak leaders, while admiring Peter the Great and Stalin.

[11:00] **1990 and the rise of republican nationalism**

[事实] By 1990, the Warsaw Pact had drifted away, the Soviet Union was moving toward multi-party politics, and industry was collapsing.

[事实] Reform-minded allies were abandoning Gorbachev because they thought he was not moving fast enough.

[事实] Party elites and state-enterprise managers in the republics began using nationalism to protect their own power.

[推测] Nationalism in this account was not only ideological; it was also a tool for local elites to escape Moscow’s weakening authority.

[12:00] **Yeltsin makes Russia a rival power center**

[事实] In spring 1990, Yeltsin became the elected leader of Russia.

[事实] Russia represented more than half of the Soviet population.

[事实] Yeltsin established Russia as a counterweight to the Soviet Union, creating a sense of parallel governments with different advisers and economic programs.

[推测] The Soviet state became harder to govern once Russia itself acted like a competing sovereign authority.

[13:00] **Vilnius and the new Union Treaty**

[事实] In Vilnius, Gorbachev was persuaded to use troops against the TV station, and 15 people were killed.

[事实] Yeltsin opposed violence and told Russian troops in the Baltic states not to use force.

[事实] Gorbachev proposed a new Union Treaty to replace the 1922 arrangement with a more decentralized Soviet model.

[推测] The treaty was an attempt to preserve the Soviet Union after the old constitutional and political basis had already weakened.

[14:02] **The economic collapse and the missed bailout**

[事实] In summer 1991, industry was said to have collapsed by 18% and agriculture by 17%.

[事实] The Soviet Union was running a massive deficit and could no longer afford to import consumer goods.

[事实] Gregory Levinsky and Graham Allison proposed a Western-funded rescue plan, but George H. W. Bush’s administration rejected it.

[推测] The hosts suggest such a bailout probably would not have saved Gorbachev, though it might have softened Russia’s transition.

[15:54] **Yeltsin, Russia, and the republics**

[事实] The hosts discuss whether Yeltsin thought Russia would be better off without poorer or more difficult Soviet republics.

[事实] They note that around 25 million Russian speakers lived outside Russia, creating a long-term problem.

[事实] Yeltsin is described as more fixated on defeating Gorbachev than on strategically planning Russia’s future.

[推测] This lack of long-term strategic thinking helped leave unresolved questions about borders, identity, and Russian-speaking minorities.

[17:26] **The August 1991 coup**

[事实] Hardline communists launched a coup in August 1991 while Gorbachev was on holiday in Crimea.

[事实] They cut off Gorbachev’s communications and tried to force him to resign.

[事实] The coup failed to arrest Yeltsin, control communications, or control the streets.

[事实] Yeltsin climbed onto a tank outside the Russian White House and denounced the coup.

[推测] The coup unintentionally gave Yeltsin the heroic public role he needed to displace Gorbachev.

[20:39] **Yeltsin turns the tables on Gorbachev**

[事实] After the coup failed, Gorbachev returned from Crimea.

[事实] Yeltsin publicly humiliated Gorbachev by making him read out the names of aides and appointees who had collaborated with the coup.

[事实] Yeltsin banned the Communist Party in Russia and announced a move toward a market economy.

[推测] The failed coup shifted momentum decisively from the Soviet presidency to the Russian presidency.

[21:53] **Ukraine, Belarus, and the elite breakup of the USSR**

[事实] Ukrainian leader Leonid Kravchuk declared Ukrainian sovereignty and called a referendum for 1 December 1991.

[事实] The hosts describe Kravchuk as an apparatchik who moved onto the Ukrainian nationalist path to preserve power and control.

[事实] Yeltsin, the Ukrainian leader, and the Belarusian leader met at Belavezha and agreed to independence and a loose Commonwealth of Independent States.

[事实] The episode says only about 20% of Soviet citizens wanted the USSR dissolved, while 80% did not.

[推测] The hosts present the Soviet breakup as driven more by elite self-protection and rivalry than by overwhelming public demand.

[24:42] **Crimea, Donbass, and Yeltsin’s priorities**

[事实] People around Yeltsin reportedly asked whether he would raise Crimea and Donbass in December 1991.

[事实] The episode says Yeltsin did not raise those issues because he was focused on defeating Gorbachev.

[事实] The hosts also mention drinking around the Belavezha meeting, while cautioning against reducing Yeltsin to a stereotype.

[推测] Later territorial conflicts are presented as partly rooted in questions left unresolved at the moment of Soviet dissolution.

[25:39] **Gorbachev’s final loss of authority**

[事实] After the Belavezha agreement, the other republics also moved away from the Soviet Union.

[事实] Gorbachev was left in the Kremlin with the army and interior ministry no longer listening to him.

[事实] On 25 December 1991, Gorbachev resigned as president of the USSR.

[事实] The Soviet flag came down and was replaced by the Russian tricolor.

[27:20] **Russia after empire**

[事实] Putin watched the collapse with horror and later called it the greatest geopolitical catastrophe of the century.

[事实] The 15 Soviet republics went their own ways.

[事实] Russia emerged as a state with many federal subjects and many ethnic groups, including Chechens, Tatars, Tuvans, and others.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that Russia had lost status and had many Russian or Russian-speaking people outside its borders.

[推测] The episode presents the post-Soviet Russian problem as a mixture of wounded status, imperial inheritance, and unresolved national identity.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it makes the Soviet collapse feel political and human rather than inevitable. The Gorbachev-Yeltsin rivalry gives the story a clear dramatic spine, while the economic and constitutional details explain why personal conflict mattered so much.

[推测] A strong point is the connection between high politics and daily life: queues, rationing, public confusion, television debates, and historical revelations all appear as part of the same crisis.

[推测] Its limitation is that the story is told quickly and from a broad interpretive angle, so listeners looking for detailed treatment of each republic, economic policy, or nationalist movement would need more background.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in late Soviet history, the origins of post-Soviet Russia, and the background to later conflicts involving Russia, Ukraine, and Russian-speaking populations outside Russia.
