# Custer's Last Stand at the Little Bighorn

## 概览

This episode reconstructs the final phase of the Battle of the Little Bighorn, focusing on what happened after Custer sent his last known message to Benteen and disappeared from the surviving record.

The hosts argue that the famous “last stand” is both historically powerful and inherently uncertain: no soldier with Custer survived, so the account depends on Native eyewitness testimony, archaeology, later recollections, and interpretation.

The discussion also shifts from blaming individual U.S. officers to recognizing the Lakota and Cheyenne victory as the result of numbers, leadership, battlefield confidence, and the roles of Sitting Bull, Crazy Horse, and Gall. The episode ends by stressing the grim irony that this great Native victory made harsher U.S. retaliation almost inevitable.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **A graphic warning and the Custer myth**

[事实] The producer warns that the episode is very gory.
[事实] The hosts open with a poem by Frederick Whittaker that helped turn Custer’s death into a heroic “last stand” myth.
[推测] The opening frames the episode as an attempt to separate legend from the uncertain historical record.

[02:50] **Recap of Custer’s situation**

[事实] On June 25, 1876, Custer had found a huge Lakota Sioux and Cheyenne village.
[事实] He had divided his force, sending Benteen to the left and Reno toward the river.
[事实] Reno’s attack had collapsed into a bloody retreat, leaving his men on a hill.

[03:30] **Benteen receives Custer’s last message**

[事实] Custer’s last written instruction to Benteen told him to come quickly, noted a big village, and asked him to bring the ammunition packs.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that the order was contradictory because moving fast and bringing slow ammunition mules were difficult to reconcile.
[事实] Benteen was already resentful because he believed Custer had sent him away from the glory.
[推测] Benteen’s delay may have reflected both confusion and personal bitterness.

[07:51] **Benteen finds Reno instead of Custer**

[事实] Benteen encountered Reno on the hill in a distressed state, with Reno saying he did not know where Custer was.
[事实] Reno argued that they could not move until ammunition arrived.
[事实] Benteen later said he assumed Custer could take care of himself.
[推测] Staying with Reno likely saved part of the Seventh Cavalry, even if Benteen’s motives were mixed.

[09:40] **Gunfire from Custer’s direction**

[事实] Reno’s and Benteen’s men heard firing from beyond the bluffs.
[事实] They later heard weapons that sounded like Seventh Cavalry carbines being used against them.
[事实] Captain Weir tried to move toward Custer, but the force was driven back by large numbers of warriors.
[推测] The gunfire suggests Custer’s command was still fighting for some time after Benteen reached Reno.

[11:28] **Reno Hill under siege**

[事实] Reno’s and Benteen’s men built defensive positions from boxes, saddlebags, pack gear, and dead animals.
[事实] Benteen initially behaved poorly by discouraging defensive preparation, but later became more effective.
[事实] The Lakota did not make a full charge that might have overwhelmed the defenders.
[推测] The survival of Reno Hill depended on endurance, improvised cover, and Benteen’s later steadiness.

[13:29] **Thirst, wounds, and Benteen’s recovery**

[事实] On June 26, the men on Reno Hill had no water, many were wounded, and the heat was intense.
[事实] Some chewed grass, sucked pebbles, or gnawed bullets to produce saliva.
[事实] Benteen inspired volunteers to fetch water from the river under fire, and those men later received Medals of Honor.
[推测] This section presents Benteen as morally unattractive but capable of real battlefield courage.

[17:25] **The village leaves and Terry arrives**

[事实] Around three o’clock, the firing died down as the Lakota village began moving toward the Bighorn Mountains.
[事实] Some soldiers thought Custer might be arriving with reinforcements and had scared the village away.
[事实] General Terry reached the survivors after the village had gone.
[推测] The survivors still did not fully understand that Custer’s command had already been destroyed.

[19:43] **The battlefield is discovered**

[事实] Terry told Benteen that Custer and his command were believed to be dead on a ridge nearby.
[事实] Benteen initially refused to believe Custer was dead and suggested Custer might have abandoned them.
[事实] Benteen was ordered to investigate and found dismembered bodies, arrows marking dead soldiers, and severe mutilation.
[事实] The searchers counted 197 dead soldiers in the area.

[22:45] **Custer’s body**

[事实] Private Jacob Adams found Custer’s body and called Benteen over.
[事实] Custer lay near his brothers Tom and Boston and his nephew Autie Reed.
[事实] Custer had two bullet wounds, one near the heart and one in the temple, and he had not been scalped.
[事实] The hosts say Custer was probably not recognized by the Lakota or Cheyenne during or after the battle.

[26:02] **Why the last stand cannot be known with certainty**

[事实] No one who remained with Custer survived to give a U.S. Army account.
[事实] The hosts identify Native eyewitness accounts and archaeological evidence as the main sources.
[事实] A 1983 fire exposed parts of the battlefield and enabled major archaeological work.
[推测] The best reconstruction remains probabilistic rather than certain.

[28:19] **Contested stories about Custer**

[事实] Crow scouts later claimed Custer watched Reno’s defeat from the bluffs without helping.
[事实] Theodore Roosevelt doubted that story but said anything was possible in battle.
[事实] Private Peter Thompson told another strange story about seeing Custer alone near the river.
[推测] The hosts treat these stories cautiously because Custer’s death quickly became a marketable legend.

[31:24] **Topography and Custer’s likely objective**

[事实] The village was west of the Little Bighorn River, while Custer was on the eastern bluffs.
[事实] The bluffs and ravines made movement down to the river difficult.
[事实] The hosts suggest Custer was probably trying to reach the village and seize women and children as hostages.
[推测] That tactic would have matched earlier U.S. cavalry practice and Custer’s own past behavior.

[34:44] **Calhoun Hill and the disputed last stand**

[事实] Custer likely left part of his command on what became Calhoun Hill under Keogh and Calhoun.
[事实] Some Native accounts say the fight was very brief, but the hosts doubt that Custer died immediately at the river.
[事实] Shell casings and body locations support the idea of fighting on the ridge.
[推测] Custer probably realized during the afternoon that he had badly misjudged the size and strength of the enemy.

[37:33] **Crazy Horse, Gall, and the collapse of Calhoun Hill**

[事实] Crazy Horse entered the fighting after a period of ritual preparation.
[事实] Gall, whose family members had been killed by Reno’s opening attack, helped lead attacks against Calhoun Hill.
[事实] Archaeology suggests many Native fighters had repeating rifles, while Custer’s men had single-shot carbines that could overheat.
[事实] Native accounts describe some U.S. soldiers panicking, trying to surrender, or killing themselves.

[41:53] **Last Stand Hill and the end**

[事实] The remaining men around Custer likely killed horses for cover on what became Last Stand Hill.
[事实] The hosts say the scene was less a clean heroic tableau than a desperate and terrifying collapse.
[事实] Keogh’s horse Comanche survived, recovered, and later became a memorial symbol.
[推测] The “laughing hero” image of Custer’s death belongs more to later myth than to the likely battlefield reality.

[45:50] **Casualties and the news reaches America**

[事实] The Seventh Cavalry lost 258 killed and 60 wounded.
[事实] The Lakota and Cheyenne losses are given as about 31 warriors, six women, and four children.
[事实] News reached the East just after the United States centennial celebrations.
[事实] Libbie Custer learned of her husband’s death at Fort Lincoln and personally visited other widows.

[49:04] **Who was blamed**

[事实] Terry called the defeat a terrible blunder.
[事实] Reno became the subject of the only formal investigation and was later heavily attacked by Custer’s defenders.
[事实] The hosts criticize Reno’s battlefield collapse but also express some sympathy for a man who broke under extreme pressure.
[事实] They argue Benteen behaved badly in some ways but saved many surviving soldiers by staying with Reno.

[53:30] **Custer’s responsibility and reputation**

[事实] Early newspaper reactions split partly along party lines, with Republican papers more critical of Custer and Democratic papers more admiring.
[事实] The hosts say Custer failed to reconnoiter properly, ignored warnings, divided his troops, and chose Reno for a crucial role.
[事实] They also note that Custer’s dash and confidence had previously brought him success.
[推测] Custer’s strengths as a commander became disastrous when combined with bad intelligence and overwhelming opposition.

[56:23] **Reframing the battle as a Native victory**

[事实] The hosts argue that the battle should not only be seen as an American blunder.
[事实] Sitting Bull united disparate groups into an enormous encampment.
[事实] Crazy Horse’s leadership at the Rosebud and Little Bighorn gave warriors confidence that they could defeat U.S. soldiers.
[事实] The hosts conclude that the Lakota victory also made a massive U.S. response inevitable.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its balance between narrative drama and historical uncertainty. It gives listeners a vivid sequence of events while repeatedly warning that Custer’s final movements cannot be known with precision.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the reassessment of Benteen, the use of archaeology to complicate the “last stand” myth, and the reframing of Little Bighorn as a victory won by Lakota and Cheyenne leadership rather than simply a defeat caused by U.S. mistakes.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in military history, U.S. frontier history, historical mythmaking, and contested evidence. It is not suitable for listeners who want a restrained account, because the transcript includes repeated graphic descriptions of wounds, mutilation, bodies, and battlefield suffering.
