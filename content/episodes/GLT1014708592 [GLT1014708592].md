# Undead Rome: The Afterlives of the Roman Empire

## 概览

This episode continues the question of when the Roman Empire ended, moving from Heraclius and the Arab conquests to Byzantium, Constantinople, the Holy Roman Empire, Muscovy, Russia, Islamic apocalyptic imagery, and even medieval Wales.

The hosts argue that the seventh century was a genuine rupture: the empire lost Syria, Egypt, North Africa, and much of its older continuity, yet Constantinople survived and its people continued to identify as Romans. They treat 1453 as the obvious symbolic endpoint, while noting that Trebizond’s fall in 1461 is the stricter final date.

The second half shifts from political survival to ideological afterlife. Charlemagne, the Holy Roman Empire, the Ottomans, Moscow as the “Third Rome,” modern Russian rhetoric, Islamic State propaganda, and the Prince of Wales are all examined as examples of Rome being revived, claimed, reimagined, or weaponized long after the empire’s practical end.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Heraclius and the Seventh-Century Rupture**

[事实] The episode opens with Heraclius restoring the True Cross to Jerusalem in 629 after defeating the Persians and taking titles including “King of Kings” and “Basileus.”

[事实] Tom Holland argues that Heraclius’s reign was both glorious and disastrous: he lost to the Persians, defeated them, and then faced devastating Arab victories.

[事实] The hosts identify the seventh century as a major rupture because of war, economic collapse, plague, the rise of Islam, and the shift toward a Greek-speaking Christian empire.

[推测] The discussion frames Heraclius less as a neat “last Roman emperor” than as the ruler whose reign exposes how dramatically Roman power and identity were transformed.

[03:23] **From East Rome to Byzantium**

[事实] Holland agrees that the Arab conquests mark a radical break, as Constantinople lost Syria, Egypt, and later North Africa while also facing Balkan pressure.

[事实] Despite those losses, Constantinople survived Arab sieges and later recovered enough to become a major regional power in the tenth and eleventh centuries.

[事实] Byzantines still saw themselves as Romans and were angered when Charlemagne’s western imperial claim challenged that identity.

[推测] The label “Byzantium” is presented as historically useful but artificial, because the people themselves maintained Roman self-understanding.

[06:14] **Christian Rome and Apocalyptic Destiny**

[事实] Later Byzantines often no longer understood older imperial monuments and sometimes interpreted statues as demonic or prophetic figures.

[事实] They still believed Rome’s empire was eternal because of its Christian role in apocalyptic history.

[事实] Holland describes the prophecy of the Last Roman Emperor, who defeats Gog and Magog, travels to Jerusalem, dies before the Cross, and helps usher in the final drama before Christ’s return.

[推测] This myth restored cosmic importance to the Roman Empire after Augustine had argued that the empire itself was not spiritually central.

[10:18] **Yarmouk, Roman Identity, and the Byzantine Nation Question**

[事实] The Battle of Yarmouk in August 636 is identified as a conventional landmark defeat of Byzantium by the Arabs.

[事实] The hosts discuss Anthony Kaldellis’s argument that Byzantium can be understood as a Roman nation state.

[事实] Holland accepts that Byzantium developed a coherent, embattled Christian identity centered on Constantinople, but calls “nation state” an anachronistic frame.

[推测] The argument suggests that Byzantine survival depended partly on a strong shared identity rather than merely institutions or military capacity.

[13:41] **Turks, Manzikert, and the Sultanate of Rome**

[事实] After Manzikert and subsequent civil wars, Turks moved into Anatolia, which had been a key recruiting ground for Byzantine armies.

[事实] An Islamic sultanate established itself in former Byzantine Christian territory and called itself the Sultanate of Rome.

[事实] Muslim traditions also gave Constantinople and Rome roles in apocalyptic expectations, with sayings attributed to Muhammad about their eventual fall.

[推测] Roman prestige remained powerful even for conquerors who did not identify as Roman, because Constantinople still represented world-historical authority.

[15:01] **1204, 1453, and 1461 as End Dates**

[事实] In 1204, Crusaders captured and sacked Constantinople, an event described as a disgraceful and catastrophic moment in European history.

[事实] Holland rejects 1204 as the final end because the Byzantines later recaptured Constantinople and preserved a continuous imperial line.

[事实] He calls 1453 the obvious endpoint because Mehmet II captured Constantinople, but says 1461 is more strictly accurate because Trebizond was the final holdout.

[事实] The fall of Constantinople in 1453 shocked Christendom and inspired counterfactual romance in which the city was retaken.

[推测] The hosts distinguish symbolic endings from technical endings: 1453 mattered emotionally and imaginatively, while 1461 matters for strict political continuity.

[20:57] **Charlemagne and the Holy Roman Empire**

[事实] After the break, the hosts turn to attempts to revive or reanimate Rome, beginning with Charlemagne and the Holy Roman Empire.

[事实] Charlemagne was crowned emperor in Rome because the pope needed protection and Constantinople could no longer provide it effectively.

[事实] The relationship between emperor and pope became a major medieval issue, tied to whether empire or church had primacy.

[事实] Holland disputes Voltaire’s joke by saying the Holy Roman Empire was holy, Roman-ish, and imperial in the sense that it claimed imperium.

[推测] The Holy Roman Empire is treated as a credible afterlife of Roman authority, though not a simple continuation of the ancient empire.

[24:45] **Ottomans and the Limits of Roman Succession**

[事实] The hosts consider whether the Ottomans saw themselves as successors to Rome after capturing Constantinople.

[事实] Holland argues that Ottoman Roman succession was weaker because monuments such as Hagia Sophia were converted into signs of Islam’s triumph rather than preserved as Roman-Christian continuity.

[事实] Mehmet II had some consciousness of Roman inheritance, but the broader sense of being heir to Rome faded relatively quickly in Constantinople.

[推测] The Ottoman case shows that occupying Rome’s capital did not automatically create a lasting Roman identity.

[25:31] **Muscovy and the Third Rome**

[事实] Holland identifies Muscovy as an eastern power with a much stronger claim of continuity with the second Rome.

[事实] A Russian monk named Philotheus wrote in 1510 that two Romes had fallen, the third stood, and there would be no fourth.

[事实] This claim was addressed to Vasili, whose lineage connected him to the last Byzantine emperor through his mother’s family.

[事实] The title “Tsar” and the idea of translatio imperii supported the claim that imperial authority had moved from Rome to Constantinople to Moscow.

[推测] Moscow’s claim was less architectural or classical than spiritual, dynastic, and Orthodox.

[28:31] **Crimea, Catherine the Great, and Russian Roman Memory**

[事实] Crimea is presented as important because it linked Muscovy to the classical Greek and Roman world and to Byzantine Christianity.

[事实] Chersonesus had Greek origins, had been part of the Roman Empire, and was where Vladimir of Kiev was baptized.

[事实] In 1783, Potemkin described Catherine the Great’s annexation of Crimea as attaching lands associated with Alexander, Pompey, and the source of Russian Christianity to Russia.

[推测] The discussion implies that Crimea’s symbolic weight in Russian history comes partly from this imagined chain linking Russia to both first Rome and second Rome.

[30:29] **Russia, America, France, and Roman Inheritance**

[事实] The hosts contrast Russia with the United States and France, which used Roman republican ideals and iconography without claiming to be literally Roman.

[事实] Holland argues that Russia differs because some Russians have treated their link to Rome as organic, Orthodox, and continuous.

[事实] Washington may look more Roman architecturally, but lacks the same supernatural or dynastic claim of descent.

[推测] Roman identity is shown as flexible: it can be aesthetic, political, religious, dynastic, or geopolitical depending on who invokes it.

[32:25] **Stalin, Putin’s Russia, and Byzantine Echoes**

[事实] Holland recounts stories, which he says are probably mythical, that Stalin had an icon of the Virgin flown around Moscow when Nazi forces approached the city.

[事实] He connects this story to Byzantine practices of carrying icons around city walls during sieges.

[事实] Holland says some people around Putin appear to take the Third Rome idea seriously.

[事实] A 2008 Russian television film about Constantinople blamed its fall less on the Turks than on the Crusaders, using that history as a commentary on Western capitalism.

[事实] Lavrov is quoted as saying Rus was regarded by East and West as successor to the Byzantine Empire.

[推测] The episode suggests that Byzantine-Roman imagery still influences modern Russian self-presentation and geopolitical rhetoric.

[36:00] **Islamic State and Rome as Apocalyptic Enemy**

[事实] Holland says Islamic State used Roman Empire imagery in its propaganda, especially through the town of Dabiq and hadith about Romans appearing before the last hour.

[事实] Islamic State’s magazine used images connected to Rome and the Vatican and described a future conquest of Rome.

[事实] Holland also mentions Yusuf al-Qaradawi and a Saudi defence academy imam discussing Rome as a future site of Islamic conquest.

[事实] In these examples, Rome means both the literal city and the broader West, including the United States and its allies.

[推测] Rome’s ghost survives not only among self-proclaimed heirs but also among enemies who need Rome as an apocalyptic opponent.

[39:41] **Roman Britain, Wales, and the Prince of Wales**

[事实] Holland proposes northern Wales as a surprising candidate for the last western Roman territory where people retained a Roman identity.

[事实] He links Welsh princely claims to Magnus Maximus, a Roman usurper remembered in Welsh tradition as Maxen Wledig.

[事实] Edward I ended these Welsh princely lines, then made his own son Prince of Wales in 1301.

[事实] Holland jokingly argues that the Prince of Wales could be seen as heir to the Roman Empire in the West.

[推测] The claim is presented playfully and is challenged by the co-host as ridiculous, but it reinforces the episode’s larger theme that Roman inheritance can survive in unexpected forms.

[43:02] **Final Jokes and Closing**

[事实] The hosts joke that Vladimir Putin and Prince Charles might represent eastern and western Roman claims.

[事实] They close by saying they have covered Rome’s fall much faster than Edward Gibbon and preview upcoming episodes on JFK, lethal fashion, and oil or energy.

[推测] The ending deliberately undercuts the grand historical argument with comic exaggeration, fitting the episode’s theme of Rome’s strange afterlives.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value lies in treating “the fall of Rome” not as one date but as a layered historical problem: political collapse, religious identity, imperial continuity, symbolic inheritance, and propaganda all produce different answers.

Its strongest sections are the comparisons between Byzantium, the Holy Roman Empire, Muscovy, modern Russia, and Islamic apocalyptic rhetoric. These examples show how Rome remained politically useful long after Roman state power disappeared.

A limitation is that some claims, especially the Wales and Prince of Wales argument, are clearly playful and speculative within the conversation itself. The episode also moves quickly across many centuries, so listeners wanting detailed treatment of each successor claim would need additional context.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in big historical continuities, political theology, Byzantium, medieval empire, and the modern afterlife of ancient ideas, rather than those looking for a single definitive date when Rome “ended.”
