# Varun Sharma on Rejection, Resilience, Comedy, and the Human Side of Cinema

## Overview

Actor Varun Sharma traces his journey from a childhood in Jalandhar to becoming known for roles such as Chucha in *Fukrey* and Sexa in *Chhichhore*. He describes how an early fascination with acting, formal media studies, theatre, casting work, and persistent auditions gradually led to his first major film opportunity.

A central theme is that “finding work is work.” Sharma speaks candidly about financial uncertainty, repeated rejection, the psychological strain of auditioning, and the crucial support he received from his mother and sister. He reframes his early years not as a struggle but as a period of learning, information gathering, and relationship building.

The conversation later explores comedy as a collaborative craft, mental health in the entertainment industry, changing audience habits, and cinema’s enduring emotional appeal. Sharma also discusses AI as a useful creative tool that can accelerate work, while arguing that lived experience, consent, human judgment, and emotional connection must remain central.

## 分段落总结

[01:47] **Childhood, family roots, and the desire to act**

[事实] Sharma was born and raised in Jalandhar; his father was in business, while his mother taught painting and headed a fine arts department.

[事实] He recalls deciding at about seven years old that he wanted to become an actor after watching *Baazigar* and singing along to one of its songs.

[事实] After school, he studied media entertainment and film technology in Chandigarh and performed serious theatre, never initially imagining that comedy would become his strength.

[事实] He later moved to Mumbai for a casting internship because it was the professional field he considered closest to acting.

[07:38] **The faith of his mother and sister**

[事实] Sharma credits his mother and elder sister with supporting him emotionally and financially despite knowing little about how the Hindi film industry worked.

[事实] During periods of unsuccessful auditions, high rent, and dwindling money, they consistently reassured him and remained available in whatever capacity he needed.

[事实] He describes their defining qualities as unconditional love and faith, arguing that family support removes a major source of anxiety for a person pursuing an uncertain dream.

[推测] His account presents confidence less as an individual trait than as something sustained by dependable relationships.

[16:45] **Reframing struggle as learning**

[事实] Sharma says he does not like describing his early career as a “struggle”; he prefers to call it a learning phase in which he studied casting, auditions, films, and his own craft.

[事实] After spending six months securing one advertisement, a relative dismissed the achievement by asking what he had done during the remaining months.

[事实] Sharma responds that finding one day of paid work required months of waking up, visiting offices, waiting in lines, auditioning, being shortlisted, and facing rejection.

[事实] He concludes that, in Mumbai’s entertainment industry, finding work is itself a full-time job.

[21:22] **Learning the industry from inside casting rooms**

[事实] Through a personal connection, Sharma contacted casting director Nandini Shrikent and secured an internship that exposed him to productions associated with major banners.

[事实] Watching many actors interpret the same scene taught him how differently a role could be performed and what directors look for during auditions.

[事实] He assisted with casting on projects including *Student of the Year* and *The Reluctant Fundamentalist* and continued working in casting because he considered it practical training for acting.

[事实] He rejects the idea that the film industry is uniformly unwelcoming, saying his own entry developed through people sharing information and offering opportunities.

[25:01] **Information, initiative, and a constructive view of rejection**

[事实] Sharma describes information as one of the most valuable assets in any profession, saying the modern world is driven less by ammunition than by information.

[事实] Before social media and large messaging networks became common, he gathered audition leads by visiting studios, speaking with other actors, locating offices, and distributing photographs and introductions.

[事实] He treated rejection as a new professional connection: even if a director did not cast him immediately, the meeting could make him memorable for a future project.

[事实] He says actors cannot simply wait beside the phone; they must actively learn which projects are being made and where auditions are happening.

[31:25] **Rejection, mental health, and financial insecurity**

[事实] Sharma acknowledges that repeated rejection can damage a person’s thoughts, moods, behaviour, personality, and mental health.

[事实] As a young actor, he did not yet possess the language or awareness to identify mental-health concerns; calls from his mother helped him recover after particularly difficult days.

[事实] He travelled on foot and by bus until earnings from his first advertisement allowed him to use an auto-rickshaw.

[事实] He regards purchases and experiences funded by his own earnings—his first ticket, business-class journey, luxury car, and family trip—as enduring personal memories.

[35:39] **First earnings and formative side jobs**

[事实] Sharma’s first advertisement was for a mobile telecommunications company, and the shoot happened on his birthday.

[事实] Before establishing himself as an actor, he took hosting and event-logistics jobs to earn money and increase his chances of being noticed.

[事实] He earned ₹1,500 from an unsuccessful three-day hosting assignment and says his mother preserved the three ₹500 notes with the date and his signature.

[事实] He recounts these early professional mistakes humorously, treating them as part of his education rather than concealing them.

[41:44] **Nine months of auditions for Chucha**

[事实] Sharma auditioned repeatedly for the role of Chucha in *Fukrey*, reading scenes with actors who had already been cast.

[事实] He says the selection moved from roughly 500 candidates to 50, then ten and five, with the full process lasting about nine months.

[事实] Because he had never performed comedy before, director Mrighdeep Singh Lamba worked with him through different variations and takes before confirming him for the pivotal role.

[事实] When Sharma told his mother that he had secured the film and might appear on its poster, her long silence made him understand the expression “silence speaks a lot.”

[54:10] **Success, failure, and films that mattered**

[事实] Sharma says *Chhichhore* prompted messages from viewers who connected with its idea that failure is acceptable and that finishing first is not always necessary.

[事实] He explains that a film can take two or three years to write, cast, prepare, and shoot, yet appear to collapse within three days of release.

[事实] His response to failure is to examine what audiences disliked, where the production went wrong, and what he could improve in his own performance.

[事实] He believes *Roohi* and his character Katanni deserved greater recognition, noting that the film opened during the pandemic with limited cinema occupancy.

[57:16] **Making Chhichhore and remembering Sushant Singh Rajput**

[事实] Sharma joined *Chhichhore* after being contacted through casting director Mukesh Chhabra and meeting director Nitesh Tiwari.

[事实] His boarding-school experience helped him relate to the film’s college friendships, jokes, and group dynamics; much of the film was shot at IIT Bombay.

[事实] He describes Sushant Singh Rajput, with whom he worked on *Raabta* and *Chhichhore*, as exceptionally intelligent, learned, and deeply interested in astronomy.

[事实] Sharma says Rajput’s death surprised him and that he has not rewatched *Chhichhore* since, because every scene carries memories of its production.

[63:38] **Mental health, self-conversation, and digital overload**

[事实] Sharma supports public discussion of mental health and praises people such as Deepika Padukone for helping normalize the subject.

[事实] His personal method for handling stress is to walk without his phone and speak honestly to himself about what he is feeling and why.

[事实] He identifies overthinking as one of his own tendencies and stresses the importance of talking to oneself, trusted friends, or loved ones rather than keeping distress inside.

[事实] While travelling, he limits phone use, contacts his family in the morning and evening, and otherwise tries to experience the trip directly.

[推测] His broader concern is that constant digital broadcasting can replace reflection, presence, and intimate human exchange.

[73:13] **Why comedy is a collaborative craft**

[事实] Sharma considers making another person laugh extremely difficult and says comedic timing is only one component of the result.

[事实] Screen comedy depends on the other performers’ reactions, background music, editing, direction, writing, and the execution of the entire sequence.

[事实] Even stand-up comedy is strengthened by the audience’s laughter and the ripple it creates through the room.

[事实] Sharma describes his off-screen personality as calm and composed, contrasting it with the energetic characters audiences often associate with him.

[事实] He says every actor brings individual body language and mannerisms to a performance, but an on-screen personality should not be assumed to match the actor’s private self.

[77:07] **AI as a production tool, not a replacement for emotion**

[事实] Sharma says AI has already helped film teams visualize possible character looks more quickly and has helped him improve the grammar of professional emails.

[事实] He sees AI as a tool that accelerates existing processes, while acknowledging that creating strong AI-generated work still requires knowledge, skill, and artistic judgment.

[事实] He argues that stories depend on lived experiences, personal memories, emotional detail, and human relatability that technology can assist but not simply substitute.

[事实] He is not currently alarmed about AI eliminating actors because film, television, streaming, gaming, animation, and micro-dramas have developed distinct audiences rather than fully replacing one another.

[推测] His position is cautiously optimistic: creators should learn emerging tools without surrendering the human experiences that give creative work meaning.

[86:00] **Consent, judgment, and the risks of outsourcing thought**

[事实] The host identifies consent, responsible direction, and knowing what not to outsource as three principles for using technology.

[事实] Sharma agrees that AI should enhance artists rather than reproduce or replace them without permission.

[事实] He mentions writers who seek an AI system’s approval immediately after completing a script and suggests first sharing the work with several people to observe human reactions.

[事实] He does not condemn AI feedback, but argues that it should complement rather than displace human conversation and emotional judgment.

[88:18] **Rapid-fire reflections on films and identity**

[事实] Sharma names *Daud* as the first film he saw in a theatre and *Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge* and *Andaz Apna Apna* among his most rewatched films.

[事实] He says he would work with Shah Rukh Khan without charging and identifies a large ensemble scene from *Dilwale* as one he especially values.

[事实] He calls Chucha “life,” Sexa “heart,” Jalandhar his birthplace, and AI a tool that expedites thought.

[事实] He again singles out Katanni from *Roohi* as a character he wishes audiences discussed more.

[92:22] **Advice for aspiring actors and their families**

[事实] Sharma tells aspiring actors that dreaming and beginning the work already constitutes half the battle.

[事实] He warns against postponing auditions until achieving a supposedly perfect body, because a casting team may be seeking precisely the applicant’s current appearance and individuality.

[事实] His advice is to accept oneself, develop confidence, gather information, keep auditioning, and actively pursue opportunities rather than waiting for them to arrive.

[事实] He asks parents and siblings to express belief in a loved one’s ambition, arguing that such support provides extraordinary confidence and strength.

[事实] He presents his own progression from open auditions to a major film poster as evidence that the process can work for other outsiders as well.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest contribution is its detailed portrayal of the invisible labour behind an acting career. Sharma moves beyond a simple success story to explain audition networks, casting-room education, financial limitations, repeated rejection, and the months of work that may precede a single paid assignment.

His discussion of family support and mental health gives the conversation emotional weight. Rather than presenting resilience as endless positivity, he acknowledges that rejection hurts, failure can overwhelm years of effort, and encouragement from trusted people may be what makes another attempt possible.

The later discussion of comedy and AI broadens the episode effectively. Sharma’s description of comedy as an ensemble achievement is especially valuable, while his AI perspective offers a practical distinction between accelerating production and outsourcing human judgment.

[推测] The episode will be most useful to aspiring actors, creative professionals, and families supporting unconventional careers. Its main limitation is that several topics—particularly clinical mental health and the long-term consequences of AI—are discussed primarily through personal experience rather than specialist expertise.
