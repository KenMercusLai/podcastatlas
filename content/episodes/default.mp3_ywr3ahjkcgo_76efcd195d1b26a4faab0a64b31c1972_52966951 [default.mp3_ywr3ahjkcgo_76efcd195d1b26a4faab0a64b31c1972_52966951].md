# Why Has U.S. Construction Become So Expensive—and Can Modular Building Fix It?

## 概览

U.S. construction productivity has declined for roughly five decades, diverging sharply from industries such as manufacturing, agriculture, and information technology. Even after adjusting housing output for size and quality, productivity has remained essentially flat since the mid-1970s, contributing to higher costs across housing, infrastructure, schools, and factories.

The episode finds no single explanation. Land-use restrictions inhibit large-scale development; fragmented firms and project-based contracts weaken incentives for efficiency; public participation adds procedural complexity; and high financial and safety risks make companies reluctant to adopt unfamiliar technology. More sophisticated buildings and stronger safety standards also complicate simple historical comparisons.

Modular construction offers a potential route toward manufacturing-style productivity through factory production, parallel work streams, automation, and reduced waste. Yet its U.S. market share remains small because the industry lacks scale, coordinated regulation, sustained investment, and sufficient demand certainty.

The broader conclusion is that technology alone cannot solve the problem. Meaningful improvement would require complementary changes to zoning, building codes, procurement, research funding, workforce support, and the incentives governing development.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Housing, Fertility, and the Construction Problem**

[事实] The episode is presented as a companion to a series arguing that insufficient affordable housing contributes significantly to declining fertility.

[事实] A 1968 federal report had already warned that U.S. construction-productivity growth had stalled after rising by about 2 percent annually during the 1940s and 1950s.

[事实] A 2023 study estimated that the value added by a construction worker was about 40 percent lower than in 1970.

[推测] The construction crisis is therefore framed as a long-running structural problem rather than a temporary consequence of recent inflation or supply disruptions.

[05:12] **Five Decades of Falling Productivity**

[事实] Across the U.S. economy, labor productivity has historically risen by about 2 percent per year, allowing output per worker-hour to double approximately every 35 years.

[事实] Construction productivity rose faster than the broader economy from 1950 until the late 1960s, then reversed direction and continued falling for decades.

[事实] Construction accounts for about 4 percent of U.S. GDP, making its poor performance consequential for the entire economy.

[事实] Economist Chad Syverson argues that the duration and scale of the decline make a single-cause explanation unlikely.

[09:11] **What the Evidence Rules Out**

[事实] Capital investment per construction worker has broadly kept pace with the rest of the economy, weakening the claim that insufficient equipment investment is the primary cause.

[事实] Construction wages and material-input prices have not risen unusually quickly compared with other sectors, and industry profit margins do not explain the trend.

[事实] After adjusting housing for observable changes such as size and number of bathrooms, productivity is no longer declining but remains roughly flat over 50 years.

[事实] A housing-construction worker now produces about the same number of square feet as a worker did in the mid-1970s.

[11:42] **Contracts, Fragmentation, and Misaligned Incentives**

[事实] Contractors may shift labor from an existing job to begin a newly won project, slowing the first job while ensuring that the next one gets underway.

[事实] Syverson says firms can sometimes make money even when projects do not proceed as smoothly as possible, weakening incentives to maximize efficiency.

[事实] Designers and builders frequently recreate information as responsibility passes between separate organizations.

[推测] The project-by-project structure of construction makes local optimization rational for individual firms even when it reduces productivity across the complete project.

[13:12] **Housing Shortages and the Cost of Regulation**

[事实] Freddie Mac estimates that the United States is short by nearly four million housing units.

[事实] Economist Edward Glaeser argues that minimum lot sizes, wetlands restrictions, and other land-use rules constrain construction and prevent mass production.

[事实] He links housing scarcity to higher prices, reduced migration into productive cities, and a wealth transfer benefiting established homeowners at the expense of younger buyers.

[事实] Glaeser acknowledges that safety, sanitation, and structural regulations provide real benefits, but argues that they should be subjected to cost-benefit analysis.

[推测] The central policy challenge is not simply removing regulation, but distinguishing protections with broad public value from restrictions that mainly preserve incumbent advantages.

[17:49] **Why Construction Firms Stay Small**

[事实] Tighter restrictions and smaller development sites have contributed to an industry dominated by small establishments and small projects.

[事实] Construction has almost 600,000 establishments with fewer than five employees but only 101 with more than 1,000 employees.

[事实] In a sample of approximately 3,700 residential parcels, the median development covered 7.3 acres and contained only 3.2 units.

[事实] Glaeser argues that accessory dwelling units may add supply but cannot deliver the economies of scale associated with mass production.

[21:00] **Citizen Voice and Rising Complexity**

[事实] Research on U.S. highways found that spending per mile more than tripled between the 1960s and 1980s.

[事实] The researchers identify “citizen voice”—social movements, legislation, and judicial doctrine expanding public influence over government decisions—as one contributor.

[事实] Greater public involvement can protect communities while also adding hearings, land-use disputes, delays, and costs.

[推测] Democratic participation creates a genuine trade-off: it can prevent harmful development, but poorly designed processes can also make socially necessary construction prohibitively difficult.

[22:22] **Complex Buildings, High Risks, and Slow Technology Adoption**

[事实] Modern buildings contain far more complicated heating, cooling, and computerized systems than buildings of 50 or 100 years ago.

[事实] Expensive equipment, large financial commitments, and severe safety consequences make construction companies reluctant to test unfamiliar methods on active projects.

[事实] Cell phones were adopted quickly because their immediate value to job-site coordination was clear.

[事实] Building Information Modeling has spread more slowly because it requires coordination across firms and can blur established divisions of responsibility and liability between designers and builders.

[推测] Construction does not reject innovation categorically; adoption is fastest when benefits accrue directly to the user and do not redistribute risk among project participants.

[29:08] **The Case for Modular Construction**

[事实] Volumetric Building Companies constructs factory-made modules for apartments and hotels, including projects exceeding 20 stories and hundreds of units.

[事实] Its CEO says modular buildings can be completed in less than half the time required for conventional on-site construction because factory production and foundation work proceed simultaneously.

[事实] A delivered hotel-room module can arrive about 90 percent complete, including furniture and a mattress.

[事实] Factory conditions allow workers to use computers and robotics, reduce physical barriers to employment, and provide safer, more controlled workplaces.

[事实] One independent analysis found that modular construction produces 40 percent fewer emissions than conventional construction.

[32:43] **Why U.S. Modular Building Remains Marginal**

[事实] Only about 5 percent of U.S. construction is modular, compared with prefabricated elements in roughly 15 percent of Japanese houses and 80 percent of Swedish houses.

[事实] Off-site construction has existed for more than a century; Sears once offered around 450 house designs whose components could be shipped by rail for assembly.

[事实] HUD’s Operation Breakthrough produced nearly 3,000 prototype units beginning in the late 1960s, but funding cuts, the energy crisis, and an economic downturn caused many participating companies to fail.

[事实] Comparable efforts persisted elsewhere, while the United States lost companies, knowledge, and momentum toward regulatory reform.

[37:06] **Katerra’s Failure and Legacy**

[事实] Katerra raised more than $800 million from SoftBank and attempted to build a vertically integrated, technology-driven off-site construction business.

[事实] After operating factories and completing several major projects, the company filed for bankruptcy in 2021.

[事实] Vaughn Buckley argues that Katerra tried to reinvent too many parts of a 150-year-old industry within an unrealistically short period.

[事实] Ivan Rupnik says Katerra nevertheless trained specialists, demonstrated that some foreign construction methods could work technically in the United States, and left factories now used by other companies.

[推测] Katerra illustrates that abundant capital can accelerate experimentation but cannot by itself overcome fragmented regulation, limited demand, and the operational complexity of construction.

[40:00] **Scaling Modular Production Without Abandoning Small Firms**

[事实] Centralized modular production conflicts with a U.S. construction market dominated by small companies.

[事实] Glaeser proposes a model resembling the solar industry: large factories manufacture standardized components while small local firms install them.

[事实] He acknowledges that such a transition would create winners and losers and could displace some traditional construction work.

[事实] He favors education and a strong safety net for affected workers rather than blocking technological change.

[推测] A hybrid system could preserve local installation businesses while relocating much of the repetitive, productivity-sensitive work to factories.

[43:18] **Proof That Modular Buildings Can Be Large and Attractive**

[事实] Two modular residential towers in Croydon, London, rise 44 and 38 stories and contain approximately 1,500 modules and 546 housing units.

[事实] The project took about three and a half years, compared with an estimated five years using conventional methods.

[事实] Its speed depended partly on simultaneous factory and site work and partly on unusually strong alignment among the developer, architects, planners, and engineers.

[事实] Singapore requires buildings on government land to incorporate at least 70 percent off-site construction, supporting a strong domestic modular industry.

[推测] Croydon suggests that modular technology performs best when regulation, planning, design, and manufacturing capacity are aligned before construction begins.

[47:10] **The Innovation and Capacity Gap**

[事实] Moving the United States substantially toward Singapore’s off-site construction share would require far more manufacturing capacity and innovation.

[事实] Construction companies often lack dedicated research-and-development budgets, so experimentation must occur within active projects.

[事实] Carrie Sturtz-Dossick envisions future automation in which robots could construct houses rapidly and suggests that space construction may stimulate technologies later used on Earth.

[推测] Expecting individual projects to finance industry-wide innovation places experimentation in precisely the environments least able to tolerate failure.

[49:03] **Policy Carrots, Sticks, and Local Resistance**

[事实] California’s Homekey program rewards housing delivered within 12 months, a schedule generally achievable through renovation or factory production.

[事实] Ireland has required new public housing units to be factory-built after concluding that conventional field construction cannot meet projected demand.

[事实] Glaeser says most U.S. land-use rules are local, while existing homeowners have a financial incentive to resist changes that might reduce property values.

[事实] He proposes allowing very high density in selected expensive areas while retaining essential structural-safety rules.

[推测] State or national incentives may be necessary because local political incentives often favor scarcity even when broader society benefits from additional housing.

[52:51] **California Forever as a Real-World Test**

[事实] California Forever, backed by several technology investors, purchased tens of thousands of acres with plans for a dense new city.

[事实] A proposal for 170,000 homes between San Francisco and Sacramento was blocked at the county level.

[事实] The developers then pursued annexation into nearby Suisun City, and the project became a central issue in a mayoral election won by the candidate who was not opposed to it.

[事实] The project remained contested at the time of the episode.

[推测] Its progress may test whether concentrated capital and ambitious planning can overcome the same local political and regulatory barriers identified throughout the episode.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest feature is its refusal to reduce the productivity decline to a single villain. Economic data, institutional incentives, safety concerns, land-use rules, technological adoption, and industry structure are treated as interacting causes rather than competing slogans.

Its modular-construction case studies add practical depth. Successful projects demonstrate credible gains in speed, labor accessibility, and emissions, while Operation Breakthrough and Katerra show why promising technology can fail without stable demand, regulatory compatibility, manufacturing capacity, and patient implementation.

A limitation is that several proposed remedies remain hypothetical, and some quantitative claims come from advocates with commercial or policy commitments. The episode acknowledges uncertainty, but it offers less evidence about the long-term maintenance costs, financing risks, and comparative quality of modular buildings.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for listeners interested in housing policy, urban economics, construction management, or industrial innovation. Its lasting lesson is that increasing housing supply requires an institutional production system—not merely a better building technique.
