# Britain’s First Brexit Referendum: Harold Wilson, Europe, and the 1975 Crisis

## 概览

This episode uses *Fawlty Towers* and its 1975 episode “The Germans” as a comic entry point into Britain’s mid-1970s mood: shabby institutions, post-war anxieties, class tension, strikes, inflation, and a sense of national decline.

The main historical thread is Harold Wilson’s second premiership, his fragile three-seat majority, and the two crises pressing on him in 1975: an economic emergency marked by near-25% inflation and spiralling public borrowing, and Labour’s deep split over Britain’s membership of the European Community.

The episode argues that the 1975 referendum was less an expression of British enthusiasm for Europe than a tactical solution to Labour’s internal divisions. Wilson renegotiated terms, recommended a Yes vote, and allowed ministers to campaign on either side. The public voted decisively to stay in, but the economic crisis remained unresolved.

## 分段落总结

[01:01] **Fawlty Towers and the Mood of 1975**

[事实] The episode opens with Basil Fawlty’s “don’t mention the war” scene from *Fawlty Towers*, first broadcast on 24 October 1975.

[事实] The hosts describe *Fawlty Towers* as a window into mid-1970s Britain: shabby, class-conscious, strike-ridden, and still psychologically shaped by the Second World War.

[事实] Basil’s complaints about “bloody Wilson” introduce the episode’s focus on Harold Wilson, the 1975 European referendum, and Britain’s worsening economic crisis.

[推测] The sitcom is used as shorthand for a country that felt simultaneously comic, exhausted, and trapped by old assumptions.

[05:04] **Wilson’s Fragile Return to Power**

[事实] The hosts recap that Ted Heath’s wage controls and confrontation with the miners led to the February 1974 election, stalemate, and Harold Wilson’s return as prime minister.

[事实] Wilson won a second election in October 1974 but only with a three-seat majority, making him dependent on every Labour MP behind him.

[事实] Wilson had previously won four elections out of five and was described as pragmatic, resilient, cunning, and personally modest.

[推测] His small majority made decisive economic action politically dangerous, especially when Labour’s left wing was powerful.

[06:14] **Wilson’s Personality, Health, and Private Strains**

[事实] Wilson was born in West Yorkshire in 1916, studied at Oxford, became an economics don and government statistician, then rose through Labour politics.

[事实] By 1974-75 he appeared tired, ill, and stressed; aides thought some ailments might have been stress-related or early signs of later dementia.

[事实] Bernard Donoghue’s diaries described Wilson drinking heavily, including several glasses of brandy or whisky at lunch.

[事实] The hosts also discuss Wilson’s difficult private circle, including his wife Mary, political secretary Marcia Williams, and reported tensions around Janet Hewlett Davis.

[推测] The personal chaos around Wilson amplified the difficulty of governing during an already severe national crisis.

[15:20] **Inflation, Borrowing, and the Social Contract**

[事实] Britain’s inflation was moving toward 25%, while public borrowing was far higher than expected.

[事实] Public spending rose by 35% in cash terms in 1974, partly because Chancellor Denis Healey funded pensions and election-related giveaways.

[事实] Labour raised the top income tax rate to 83% and the top rate on investment income to 98%, but this still did not cover spending needs.

[事实] Labour’s “social contract” gave unions stronger rights and protections in return for restraint on pay demands, but union leaders struggled to deliver restraint.

[推测] The social contract failed because union leaders faced pressure from members and competition from other unions seeking better settlements.

[20:20] **Union Power and National Gloom**

[事实] The government granted large pay increases, including 30% for railway workers, 31% for power workers, 32% for civil servants, and 35% for doctors.

[事实] By spring 1975, British prices were rising five times faster than in any other European country.

[事实] People outside powerful unions, including pensioners, the self-employed, the young, and savers, were especially harmed by inflation.

[事实] At a Chequers meeting, senior ministers admitted they did not know what to do; Jim Callaghan said that if he were a young man, he would emigrate.

[推测] The crisis created a sense that Britain’s governing class had lost confidence in the country’s future.

[22:48] **Britain’s International Reputation**

[事实] Britain’s population fell in 1975, 1976, and 1977, which the hosts link to emigration to countries such as New Zealand, Australia, South Africa, and Canada.

[事实] Henry Kissinger told Gerald Ford in January 1975 that Britain had become a “scrounger” while waiting for North Sea oil.

[事实] The *Wall Street Journal* warned that Britain was sinking and advised people with money to get it out of the country.

[事实] Northern Ireland was also damaging Britain’s international reputation, with hundreds dying each year.

[推测] Compared with today’s Britain, the hosts present the mid-1970s as a more severe economic and security crisis.

[25:05] **Why Europe Became the Next Crisis**

[事实] Britain had been a member of the European Community for two years when Wilson moved toward a referendum on whether to remain.

[事实] The hosts say Britain and Europe had not been a major topic on the podcast before partly because the subject can seem dull in British political culture.

[事实] The Treaty of Rome was signed in 1957 by six countries to build the European Economic Community and move toward closer union.

[事实] From London, early European integration was often seen as a project for defeated or continental powers rather than for Britain.

[推测] Britain’s ambivalence toward Europe was rooted in empire, the Commonwealth, wartime memory, Protestant suspicion of Catholic Europe, and a sense of national separateness.

[29:24] **From Imperial Distance to Reluctant Entry**

[事实] By the early 1960s, British elite opinion began shifting as empire faded, Suez damaged the US relationship, and the British economy lagged behind continental Europe.

[事实] Harold Macmillan applied to join in 1961, but Charles de Gaulle vetoed Britain’s application in January 1963.

[事实] Harold Wilson made another approach in 1967, which de Gaulle also rejected.

[事实] Ted Heath finally took Britain into the European Economic Community in January 1973.

[推测] The hosts argue Britain joined Europe out of weakness and lack of alternatives more than positive European enthusiasm.

[31:48] **Terms of Entry and Sovereignty**

[事实] Britain accepted thousands of pages of European rules on farming, fishing, and related matters upon entry.

[事实] The hosts say this later fed a long-running British complaint about having to swallow rules made before Britain joined.

[事实] Some Conservatives led by Enoch Powell opposed entry because they believed it threatened British sovereignty.

[事实] Denis Healey later said Heath could not have secured entry if he had been fully honest about the sovereignty implications.

[推测] The episode presents sovereignty as the central unresolved tension behind later Euroscepticism.

[34:03] **The Public’s Cultural Distance from Europe**

[事实] British people in the 1970s were increasingly taking foreign holidays and drinking more wine, but did not generally see themselves as Europeans.

[事实] Television depictions of Europe often involved comic holiday mishaps, German wartime stereotypes, or Second World War documentaries.

[事实] In 1970 only one in five people supported joining Europe, and a year later 70% opposed Heath’s application.

[事实] Surveys found many Britons disliked “European” customs such as pavement cafes, Sunday opening, continental breakfasts, and all-day pub opening.

[推测] Cultural unease mattered as much as constitutional or economic arguments in shaping public attitudes toward Europe.

[39:04] **Labour’s Split Over Europe**

[事实] Wilson was not instinctively pro-European and described himself as a Commonwealth man.

[事实] Labour was divided between pro-European figures such as Roy Jenkins and anti-European left-wing figures such as Michael Foot.

[事实] Foot described the European Community as a rich nations’ club hostile to British democracy and the economy.

[事实] Wilson’s solution was to say he supported Europe in principle but wanted to renegotiate Heath’s terms and then hold a referendum.

[推测] The referendum was primarily a party-management device designed to stop Labour splitting apart.

[41:35] **The Referendum Is Set**

[事实] At that point Britain had never held a referendum, partly because parliamentary sovereignty was treated as sufficient.

[事实] Wilson renegotiated with European leaders, who were annoyed but wanted Britain to remain inside the Community.

[事实] On 17 March 1975 Wilson told cabinet he had secured a good deal, though the hosts say it was largely similar to Heath’s deal.

[事实] The referendum was set for Thursday, 5 June 1975, with the question asking whether the United Kingdom should stay in the European Community.

[推测] Wilson’s renegotiation gave him political cover more than a fundamentally new European settlement.

[45:55] **A Slow and Unenthusiastic Campaign**

[事实] After the break, the hosts say the referendum campaign took a long time to become lively because voters were tired after two recent elections.

[事实] Wilson supported Yes but did so half-heartedly; Bernard Donoghue wrote that Wilson was at heart a “little Englander.”

[事实] Labour was deeply split, with seven cabinet ministers and around half its MPs backing Leave.

[事实] The unions were also divided, though some major unions opposed membership.

[推测] Public indifference made elite campaigning and press influence especially important.

[48:01] **Thatcher, Heath, and the Yes Campaign**

[事实] The Conservative Party was much more strongly pro-European in 1975 than it later became.

[事实] Margaret Thatcher supported staying in, arguing there was no genuine alternative to close European cooperation.

[事实] Thatcher appeared with Ted Heath despite their mutual hostility and later wore a jumper displaying European flags.

[事实] The Yes campaign had major business backing from companies including Shell, Ford, IBM, and ICI, and held a £1.5 million war chest.

[推测] In 1975, European membership looked to many Conservatives and businesses like a defence of free markets against socialism.

[52:21] **The No Campaign’s Strange Coalition**

[事实] The No campaign brought together Labour leftists, communists, anarchists, right-wing Conservatives, and fringe nationalist elements in some local areas.

[事实] Paul McCartney was one of the few celebrities to support leaving in 1975.

[事实] The No campaign had only £133,000, most of it from the government, compared with the Yes campaign’s £1.5 million.

[事实] The campaign’s main public figures included Enoch Powell, Michael Foot, and Tony Benn.

[推测] The No campaign’s ideological diversity made it look disorganised and politically eccentric to many voters.

[56:11] **Tony Benn as the Face of Leave**

[事实] Tony Benn was a defining Labour left figure in the 1970s and highly popular with left-wing party activists.

[事实] Benn opposed Europe because he saw it as a capitalist cartel that would block democratic socialism in one country.

[事实] He advocated workers’ control, industrial democracy, leaving NATO, and abandoning nuclear weapons.

[事实] Wilson distrusted Benn but made him industry secretary because he could not afford to alienate Labour’s left.

[推测] Benn’s prominence helped sharpen the sovereignty argument but also made the No campaign easier for opponents to portray as extreme.

[59:39] **Benn’s Industrial Plans and Public Controversy**

[事实] Benn wanted major companies to sign planning agreements with the state and wanted a National Enterprise Board to take over companies in the public interest.

[事实] He even wanted to use emergency powers to take over Britain’s 25 largest companies.

[事实] His plans alarmed the City and were restrained after pressure from Jim Callaghan and Denis Healey.

[事实] Benn backed worker cooperatives, including the Scottish Daily News, a Birmingham motorbike cooperative, and a Merseyside factory that proposed making car radiators and orange juice.

[推测] The discussion uses Benn’s industrial policy to show why business and much of the press feared Labour’s left.

[62:28] **Press Attacks and Benn’s Democratic Argument**

[事实] The press heavily attacked Benn, including cartoons and editorials portraying him as dangerous.

[事实] The *Times* called Benn a politician who could bring Britain to economic ruin and build a collectivist regime on the rubble.

[事实] Benn toured the country warning about Europe and democracy.

[事实] The hosts praise Benn, Foot, and Powell as powerful speakers even while disagreeing with much of their politics.

[推测] Benn’s strongest argument was that power should be removable by voters, which made European authority a democratic problem in his eyes.

[64:42] **The Quality of the 1975 Debate**

[事实] The hosts compare 1975 debates favourably with the 2016 Brexit referendum debates.

[事实] They cite debates involving Roy Jenkins, Tony Benn, and Enoch Powell as examples of deeper intellectual engagement.

[事实] They suggest the media ecosystem, the calibre of politicians, and audience attention spans have all changed.

[推测] The episode presents 1975 as a more serious and substantive political culture, at least in televised debate.

[65:56] **Why Yes Won Despite Euroscepticism**

[事实] Polls before and after 1975 showed that many Britons were Eurosceptic or indifferent toward Europe.

[事实] Newspapers were overwhelmingly pro-European during the campaign.

[事实] The *Daily Mail* warned that leaving could lead to shortages of items such as coffee, wine, beans, bananas, and oranges.

[事实] The hosts argue the economic crisis made voters reluctant to risk more instability.

[推测] Yes won not because voters loved Europe, but because remaining had become the status quo during a frightening economic moment.

[68:12] **The Result of the 1975 Referendum**

[事实] The final campaign event was a two-hour Oxford Union debate broadcast live on the BBC.

[事实] On Thursday 5 June 1975, Wilson voted Yes, while Mary Wilson and Marcia Williams voted No.

[事实] Turnout was about 65%, and 67% voted to stay in the European Community.

[事实] The most pro-European areas included Buckinghamshire, Surrey, and West Sussex; the least pro-European included Scotland and Northern Ireland.

[推测] The regional pattern is striking because it contrasts sharply with the 2016 Brexit referendum.

[69:28] **Aftermath: Heath, Benn, and the Unresolved Crisis**

[事实] The press celebrated the Yes victory, and the *Daily Mail* called it one of the most crushing victories in British political history.

[事实] Ted Heath was praised as a hero of the Yes campaign, though he reacted coldly to Thatcher’s tribute.

[事实] Tony Benn accepted the result and said he had received a clear message from the British people.

[事实] Wilson moved Benn from industry to energy after the referendum, weakening his position.

[推测] The referendum settled Labour’s immediate Europe problem but did not solve Britain’s economic emergency.

[71:17] **The Economic Tiger Remains**

[事实] After the referendum, a cartoon in *The Sun* showed Wilson’s government finishing with the “referendum sideshow” while a larger “economic crisis” tiger loomed behind them.

[事实] Inflation was almost 30%, and the pound began to slide shortly after the referendum.

[事实] Six weeks after the referendum, Wilson told Joe Haynes he had reached a dramatic decision, which the hosts leave for the next episode.

[推测] The episode frames Europe as a dramatic but secondary issue beside the deeper crisis of inflation, sterling, and state authority.

[72:40] **Club Preview: Photography and Fashion**

[事实] After the main episode, Dominic introduces a members-only series on photography and history with photographer Chris Floyd.

[事实] The preview discusses Glen Luchford’s Prada images and their cinematic quality.

[事实] Luchford connected the images to Bill Eppridge’s photograph of Robert Kennedy after the shooting at the Ambassador Hotel.

[事实] The discussion raises the photographer’s dilemma of documenting events versus intervening.

[推测] This preview is promotional rather than part of the main 1975 referendum narrative.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it connects high politics, economic crisis, popular culture, and political memory into one narrative. The use of *Fawlty Towers* makes the atmosphere of 1975 vivid before the discussion moves into inflation, Labour factionalism, and Europe.

[推测] Its strongest historical insight is that the 1975 referendum was not simply an early version of 2016. The alignments were different: Conservatives and big business largely backed staying in, Labour was split, Scotland was relatively Eurosceptic, and many voters chose stability rather than enthusiasm.

[推测] A limitation is that the tone is comic and personality-driven, so listeners looking for a technical account of European institutions or macroeconomic policy may find parts of the discussion indirect. The episode is best suited to listeners interested in British political history, Labour Party conflicts, Brexit’s prehistory, and the culture of the 1970s.
