# Coronations: The Deep Roots of a Living Ritual

## 概览

This episode presents the coronation of Charles III as the first British coronation since 1953 and as a rare chance to watch a civic ritual with unusually deep historical roots.

The hosts argue that British coronation is not just Victorian pageantry. Its order of service can be traced to 10th-century England, while some of its ideas reach back through Carolingian, Roman, biblical, Syrian and Egyptian traditions.

The discussion moves from regalia and Westminster Abbey to Edgar’s coronation in 973, then to pagan survivals, Scottish coronation stones, Roman imperial models and the theology of anointing. It ends by asking whether a modern audience will find the ritual moving, strange or absurd.

## 分段落总结

[01:13] **Coronation as ancient spectacle**

[事实] The hosts open with "Zadok the Priest," written by Handel for the coronation of George II in 1727.

[事实] They describe Charles III’s coronation as the first British coronation since 1953 and as a major national, patriotic and historical moment.

[事实] Tom argues that the ritual’s roots can be traced through the 10th century, Charlemagne, the Roman Empire, the Old Testament, and ultimately Egypt and Syria.

[推测] The comparison to seeing a mammoth stresses that the ceremony is valuable because it preserves ancient forms in living public practice.

[05:40] **Why coronations reveal history**

[事实] The episode is introduced as the first of a trilogy: the first covers deep roots, the second medieval, Tudor and Stuart coronations, and the third modern coronations.

[事实] The hosts compare coronations to the Olympics and World Cups because they reflect wider political, social, economic and ideological conditions.

[推测] The series frames coronations not only as royal ceremonies, but as windows into changing British and English history.

[07:25] **Regalia and surviving objects**

[事实] Much of the coronation regalia dates from Charles II’s coronation in 1661 because earlier royal regalia was largely destroyed during the interregnum.

[事实] Some ceremonial swords survived, but the only medieval piece of regalia said to survive is the spoon used for anointing.

[事实] The spoon may go back as far as the coronation of Richard I.

[推测] The survival of the anointing spoon gives the most sacred part of the ceremony a rare material link to medieval practice.

[09:43] **Edward the Confessor and Westminster**

[事实] Medieval people believed much of the older regalia ultimately came from Edward the Confessor.

[事实] Westminster Abbey is central because Edward the Confessor built it, and Henry III rebuilt it while preserving Edward’s memory.

[事实] The Cosmati pavement links the coronation site with Edward’s shrine, and Edward I’s coronation chair was also associated with the Confessor.

[推测] The ceremony uses place, objects and saintly memory to claim continuity across dynastic change.

[11:55] **The Liber Regalis**

[事实] The coronation order of service, or ordo, goes back beyond Edward the Confessor.

[事实] A key version is the Liber Regalis, associated with Richard II’s coronation in 1377 and still preserved at Westminster Abbey.

[事实] The hosts say later coronations adjusted the ritual to reflect Catholic or Protestant pressures, but the core structure remained.

[推测] The ritual survived because it could be adapted without losing its central symbolic logic.

[13:08] **Edgar’s coronation in 973**

[事实] Edgar was crowned at Bath in 973, and his coronation is presented as a foundation for later English coronation practice.

[事实] Edgar was remembered as Pacificus, the peace-giver, but the hosts stress that he imposed order brutally.

[事实] His coronation came several years into his reign and was likely shaped by Dunstan, Archbishop of Canterbury.

[推测] The late timing suggests the ceremony was meant to proclaim and legitimize a united English kingdom rather than simply begin Edgar’s rule.

[15:11] **Kingship, Rome and Pentecost**

[事实] Under Edgar, England was being held together as a more unitary kingdom, and Edgar asserted control over legal coinage.

[事实] Bath’s Roman remains helped evoke imperial authority, and Edgar used the title imperator.

[事实] The coronation took place on Pentecost, linking the king with the descent of the Holy Spirit.

[推测] The ceremony combined state-building, Roman inheritance and Christian sacred authority.

[16:32] **Humility and royal oaths**

[事实] The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle preserves Edgar’s coronation in both prose and poetry.

[事实] Edgar placed his crown on the altar and prostrated himself before it, and Dunstan was said to weep at the sight.

[事实] Edgar swore oaths to preserve peace, show justice and mercy, and refrain from violence.

[推测] This made kingship appear as service before God, not just domination over subjects.

[19:05] **Bishops and queens**

[事实] Charles III was to be escorted by the Bishops of Durham and Bath and Wells, echoing Edgar’s entrance with two bishops.

[事实] Dunstan’s role helped establish the Archbishop of Canterbury as the normal crowning figure, though there were exceptions.

[事实] Edgar’s wife Alfrith was anointed and crowned alongside him, helping establish the coronation of queens.

[推测] The ceremony’s continuity includes both stable patterns and exceptions shaped by politics and religion.

[23:00] **Pagan shadows and Columba**

[事实] The hosts say pre-Christian kings may have been inaugurated with helmets rather than crowns and raised on shields by warlords.

[事实] Athelstan seems to have been the first English king crowned with a crown rather than a helmet.

[事实] Saint Columba’s inauguration of Aidan in Dalriada is presented as the first clear account of Christian royal inauguration in Britain, though the account is later.

[推测] Dunstan’s ceremony may have Christianized older martial and local customs rather than inventing everything from scratch.

[26:11] **The Stone of Scone**

[事实] Athelstan and other kings were said to be crowned on a stone at Kingston upon Thames, though the authenticity is uncertain.

[事实] Scottish coronation practice is linked to Iona, Dunadd and Scone, with Dunadd’s foot-shaped rock symbolizing the king’s bond with the land.

[事实] The Stone of Scone, or Stone of Destiny, acquired legends connecting it to Jacob, Ireland, Tara, Columba and Scotland.

[事实] Edward I took the stone to Westminster Abbey to weaken Scottish kingship, and it was placed beneath the coronation chair.

[推测] The stone’s political power came from belief and symbolism more than from its physical origin.

[30:41] **Roman and Carolingian models**

[事实] Roman emperors came to use crowns and diadems, and by the late fifth century emperors were formally crowned in Hagia Sophia.

[事实] Westminster Abbey’s Cosmati pavement is ultimately modeled on the marble pavement of Hagia Sophia.

[事实] Charlemagne was crowned by the Pope in Rome on Christmas Day in 800, and Carolingian practice influenced English coronations.

[推测] English coronation borrowed Roman and Carolingian forms to make local kingship look imperial and sacred.

[34:06] **Anointing as the holiest moment**

[事实] Elizabeth II regarded anointing as the holiest part of the coronation, and Charles III’s anointing was not to be filmed.

[事实] The earliest royal anointing mentioned is Visigothic king Wamba in 672, while Pippin’s anointing at Soissons in 751 is treated as especially important.

[事实] The Carolingians used anointing to gain sacred legitimacy after replacing the Merovingians.

[推测] Anointing gave rulers a form of authority that inheritance or conquest alone could not provide.

[35:46] **Biblical kings and holy oil**

[事实] Louis the Pious was crowned and anointed in 816 after Charlemagne’s death.

[事实] Christian use of chrism drew on Old Testament practices of anointing priests and sacred objects.

[事实] The hosts connect royal anointing to Saul, David and Solomon, especially the biblical pattern behind "Zadok the Priest."

[推测] The episode treats anointing as the deepest bridge between medieval monarchy and biblical kingship.

[42:01] **Messiah, Christ and service**

[事实] Tom says "Messiah" and "Christ" both relate to the idea of the anointed one.

[事实] He argues that Israelite royal anointing may preserve traditions influenced by Syria and Egypt, where anointing had Bronze Age precedents.

[事实] The Israelite and Christian traditions reworked royal sacrality into the idea that the ruler serves God and the people.

[推测] The ritual makes sacred kingship less about divinizing the monarch and more about binding power to responsibility.

[44:29] **Modern reactions and future episodes**

[事实] The hosts say the meaning of coronation and anointing has changed over time with theology, culture and politics.

[事实] Tom describes the British coronation as basically the last surviving example of this ancient ritual form, with Tonga mentioned as a copy of the British version.

[事实] They ask whether modern viewers will be moved by the ancient sacred ritual or reject it as absurd in the 21st century.

[事实] The next episodes will examine how the ceremony’s spiritual grandeur survives contact with messy historical reality.

[推测] The central tension is between the ceremony’s immense symbolic weight and the flawed human figures who perform it.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value lies in making a familiar public event feel historically layered. It shows how one ceremony can preserve traces of Anglo-Saxon state-building, Roman imperial imagination, biblical theology and older Near Eastern ritual.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the movement between concrete details, such as the spoon, the coronation chair and the Stone of Scone, and large arguments about legitimacy, sacred power and service.

[推测] A limitation is that the conversation moves quickly across many periods, so listeners without background in early medieval history may find some names and transitions dense.

[推测] It is best suited to listeners interested in monarchy, ritual, British history, religious symbolism and the way ancient ideas survive inside modern public ceremonies.
