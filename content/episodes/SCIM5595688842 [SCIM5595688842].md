# Rick Rubin on Protocols for Creativity, Daily Practice, and Staying Open

## 概览

Andrew Huberman brings Rick Rubin back to answer listener questions about the creative process, especially practical questions about daily routines, blocks, uncertainty, idea capture, and how Rubin approaches making work without being governed by outcomes.

Rubin’s core position is that creativity works best when treated as a personal, honest act rather than a performance for approval. He repeatedly returns to ideas of diary-like honesty, loose beliefs, openness to surprise, and protecting the internal life that allows sensitive perception to become work.

The conversation also links creative practice with lifestyle practices: coherent breathing, meditation, sunlight, red-light evenings, movement, diet, sleep, and boundaries around stimulation. [推测] The episode frames creativity less as a specialized talent and more as a way of arranging attention, body state, relationships, and values.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode Frame and Listener Questions**

[事实] Huberman introduces Rick Rubin as a major music producer and author of *The Creative Act: A Way of Being*.
[事实] The episode is a follow-up Q&A built from thousands of listener and viewer questions.
[事实] Huberman says many questions ask about Rubin’s daily process, creative protocols, music-making, and dreams.
[推测] The episode is designed to move from Rubin’s general philosophy toward practical creative habits.

[02:40] **Coherent Breathing and HRV**

[事实] Rubin suggests doing a coherence breathing exercise and says he began it because his heart rate variability was low.
[事实] He describes using six breaths per minute and often counting during the practice.
[事实] Rubin aims for about 10 minutes daily and notices stronger HRV effects when he reaches 20 minutes.
[事实] He pairs the practice flexibly with activities such as air squats, treading water, sunlight, and podcast listening.

[07:58] **Meditation as a Long-Term Practice**

[事实] Rubin learned Transcendental Meditation at age 14 and says it remains a default practice for him.
[事实] His meditation can shift into breathing, gratitude, or metta loving-kindness phrases.
[事实] He says decades of meditation have made the benefits part of him, even when he is not practicing daily.
[推测] Rubin treats meditation less as an isolated session and more as a training that changes how one reacts in life.

[11:52] **Sunlight, Technology, and Natural Living**

[事实] Rubin tells a story about advising a dermatologist with insomnia that wearing sunglasses in the sun may interfere with sleep signals.
[事实] Huberman explains that morning and evening sunlight provide circadian cues through color contrasts.
[事实] They discuss avoiding Bluetooth headphones, keeping phones out of the bedroom, and reducing nighttime Wi-Fi or artificial stimulation.
[事实] Rubin says he tries to live naturally, eat few processed foods, and lost 135 pounds through a high-protein, low-calorie, low-carb diet.

[20:59] **AI, Childhood, and Magic**

[事实] Rubin says he does not know enough about AI to give a firm view, but he values the point of view of the person making art.
[事实] As a child, he loved reading, listening to music, playing along with guitar, and learning magic.
[事实] He says music eventually took over from magic as a full-time pursuit.
[事实] Rubin describes mentalism as his favorite form of magic because it feels pure and not dependent on props.

[24:32] **Writer’s Block and Judgment**

[事实] Rubin says creative blocks usually come from self-judgment or fear of outside judgment.
[事实] He frames each work as a diary entry: personal, honest, and not something others can invalidate.
[事实] He says ideas are always available, and a walk can provide a seed when nothing appears at the desk.
[推测] His method reduces pressure by separating honest making from public evaluation.

[30:05] **Deadlines, Uncertainty, and Sensitivity**

[事实] Rubin says deadlines do not help at the beginning of a project because early work needs openness and freedom.
[事实] He says deadlines can help near the end, after the project’s “code” has been cracked.
[事实] Rubin accepts uncertainty but still feels anxiety when starting new work because he wants it to be good.
[事实] He describes himself as unusually sensitive and says nurturing internal life requires sacrifices.

[36:27] **Wrestling and Reality**

[事实] Rubin says professional wrestling relaxes him because the performers protect each other and the stakes are not real in the ordinary sense.
[事实] He describes wrestling as dynamic, surreal theater that can feel more like the real world than straightforward narratives.
[事实] He connects wrestling to news and public stories, where the viewer often cannot know what is true.
[推测] Wrestling becomes an analogy for controlled danger, ambiguity, and the instability of public narratives.

[42:17] **Johnny Cash and Outcome-Free Work**

[事实] Rubin describes Johnny Cash as soulful, serious, shy, quiet, knowledgeable, humble, and honest.
[事实] He says Cash’s power came from the human being underneath, not only from singing or songwriting ability.
[事实] Rubin says asking whether work is “worth it” depends on outcome, and outcome is outside the artist’s control.
[事实] He describes making work as a devotional practice focused on doing the best thing possible.

[47:18] **Being a Vessel and Staying Open**

[事实] Rubin says ideas do not come only from the self; the person acts as a vessel that assembles information into a constellation.
[事实] He says loose beliefs, open-mindedness, and willingness to be surprised give the artist more material to work with.
[事实] Rubin says he updates his life when new information changes what works for him.
[事实] With artists, he handles resistance case by case, but says most resistance is already past by the time they work together.

[53:06] **Business, Project Timing, and Unfinished Ideas**

[事实] Rubin says he stays out of finances and negotiations and focuses on making the beautiful thing in the moment.
[事实] Huberman recalls Rubin redirecting a dinner conversation away from business and back toward art.
[事实] Rubin says he has many ideas that have not yet come to fruition, but he does not think of them as abandoned.
[事实] He works with many projects at different stages and avoids fighting projects when they are not moving.

[58:01] **Internet, Smartphones, and Tools**

[事实] Rubin says smartphones and the internet are both useful and difficult because all information is available, but sorting it can be hard.
[事实] He initially thought streaming would make him want to choose all his own music, then learned he enjoys outside curation and discovery.
[事实] They discuss YouTube as an archive for rare performances, lectures, old films, and Bible interpretation.
[事实] Rubin says tools do not make or break art; they can be used or misused.

[62:31] **Dreams, the Unconscious, and Motivation**

[事实] Rubin has kept dream journals during some periods and says later review can reveal patterns tied to life experience.
[事实] Huberman discusses Paul Conti’s view that the unconscious is the brain’s supercomputer.
[事实] Rubin says instinct and the unconscious are where great ideas live, while purely intellectual ideas carry less charge.
[事实] Rubin uses Andrew Dice Clay as an example of an artist whose work suffered when he tried to please critics rather than stay true to his audience.

[68:08] **Advice, Drafting, and Journaling**

[事实] Rubin’s advice to a starting comedian is to be true to yourself and not listen to anyone.
[事实] He advises Huberman to complete a full book draft before getting stuck refining individual details.
[事实] Huberman describes his own diary entries as one to eight handwritten pages that help clear frustration or capture positive reflection.
[事实] Huberman explains James Pennebaker’s expressive writing protocol as repeated writing about a difficult or traumatic experience.

[73:55] **Industry, Idea Capture, and Money**

[事实] Rubin says he focuses on the work and does not see himself as part of the entertainment industry machine.
[事实] He captures ideas as written notes on his phone, though he sometimes later cannot remember what a note means.
[事实] Rubin calls money another form of energy but says he tries not to think about it.
[事实] He says punk DIY taught him to make work with whatever means are available.

[79:17] **Obscurity and Creative Constraints**

[事实] Rubin says he has never withheld something because it seemed too obscure for a mainstream audience.
[事实] He says many major creative moves were described to him as terrible ideas, including work with Ghetto Boys, LL Cool J, Beastie Boys, Slayer, Adele, Eminem, and Johnny Cash.
[事实] Rubin’s Tetragrammaton ads arose from solving his discomfort with conventional host-read advertisements.
[推测] The episode presents constraints as chances to make something more personal rather than as reasons to conform.

[83:30] **Alcohol, Peer Pressure, and Psychedelics**

[事实] Rubin says he does not drink alcohol and only tasted it once as part of a class experiment.
[事实] He says alcohol and drugs were never interesting to him.
[事实] He suggests being an only child may have made him less vulnerable to peer pressure, but marks that as a guess.
[事实] Rubin is curious about psychedelics but has never used them; Huberman discusses psilocybin, ibogaine, and MDMA in therapeutic contexts with risks.

[89:08] **Freedom, Organization, and Shock**

[事实] Rubin says early creativity can look chaotic, but he prefers to call it free, playful, and open.
[事实] Once something interesting appears, the process becomes more controlled.
[事实] He says Ghetto Boys pushed rap into horror-rap territory beyond earlier boasting, social commentary, and gangster rap.
[事实] Rubin says he does not like horror or visual adrenaline, and some films can affect his mood for a long time.

[96:16] **News, Wonder, and Uncertain Stories**

[事实] Rubin says he treats news like wrestling: a story may be interesting without being assumed true.
[事实] Huberman says he has seen media publish false claims despite him giving no information.
[事实] Rubin values childlike wonder and gives an example where explaining luminescence reduced a friend’s mystical experience.
[事实] The Paul Ramone/Ramones and Henry Rollins/Johnny Cash/Joe Strummer stories illustrate how memory and rumor can become uncertain “facts.”

[103:50] **Daily Routine and Evening Wind-Down**

[事实] Rubin’s non-travel mornings often include waking slowly, going into the sun, walking on the beach or going to the gym, and sometimes stretching.
[事实] He avoids work until around 11 a.m. or 1 p.m., then does focused work until about 6 p.m.
[事实] After sunset he uses red glasses and red light, spends time with family, avoids stimulating work calls, and is usually in bed by 10.
[事实] Huberman says Rubin taught him to switch his phone to a red display at night and that he sleeps better because of it.

[113:33] **Education, Home, Relationships, and Books**

[事实] Rubin says formal education seems obsolete unless the subject can only be learned in an institution.
[事实] He favors internships, mentors, and learning directly from practitioners.
[事实] Rubin says home should be a safe place from which a person can go out and do demanding creative work.
[事实] He says honest relationships require both people to tell the truth about how they see things.

[120:02] **Mystery, Listening, Albums, and Videos**

[事实] On ancestral or genetic influence in art, Rubin says it is possible but unknowable and not necessarily helpful to know.
[事实] As a producer, he listens for whether music makes him lean forward, surprises him, works, and carries intention from the first sound.
[事实] He says albums can be made in a weekend or over years, and neither timeline is inherently better.
[事实] Rubin appears in music videos only when artists ask; he says videos can improve a song but can also limit the listener’s imagination.

[129:17] **Current Work and Podcasting**

[事实] Rubin says he is working on documentary projects and albums, including projects involving Marcus King and The Gossip.
[事实] For documentaries, he watches many examples to learn what formats he does not want to repeat.
[事实] He describes Tetragrammaton as an intimate, overheard conversation rather than a performance aimed at the audience.
[事实] Huberman closes by saying the discussion fleshes out Rubin’s practical process and thanks him as an educator and friend.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its combination of concrete routines and a clear creative philosophy: breathe, meditate, protect attention, gather ideas, stay open, and make work honestly before thinking about outcome.

[推测] Its strongest moments come when Rubin turns common creative anxieties into simpler principles, especially the diary-entry model, the distinction between early freedom and later control, and the warning that outcome-thinking undermines the work.

[推测] Its limitation is that many health and workflow practices are personal and anecdotal, even when Huberman adds scientific context. Listeners looking for a rigid step-by-step creativity system may find Rubin’s approach intentionally open-ended.

[推测] This episode is best suited for writers, musicians, podcasters, artists, entrepreneurs, and anyone trying to build a more honest relationship with attention, routine, and uncertainty.
