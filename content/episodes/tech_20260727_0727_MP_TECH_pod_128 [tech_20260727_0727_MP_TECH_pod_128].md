# AI Nudify Apps Are Spreading Through Mainstream Platforms

## 概览

This episode examines how AI tools that create sexualized images of real people have become a multi-million-dollar industry, even though non-consensual intimate imagery is banned by many platforms and restricted by law.

Sina Laubenstein of the Institute for Strategic Dialogue says her team quickly found 181 websites offering tools that generate “nudified” images or videos from real people’s photos. The report found that mainstream platforms, especially YouTube and X, helped drive traffic to these services, while Facebook and Instagram also hosted ads promoting them.

The discussion argues that enforcement cannot focus only on social media platforms. App stores, payment networks, crypto intermediaries, and legal mechanisms all form part of the ecosystem that allows these services to remain accessible and profitable.

## 分段落总结

[00:01] **Nudify tools and platform gateways**

[事实] The episode opens by saying nudified images are generally banned online, but tools for making them are being promoted across social media.
[事实] The Institute for Strategic Dialogue reported that AI apps creating sexualized images of real people have grown into a multi-million-dollar industry.
[事实] Social media platforms are described as de facto gateways to these services, despite policies against non-consensual intimate imagery.
[推测] The framing suggests the main problem is not only the existence of the tools, but also how easily mainstream platforms help users find them.

[00:57] **The scale of the ecosystem**

[事实] Sina Laubenstein says her team found 181 websites offering tools that let users upload images of real people and generate nudified images or videos.
[事实] She says traffic to these sites was driven by major platforms including YouTube, X, Facebook, and Instagram.
[事实] YouTube and X are described as especially strong drivers of traffic.
[推测] The findings imply that access to these services is not limited to obscure internet spaces.

[01:50] **How the tools are promoted**

[事实] Laubenstein says the tools appeared through simple searches such as “nudified tool” or searches for nudified content.
[事实] On Meta platforms, the researchers found ads promoting these tools on Facebook and Instagram.
[事实] One set of identical ads promoting a nudify tool was posted more than 600 times, and some ads ran for months.
[事实] The ads often used stock or AI-generated images and repeated plain text, rather than coded language.
[推测] The lack of coded language suggests enforcement failures are not mainly caused by hidden or evasive terminology.

[04:18] **Platform and app store responses**

[事实] Laubenstein says some platforms mentioned in the report, as well as some not mentioned, contacted the researchers for URLs and keywords to help remove content.
[事实] App stores also took action by removing some apps or changing search settings so users were not guided as easily to these tools.
[事实] She says this does not mean the apps no longer exist in app stores.
[推测] The response shows that platforms can reduce visibility when given specific evidence, but the underlying availability problem remains.

[05:35] **Legal limits and enforcement challenges**

[事实] The host notes that the tools appear to violate platform terms of service and app store rules.
[事实] The host also says non-consensual intimate imagery is illegal at the federal level in the United States under the Take It Down Act, and many states have laws banning it.
[事实] Laubenstein says the technology has existed in different forms for years and that newer areas such as AI companions, AI girlfriends, wearable devices, and smart devices may not be covered by legislation.
[推测] The discussion suggests law and policy often lag behind how these technologies evolve.

[06:18] **Payments and intervention points**

[事实] The report found that nudify websites used trusted payment options such as PayPal, Mastercard, or Visa while routing transactions through crypto intermediaries.
[事实] Laubenstein says these arrangements can bypass banking oversight.
[事实] She argues that policymakers should look at banking mechanisms and other intervention points, not only social media platforms.
[推测] The episode presents financial infrastructure as a practical pressure point for reducing the profitability of these services.

[06:57] **App store accountability**

[事实] The host mentions that San Francisco’s city attorney sent cease-and-desist letters to Apple and Google under California law.
[事实] The letters urged the companies to stop profiting from offering nudify apps.
[事实] Laubenstein says using California law is another way to move enforcement forward.
[事实] She says app stores make money from these apps and should improve their response, just as platforms should.
[推测] The segment implies that accountability may need to extend to companies that distribute or monetize these apps, even if they do not create the content themselves.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it treats AI-generated non-consensual imagery as an ecosystem problem rather than a narrow content moderation issue. The strongest point is the concrete detail that promotion was visible through plain searches, repeated ads, and mainstream platform traffic.

The discussion is concise and focused, with clear examples involving social platforms, app stores, payment systems, and state-level enforcement. It is especially useful for listeners interested in platform governance, online safety, AI abuse, and tech regulation.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode relies mainly on the report’s findings and does not include direct responses from the named platforms or app stores. [推测] It is best suited for listeners who want a high-level policy and enforcement overview rather than a technical explanation of how the tools work.
