# Genes, Behavior, and Human Adaptation with Dr. Melissa Allardo

## 概览

Andrew Huberman speaks with Dr. Melissa Allardo about human genetics, epigenetics, and the old “nature versus nurture” question. The conversation emphasizes that some traits are strongly inherited, while gene expression can also shift in response to behavior, environment, trauma, starvation, and selection pressures.

A major thread is human adaptation under extreme conditions. Allardo discusses sea-nomad divers in Indonesia and women divers in Korea, using them to explain the mammalian dive reflex, spleen contraction, oxygen availability, thyroid-linked genetic variants, pregnancy-related blood pressure adaptations, and how cultural practices can interact with biology over generations.

The episode later widens into mate selection, immune-system diversity, underwater vision, elite physical and cognitive performance, genetic determinism, mindset effects, CRISPR, embryo sequencing, admixture, and whether human genetic variation challenges the idea that Homo sapiens is one species.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode Setup and Main Themes**

[事实] Huberman introduces Dr. Melissa Allardo as a professor of biomedical informatics and an expert in human genetics and epigenetics.
[事实] The episode is framed around how behavior and environment can shape gene expression, including across generations.
[事实] Huberman previews topics including eye color, the mammalian dive reflex, mate selection, immune-system differences, external traits, organ function, and gene editing.

[02:40] **Nature, Nurture, and Modifiable Gene Expression**

[事实] Allardo says science is still learning how much gene expression can be changed by environment and behavior.
[事实] She distinguishes between things “written in our genes” and newer evidence that more traits may be modifiable than previously assumed.
[推测] The discussion treats genetics less as a fixed script and more as a set of biological potentials shaped by context.

[05:24] **Eye Color and Inherited Traits**

[事实] Allardo says everyone with blue eyes descends from one original person who first had that eye-color change.
[事实] Brown eyes are described as the original human eye color, while green eyes are discussed as rare and likely involving multiple genetic origins.
[事实] Huberman notes that eye color is mostly not behaviorally changeable, while also raising the possibility that ultraviolet exposure can darken eyes over time.

[10:33] **Epigenetics Across Minutes, Lifetimes, and Generations**

[事实] Allardo says genes constantly change expression in response to environmental stimuli, sometimes over minutes or hours.
[事实] She describes epigenetic modifications as molecules attaching to the genome and changing gene expression.
[事实] Refugee trauma and the Dutch famine are discussed as examples where epigenetic changes may be inherited across generations.
[推测] The famine example suggests that a once-adaptive response can become less beneficial when the environment changes.

[14:05] **Mate Selection, Smell, and Immune Diversity**

[事实] Allardo discusses studies where people rated sweaty T-shirts from potential mates and were more attracted to people with immune-system profiles different from their own.
[事实] Huberman connects this to major histocompatibility complex differences and the possibility of broader immune protection in offspring.
[推测] Smell is presented as one unconscious channel through which mate preference may reflect biological compatibility.

[15:02] **Hybrid Vigor, Inbreeding, and Global Mixing**

[事实] Allardo says globalization creates genetic combinations that were previously impossible in human history.
[事实] She says close relatives reproducing increases the chance that harmful variants will combine in offspring.
[事实] The discussion contrasts genetic mixing outside close family lines with the disease risks associated with incest.

[18:41] **Humans Are Still Evolving**

[事实] Allardo says humans continue to evolve as long as traits affect reproduction.
[事实] She gives Tibetan high-altitude adaptation as an example likely involving genetic material from Denisovans.
[事实] The gene became advantageous when ancestral Tibetans moved into high-altitude environments.
[推测] Existing variation can remain neutral until a new environment makes it valuable.

[23:12] **Evolution Is Not a March Toward Perfection**

[事实] Allardo criticizes the classic “ape to human” evolution diagram because it implies humans are the pinnacle of evolution.
[事实] She reframes “survival of the fittest” as “best fit,” meaning evolution favors fit to environment rather than general superiority.
[事实] Most mutations are described as harmful, and many are never seen because they prevent viable development.

[25:13] **Standing Variation and Faster Adaptation**

[事实] Allardo explains “standing variation” as genetic variation already present in a population.
[事实] She says adaptation can happen faster when an environment makes existing variation useful, rather than waiting for a new beneficial mutation.
[事实] Breath-hold diving is introduced as a behavior that may select for existing advantageous variation.

[27:06] **Bajo Sea Nomads and Breath-Hold Diving**

[事实] Allardo studied the Bajo in Indonesia, a sea-nomad population traditionally living at sea and obtaining resources through fishing and breath-hold diving.
[事实] She was told of a Bajo diver holding his breath for 13 minutes, though she did not personally observe or record it.
[事实] Bajo children are described as spending so much time in water that some learn to swim before walking.
[事实] Huberman and Allardo warn that breath-hold diving is dangerous and should not be self-tested without expert training.

[35:57] **Mammalian Dive Reflex and the Spleen**

[事实] Allardo explains that holding the breath and immersing the face in cold water can trigger the mammalian dive reflex.
[事实] The response includes slowed heart rate, constricted blood vessels in the extremities, and spleen contraction.
[事实] The spleen stores oxygen-carrying red blood cells and can release them into circulation, giving roughly a 10% oxygen boost during breath holding.
[事实] The spleen later reabsorbs those red blood cells after the breath hold ends.

[41:04] **Why Mammals Have a Dive Reflex**

[事实] Allardo says facial immersion is important because vagal nerve stimulation helps trigger the dive reflex.
[事实] She says the reflex exists across mammals and has even been measured in mice trained to dive.
[事实] She considers it more likely that the response comes from a very old mammalian ancestor than from a specifically human “aquatic ape” explanation.

[46:07] **Infants, Bajo Traditions, and Larger Spleens**

[事实] Allardo says babies show innate breath-holding responses, including when air is blown on their face.
[事实] She describes a Bajo story that babies are passed under a canoe as a test, while also stating she does not know whether this is actually practiced.
[事实] Her study found the Bajo had spleens about 50% larger on average than a nearby farming population.
[事实] The larger spleens appeared in both divers and non-divers, suggesting a likely genetic component.

[49:51] **Thyroid Hormone, Red Blood Cells, and Performance Questions**

[事实] Allardo says a gene evolving in the Bajo correlates with higher-than-average thyroid hormone levels, larger spleens, higher hemoglobin, higher hematocrit, and higher red blood cell count.
[事实] Mouse work replicated larger spleens and increased red blood cell count without changes in erythropoietin.
[推测] This may point to a performance-relevant oxygen mechanism distinct from the erythropoietin pathway used in some endurance doping contexts.
[事实] Huberman raises possible immune and performance implications, while Allardo says direct studies on immune enhancement from the dive reflex have not been done.

[55:25] **Jeju Haenyeo Women Divers**

[事实] Allardo describes the Haenyeo of Jeju, Korea, as an all-female diving population with an average current age around 70.
[事实] The Haenyeo historically dove in very cold water without wetsuits and sometimes continued diving throughout pregnancy.
[事实] Current tracked dives tended to be shallower, often not deeper than about 10 meters, while older reports for Bajo divers include much deeper dives.
[事实] The Haenyeo are now recognized with UNESCO intangible cultural heritage status.

[64:22] **Diet, Diving, and Cardiovascular Adaptation**

[事实] Allardo says diet is a strong driver of selection and cites lactase persistence and Greenlandic Inuit lipid metabolism as examples.
[事实] In the Haenyeo, a lifetime of training is associated with a stronger diving-related heart-rate drop.
[事实] One diver’s heart rate dropped more than 40 beats per minute in less than 15 seconds.
[事实] Allardo also describes a genetic variant linked to lower diastolic blood pressure while diving, which may protect pregnant divers from hypertensive disorders.

[70:12] **Miscarriage, Visible Traits, and Internal Physiology**

[事实] Allardo says most mutations create non-viable embryos, especially when important proteins affect many systems.
[事实] Huberman raises examples where external traits in lab animals can correlate with internal organ or neural differences.
[事实] Allardo says genes such as phosphodiesterases can affect many functions beyond the system being studied.
[推测] External attractiveness or visible traits could sometimes correlate with hidden physiological advantages, but the transcript does not provide direct evidence for a specific human marker.

[79:11] **Underwater Vision and Training**

[事实] Allardo discusses Moken children who were found to see underwater better than European children.
[事实] The same researcher later trained European children to perform similarly underwater.
[事实] Allardo argues that trainability does not prove there was no genetic or developmental advantage in the original population.
[事实] Huberman explains that underwater vision can improve through strong pupil constriction rather than necessarily flattening the eye.

[82:55] **Elite Physical and Cognitive Performance**

[事实] Huberman uses elite marathon running and Eliud Kipchoge as examples of extraordinary human performance linked to specific world regions.
[事实] Allardo says some East African runners may combine biomechanical advantages with physiological adaptation to altitude.
[事实] The discussion then turns to cognitive traits, autism-spectrum traits, engineering, hyperfocus, Tourette’s, and processing speed.
[事实] Allardo says it is difficult to connect traits like creativity or mathematical ability to genes because those traits are hard to quantify and strongly shaped by environment.

[93:04] **Genetic Determinism and Mindset**

[事实] Allardo warns against genetic determinism, the idea that genes determine everything about a person.
[事实] She describes a study where people told they had favorable training genetics improved more, even though the groups did not actually differ genetically.
[事实] Huberman connects this to mindset research showing that beliefs about stress can change health outcomes.
[推测] Genetic information can motivate people, but it can also constrain them if interpreted too rigidly.

[97:06] **CRISPR, Embryo Sequencing, and Ethics**

[事实] Huberman discusses the case of genetically modified babies in China and frames it as deliberate gene insertion or removal in embryos.
[事实] Allardo says CRISPR has been a blunt tool, with off-target effects as a major concern, though the technology is improving rapidly.
[事实] She says preventing disease would be a dream use case, but the boundary between correction, normal variation, and enhancement is ethically difficult.
[事实] Embryo sequencing in IVF is discussed as costly and potentially unequal, especially when tests identify correlations rather than clear causal outcomes.

[106:42] **Admixture and Whether Humans Are One Species**

[事实] Allardo defines admixture as the mixing of different ancestry populations.
[事实] She says admixture complicates genetic studies because ancestry differences can confuse analysis if not quantified.
[事实] She says the scale matters: someone may be admixed at one ancestry level but not another.
[事实] Allardo says she does not know of any human population with enough genetic difference to call it a separate species.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value lies in connecting abstract genetics to concrete human behaviors: choosing mates, diving, surviving at altitude, eating specific diets, training vision, and interpreting genetic tests. The strongest sections are the real-world population examples, especially the Bajo and Haenyeo, because they show how culture, ecology, physiology, and selection can interact.

The episode is also careful in several places: breath-hold diving is repeatedly treated as dangerous, the immune and longevity implications are not overstated, and Allardo often distinguishes between proven findings and open questions. That restraint makes the discussion more useful than a simple “genes explain everything” narrative.

[推测] The main limitation is that the conversation ranges widely, so some topics such as creativity, autism-spectrum traits, embryo sequencing, and CRISPR ethics are introduced more as framing questions than fully resolved scientific reviews.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in human biology, evolutionary medicine, performance, genetics, and the ethical future of genetic testing and editing.
