# Iran’s Revolution, Khomeini’s Return, and Jimmy Carter’s Crisis

## 概览

This episode follows the decisive months from late 1978 into 1979, when the Shah’s regime collapsed, Ayatollah Khomeini returned from exile, and Iran’s revolution moved from mass protest to Islamic rule.

The hosts frame the crisis through three main figures: the Shah, weakened by illness and indecision; Khomeini, austere but media-savvy and ideologically radical; and Jimmy Carter, whose administration was divided, poorly informed, and increasingly overwhelmed by the global consequences of Iran’s turmoil.

A central theme is misreading. Western observers and American officials underestimated the religious and revolutionary force behind Khomeini, while many Iranian liberals and leftists believed they could use him as a temporary figurehead. The episode argues that both assumptions quickly proved dangerous.

The second half connects Iran’s revolution to America’s domestic crisis: oil disruption, inflation, gas lines, Carter’s “malaise” speech, and the loss of confidence that set the stage for the coming embassy hostage crisis.

## 分段落总结

[03:00] **Khomeini’s Muharram Message**

[事实] The episode opens with Khomeini’s November 1978 message from Paris, framed around Muharram, sacrifice, martyrdom, and resistance to tyranny.

[事实] The hosts say this message helped trigger the final uprisings that led to the Shah’s downfall and Khomeini’s return from exile.

[推测] The opening positions religious symbolism as the emotional and political engine of the revolution, rather than as a secondary decoration around ordinary politics.

[04:16] **The Three Central Figures**

[事实] The hosts recap the Shah as a ruler who used oil wealth to modernize Iran but was widely seen as corrupt, repressive, weak, and dependent on America.

[事实] Khomeini is presented as an elderly Shiite cleric, long exiled in Iraq, who fiercely opposed the Shah’s secularizing and modernizing program.

[事实] Jimmy Carter is described as an evangelical, Southern populist, and technocratic micromanager who initially backed the Shah as part of America’s anti-communist alliance system.

[推测] The episode sets up the crisis as a collision between monarchy, revolutionary Islam, and uncertain American Cold War strategy.

[07:07] **The Revolutionary Coalition**

[事实] By autumn 1978, demonstrations, riots, and repression had created a revolutionary cycle in Iran.

[事实] The anti-Shah coalition included bazaar merchants, urban artisans, rural migrants, students, and Shiite clerics alienated by the Shah’s policies.

[事实] The Cinema Rex fire of August 1978, which killed about 500 people, intensified anger after it was blamed on the Shah’s secret police.

[推测] The coalition was broad enough to destroy the old regime, but its members did not necessarily share the same vision for what should replace it.

[08:26] **The Shah’s Paralysis**

[事实] The Shah failed either to appease the crowds or decisively clear the streets.

[事实] The hosts link his passivity partly to leukemia and describe him as increasingly listless, unhappy, and disengaged.

[事实] His generals reportedly begged him to authorize a crackdown, but he said he would not preside over a massacre.

[推测] The episode treats the Shah’s combination of repression and weakness as especially fatal for his regime.

[11:04] **America Fails to Understand the Revolution**

[事实] The hosts say American officials still had not grasped that much of the opposition to the Shah was theological and could not be solved through ordinary secular reforms.

[事实] Carter’s foreign policy team was split between Secretary of State Cyrus Vance, who was cautious and dovish, and National Security Advisor Zbigniew Brzezinski, who was hawkish and anti-Soviet.

[事实] This bureaucratic rivalry contributed to confusion over whether to keep backing the Shah or seek contact with Khomeini.

[推测] Washington’s internal divisions made it slower to adapt just as events in Iran were accelerating.

[14:16] **Khomeini Leaves Iraq for Paris**

[事实] Saddam Hussein’s Iraq expelled Khomeini in September 1978 after tolerating his exile there for years.

[事实] Kuwait and Syria would not take him, so Khomeini accepted advice to go to the West, where he would have freer access to the global press.

[事实] On October 6, 1978, Khomeini flew to Paris and settled near the city, becoming an international media figure.

[推测] Moving to France, despite Khomeini’s anti-Western views, gave him a modern communications platform that helped amplify his revolution.

[18:23] **Khomeini’s Media Strategy and Ideology**

[事实] In Paris, Khomeini gave around 130 interviews to the world press.

[事实] His public message to Western audiences downplayed Islamism and emphasized opposition to the Shah, corruption, secret police, and foreign domination.

[事实] The hosts explain his doctrine of velayat-e faqih, or guardianship of the jurist, as the basis for a future Islamic Republic.

[事实] This doctrine was both conservative in its grounding in Shiite law and revolutionary because it proposed clerical rule over the state.

[推测] Khomeini’s vagueness was politically useful because different anti-Shah groups could project their own hopes onto him.

[25:12] **Muharram and Ashura Mobilize the Streets**

[事实] Khomeini’s Muharram message urged sacrifice and resistance, drawing on Shiite themes of martyrdom and the Battle of Karbala.

[事实] During the mourning period, thousands took to the streets in white shrouds and confronted the Shah’s soldiers.

[事实] On Ashura, December 10-11, 1978, massive crowds in Tehran chanted against the Shah and America.

[推测] The episode presents Shiite ritual time as a powerful structure for revolutionary mobilization.

[27:46] **The Missed American Opening**

[事实] Ambassador William Sullivan believed the United States should contact Khomeini and explore a possible deal because Khomeini also opposed communism.

[事实] Brzezinski strongly rejected this approach and argued that America should stay with the Shah and encourage force.

[事实] Carter read material about Khomeini and dismissed him as irrational, refusing to send an emissary to Paris.

[推测] The hosts argue that Carter missed a critical window, even if contact with Khomeini might not have prevented an Islamic Republic.

[31:12] **Oil Shutdown and Regime Breakdown**

[事实] By late December 1978, many Americans had left Iran, but about 10,000 remained.

[事实] Paul Grim, a senior oil executive, was killed by militants on December 23, prompting oil companies to evacuate more workers.

[事实] Iran’s oil fields were largely shut down by the end of December, causing fuel rationing inside Iran and rising oil prices abroad.

[推测] The revolution had moved from a domestic political crisis to a global economic shock.

[33:21] **Bakhtiar and the Shah’s Exit Plan**

[事实] On the last day of 1978, the Shah dismissed his military government and appointed the moderate liberal Shapoor Bakhtiar as prime minister.

[事实] The Shah told Sullivan he understood he would have to leave Iran.

[事实] Sullivan helped arrange a possible American refuge at Walter Annenberg’s house in Palm Springs, though the Shah planned first to visit Egypt.

[推测] Bakhtiar’s appointment came too late to restore legitimacy because he was still seen as tied to the Shah.

[34:16] **The Shah Leaves Iran**

[事实] On January 19, 1979, the Shah and Empress Farah left the palace for Tehran Airport.

[事实] The airport was largely deserted because strikes had shut it down, while Iran’s cities were suffering fuel shortages and disorder.

[事实] As the Shah departed, crowds in Tehran celebrated, tore down symbols of the monarchy, and burned portraits.

[推测] The departure marked the effective end of the Pahlavi monarchy before any stable replacement had been created.

[38:32] **Khomeini Returns**

[事实] On February 1, 1979, Khomeini returned to Iran on an Air France 747 after 15 years in exile.

[事实] Huge crowds, reporters, clerics, and airport staff greeted him, making the return one of the iconic scenes of 20th-century history.

[事实] Khomeini immediately spoke against foreign influence and asked God to remove evil foreigners and their helpers from Iran.

[推测] His return combined Islamic authority, anti-imperial rhetoric, and Iranian nationalism in a way that Western observers often failed to understand.

[42:36] **The Vacuum of Power**

[事实] Bakhtiar was still formally prime minister, but Khomeini had the initiative and refused to recognize his government.

[事实] Khomeini created a revolutionary council at a girls’ school in Tehran and appointed Mehdi Bazargan as his own rival prime minister.

[事实] Bazargan promised a constituent assembly, a new constitution, and democratic elections.

[推测] Liberal and secular revolutionaries appear to have believed Khomeini could be used as a transitional symbol, but the episode suggests this was a grave miscalculation.

[45:40] **Islamic Republic Versus Republican Politics**

[事实] Khomeini warned that the new government would be based on Sharia and that opposing it meant opposing Islam.

[事实] The hosts describe an enduring tension in the Islamic Republic between republican forms, such as elections and parliament, and Islamic foundations that limit opposition.

[事实] Women retained the vote, but the new regime began reversing some rights associated with the Shah’s modernization.

[推测] The revolution’s hybrid structure made it harder for outsiders and some Iranians to see how quickly religious authority could dominate political institutions.

[47:04] **The Army Stands Aside**

[事实] Carter had sent General Huyser to encourage Iranian generals to stand firm and prepare for a coup.

[事实] The generals could not rely on their own conscripts, many of whom were drawn to Khomeini’s message and began deserting.

[事实] On February 11, 1979, the army declared neutrality and ordered troops to remain in barracks.

[事实] Khomeini’s supporters then took over ministries, prisons, police stations, and television stations.

[推测] The army’s neutrality was the decisive institutional collapse that allowed the revolution to become a new regime.

[49:12] **America’s Shock and Economic Exposure**

[事实] Many Americans had paid little attention to Iran until the Shah left.

[事实] Henry Kissinger blamed liberal and human-rights pressures for undermining the Shah and warned that his fall would be disastrous.

[事实] Major American companies, including Gillette, Pepsi, Colgate, Coke, Johnson & Johnson, and General Motors, had investments in Iran that were now at risk.

[推测] The episode links elite geopolitical anxiety with corporate financial exposure, showing why Iran quickly became a major American concern.

[51:23] **Oil Panic in the United States**

[事实] Iran was the world’s second-largest oil exporter, but production had been disrupted since December 1978.

[事实] Crude oil prices rose sharply, and fears spread that revolution could affect other oil-producing countries.

[事实] American consumers faced gas lines, rationing, and inflationary pressure.

[推测] For ordinary Americans, the Iranian Revolution mattered less as foreign news than as a crisis in prices, fuel, and daily life.

[54:00] **Carter’s Energy Response**

[事实] On April 5, 1979, Carter told Americans they would have to pay more for gasoline and asked them to drive less, carpool, use buses, walk, and save energy.

[事实] The hosts emphasize how unusual it was for an American president to speak in such austere, self-denying terms.

[事实] By spring 1979, some Democrats and labor figures were already questioning Carter’s leadership.

[推测] Carter’s moral seriousness became a political liability because many voters wanted practical relief rather than a lecture on sacrifice.

[57:48] **Conspiracy, Nationalism, and Gas-Line Violence**

[事实] Polls showed many Americans believed hidden forces were controlling the energy crisis.

[事实] Some public anger took nationalist and anti-Middle Eastern forms, including aggressive slogans about taking oil by force.

[事实] The gas crisis peaked on June 23-24, 1979, when about two thirds of American gas stations had closed.

[事实] Levittown, Pennsylvania saw riots, looting, fires, and a local state of emergency.

[推测] The hosts draw a parallel between Iranian and American conspiracy thinking during moments of crisis.

[58:53] **The “Malaise” Speech**

[事实] Carter canceled a planned energy address after advisers persuaded him the crisis reflected a deeper spiritual and national loss of confidence.

[事实] He consulted many intellectuals and then gave a televised speech on July 15, 1979, about consumerism, purpose, and a crisis of confidence.

[事实] Afterward, he purged several cabinet officials, and his approval rating fell to 23%.

[推测] The speech became a symbol of Carter’s perceived weakness because it diagnosed the national mood without giving voters the confidence they expected from a president.

[63:00] **Carter’s Public Humiliation**

[事实] The hosts describe growing contempt for Carter in the press and Washington establishment.

[事实] Carter collapsed during a 10K run near Camp David, reinforcing a public image of physical and political exhaustion.

[事实] A later press story claimed a swimming rabbit had attacked Carter while he was fishing, and Carter had to clarify that he had only splashed water at it.

[推测] The rabbit story became politically damaging because it fit an existing narrative that Carter was weak and out of control.

[65:48] **Iran’s Revolutionary Consolidation**

[事实] While America was consumed by energy politics, Iran’s streets were increasingly controlled by paramilitary groups loyal to Khomeini, including the Islamic Revolutionary Guard.

[事实] Revolutionary courts operating under Sharia held secret trials and executed officials loyal to the Shah.

[事实] The new regime brought back the veil, reduced some women’s divorce and child custody rights, imposed censorship, and shut liberal newspapers.

[事实] Iran’s new constitution pledged to export the revolution and unite the Islamic world.

[推测] The revolution was no longer merely anti-Shah; it was becoming a durable Islamic state with international ambitions.

[67:07] **Toward the Embassy Crisis**

[事实] By late summer 1979, Khomeini’s supporters remained focused on threats from the Shah and from the United States.

[事实] The Shah had not gone to America yet and was moving from country to country while still seeing himself as Iran’s rightful ruler.

[事实] Khomeini’s supporters viewed the US Embassy in Tehran as a center of espionage and a possible base for another 1953-style coup.

[事实] The episode ends by setting up the coming seizure of the US Embassy and the hostage crisis.

[推测] The final setup suggests that anti-Americanism became a unifying revolutionary force after the Shah’s fall.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it keeps the Iranian and American stories connected. It shows the revolution not only as the fall of the Shah and rise of Khomeini, but also as a shock that reshaped oil markets, American politics, and Carter’s presidency.

Its strongest sections explain why Khomeini was underestimated: he appeared to some outsiders as an austere anti-corruption figure, while his doctrine of clerical guardianship carried far more radical implications. The discussion also highlights how religious symbolism, especially Muharram and Ashura, gave the revolution emotional force.

The main limitation is that the episode is told largely through high politics, diplomacy, media perception, and elite decision-making. [推测] Listeners looking for more detail on ordinary Iranians, women, ethnic minorities, or left-wing groups inside Iran may need additional sources.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in modern Middle Eastern history, Cold War diplomacy, US presidential politics, and the origins of the Iran hostage crisis.
