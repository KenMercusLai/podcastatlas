# Top 10 Mistresses in History: Intimacy and Influence

## 概览

This episode uses a countdown of historical mistresses to ask how people with little formal power could influence politics, dynasties and public memory through intimate access to rulers or commanders.

The hosts set a loose rubric: the strongest candidates are those who “came from nowhere” and then affected the course of history. That means figures such as Cleopatra rank lower than expected because she was already a ruler, while La Malinche ranks highest because her linguistic and diplomatic role was presented as decisive.

The discussion moves chronologically and geographically across Persia, Rome, medieval England, Restoration Britain, Napoleonic Europe, classical Athens and the conquest of Mexico. Its recurring theme is that sexual or romantic proximity could become a route to patronage, factional conflict, cultural fame or state-changing decisions.

## 分段落总结

[00:09] **Setup: Mistresses as Political Actors**

[事实] The episode is framed as a “top 10 mistresses in history” list, with Dominic absent because of Covid and Ali Ansari joining the discussion.

[事实] The stated criterion is influence: the figures should have come from nowhere and exerted exceptional historical impact.

[推测] The hosts treat mistresses as comparable to eunuchs: people outside normal power structures who could still affect politics through intimate access.

[03:54] **No. 10: The Nightingale / Tuti**

[事实] Ali includes the Nightingale, also called Tuti, as a Persian representative on the list.

[事实] She is described as a dancing girl from Shiraz and a concubine of Fath-Ali Shah, who is said to have had about 1,000 concubines.

[事实] Her influence is described as slight, but British observers noticed that she had captured the king’s heart and that he mourned her early death.

[08:26] **No. 9: Cleopatra**

[事实] Cleopatra is ranked low because she was already queen of Egypt rather than someone who came from nowhere.

[事实] The hosts reject the image of Cleopatra as indiscriminately sexual, saying she is known for relationships with Julius Caesar and Mark Antony, both powerful men.

[事实] Her relationships are presented as strategic and dynastic, tied to securing her throne, Caesarion, Antony’s ambitions and the conflict with Octavian.

[推测] Cleopatra’s case shows the list’s tension between “mistress” as scandalous label and “mistress” as political operator.

[14:52] **No. 8: Piers Gaveston**

[事实] Piers Gaveston is introduced as Edward II’s favourite and as a male entry in the list.

[事实] The hosts say the exact sexual nature of Edward and Gaveston’s relationship cannot be known with certainty.

[事实] Gaveston is portrayed as a showy royal favourite whose closeness to Edward and status at court angered the traditional aristocracy.

[推测] His importance lies less in private romance than in how royal intimacy could destabilize baronial politics.

[21:55] **No. 7: Emma Hamilton**

[事实] Emma Hamilton is linked above all to Admiral Nelson, not to a monarch.

[事实] The hosts describe her as born in Cheshire in 1765, moving to London, entering theatrical and elite circles, and using beauty, wit and intelligence to rise socially.

[事实] She becomes connected with Charles Greville, then Sir William Hamilton in Naples, and later forms a scandalous relationship with Nelson.

[事实] After Nelson dies at Trafalgar, Emma loses money, sells Merton, goes to debtors’ prison and dies in Calais around 1815.

[推测] Her ranking reflects tragedy: celebrity and intimacy gave her fame, but not lasting security.

[32:35] **No. 6: Nell Gwyn**

[事实] Nell Gwyn is presented as Charles II’s mistress and as a more successful contrast to Emma Hamilton.

[事实] She is described as coming from the slums around Drury Lane and Covent Garden, becoming an orange seller and actress.

[事实] The hosts stress her public popularity as a witty, earthy Protestant Londoner, especially compared with Charles II’s French Catholic mistress Louise de Kerouaille.

[事实] Her sons by Charles II are accepted into aristocratic life, including the Duke of St Albans line.

[39:57] **No. 5: Lillie Langtry**

[事实] Lillie Langtry, the Jersey Lily, is introduced as a mistress of the Prince of Wales, the future Edward VII.

[事实] She moves from Jersey to London, becomes admired for her beauty, models for painters and gains royal attention.

[事实] The hosts say she converts scandal and fame into an acting career, including success in America.

[推测] She is treated as a prototype of the modern celebrity mistress, with Marilyn Monroe and Camilla mentioned as later reference points.

[47:27] **No. 4: Madame de Pompadour**

[事实] Madame de Pompadour is introduced as the mistress of Louis XV of France.

[事实] The hosts identify her as Jean-Antoinette Poisson and say she was groomed from childhood to enter the king’s orbit.

[事实] Even after the sexual relationship faded, Louis XV still relied on her politically.

[事实] Her influence is connected to the French-Austrian alliance, the Seven Years’ War and the phrase “apres nous, le deluge.”

[52:41] **No. 3: Aspasia**

[事实] Aspasia is described as coming from Miletus to Athens in the fifth century BC and becoming connected with Pericles.

[事实] The hosts describe Athens as highly restrictive for women, while hetairai could occupy a more sociable and intellectual role.

[事实] Comic and hostile traditions connect Aspasia with accusations around Pericles, prostitution and even the origins of the Peloponnesian War.

[事实] Other traditions link her to Pericles’ funeral oration, Socrates and possibly the figure of Diotima in Plato’s Symposium.

[推测] She is ranked high because her possible intellectual influence is enormous, but the evidence remains uncertain.

[60:52] **No. 2: Marie Walewska**

[事实] Marie Walewska is presented as a Polish noblewoman during the period when Poland had been partitioned.

[事实] Polish nobles encourage her to become Napoleon’s mistress in the hope that personal access will help Poland.

[事实] The hosts say she becomes fond of Napoleon and visits him on Elba after his fall in 1814.

[事实] Her son Alexander proves Napoleon can father a child, making the dynastic question around Josephine more consequential.

[65:37] **No. 1: La Malinche**

[事实] La Malinche is presented as the top-ranked figure and as someone who came from extreme vulnerability, having been sold or given away as a child.

[事实] She speaks Nahuatl, learns Maya, and is given to the Spaniards among twenty women.

[事实] Through Jeronimo de Aguilar and later through her own Spanish, she enables communication between Cortez, Aztec rulers and anti-Aztec allies.

[事实] The hosts say her role was central to building the coalition that overthrew the Aztec Empire and created New Spain.

[推测] Her number-one ranking rests on the idea that without her linguistic and diplomatic role, the Spanish conquest of Mexico would have been far harder.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it turns a sensational topic into a discussion of informal power: language, sexuality, patronage, celebrity and court access all become political forces.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the ones where the hosts complicate inherited reputations, especially Cleopatra as strategist, Nell Gwyn as politically useful popularity, Aspasia as uncertain intellectual influence, and La Malinche as both victim and historical actor.

[推测] The main limitation is that several cases depend on anecdotes, hostile sources or contested traditions, so the rankings work best as a lively historical argument rather than a definitive hierarchy.
