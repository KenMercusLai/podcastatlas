# Nelson at Copenhagen: The Battle, the Blind Eye, and the Baltic Crisis

## 概览

This episode follows Nelson in spring 1801, when Britain faces a strategic crisis in the Baltic. The League of Armed Neutrality threatens Britain’s ability to sustain naval supremacy against France, so the Admiralty sends a fleet to break Danish resistance at Copenhagen.

The core narrative is the Battle of Copenhagen: Nelson’s careful planning, the dangerous approach through shoals and narrow waters, the ferocity of Danish resistance, Hyde Parker’s order to withdraw, and Nelson’s famous decision to ignore it.

The episode also stresses Nelson’s contradictions. He is shown as inspirational, brave, theatrical, politically shrewd, physically fragile, and emotionally consumed by Emma Hamilton. The victory at Copenhagen restores his standing, but the episode ends with a new threat: Napoleon preparing an army aimed at England.

## 分段落总结

[01:41] **Opening Scene and Historical Framing**

[事实] The episode opens with a dramatic reading from Dominic Sandbrook’s book on Nelson, set on the eve of the Battle of Copenhagen.
[事实] Nelson is portrayed waiting for a favorable wind, emotionally transformed by the prospect of command and battle.
[推测] The opening frames Copenhagen as both a military crisis and a key episode in Nelson’s self-image as a heroic commander.

[03:22] **Britain’s Baltic Crisis**

[事实] Britain needs command of the seas to survive against France, but Tsar Paul I has organized the League of Armed Neutrality.
[事实] The hosts say the league threatens Britain’s naval supply system and could help the French navy grow.
[事实] The Admiralty sends a squadron east to break the league by force, beginning with the Danish fleet and dockyards at Copenhagen.

[07:11] **Nelson’s Squadron and the Elephant**

[事实] Nelson is not the overall commander; Admiral Sir Hyde Parker leads the fleet, while Nelson is assigned to lead the attack.
[事实] Nelson moves his flag to HMS Elephant because it has a shallower draft suited to Copenhagen’s waters.
[事实] His force includes ships of the line, frigates, fire ships, and seven bomb vessels intended to shell Danish dockyards after the defenses are cleared.

[09:38] **Entering the Øresund**

[事实] On 30 March, after days of waiting, the wind shifts and the British sail south through the strait between Denmark and Sweden.
[事实] The fleet avoids the main Danish guns at Kronborg by moving toward the Swedish side, and the Swedes do not fire.
[事实] The British then anchor near Copenhagen in cold, difficult weather while scouting parties examine the Danish defenses.

[11:04] **Scouting and the “Nelson Touch”**

[事实] Nelson personally joins night scouting missions in icy waters, despite being the commander.
[事实] He holds a council of war aboard the Elephant with captains including Thomas Foley, Thomas Fremantle, George Murray, and Thomas Masterman Hardy.
[事实] The hosts emphasize Nelson’s ability to inspire officers and create a sense of shared dramatic purpose.

[14:02] **The Battle Plan**

[事实] Nelson plans to sail around the Middle Ground shoal and enter the King’s Deep from the south.
[事实] Captain Edward Rieu is entrusted with marking the shoals and later receives a flexible role with a squadron of frigates.
[事实] Nelson’s plan depends on closing with the Danish line so that British gunnery and carronades can do maximum damage.

[17:51] **Final Preparations and Advance**

[事实] On 1 April and the morning of 2 April, Nelson briefs pilots and captains and distributes detailed orders.
[事实] At 8:00, he hoists the signal to prepare for battle; around 9:45, the first ships are ordered forward.
[推测] The hosts present the advance as intensely cinematic because the ships move slowly into extreme danger while Copenhagen’s citizens watch from shore.

[23:04] **The Battle Begins**

[事实] Around 10:15, the Danish ship Pruvestinen opens fire on the Edgar, and the battle expands along the line.
[事实] Smoke becomes so thick that spectators and ships struggle to see clearly.
[事实] Three British ships, Agamemnon, Bellona, and Russell, run aground, shortening Nelson’s battle line.

[25:23] **Danish Resistance and British Losses**

[事实] The hosts stress that the Danes fight with great courage because they are defending their own capital in front of their families and fellow citizens.
[事实] British ships including Edgar, Ardent, and especially Monarch suffer severe casualties.
[事实] Nelson remains on the quarterdeck under fire and continues to project confidence, using his favored signal to “engage the enemy more closely.”

[31:11] **Parker Orders Withdrawal**

[事实] By about 1:00, several Danish ships are badly damaged, burning, disabled, or drifting.
[事实] Around 1:30, Hyde Parker hoists Signal 39, ordering Nelson to discontinue the action.
[事实] The hosts frame this as a decisive moment because withdrawal under Danish fire could expose the British line to even greater danger.

[34:31] **Nelson Ignores the Signal**

[事实] Nelson decides not to repeat Parker’s order and keeps Signal 16, “engage the enemy more closely,” flying.
[事实] The episode recounts the famous story that Nelson put a telescope to his blind eye and said he did not see the signal.
[推测] The hosts treat the telescope story as possibly mythic but consistent with Nelson’s theatrical personality.

[40:07] **Rieu’s Fatal Obedience**

[事实] Edward Rieu, as a junior officer, reluctantly obeys Parker’s withdrawal order after continuing to fight for a time.
[事实] As Rieu’s frigates withdraw, Danish fire becomes clearer and deadlier.
[事实] Rieu is killed when a shot cuts him in two, and Nelson later regards his loss as one of the worst of the battle.

[42:44] **Nelson’s Diplomatic Move**

[事实] With Danish resistance weakening but British ships still vulnerable, Nelson sends a note under flag of truce to Crown Prince Frederick.
[事实] Nelson addresses the Danes as “brothers of Englishmen” and offers a path to peace while warning that he can continue the destruction.
[事实] The Crown Prince accepts a ceasefire and talks begin; fighting dies down after about half an hour.

[48:38] **Cost of the Battle**

[事实] The British lose about 250 killed, while the Danes lose about 500.
[事实] Two Danish ships are destroyed and twelve are captured.
[事实] British veterans describe the carnage at Copenhagen as more intense than earlier battles such as the Nile.

[50:08] **Nelson After Victory**

[事实] After the battle, Nelson records the day, feels unwell, and then writes to Emma Hamilton accusing her of keeping scandalous company with the Prince of Wales.
[事实] He then writes Emma a poem and sleeps for about six hours.
[推测] The hosts use this sequence to highlight the strange combination of heroism, exhaustion, jealousy, and emotional dependence in Nelson’s character.

[52:47] **Negotiations and Denmark’s Neutrality**

[事实] On 3 April, Nelson and Hardy go ashore in Copenhagen and are received by friendly crowds.
[事实] Nelson argues that Denmark has been misled by Russia and that Britain cannot accept armed neutrality because it threatens national survival.
[事实] After several days of talks, Denmark agrees to remain neutral for a few months and to resupply the British fleet.

[55:14] **The League Collapses**

[事实] The Crown Prince has intelligence from St Petersburg that Tsar Paul I has been assassinated.
[事实] The hosts say Paul’s successor, Alexander I, soon makes peace with Britain.
[事实] This means the League of Armed Neutrality is effectively doomed.

[56:57] **Illness, Homecoming, and the Next Threat**

[事实] Nelson remains anxious about Sweden and spends time in harsh Baltic weather while suffering fever, coughing, and “heart strokes.”
[事实] Reports of his condition alarm officials, and he is recalled; he returns to Yarmouth to crowds and bells.
[事实] His wife Fanny still hopes for reconciliation, but Nelson ignores her and says his mind is fixed.
[事实] At the Admiralty, Nelson is treated as having redeemed himself, but the episode ends with news that Napoleon is raising an army aimed at England.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its strong narrative reconstruction of Copenhagen as both a tactical battle and a psychological test of Nelson’s command. It explains why the Baltic crisis mattered without losing sight of the human drama aboard the ships.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the descriptions of the battle line, Parker’s withdrawal signal, the blind-eye story, and Nelson’s diplomatic note. These moments show Nelson not only as a warrior but also as a performer and political operator.

[推测] The main limitation is that the episode is deliberately admiring and comic in tone, so listeners looking for a colder or more Danish-centered account may want additional perspectives.

[推测] It is especially suited to listeners interested in naval history, Nelson’s personality, the Napoleonic Wars, and historical storytelling that mixes strategy, character, and anecdote.
