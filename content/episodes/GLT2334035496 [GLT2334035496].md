# Mary, Queen of Scots: The Bloody Letter and Fotheringhay

## 概览

This episode follows the final phase of Mary, Queen of Scots’ captivity in England, centered on the Babington Plot and the intercepted letter that gave Elizabeth I’s ministers the evidence they needed to move against her.

The discussion explains how Francis Walsingham’s spy network, especially the cryptanalyst Thomas Phillips and the double agent Gilbert Gifford, trapped Mary into correspondence that appeared to endorse Elizabeth’s assassination. It then traces Mary’s arrest, trial at Fotheringhay, conviction, and execution.

A major theme is Elizabeth’s reluctance to authorize the public execution of an anointed queen, despite pressure from Burleigh and Walsingham. The episode ends by linking Mary’s death to the escalation toward the Spanish Armada in 1588.

## 分段落总结

[01:53] **The Bloody Letter**

[事实] The episode opens with an extract from a letter written on Mary’s behalf on 17 July 1586 and sent in cipher to Anthony Babington.

[事实] The letter refers to setting “the six gentlemen to work,” which the hosts interpret as Mary approving the assassination plan against Elizabeth.

[事实] Babington had earlier proposed that six men kill Elizabeth while a Catholic rising, foreign invasion, and Mary’s release were coordinated.

[推测] The letter became decisive because it gave Mary’s enemies a written basis for treating her not merely as a claimant or captive, but as an active conspirator.

[05:02] **Mary’s Political Danger**

[事实] Mary is described as Elizabeth’s cousin, obvious heir if Elizabeth died, and a Catholic figurehead for English, Scottish, and continental Catholics.

[事实] Protestants saw Mary as a threat to Elizabeth and to the Protestant settlement.

[事实] William Cecil, Lord Burleigh, and Sir Francis Walsingham had long wanted Mary executed as a traitor.

[推测] Mary’s danger increased because England was already drifting toward open war with Spain, making any Catholic succession crisis feel existential.

[09:07] **Elizabeth’s Reluctance**

[事实] Elizabeth did not want Mary publicly executed because it could inflame Europe, justify Spanish action, and set a precedent for killing an anointed queen.

[事实] Burleigh and Walsingham wanted a public execution because it would remove Mary, brand her a traitor, and unite English Protestants.

[事实] The 1585 Act for the Queen’s Safety created a legal mechanism to try and execute any claimant implicated in plots against Elizabeth.

[推测] Burleigh’s strategy was to trap Elizabeth politically by making Mary’s execution appear as a consequence of law rather than personal royal vengeance.

[13:28] **Mary in Captivity**

[事实] By July 1586, Mary was 43, in poor health, overweight, stooped, and suffering from serious ailments.

[事实] Her condition is linked to years of captivity, limited exercise, and the continued royal protocol that gave her lavish meals and many servants.

[事实] After the Throckmorton Plot, her conditions became harsher at Chartley Manor, where Sir Amias Paulet acted as her strict jailer.

[推测] The contrast between royal ceremony and prison restrictions made Mary’s situation both privileged and humiliating.

[18:12] **The Beer-Barrel Channel**

[事实] Paulet restricted Mary’s contact with the outside world through searches, controls on letters, and strip searches of returning household members.

[事实] Gilbert Gifford, a Catholic courier connected to Mary’s supporters, arranged for letters to be smuggled inside beer barrels delivered to Chartley Manor.

[事实] Babington’s letter reached Mary through this channel, and Mary’s reply was sent back the same way.

[推测] Mary’s willingness to reply shows how desperate she had become to recover agency, despite knowing that spies were everywhere.

[24:30] **Thomas Phillips and the Trap**

[事实] Mary’s reply did not first reach Babington; it went to Thomas Phillips, Walsingham’s highly skilled cryptanalyst.

[事实] Gifford had been turned by Walsingham, and the beer-barrel route was part of a supervised intelligence operation.

[事实] Phillips decrypted Mary’s letter, copied it for Walsingham, drew a gallows on it, and called it the “bloody letter.”

[推测] Walsingham’s operation succeeded because it allowed conspirators to believe their communications were secure while every message was being read.

[28:01] **Mary’s Arrest and Babington’s Fall**

[事实] On 11 August, Paulet took Mary on what appeared to be a deer hunt, during which she was informed that a plot against Elizabeth had been uncovered.

[事实] Mary was arrested, and Babington was arrested three days later.

[事实] Babington and other conspirators confessed to planning Mary’s release, foreign invasion, and the restoration of Catholicism, but denied plotting Elizabeth’s murder.

[事实] Babington was executed on 20 September, and other conspirators were executed the next day.

[31:07] **Arrival at Fotheringhay**

[事实] Mary left Chartley Manor on 21 September and reached Fotheringhay Castle on 25 September.

[事实] Fotheringhay was chosen partly because it was secure, inland, surrounded by difficult ground, and had strong fortifications.

[事实] It also had large ceremonial rooms suitable for staging a trial.

[推测] The setting was selected not only to imprison Mary securely but to turn her judgment into a formal political theatre.

[35:20] **The Trial Opens**

[事实] Mary’s trial began on 14 October 1586 in the presence chamber at Fotheringhay.

[事实] Elizabeth was absent, but her empty throne dominated the courtroom.

[事实] Mary argued that, as a queen and not Elizabeth’s subject, she should not be tried under English law.

[事实] Christopher Hatton persuaded her to attend by suggesting that refusal would damage her reputation and look like guilt.

[39:00] **Mary Faces Her Enemies**

[事实] Mary appeared in black velvet with a white cap and veil, and the assembled men stood and removed their hats.

[事实] She had no lawyer and was surrounded by hostile commissioners, including Burleigh and Walsingham.

[事实] The evidence included Babington’s correspondence, Mary’s correspondence, and especially the “bloody letter.”

[推测] Mary may still have had some hope before seeing the evidence, because she believed she had avoided writing incriminating material in her own hand.

[42:00] **Mary’s Defence**

[事实] Mary argued that she was not responsible for Babington’s plans and that letters attributed to her were not in her handwriting.

[事实] She accused Walsingham of tampering with her letters, which the hosts say was essentially correct.

[事实] She confronted Burleigh directly, and Burleigh answered that he was the enemy of Elizabeth’s enemies.

[事实] At the end of the second day, Mary declared the trial a charade, spoke privately to Walsingham, and placed her cause in God’s hands.

[45:24] **Conviction and Elizabeth’s Delay**

[事实] Mary was found guilty, with the verdict delivered in the Star Chamber at Westminster ten days after the trial.

[事实] Elizabeth delayed because she feared the consequences of executing an anointed queen and the implication that Parliament could authorize regicide.

[事实] Elizabeth hoped someone might kill Mary privately under the Bond of Association rather than force her to use a signed death warrant.

[推测] Elizabeth wanted Mary removed but also wanted to avoid personal and constitutional responsibility for the removal.

[49:08] **Pressure on Elizabeth**

[事实] Burleigh and Walsingham pushed Elizabeth toward action, including through Parliament’s petition for Mary’s execution.

[事实] Burleigh revealed another assassination plot and fabricated a rumour that Spanish troops had landed in Wales.

[事实] Elizabeth signed Mary’s death warrant on 1 February 1587, while still not wanting it used immediately.

[事实] Paulet refused to murder Mary privately, saying he would not shed blood without law or warrant.

[53:03] **The Warrant Goes to Fotheringhay**

[事实] Burleigh gathered supportive privy councillors and sent the warrant to Fotheringhay despite Elizabeth’s hesitation.

[事实] Robert Beale carried the warrant north, accompanied by the Earl of Kent and the Earl of Shrewsbury.

[事实] Shrewsbury, who had once been Mary’s jailer and liked her, had to supervise the execution.

[事实] The executioner, known as Bull, travelled with his axe hidden in a trunk.

[55:18] **Mary Learns She Will Die**

[事实] On the evening of 7 February, Beale, Kent, Shrewsbury, and Paulet came to Mary’s quarters and read out the warrant.

[事实] Mary was told she would be executed the next morning.

[事实] She swore on the Bible that she was innocent and requested a Catholic priest, but this was refused.

[事实] The Earl of Kent told her that her life would be the death of their religion and her death its life.

[推测] Kent’s remark strengthened Mary’s decision to present herself as a Catholic martyr.

[56:30] **Mary’s Final Performance**

[事实] Mary wrote her final letter to Henry III of France, framing her condemnation around Catholic faith and her claim to the English crown.

[事实] On 8 February, she arrived an hour late for her execution in the Great Hall.

[事实] The hall and scaffold had been draped in black, with guards, spectators, masked executioners, and Bull holding the axe.

[事实] Mary wore black velvet, a white veil, and white lace, carefully staging her final appearance.

[60:01] **Prayer, Defiance, and Martyrdom**

[事实] Robert Fletcher, the preacher chosen by Elizabeth, tried to deliver a sermon and prayers.

[事实] Mary and her attendants answered with Latin Catholic prayers, drowning him out.

[事实] Mary prayed in English for her enemies, Elizabeth, and England.

[事实] When her black outer garments were removed, she was revealed in crimson, which the hosts describe as the color of martyrdom.

[63:27] **The Execution**

[事实] Mary knelt, was blindfolded with a white Corpus Christi cloth, and placed her neck on the block while praying in Latin.

[事实] Bull’s first axe blow struck the back of her head rather than her neck.

[事实] The second blow still did not fully sever the head, and the executioner had to finish the task with the blade.

[事实] When Bull lifted the head, he found he was holding Mary’s wig, while her actual head had rolled away.

[64:02] **Aftermath and the Armada**

[事实] A small dog had been hidden in Mary’s skirts and was discovered after her death.

[事实] The hosts say Mary embarrassed her enemies even in death.

[事实] The episode connects Mary’s execution to the escalation that followed, especially Philip II’s attempt to invade England in 1588 with the Spanish Armada.

[推测] Mary’s death removed one immediate dynastic threat but intensified the wider Catholic-Protestant and Anglo-Spanish conflict.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is its clear narrative arc: it turns a complicated intelligence operation, legal process, and execution into a coherent story about power, conscience, espionage, and political theatre.

[推测] A major highlight is the attention to staging, from Elizabeth’s empty throne at the trial to Mary’s crimson clothing at the scaffold. The hosts show how symbolism mattered as much as formal law.

[推测] The limitation is that the tone sometimes leans into comic commentary and vivid personality judgments, which may not suit listeners looking for a strictly sober treatment of Mary’s final months.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in Elizabethan politics, espionage, Reformation conflict, and the road to the Spanish Armada.
