# Robespierre, the Supreme Being, and the Great Terror

## 概览

This episode examines the Festival of the Supreme Being, the spectacular civic celebration staged by Maximilien Robespierre in Paris on 8 June 1794. Designed with Jacques-Louis David, the festival combined mass choirs, allegorical statues, patriotic hymns, pyrotechnics, and a vast papier-mâché mountain.

The hosts explore the political theology behind the spectacle. Robespierre believed that universal rights and revolutionary virtue required a divine foundation: an all-seeing Supreme Being who guaranteed moral justice and watched over every citizen. He hoped this belief could help France move beyond emergency government and eventually implement its democratic constitution.

Yet the festival also intensified suspicions that Robespierre wanted to dominate the Revolution morally and politically. The episode contrasts his regulated, virtue-based conception of terror with the atrocities committed by representatives such as Jean-Baptiste Carrier and Joseph Fouché.

It concludes with the Law of 22 Prairial, which removed basic legal protections and greatly expanded the category of “enemies of the people.” As executions accelerated, threatened deputies—including Fouché—began considering how Robespierre might be overthrown.

## 分段落总结

[02:16] **Robespierre opens the Festival of the Supreme Being**

[事实] On 8 June 1794, Robespierre addressed a vast public gathering in the Tuileries Gardens and urged the French to rely upon virtue, constancy, and moral character in their struggle against tyranny.

[事实] He had announced the festival a month earlier and invested substantial political capital in making it an inclusive civic occasion.

[事实] Robespierre had recently been elected president of the National Convention, allowing him to occupy the constitutionally appointed central role in the ceremonies.

[事实] The guillotine was moved away from the nearby Place de la Révolution so that executions would not disturb the festival’s solemnity.

[05:21] **David’s revolutionary spectacle**

[事实] Jacques-Louis David designed the festival’s sets, costumes, special effects, floral displays, and a combined choir of approximately 2,400 voices.

[事实] Robespierre appeared in an elaborate blue coat, tricolour sash, and plumed hat, but his nervousness was evident: he forgot his bouquet and initially avoided breakfast.

[事实] The event attracted an enormous and enthusiastic crowd, though estimates claiming half a million attendees may have been exaggerated.

[事实] Robespierre burned a cardboard figure representing atheism, revealing a second, slightly singed statue representing wisdom.

[事实] The afternoon ceremonies moved to the Champ de Mars, where deputies and singers assembled on an enormous papier-mâché mountain topped by Apollo and a tree.

[12:50] **A celebration that divided the Revolution**

[事实] Many spectators enjoyed the festival, but some deputies regarded it as both ridiculous and sinister.

[事实] Atheists were particularly offended because the ceremonies portrayed atheism as something to be destroyed.

[事实] The festival honoured a distant deity whom Robespierre called the Supreme Being rather than celebrating the Revolution alone.

[事实] Robespierre had previously argued that atheism was aristocratic and that belief in justice beyond this life was necessary to prevent cynicism and despair.

[推测] The spectacle strengthened the impression among his opponents that Robespierre was placing himself at the centre of a new revolutionary religion.

[16:08] **Universal rights and an all-seeing God**

[事实] Robespierre connected revolutionary virtue to absolute morality and rejected the idea that the Revolution’s principles were culturally relative or merely products of circumstance.

[事实] The Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen described rights as natural, inalienable, and sacred, and stated that it was promulgated under the auspices of the Supreme Being.

[事实] Robespierre believed the Supreme Being had inscribed eternal justice in every human heart.

[事实] He thought citizens needed to feel continually observed if they were to remain virtuous and prevent the return of tyranny.

[事实] Historian Dan Edelstein describes the festival’s disembodied eye as an “internalized surveillance mechanism.”

[21:17] **The democratic constitution suspended by terror**

[事实] Robespierre’s commitment to order and rules shaped his fear of atheism and moral individualism.

[事实] The Constitution of 1793, created by Robespierre and his allies, was described as exceptionally democratic and had been overwhelmingly approved in a popular referendum.

[事实] The constitution was ceremonially placed inside a model Ark of the Covenant and suspended above the speaker’s platform in the National Convention.

[事实] Despite its ratification, war, rebellion, and the continuing state of emergency prevented the constitution from taking effect.

[推测] The constitution’s physical suspension symbolised the contradiction between the Revolution’s democratic promises and its increasingly indefinite dictatorship.

[26:16] **Virtue, suspicion, and the logic of terror**

[事实] The hosts disagree over whether Robespierre would ever have judged France ready to end emergency rule and implement the constitution.

[事实] Robespierre increasingly struggled to identify the truly virtuous and suspected that people who appeared most loyal might be concealed counter-revolutionaries.

[事实] Although personally opposed to the death penalty, he argued that revolutionary government required both virtue and terror.

[事实] He presented terror as a moral responsibility necessary to protect the Revolution rather than as an end in itself.

[推测] His rigid expectations of human behaviour trapped him in an escalating cycle: every failure to meet his standards appeared to justify further coercion and execution.

[29:56] **The Supreme Being as a way out**

[事实] Robespierre hoped that belief in an all-seeing Supreme Being could make divine surveillance replace the Committee of Public Safety’s enforcement of terror.

[事实] The festival’s success appeared to reassure him that the French could be taught to respect revolutionary morality voluntarily.

[事实] The hosts report that many historians regard the festival as the happiest day of Robespierre’s life.

[推测] What Robespierre saw as a route away from terror also exposed him to accusations that he wanted to act as the Revolution’s priest—or even its god.

[33:40] **Atheists denounce Robespierre’s new religion**

[事实] Convention deputy Jacques-Alexis Thuriot reportedly complained that it was not enough for Robespierre to be master; he also wanted to be God.

[事实] Thuriot and other de-Christianisers saw Robespierre’s defence of religious observance as reactionary and potentially counter-revolutionary.

[事实] Robespierre opposed forced suppression of private worship and declared that consciences could not be commanded.

[事实] He nevertheless refused to tolerate priests or religious movements that actively supported monarchy and counter-revolution; approximately a thousand priests were executed during the Terror.

[事实] Robespierre’s Cult of the Supreme Being retained recognisably Christian—and especially Protestant—ideas even while attempting to replace Christianity.

[42:42] **A revolutionary calendar replaces Christian time**

[事实] The revolutionary government ended the Christian system of dating and counted years from the abolition of the monarchy on 22 September 1792.

[事实] It introduced new months named for seasons and agricultural cycles while abolishing the traditional seven-day week and its Sundays.

[事实] Robespierre supported the calendar and successfully proposed that one of its supplementary days become an annual Festival of Virtue.

[事实] He announced the Festival of the Supreme Being using its revolutionary date, 20 Prairial Year II.

[推测] The new calendar demonstrates both the Revolution’s ambition to reconstruct everyday life and its inability to escape patterns inherited from Christianity.

[47:19] **De-Christianisation becomes mass violence**

[事实] Robespierre accepted controlled religious reform but opposed de-Christianisation when it became decentralised, violent, or deliberately blasphemous.

[事实] Revolutionary reprisals in the Vendée killed vast numbers of people, including civilians; the hosts cite estimates ranging from one-quarter to one-third of the population.

[事实] Jean-Baptiste Carrier was accused of drowning bound prisoners in deliberately sabotaged barges and mockingly calling the killings “Republican baptisms.”

[事实] Carrier also allegedly called the binding of naked male and female prisoners before drowning them “Republican marriages.”

[事实] Disgusted by Carrier’s violence and blasphemy, Robespierre recalled him to Paris in February 1794.

[53:03] **Joseph Fouché emerges from the shadows**

[事实] Joseph Fouché was a former Oratorian physics teacher who had briefly courted Robespierre’s sister Charlotte.

[事实] After entering the National Convention, Fouché voted for the execution of Louis XVI and became known in the Jacobin Club for ability, self-control, and radical zeal.

[事实] Sent to Nièvre in 1793, he conducted France’s first systematic campaign of de-Christianisation by closing churches, forcing priests to renounce their vows, and seizing precious metals.

[事实] He promoted cults of reason and placed inscriptions declaring that death was an eternal sleep at cemetery entrances.

[事实] Fouché’s campaign influenced later de-Christianisation in Paris and helped secure his appointment to punish Lyon.

[60:08] **Fouché’s atrocities in Lyon**

[事实] Fouché arrived in Lyon in November 1793, organised a parody of the Mass, and presided over iconoclasm and public attacks on Christianity.

[事实] Considering the guillotine too slow, he ordered groups of prisoners to be blasted with grapeshot and had survivors killed by cavalrymen.

[事实] By April 1794, Fouché had recorded responsibility for more than 2,000 deaths and defended his actions as terror exercised for humanity and the Revolution.

[事实] Robespierre condemned these killings as barbaric, sadistic, and contrary to the rules of revolutionary virtue.

[推测] The disagreement exposed an unstable distinction in Robespierre’s thinking between legitimate state terror and atrocities committed through ambition, cruelty, or personal appetite.

[64:48] **Fouché outmanoeuvres Robespierre**

[事实] Recalled to Paris, Fouché immediately addressed the Convention, openly defended his conduct, and presented it as virtuous revolutionary terror.

[事实] He then confronted Robespierre privately and survived the encounter, despite receiving a severe reprimand.

[事实] On 6 June, two days before the festival, Fouché successfully won election as president of the Jacobin Club.

[事实] Robespierre interpreted Fouché’s victory as a direct challenge and resolved to remove him.

[推测] Fouché survived by seizing the initiative and turning his vulnerability into a public political contest before Robespierre could isolate him.

[67:03] **The Law of 22 Prairial**

[事实] On 10 June 1794, two days after the festival, Georges Couthon formally introduced legislation largely written by Robespierre.

[事实] The Law of 22 Prairial abolished defence counsel for suspects and removed the requirement for evidential testimony.

[事实] Jurors could convict on their inner conviction that an accused person was an enemy of the people.

[事实] The law defined offences so broadly that criticism, false news, insults to morality, abuse of office, interference with food supplies, or conduct judged hostile to the Revolution could lead to prosecution.

[事实] Death was the only available sentence for those found guilty.

[70:32] **Fear unites Robespierre’s enemies**

[事实] The day after introducing the law, Robespierre denounced Fouché at the Jacobin Club as a contemptible cheat and warned unnamed conspirators of the people’s vengeance.

[事实] Fouché was removed from the Jacobin presidency and expelled from the club.

[事实] Robespierre continued making vague warnings about conspiracies, leaving deputies unsure whether they themselves were among his targets.

[事实] Executions accelerated and increasingly included ordinary people accused of trivial or vaguely defined offences.

[推测] By making almost anyone prosecutable while refusing to identify his intended targets, Robespierre gave threatened factions a powerful shared motive to move against him.

[73:17] **The Terror turns toward Thermidor**

[事实] The hosts argue that Robespierre’s political judgement was deteriorating even though he had previously defeated the Girondins, Hébertists, and Dantonists.

[事实] Fouché, knowing he was marked for destruction, began working discreetly among deputies who feared for their lives.

[事实] As the revolutionary month of Messidor gave way to Thermidor, the question was no longer only whom Robespierre would purge, but whether his opponents could bring him down first.

[事实] The episode ends by setting up the series finale on the fall of Robespierre.

[推测] The machinery Robespierre strengthened to defend revolutionary virtue was now accelerating the coalition that would threaten his own survival.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s chief strength is its connection of political spectacle, religious belief, constitutional theory, and state violence. The Festival of the Supreme Being is treated not as an eccentric diversion but as an attempted solution to Robespierre’s central dilemma: how to create virtuous citizens without relying indefinitely on terror.

The discussion also draws an illuminating contrast between Robespierre and Fouché. Both accepted lethal revolutionary violence, but Robespierre insisted that it be centralised, rule-bound, and morally justified, while Fouché proved more opportunistic and adaptable. Their conflict helps explain how personal survival, ideology, and political skill converged before Thermidor.

The hosts use humour and modern comparisons to make dense material accessible, though some analogies risk distracting from the historical argument. They also distinguish disputed reports—especially allegations concerning Carrier—from better-documented events.

This episode is particularly valuable for listeners interested in the French Revolution, political religion, emergency government, and the way universal moral ideals can become entangled with surveillance and coercion.
