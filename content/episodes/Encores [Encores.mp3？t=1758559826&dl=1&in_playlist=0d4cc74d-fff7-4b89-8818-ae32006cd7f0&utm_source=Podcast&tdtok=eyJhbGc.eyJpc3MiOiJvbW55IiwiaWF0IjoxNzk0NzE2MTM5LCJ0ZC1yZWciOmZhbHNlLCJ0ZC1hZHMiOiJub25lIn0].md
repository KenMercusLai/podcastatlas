# Encores: Four Musical Journeys from the Revisionist History Archive

## 概览

This encore episode revisits four of Malcolm Gladwell’s favorite music stories from the *Revisionist History* archive. Though he does not play an instrument or understand musical theory, Gladwell describes music—and conversations with musicians—as a profound source of joy.

The selected episodes explore sad songs in Nashville, Elvis Presley, an afternoon with Randy Newman, and the story behind Leonard Cohen’s “Hallelujah.” Together, they examine how particular songs enter the listener’s consciousness and remain there for decades.

## 分段落总结

[00:11] **Why Certain Songs Stay with Us**

[事实] Gladwell says the music episodes are among his favorite installments of *Revisionist History*.

[事实] Looking back, he realizes that these stories share a concern with how particular songs entered his consciousness 10, 20, or 30 years ago and never left.

[事实] He describes this attachment as surprising because he neither plays an instrument nor understands concepts such as chords.

[事实] Despite lacking musical training, he finds few things more joyful than sitting down with a musician.

[00:51] **Four Favorites from the Archive**

[事实] Gladwell introduces four music episodes that the podcast will reprise from the *Revisionist History* archive.

[事实] The first returns to Nashville for a discussion of sad songs, while the second visits Nashville to explore Elvis Presley.

[事实] The third centers on what Gladwell calls a “magical afternoon” with Randy Newman.

[事实] The final selection traces the story of Leonard Cohen’s “Hallelujah,” taking Gladwell to the Metropolitan Opera and a pub in North London.
