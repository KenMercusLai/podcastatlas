# Coleman Ruiz’s Hero’s Journey Through Discipline, Loss, Trauma, and Recovery

## 概览

Andrew Huberman interviews Coleman Ruiz, a former Tier 1 Navy SEAL operator, about the arc of his life from a modest childhood in New Orleans to wrestling, the Naval Academy, the SEAL teams, war, deep loss, civilian transition, depression, and recovery.

The conversation repeatedly returns to the idea that intensity, fear, discipline, and team belonging can be adaptive for achievement, but can become damaging when they replace reflection, emotional processing, and asking for help. Ruiz describes wrestling as the first structure that redirected his adolescent wildness into discipline, and the SEAL teams as a place where team identity and high standards gave his life purpose.

A major theme is the cost of repeated exposure to death, combat, and grief. Ruiz explains that after losing many people he personally knew, including close teammates, he did not understand the accumulated psychological impact until years later, after leaving the military and experiencing severe depression.

The episode’s central conclusion is that toughness is not only physical endurance or combat readiness. Ruiz argues that real range includes the ability to be disciplined, protective, kind, vulnerable, and willing to seek help before pain becomes catastrophic.

[事实] Huberman introduces Coleman Ruiz as a former Tier 1 Navy SEAL special operator who previously lived a largely covert professional life.

[事实] Huberman frames the episode as Ruiz’s life story, including childhood, military service, fatherhood, loss, triumph, and post-military civilian life.

[推测] The introduction positions Ruiz’s story less as a military résumé and more as a broader study of resilience, identity, and recovery.

[03:10] **Childhood in New Orleans**

[事实] Ruiz says he was born in New Orleans East, had an older sister and two younger brothers, and grew up in a modest household with a welder father and dental-assistant mother.

[事实] He remembers skateboarding, BMX, football, baseball, and roaming the neighborhood, while also acknowledging that later therapy helped him see childhood memories with more complexity.

[推测] His early life combined warmth, economic constraint, independence, and unstructured physical energy.

[05:03] **Adolescent Wildness and Trouble at School**

[事实] Ruiz describes sixth and seventh grade at Holy Cross High School as a difficult period involving detentions, fistfights, and falling in with the wrong crowd.

[事实] When asked why he fought, Ruiz says it was mostly “wildness” rather than malicious intent.

[事实] Huberman connects this to adolescent dispersal, describing a neurobiological drive toward exploration, risk, and testing boundaries.

[08:20] **Wrestling as a Channel**

[事实] Ruiz says wrestling became an immediate and major inflection point in his life, giving him a place to put his wildness.

[事实] He had liked physical effort from an early age, including running road races and winning physical-training competitions as a child.

[事实] Ruiz says he loved combat sports because of the close contact, the fight, the effort, and especially the respect between competitors.

[推测] Wrestling gave him a socially sanctioned way to experience intensity, pain, hierarchy, and self-mastery.

[14:35] **Discipline Improves School and Behavior**

[事实] Ruiz says his grades went up during wrestling season and that the fights stopped because he could put that energy into the wrestling room.

[事实] He describes Louisiana wrestling as relatively niche, but says his school had strong coaching and older wrestlers who pushed him hard.

[事实] He says life got better “in every sense” because of wrestling.

[17:50] **High School, Partying, and Athletic Ambition**

[事实] Ruiz says he wrestled throughout high school, drank and partied in New Orleans outside the season, but cared enough about wrestling and consequences to keep his life mostly within limits.

[事实] He says his grades were excellent, his SAT scores were not, and he wanted to wrestle in college.

[事实] He says he went 89-0 in his final two high school years and was state runner-up as a sophomore.

[19:05] **The Naval Academy as Escape and Opportunity**

[事实] Ruiz says an uncle began sending him Naval Academy materials after a family reunion, even though Ruiz knew little about the military.

[事实] He says his parents divorced during his senior year of high school, and the Naval Academy represented a way to leave New Orleans and do something with his life.

[事实] He recalls reacting to the divorce by thinking it had nothing to do with him and that he needed to get out.

[推测] The Naval Academy became both an achievement target and a psychological exit from family disruption.

[23:17] **Rejection, Prep School, and Luck**

[事实] Ruiz says he was initially rejected by the Naval Academy and had only applied to the Naval Academy and Stanford.

[事实] A Naval Academy wrestling coach later offered him a prep-school spot in Newport, Rhode Island, after another recruit chose a different path.

[事实] Ruiz says he was lucky and took the opportunity extremely seriously because he recognized it as his chance.

[28:27] **Military Structure and a Bigger World**

[事实] At the Naval Academy prep school, Ruiz studied subjects like calculus, physics, chemistry, and English while also entering an enlisted military environment.

[事实] He says the school quickly exposed him to colonels, admirals, generals, and a seriousness larger than anything he had understood before.

[事实] Huberman notes that being around high-achieving people can expand a person’s sense of what is possible.

[32:37] **Fear as Fuel**

[事实] Ruiz says he did not feel he had “made it” at the Naval Academy; instead, he felt he had to earn his place every day.

[事实] He describes operating out of fear for decades, while also believing he could reach others’ level through enough work.

[事实] Huberman connects this to growth mindset, while Ruiz acknowledges that the fear-based drive was adaptive in some ways and maladaptive in others.

[38:20] **The Naval Academy and Doug Zembeck**

[事实] Ruiz entered the Naval Academy in 1994 and later met his wife Bridget there in 1996.

[事实] He describes Doug Zembeck as a crucial mentor, teammate, and example of extraordinary physical and mental toughness.

[事实] Ruiz recounts a dangerous late-night Naval Academy diving-well incident during a recruiting visit, where Doug defended him when others blamed him for damaging a ceiling tile.

[推测] Doug became Ruiz’s model for what “right” looked like inside a demanding culture.

[48:06] **Bridget and Staying at the Academy**

[事实] Ruiz describes Bridget as exceptionally accomplished: a successful swimmer, strong student, and the top female graduate in their class.

[事实] He says he was drawn to her because she was nice and normal, not because of her achievements.

[事实] Ruiz says that during a miserable sophomore wrestling season, he considered leaving the Naval Academy, but meeting Bridget helped him decide to stay.

[55:01] **Choosing the SEAL Teams**

[事实] Ruiz initially thought he might join the Marine Corps, partly influenced by mentors, but he did not love his summer training at Quantico.

[事实] He became interested in the SEAL teams because he wanted something highly physical and was comfortable in the water.

[事实] He describes the Naval Academy SEAL-selection process, including hard screening weekends, mini-BUD/S, interviews, and selection into a limited number of billets.

[59:21] **What Predicts Getting Through BUD/S**

[事实] Ruiz says that as a BUD/S instructor, he and others informally observed that many successful candidates had been varsity athletes, had divorced parents, or had been suspended from school.

[事实] He stresses that this was anecdotal and not formal scientific evidence.

[事实] Huberman and Ruiz discuss varsity athletics as training in structure, coachability, effort, and teamwork.

[推测] Ruiz’s informal model suggests that both structured challenge and early disruption can prepare some people for extreme selection environments.

[62:29] **Belonging After Family Disruption**

[事实] Ruiz says that for him, having divorced parents created a feeling of being alone unless he had the team.

[事实] He says that in BUD/S he felt he could not leave because, without the team, he did not know what team he had.

[事实] Huberman identifies this as a powerful need for belonging and family outside the household.

[66:03] **Rule-Breaking and Chaotic Environments**

[事实] Ruiz says suspension from school reflected a “wild” resistance to authority that could be useful in special operations when real-world conditions did not match training manuals.

[事实] He says combat required following rules of engagement and the laws of armed conflict, while adapting to chaotic environments that did not follow predictable patterns.

[事实] He recalls realizing during the 2003 invasion of Iraq that no instructor had the experience to fully prepare him for what he was facing.

[71:16] **Patriotism and Seriousness in the Teams**

[事实] Ruiz says the Naval Academy gave him a sense of patriotism and scale, but his personal patriotic feeling deepened after 9/11.

[事实] At SEAL Team 3, he met Vietnam veterans and other highly experienced people who made him feel that playtime was over.

[事实] He loved the work, the team component, and the unpredictability of the SEAL teams.

[73:39] **Moving Toward Tier One**

[事实] Ruiz says he was about ten years into his career when he raised his hand to try out for a special mission unit.

[事实] The process included psychological screening, physical testing, interviews, and a nine-month advanced training program known as Green Team.

[事实] He says he moved his family from the West Coast to the East Coast for the chance, despite the risk of not making it through.

[77:12] **A Different Operational Level**

[事实] Ruiz explains the difference between regular SEAL freefall training and special mission unit freefall by comparing lower-altitude, simpler jumps to high-altitude, night-vision, oxygen-supported jumps with heavy gear and complex navigation.

[事实] He says that when he arrived at the command, he realized it was another level compared with what he had previously experienced.

[事实] He estimates that in his Green Team class, perhaps 10 to 15 of about 65 did not make it through.

[82:14] **Losses in the Tier One Years**

[事实] Ruiz says he served at the command from 2006 to 2011 and personally knew exactly 40 people who were killed, while the broader number was higher.

[事实] Huberman mentions Adam Brown and the book about him, emphasizing that the story is also about addiction, family, and tenacity.

[事实] Ruiz says Adam Brown was in his Green Team class, though they were not best friends.

[84:08] **Funerals, Memorials, and Repeated Grief**

[事实] Ruiz says that from 2006 to 2011, he was effectively at a funeral or memorial about every 90 days on average.

[事实] He describes the routine in simple terms as going to a funeral, toasting, and returning to work, while noting that the reality was much more complex.

[事实] He says the repeated losses became part of the tempo of life.

[85:06] **Doug’s Death and the Collapse of Certainty**

[事实] Ruiz says Doug Zembeck was killed in 2007, and the news felt as if the scaffolding of the world disappeared.

[事实] He says Doug had seemed like the person who should be immortal, so his death forced Ruiz to realize that no one was.

[事实] Ruiz says that after Doug’s death, he began looking over his shoulder and working even harder to stay alive.

[推测] Doug’s death shattered Ruiz’s belief that toughness, focus, and training could make someone untouchable.

[93:20] **Notifying Families**

[事实] Ruiz describes notifying Tommy Valentine’s family after Tommy was killed in a parachute accident following an intense Iraq deployment.

[事实] He says team members were often sent to notify families, even though they were not trained like casualty assistance officers.

[事实] He later describes being asked to help notify Adam Brown’s wife, Kelly, and says that experience was the worst.

[事实] Ruiz says he was trying to live two lives: a military life full of consequences and a home life with Bridget and their sons.

[事实] He says Bridget and other spouses put enormous effort into making home life stable and comfortable.

[事实] He remembers fun family moments, but says there are stretches of the work and deployment cycles he does not remember.

[107:01] **Leaving the Military**

[事实] Ruiz left the military in fall 2011 after 13 years and says he does not regret that decision.

[事实] He says it was the right decision for him and his family, though it happened like a snap decision.

[事实] Huberman and Ruiz briefly discuss the 2011 Extortion 17 tragedy as a major special-operations loss.

[108:24] **Civilian Transition and Disorientation**

[事实] Ruiz says that after leaving, he turned in his gear and badge and quickly became unable to access the command without an escort.

[事实] He says he did not know what to do with his time, thoughts, feelings, nights, or basic civilian logistics like finding a dentist.

[事实] He initially thought PTSD was a joke until he read the symptoms and recognized them.

[事实] Ruiz says Joseph Campbell’s *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* became one of the most important books of his post-military life.

[事实] He explains the “return” portion of the hero’s journey, including the ultimate boon, refusal of return, magic flight, crossing the return threshold, master of two worlds, and freedom to live.

[事实] He says he realized he was trapped somewhere in the return and lacked a mentor to help him cross back into ordinary life.

[事实] Ruiz recalls Medal of Honor recipient Paul “Buddy” Bucha telling him he had PTS and would have to deal with it.

[事实] Ruiz says he initially rejected that warning and believed his training had prepared him for everything he had experienced.

[事实] He later recognized the warning as significant when he began learning more and getting help.

[事实] Ruiz says he explored craniosacral therapy, somatic bodywork, massage-like practices, Sam Harris’s work, Buddhism-related material, and broader spiritual or contemplative ideas.

[事实] He says he needed to back away from the narrow 24-hour focus that had driven much of his life.

[127:44] **Psychedelics and the Need for Preparation**

[事实] Ruiz says he eventually explored plant medicines in a controlled and curated environment and found the experience extremely powerful.

[事实] Huberman notes that psychedelics remain illegal in many places and can heal or harm depending on circumstances, screening, and support.

[事实] Ruiz later advises others not to begin with intense psychedelic experiences until they have stabilized their situation through slow, deliberate professional help.

[事实] Huberman recalls Ruiz describing an ibogaine and DMT experience as one of the most profound of his life.

[事实] Ruiz says 5-MeO-DMT produced a powerful visual experience of blue-and-white geometric imagery and a felt connection to warrior culture across time.

[事实] A few months later, Ruiz says “the bottom dropped out.”

[135:22] **Severe Depression After the Door Opened**

[事实] Ruiz says the experience felt as if a door had been kicked open and an ice sculpture had shattered, leaving him alone to put it back together.

[事实] He describes severe depression and says it took more energy than anything else in his life just to put his feet on the ground in the morning.

[事实] He says he gained deep respect for people who work through depression or other severe emotional states.

[事实] Ruiz stopped drinking during that depressive period and says his sleep and fitness improved.

[事实] He says delaying coffee by about 90 minutes after waking helped his daytime energy, though he dislikes waiting.

[139:28] **Twelve Steps, Low-Grade Pain, and Help**

[事实] Ruiz read about AA and the 12 steps, not because of alcohol dependence, but because he recognized parallels with PTSD and the need to work through steps.

[事实] He describes a long-standing low-grade irritation or “unsatisfactoriness” that he carried around.

[事实] He says close friends rallied when he finally told them he was struggling.

[推测] For Ruiz, admitting distress transformed support from an abstract idea into a real protective network.

[144:52] **The Suicidal Line**

[事实] Huberman asks how close Ruiz came to the line of considering taking his own life.

[事实] Ruiz says being very close to that line lasted one day, while the surrounding danger likely lasted a couple of weeks.

[事实] He describes being awake, sweating, shaking, and believing either he was fundamentally bad or something bad had entered him that he did not know how to remove.

[149:52] **Outsourcing Identity When Judgment Is Impaired**

[事实] Huberman told Ruiz that his “goggles” were foggy and that he needed to outsource his decisions and possibly his identity for a period of time.

[事实] Ruiz says that framing helped because it allowed trusted people to hold parts of his identity and judgment while he was unstable.

[事实] Another friend told him that hurting himself would contradict everything he had stood for, and Ruiz says that tough-love statement became a pivot.

[153:33] **Therapy as the Hardest Work**

[事实] Ruiz entered regular therapy after Bridget made clear that full-time help was necessary.

[事实] He says sitting down with someone to talk about past, present, and future was scarier to him than jumping from aircraft or going into gunfights.

[事实] He describes the first months as slow, difficult work with small gains rather than dramatic breakthroughs.

[157:19] **Medication, Sleep, and Recovery**

[事实] Ruiz says he used a low dose of Wellbutrin for about four months, after a friend framed medication as a tool like a weapon sight or glasses.

[事实] Huberman says pharmacology can sometimes help people get over a bump, while some people may need longer-term medication.

[事实] Ruiz says therapy, medication, stopping drinking, stabilizing sleep, and support from friends all contributed to his recovery.

[160:38] **A Different Person, Still in Process**

[事实] Ruiz says he now feels like a completely different person in some ways, while acknowledging that the process never fully ends.

[事实] He says without the crisis and without people forcing him to get help, he might have continued living in the same hard, narrow way or worse.

[事实] He says he wants to go back and gather friends who died by suicide and tell them it does not have to go there.

[164:44] **Low-Grade Pain and Nervous System Shock**

[事实] Ruiz describes a low-grade burning or unsatisfactoriness that likely began around 2007 or earlier.

[事实] He compares repeated combat, loss, and close calls to repeated shocks to the nervous system.

[事实] He says that when he finally turned the engine down, all of those experiences came out at once.

[167:31] **Where Healing Starts**

[事实] Huberman asks where healing begins for people facing loss, fear, languishing, or accumulated pain.

[事实] Ruiz says he hopes it does not require rock bottom, but many stories suggest some version of bottom often starts the process.

[事实] He cites the idea that people change when the pain of staying the same becomes worse than the pain of change.

[169:39] **Current Routines and Guardrails**

[事实] Ruiz says he trains about five days out of seven, uses sauna on non-training days, pays close attention to sleep, and no longer drinks.

[事实] He says physical movement helps him feel clear-headed and functional.

[事实] He eats lightly throughout the day and has cut extra unnecessary commitments from his life.

[推测] His current recovery strategy relies more on simple, repeatable constraints than on dramatic interventions.

[176:16] **Range, Masculinity, and Letting Go**

[事实] Ruiz says the key idea for him is range: keeping toughness and protective capacity while also developing kindness, calm, gentleness, and openness.

[事实] He says men can over-grip control because they fear losing control in an uncontrollable world.

[事实] He says he tries to loosen the reins as a father and let his sons make their own decisions while keeping them within basic boundaries.

[推测] Ruiz’s view of masculinity is not anti-toughness; it argues that toughness becomes healthier when it is only one tool rather than the whole identity.

[184:13] **Life Now and Work in the Ordinary World**

[事实] Ruiz says he works as chief operating officer at Lids Sports Group, which he describes as the largest brick-and-mortar licensed sports retailer in North America.

[事实] He says he works a lot, but now feels more okay with busyness than he did immediately after leaving the Navy.

[事实] He says he had expected civilian life to be easier after military service, but learned there was no automatic rest in the ordinary world.

[188:11] **Mentorship Through the Story**

[事实] Huberman says Ruiz has provided mentorship by sharing his hero’s journey openly.

[事实] Huberman emphasizes that although Ruiz’s military experience is extraordinary, many people face their own versions of extraordinary challenge, return, and renewal.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value is that it turns an elite military biography into a broader conversation about discipline, identity, grief, depression, and asking for help. Ruiz’s story makes the strongest points when he contrasts what looked hard from the outside with what actually felt hardest from the inside.

The standout theme is “range.” The episode does not reject toughness, physical training, ambition, or warrior identity; instead, it argues that those traits become incomplete and potentially dangerous without reflection, emotional language, trusted relationships, and professional support.

Its limitation is that many details of special operations work are necessarily broad or withheld, and some claims, such as predictors of BUD/S success, are explicitly anecdotal rather than scientific. The discussion of psychedelics is also cautious but personal, so it should not be read as a universal treatment recommendation.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for veterans, first responders, athletes, high-achieving people, parents of intense young men, and anyone who has relied on endurance while postponing emotional repair.
