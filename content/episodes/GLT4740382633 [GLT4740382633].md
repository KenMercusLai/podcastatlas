# Gold, Cusco, and the Fall of the Inca North

## 概览

This episode follows the aftermath of Atahualpa’s execution and frames the Spanish advance into Cusco as one of history’s great gold heists. The hosts trace how Pizarro used Inca civil-war politics to install puppet rulers while pushing toward the sacred and political heart of the empire.

The discussion moves from the Spanish entry into Cusco and the looting of its temples to the struggle for the northern Inca lands around Quito. It emphasizes that the conquest was not simply “Spanish versus Inca,” but a conflict shaped by Inca factionalism, Indigenous allies, horses, morale, disease, and relentless Spanish greed.

By the end, the Spanish have taken Cusco, seized Quito, defeated or outlasted major northern commanders, and begun converting conquest into colonial settlement. The episode closes with Manco realizing that Spanish rule is consuming his empire, setting up the coming revolt and siege of Cusco.

## 分段落总结

[01:41] **The Temple of the Sun and the Episode’s Frame**

[事实] The episode opens with a passage from Peter Schaffer’s *The Royal Hunt of the Sun* describing Coricancha, the Temple of the Sun in Cusco, as a place filled with gold objects, including golden maize, birds, trees, and llamas.

[事实] The hosts say Schaffer’s description is closely based on Spanish chroniclers writing after the fall of the Incas.

[事实] The episode is framed as a story about gold, greed, Cusco’s temples and treasure houses, major battles, a terrifying northern Inca warlord, and the return of Pedro de Alvarado.

[05:03] **After Atahualpa: Pizarro Needs a Puppet Emperor**

[事实] After killing Atahualpa, Pizarro and his men are free to move toward Cusco, about 750 to 800 miles south.

[事实] Pizarro installs Tupac Hualpa, a younger brother of Huascar, as a puppet emperor after Atahualpa’s Christian funeral.

[事实] The hosts say the Spaniards at this stage make little effort to suppress local cults or impose Christianity compared with the conquest of Mexico.

[推测] The arrangement temporarily suits some southern Inca elites because it appears to restore Huascar’s faction, while real power increasingly lies with Pizarro and Charles V.

[10:04] **The Gold Rush Begins in Spain**

[事实] Hernando Pizarro reaches Seville in January 1534 with gold, becomes a sensation, and is invited to Charles V’s court in Toledo.

[事实] Cristobal de Mena and Francisco Jerez write accounts of Peru that become major 16th-century bestsellers and are translated into Italian and German.

[事实] News of Peru’s wealth encourages more adventurers to sail to the region.

[推测] The hosts suggest that once this “sniff of gold” spreads, the Incas are effectively doomed because many more Spaniards will arrive.

[12:40] **Could the Incas Have Survived?**

[事实] The hosts discuss whether an alternative history was possible in which the Incas resist Pizarro, acquire horses or weapons, and use Andean terrain to survive.

[事实] They note that the Incas lacked many bows, arrows, and pikes, which might have helped against Spanish cavalry.

[事实] They also identify Spanish technology, growing numbers, and disease as major pressures against any long-term Inca survival.

[推测] A surviving Andean highland state is presented as imaginable because of the terrain, but the broader comparison with the Americas makes long-term survival unlikely.

[13:20] **The March from Cajamarca to Cusco**

[事实] Pizarro and Almagro set off south on 11 August 1533 across the Central Andes, crossing valleys, rivers, rope bridges, and high mountain terrain.

[事实] Many locals they meet are supporters of Huascar and greet the Spaniards as liberators from the northern Quito army.

[事实] At Jauja, the Spaniards pass the bodies of thousands killed by the Quito army and later chase northern forces who had tried to burn storehouses.

[事实] The hosts note that Spanish sources mention taking servants and “many beautiful women,” and they infer that abuses likely occurred off stage.

[16:40] **Tupac Hualpa Dies and the Spanish Push On**

[事实] Tupac Hualpa falls ill and dies during the march, leaving Pizarro without his puppet emperor.

[事实] Some people suggest Manco, another brother of Huascar, as the next ruler, while others favor a son of Atahualpa.

[事实] The Spaniards continue toward Cusco through dangerous mountain terrain and fight further skirmishes with Quito forces.

[事实] The hosts stress that Spanish success depends on Indigenous allies as well as horses, not on Spanish manpower alone.

[20:05] **Manco Appears and Chalcuchima Is Executed**

[事实] In November 1533, Manco presents himself to the Spanish camp as Huascar’s younger brother and a candidate of the Cusco faction.

[事实] Pizarro tells Manco that he has come to liberate Cusco from Quito domination, and Manco appears willing to cooperate.

[事实] Manco accuses Chalcuchima of sending orders to Quisquis, after which the Spaniards burn Chalcuchima to death.

[事实] Quisquis fights one more mountain-pass battle but withdraws after failing to finish off the Spaniards before nightfall.

[23:30] **The Entry into Cusco**

[事实] On Saturday 15 November 1533, Pizarro and Manco enter Cusco as conquerors.

[事实] Cusco is described as the political and spiritual center of the Inca world, with paved roads, water channels, palaces, temples, and a central square lined with royal residences.

[事实] The Spaniards praise the city’s beauty and wealth, calling it remarkable even by Spanish standards.

[推测] The hosts suggest that Cusco’s scale and order may have seemed especially impressive after the Spaniards’ long journey through smaller settlements.

[25:00] **Royal Mummies, Storehouses, and Sacred Wealth**

[事实] Each Inca ruler had a palace or mansion that served as a resting place for his mummified body, with possessions and attendants associated with him.

[事实] Spanish accounts describe Cusco’s storehouses as filled with cloaks, gold, weapons, shields, and other goods.

[事实] The temples contain relics, ornaments, holy objects, and wealth gathered from across the Inca empire.

[事实] The hosts compare the Spanish reaction to Cusco with earlier Spanish awe at Tenochtitlan, while noting that none of these conquistadors had seen Mexico.

[30:00] **Looting Coricancha**

[事实] When the Spaniards enter the Temple of the Sun, the high priest Vilac Umu protests that entry requires fasting, bare feet, and ritual burden-bearing.

[事实] The Spaniards ignore him and find golden llamas, women, pitchers, jars, and other objects.

[事实] The Garden of the Sun is described as containing flowers, fruits, and leaves made of beaten gold.

[事实] The Pun Chao, a major golden image of the sun set with precious stones, disappears, and no one knows what happened to it.

[32:00] **Manco’s Coronation**

[事实] Pizarro chooses Manco as the new Sapa Inca, and Manco fasts for three days before his coronation.

[事实] The coronation includes royal mummies on thrones, women serving them as if they were alive, and major public celebrations.

[事实] Pedro Sancho reads the Spanish Requirement, asserting the authority of the pope, the church, and the king of Spain.

[事实] Manco and Pizarro share a golden cup, chiefs pay tribute to the Spanish flag, and Miguel de Estete says he doubts the Incas truly meant their song of thanks for Spanish rule.

[36:50] **The Chaco Hunt and Fragile Cordiality**

[事实] Manco celebrates with a traditional royal hunt called a Chaco, using 10,000 beaters to encircle animals near Cusco.

[事实] Manco, Pizarro, and about 50 armed Spaniards enter the ring and kill large numbers of trapped animals with sticks.

[事实] John Hemming is cited as saying the event was a moment of cordiality between Spaniards and Peruvians.

[推测] The hunt symbolizes a brief shared elite ritual, but the hosts make clear that this cordiality will not last.

[40:45] **Cusco’s Gold Is Melted Down**

[事实] By January 1534, Pizarro appears extraordinarily lucky: he has Cusco, palaces, temples, and the compliant young Manco.

[事实] The hosts say Cusco contains much more gold and four times as much silver as the Spaniards had already divided.

[事实] On 15 December 1533, Pizarro orders the melting down of gold and silver, and the forges run for two months.

[事实] Cristobal de Molina criticizes the Spaniards for destroying things more perfect than anything they possessed in their pursuit of wealth.

[43:15] **The Northern Problem**

[事实] Pizarro knows he must avoid being trapped like Cortes in Tenochtitlan and must deal with the wider Inca empire.

[事实] The southern third of the empire is believed loyal to Manco, while the northern third around Ecuador and southern Colombia remains Atahualpa’s heartland.

[事实] Quisquis still has about 20,000 battle-hardened but exhausted men moving north.

[事实] Another northern commander, Rumiñawi, emerges as a ruthless warlord in Quito.

[45:00] **Rumiñawi and the Human Drum**

[事实] After Atahualpa’s death, his brother Quiliscacha tries to claim leadership or act for Atahualpa’s sons.

[事实] Rumiñawi organizes a wake, drugs Quiliscacha and his entourage, has their throats cut, and turns Quiliscacha’s body into a kettle drum.

[事实] Rumiñawi then rules Quito as an independent warlord.

[推测] The episode presents Rumiñawi’s violence as both a political warning and a sign of the brutal instability inside the northern Inca faction.

[47:30] **Pedro de Alvarado Enters Peru’s Story**

[事实] Pedro de Alvarado lands on the coast of Ecuador in February 1534 with about 500 Spanish infantry and crossbowmen and 4,000 Guatemalans.

[事实] He has previously been active in Guatemala and is described as having a record of slaughtering Indigenous people.

[事实] Alvarado enslaves women and children, hangs chiefs, burns people alive, and feeds one chief to dogs.

[事实] Other conquistadors are shocked enough that his conduct later leads to a judicial inquiry.

[51:40] **The Race for Quito**

[事实] Sebastian de Benalcázar advances from the coast with about 200 men, while Almagro also moves toward Quito.

[事实] Benalcázar gains Indigenous Cañari allies, who hate the Incas because of Atahualpa’s cruelty toward them.

[事实] At Teocajas, near Mount Chimborazo, Benalcázar meets Rumiñawi’s large army.

[事实] Gonzalo Fernández de Oviedo claims around 50,000 people fought there, with Indigenous combatants making up nine out of ten.

[53:40] **Battle of Teocajas and the Burning of Quito**

[事实] Spanish cavalry charges at Teocajas, and Rumiñawi’s men fight hard enough to kill some horses and force the Spaniards back before nightfall.

[事实] The Spaniards leave campfires burning overnight and slip away, allowing Benalcázar to enter Quito.

[事实] Rumiñawi has already removed the treasure, Atahualpa’s family, and 4,000 women, then burned palaces and storehouses.

[事实] Rumiñawi’s men kill 300 Virgins of the Sun after they refuse to leave with him.

[56:00] **Spanish Atrocities and Alvarado’s Disaster**

[事实] Unable to find Quito’s treasure, the Spaniards raid the countryside and kill women and children while demanding gold.

[事实] Alvarado’s inland expedition gets lost in jungle, suffers insects and disease, is covered by volcanic ash, and then enters the Andes by the wrong mountain pass.

[事实] Eighty-five Spaniards die of exposure or hypothermia, and the Guatemalans, porters, and many female camp followers freeze to death.

[事实] Alvarado eventually sells his ships and gear to Almagro for 100,000 gold pieces, leaves Peru, and returns to Guatemala.

[60:00] **The Fall of Quisquis and Rumiñawi**

[事实] Quisquis reaches the Quito region and discovers that the Spanish have captured the northern capital.

[事实] His men ask him to seek peace because they believe the Spaniards are invincible.

[事实] Quisquis refuses to surrender, saying he would rather starve in the wilds than bend the knee, and his officers bludgeon him to death.

[事实] Rumiñawi is captured near a mountain lake, tortured in Quito, and executed in June 1535.

[62:45] **Conquest Turns into Colonization**

[事实] The hosts say the conquest of the north is completed rapidly, less by Spanish battle strength alone than through lost morale among the Quito forces and the help of Indigenous allies.

[事实] Pizarro refounds Cusco as a Spanish city under Spanish law and tells locals they should be well treated.

[事实] His own men continue looting and abusing people, forcing Pizarro to issue repeated orders against mistreatment.

[事实] Gonzalo Maldonado imprisons the High Priest of Cusco for gold and silver, and Pizarro threatens to execute him to force the priest’s release.

[65:30] **Lima, Encomienda, and Manco’s Breaking Point**

[事实] News of Peru’s wealth draws more Spaniards from the Caribbean and Central America.

[事实] In January 1535, Pizarro selects a coastal site for a new city he wants to call Ciudad de los Reyes, which becomes known as Lima.

[事实] Pizarro distributes land, villages, and laborers to Spaniards through an encomienda-style system, despite royal officials warning that Indigenous people are free subjects of the crown.

[事实] Manco sees his empire being given away, faces complaints about Spanish abuses, and becomes increasingly aware that he is treated as a puppet.

[67:45] **Setup for Revolt**

[事实] In summer 1535, one of the Pizarro brothers takes an interest in Manco’s sister, who is also his wife.

[事实] At the same time, the feud between the Pizarro brothers and Diego de Almagro reaches boiling point.

[事实] The hosts say the next episode will cover Manco summoning his people to war, the battle for Cusco, Pizarro’s darkest hour, and the climax of the feud with Almagro.

[推测] The episode positions these tensions as the immediate causes of the coming Inca revolt.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it treats the conquest of Peru as a political collapse as much as a military conquest. The hosts repeatedly show how civil war, local alliances, elite calculations, and morale made Spanish success possible despite tiny Spanish numbers.

[推测] Its strongest material is the vivid contrast between sacred Inca wealth and Spanish destruction: Coricancha, the Garden of the Sun, the Pun Chao, the mummies, and the melting down of priceless objects all make the scale of loss clear.

[推测] The limitation is that the story still depends heavily on Spanish chroniclers, and the hosts note several points where details are uncertain or poorly recorded, especially around battles, motives, and what happened to missing treasures.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners following the wider conquest series, especially those interested in empire, civil war, colonial extraction, and the moral psychology of greed.
