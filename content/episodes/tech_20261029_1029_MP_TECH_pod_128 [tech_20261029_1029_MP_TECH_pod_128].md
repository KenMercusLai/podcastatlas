# AI’s Memory-Chip Hunger Is Making Smartphones More Expensive

## 概览

AI data centers and consumer electronics compete for memory chips made by only a handful of companies. The resulting shortage has pushed global smartphone prices up by an average of 15%, with the greatest pressure falling on entry-level and mid-range devices.

Manufacturers are cutting specifications—including camera resolution, displays, and battery configurations—to control costs. Rising new-phone prices are also affecting refurbished devices, while brands’ efforts to reuse memory components may reduce the supply reaching the secondhand market.

Consumers are responding by keeping phones for roughly four years or longer. In price-sensitive markets such as India and Southeast Asia, sustained memory costs could make financing more important and prompt broader responses from carriers, manufacturers, governments, and regulators.

## 分段落总结

[00:20] **AI Demand Drives a Memory-Chip Shortage**

[事实] Consumer electronics and AI data centers both require memory chips, but only a small number of companies manufacture them.

[事实] Surging demand has caused memory shortages and raised prices across consumer electronics, including smartphones.

[事实] Counterpoint Research reported that average global phone prices had increased by 15%, with even larger increases in markets where consumers have less ability to absorb them.

[推测] Competition with AI infrastructure is turning memory from an ordinary component cost into a major constraint on smartphone affordability.

[00:53] **Lower-Cost Phones Bear the Heaviest Impact**

[事实] Memory now accounts for a much larger share of a device’s total cost.

[事实] Entry-level and mid-range phones are more affected by higher memory prices than premium models.

[事实] Manufacturers have reduced camera resolution or changed displays and battery configurations to keep products within their target cost and pricing structures.

[推测] Budget-conscious buyers may pay more for new phones that offer weaker specifications than earlier models in the same category.

[01:28] **Refurbished Phones Face Supply and Price Pressure**

[事实] Price increases for new devices also affect refurbished-phone prices.

[事实] Some brands are considering recovering components—especially memory—from existing devices and repackaging them into new products.

[事实] Reusing components this way leaves less inventory available for refurbishment and sale to consumers.

[推测] The refurbished market may provide less relief for price-sensitive buyers if both its supply tightens and its prices rise.

[02:05] **Consumers Extend Smartphone Replacement Cycles**

[事实] The global smartphone replacement cycle has moved beyond 42 months and is approaching 48 to 50 months.

[事实] Consumers recognize that their existing devices remain adequate for longer and are less willing to upgrade merely because a traditional two-year cycle has elapsed.

[事实] Higher prices are not being matched by innovations that many buyers consider meaningful enough to justify upgrading.

[事实] Some newer models may offer downgraded cameras, batteries, or other components, weakening the appeal of purchasing a more expensive device.

[事实] Consumers across regions are keeping their phones longer.

[03:38] **Affordability Risks Grow in Price-Sensitive Markets**

[事实] India and Southeast Asia have experienced some of the largest percentage increases in smartphone prices.

[事实] Smartphones in these markets can serve as people’s primary means of connectivity and may be important to their livelihoods and access to essential services.

[事实] Governments and regulatory bodies have begun considering whether they may need to respond over the longer term.

[事实] More financing options may be needed, creating additional pressure on mobile carriers and smartphone brands.

[事实] Memory prices are expected to keep increasing for some time and are unlikely to quickly return to levels seen two years earlier.

[推测] Sustained price pressure could require significant changes across the smartphone ecosystem to preserve access for lower-income consumers.
