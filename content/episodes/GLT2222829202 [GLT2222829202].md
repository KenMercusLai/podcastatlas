# Australian Prime Ministers from Bob Hawke to Scott Morrison

## 概览

This episode continues the podcast’s survey of wartime and postwar Australian prime ministers, focusing on the period from Bob Hawke through Scott Morrison. The hosts frame Australian politics as unusually combative, colourful, and rich in “sledging,” while also arguing that British listeners often underestimate its interest and influence.

The central political arc runs from the Hawke-Keating Labor era, presented as both socially progressive and economically liberal, through John Howard’s long conservative premiership, and then into a period of repeated leadership coups or “spills” involving Kevin Rudd, Julia Gillard, Tony Abbott, Malcolm Turnbull, and Scott Morrison.

A major conclusion is that Australian politics has mattered beyond Australia: Hawke and Keating are described as influential on Clinton, Blair, Brown, and the “third way,” while Tony Abbott’s border policy is described as influential on later British Conservative thinking.

## 分段落总结

[00:50] **Australian Political Sledging and the Setup**

[事实] The hosts open with Bob Hawke’s insult about Tony Abbott being “as mad as a cut snake,” using it to introduce Australia’s tradition of sharp political language.
[事实] They explain “sledging” as a great Australian art, comparing political insults to the abuse used in cricket.
[事实] The episode is presented as the final part of a longer-than-planned series on Australian prime ministers.

[02:03] **Bob Hawke as the Archetypal Australian Prime Minister**

[事实] Hawke is described as a square-jawed, craggy, beer-loving figure who fits a British stereotype of an Australian prime minister.
[事实] He was a Rhodes scholar at Oxford and is said to have set a world record for drinking a yard of ale in 11 seconds.
[事实] The hosts contrast his hard-drinking public image with a deeply religious upbringing.

[03:17] **Hawke’s Religious Background and Sense of Mission**

[事实] Hawke’s father was a Congregationalist pastor, and Hawke grew up in an intense Christian atmosphere.
[事实] After a serious motorbike crash, Hawke reportedly believed God had spared his life, which strengthened his sense of mission.
[事实] He later lost his faith after seeing the contrast between Christian youth conference privilege and poverty in India.
[推测] The hosts interpret Hawke’s Labor politics as a secularized version of Christian moral principles.

[06:13] **Trade Unionism, Patriotism, and Pro-American Labor Politics**

[事实] Hawke’s loss of faith did not remove his sense of mission, and the hosts connect that mission to his move into trade unionism.
[事实] He was a prominent trade union leader before entering parliament.
[事实] The hosts describe him as pro-American, anti-communist, patriotic, and aligned with a pro-Western left-wing tradition also seen in British Labour figures.

[07:21] **Hawke’s Premiership and Economic Reform**

[事实] Hawke entered parliament only three years before becoming prime minister.
[事实] His government revived and intensified universal healthcare efforts associated with Gough Whitlam.
[事实] His economic reforms included floating the Australian dollar, deregulating the economy, reducing tariffs, and opening Australia to globalization.
[推测] The hosts argue that Hawke’s economic program, though left-wing in presentation, closely resembled Thatcher-era reforms in Britain.

[09:03] **The Hawke-Keating Partnership**

[事实] Paul Keating is introduced as Hawke’s treasurer and political partner.
[事实] Keating is described as Catholic, of Irish descent, working class, the son of a boilermaker, and someone who left school at 14.
[事实] Hawke could play the ordinary larrikin despite elite credentials, while Keating had working-class roots but loved Mahler and collected French antique clocks.
[推测] The hosts see the Hawke-Keating partnership as a model for later “third way” politics associated with Clinton, Blair, and Schroeder.

[12:00] **Kirribilli, Betrayal, and Keating’s Rise**

[事实] The hosts describe a “homicidal Game of Thrones” quality in Australian party politics.
[事实] Hawke and Keating had an agreement at Kirribilli House in 1988 that Hawke would hand over power, but Hawke did not do so.
[事实] Keating challenged Hawke in 1991, failed the first time, then won a second challenge in December 1991 and became prime minister.
[推测] The hosts compare this dynamic to the Blair-Brown pact in British politics.

[13:18] **Keating’s Republicanism and Anti-British Strain**

[事实] Keating is described as motivated by resentment toward Britain as class-ridden, monarchical, antiquated, and imperial.
[事实] He blamed Britain for decisions around Singapore and Australia’s defence in the Second World War.
[事实] Keating wanted Australia to become a republic and told Queen Elizabeth II during her 1992 visit that Australia wanted that change.
[事实] British tabloids attacked him after he briefly touched the Queen’s back, calling him the “Lizard of Oz.”

[15:21] **Keating’s Insults and Political Style**

[事实] Keating is presented as exceptionally skilled at insults.
[事实] The hosts cite his line to John Hewson, “I want to do you slowly,” and several insults aimed at Andrew Peacock, John Howard, the Liberal Party, the National Party, and Peter Costello.
[事实] His insult about being attacked by Costello being like being flogged with warm lettuce is highlighted.
[推测] The hosts treat Keating’s language as part of a broader Australian political style that is direct, aggressive, and theatrical.

[17:06] **Keating’s Substantive Legacy**

[事实] Keating gave the 1992 Redfern Park speech on Aboriginal reconciliation.
[事实] The hosts describe him as the first prime minister to go especially far in confronting racism and the legacy of the White Australia policy.
[事实] He famously called the downturn “the recession we had to have,” a phrase the Liberal Party used against him in the 1993 election.
[事实] Despite that, he won the 1993 election.

[18:44] **Keating’s Asia Pivot and AUKUS Criticism**

[事实] Keating continued to argue that Australia should pivot toward Asia and China.
[事实] He said Australia’s most important partner was Indonesia rather than Britain or America.
[事实] He opposed the AUKUS deal signed by Scott Morrison, saying Australia should not rely on what he called the faded Anglosphere.
[事实] Keating remained prime minister until losing the 1996 election to John Howard.

[20:33] **John Howard’s Long Conservative Premiership**

[事实] John Howard served as prime minister from 1996 to 2007, making him the second-longest-serving Australian prime minister after Robert Menzies.
[事实] He is described as Anglophile, monarchist, conservative, Christian, a cricket fan, and close to George W. Bush.
[事实] The hosts say he was strongly supportive of the War on Terror.
[推测] One host argues that Howard benefited from Keating’s economic stewardship, with the Australian economy performing strongly during his premiership.

[22:46] **Howard, Pauline Hanson, Immigration, and Howard Battlers**

[事实] Pauline Hanson is discussed as a figure who rose and fell during Howard’s period and was hostile to the end of White Australia.
[事实] Howard is described as known in Europe for stern immigration policy.
[事实] His electoral base included “Howard battlers,” compared to Reagan Democrats in the United States and the Red Wall in Britain.
[事实] These voters are described as aspirational, respectable working-class or lower-middle-class people attracted to law and order and strong borders.

[24:10] **Howard’s Defeat and the Start of the Spill Era**

[事实] Howard won four elections but was defeated and lost his own seat.
[事实] He was later nominated by Australia and New Zealand to lead the International Cricket Council but was rejected.
[事实] Howard’s defeat brought Labor leader Kevin Rudd to power.
[推测] The hosts treat Howard’s long period of stability as being followed by a period of political civil war.

[25:04] **Kevin Rudd and the Culture of Spills**

[事实] Kevin Rudd is described as impressive, a Mandarin speaker, a former diplomat, and someone who studied Chinese.
[事实] The hosts describe the 2010s as a period of constant leadership assassinations and comebacks.
[事实] Rudd introduced a stimulus package during the 2008 financial crisis, and Australia avoided recession while many other industrialized countries suffered badly.
[推测] One host suggests Australia’s broader stability may have allowed its politics to become more internally dramatic.

[26:40] **Julia Gillard’s Rise and Political Position**

[事实] Julia Gillard was born in Barry, Wales, moved to Australia as a child, and came from a Baptist background.
[事实] She was a lawyer, feminist, unmarried, and had no children, which became a subject of criticism from some on the right.
[事实] She was Rudd’s deputy prime minister and education minister before removing him.
[事实] After becoming prime minister, she governed through a hung parliament with support from Greens and independents.

[28:18] **Gillard, Abbott, and the Misogyny Speech**

[事实] Gillard faced constant attacks and had a major feud with Tony Abbott.
[事实] The hosts frame Gillard as resembling the Guardian newspaper in human form and Abbott as resembling the Daily Mail in human form.
[事实] Gillard became famous for a parliamentary speech rejecting being lectured on sexism and misogyny by Abbott.
[事实] Rudd later returned, removed Gillard, and then lost the election to Abbott after only a short time back in power.

[30:13] **Tony Abbott’s British, Catholic, and Conservative Profile**

[事实] Tony Abbott is described as representing the more overtly pro-British strain in Australian politics.
[事实] He was born in London, had an English father, was a Rhodes scholar at Queen’s College, Oxford, and had trained as a priest.
[事实] His nickname was “the mad monk.”
[事实] He reintroduced knighthoods and damehoods and gave a knighthood to Prince Philip, which the hosts describe as controversial in Australia.

[32:05] **Abbott, Climate Change, Mining, and Borders**

[事实] The hosts say Australia’s economy benefited greatly from China’s demand for raw materials.
[事实] They describe climate change, bushfires, rising temperatures, and mining as politically sensitive issues in Australia.
[事实] Abbott is described as belonging to the school that treated climate change concerns as nonsense.
[事实] His Operation Sovereign Borders policy sent boat arrivals to processing centres outside Australia rather than bringing them into Australia.

[33:58] **Australian Political Combativeness and Abbott’s Fall**

[事实] The hosts say Australian political rhetoric is unusually full-throated compared with British politics.
[事实] They describe both right-wing and left-wing Australian politicians as rhetorically intense and direct.
[事实] Abbott was removed after two years in power by Malcolm Turnbull.
[推测] The hosts imply that Australia’s parliamentary culture rewards sharper and more personal confrontation than British politics usually does.

[35:04] **Malcolm Turnbull’s Liberal Republican Profile**

[事实] Malcolm Turnbull attended Brasenose College, Oxford, as did David Cameron, and they were prime ministers at the same time.
[事实] Turnbull played a leading role in defending Peter Wright over the publication of Spycatcher in Australia.
[事实] The hosts say British officials were humiliated during the case.
[事实] Turnbull was a prominent republican and chaired the campaign for a republic in the 1999 referendum.

[37:31] **Turnbull’s Fall and Scott Morrison’s Image**

[事实] Turnbull lasted about three years before being removed by Abbott’s faction and replaced by Scott Morrison.
[事实] Morrison is described in comic terms as looking like a man at a garden centre or like a koala.
[事实] The hosts mention campaign incidents in which he knocked over a child while playing football and was told by a homeowner to get off his lawn.
[推测] Morrison is portrayed as a politician prone to awkward public mishaps.

[38:35] **Morrison’s Crises: Bushfires, COVID, AUKUS, and Djokovic**

[事实] Morrison was criticized for being on holiday in Hawaii during the severe 2019-20 Australian bushfires.
[事实] The hosts say he handled COVID well through “fortress Australia” and vaccine rollout, resulting in a lower death rate than Europe.
[事实] He annoyed France through the AUKUS submarine deal.
[事实] He presided over the Novak Djokovic visa controversy, which the hosts describe as entangled with federal and state government issues.

[39:35] **Morrison’s Pentecostalism and the Question of Decline**

[事实] Morrison is described as a Pentecostalist who has suggested social media trolls are literally emanations of the devil.
[事实] The hosts ask whether the culture of spills damaged good government by making prime ministers constantly watch their backs.
[事实] They discuss whether recent Australian prime ministers are of the same stature as earlier figures such as Menzies, Hawke, Keating, and Whitlam.
[推测] The hosts do not settle the question, noting that people often think contemporary politicians are worse than past ones.

[41:18] **Australia’s Global Position and Political Influence**

[事实] The hosts describe Hawke and Keating as exceptional politicians whose influence extended beyond Australia.
[事实] They say Australia’s mineral resources and relationship with China have partly uncoupled it from trends in Europe and America.
[事实] They connect Morrison’s AUKUS deal to renewed instability around Australia’s relationship with China.
[事实] The hosts conclude that Australian politics is much more interesting and internationally influential than many British listeners might assume.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it turns a sequence of leadership biographies into a readable political argument: Australian politics is not a peripheral curiosity, but a system that has generated influential policy models, vivid personalities, and recurring tensions over Britain, Asia, race, immigration, religion, and national identity.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the portraits of Hawke and Keating, where personal background, economic policy, rhetorical style, and international influence are tied together clearly. The discussion of Australian political insults also gives the episode a distinctive tone and helps explain the podcast’s fascination with the subject.

[推测] The limitation is that later prime ministers receive more compressed and comic treatment, especially Howard, Turnbull, and Morrison. The hosts acknowledge that they do not know enough to judge some questions fully, including whether the quality of Australian political leadership has truly declined.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in political character, Westminster-style leadership drama, Australian-British relations, and the international circulation of policy ideas rather than a strictly comprehensive institutional history.
