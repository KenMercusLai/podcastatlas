# The July Crisis: From Belgrade to Russian Mobilisation

## 概览

This episode traces the July Crisis after the expiry of Austria-Hungary’s ultimatum to Serbia, focusing on the days when leaders still believed a wider war might be avoided even as military and diplomatic mechanisms narrowed their options.

A major thread is the contrast between personal monarchy and state machinery. Kaiser Wilhelm II and Tsar Nicholas II exchange intimate “Nicky-Willy” messages and both appear, at moments, anxious to avoid catastrophe, but ministers, generals, alliances, mobilisation timetables, and public commitments keep pushing the crisis forward.

The discussion moves from Austria-Hungary’s declaration of war and the first shelling of Belgrade to Britain’s unresolved debate over Belgium and neutrality, France’s effort to avoid appearing the aggressor, and Russia’s decisive move toward general mobilisation.

## 分段落总结

[00:14] **The Kaiser’s Corfu Letter**

[事实] The episode opens with a 1909 letter from Kaiser Wilhelm II to Tsar Nicholas II, written from Corfu and filled with birthday wishes, family affection, and admiration for the Achilleion and its surroundings.

[事实] The hosts identify the letter as part of the wider “Nicky-Willy” correspondence between the Kaiser and the Tsar, who were cousins as well as heads of rival imperial systems.

[推测] The opening letter frames the episode’s central tension: personal warmth between monarchs could coexist with state systems preparing for war.

[02:01] **Private Affection and Public Diplomacy**

[事实] The hosts explain that the Nicky-Willy messages were partly private and partly shaped by officials who inserted diplomatic language about peace and disputes.

[事实] Wilhelm also complained about press hostility and claimed newspapers helped inflame antagonism between nations.

[事实] The Achilleion connects Wilhelm to Austro-Hungarian imperial culture through Empress Elisabeth, known as Sisi, who had lived there.

[推测] The episode uses Wilhelm’s love of Corfu and his sentimental tone to complicate a purely warlike image of the Kaiser.

[06:00] **After the Austrian Ultimatum**

[事实] The previous episode ended with the Austrian ultimatum expiring on Saturday, 25 July, followed by celebrations in Vienna.

[事实] On Sunday, 26 July, Austrian leaders asked General Conrad if he was ready, and he suggested mid-August would be ideal rather than immediate action.

[事实] Sir Edward Grey proposed mediation by Britain, France, Germany, and Italy to handle the Austro-Serbian dispute.

[事实] By Monday, 27 July, Germany was unwilling to support Grey’s plan because it would appear to put Austria-Hungary, its ally, before a court.

[08:00] **Britain’s Gloom and Asquith’s Detachment**

[事实] Grey told the House of Commons that failure to resolve the crisis could bring the greatest catastrophe Europe had experienced.

[事实] The British cabinet mood was gloomy, and some Liberals questioned why great powers should fight over Serbia.

[事实] Prime Minister Asquith wrote to Venetia Stanley about the cabinet discussion and then shifted to domestic social plans and bridge.

[推测] The hosts portray Asquith as calm to the point of detachment, though they also note this could be read as composure rather than neglect.

[10:00] **The Kaiser Returns From Norway**

[事实] Wilhelm had been away on his yacht and was called back from Norway as the crisis worsened.

[事实] When shown the Austrian ultimatum, he believed Serbia would not risk war with Austria and Russia would not intervene.

[事实] Bethmann Hollweg advised him not to enter central Berlin because crowds might interpret his return as a signal of war.

[事实] In Berlin, officials still believed the crisis might be localised and that Britain wished to remain neutral.

[13:00] **The Serbian Reply and “Stop in Belgrade”**

[事实] On Tuesday, 28 July, Wilhelm read Serbia’s reply to the ultimatum and judged it a strong Austrian moral victory that removed the reason for war.

[事实] Wilhelm proposed that Austria-Hungary occupy Belgrade as a “deposit” and then stop, rather than launch a full campaign.

[事实] German officials passed this idea on weakly, while figures such as Falkenhayn dismissed Wilhelm’s intervention as another senseless utterance.

[推测] The “stop in Belgrade” idea appears in the discussion as one of the last plausible off-ramps from a wider war.

[17:00] **Why Austria Rejected Limitation**

[事实] Austria-Hungary rejected the limited Belgrade option because it would not capture the Serbian networks it believed responsible for the assassination.

[事实] Conrad’s war plan did not focus on a direct strike on Belgrade but on defeating the Serbian army from another direction.

[事实] German leaders had tied their honour and credibility to backing Austria-Hungary, making it difficult to reverse course.

[推测] The hosts suggest that Austria-Hungary’s need for visible retribution made symbolic occupation politically inadequate.

[18:00] **Moltke and German Military Fatalism**

[事实] Helmuth von Moltke the Younger is introduced as German Chief of Staff, overshadowed by his famous uncle and marked by pessimism and fatalism.

[事实] Moltke believed war with Russia was inevitable and that delay would only worsen Germany’s position.

[事实] He argued that the coming conflict could annihilate European civilisation, yet still saw preparation as necessary.

[事实] Bethmann Hollweg resisted immediate military escalation, showing that civilian leaders were still restraining the army at this stage.

[22:00] **Austria-Hungary Declares War**

[事实] On Tuesday, 28 July, exactly one month after the murders of Franz Ferdinand and Sophie, Emperor Franz Joseph signed the declaration of war on Serbia.

[事实] The proclamation was issued in ten languages and presented the war as forced and defensive.

[事实] Only after declaring war did Berchtold ask Conrad whether Austria-Hungary was prepared if Russia intervened, and Conrad’s answer was effectively no.

[推测] The discussion presents Austro-Hungarian decision-making as determined but badly coordinated.

[23:00] **Russia Faces the Mobilisation Choice**

[事实] After Austria-Hungary declared war, Sazonov and the French ambassador Maurice Paléologue agreed that Russia and France should not back down.

[事实] Sazonov asked Tsar Nicholas II to approve mobilisation in principle, with a choice between partial mobilisation in four western districts and general mobilisation across the empire.

[事实] Doing nothing was no longer treated as an option in St Petersburg.

[推测] The hosts regard this as a major escalation because mobilisation decisions began to drive events faster than diplomacy could contain them.

[26:00] **The First Shots at Belgrade**

[事实] In the early hours of Wednesday, 29 July, Serbs connected to Major Vojislav Tanković blew up the railway bridge linking Belgrade and Austria-Hungary.

[事实] At around 2 a.m., the Austro-Hungarian river warship Bodrog opened fire on the Kalemegdan Citadel and Serbian positions.

[事实] The hosts describe these shots as revenge for Franz Ferdinand and Sophie and as the effective opening shots of the First World War.

[28:00] **Vienna Celebrates and Britain Debates Belgium**

[事实] Crowds celebrated in Vienna after news of war, including veterans’ associations and many young men.

[事实] Sigmund Freud wrote that he felt Austrian for the first time in thirty years and expressed emotional commitment to Austria-Hungary.

[事实] In Britain, Grey led a cabinet discussion about Belgium and the treaties of 1839 and 1870.

[事实] Grey explained that Britain’s response to a German violation of Belgian neutrality would be a policy decision, not simply an automatic legal obligation to fight.

[30:00] **Belgium, the Schlieffen Logic, and British Ambiguity**

[事实] Grey said Germany was expected to strike west against France and likely move through Luxembourg and Belgium because the French frontier was heavily fortified.

[事实] Lloyd George suggested Germany might only pass through southern Belgium, and even Churchill did not see a limited incursion as necessarily enough reason for Britain to enter the war.

[事实] Grey refused to declare British neutrality publicly because he wanted to preserve freedom of action and avoid encouraging Germany.

[推测] The hosts see Grey’s ambiguity as understandable but increasingly difficult to sustain.

[34:00] **Was London the Key Arena?**

[事实] The hosts discuss the argument associated with Niall Ferguson that Grey’s ambiguity may have encouraged war and left Britain trapped.

[事实] They also argue that the crisis was driven more by Vienna, Belgrade, Berlin, and St Petersburg than by London.

[事实] Britain remained crucial because it was the world’s greatest power and had not yet decided whether to enter.

[推测] The episode treats Britain as important but not fully in control of the crisis.

[36:00] **Nicky and Willy Try to Pull Back**

[事实] Tsar Nicholas sent Wilhelm a telegram asking him, in the name of their friendship, to restrain Austria-Hungary.

[事实] Wilhelm sent Nicholas a message saying he was trying to restrain Austria and asking Russia not to mobilise because that would make mediation impossible.

[事实] Nicholas hated the thought of war and told Sazonov he would not be responsible for a monstrous slaughter.

[事实] Russian ministers had already been pushing toward general mobilisation, and the general staff continued preparations even after Nicholas resisted.

[40:00] **Berlin Hopes Britain Will Stay Out**

[事实] Falkenhayn wanted Germany to move to a “state of imminent war,” but Bethmann Hollweg resisted, saying more time was needed for talks.

[事实] Prince Heinrich returned from Cowes and reported that George V hoped Britain and his government would remain neutral.

[事实] Wilhelm took this as highly reassuring and told his admirals he trusted the word of a king.

[事实] News then arrived that Russian partial mobilisation had begun, shocking German leaders who had assumed Russia would not follow through.

[44:00] **Germany Pressures Austria Too Late**

[事实] Bethmann Hollweg still refused immediate German mobilisation and preferred that Russia appear to be the party forcing war.

[事实] Germany began urging Austria-Hungary more seriously to consider Wilhelm’s limited “stop in Belgrade” idea.

[事实] Austria-Hungary refused, arguing that backing down under Russian pressure would rescue Serbia again and show that the empire could never defend itself.

[事实] Austrian leaders were simultaneously panicking about whether to redirect troops east toward Russia, exposing logistical confusion.

[47:00] **Wilhelm Feels Betrayed**

[事实] Wilhelm received another telegram from Nicholas saying Russian partial mobilisation had been planned for days.

[事实] Wilhelm interpreted this as evidence that Russia had been planning behind his back.

[事实] He blamed encirclement and England’s anti-German policy, and he raged that Edward VII was stronger after death than Wilhelm was alive.

[推测] The hosts read this moment partly as personal humiliation and family trauma, not only strategic calculation.

[48:00] **France Keeps Its Head Down**

[事实] French leaders had just returned from their ship, with Viviani anxious and Poincaré taking a much firmer line.

[事实] Poincaré insisted there should be no settlement and that France must stand by Russia.

[事实] French leaders wanted to prepare quietly, keep troops back from the frontier, and make sure France and Russia did not appear to fire first.

[事实] Abel Ferry said the aim was to show British public opinion and the British government that France and Russia would not be the aggressors.

[50:00] **Russia Orders General Mobilisation**

[事实] On Thursday, 30 July, Russian ministers continued pressing Nicholas to approve general mobilisation.

[事实] Nicholas initially resisted and told his war minister on the phone that he would cut him off.

[事实] Sazonov visited the Tsar at Peterhof and argued there was no chance of avoiding war and that delay would leave Russia vulnerable.

[事实] Nicholas gave way, and Sazonov told General Yanushkevich to transmit the order and then smash the telephone so the Tsar could not reverse it.

[52:00] **The Abyss Opens**

[事实] The mobilisation order was carried into the central telegraph office in St Petersburg and transmitted across the Russian Empire.

[事实] The hosts end by saying the abyss is now open and that the final episode will show everyone tumbling into it.

[推测] The episode presents Russian general mobilisation as the point after which avoiding a wider European war became extremely difficult.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s main value is its close attention to timing. It shows how, between 25 and 30 July 1914, leaders repeatedly saw possible exits from the crisis, yet each delay, mobilisation plan, alliance promise, and public commitment made those exits harder to use.

Its strongest material is the contrast between personality and machinery: Wilhelm and Nicholas appear indecisive, sentimental, frightened, and sometimes sincere in wanting peace, while their governments and armies continue moving toward conflict. This gives the episode more nuance than a simple story of leaders eagerly rushing into war.

A limitation is that the account is explicitly told partly from a British perspective, even while the hosts stress that London was not the main cockpit of events. [推测] Listeners looking for a fuller Serbian or Austro-Hungarian internal view may need earlier or companion episodes for more context.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in diplomatic history, the origins of the First World War, and how large systems can turn personal hesitation into collective escalation.
