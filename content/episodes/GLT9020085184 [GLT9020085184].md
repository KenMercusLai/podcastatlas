# Nelson Goes to War

## 概览

This episode follows Horatio Nelson from the outbreak of war between Revolutionary France and Britain in 1793 through the Mediterranean campaign, ending on the eve of the Battle of Cape St Vincent.

The discussion emphasizes Nelson’s preparation of the Agamemnon, his concern for supplies, crew health, discipline, and morale, and his growing belief that aggressive action at sea could deliver decisive results.

A second thread compares Nelson with Napoleon Bonaparte. The hosts present both as ambitious, self-believing figures, but distinguish Nelson as institutionally loyal and conservative, while Napoleon is reshaping war and politics on land.

The episode closes with Britain retreating from the Mediterranean after Napoleon’s Italian successes, Nelson forming a lasting bond with Thomas Masterman Hardy, and the Spanish fleet moving into position for the next major naval confrontation.

## 分段落总结

[00:31] **Opening Frame: Toulon and Nelson’s Enemy**

[事实] The episode opens with a dramatic reading about Nelson witnessing the violence unleashed by the French Revolution at Toulon.

[事实] The hosts identify the passage as coming from Dominic Sandbrook’s children’s book about Nelson.

[事实] They frame the episode as the moment Nelson goes to war after France declares war on Britain.

[推测] The opening deliberately sets up Nelson and Napoleon as symbolic rivals, one defending Britain at sea and the other rising through revolutionary warfare.

[02:40] **Preparing the Agamemnon**

[事实] At the beginning of 1793, Nelson is 34 and preparing his new ship, the Agamemnon, at Chatham Dockyard.

[事实] The Agamemnon is described as 12 years old, built of English oak, 200 feet long, and 50 feet wide.

[事实] Nelson pays close attention to provisions, including biscuit, flour, water, beer, wine, citrus fruit, tea, Turkish coffee, and even live bullocks.

[事实] He also orders prayer books and hymn books because he values regular religious services for the crew.

[推测] The hosts use these details to show that Nelson’s effectiveness rested not only on battlefield courage but also on logistics and shipboard management.

[05:27] **Recruiting and Caring for the Crew**

[事实] Nelson recruits men through posters and pamphlets in Norfolk and brings in old Caribbean friends as lieutenants.

[事实] His midshipmen include sons of well-to-do Norfolk families and his 13-year-old stepson Josiah.

[事实] He also takes 20 ship’s boys from London’s Marine Society, a charity for poor and destitute boys; only six of them could read and write.

[事实] A midshipman wrote that Nelson was considered one of the first captains in the service and was beloved by his men and officers.

[推测] The crew’s loyalty appears tied to Nelson’s reputation for looking after his men and offering chances of prize money.

[08:29] **Britain’s Naval Strategy in 1793**

[事实] Nelson joins Admiral Hood’s squadron off Cornwall, which includes about 50 ships, 18 of them ships of the line.

[事实] The Royal Navy at this point is described as having more than 600 ships, 14,000 guns, and 100,000 men.

[事实] Britain’s strategy is described as relying on naval power while subsidizing land powers such as Austria and Prussia to fight France on land.

[事实] The hosts say British leaders did not foresee that the war would last roughly two decades.

[推测] The episode presents Britain’s early assumptions about the war as seriously underestimating the scale of the revolutionary and Napoleonic challenge.

[09:54] **Toulon and the Turn to Naples**

[事实] Admiral Hood’s Mediterranean squadron is tasked with aiding Austria in Italy and strengthening ties with smaller European powers such as Naples.

[事实] Toulon is described as France’s major Mediterranean naval base and essential to French sea power in the region.

[事实] Hood persuades Toulon’s royalists to hand over dockyards, ships, and forts in exchange for British protection.

[事实] Nelson is sent to Naples to secure supplies, where King Ferdinand and Queen Maria Carolina are sympathetic to Britain and hostile to the French Revolution.

[推测] Naples becomes important not only strategically but also personally because it brings Nelson into contact with the Hamiltons.

[12:31] **Nelson Meets the Hamiltons**

[事实] In Naples, Nelson meets Sir William Hamilton, the British envoy, and Emma Hamilton, William’s much younger wife.

[事实] The hosts describe Sir William as interested in classical scholarship, volcanoes, and hosting notable visitors.

[事实] They sharply disagree over Emma Hamilton, with one emphasizing her warmth, bravery, loyalty, and fun, while the other stresses negative traits.

[事实] The Hamiltons take to Nelson and promote his interests with the Neapolitan court.

[推测] The exchange foreshadows Emma Hamilton’s later importance in Nelson’s life without developing that story in this episode.

[14:30] **The Fall of Toulon and Nelson Versus Napoleon**

[事实] When Nelson returns toward Toulon, Napoleon Bonaparte is using artillery from the heights to bombard the forts.

[事实] Nelson is not present for the fall of Toulon because Hood sends him to patrol the Italian coast.

[事实] At Livorno, Nelson sees refugees from Toulon and writes to Fanny about separated families and fathers without families.

[事实] The French retake and sack Toulon because it had been full of counter-revolutionaries during the Terror.

[推测] Nelson’s reaction to Toulon reinforces his hostility to revolutionary France and gives the hosts a point of contrast with Napoleon.

[15:47] **Comparing Nelson and Napoleon**

[事实] The hosts say Nelson and Napoleon are often seen in opposition to each other.

[事实] They describe both men as enormous egotists with strong belief in their own star quality.

[事实] Nelson is described as constrained by institutional loyalty, conservative temperament, and a lack of interest in political power.

[事实] The hosts say Nelson wanted complete victory at sea, just as Napoleon sought decisive victory on land.

[推测] The comparison suggests that Nelson’s ambition was channeled through service to the Royal Navy, while Napoleon’s ambition transformed political power itself.

[17:21] **Corsica as Britain’s New Mediterranean Base**

[事实] After Toulon is lost, Admiral Hood identifies Corsica as an ideal base between France and Italy.

[事实] In spring 1794, the British target Bastia on Corsica’s eastern coast.

[事实] Nelson is in the thick of the action and nearly killed when a musket ball strikes a boulder beside him.

[事实] The hosts stress that Nelson leads from the front and appears in full uniform so his men know where he is.

[推测] Nelson’s visible courage strengthens his authority but also exposes him to the dangers that later define his career.

[18:56] **Calvi and the Loss of Sight in One Eye**

[事实] After Bastia falls, the British move against Calvi, the last French fortress on Corsica.

[事实] Calvi is described as backed by mountains and defended by around 100 guns.

[事实] On the morning of 12 July, a French shot hits nearby sandbags and sends stones and debris into Nelson’s face.

[事实] From that point onward, Nelson can no longer see out of his right eye, though he never wears an eye patch.

[事实] Nelson downplays the injury, does not list himself officially among the wounded, and tells Fanny it is only a small scratch.

[推测] His concealment of the injury fits the episode’s portrayal of Nelson as determined to maintain an image of resilience and command.

[22:53] **After Calvi: Blockade, Lull, and Livorno**

[事实] After the break, the hosts confirm that Nelson’s eye injury does not end his career.

[事实] With Corsica in British hands, Nelson spends time blockading and operating off the Italian coast.

[事实] The hosts say it seems likely Nelson had either a flirtation or a full relationship with the opera singer Adelaide Correglia.

[事实] Admiral Hood is replaced by Admiral William Hotham, who is described as decent but cautious.

[推测] This quieter phase highlights Nelson’s frustration with commanders who do not share his appetite for decisive action.

[25:22] **First Fleet Action Against the French**

[事实] Nelson receives reports of French sails off Genoa and joins the pursuit.

[事实] By this point he has been in the Navy for 24 years but has not yet seen a fleet in action.

[事实] He attacks the Ca Ira, described as the largest two-decker ship in the world, with 84 guns and heavier armament than the Agamemnon.

[事实] Nelson holds fire until close range and then repeatedly fires broadsides into the French ship.

[事实] The fighting continues into a second day, and the Ca Ira and the Censeur eventually surrender.

[推测] The action confirms Nelson’s belief that aggressive tactics and superior British gunnery can overcome larger French ships.

[28:14] **Hotham’s Caution and Nelson’s Frustration**

[事实] Nelson’s ship loses three men, with four wounded, while the French lose more than 100.

[事实] Nelson wants to pursue the rest of the French fleet after the captures.

[事实] Admiral Hotham refuses, saying they must be content with what they have achieved.

[事实] Nelson is deeply disappointed because he sees a missed opportunity to knock the French out of that part of the Mediterranean.

[推测] The episode treats this as an early example of the “Nelson touch”: seizing even a small chance for complete victory.

[29:35] **John Jervis Takes Command**

[事实] Hotham is replaced at Christmas 1795 by John Jervis.

[事实] Jervis is described as “Old Oak,” strict, forceful, and intolerant of slackness.

[事实] He improves discipline by addressing dirty ships, drinking, and lax standards.

[事实] Nelson and other younger captains admire Jervis because they believe he will take the war to the French.

[推测] Jervis matters because he recognizes and backs aggressive talent lower down the chain of command.

[31:10] **Napoleon’s Italian Campaign and Britain’s Retreat**

[事实] Napoleon has taken command in Italy and smashed Austrian forces west of Genoa.

[事实] He enters Milan, defeats the Pope’s army, and rolls up allied positions on land.

[事实] These French successes force ports such as Genoa to close to the Royal Navy.

[事实] Spain changes sides and backs France.

[事实] In autumn 1796, Jervis receives orders to withdraw from Corsica, end Mediterranean operations, and pull back to Gibraltar.

[推测] The hosts frame this as proof that command of the sea can protect Britain but cannot by itself decide the fate of empires.

[33:02] **Elba, Hardy, and a Last Mediterranean Task**

[事实] Before the withdrawal, Jervis sends Nelson to Elba to retrieve a British garrison.

[事实] On the return journey, Nelson skirmishes with Spanish frigates while aboard the Minerve because the Agamemnon is being repaired.

[事实] Nelson becomes closely acquainted with Lieutenant Thomas Masterman Hardy.

[事实] Hardy is described as huge, slow, hardworking, brave, and loyal to Nelson.

[事实] Hardy is captured by the Spanish in a way that helps Nelson escape with prizes, and Nelson later saves Hardy after he goes overboard.

[推测] Their shared experiences establish a bond that will continue until the final moments of Nelson’s life.

[34:30] **Through the Spanish Fleet Toward Cape St Vincent**

[事实] Nelson heads toward Gibraltar and is told to rendezvous with Jervis off the southwestern coast of Portugal.

[事实] The British have news that the Spanish fleet has sailed and may join the French fleet off Brittany.

[事实] In thick fog overnight on 11 February, Nelson’s ship passes through the Spanish fleet without being detected.

[事实] At dawn, Nelson reaches Jervis’s fleet off Cape St Vincent and brings news of the Spanish.

[事实] Jervis is aboard his flagship, Victory.

[推测] The episode ends as a deliberate cliffhanger, setting up the Battle of Cape St Vincent as the next major test of Nelson’s style of command.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value lies in how it links character, logistics, and strategy. Nelson is not presented only as a heroic battlefield figure; the hosts spend significant time on provisioning, health, discipline, recruitment, and morale.

The strongest material is the contrast between Nelson and Napoleon. Both are portrayed as ambitious men shaped by the same revolutionary war, but operating through very different institutions and values.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode is openly shaped by a British naval perspective and comic hostility to Revolutionary France, so listeners looking for a balanced French or Corsican view may need other sources.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in naval history, the French Revolutionary Wars, Nelson’s early career, and the wider prelude to the Napoleonic Wars.
