# The Abyssinian Expedition of 1868: Teodros, Hostages, and the Road to War

## 概览

This episode begins the story of the British Abyssinian expedition of 1868, framing it as a dramatic but less widely remembered Victorian campaign: an operation to rescue European hostages held by Emperor Teodros II at Magdala.

The hosts trace the background of Abyssinia, modern Ethiopia, as an ancient Christian kingdom with deep biblical traditions, a long history of isolation, and growing pressure from Ottoman, Egyptian, and European interests. Against this setting, Teodros emerges first as a charismatic modernising ruler who hopes to reunite and strengthen his country.

The episode then follows the deterioration of Teodros’s rule and relations with Britain: ignored letters, missionary tensions, paranoia, hostage-taking, failed diplomacy, and escalating British frustration. By the end, diplomacy has collapsed, and the British cabinet has decided that military action is unavoidable.

## 分段落总结

[00:15] **The Campaign Is Introduced**

[事实] The episode focuses on the Abyssinian expedition of 1868, described as a strange and dramatic British military campaign.
[事实] The immediate purpose of the expedition was to rescue nearly 60 European hostages held by Emperor Teodros II at Magdala.
[事实] The hosts note that objects taken after the British stormed Magdala later entered major institutions such as the British Museum, British Library, V&A, Bodleian, and Royal Ontario Museum.
[推测] The episode positions the expedition as both an adventure story and a morally complicated imperial episode.

[03:24] **Abyssinia’s Historical Setting**

[事实] Abyssinia, now Ethiopia, is described as a Christian kingdom that was never colonised and had adopted Christianity very early.
[事实] The hosts connect Ethiopia’s Christian identity with traditions about the Ark of the Covenant and descent from Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.
[事实] By the nineteenth century, Abyssinia was under pressure from Muslim powers including the Ottomans and Egyptians.
[推测] This background helps explain why Teodros viewed foreign diplomacy through a religious and defensive lens.

[07:38] **Walter Plowden and British Interest**

[事实] Walter Plowden arrived in Abyssinia in 1843 and became fascinated by the country’s warfare, feasts, chivalric culture, and political drama.
[事实] Plowden later became Britain’s first consul at Massawa, the Red Sea gateway into Abyssinia.
[事实] His ambition was to secure a lasting Anglo-Abyssinian friendship treaty.
[推测] Plowden’s enthusiasm made him more sympathetic to Abyssinian politics than later British representatives would be.

[09:35] **The Rise of Teodros II**

[事实] Abyssinia was politically fragmented, with warlords and regional strongmen competing for power.
[事实] A tradition existed that a messianic ruler named Teodros would reunite the country and restore its greatness.
[事实] A man named Kassa defeated his rivals, became emperor in 1855, and took the name Teodros II.
[事实] Plowden initially saw Teodros as energetic, charismatic, tactful, and capable of restoring order.

[12:27] **Teodros as Moderniser and Zealot**

[事实] Teodros wanted to crush regional warlords, build roads, reform royal justice, fight slavery, and modernise dress and military capability.
[事实] Plowden also noted Teodros’s violent anger, pride in divine kingship, and religious zeal.
[事实] Teodros was fascinated by Britain, France, and the Crimean War, but struggled to understand why Britain and France allied with the Ottoman Empire against Russia.
[推测] His admiration for Britain coexisted with suspicion that British policy lacked true Christian solidarity.

[17:50] **Religious Tensions and an Ignored Letter**

[事实] The Coptic Patriarch Cyril IV criticised Abyssinian religious practice during a visit, angering Teodros.
[事实] Cyril warned Teodros not to trust the English, claiming they meant to undermine and conquer his country.
[事实] Teodros dictated a friendly letter to Queen Victoria, asking for Christian friendship.
[事实] Plowden also asked London to send Teodros guns and ammunition, but the Foreign Office responded only with a rifle, screwdriver, and case after a long delay.

[21:08] **Personal Loss and Growing Violence**

[事实] Teodros faced continuing rebellions and became increasingly frustrated with his people.
[事实] His wife Tawabak, described as a moderating influence, died in 1858.
[事实] He remarried for political reasons, began drinking heavily, and confessed to missionaries that he was living an unchristian life.
[事实] Plowden was ambushed by rebels in 1860 and died nine days later.

[22:50] **Paranoia, Punishment, and Guns**

[事实] After Plowden’s death, Britain took two years to send a new representative.
[事实] Missionaries reported that Teodros became increasingly vengeful, angry, and suspicious.
[事实] He punished suspected traitors with mutilation and came to call himself the “flail of the wicked.”
[事实] Teodros became obsessed with artillery and pressured Swiss and German missionaries at Gafat to build larger guns.

[26:02] **Captain Cameron Arrives**

[事实] Captain Charles Duncan Cameron arrived as Britain’s new envoy in October 1862.
[事实] Cameron gave Teodros two duelling pistols from Queen Victoria in recognition of Teodros’s kindness to Plowden.
[事实] Cameron was less sympathetic than Plowden and was disturbed by Teodros’s violent language and ambitions against Egypt.
[事实] Teodros sent Cameron away with another letter for Queen Victoria, asking for safe passage for Abyssinian ambassadors and drawing attention to Muslim oppression of Christians.

[28:48] **The Stern Crisis and Hostage-Taking**

[事实] Teodros became increasingly angry that Queen Victoria had not replied to his letter.
[事实] Missionary Henry Aaron Stern, who had written harshly about Abyssinia and mocked Teodros’s genealogy, was arrested and beaten.
[事实] Stern and Henry Rosenthal were tried, accused of insulting Teodros and undermining Ethiopian Christianity, and sentenced to death.
[事实] On 3 January 1864, Stern realised that Cameron and other Europeans had also been chained and imprisoned.

[35:38] **London Discovers Its Mistake**

[事实] In May 1864, Lord Russell received a smuggled note from Cameron saying that he and several others were chained and would not be released until Teodros received a civil answer to his letter.
[事实] Officials discovered that Teodros’s earlier letter had reached Britain but had been filed away and forgotten.
[事实] Queen Victoria’s reply was drafted and sent in hopes of securing the prisoners’ release.
[事实] British concern was shaped partly by fear that Teodros might turn toward France during the era of Suez Canal rivalry.

[39:24] **Hormuzd Rassam’s Mission**

[事实] Hormuzd Rassam, an Assyrian-born archaeologist and British political agent in Aden, was chosen to carry Victoria’s reply.
[事实] Rassam arrived at Massawa in July 1864 with Dr. Henry Blanc and Lieutenant Prideaux.
[事实] He waited for months while gathering reports about Teodros’s unstable reputation.
[事实] Messages from the hostages warned that only military action might secure their release and that Rassam himself risked imprisonment.

[46:19] **Rassam Meets Teodros**

[事实] Rassam received permission to travel inland after Teodros sent a grievance-filled reply that implied an invitation.
[事实] On 28 January 1866, Rassam met Teodros and delivered Queen Victoria’s letter.
[事实] Rassam handled Teodros carefully, expressing sympathy and encouraging patience.
[事实] Teodros was delighted by the letter and promised to release the captives.

[50:08] **Temporary Hope and Renewed Dependency**

[事实] Teodros asked Rassam to accompany him while hostages were brought down from Magdala.
[事实] During the journey, Teodros repeatedly questioned Rassam about Britain, the empire, the Crimean War, and missionary insults.
[事实] He wanted a formal British treaty and a dedicated British contact in India.
[事实] Teodros sent Rassam many gifts, including weapons, manuscripts, animals, money, a medal, shields, and a golden saddle.

[55:11] **Rassam Is Arrested**

[事实] On 13 April 1866, Rassam, Blanc, and Prideaux crossed Lake Tana to say goodbye to Teodros.
[事实] They entered Teodros’s pavilion, found the throne empty, and were seized by guards.
[事实] The missionaries were also rounded up again and returned to shackles.
[推测] The hosts suggest several explanations: Teodros may have planned deception, been overcome by hatred of the missionaries, or been unable to let Rassam leave.

[57:30] **Teodros Demands Technicians and Guns**

[事实] Teodros claimed Rassam had offended him by failing to bring the missionaries to say goodbye.
[事实] He wrote to Queen Victoria saying he was keeping the envoys and others as guests.
[事实] He demanded skilled workers, including blacksmiths, gunsmiths, and artillery specialists, to make weapons.
[事实] Reverend Martin Flad was sent to London with the letter, leaving his family behind in Abyssinia.

[59:18] **Britain Tries One More Bargain**

[事实] Flad warned the Foreign Office that sending troops would cause Teodros to slaughter the hostages.
[事实] Britain prepared items including a steam engine, gunpowder mill, smelting equipment, hunting rifle, and telescope.
[事实] The Foreign Office told Flad that Teodros would receive the equipment only after releasing the hostages.
[事实] When Flad returned, he found messages from hostages warning that Teodros was unlikely to let anyone go.

[61:04] **Captivity at Magdala and Stalemate**

[事实] Teodros alternated between friendly gestures, such as celebrating Queen Victoria’s birthday, and violent episodes, including forcing prisoners to witness punishments.
[事实] He once told the captives that his father had been mad and that he now believed he was mad too.
[事实] The hostages were sent back to Magdala, chained in huts at the mountain citadel.
[事实] Teodros continued fighting rebels with extreme cruelty, while the diplomatic deadlock lasted for months.

[65:24] **Ultimatum and War**

[事实] In March 1867, Dr. Blanc smuggled a note arguing that only force remained.
[事实] In early summer 1867, Teodros received a British ultimatum giving him three months to release the hostages.
[事实] Teodros did not accept the ultimatum.
[事实] On 19 August 1867, Lord Derby’s cabinet met in London and decided that diplomacy had failed and war was necessary.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its clear narrative structure: it turns a relatively obscure Victorian expedition into a gripping chain of misunderstandings, neglected correspondence, religious tension, personal instability, and imperial calculation.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the portrait of Teodros II as more than a caricature. The hosts show him as moderniser, Christian monarch, admirer of Britain, suspicious ruler, and increasingly violent hostage-taker.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode necessarily relies heavily on British and missionary perspectives, so Teodros and Abyssinian society are often filtered through hostile or anxious European observers.

[推测] This episode is especially suited to listeners interested in Victorian empire, Ethiopia, hostage diplomacy, nineteenth-century global communications, and the way small diplomatic failures can escalate into war.
