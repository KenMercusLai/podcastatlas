# The Future of Healthcare Technology with Eli Lilly and Company’s Diogo Rau

## 概览

Malcolm Gladwell speaks with Diogo Rau, Eli Lilly’s chief information and digital officer, about bringing a technology-industry perspective to pharmaceuticals. Rau contrasts Silicon Valley’s short planning cycles with drug development, where companies make decisions more than a decade ahead and scientists may never see their work reach patients.

The conversation explores how healthcare could become more consumer-centered. Rau argues that the industry should design around the patient experience, reduce the barriers between deciding to seek treatment and receiving medicine, and give patients direct access to their own health data.

Concrete examples include cryptographically secure medicine packaging, wearable sensors, continuous health measurement, and tools that provide rapid feedback on whether treatment is working. Rau defines success as enabling same-day access to appropriate medicine, supporting long-term adherence, and using AI to discover medicines that humans might never have conceived.

## 分段落总结

[01:33] **From Apple to Eli Lilly**

[事实] Rau spent ten years at Apple after earlier technology work at McKinsey and in startups.

[事实] He joined Eli Lilly after conversations with its leadership and research organization showed him that significant innovation was happening beyond the technology industry.

[事实] Rau began his role at Lilly on May 17, 2021.

[推测] His outsider status gives him a useful basis for questioning healthcare practices that industry veterans may take for granted.

[04:36] **Pharmaceutical development operates on unusually long timelines**

[事实] Rau was surprised that a 2021 executive meeting discussed revenue forecasts for 2030, whereas an 18-month forecast can already seem unrealistic in technology.

[事实] Lilly was discussing a potential 2036 product launch, although approval could not be guaranteed.

[事实] The company assigns a “probability of technical success” at each development stage, and bringing a medicine to market can take ten years.

[事实] Rau says many scientists may never see the medicines they work on reach patients.

[推测] These timelines encourage a corporate culture that places long-term outcomes above short-term gains.

[07:36] **A mandate to put technology into every part of Lilly**

[事实] Lilly’s CEO asked Rau to introduce technology throughout the company and to change existing ways of working rather than merely maintain them.

[事实] Rau’s remit extends from molecule discovery to the consumer-facing side of healthcare.

[事实] He believes the process through which patients obtain medicines has changed surprisingly little since the 1950s.

[事实] Rau says pharmaceutical companies have not consistently worked backward from the experience they want to create for patients.

[推测] His transformation agenda treats technology as an operating model for the whole organization, not simply an internal IT function.

[09:54] **Designing healthcare around the patient experience**

[事实] Rau identifies Apple’s practice of beginning with the desired customer experience and working backward as the most important lesson from his time there.

[事实] In that approach, customer experience is the principle guiding business and product decisions rather than one consideration among several.

[事实] Rau acknowledges that Lilly has not yet fully reached this standard.

[推测] Applying this philosophy to healthcare may require changing existing business processes when they conflict with a simpler patient journey.

[12:58] **Using secure packaging to fight counterfeit medicines**

[事实] Rau says medicines can be easier to counterfeit than electronic devices because copying packaging and vial labels can be sufficient to deceive buyers.

[事实] Counterfeit GLP-1 medicines are a particular concern because demand is high and many insurance plans do not cover chronic weight management.

[事实] Counterfeit products may be ineffective and can also be dangerous when they are produced outside sterile manufacturing environments.

[事实] Rau demonstrates packaging with a cryptographically secure NFC tag that a phone can scan to verify that the package matches one produced by the manufacturer.

[事实] Unlike a printed QR code, the tag is designed not to be reproducible simply by copying the package.

[15:42] **From product authentication to medication safety**

[事实] Rau describes the secure tag as a potential foundation for direct interaction between patients and manufacturers, not merely an authentication mechanism.

[事实] He says the concept could eventually help confirm that a patient has the correct medicine, prevent repeated doses, and warn hospital staff before administering the wrong drug.

[事实] The demonstrated version places the tag on the box; a later version could place it on an individual medicine.

[事实] Rau estimates that embedding such technology in a pill could be technically possible within about five years, but consumer acceptance may prevent it.

[推测] The main obstacle to ingestible tracking may be trust and social acceptance rather than technical feasibility.

[17:50] **The role and limits of wearable health devices**

[事实] Lilly has not produced its own wearable and mainly uses wearable devices in clinical trials.

[事实] Rau says launching one is complicated for a pharmaceutical manufacturer because questions arise about whether the device is tied to, or required for, use of a medicine.

[事实] Looking ten years ahead, he expects even highly engaged users to wear only two or three devices, such as a ring, watch, heart patch, or sensor-equipped insole.

[事实] Gladwell cites eye-tracking research as an example of how sensors can reveal differences between novice and expert behavior.

[推测] Wearable adoption is likely to depend as much on physical convenience and regulatory context as on the amount of data a device can collect.

[20:29] **Combining medicine with continuous feedback**

[事实] Rau says some clinical-trial participants already receive devices alongside their treatment.

[事实] He argues that feedback can encourage patients to remain on medication, using scales and weight-management medicines as an example.

[事实] He contrasts this with statins and cholesterol treatments, whose effects are generally not visible to patients from one day to the next.

[事实] Rau explicitly presents the claim that feedback improves adherence as a hypothesis.

[推测] A future therapeutic offering could combine medicine, sensing, and feedback into one continuing service rather than treating the drug as a standalone product.

[21:46] **Health data should belong to the patient**

[事实] Rau hopes direct digital relationships between manufacturers and patients can remove some intermediaries.

[事实] He argues that health data should primarily be available to patients, who can then choose to share it with healthcare providers.

[事实] He rejects the industry assumption that providers should necessarily see or interpret data before patients do.

[事实] Rau agrees that continuous monitoring could assist someone with a condition such as arthritis, while insisting that the individual should still be able to see the underlying data.

[推测] This model shifts patients from passive recipients of professional interpretation toward active participants in managing their health.

[23:49] **Moving healthcare from clinics into the home**

[事实] Rau agrees that the primary point of healthcare contact is moving from hospitals and doctors’ offices toward the home.

[事实] He identifies multiple barriers to treatment, including long waits for appointments and limited physical access to pharmacies.

[事实] Rau states that 45 million Americans live in pharmacy deserts and that 46% of US counties lack a pharmacy within 15 minutes of residents’ homes.

[事实] His goal is to reduce the interval between deciding to seek help and receiving medicine from months to the same day, using telehealth and delivery when treatment is appropriate.

[推测] The proposed model treats speed and accessibility as essential parts of therapeutic effectiveness rather than as secondary logistical concerns.

[25:18] **Rau’s definition of long-term success**

[事实] Rau says his principal consumer-side goal is to let people seek appropriate medicine, receive it the same day, and use continuing tools to remain engaged with treatment.

[事实] On the discovery side, he hopes to help develop new medicines with AI.

[事实] He predicts that the next decade will produce medicines that no human could have imagined, although long development timelines mean they may take about 15 years to reach patients.

[推测] Rau measures digital transformation primarily by its effect on medicine access, adherence, and discovery rather than by the deployment of technology itself.

[26:36] **Why speed and rapid feedback matter**

[事实] Gladwell compares healthcare with deterrence theory, in which certainty, severity, and swiftness all shape behavior but swiftness is often neglected.

[事实] Rau agrees that receiving medicine quickly and rapidly seeing whether it works could change patient behavior and improve adherence.

[事实] He applies the same principle to technology failures, emphasizing time to mitigation and the speed with which teams absorb feedback and adapt.

[事实] Rau argues that risks are more manageable when decisions are reversible and systems can change course quickly.

[推测] Faster feedback could turn treatment into an iterative process in which ineffective options are abandoned sooner and visible progress reinforces continued use.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest feature is its use of tangible examples—secure packaging, wearables, scales, telehealth, and same-day delivery—to make healthcare’s digital transformation concrete. Rau also offers an illuminating contrast between the rapid cycles of consumer technology and the decade-long horizons of pharmaceutical development.

Its most valuable theme is that access, feedback, and data ownership are not peripheral conveniences. They may directly affect whether patients begin treatment, understand its effects, and continue using it. The discussion is particularly relevant to healthcare leaders, product designers, digital-health professionals, and listeners interested in patient-centered technology.

The conversation remains largely strategic and optimistic. Several ideas are prototypes, hypotheses, or future possibilities, and it offers limited discussion of cost, privacy, regulation, clinical validation, or unequal access to digital services. [推测] These unresolved issues would materially influence whether the proposed consumer-centered model can work at scale.
