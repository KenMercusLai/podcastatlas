# From Supersonic Crisis to AI Power: How Boom Turned an Engine Failure into a New Business

## 概览

Boom Supersonic founder Blake Scholl recounts how the collapse of the company’s Rolls-Royce engine relationship made Boom nearly unfundable. He now regards outsourcing the engine as a fundamental mistake: a critical technology could not be entrusted to a slow, politically constrained incumbent.

Boom responded by developing its own engine with an experienced external engineering team. That decision unexpectedly enabled boomless supersonic cruise, closer integration between aircraft and cabin design, and a derivative 42-megawatt gas turbine aimed at data centers facing AI-driven power shortages.

The conversation expands into a critique of aerospace, energy, and defense incumbents. Scholl argues that regulation, financial short-termism, outsourcing, and government-supported industrial stagnation suppressed innovation for decades. His proposed alternative is a flexible commercial manufacturing base built around digital engineering, rapid production, and the ability to pivot during national emergencies.

## 分段落总结

[00:34] **The failed Rolls-Royce engine strategy**

[事实] Scholl calls outsourcing development of Boom’s semi-custom supersonic engine to an established company one of his most disastrous decisions.

[事实] Boom initially hired an engine designer and considered building its own engine, but became attracted to adapting an existing Rolls-Royce platform because it appeared capable of eliminating an entire development program.

[事实] Industry expectations also pushed Boom toward one of the three major engine manufacturers, and negotiations advanced as far as a Rolls-Royce board meeting in early 2022.

[事实] At that meeting Rolls-Royce’s CEO was removed but left temporarily in office, creating organizational paralysis and internal political competition.

[02:55] **A private breakdown becomes a public crisis**

[事实] As the Rolls-Royce relationship deteriorated during 2022, Boom explored alternatives while expecting the relationship to remain publicly intact through the end of the year.

[事实] Boom announced additional partners at the Farnborough Airshow, and American Airlines followed United Airlines with a preorder.

[事实] After the American Airlines announcement, Rolls-Royce publicly ended its relationship with Boom without warning Scholl.

[事实] Scholl says the proposed Rolls-Royce engine depended on a next-generation platform whose development had already stopped, information that Rolls-Royce had not shared with Boom.

[推测] Scholl believes Boom’s growing credibility made the failed relationship politically dangerous for Rolls-Royce executives because another manufacturer might ultimately power a successful aircraft.

[06:18] **Boom becomes “unfundable”**

[事实] Following the public split, Scholl says investors, partners, and airlines stopped engaging with Boom, while aerospace reporters repeatedly pressed him to identify an engine supplier.

[事实] The proposed adapted engine would have required substantial compromises, including an inability to support boomless cruise.

[事实] Scholl now believes Rolls-Royce probably would never have delivered the engine even if the companies had signed a contract.

[07:39] **Choosing to build the engine internally**

[事实] Boom found a Florida-based contract engineering group largely composed of former Pratt & Whitney supersonic specialists.

[事实] The team had formed after Pratt & Whitney closed its West Palm Beach office and attempted to relocate employees to Hartford.

[事实] A talk by Airbnb cofounder Brian Chesky argued that companies facing crises should become more fully what they were always meant to be, rather than compromise their identity merely to survive.

[事实] Scholl interpreted that message as confirmation that Boom should build its own engine and committed the company to that path.

[事实] Boom assembled limited partnerships involving the Florida engineering team, additive manufacturing support, and a maintenance provider to establish credibility outside the major engine manufacturers.

[10:22] **Internal engine development unlocks boomless flight**

[事实] Scholl argues that established engine companies rarely create genuinely new engines, instead modifying existing platforms through slow and expensive multilayered supply chains.

[事实] By controlling both aircraft and engine design, Boom could optimize transonic performance rather than accept the limitations of the proposed Rolls-Royce engine.

[事实] Boom’s engineers found that at sufficiently high altitude, a sonic boom could turn upward in the atmosphere and never reach the ground, provided the aircraft performed well near the speed of sound.

[事实] After rerunning simulations, the team discovered that engine optimization had effectively solved the boom problem about six months earlier without their realizing it.

[事实] Boom began sketching its engine in mid-2022, publicly unveiled it at the end of that year, and flew the XB-1 test aircraft supersonically in early 2025.

[13:35] **Searching for a revenue product**

[事实] Scholl identifies financing a supersonic airliner before revenue as Boom’s central business problem.

[事实] Boom’s engineers had previously noted that a supersonic engine resembles a power turbine because both require continuous full-power operation in high-temperature conditions.

[事实] In March 2025, Scholl saw reports that AI data centers faced power shortages and that turbine availability had become a major constraint.

[事实] Sam Altman told Scholl that he would want as many 40-megawatt natural-gas turbines as Boom could produce, while Boom’s chief engineer said the company already had a technical plan but had not recognized the market.

[事实] After roughly two months of market and technical validation, Boom committed to the turbine business.

[16:23] **A 42-megawatt turbine for AI data centers**

[事实] Removing the thrust fan from Boom’s aircraft engine and connecting additional turbine stages to a generator turns the architecture into a 42-megawatt power system.

[事实] Scholl says this capacity is well suited to modular data-center deployment: approximately 24 units could provide one gigawatt of power.

[事实] Medium-sized units provide a balance between efficiency, operational complexity, and redundancy, allowing customers to deploy them in N-plus-one arrays.

[事实] Scholl compares the concept to blade servers for energy and says Boom was already three years into developing the relevant core technology.

[事实] The new strategy helped Boom raise capital and accelerate manufacturing work that had previously been constrained by limited funding.

[18:28] **Why hard-tech fundraising is different**

[事实] Before the power business, Scholl frequently heard that people wanted to fly on Boom’s aircraft but did not want to invest in the company.

[事实] He argues that hard-tech companies are not necessarily more capital-intensive overall than software companies, but require more investment before reaching their first decisive proof point.

[事实] A software company can demonstrate product-market fit by launching and measuring growth, whereas a supersonic aircraft company cannot cheaply build and release its complete product for testing.

[事实] Scholl says Douglas Aircraft, founded in 1921, was the last entrepreneur-founded commercial aircraft company before Boom, leaving venture investors with little relevant historical pattern to follow.

[事实] Short funding runways made Boom less efficient because the team could not confidently make long-term decisions.

[21:20] **Power revenue remains tied to the supersonic mission**

[事实] Boom raised money around the turbine strategy at the end of 2025, while maintaining that it would still develop the supersonic passenger aircraft.

[事实] Scholl expects the turbine to become a high-margin revenue product capable of funding aircraft development.

[事实] The supersonic mission also acts as a recruiting advantage: engineers attracted by aircraft development can advance technology shared with the power turbine.

[推测] The turbine business is presented not as a diversion but as a bridge product that aligns revenue, technical development, and talent acquisition.

[22:27] **Energy stagnation and incumbent incentives**

[事实] Scholl says US energy production per person grew exponentially until around 1971, then slowed and eventually flattened.

[事实] He associates this shift with nuclear stagnation, environmental regulation, and laws such as the National Environmental Policy Act, while acknowledging that he does not know every cause.

[事实] Scholl argues that regulation often combines a moral justification with economic protection for an incumbent that does not want new competition.

[推测] He suggests the coal industry may have supported opposition to nuclear power, but explicitly says he does not know that to be true.

[事实] He contends that AI-driven demand is forcing the United States to restart an energy-production system that has lacked sufficient expansion for decades.

[25:14] **Legacy turbine and aerospace companies stop innovating**

[事实] Scholl says employees at incumbent turbine companies initially laughed at Boom and placed bets on when it would fail.

[事实] He cites GE’s LM6000 midsized turbine as an example of a current product derived from a 747 engine dating to 1971.

[事实] He says Boeing’s last genuinely new commercial aircraft design began in 2004, with subsequent work centered on modifying existing products.

[推测] Boom benefits in the short term from incumbents dismissing it, because they do not yet view the company as a competitive threat.

[27:27] **Co-designing the engine, aircraft, and cabin**

[事实] Owning the engine lets Boom optimize the propulsion system and airframe together, rather than force an external engine into a fixed aircraft design.

[事实] A new cabin concept significantly improved the intended passenger experience but initially reduced simulated range by about 1,000 miles on a 4,000-mile aircraft.

[事实] Inspired by Apple’s decision to pursue the more ambitious “Purple Two” iPhone concept, Scholl asked the company to commit to the better cabin and recover the lost range through engineering.

[事实] Boom modeled roughly a dozen buildable engine configurations using its Makeboom software, which wraps an aircraft design around an engine and simulates a complete flight.

[事实] One configuration recovered most of the lost range, demonstrating the advantage of optimizing the aircraft and engine as one system.

[32:01] **Designing a more lovable flying experience**

[事实] Scholl wants Boom’s aircraft to reflect the product care he associates with Apple rather than the seams, awkward fittings, dirt, and afterthoughts common in existing airliners.

[事实] Boom aims to eliminate middle seats and board and deplane the entire aircraft in approximately five minutes each.

[事实] The aircraft is expected to include Starlink connectivity.

[事实] Scholl notes that airborne Wi-Fi emerged after the latest clean-sheet commercial aircraft designs, which is why current antennas are commonly added as external humps.

[事实] Boom plans to integrate the antenna into the original aircraft design, avoiding the visible structure and associated drag.

[34:33] **How financial management hollowed out Boeing**

[事实] Scholl attributes part of Boeing’s decline to management practices brought in after the McDonnell Douglas merger and influenced by Jack Welch-era GE.

[事实] He criticizes “return on net assets” for encouraging companies to remove manufacturing and supply-chain assets from their balance sheets.

[事实] He also argues that managing primarily to beat quarterly Wall Street forecasts discourages long-term product investment.

[事实] Scholl cites former Boeing CEO Jim McNerney’s “no more moonshots” approach as effectively meaning no new products, only modifications to the existing portfolio.

[36:22] **The missing generation in aerospace**

[事实] Scholl identifies speed as a historic driver of commercial aviation innovation and argues that the 1973 US ban on civilian supersonic flight removed that pathway.

[事实] He says defense innovation weakened after the Cold War because programs increasingly became jobs programs and corporate support rather than urgent responses to an adversary.

[事实] With little ambitious work available, many aviation enthusiasts of Scholl’s generation entered software and SaaS instead of aerospace.

[推测] Scholl concludes that stagnating industries select for employees comfortable with maintaining existing systems, further reducing their capacity to innovate.

[38:34] **Apollo, Concorde, and unsustainable prestige projects**

[事实] Scholl contrasts the achievements of 1969—Apollo’s Moon landing and Concorde’s first supersonic flight—with the subsequent inability to sustain either capability commercially.

[事实] He argues that both programs were Cold War prestige projects rather than economically durable industries.

[事实] Apollo consumed a substantial share of the US federal budget and relied on large cost-plus contracts, making the model unsustainable after the political objective changed.

[事实] Scholl describes the Space Shuttle as a failure, noting that two of five orbiters were lost in fatal accidents.

[事实] He credits private spaceflight, particularly SpaceX, with reviving an industry that had stagnated under the earlier model.

[41:06] **A commercial industrial base as national defense**

[事实] Scholl argues that keeping defense incumbents alive through continuous government work made them slow and unable to scale rapidly when urgently needed.

[事实] He contrasts this with World War II, when strong commercial manufacturers such as Ford, GM, and Chrysler retooled factories to produce military equipment.

[事实] He says US aerospace manufacturing remained domestic for national-security reasons but became extremely slow and expensive; a component Boom could print in three days might otherwise take 18 months to procure.

[事实] Boom’s intended defense role is to remain a competent commercial manufacturer capable of pivoting production during wartime.

[事实] Its digital engineering and reconfigurable manufacturing systems are designed to reduce dependence on hard tooling, with a stated goal of moving from a design idea to a high-performance turbine component within 24 hours.

[45:14] **A new generation returns to hard tech**

[事实] The hosts note that roughly a quarter of a recent Y Combinator batch consisted of hard-tech companies.

[事实] Scholl says today’s aerospace and mechanical-engineering students are highly capable and that talented young people are choosing the industry again.

[事实] He credits Elon Musk with making ambitious aerospace work inspiring to a new generation.

[事实] Boom livestreamed XB-1’s supersonic flight partly to inspire young viewers, and Scholl received images and videos of children celebrating the moment it broke the sound barrier.

[推测] He sees public demonstrations of real technological progress as a recruiting mechanism whose effects may extend for decades.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is strongest as a detailed founder narrative about turning a potentially fatal dependency into vertical integration. The sequence from the Rolls-Royce breakup to Boom’s own engine, boomless cruise, and the data-center turbine provides a coherent example of how control over core technology can unlock both product performance and a viable financing strategy.

Its second major value is the connection it draws among product design, industrial capacity, talent, and national resilience. The discussion moves beyond aircraft specifications to show how manufacturing architecture and commercial incentives can determine whether an industry remains capable of rapid innovation.

[推测] The episode’s broad historical and political claims deserve independent verification. Several arguments about regulation, incumbent motives, energy trends, Boeing, defense readiness, and current geopolitical constraints are presented from Scholl’s perspective rather than tested against competing evidence.

It is especially suitable for founders, hard-tech investors, aerospace and energy professionals, and listeners interested in vertical integration or industrial policy. Those seeking a neutral assessment of Boom’s technical schedule, commercial risks, or turbine market prospects will find less counterargument than founder-driven conviction.
