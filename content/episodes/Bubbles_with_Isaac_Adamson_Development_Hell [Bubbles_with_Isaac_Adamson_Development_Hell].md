# Bubbles: A Chimpanzee’s View of Michael Jackson and Hollywood’s Development Hell

## 概览

This episode examines *Bubbles*, Isaac Adamson’s acclaimed but unproduced screenplay about Michael Jackson, told from the perspective of Jackson’s famous chimpanzee. The script topped the 2015 Black List and was later acquired by Netflix, yet collapsed after years of development.

Adamson presents Bubbles as a dignified, exiled heir who mistakes the “King of Pop” for a literal monarch. His grandiose, pseudo-Shakespearean narration creates comedy while preserving his innocence, pride, and emotional vulnerability. That unreliable viewpoint also allows the story to approach Jackson’s celebrity, cosmetic surgery, family dynamics, and relationships with children from an unfamiliar angle.

The project was well into pre-production when *Leaving Neverland* transformed the cultural climate around Jackson. The screenplay was revised to make Bubbles less ambiguous about what happened at Neverland, but director Taika Waititi eventually withdrew and Netflix shut down production.

The episode’s central argument is that the controversy which made *Bubbles* commercially dangerous also made it more artistically meaningful. Bubbles’ loyalty and willful blindness can be read as a reflection of the public’s own reluctance to confront disturbing allegations against a beloved performer.

## 分段落总结

[00:11] **An Exiled King in a Sanctuary**

[事实] The episode opens with a scene from the screenplay in which the aging Bubbles lives at Florida’s Center for Great Apes and regards himself as king of its retired chimpanzees.

[事实] Bubbles recalls having once been heir to a far greater kingdom ruled by an extraordinary king.

[事实] His observation that every kingdom is also a kind of cage establishes confinement as a central motif.

[02:18] **The Celebrated Screenplay Hollywood Would Not Make**

[事实] The host introduces *Development Hell* as a series about stories Hollywood admires but does not produce.

[事实] *Bubbles* ranked first on the 2015 Black List, an annual survey of film executives’ favorite unproduced screenplays.

[事实] Despite that recognition, the screenplay remained unmade years later.

[事实] The host describes its narrator as a chimpanzee who rose extraordinarily high and then fell.

[04:30] **Bubbles as a Tragic Comic Hero**

[事实] Adamson characterizes Bubbles as a would-be king in exile, separated from the life and inheritance he expected.

[事实] Although the screenplay is fundamentally a comedy, Adamson also regards Bubbles as a tragic figure.

[事实] The host compares the story to *The Remains of the Day* meeting “Puff, the Magic Dragon.”

[推测] The mixture of comedy and tragedy allows Bubbles’ inflated self-image to be funny without making his suffering disposable.

[06:01] **The Real Bubbles and the Origin of the Script**

[事实] Bubbles is a real chimpanzee who lived with Michael Jackson at the height of Jackson’s fame and later resided at a Florida ape sanctuary.

[事实] Jackson treated Bubbles as a companion, brought him on international trips, and took him to a meeting with Osaka’s mayor.

[事实] Adamson first became interested after reading an article asking what had happened to Bubbles, then encountered the premise of a story narrated by a famous chimpanzee through discussion of the book *Me Cheeta*.

[事实] He wanted Bubbles to be the genuine protagonist rather than merely an observer of Jackson’s life.

[08:38] **A Pseudo-Shakespearean Chimpanzee**

[事实] Adamson based Bubbles’ personality on the idea that Jackson was a literal king and that the chimpanzee believed himself to be the king’s prospective successor.

[事实] Because chimpanzees are hierarchical animals, Bubbles interprets human life largely through rank, power, and succession.

[事实] Adamson adopted pseudo-Shakespearean language to give the imagined tragedy of a king and his heir greater elevation.

[事实] The host argues that Bubbles’ formal speech and intense pride give him dignity, raising the emotional stakes whenever he is humiliated.

[推测] The contrast between a chimpanzee’s comic physical appearance and his solemn inner voice is the screenplay’s principal tonal device.

[11:14] **The Unreliable Narrator and Jackson’s Transformation**

[事实] Bubbles misunderstands the title “King of Pop” as proof that Jackson is an actual monarch.

[事实] In a scene about skin cream and cosmetic surgery, Bubbles interprets Jackson’s altered appearance as war paint, battlefield wounds, and miraculous healing.

[事实] The host compares Bubbles to the loyal butler in *The Remains of the Day*, who cannot recognize the moral failings of the master he serves.

[事实] Adamson says Bubbles’ misinterpretations preserve an innocence that a human witness could not retain.

[事实] Bubbles nevertheless recognizes power dynamics, including the damaging authority Jackson’s father Joe continues to exert over him.

[14:53] **Celebrity Cameos and Historical Research**

[事实] The screenplay incorporates figures including Elizabeth Taylor, Quincy Jones, Bon Jovi, La Toya Jackson, Corey Feldman, and Prince.

[事实] Adamson says many of the seemingly extravagant incidents were drawn from reported events, including Bon Jovi spending an evening with Bubbles in Osaka and an encounter involving Prince.

[事实] The story focuses on the period when Jackson was making and touring *Bad*.

[事实] Adamson researched accounts from that era and later heard additional stories about Jackson and Bubbles from people familiar with Neverland.

[17:30] **From Whimsy to a Darker Story**

[事实] Adamson conceived the film as stop-motion animation, avoiding the practical difficulty of placing a real chimpanzee at the center of production.

[事实] The screenplay does not omit the allegations of child sexual abuse against Jackson, which had already received major public attention before *Leaving Neverland* appeared.

[事实] Netflix acquired the project in 2017, after which Adamson entered an extended development process.

[事实] Netflix required detailed annotations identifying sources for events in the screenplay, and some names and characters were changed during legal review.

[19:49] **Bubbles Is Displaced**

[事实] The screenplay’s second act becomes darker as children increasingly occupy Jackson’s attention and Bubbles sees them as rivals for his place in the hierarchy.

[事实] Bubbles fears that the children, rather than he, have become Jackson’s intended successors.

[事实] Adamson compares Bubbles’ rejection with accounts from children who felt favored by Jackson and were later abruptly displaced.

[事实] He cites Corey Feldman as someone who denied sexual misconduct by Jackson toward him but still described being hurt by the loss of their friendship.

[推测] Bubbles’ jealousy provides an emotionally comprehensible route into the darker material before he fully understands its moral significance.

[22:44] **A Chimpanzee’s View as a Distancing Device**

[事实] Adamson says Bubbles’ perspective works as a distancing device, allowing viewers to reconsider familiar events through interpretations that are often mistaken.

[事实] He recalls that Jackson’s popularity in the 1980s crossed musical subcultures and that almost everyone around him admired Jackson.

[事实] Neverland appeared to children as an idyllic world of movies, ice cream, rides, and access to a global superstar.

[事实] Adamson contrasts that fantasy with the darkness and emotional hollowness later associated with Jackson’s relationships.

[25:00] **Victimhood, Loyalty, and the Problem of Character Growth**

[事实] Adamson believes Jackson may appear less sympathetic because the story is narrated from the viewpoint of someone Jackson has harmed and abandoned.

[事实] In the original version, Bubbles could not bring himself to hate Jackson and ended the story still hoping his king would return.

[事实] Adamson says the hardest technical challenge was keeping Bubbles—not Jackson—as the protagonist and giving him personal stakes in historical events.

[事实] The host observes that an unreliable narrator creates pathos by remaining blind while the surrounding world changes.

[27:01] **Rewriting Bubbles After _Leaving Neverland_**

[事实] After *Leaving Neverland*, Adamson and the producers decided Bubbles could no longer remain so ambiguous about events at Neverland.

[事实] Later drafts gave him a clearer understanding, reduced his innocence, and made his separation from Jackson more final.

[事实] Adamson acknowledges that these changes may weaken the unreliable narration that made the original conception distinctive.

[事实] He says the documentary’s first-person accounts made the alleged victims’ pain more immediate than court records or magazine reporting had done.

[30:16] **How the Production Collapsed**

[事实] Adamson’s manager warned him that Jackson had become toxic as a subject and that the whimsical film many participants expected would now be difficult to make.

[事实] Taika Waititi was attached to direct, Mark Gustafson was to oversee the stop-motion work, and Dan Harmon’s Starburns company was producing.

[事实] Netflix had acquired the project at Cannes, department heads had been hired, and more than forty animators were working on it.

[事实] Waititi left to make *Jojo Rabbit* and later withdrew from *Bubbles* while the production needed his directorial involvement.

[事实] Netflix initially allowed two months to find another director but reversed its decision three days later and terminated the project.

[32:43] **Could _Bubbles_ Return?**

[事实] The rights have reverted to Adamson, although he has not seriously pursued a revival.

[事实] Adamson notes that Jackson has re-entered mainstream culture through a new biopic and public tributes—visibility he believes would have been less likely immediately after *Leaving Neverland*.

[事实] The host argues that *Bubbles* is more interesting after the documentary because audiences now recognize Jackson as a deeply problematic figure.

[推测] A revival remains possible, but the project would still need to overcome the commercial and reputational risks attached to its subject.

[33:33] **Why the Controversy Gives the Story Greater Stakes**

[事实] The host argues that before *Leaving Neverland*, the film might have been merely a clever, whimsical addition to Jackson’s celebrity mythology.

[事实] With greater awareness of the allegations, Bubbles’ failure to see his king clearly becomes emotionally painful rather than simply comic.

[事实] Adamson says the allegations themselves were not entirely new, but the cultural response changed after the Me Too movement encouraged people to take such testimony more seriously.

[事实] The host presents Bubbles as a stand-in for an audience that overlooked Jackson’s alleged behavior because of affection for his talent and music.

[推测] Bubbles’ loyalty turns the screenplay into a story about collective self-deception as much as one chimpanzee’s abandonment.

[36:42] **Artistic Value Versus Commercial Fear**

[事实] Adamson says the intense public anxiety surrounding any association with pedophilia made studios and filmmakers reluctant even to discuss projects connected to the subject.

[事实] He understands why a prominent director might not want to spend a publicity campaign answering questions about Jackson and child abuse.

[事实] The host criticizes Hollywood for retreating from its potential role as a cultural authority capable of confronting morally complex subjects.

[事实] Adamson contrasts creative participants who recognize the project’s interest with marketing, publicity, and financial personnel who primarily see another product.

[推测] The production’s failure illustrates how institutional risk management can prevent precisely the difficult cultural examination that film is equipped to provide.

[39:30] **The Limit of Loyalty**

[事实] In the closing excerpt, Bubbles watches Jackson lead children through Neverland and initially describes them as intruders entering the heart of the kingdom.

[事实] He begins to suspect that Jackson’s reckless conduct conceals something darker.

[事实] Bubbles finally concludes that loyalty has reached its limit and that he can no longer serve Jackson.

[推测] This ending completes Bubbles’ movement from devoted heir to morally disillusioned witness, while sacrificing some of the original version’s tragic blindness.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its close examination of narrative perspective. It shows how an apparently absurd premise—a Michael Jackson story narrated by a chimpanzee—can support comedy, tragedy, historical commentary, and a study of loyalty at the same time.

Its account of the failed production also offers a revealing case study in development hell. The project did not collapse because nobody valued the writing or because it lacked major talent; it collapsed after cultural events changed the perceived meaning and commercial risk of the subject.

A limitation is that the discussion centers primarily on the screenplay’s artistic intentions and production history. The allegations against Jackson are discussed through their effect on *Bubbles* and its development, rather than examined independently or comprehensively.

The episode is especially valuable for screenwriters, filmmakers, and listeners interested in unreliable narrators, tonal balance, adaptation under changing cultural conditions, and the conflict between creative ambition and institutional risk.
