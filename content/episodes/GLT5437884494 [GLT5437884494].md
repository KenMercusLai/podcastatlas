# Hitler, Stalin, and the Last Days Before the Invasion of Poland

## 概览

This episode traces the final diplomatic and psychological steps that led from Hitler’s decision to attack Poland to the outbreak of the Second World War in Europe on 1 September 1939.

The central thread is Hitler’s belief that Britain and France would not fight for Poland, especially after Germany secured the Nazi-Soviet Pact. The hosts emphasize that this was a major misreading: Britain and France were shaken by the pact, but they reaffirmed their guarantee to Poland.

The episode also focuses on the extraordinary cynicism of the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact, in which Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union agreed to divide Eastern Europe. Stalin is portrayed as using the deal to buy time after the damage inflicted on the Red Army by the Great Terror, while Hitler sees it as clearing the way for war.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Hitler Announces War on Poland**

[事实] The episode opens with Hitler’s Reichstag speech on 1 September 1939, in which he claimed Polish forces had fired on German territory and said Germany was now returning fire.

[事实] The hosts note that Hitler sounded hesitant and nervous in the speech, even getting the attack time wrong by saying 5:45 instead of 4:45.

[推测] His references to wearing the uniform until victory or death suggest he was already framing the war in fatalistic, self-dramatizing terms.

[03:37] **The Episode’s Setup**

[事实] This is the second episode in a trilogy about the Nazi war on Poland.

[事实] The previous episode ended with Hitler and his generals drawing up the plan of attack on 11 April 1939 after Poland resisted pressure over Danzig and German demands.

[事实] Ribbentrop proposed the apparently unthinkable idea of making a deal with Stalin to divide Poland.

[04:55] **Hitler’s Fiftieth Birthday and Sense of Urgency**

[事实] Hitler turned 50 on 20 April 1939, shortly after the plan for Poland had been prepared.

[事实] Goebbels organized a huge celebration of the Fuhrer cult, including parades, architectural symbolism, and a five-hour military display.

[事实] The hosts argue that most dictators might have stopped after achieving so much, but Hitler felt time was running out and wanted war.

[推测] Hitler’s anxiety about age, health, and death helped intensify his desire to strike soon.

[07:28] **Hitler Turns from Poland to a Wider War**

[事实] Eight days after his birthday, Hitler renounced the non-aggression pact with Poland and tore up the naval treaty with Britain.

[事实] He publicly emphasized grievances over Danzig and the Polish corridor.

[事实] In a later meeting with commanders, he identified Poland as the target but also began describing Britain as an enemy in a life-and-death struggle.

[推测] The hosts present this as the point when Hitler began seriously misreading London and overestimating his ability to intimidate Britain and France.

[11:01] **Diplomatic Preparation and the Weakness of Democracies**

[事实] Ribbentrop’s diplomats made non-aggression pacts with countries including Denmark, Latvia, and Estonia, and secured economic deals with Yugoslavia and Romania.

[事实] The hosts discuss the sense across Europe that democracy looked weak after Munich and that authoritarian regimes seemed modern, decisive, and powerful.

[推测] This perception helped Germany expand its influence among states that doubted France or Britain would protect them.

[12:55] **Mussolini, Propaganda, and Danzig**

[事实] Mussolini did not want war but was drawn into signing the Pact of Steel after Ribbentrop assured him Germany was not planning war for five years.

[事实] Hitler spent the summer appearing relaxed while Goebbels’s propaganda machine amplified claims of Polish mistreatment of Germans.

[事实] Albert Forster, the Nazi leader in Danzig, helped stage provocations and spread stories about alleged Polish abuses.

[推测] The hosts suggest German public opinion was receptive because many Germans already believed Danzig should belong to Germany and held deep prejudices against Poles.

[17:15] **Why Stalin Was Open to a Deal**

[事实] Hitler feared facing Britain and France in the west while also facing Poland and the Soviet Union in the east.

[事实] Stalin had recently carried out the Great Terror, killing nearly a million people and damaging the Red Army’s leadership and industrial expertise.

[事实] Stalin knew Hitler was likely to attack the Soviet Union eventually but believed the Soviet Union was not ready.

[推测] Stalin saw a pact with Germany as a way to buy time and exploit the weakness of British and French diplomacy.

[19:01] **Failed Western Overtures to Stalin**

[事实] Britain and France sent a military mission to Stalin, but it was led by relatively junior figures.

[事实] Stalin regarded the Western approach as unserious and understood that democratic governments had parliaments and electorates to answer to.

[事实] Stalin replaced Maxim Litvinov, who was Jewish and anti-Nazi, with Vyacheslav Molotov, which the Nazis read as a promising signal.

[推测] The personnel change signaled that Stalin was preparing space for a deal with Berlin.

[20:23] **The Breakthrough Toward the Nazi-Soviet Pact**

[事实] On 19 August, the Soviets agreed a trade deal with Germany, exchanging raw materials for German manufactured goods.

[事实] Stalin then invited Ribbentrop to Moscow to discuss a non-aggression pact and a division of Poland.

[事实] Hitler personally asked Stalin to move the meeting earlier because he wanted to attack Poland before autumn rains affected German tanks.

[事实] Stalin agreed to receive Ribbentrop on 23 August, and Hitler reacted with intense excitement.

[24:04] **Public Shock and Hitler’s Berghof Speech**

[事实] News of Ribbentrop’s trip to Moscow was announced before the pact was formally signed.

[事实] Some old Nazis were appalled by cooperation with Bolshevism, while many Germans were relieved that war with Russia seemed avoided.

[事实] On 22 August, Hitler told senior officers at the Berghof that war had to happen now because everything depended on him.

[推测] Hitler’s speech shows both ideological fanaticism and fear that his own lifespan might limit Germany’s opportunity for conquest.

[27:43] **Ribbentrop in Moscow**

[事实] Ribbentrop flew to Moscow on 23 August and was met by Molotov and Stalin in the Kremlin.

[事实] Stalin joked about years of mutual hostility and suggested a ten-year pact after Ribbentrop proposed a hundred-year agreement.

[事实] The secret protocol gave the Soviet Union influence over Finland, Estonia, much of Latvia, eastern Poland, and Bessarabia.

[事实] Stalin requested Latvia’s coast, and Hitler approved the concession after consulting a map.

[31:53] **Signing the Pact**

[事实] The final documents were prepared around midnight, with caviar, vodka, and Crimean sparkling wine served.

[事实] Stalin toasted Hitler’s health.

[事实] Stalin insisted that glasses and food be cleared before photographs were taken, to avoid the appearance that the pact had been signed drunkenly.

[事实] Ribbentrop told Hitler the negotiations had been a complete success.

[33:51] **Hitler, Stalin, and the Pact’s Aftermath**

[事实] Ribbentrop returned to Berlin to a hero’s reception, and Hitler called him a second Bismarck.

[事实] Hitler and Ribbentrop believed the pact guaranteed a free hand against Poland because Britain and France would not fight without the Soviet Union.

[事实] Hitler was fascinated by Stalin and questioned Hoffman about Stalin’s manner, health, handshake, smoking, and even earlobes.

[事实] Hitler had Stalin’s cigarettes removed from official photographs because he thought German public opinion would dislike the image.

[37:19] **Stalin’s Reaction and Global Shock**

[事实] Stalin told his entourage that the pact was a game of who could fool whom and claimed he had tricked Hitler.

[事实] The wider world reacted with shock, including communists and British and French observers.

[事实] Chamberlain was deeply shaken, but Britain did not abandon its guarantee to Poland.

[推测] Stalin was more clear-eyed than Hitler about the pact’s temporary nature, though he would still be surprised by Germany’s later attack.

[38:04] **Britain Reaffirms Poland’s Guarantee**

[事实] On 22 August, the British cabinet reaffirmed its commitment to Poland.

[事实] Ambassador Neville Henderson was sent to Hitler with a letter warning that Britain would not change its position.

[事实] Hitler ranted at Henderson and assumed Chamberlain’s cabinet would collapse, which did not happen.

[推测] Hitler’s reaction shows that he had lost the ability to judge British political resolve accurately.

[41:24] **A Last Offer to Britain and the Cancelled Attack**

[事实] Hitler offered Britain a deal: let Germany solve the Polish issue and he would guarantee the British Empire.

[事实] While presenting himself as an artist who hated war, Hitler’s generals were preparing to invade Poland on 26 August.

[事实] Hitler ordered the attack for 4:30 a.m. on 26 August, then cancelled it at 7:30 p.m. the previous evening.

[事实] The cancellation followed Mussolini’s message that Italy was not ready for war.

[45:03] **The Delay’s Consequences**

[事实] Some German units still acted despite the cancellation, including an attack on a railway tunnel in the Carpathians that the Poles repelled.

[事实] Poland wanted general mobilization, but Britain and France urged restraint to avoid provoking Hitler.

[推测] This delay hurt Poland because it was not fully ready when the invasion actually began.

[45:33] **Backchannels and Hitler’s Final Decision**

[事实] Goering used Swedish industrialist Birger Dahlerus as a go-between with London.

[事实] Dahlerus carried an offer in which Germany would get Danzig, the Polish corridor, and old colonies while promising guarantees to Britain and Poland.

[事实] Hitler continued preparing for invasion and told Nazi leaders the attack would happen on Friday, 1 September, with no more postponements.

[推测] The backchannel diplomacy was less a real alternative to war than a last attempt to split Britain from Poland.

[48:19] **Henderson Confronts Hitler**

[事实] Henderson met Hitler again on 28 August and warned that Britain would fight if Germany invaded Poland.

[事实] On 29 August, Hitler reverted to ranting accusations against Britain and demanded that Poland send someone the next day to concede his demands.

[事实] Henderson lost his temper and said that if Hitler wanted war, he would have it.

[事实] Chamberlain rejected further concessions and refused to be bullied again.

[50:24] **Polish Mobilization and the Final Orders**

[事实] Poland began to mobilize on 30 August, later than it had wanted because of British and French pressure.

[事实] On 31 August, Hitler signed and confirmed the order for the invasion to begin the next day.

[事实] German authorities cut telephone lines to and from the Polish embassy in Berlin.

[推测] By this point, diplomacy had become largely theatrical because the military decision had already been made.

[52:05] **The Gleiwitz False-Flag Operation**

[事实] Reports emerged on the evening of 31 August of incidents on the Polish frontier, including an alleged Polish attack on a German radio station at Gleiwitz.

[事实] The hosts explain that the attack was organized by the SS and planned by Reinhard Heydrich.

[事实] SS men played both attackers and defenders, fired blanks, and left bodies dressed to support the false story.

[事实] One victim was a local Polish farmer and German citizen known for Polish patriotism, who was arrested, drugged, taken to the site, shot, and left there.

[54:02] **The Invasion Begins**

[事实] The war began at 4:45 a.m. on Friday, 1 September, when the Schleswig-Holstein fired on the Polish depot at Westerplatte.

[事实] Around the same time, 60 German divisions and about 1.5 million men crossed into Poland.

[事实] By the afternoon, reports of bombings and Stuka attacks were coming from western Poland, and the Polish army was beginning to fall back.

[事实] Hitler addressed the Reichstag at 10 a.m. wearing his Wehrmacht uniform.

[55:13] **Berlin’s Gloom and Hitler’s Last Message**

[事实] Berlin showed little public enthusiasm for war, according to the American journalist William Shirer.

[事实] Hitler made another attempt through Dahlerus to warn Britain that this was its last chance to avoid conflict.

[事实] Britain and France ordered full mobilization and began evacuating children from cities.

[推测] The lack of public excitement contrasted sharply with Nazi propaganda and showed that ordinary Germans did not greet the war with simple enthusiasm.

[56:36] **Britain Declares War**

[事实] Chamberlain gave a poorly judged speech on 2 September suggesting talks might still be possible if Germany withdrew from Poland.

[事实] His cabinet pushed him to issue an ultimatum.

[事实] On 3 September, Henderson delivered Britain’s ultimatum through interpreter Paul Schmidt because Ribbentrop would not meet him.

[事实] Germany did not reply before the 11 a.m. deadline, and Britain entered the war.

[58:36] **Hitler’s Silence**

[事实] Schmidt recalled translating the ultimatum to Hitler while Ribbentrop stood by the window.

[事实] Hitler sat silently and motionless after hearing it.

[事实] After a long pause, Hitler turned to Ribbentrop and asked what came next.

[推测] The moment underlines the episode’s central argument: Hitler had gambled on Britain backing down and was confronted with the failure of that assumption.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest feature is its tight focus on contingency: the Nazi-Soviet Pact, Mussolini’s hesitation, British cabinet decisions, backchannel diplomacy, and false-flag operations are all shown as parts of a rapidly narrowing path to war.

It is especially valuable for showing Hitler not as an all-controlling mastermind, but as nervous, erratic, vain, and prone to grave misjudgment. The discussion also makes clear that Stalin’s pact with Hitler was cynical and strategic, not an ideological reconciliation.

The tone mixes dark humor with serious historical narrative. That makes the episode accessible, though some listeners may find the comic asides distracting given the subject matter.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who already know the broad outline of 1939 and want a vivid account of the diplomacy, personalities, and final decisions behind the invasion of Poland.
