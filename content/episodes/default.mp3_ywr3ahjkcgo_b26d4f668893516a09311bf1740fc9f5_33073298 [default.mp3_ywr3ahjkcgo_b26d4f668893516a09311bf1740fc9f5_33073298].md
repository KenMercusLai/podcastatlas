# Inside Zydor, the Psychedelic Megachurch

## 概览

This episode investigates how psychedelic churches in the United States operate in a legal gray area, using religious freedom arguments to distribute substances such as psilocybin mushrooms and DMT that remain federally illegal.

The reporting centers on Zydor Church in Oakland, led by Dave Hodges. The church presents mushrooms as sacrament, has more than 135,000 people who have physically come through, and uses a cash-donation model rather than describing transactions as sales.

The episode also follows lawyer John Rapp, who advises psychedelic churches on reducing legal risk. Through his story, the show explains the Controlled Substances Act, religious exemptions, the 2006 Supreme Court ayahuasca case, the DEA exemption process, and the Myers Test for religious legitimacy.

The core tension is whether Zydor is a sincere religious institution, a harm-reduction service, a business-like workaround for drug laws, or some mixture of all three. The episode does not resolve that tension, but shows why Zydor may become a test case for the broader psychedelic church movement.

## 分段落总结

[00:59] **Arrival at Zydor Church**

[事实] The host visits Zydor Church in Oakland, California, describing it as hidden inside a nondescript warehouse surrounded by auto shops and row houses.

[事实] Visitors are greeted by an armed security guard, pass through a metal detector, and encounter ATMs rather than traditional church pews.

[事实] Dave Hodges, the founder and leader of the church, gives the host a tour of a space filled with psychedelic murals and mushroom imagery.

[推测] The physical setting immediately frames the church as both religiously unconventional and commercially or legally guarded.

[02:47] **A Church Built Around Mushrooms**

[事实] Zydor is described as a mushroom church organized around the belief that psilocybin mushrooms provide direct access to the divine.

[事实] The episode says Zydor is one of a growing number of psychedelic churches claiming religious freedom protections for producing, distributing, and consuming controlled substances.

[事实] Instead of communion wafers or wine, members are offered psychoactive substances that the church calls sacrament.

[推测] Zydor’s model depends on treating drug access not as recreation or commerce, but as religious practice.

[03:35] **Membership and the Sacrament Room**

[事实] Joining Zydor requires an application pledging sincere religiosity and answering questions such as who referred the applicant and whether they work with law enforcement.

[事实] Dave says the church asks people connected to law enforcement to sign a document threatening a $100,000 lawsuit if they are undercover or working with police.

[事实] In the sacrament room, members make cash donations and receive sacrament from providers behind a counter.

[事实] Dave emphasizes that the church does not call these exchanges sales.

[04:50] **What Zydor Provides**

[事实] The sacrament room contains cannabis products, multiple strains and potencies of psychedelic mushrooms, mushroom chocolates, taffy, pressed candies, DMT, and DMT vape pens.

[事实] Dave says a DMT item might require a donation of about $100, depending on the product.

[事实] On busy weekends, Dave says the church can see more than 300 members a day in the sacrament room.

[推测] The variety and volume of substances make Zydor resemble a large retail operation, even though it describes itself in religious terms.

[06:10] **Scale and Episode Question**

[事实] Dave says Zydor has a little over 135,000 members who have physically come through the church to get sacrament.

[事实] The host frames the episode around the broader rise of psychedelic churches operating in legal gray areas.

[事实] The show promises to examine legal risk, religious definitions, police raids, and how the government decides what counts as religion.

[推测] Zydor’s size makes it unusually important because its success could influence how authorities view similar churches.

[08:10] **John Rapp’s Legal Role**

[事实] The host calls John Rapp, a lawyer who says he helps create, defend, advise, counsel, and warn psychedelic churches.

[事实] Rapp previously worked for large companies including Exxon, Microsoft, and Lockheed.

[事实] He says people in the plant-medicine movement once suspected he was a narc, while other lawyers now call him a hippie lawyer.

[推测] Rapp’s background gives him credibility with conventional legal systems while placing him in an unconventional legal niche.

[09:09] **Rapp’s Path to Psychedelic Law**

[事实] Rapp was raised conservative Christian during the war-on-drugs era and says he once feared substances such as cocaine and LSD as if they were essentially the same.

[事实] His son struggled with opioid addiction for more than a decade after a surgery-related prescription and died in 2020.

[事实] Rapp later tried ayahuasca after remembering that his son had found relief in ayahuasca ceremonies.

[事实] Rapp says the experience helped him process grief and changed his view of psychedelic substances.

[推测] His legal work is presented as partly personal, tied to grief, harm reduction, and honoring his son.

[12:33] **Religious Exemptions and the Supreme Court**

[事实] The episode explains that the Native American Church began receiving exemptions for ceremonial peyote use as early as the 1960s.

[事实] A Brazilian ayahuasca church branch in Santa Fe sued the government after customs confiscated imported ayahuasca tea.

[事实] In 2006, the Supreme Court agreed that the government had not demonstrated sufficient harm or diversion risk in that case.

[事实] Rapp says the case helped show that psychedelic churches beyond the Native American Church could assert religious rights and win in court.

[14:11] **Risk, DEA Exemptions, and Legal Exposure**

[事实] Rapp says there is no full legal protection unless the government has specifically granted an exemption.

[事实] Psychedelic churches can still attract law enforcement attention and be taken to court under the Controlled Substances Act.

[事实] The DEA created a formal process for religious exemptions, but the episode says it can be slow and requires applicants to pause illegal substance use during review.

[事实] Rapp says applying can create a catch-22 because denied applicants may have given the government detailed evidence about potentially illegal conduct.

[推测] For many churches, avoiding or delaying formal exemption requests may feel safer than openly documenting their practices.

[16:08] **The Myers Test and Making Religion Legible**

[事实] Rapp uses clues from legal precedent, including the Myers Test, to help churches understand what courts may consider evidence of sincere religion.

[事实] Criteria include a founder or spiritual leader, regular services, ceremonies, rituals, developed theology, and sacred texts.

[事实] Rapp says he helps people write Bibles and translate visions into canons, bylaws, codes of ethics, religious clothing, and holidays.

[事实] One holiday he mentions is Bicycle Day on April 19, tied to Albert Hofmann’s LSD experience.

[推测] Rapp’s work turns private visions and loose communities into institutions that courts and agencies can recognize.

[18:43] **Security Rules and Zydor’s Economic Outlier Status**

[事实] Rapp says churches with exemptions must follow DEA protocols around handling and securing psychedelics, such as lock boxes and video surveillance.

[事实] He emphasizes that legal work reduces risk rather than eliminating it.

[事实] Rapp estimates there are more than 300 psychedelic religious organizations operating in the United States.

[事实] Rapp guesses that 70 or 80 percent of all U.S. psychedelic church revenue goes to Zydor.

[推测] Even within a fast-growing movement, Zydor appears financially and operationally unusual.

[21:30] **Dave Hodges’ Origin Story**

[事实] The host says he was skeptical about how Zydor could balance being a house of worship and a business.

[事实] Dave says he did not initially intend to run the largest psychedelic church in the world.

[事实] Zydor, or the broader Church of Ambrosia organization, began as a marijuana church in 2019.

[事实] Dave says a mushroom vision in July 2019 convinced him that he needed to protect the psychedelic movement.

[推测] Dave’s story functions as Zydor’s founding revelation, giving the church a religious origin narrative.

[22:58] **High-Dose Practice and Early Growth**

[事实] Dave recentered the church around psychedelic mushrooms and especially around taking doses much higher than typical recreational use.

[事实] The episode describes those doses as about ten times more than recreational doses.

[事实] Early growth was relatively slow and included people who heard about the church on Reddit.

[推测] Zydor’s high-dose emphasis likely strengthens Dave’s claim that the practice is spiritual rather than casual.

[23:23] **The Oakland Police Raid**

[事实] In August 2020, Oakland police raided the church building while Dave was checking on a marijuana grow house.

[事实] According to Dave, police swept the sacrament room, cut into a safe, and confiscated about $200,000 worth of mushrooms and marijuana plus several thousand dollars in cash.

[事实] Dave says he had prepared for this kind of confrontation and resumed serving sacrament the next day.

[事实] Dave says the raid turned Zydor into a national news story and helped accelerate its growth.

[推测] The raid became a publicity event that strengthened Zydor rather than shutting it down.

[24:39] **Low Barrier to Entry and Revenue Questions**

[事实] The episode says Zydor has a lower barrier to entry than other psychedelic churches, requiring a form agreement, a statement of belief, confirmation that the person is not a cop, a $10 initiation fee, and $5 per month.

[事实] Dave estimates about 4,000 members come into the church each month to get sacrament.

[事实] The host calculates that monthly membership fees alone would be at least $20,000, and that $60 per visit could imply several million dollars in annual revenue.

[事实] Dave rejects the idea that this is an easy wealth-building operation, citing the risk of police raids and asset seizure.

[推测] Zydor’s scale creates the strongest skepticism about whether it is primarily religious, commercial, or both.

[26:07] **Costs, Cash, and Community Spending**

[事实] Dave says Zydor has costs including rent, a separate house for high-dosage ceremonies, payroll, sacrament supply, potency testing, quality control, and security.

[事实] He says security is expensive and necessary because the church is in Oakland.

[事实] Because the substances are federally outlawed, the church cannot use the normal banking system and operates in cash.

[事实] Dave says the church supports community efforts such as toy drives and helps other psychedelic churches prepare for legal conflicts.

[推测] The cash-only model increases both robbery risk and law-enforcement seizure risk.

[27:55] **Patchwork Legality and Ongoing Danger**

[事实] The episode says cities such as Oakland and Seattle have decriminalized mushrooms, making mushroom offenses a low priority for local law enforcement.

[事实] Mushrooms and other psychedelics remain illegal under many state laws and under federal law.

[事实] After Zydor’s raid, no charges were filed, but Dave says the church did not recover the seized property.

[事实] The episode mentions other legal cases, including a Florida ayahuasca church found negligent after a participant died following a ceremony.

[推测] Local decriminalization reduces some practical risk but does not settle the church’s legal status.

[28:55] **Faith, Recreation, and Dave’s Theology**

[事实] The host asks how Dave can know whether Zydor’s many members are acting from faith rather than recreational interest.

[事实] Dave says that, from his perspective, people are having a religious experience whether they know it or not.

[事实] Dave says mushrooms affect the border between this world and the next and bring people closer to the soul.

[事实] He says people eventually understand and “convert themselves.”

[推测] Dave’s theology makes intention less important than the experience produced by the mushrooms.

[30:07] **Internal Criticism and Harm Reduction**

[事实] The episode says other psychedelic churches can be apprehensive about Zydor’s scale, permissive membership, and focus on high dosages.

[事实] One pastor tells the host he is grateful Zydor offers an entry point to psychedelic spirituality but worries it could confuse the public and law enforcement.

[事实] Rapp says he believes Zydor is a true church and also an exercise in harm reduction.

[事实] Rapp contrasts access to high-dose mushrooms with substances such as alcohol, cocaine, and opioids.

[推测] Rapp’s defense reframes Zydor less as a loophole and more as a safer alternative to more harmful drug markets.

[31:19] **The Movement’s Future**

[事实] Rapp hopes the federal government will shift its treatment of psychedelics and that psilocybin will move through FDA clinical trials.

[事实] The episode says there have been recent court wins for psychedelic churches.

[事实] A church in Washington state recently became the first psychedelic church to receive a DEA exemption without going to court.

[事实] Dave says critics accuse Zydor of not being a religion or of being a way to get wealthy, while supporters report changed lives and healing from trauma.

[推测] The episode suggests that figures like Dave and Rapp are testing boundaries before psychedelic religion becomes settled law.

[32:37] **Zydor’s Unfinished Scripture**

[事实] As the host leaves, Dave gives him a copy of the church’s Bible.

[事实] The cover shows a gorilla in space holding a psychedelic mushroom.

[事实] Dave says the Bible is not finished and is a living document.

[事实] The back cover reads “Draft V 2.0.”

[推测] The unfinished Bible underscores how new and still-forming Zydor’s religious framework is.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it treats psychedelic churches as a serious legal, economic, and religious phenomenon rather than only as a novelty. The strongest reporting comes from combining an on-site visit, Dave Hodges’ own explanation, and John Rapp’s legal framework.

[推测] Its biggest strength is the unresolved tension: Zydor can look like a church, a market, a political protest, and a harm-reduction project at the same time. The episode lets those categories collide instead of forcing a simple answer.

[事实] The episode acknowledges limits in what it can verify, especially around Dave’s finances, saying that without a forensic dive the host cannot know for sure how much wealth the church creates for him.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in drug policy, religious freedom, legal gray markets, psychedelics, and the economics of institutions that operate between activism, commerce, and faith.
