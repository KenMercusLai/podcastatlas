# Christmas Day: Charlemagne’s Coronation and the End of the Soviet Union

## 概览

This episode opens the hosts’ “12 Days of Christmas” series, in which each installment discusses events that happened on one of the Christmas-season dates. For Christmas Day, Tom chooses the coronation of Charlemagne in Rome in AD 800, while Dominic chooses the resignation of Mikhail Gorbachev and the effective end of the Soviet Union on 25 December 1991.

The first half explains how the Pope, the Franks, Byzantium, and Charlemagne’s expanding power converged to revive the idea of a western Roman emperor. The second half traces how Gorbachev’s reforms, the failed August coup, Yeltsin’s rise, Ukraine’s independence vote, and the republics’ breakaway left the Soviet presidency hollow.

The episode’s central contrast is the “birth” of one empire and the “death” of another. Both stories are presented as turning points whose consequences lasted far beyond the day itself: Charlemagne’s coronation leads toward the Holy Roman Empire, while the Soviet collapse reshapes Russia, Western expectations, Putin’s worldview, and Chinese Communist thinking.

## 分段落总结

[00:15] **Introducing the Christmas Day Series**

[事实] The hosts greet listeners on Christmas Day and introduce the first episode in a planned series themed around the 12 Days of Christmas.
[事实] Tom explains that instead of regular episodes, they will discuss historical events linked to each Christmas-season date.
[事实] Dominic chooses the end of the Soviet Union on 25 December 1991, while Tom chooses Charlemagne’s coronation in Rome on Christmas Day AD 800.

[03:39] **Charlemagne and the Western Christian World**

[事实] Charlemagne is introduced as king of the Franks, whose kingdom had become the strongest power in the former western Roman Empire.
[事实] The other major powers in Christian Europe are described as the emperor in Constantinople and the Bishop of Rome, the Pope.
[事实] Rome’s status is tied to Peter and Paul, its place as the old imperial capital, and its role among the five patriarchates.
[事实] The Pope’s position is described as exposed because Byzantine control in Italy is weakening while the Lombards press on Rome.

[06:04] **The Papacy Turns to the Franks**

[事实] By the eighth century, the Pope begins looking for another protector, and the obvious candidate is the king of the Franks.
[事实] The Carolingians also need legitimacy after replacing the Merovingian dynasty.
[事实] The Pope crowns Pepin, son of Charles Martel, creating a mutually beneficial alliance between the papacy and the Carolingians.
[事实] Charlemagne, Pepin’s son, is described as having conquered the Saxons, pushed east, and defeated Saracens in Spain.

[07:51] **Leo III’s Crisis and Appeal to Charlemagne**

[事实] Pope Hadrian I dies in 795 and is succeeded by Leo III, whose less aristocratic background angers Hadrian’s relatives.
[事实] In 799, Leo is ambushed, blinded, and has his tongue cut out, though the hosts discuss the later claim that he miraculously recovered.
[事实] Leo escapes and travels north to meet Charlemagne at Paderborn, where he stays for three months.
[推测] The meeting suggests that both Leo and Charlemagne were exploring a major political settlement before the events in Rome.

[09:53] **Charlemagne Comes to Rome**

[事实] On 23 November 800, Charlemagne approaches Rome with a large army and retinue, and Leo goes out to meet him.
[事实] Charlemagne convenes a synod, clears Leo of charges, and affirms that a pope cannot be judged.
[事实] At the same time, events in Constantinople affect the legitimacy of the eastern imperial throne.

[10:38] **Irene, Constantinople, and Imperial Legitimacy**

[事实] Irene, a major Byzantine empress from an aristocratic Athenian background, blinds her son Constantine VI and becomes empress.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that a woman ruling as emperor in Constantinople is used by contemporary western writers as one reason to justify Charlemagne’s coronation.
[事实] On 23 December, Charlemagne’s servants arrive from Jerusalem with the keys to the Holy Sepulchre and a holy banner.
[推测] The timing of the Jerusalem delegation is treated skeptically by the hosts and may have been staged to strengthen Charlemagne’s sacred authority.

[12:08] **Charlemagne Crowned Emperor**

[事实] On 25 December AD 800, Charlemagne is crowned emperor in Rome by the Pope.
[事实] The coronation is framed as a revival of the western imperial line that had ended with Romulus Augustulus in the fifth century.
[事实] Einhard later claims Charlemagne would not have entered the church had he known the Pope’s plan beforehand.
[推测] Tom argues that Charlemagne almost certainly knew what was going to happen.

[13:56] **Why the Coronation Was Justified**

[事实] A contemporary chronicler gives three reasons for Charlemagne’s imperial title: Irene’s rule in Constantinople, Rome’s status as the seat of the Caesars, and Charlemagne’s rule over many provinces.
[事实] Dominic accepts the territorial argument as the strongest because Charlemagne was effectively ruling an empire.
[事实] The hosts describe the coronation as the dawn of the Holy Roman Empire, though they note there is later fragmentation and a break before Otto the Great.
[事实] Napoleon’s coronation is described as modeled on Charlemagne’s, though Napoleon crowns himself.

[16:26] **Turning to the Soviet Union**

[事实] The second half moves to 25 December 1991, when Mikhail Gorbachev is preparing to resign as president of the Soviet Union.
[事实] Dominic notes that the date was not Christmas Day in Russia, because Orthodox Christmas falls later.
[事实] The Soviet Union is described as already effectively dead, with only hours left.
[事实] Putin’s later description of the Soviet collapse as a major geopolitical tragedy is mentioned.

[17:52] **Gorbachev’s Weakening Position**

[事实] At the start of 1991, the Cold War is over and Eastern Europe is no longer communist, but many observers do not expect the Soviet Union itself to disappear.
[事实] The hosts explain that the Soviet Union includes Russia and many other republics, including the Baltic states, Georgia, Central Asia, and the Caucasus.
[事实] Gorbachev is admired in the West but deeply unpopular in Russia because economic conditions are worsening.
[事实] His simultaneous political and economic reforms alienate both ordinary Russians and parts of the elite.

[20:11] **The Failed August Coup and Yeltsin’s Rise**

[事实] Hardliners oppose Gorbachev’s proposed new union treaty and launch a coup while he is on holiday in Crimea.
[事实] They imprison Gorbachev in his dacha and seize power in Moscow.
[事实] The coup plotters fail to arrest Boris Yeltsin, who becomes the face of resistance by rallying people from a tank outside the Russian parliament.
[事实] The coup collapses within days, and Gorbachev returns with his authority badly weakened.

[22:21] **The Republics Break Away**

[事实] After the coup fails, Yeltsin outlaws the Communist Party in Russia.
[事实] On 1 December, Ukraine holds a referendum and votes to leave the Soviet Union.
[事实] On 8 December, Yeltsin and the leaders of Ukraine and Belarus meet behind Gorbachev’s back and agree to create independent states within a Commonwealth of Independent States.
[事实] More republics rush to declare independence, partly because local party leaders see nationalism as a way to retain power.

[24:38] **Gorbachev’s Resignation Is Arranged**

[事实] Gorbachev is left in the Kremlin leading a state that is collapsing around him.
[事实] On 23 December, he meets Yeltsin and agrees that two days later he will hand over the nuclear briefcase and give up power.
[事实] The resignation is set for 25 December 1991 and will be broadcast internationally.
[事实] Dominic stresses the extraordinary nature of a successor to Lenin, Stalin, Khrushchev, and Brezhnev resigning on live television as the state collapses.

[25:37] **The Symbolism of the Broadcast**

[事实] Gorbachev refuses a request to sign his resignation live on camera.
[事实] Russian television equipment is described as antiquated, while CNN has the facilities needed for the global satellite broadcast.
[事实] When Gorbachev’s pen does not work, he signs the resignation with a Montblanc pen borrowed from CNN president Tom Johnson.
[推测] The hosts present the borrowed capitalist pen as a powerful symbol of the Soviet system’s collapse.

[27:10] **The Red Flag Comes Down**

[事实] Gorbachev gives a speech defending his reforms and arguing that change had been necessary.
[事实] He remains highly unpopular in Russia, where many blame him for destroying the country and causing economic disaster.
[事实] At 7:32, the Soviet red flag is lowered over the Kremlin and replaced with Russia’s white, blue, and red tricolor.
[事实] Gorbachev and some aides drink cognac afterward, while accounts differ over how Yeltsin reacted.

[29:26] **Aftermath for Russia, the West, and China**

[事实] George H. W. Bush interrupts Christmas Day in America to tell the public that the Soviet Union is over.
[事实] Dominic argues that Russia’s post-Soviet experience becomes disastrous for many ordinary Russians, whose savings, pensions, and living standards collapse.
[事实] The hosts say Chinese leaders view Gorbachev’s fate as a warning against combining political reform with economic reform.
[事实] They also connect the 1990s Russian experience to why many Russians later see Putin as preferable to the alternative.

[31:45] **Birth and Death of Empires**

[事实] The hosts frame the episode as a symmetry between the birth of an empire through Charlemagne and the death of an empire through the Soviet collapse.
[事实] They say later Christmas-season episodes will be less heavyweight and mention future topics including William McGonagall, the Battle of Wakefield, Queen Emma of Hawaii, and J. R. R. Tolkien.
[推测] The contrast gives the episode a strong thematic arc despite the hosts’ comic tone.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is its pairing of two major Christmas Day events that are rarely considered together. The Charlemagne segment explains how papal vulnerability, Frankish power, Byzantine weakness, and sacred symbolism combined to create a new western imperial order.

The Soviet segment is especially strong on the speed and uncertainty of collapse. It emphasizes that the end of the USSR was not simply a Western triumph, but also a traumatic event for many people inside Russia and a warning lesson for later authoritarian leaders.

The tone is conversational and humorous, with jokes about Christmas, mutilation, and political symbolism, but the historical claims are organized around clear turning points. [推测] Listeners who enjoy broad historical comparison and political aftermath will get more from this episode than those looking for a narrow, deeply sourced specialist account.
