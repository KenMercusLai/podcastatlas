# The Laws of the Office

## 概览

This episode of Planet Money revisits a 2018 story about “laws of the office”: informal principles that explain why incentives, deadlines, promotions, and social norms can go wrong at work. Kenny Malone and Sarah Gonzalez frame the rerun around a new Planet Money book and a limited office-safety-style poster based on the episode.

The core through-line is that workplace behavior often changes when institutions measure, reward, or socially signal something. Goodhart’s Law shows how metrics can be gamed; Parkinson’s Law shows how work expands to fit deadlines; the Peter Principle shows how promotion can push people into roles they are bad at.

The final section moves from joke-origin “laws” to a documented social-change dynamic: people are more likely to change when they see others changing first. The episode closes by testing that idea with an unscientific but memorable office dishwashing trophy.

## 分段落总结

[00:24] **Framing the “Laws of the Office”**

[事实] Kenny and Sarah introduce an older episode in which Kenny confessed to breaking a rule while working as a cashier.

[事实] They describe the episode as being about incentives going rogue and say the office laws have some kind of economic backing.

[事实] They connect the rerun to a Planet Money book and a limited-edition poster for people who pre-order it.

[03:08] **Kenny’s Cashier Story and Goodhart’s Law**

[事实] Kenny says that as a teenage grocery cashier, managers measured “items scanned per minute” and posted cashier performance.

[事实] When an item would not scan, he let it pass unscanned so his speed metric would not fall.

[事实] The story is used to explain Goodhart’s Law: once an organization measures something, employees may find ways to make the number look good in ways leaders did not intend.

[推测] The example suggests that badly chosen metrics can reward behavior that undermines the organization’s real goal.

[07:28] **Charles Goodhart and the Original Law**

[事实] Charles Goodhart is introduced as an economist, former adviser to the Bank of England, and professor emeritus at the London School of Economics.

[事实] His original point came from a monetary policy paper and said that a statistical regularity tends to collapse once pressure is placed on it for control purposes.

[事实] The episode gives a hospital example: when British hospitals were pressured to see emergency patients within four hours, some patients were kept waiting in ambulances until they could be counted within the target window.

[事实] Goodhart says he has mixed feelings because he is best known for what began as a joking side comment rather than his broader academic work.

[10:44] **Parkinson’s Law and Deadline Pressure**

[事实] Parkinson’s Law is introduced as the idea that work expands to fill the time allotted for it.

[事实] Kenny and Sarah demonstrate the idea by giving Alexi Horowitz-Ghazi one day to report and produce the segment instead of a week.

[事实] The law originated in a humorous 1955 Economist essay by C. Northcote Parkinson about bureaucracies growing regardless of how much work they actually have.

[事实] Meng Zhu says later researchers tested Parkinson’s Law in labs and field contexts, and found that longer deadlines can lead people to expand the work.

[15:28] **How to Fight Parkinson’s Law**

[事实] Meng Zhu says possible responses include shortening deadlines and offering rewards for fast task completion.

[事实] Alexi completes the segment in one day, and the hosts say it was as good as if they had spent a week on it.

[事实] The interview itself is cut off when the allotted studio time ends, which the hosts treat as an example of the same law.

[推测] The episode frames constraint as useful when it forces focus and prevents unnecessary expansion of work.

[16:52] **Alexi Reflects Eight Years Later**

[事实] In the 2026 framing, Kenny notes that Alexi Horowitz-Ghazi is now a Planet Money co-host.

[事实] Alexi says the one-day assignment was stressful but taught him to get out, talk to people, and make the story make sense.

[事实] Alexi also discusses the Planet Money book, saying it applies an economics lens and playful sensibility to choices ranging from breakfast to life partners to free time.

[18:44] **The Peter Principle**

[事实] The Peter Principle is described as the idea that in a hierarchy, employees tend to rise to their level of incompetence.

[事实] The episode explains that someone who performs well may be promoted repeatedly until they reach a job they are not good at.

[事实] The principle is traced to a bestselling satirical book by Lawrence J. Peter.

[推测] The segment suggests that promotion systems often assume excellence in one role will transfer to a different role, even when the skills do not match.

[19:32] **Stephanie Byrne’s Self-Demotion**

[事实] Stephanie Byrne loved her job as a social media specialist for a large university because it involved creativity, independence, and behind-the-scenes work.

[事实] After promotion to a web content role, she had more meetings, had to tell others how to do their jobs, and had to lead trainings despite being introverted.

[事实] Stephanie decided to step down into a role more like the one she had loved and felt good at.

[事实] The hosts present self-demotion as one way to beat the Peter Principle.

[24:22] **A Nameless Law of Social Change**

[事实] Alice Evans of King’s College London introduces a documented but unnamed idea: social change accelerates when people see that others are changing.

[事实] She explains that people may want to change but often want to see others do it first.

[事实] The episode describes this as a snowball process, with the hard part being how to get the snowball moving.

[25:25] **Changing Norms by Showing Real Behavior**

[事实] In Uganda, Innovations for Poverty Action ran a video campaign showing people reporting gender-based violence and being supported by their community.

[事实] The episode says that within six months, reporting increased rapidly and gender-based violence fell.

[事实] For college binge drinking, researchers used signs showing that classmates did not drink as much as students thought, and that approach seemed to work.

[推测] These examples point to social proof as a tool for changing behavior when the message is truthful.

[26:33] **Why Fake Norm Campaigns Are Risky**

[事实] Kenny asks whether the office could use posters implying everyone else washes dishes often.

[事实] Alice says the approach only works if it is truthful and warns that fake posters could undermine trust in the office.

[事实] She instead points to rural Zambia, where health care workers responded to a trophy that recognized their effort without money attached.

[推测] The advice shifts the dishwashing problem from false social pressure toward visible appreciation.

[30:26] **The Dishwashing Trophy Experiment**

[事实] Former Planet Money intern Shane McKee helped order a five-foot trophy with a gold-painted mug on top.

[事实] The trophy appeared when the dishes were clean and disappeared when they were not.

[事实] Office reactions included people recognizing the experiment, feeling tricked by the trophy, and washing dishes to bring it back.

[事实] Kenny says the experiment was unscientific but seemed to result in fewer unwashed dishes.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it makes workplace economics concrete through funny, easy-to-remember stories. The cashier metric, the deadline experiment, the unhappy promotion, and the dishwashing trophy all translate abstract management ideas into recognizable office behavior.

[推测] Its strongest feature is structure: each “law” is paired with a real or semi-real test case, so the audience can see the mechanism rather than just hear a definition. The tone is playful, but the examples still carry practical lessons about metrics, deadlines, promotions, and social norms.

[推测] The main limitation is that some examples are deliberately informal, especially the office trophy experiment. The episode is best for listeners who want accessible behavioral economics and workplace insight, not a rigorous academic review of each principle.
