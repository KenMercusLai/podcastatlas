# AfD’s Chance in Saxony-Anhalt, Palantir in Britain, and China’s Chocolate Push

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* examines three separate stories: the AfD’s real chance of winning power in Saxony-Anhalt, the controversy around Palantir’s growing role in British public services, and the emergence of serious Chinese chocolate brands.

The first segment focuses on Ulrich Siegmund and the AfD’s campaign in Saxony-Anhalt, where the party could potentially win a majority and form a government without coalition partners. The discussion stresses both the radical nature of the party’s programme and the practical limits it would face in office.

The second segment weighs Palantir’s usefulness against its political and reputational baggage. The episode argues that although concerns about data, sovereignty and vendor dependence are real, Britain’s public services also have urgent problems that Palantir’s technology appears able to address.

The final segment turns to China’s small but changing chocolate market. It explains why chocolate has historically had limited appeal in China, then explores how local firms such as Choc Revive are trying to build a more Chinese version of premium chocolate.

## 分段落总结

[00:19] **Opening Line-Up**

[事实] The hosts introduce the episode as covering Palantir and whether Chinese consumers are starting to embrace chocolate.

[事实] The first report begins with Ulrich Siegmund campaigning in Saxony-Anhalt ahead of a state election on Sunday, September 6.

[00:59] **Ulrich Siegmund and the AfD Rally**

[事实] Ulrich Siegmund is described as the AfD’s lead candidate in Saxony-Anhalt.

[事实] Tom Nuttall attended one of Siegmund’s rallies in Zerbst and described it as closer to a celebrity meet-and-greet than a typical political rally.

[事实] *Der Spiegel* had put Siegmund on its cover and called him “the most dangerous man in Germany,” which he used enthusiastically at the event.

[推测] The rally atmosphere suggests that hostile media attention may strengthen Siegmund’s appeal among supporters rather than damage him.

[02:13] **AfD’s Route to Power**

[事实] If the AfD won power in Saxony-Anhalt, Siegmund could become the first right-wing nationalist leader of a German state since the Second World War.

[事实] The AfD has long been excluded from power by Germany’s “Brandmauer,” under which mainstream parties refuse coalitions with it.

[事实] This election is different because the AfD may be able to win a majority of seats on its own.

[03:24] **What the AfD Stands For**

[事实] The AfD is described as sitting on the more extreme end of Europe’s populist nationalist right.

[事实] It is anti-immigration, wants an end to asylum rights, promotes “remigration,” is pro-Russian, and wants Germany to stop supporting Ukraine.

[事实] The party portrays other parties as “cartel parties” and campaigns strongly against elites.

[推测] The AfD’s platform combines cultural nationalism, foreign-policy revisionism and anti-establishment politics in a way that makes cooperation with other right-wing parties difficult.

[04:29] **State-Level Agenda**

[事实] Saxony-Anhalt is a state election, so many of the AfD’s best-known national positions would not be directly within the state government’s powers.

[事实] The AfD has produced a 258-page manifesto that party officials see as a blueprint for wider transformation.

[事实] Its proposals include segregating classes, removing refugee and disabled children from mainstream schools, restoring school exchanges with Russia, expanding Russian-language teaching and rewriting curricula.

[事实] The manifesto would focus more on 19th-century German achievements and less on what the party regards as excessive attention to Nazi crimes.

[05:40] **Limits on Implementation**

[事实] Saxony-Anhalt owes about €25 billion and depends heavily on transfers from the rest of Germany.

[事实] The AfD has made costly promises while also pledging not to take on new debt.

[事实] Independent analyses say the party’s fiscal plans do not add up.

[事实] The AfD has almost no governing experience beyond a few rural municipalities.

[推测] Even if the AfD wins, money shortages and administrative inexperience could slow or limit its programme.

[06:59] **Institutional Pushback**

[事实] Organisations and individuals who might be affected by an AfD government have been planning for months.

[事实] Saxony-Anhalt’s parliament changed rules on electing judges and the state parliament to limit the AfD’s ability to disrupt institutions.

[事实] Interior ministers from Germany’s other 15 states have considered excluding a Saxony-Anhalt AfD interior minister from sensitive security discussions, especially because of concerns about Russia.

[推测] German institutions are preparing containment strategies before the election result is known.

[07:59] **Voters’ Reactions and Possible Fallout**

[事实] Some voters are excited by the prospect of an AfD government, while others are horrified.

[事实] Tom Nuttall says widespread protests would probably follow an AfD victory, not only in Saxony-Anhalt but across Germany.

[事实] Some people in the state say they or people they know would consider leaving if the AfD takes power.

[事实] Some outside or adjacent to the debate argue that letting the AfD govern could expose its incompetence, but people in Saxony-Anhalt object to becoming a political testing ground.

[推测] A victory could create both symbolic national shock and concrete local costs, especially if skilled workers leave.

[10:08] **Palantir’s Expansion in Britain**

[事实] Palantir began with a strong reputation among defence and intelligence customers in the early 2000s.

[事实] The company is controversial and is associated with phrases such as “techno-militarism” and “surveillance state.”

[事实] Britain is Palantir’s biggest foreign market and accounts for a sixth of its workforce.

[事实] Palantir has won British government contracts with the NHS, armed forces, police and others.

[11:20] **What Palantir Offers**

[事实] Palantir’s software helps join up fragmented data and turn it into usable flows for non-technical experts.

[事实] Public services often struggle with disjointed systems, including police administrative burdens and nurses relying on paper because electronic patient records do not work properly.

[事实] Palantir has been used by armed forces to help commanders locate planes relative to targets.

[事实] Police used Palantir to sort through 100,000 messages in a case involving a Romanian gang.

[12:34] **The NHS Contract**

[事实] In 2023, Palantir won a contract to build a federated data platform for the NHS.

[事实] The platform links data across parts of the NHS and can support back-office functions and tools.

[事实] One cancer tool can track patients through treatment, flag missed timelines and let staff update records.

[推测] The NHS use case is controversial partly because health data is especially sensitive.

[13:11] **Why Palantir Draws Criticism**

[事实] Palantir is described as a lightning rod because of its outsider reputation, leadership and political associations.

[事实] Peter Thiel is mentioned as being known for lecturing on the Antichrist.

[事实] Palantir technology has been used by ICE to track and deport migrants in America and by Israel to target Gazans.

[事实] In Britain, the company is also known for aggressive lobbying.

[14:03] **Questions Beyond Politics**

[事实] One concern is that Palantir has used unreliable statistics about the NHS performing 110,000 extra operations because of its software.

[事实] Another concern is vendor lock-in, where a government becomes too dependent on one supplier.

[事实] Digital sovereignty is also a concern as Britain and other countries reconsider dependence on American technology.

[推测] The strongest objections are not only about Palantir’s politics but also about procurement discipline, evidence quality and strategic dependence.

[15:25] **The Lack of British Alternatives**

[事实] Critics argue Britain should use homegrown alternatives to Palantir.

[事实] Georgia Banjo says the difficulty is that Britain does not currently have equivalent domestic capability.

[事实] A select committee urged the government to use a break clause in the NHS contract from next February.

[事实] London mayor Sadiq Khan blocked a Palantir deal with the Metropolitan Police, citing the absence of an open competition process.

[16:52] **A Difficult Trade-Off**

[事实] Metropolitan Police commissioner Sir Mark Rowley said blocking the Palantir contract would cost productivity and require cuts to hundreds of frontline staff.

[事实] Georgia Banjo notes that the NHS already depends heavily on other American technology firms such as Oracle and Microsoft.

[事实] She argues that if Britain worries about sovereignty, there are bigger dependencies than Palantir, including defence and the nuclear deterrent.

[事实] Her conclusion is that Palantir has exaggerated some claims, but the problem it addresses is real and the technology seems useful.

[推测] The episode’s position is that concerns about Palantir should be handled seriously but not allowed to obscure practical public-service needs.

[18:31] **Chinese Chocolate Emerges**

[事实] Dawn Wineland visits a Choc Revive shop in Shanghai and samples Chinese-made chocolate in flavours including strawberry, passion fruit and hazelnut.

[事实] She says China is not known for chocolate or chocolatiers, but that this is changing.

[事实] A new generation of Chinese chocolatiers is starting to appear.

[19:37] **Why Chocolate Has Been Niche in China**

[事实] Chocolate is not a local product and does not feature prominently in traditional Chinese desserts.

[事实] Chinese desserts tend not to be very sweet, with red bean paste cited as a common pastry filling.

[事实] The chocolate Dawn sampled cost about 58 yuan per 100 grams, around $10, which can buy a meal for two in many places in China.

[20:16] **Low Consumption and Foreign Dominance**

[事实] Chocolate is available in Chinese grocery stores but is not especially popular.

[事实] China consumes around 2% of the world’s chocolate.

[事实] The average Chinese person eats around 100 grams of chocolate per year, about one-hundredth of French consumption.

[事实] Around four fifths of chocolate consumed in China is produced by foreign mass-market brands such as Mars, Ferrero, Nestlé and Hershey.

[21:04] **Making Chocolate More Chinese**

[事实] Consumer confidence in China is weak and shoppers are cutting back on non-essential goods.

[事实] Choc Revive’s founder argues there can still be a local market if the product becomes more Chinese.

[事实] The company uses Chinese fruits, invests in local supply chains and processes African-sourced beans in China.

[事实] It also has a research and development facility in China intended to build chocolate-making expertise.

[推测] Localisation may be the main route for premium chocolate brands to grow in a market where imported luxury food demand is under pressure.

[22:14] **Choc Revive and Competitors**

[事实] Choc Revive had about 80 outlets at the beginning of 2026 and aims to reach 300 by the end of the year.

[事实] It made about 160 million yuan, or roughly $24 million, last year and hopes to quadruple sales by year-end.

[事实] Other Chinese chocolate companies include Nibbo, which has won international chocolate awards, and Saturn Bird, which is planting cacao trees in southern China.

[事实] Dawn says it is uncertain whether Choc Revive will ultimately succeed, but China may have a serious chocolatier within a couple of years.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it moves across three very different subjects while keeping each discussion focused on a practical question: what happens if the AfD actually governs, what Britain should do about Palantir, and whether China can develop a domestic chocolate industry.

Its strongest segment is the AfD report, which combines on-the-ground observation with institutional and fiscal analysis. The discussion avoids treating the party’s rise only as a symbolic event and instead asks what power would actually let it do.

The Palantir segment is useful because it separates reputational discomfort from operational need. It does not dismiss concerns about lobbying, lock-in, unreliable claims or sovereignty, but it also argues that Britain’s public-sector data problems are real.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners who want concise international analysis rather than deep specialist detail. Its limitation is that each topic receives only a short treatment, so listeners looking for a full history of the AfD, Palantir or China’s food market would need additional sources.
