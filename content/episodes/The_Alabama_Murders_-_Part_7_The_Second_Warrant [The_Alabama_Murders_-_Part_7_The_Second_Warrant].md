# The Alabama Murders, Part 7: The Second Warrant

## Overview

The final episode of *The Alabama Murders* examines the second death warrant issued for Kenny Smith and Alabama’s decision to execute him using nitrogen hypoxia, a method never before used in a judicial execution.

Through courtroom testimony, medical analysis, and animal-research findings, the episode questions whether Alabama adequately investigated the risks of the procedure. Officials acknowledged that medical personnel had not helped develop the protocol and that key dangers—including vomiting, mask leakage, seizures, and prolonged consciousness—had received limited consideration.

The narrative then follows Smith’s family, lawyer, and other witnesses through his final hours and the execution itself. Their descriptions of convulsions and gasping contrast sharply with the state’s claim that the procedure unfolded as expected.

The series concludes by framing the case as a decades-long progression from the murder of Elizabeth Sennett to repeated institutional and moral failures, while also acknowledging the lasting grief experienced by everyone connected to the case.

## 分段落总结

[02:17] **The first execution attempt and the second warrant**

[事实] The recap describes concerns about Smith’s detailed behavior after Elizabeth Sennett’s murder, the jury’s reluctance to impose death, and the Sennett family’s 35-year wait for an execution.

[事实] Psychologist Kate Porterfield says Smith developed severe nightmares and depression after Alabama’s first failed attempt to execute him.

[事实] Smith received a second warrant in November for an execution scheduled the following January and was immediately moved from his long-term cell into an isolated death-chamber cell.

[事实] Porterfield testified that forcing Smith through another execution would severely aggravate post-traumatic stress caused by the first attempt.

[03:28] **Smith’s psychological condition under the second warrant**

[事实] Porterfield remained in contact with Smith until approximately December, when he increasingly focused on the execution he expected to face.

[事实] Smith was anxious and beginning to deteriorate, but he continued to hope that his lawyers could stop the execution.

[事实] He was especially concerned about his mother, wife, and grandson.

[事实] Porterfield argued that Smith’s PTSD resulted from the state’s previous attempt to execute him rather than from a pre-existing condition.

[06:07] **Alabama chooses nitrogen hypoxia**

[事实] Malcolm Gladwell identifies this as the final episode and says it will examine Alabama’s effort to devise and justify another way to execute Smith.

[事实] Smith’s lawyers brought a federal lawsuit seeking a preliminary injunction against the new method.

[事实] Alabama planned to strap Smith to a gurney, place a mask over his face, and supply pure nitrogen after losing confidence in its ability to establish an IV line.

[事实] The episode states that nitrogen hypoxia had never previously been used for a judicial execution anywhere.

[事实] Smith’s lawyers argued that using an untested method would violate the Eighth Amendment prohibition on cruel and unusual punishment.

[09:11] **How nitrogen deprivation works—and what can go wrong**

[事实] Anesthesiologist Joel Zivot explains that ordinary air contains mostly nitrogen, but oxygen is what supports the cellular processes necessary for life.

[事实] The theory behind nitrogen execution was that a person would inhale a non-irritating gas, lose consciousness, and die without realizing what was happening.

[事实] The episode identifies several possible complications: oxygen leaking into the mask could leave the prisoner alive with brain damage, while nausea and vomiting could cause choking.

[事实] Zivot explains that a prisoner can initially resist by holding their breath, causing carbon dioxide to rise until the urge to breathe becomes overwhelming.

[事实] He says the resulting physiological cascade may include seizures rather than immediate unconsciousness followed by death.

[13:26] **Animal research rejects nitrogen as humane euthanasia**

[事实] The episode describes a Zurich study comparing nitrogen with carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, and isoflurane in 60 rats equipped with biomedical sensors.

[事实] Researchers found that nitrogen and carbon monoxide produced longer times to unconsciousness, seizures before unconsciousness, increased stress, and greater lung damage.

[事实] The researchers concluded that nitrogen and carbon monoxide were not humane alternatives for animal euthanasia.

[推测] The episode uses the study to suggest that Alabama applied less scientific rigor to Smith’s execution protocol than researchers apply to the treatment of laboratory animals.

[17:33] **Alabama’s protocol is tested in court**

[事实] On December 20, 2023, Corrections Commissioner John Q. Hamm testified that the execution would use the same chamber, gurney, team captain, and most of the same personnel as the failed lethal-injection attempt.

[事实] The main change was replacing the IV with a mask connected to pure nitrogen.

[事实] Under cross-examination, Hamm acknowledged that the department had not consulted medical personnel about preventing or responding to vomiting inside the mask.

[事实] Hamm also said he had not reviewed expert declarations discussing the effects of vomiting during the procedure.

[推测] Reusing the same setting and personnel may have intensified the psychological harm Porterfield expected Smith to experience.

[20:32] **Mask safety and missing medical expertise**

[事实] State witnesses said the execution mask was an industrial product selected through online research because masks are not manufactured specifically for executions.

[事实] The person assigned to investigate the mask admitted having no relevant experience or expertise.

[事实] Hamm could not explain what the execution team would do to prevent outside air from entering the mask.

[事实] He agreed that the written protocol did not specify how the team would ensure a proper seal.

[推测] The testimony portrays the protocol as relying on assumptions and informal preparation rather than validated medical or engineering standards.

[22:19] **PTSD, malingering, and the vomiting question**

[事实] Porterfield had assessed Smith for more than a year and submitted a report on his mental condition.

[事实] Alabama’s attorney questioned whether Smith might be faking his PTSD symptoms.

[事实] Porterfield testified that Smith’s severe post-traumatic stress, nausea, and previous vomiting created a high likelihood that he could vomit when masked and placed under extreme distress.

[事实] The court’s practical response was to move Smith’s final meal earlier and prevent him from eating for eight hours before the execution.

[事实] With that adjustment, the effort to stop the execution failed.

[25:08] **Smith’s final preparations and farewell**

[事实] In the months before the execution, Smith put his affairs in order, selected his witnesses, and spent time expressing love to his family.

[事实] His mother, wife, sons, spiritual adviser, and lawyer Robert Grass were among those he wanted present.

[事实] Smith’s mother said she did not expect him to survive the second attempt and feared the unprecedented procedure might also endanger witnesses if the mask leaked.

[事实] Before officials took him away, Smith told his mother that they were coming for him and said, “I love you, Mom. I gotta go.”

[事实] His mother recalls him looking back and smiling as he was led away, an image she says continues to haunt her.

[28:14] **The nitrogen execution**

[事实] Witnesses saw Smith strapped across his chest and arms on a gurney while wearing the mask.

[事实] In his final statement, Smith said Alabama was taking a step backward and expressed love and peace toward those present.

[事实] Robert Grass says officials had predicted unconsciousness within seconds and described the procedure as painless.

[事实] Grass instead observed violent seizure-like movements, Smith’s head and arms straining against the restraints, and apparent gasping for air.

[事实] He says these reactions lasted for minutes rather than seconds, although he could not determine whether Smith experienced pain.

[32:27] **The aftermath for Smith’s lawyer and family**

[事实] Grass says the execution marked the unsuccessful end of approximately 18 years of legal representation intended to prevent that moment.

[事实] He felt grief for Smith, Smith’s wife, children, mother, grandchildren, and extended family.

[事实] Grass says he still thinks about Smith and wishes the story had both a different ending and a different beginning.

[推测] His reaction illustrates how capital cases can create enduring grief not only for victims’ families but also for defense lawyers and the condemned person’s relatives.

[36:46] **Alabama declares the procedure successful**

[事实] After the execution, Hamm held a press conference while Elizabeth and Charles Sennett’s two sons stood nearby.

[事实] Asked about Smith shaking and writhing for at least two minutes, Hamm attributed the movements to breath-holding, involuntary motion, and agonal breathing.

[事实] Hamm said the behavior was expected and that nothing had occurred outside the ordinary expectations for nitrogen hypoxia.

[事实] When questioned about Smith appearing conscious for several minutes, Hamm said he could not clearly see Smith’s face.

[事实] Alabama’s attorney general called the execution historic, described the method as proven, and presented it as a blueprint for other states.

[39:18] **The series’ final judgment on the Alabama case**

[事实] Gladwell summarizes the case as beginning with a husband arranging his wife’s murder and recruiting two troubled young men who later formed loving relationships while imprisoned.

[事实] He says Alabama executed one of those men by a lethal-injection procedure that harmed his lungs and subjected Smith to a failed execution before killing him with nitrogen.

[事实] The episode characterizes the broader sequence as moving from obliviousness through indifference and cruelty before ending in revision of the historical record.

[推测] Its central argument is that Alabama’s claim of a historic achievement transforms a long chain of preventable failures into an institutional success story.

[40:33] **Remembering Smith as a person**

[事实] Porterfield did not realize her last conversation with Smith would be their final one because they were still discussing his legal options.

[事实] In a letter delivered shortly before his death, she described his spirit as irrepressible and his ability to build genuine relationships from prison as miraculous.

[事实] On the night of the execution, Porterfield lit a candle for Smith and sent a photograph of it to his lawyers.

[事实] She reflects that people respond to such deaths by considering the losses suffered by the person’s family and imagining what they themselves would experience.

[推测] The quiet closing testimony shifts attention away from execution procedure and toward the human relationships and grief obscured by official language about punishment and justice.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s greatest strength is the contrast it constructs among scientific evidence, courtroom admissions, eyewitness testimony, and Alabama’s official account. The detailed cross-examination makes the state’s preparation—or lack of it—more revealing than a general argument about capital punishment would have been.

Its emotional impact comes from combining institutional scrutiny with intimate testimony from Smith’s mother, lawyer, and psychologist. Their accounts do not erase Elizabeth Sennett’s murder or her family’s suffering, but they challenge the idea that another traumatic death necessarily represents an uncomplicated form of justice.

The episode has a clear critical perspective and gives comparatively little space to a sustained defense of Alabama’s position beyond official statements. [推测] Listeners seeking a neutral legal debate may therefore find its framing strongly argumentative, while listeners interested in capital punishment, state accountability, trauma, and the ethics of experimental execution methods will find it especially valuable.
