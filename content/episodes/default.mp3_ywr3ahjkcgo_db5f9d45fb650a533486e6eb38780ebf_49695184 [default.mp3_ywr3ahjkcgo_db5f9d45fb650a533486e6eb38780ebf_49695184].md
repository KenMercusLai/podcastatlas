# Choosing Goals That Truly Fit: The Psychology of Self-Concordance

## 概览

Many people remain stuck despite abundant advice about success because they pursue goals that do not align with their deepest values and preferences. Psychologist Ken Sheldon argues that the hardest question is not how to reach a destination, but how to choose a destination that will actually support lasting happiness.

The conversation examines how consumer culture, family expectations, educational competition, status, and financial rewards can steer people toward externally defined goals. Research involving law students, lawyers, and college athletes suggests that intrinsic motivation—doing something because it is inherently engaging—predicts well-being more strongly than conventional markers such as income or prestige.

Sheldon calls the alignment between conscious goals and deeper inclinations “self-concordance.” He proposes treating self-discovery as a creative process: consciously pose important questions, allow the non-conscious mind time to work, notice emerging insights, and then test those insights in real life.

The episode also stresses that finding a meaningful direction does not eliminate hardship. When enjoyment fades, “identified motivation”—continuing because an activity expresses one’s values and remains important—can sustain long-term effort.

## 分段落总结

[00:01] **Why Good Advice Can Still Leave People Stuck**

[事实] Religions, advice columns, and self-help books offer useful skills such as planning, patience, and perseverance, yet many people still feel unable to move forward.

[事实] The episode proposes that people often pursue goals that conflict with their own deepest values and preferences.

[事实] Ken Sheldon studies how people can choose goals that are more likely to make them happy if achieved.

[01:53] **Ken Sheldon’s Search for a Direction**

[事实] After college, Sheldon moved to Seattle and tried to become a musician, but his band struggled with conflicting goals, unreliable commitments, and poor follow-through.

[事实] When his bandmates failed to complete a contest recording, he concluded that the band was unlikely to provide a viable livelihood.

[事实] He subsequently entered a graduate program in existential phenomenological psychotherapy but left after one year because it did not provide the answers he was seeking.

[事实] Feeling lost and unsuccessful, he also attended EST training, whose promised “enlightenment” culminated in the message that the present moment was already all there was.

[10:15] **From Personal Uncertainty to the Science of Goals**

[事实] Sheldon eventually enrolled in a psychology PhD program and became interested in Robert Emmons’s research on personal strivings.

[事实] Instead of measuring personality only through fixed traits, Emmons asked participants to list what they were striving to accomplish in life.

[事实] Sheldon realized that people might list goals inherited from parents, friends, or society rather than goals that genuinely suited them.

[事实] This led him to identify the central problem as deciding where one truly wants to go, not merely determining how to get there.

[13:50] **Why People Choose the Wrong Goals**

[事实] Sheldon’s research finds that people often misjudge how achieving a goal will affect their happiness; later measurements may show no improvement at all.

[事实] Material consumer culture repeatedly presents money, possessions, power, and status as measures of a successful life.

[事实] Parents, teachers, friends, and other well-intentioned people may recommend sensible paths without knowing what will make an individual happy.

[事实] Parents may also have goals of their own, such as gaining status through a child’s prestigious career, which can become entangled with genuine concern for that child.

[17:11] **Law School, Status, and the Corruption of Idealistic Goals**

[事实] Sheldon and law professor Lawrence Krieger studied law students in an environment characterized by curved grading, intense competition, and pressure to obtain prestigious jobs.

[事实] Over three years of legal education, the students’ well-being declined substantially while depression increased.

[事实] Students who began with strong idealistic motivations often earned good grades, gained status, and subsequently shifted toward goals involving appearance and prestige rather than helping others.

[事实] In a study of 6,000 practicing lawyers, income had only a small association with happiness, while enjoying the work, believing in it, and feeling that it contributed to the world had much larger associations.

[21:33] **Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivation**

[事实] Intrinsic motivation means doing something because the activity itself is interesting, rewarding, or enjoyable.

[事实] Extrinsic motivation means doing an activity primarily to obtain a later reward, even when the activity itself is not enjoyable.

[事实] Experiments associated with Edward Deci showed that paying people to complete enjoyable puzzles reduced their tendency to continue with the puzzles when the payment was removed.

[事实] External rewards and punishments may therefore undermine an interest that originally arose from curiosity or enjoyment.

[事实] Sheldon cautions that incentives can sometimes be necessary, but describes them as powerful interventions that may damage a person’s relationship with an activity.

[25:05] **How External Control Can Erode a Lifelong Passion**

[事实] Sheldon compared scholarship athletes, whose tuition and expenses were covered, with walk-on athletes who participated mainly because they wanted to play.

[事实] As many as 30 or 40 years later, former scholarship athletes were less interested in playing or following their sport than former walk-ons.

[事实] Scholarship athletes had experienced pressure from coaches, fans, public criticism, and the possibility of losing financial support.

[事实] Feeling controlled by an environment tends to undermine intrinsic motivation, making people more likely to stop once the external pressure disappears.

[27:34] **The Limits of Conscious Introspection**

[事实] Avoiding outside pressure is not enough because conscious awareness captures only a limited portion of the mind at any moment.

[事实] Thoughts accessible to consciousness can themselves be shaped by external expectations and pressures.

[事实] Sheldon describes the non-conscious mind as containing habitual inclinations, emerging intuitions, and motives that can influence behavior without direct awareness.

[事实] Choosing fitting goals requires bringing conscious decisions into closer contact with these deeper inclinations.

[31:41] **Self-Concordance and a Creative Method of Discovery**

[事实] Sheldon uses “self-concordance” to describe the alignment of goals with both conscious and non-conscious inclinations.

[事实] Non-concordant goals may be pursued because important people want them or because individuals believe they must conform to a long-standing image of who they should be.

[事实] Sheldon adapts a model of creativity to self-discovery: ask a difficult question, allow a period of incubation, notice an illuminating idea, and verify it.

[事实] Questions such as “Why am I unhappy?”, “What do I really want?”, and “What is bothering me?” can set non-conscious processing in motion even when no immediate answer appears.

[事实] Not every insight is reliable, so possible answers must be tested rather than accepted as final revelations.

[37:18] **Mindfulness and Learning to Hear Subtle Signals**

[事实] The ability to notice subtle thoughts and feelings can be improved through deliberate practice.

[事实] Sheldon recommends mindfulness meditation as a way to observe what arises while remaining present instead of being carried away by each thought, fear, or emotion.

[事实] This practice may help people detect signals at the edge of consciousness that they would otherwise overlook.

[38:29] **Amy’s Journey Back to Her Values**

[事实] Amy is a composite character created from multiple people Sheldon has worked with.

[事实] She had cared about environmental issues in college but later became a wealthy and conventionally successful law-firm partner who was miserable.

[事实] After her brother questioned why she remained in an unhappy career, a long-forgotten college friend came to mind; that friend now ran an environmental consulting firm.

[事实] Amy contacted the friend, accepted an invitation to work with her, moved cities, and took a 50 percent salary cut.

[事实] She became happier after reconnecting her work with her earlier desire to make a difference, though changing course required overcoming fears about lost income and status.

[43:18] **The Psychological Rubicon of Decision**

[事实] Research discussed by Sheldon suggests that people cross a “Rubicon” when they move from deliberating about a goal to implementing it.

[事实] After committing, the mind tends to stop questioning the choice and instead focuses on planning, preserving the goal, and collecting reasons that support the decision.

[事实] This commitment can be useful because it prevents excessive doubt and supports action.

[事实] However, discomfort and cognitive dissonance can sometimes provide valuable signals that a person should return to deliberation and reconsider the chosen path.

[44:47] **Meaningful Goals Still Require Endurance**

[事实] Self-concordance can strengthen intrinsic motivation and help people live according to their values, but it does not make every stage of a chosen path enjoyable.

[事实] Among people hiking the Pacific Crest Trail, intrinsic motivation declined sharply as the journey became increasingly difficult and repetitive.

[事实] Sheldon distinguishes intrinsic motivation from “identified motivation,” in which people continue because an activity is meaningful, important, and expressive of their values.

[事实] Identified motivation can sustain effort when enjoyment fades because the person still believes in the journey.

[事实] Sheldon concludes that people continually shape their lives through choices but are rarely taught how to ask themselves the questions needed to choose well.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s greatest value lies in separating the skill of achieving goals from the more fundamental skill of choosing worthwhile goals. Its combination of personal narrative, psychological concepts, and research examples makes “self-concordance” accessible without presenting it as a quick formula.

[推测] The discussion is especially useful for students, professionals experiencing career dissatisfaction, parents considering how to motivate children, and anyone whose success feels disconnected from fulfillment. The distinction among intrinsic, extrinsic, and identified motivation offers a practical framework for examining both short-term enthusiasm and long-term perseverance.

[推测] A limitation is that the conversation gives less attention to financial necessity, caregiving duties, structural inequality, and other constraints that may prevent people from acting on self-concordant goals. Amy’s career change is illustrative, but her ability to accept a major salary reduction may not generalize to everyone.

[推测] The episode does not promise that introspection will reveal one permanent calling. Its stronger message is that life should be treated as an ongoing experiment: ask honest questions, notice quieter responses, test possible directions, and remain willing to reconsider earlier commitments.
