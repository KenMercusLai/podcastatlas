# Australia Before Captain Cook

## 概览

This episode reframes the familiar British story of Australia by starting with the problem of Captain Cook’s “discovery” rather than accepting it. David Hunt explains that Cook was an important navigator, but not the discoverer of Australia in any simple sense: Indigenous people had been there for tens of thousands of years, and other non-British contacts preceded British settlement.

The discussion moves from Cook and the myth of Terra Australis to the much older history of Aboriginal Australia: migration, megafauna, archaeological evidence, oral traditions, Dreamtime stories, songlines, and the limits of what can be known about societies tens of thousands of years in the past.

In the second half, the episode surveys outside contacts before British colonisation: Makassan trepang fishermen, contested Chinese discovery claims, Dutch and Spanish encounters in 1606, Tasman’s naming of New Holland, and why Dutch, French, Portuguese, or Spanish colonisation did not follow.

The closing argument is that British Australia was not inevitable from Cook’s landing alone. New South Wales became attractive to Britain largely because it was isolated and could serve as a place to send convicts after the loss of the American colonies.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Opening with the Cook Myth**

[事实] The hosts open with the Captain Cook statue in Hyde Park, Sydney, whose inscription says he “discovered this territory, 1770.”
[事实] David Hunt is introduced as the guest: an Australian historian, broadcaster, and podcaster.
[推测] The episode sets up Cook not as a simple founding hero, but as a way to question British-centred versions of Australian history.

[02:20] **Changing Views of Captain Cook**

[事实] Hunt says that in the 1970s and 1980s Australian students were effectively taught that Captain Cook discovered Australia.
[事实] He says more recent Australian history curricula are more complex and more critical of colonial statues and inscriptions.
[事实] The Cook statue’s wording is described as insulting to Indigenous people who had been in Australia around 65,000 years before Cook.

[04:10] **Cook as Navigator, Not Discoverer**

[事实] Cook is described as an undisputedly great navigator from humble origins, noted for cartography and navigation.
[事实] He was chosen for the Endeavour voyage partly because the expedition’s main scientific purpose was to measure the 1769 transit of Venus in Tahiti.
[事实] Cook was promoted to lieutenant for the voyage and later received secret orders to return via New Zealand and the eastern coast of New Holland.
[推测] The discussion separates Cook’s technical achievements from later national myths about “discovery.”

[06:06] **Terra Australis and New Holland**

[事实] Europeans had long searched for Terra Australis, a supposed southern counterweight continent imagined since classical antiquity.
[事实] After New Zealand, Cook sailed toward the eastern coast of New Holland, meaning he was heading to a land already known in European geography.
[事实] Hunt notes that Cook’s Landing Place at Botany Bay gives the wrong landing date and had not been corrected for 151 years.

[08:03] **Portuguese and French Pre-Cook Claims**

[事实] Hunt says there is no evidence of European exploration of Australia’s east coast before 1770.
[事实] A theory that Portuguese sailors reached eastern Australia in the 1520s and left a “mahogany ship” near Warrnambool is described as largely discredited.
[事实] Alleged ship fragments were later tested and found to be eucalyptus rather than Portuguese mahogany.
[推测] The segment treats many pre-Cook European discovery claims as attractive stories with weak evidence.

[10:02] **First Australians and Deep Time**

[事实] Hunt says the commonly accepted date for Aboriginal presence in Australia is now around 60,000 to 65,000 years, while some push it back to 120,000 years.
[事实] Scientific evidence is said to support an out-of-Africa migration, with people island-hopping from Sunda to Sahul.
[事实] During the Ice Age, lower sea levels made Australia, New Guinea, and Tasmania part of one landmass, but crossing still required seafaring.
[事实] Some Aboriginal people maintain that they were created in Australia and have always been there.

[12:52] **Megafauna and the First Human Landscape**

[事实] Australia’s megafauna included marsupial lions, land crocodiles, larger koalas, giant kangaroos, diprotodons, and the “demon duck of doom.”
[事实] The marsupial lion is described as an ambush predator weighing up to about 130 kilograms.
[事实] The land crocodile Quinkana may have grown to around seven metres and had legs suited to walking on land.
[事实] Hunt describes diprotodon as a rhinoceros-sized wombat-like animal with a large skull and small brain.

[17:16] **Megafauna Extinction Debate**

[事实] The speakers discuss whether humans caused the disappearance of Australian megafauna.
[事实] Hunt says some megafauna coexisted with humans, but the duration is contested.
[事实] Tim Flannery’s “Blitzkrieg” theory argues that humans hunted large, naive animals and later changed the landscape through fire to hunt smaller prey.
[事实] An alternative explanation is climate change and increasing aridity during the Ice Age.
[推测] The debate is politically sensitive because it touches on whether Aboriginal Australians fundamentally transformed the landscape.

[20:12] **Archaeological and Genetic Evidence**

[事实] Hunt says radiocarbon dating can reach back around 40,000 to 50,000 years before becoming ineffective.
[事实] Mungo Man is described as about 40,000 years old and as the earliest known example of ritual burial, with ochre sprinkled on the body.
[事实] Earlier stone tools can be dated through luminescence techniques, and genetic evidence suggests a timeline of roughly 60,000 to 75,000 years.
[推测] The evidence gives strong outlines for presence and movement, but less certainty about social life in the earliest periods.

[22:04] **Languages, Waves, and Origins**

[事实] Hunt says there were over 250 Aboriginal languages in Australia.
[事实] He describes both single-wave and multi-wave theories of settlement.
[事实] The single-wave theory suggests around 1,000 to 3,000 people may have arrived about 65,000 years ago to explain later genetic diversity.
[事实] Later influences discussed include Lapita people bringing dingoes around 4,000 years ago and possible Indian genetic admixture in northern Australia around 5,000 years ago.

[24:10] **Continuous Culture, Dreamtime, and Oral Memory**

[事实] The episode addresses the claim that Aboriginal culture is the world’s oldest continuous culture.
[事实] Hunt says some Dreamtime stories suggest very ancient oral traditions, including stories that may preserve memories of megafauna or earlier landscapes.
[事实] The Bunyip is discussed as a creature associated with waterholes that may trace back to diprotodon-like animals.
[推测] The transcript supports the idea of very deep cultural continuity, but Hunt also stresses that proving exact continuity is difficult.

[25:43] **Songlines and Dreamtime as Law**

[事实] Hunt explains that Aboriginal cultures used oral transmission rather than writing.
[事实] Songlines are described as cultural songs passed through generations that encode landscape knowledge and aid navigation.
[事实] Dreamtime stories are described as codifying rules, laws, marriage systems, spiritual beliefs, and ways of living.
[事实] Bill Stanner’s term “everywhen” is used to describe the fusion of past, present, future, and spirituality.

[29:18] **Aboriginality and Continental Diversity**

[事实] Hunt says the idea of “Aboriginality” as one single identity is a colonial construct.
[事实] Australia is compared in size to Western Europe, with more than 250 language groups and many clans within those groups.
[事实] Hunt says these peoples did not see themselves as one single people, though trade and communication crossed long distances.
[事实] Shells from northern Queensland are cited as trade goods appearing in Western Australia.

[31:14] **Makassan Contact and Trepang Trade**

[事实] Hunt says Makassan fishermen from Sulawesi reached northern Australia at least by the early 1600s and probably in the 1500s.
[事实] They came for trepang, or sea cucumber, which was valued in China as an aphrodisiac.
[事实] About a thousand Makassans may have come seasonally, staying for months and processing trepang in temporary factories.
[事实] They traded glass, cloth, metal implements, alcohol, and language with the Yolngu people of Arnhem Land.

[34:30] **Why Southeast Asians Did Not Settle in Larger Numbers**

[事实] Hunt says nobody really knows why more Southeast Asian seafarers did not settle Australia.
[事实] He mentions possible Lapita DNA in northern Torres Strait Islands and southern New Guinea, alongside mainly Melanesian ancestry.
[事实] He suggests that small groups may have been deterred by resistance from territorial local peoples.
[事实] Trade still moved in both directions, including dingoes and kangaroo fleas reaching Southeast Asian dogs.

[35:41] **The Chinese Discovery Claim**

[事实] Hunt discusses Gavin Menzies’s claim that Chinese treasure fleets reached Australia in the 1420s.
[事实] The claim is described as widely ridiculed and based partly on unreliable Australian evidence.
[事实] Hunt says there is no compelling evidence that Chinese sailors came to Australia.
[事实] He says China would likely have known of the southern landmass once Makassans delivered dried trepang to Chinese markets.

[38:38] **Coins, Cannons, and the 1606 Threshold**

[事实] Hunt says old coins and cannon fragments are sometimes found on Australian beaches, but they can be explained by later sailors carrying old currency.
[事实] He says there is no compelling evidence of European contact before 1606.
[事实] New South Wales later had no formal currency at first, with rum becoming a liquid currency.
[推测] Isolated artifacts are treated as weak evidence unless they can be tied securely to early contact.

[39:36] **Dutch Contact in 1606**

[事实] The Dutch East Indies Company was formed in 1602 and displaced the Portuguese in parts of the Spice Islands.
[事实] William Jansun, captain of the Doofken, sailed south looking for trade opportunities and reached Cape York Peninsula.
[事实] He charted about 300 kilometres, thought the land was part of New Guinea, and left in geographical confusion.

[40:44] **Tasman, New Holland, and the Quokka**

[事实] Abel Tasman arrived in 1642, claimed Van Diemen’s Land, and named it after Anthony Van Diemen.
[事实] Tasman also named New Zealand and later called the Australian landmass New Holland.
[事实] The Dutch had earlier known Australia as “Beach.”
[事实] Quokkas are described as small, smiley kangaroo-like animals on Rottnest Island, eaten by hungry Dutch sailors.

[41:53] **Why Australia Did Not Become Dutch, French, Portuguese, or Spanish**

[事实] Hunt says the Portuguese east-coast theory is heavily contested and unsupported by evidence.
[事实] Torres sailed through the Torres Strait in 1606 and probably saw Australia’s northern tip, but did not stop.
[事实] Hunt says Spain might have taken a stronger colonising interest if Torres had stopped, because Spain was interested in colonisation, Christianity, and Terra Australis.
[事实] The Dutch and French were more interested in trade and scientific exploration than colonising Australia at that time.

[45:09] **Banks, Convicts, and Britain’s Afterthought**

[事实] Joseph Banks initially judged the country poorly for settlement after Cook’s 1770 voyage.
[事实] After the American War of Independence, Britain needed a new place to send convicts.
[事实] Britain considered other destinations, including East Africa, the West Indies, the Americas, and the Mosquito Coast.
[事实] New South Wales appealed because it was geographically isolated and convicts would be less able to return.

[47:02] **First Fleet, La Pérouse, and Escape**

[事实] The La Pérouse expedition arrived in Botany Bay a few days after Arthur Phillip in 1788.
[事实] Some convicts ran from Sydney to Botany Bay hoping to leave with the French.
[事实] Hunt says two convicts escaped, while the La Pérouse expedition later probably foundered near the Solomon Islands.
[推测] The anecdote undercuts the idea that geographic isolation made escape impossible, even if isolation was the strategic reason for choosing New South Wales.

[48:00] **Closing and Part Two**

[事实] The hosts say they should continue the story in a second part.
[事实] Hunt says he would like to discuss the founding of the convict colony of New South Wales.
[事实] The conversation ends with cricket jokes linking Captain Cook, Alastair Cook, and British-Australian sporting rivalry.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it makes Australian history feel much older and more contested than the simple “Cook discovered Australia” version. It is especially strong at showing how myths of discovery can coexist with real navigational achievement.

[推测] Its best feature is the combination of serious historical correction with comic delivery. The discussion covers archaeology, oral tradition, ecology, trade, and empire without becoming a dry chronology.

[推测] Its limitation is that many early-history questions remain unresolved by nature: migration waves, megafauna extinction, and oral continuity are all presented as contested rather than settled. That makes the episode more useful as an introduction to debates than as a definitive account.

[推测] It is best suited to listeners interested in Australian history, colonial myths, Indigenous deep history, and the pre-British contacts that complicate the standard schoolbook narrative.
