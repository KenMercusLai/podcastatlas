# Introducing Revisionist History Season Two

## Overview

Malcolm Gladwell previews the second season of *Revisionist History*, describing his enduring attraction to “big ideas” that might explain seemingly unrelated events. His research involved dozens of interviews, extensive reading, and time spent searching for historical subjects strange enough to deserve reconsideration.

The season’s unifying philosophical question draws on Heraclitus and the Ship of Theseus: when something changes piece by piece, does it remain the same thing? Upcoming stories range from McDonald’s former french fries and the hidden history of Los Angeles golf courses to an intensely dysfunctional friendship.

The preview leaves open whether these stories truly share one explanatory thread. Gladwell acknowledges that reasonable people may disagree, reflecting the podcast’s broader aim of giving the past a second chance.

[Fact] He began developing Season Two with the same obsession.

[Fact] He recalls interviewing roughly 30 or 40 people, reading several dozen books, and spending time in the New York University library.

[01:40] **Heraclitus and the Ship of Theseus**

[Fact] Philosopher Mark Cohen introduces the Heraclitean idea that a person cannot step into the same river twice because the water is different each time.

[Fact] The discussion moves from rivers to the Ship of Theseus: if every board of a ship is replaced one at a time, the question is whether the final ship is still the same vessel.

[Inference] This problem of identity amid continuous change appears to provide a possible conceptual framework for the season.

[Fact] Gladwell promises an episode about the french fries McDonald’s served in the past, including an evaluation of a version praised for its exceptionally crisp texture.

[Fact] Another forthcoming story concerns a deeply dysfunctional friendship in which one person may have been the only individual the other ever loved.

[Inference] Although the subjects appear unrelated, the preview suggests that each may explore what is lost, preserved, or transformed over time.

[Fact] Gladwell says it is possible that all the previewed stories are connected to the philosophical image of the river.

## Podcast Commentary/Summary

This short season trailer efficiently establishes both the intellectual ambition and the playful tone of *Revisionist History*. Its strongest feature is the use of a familiar philosophical puzzle to connect stories about food, urban space, relationships, and historical change.

The excerpts create curiosity without disclosing the full arguments of the coming episodes. Gladwell’s admission that his grand theory may be wrong also preserves room for disagreement rather than presenting the season’s premise as settled fact.

[Inference] Because this is a promotional preview rather than a complete episode, it offers limited evidence for evaluating the promised historical claims in depth. It is best suited to listeners interested in revisionist storytelling, philosophical questions, and unexpected connections between everyday subjects and larger historical themes.
