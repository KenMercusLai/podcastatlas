# Brian Chesky: How Airbnb’s Founder Learned to Become a CEO

## 概览

Brian Chesky reflects on the long gap between being a natural founder and becoming an effective CEO. Although Airbnb grew into a company worth more than $100 billion, he says its strong business model often concealed weak leadership, organizational sprawl, and his own reluctance to confront problems.

The pandemic became a life-or-death test. After Airbnb lost 80% of its business within weeks, Chesky abandoned a highly delegated management style, studied how Steve Jobs rescued Apple, cut projects, entered the details, and aligned the company around fewer priorities.

The conversation also examines the personal cost of achievement. Airbnb’s IPO brought Chesky only a brief rush, followed by loneliness and a crisis of purpose. Reconnecting with old friends, becoming more vulnerable, and separating his identity from the company helped him redefine success.

Chesky ultimately presents leadership as a balance: presence without controlling every action, empowerment without fragmented autonomy, conviction without ideological rigidity, and enduring ambition grounded in relationships, service, and the joy of creating.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **The Crisis That Forced a CEO to Emerge**

[事实] Chesky describes the pandemic as the first moment when being a good CEO became essential to Airbnb’s survival rather than merely helpful to its growth.

[事实] The episode traces how an art-school graduate with no prior business experience became CEO of a company valued at more than $100 billion.

[事实] Chesky says Airbnb often succeeded in spite of his leadership, not because of it.

[03:20] **The Emptiness After Airbnb’s IPO**

[事实] Airbnb went public on December 10, 2020, during the pandemic, with the celebration taking place through Zoom rather than in person.

[事实] Chesky woke up after the IPO alone, returned to ordinary Zoom meetings, and realized that extraordinary wealth and recognition had not made him much happier.

[事实] He concluded that his desire to be special and receive public validation had become addictive, while the emotional rush of success faded quickly.

[事实] The experience led him to question what he wanted from life and why he was continuing to build the company.

[06:17] **From Art School to an Unexpected Business Career**

[事实] Before graduating from the Rhode Island School of Design, Joe Gebbia told Chesky that they would someday start a company together and that a book would be written about it.

[事实] Chesky credits Gebbia and other early supporters with seeing leadership potential in him that he could not yet see himself.

[事实] His art-school background initially made him uncomfortable with commercial ambition and even with Airbnb having a business model.

[事实] He later became a supporter of for-profit enterprise after concluding that companies can sustain forms of innovation that are difficult for nonprofits to reproduce.

[11:30] **A Natural Founder but an Ineffective CEO**

[事实] Chesky believes he was naturally a strong founder but initially a poor CEO, partly because creating things felt familiar while organizational leadership was new and counterintuitive.

[事实] He argues that young companies should hire smart, hungry people suited to their stage rather than experienced executives whose knowledge comes mainly from managing large organizations.

[事实] When his intuition conflicted with senior executives’ experience, he usually deferred to them and later regarded that response as a mistake.

[事实] His desire to be liked made him conflict-averse, overly deferential, and slow to address poor performance.

[15:50] **Avoided Conflicts and Fragmented Direction**

[事实] Chesky avoided difficult conversations with struggling employees and sometimes performed their work himself, creating an unsustainable dependency.

[事实] He learned that leaders cannot expect underperforming employees to remove themselves from their roles; management must diagnose the problem and act.

[事实] He says a CEO must reject competing directions and ensure that executives build toward one company strategy rather than separate personal agendas.

[事实] By the late 2010s, Airbnb was still growing but had become chaotic, bureaucratic, and filled with teams pursuing too many initiatives.

[18:15] **Self-Doubt, Persistence, and the Pandemic Collapse**

[事实] Chesky frequently wondered whether he had the capacity to become a great CEO, yet believed Airbnb could not grow faster than his own learning curve.

[事实] His father had taught him never to quit, while Gebbia, Nathan Blecharczyk, and Airbnb’s board continued to express confidence in him.

[事实] During the pandemic, Airbnb lost 80% of its business in eight weeks and faced questions about whether it would survive.

[事实] Chesky says the support of his co-founders and board, combined with his refusal to quit, kept him in the role.

[23:40] **Leadership, Loneliness, and Lost Identity**

[事实] Chesky describes the CEO role as lonely because employees continually evaluate the leader, while the informal companionship of a founding team gradually disappears.

[事实] He realized that company valuation was not a durable source of happiness and that much of his emptiness came from isolation.

[事实] Barack Obama advised him that staying grounded required maintaining close relationships with people connected to his roots.

[事实] Chesky acknowledged that he had neglected old friendships, concealed vulnerability from colleagues, and allowed his identity to merge with Airbnb.

[事实] He began reconnecting with school friends through calls and annual trips, while learning that a healthy life requires connection to oneself, family, trusted friends, community, and a larger purpose.

[31:20] **Choosing Trust Despite Its Risks**

[事实] Chesky decided to give new people the benefit of the doubt instead of limiting trust to old friends.

[事实] He considered occasional betrayals less damaging than the prospect of closing himself off and living an isolated life.

[事实] Because successful people are often treated especially well, he evaluates character by observing how someone treats assistants and other people with less status.

[事实] He compares trust to entrepreneurship and relationships: avoiding all risk may prevent pain, but it also prevents meaningful participation in life.

[34:28] **How Success Produced Organizational Hubris**

[事实] Chesky attributes Airbnb’s excessive expansion partly to hubris after its core product achieved rapid success.

[事实] The company assumed it could create many new businesses simultaneously without reproducing the experimentation, discipline, and strong foundations behind the original product.

[事实] Abundant money and resources weakened constraints, encouraged too many projects, and allowed quality and organizational coherence to decline.

[事实] Chesky says leadership based on absence worsened the dysfunction; when the company most needed direction, he mistakenly believed stepping back was the responsible response.

[39:13] **The Pandemic and the Turn Toward Founder Mode**

[事实] Chesky says maturity, 12 years of experience, and the pandemic’s life-or-death stakes finally helped him become a more decisive CEO.

[事实] He studied Steve Jobs’s return to Apple and concluded that Jobs rescued the company by mastering details, eliminating projects, simplifying operations, raising standards, and forcing alignment.

[事实] During Airbnb’s crisis, Chesky reviewed expenses and worked closely with teams rather than delegating the recovery from a distance.

[事实] After the immediate danger passed, he refused to return to Airbnb’s previous operating model.

[42:37] **Skip-Level Communication and Shared Ownership**

[事实] Chesky began communicating with employees several layers below his direct reports so that information would not be filtered solely through senior executives.

[事实] Some executives regarded this approach as undermining or micromanagement, and the executive team eventually turned over.

[事实] For important projects, he brings the person doing the work and the full management chain into the same room, hears their views, and makes the final decision.

[事实] He says he should overrule leaders occasionally: constant intervention suggests the wrong team or a mistaken CEO, while never intervening means the CEO contributes no distinctive judgment.

[事实] Chesky defines the approach as collaboration in the details, not simply ordering employees to execute his ideas.

[47:28] **Empowerment Is Not the Same as Autonomy**

[事实] Chesky distinguishes autonomy over how work is performed from autonomy over the company’s overall direction.

[事实] He offers employees resources and removes organizational obstacles, but expects teams to move toward a shared destination.

[事实] He argues that founder-led management becomes credible through results and by hiring leaders familiar with highly involved founders.

[事实] Founder mode does not mean remaining in every detail forever; leaders stay close until standards become organizational “muscle memory,” then reduce their involvement while continuing to review the work.

[53:02] **Making Disagreement Safe**

[事实] Chesky relies on long-serving colleagues, co-founders, and people secure enough to tell him uncomfortable truths.

[事实] After strongly endorsing a proposed tagline, he deliberately reopened the decision and asked the creative team to find a better option, giving them explicit permission to challenge him.

[事实] He sometimes asks for others’ opinions before offering his own so that his status does not predetermine the room’s response.

[事实] He believes leaders should establish projects correctly at the outset, assign clear responsibility and resources, and maintain a regular review rhythm to prevent months of wasted work.

[事实] Chesky says he can remain meaningfully involved in roughly 20 projects operating on daily, weekly, biweekly, or monthly review cycles.

[58:13] **Becoming Less Ideological About Airbnb’s Growth**

[事实] Chesky once opposed advertising, hotels, and loyalty programs on principle, but reconsidered each position as the company matured.

[事实] Airbnb began advertising, acquired HotelTonight, and recognized that many guests also use hotels.

[事实] He says the company is exploring a loyalty-related program, although he provides no details beyond saying it would need to feel original and distinct.

[推测] His change of position illustrates his broader definition of CEO maturity: preserving a strong point of view while abandoning beliefs that no longer serve customers or the business.

[61:18] **Airbnb.org and Housing as a Form of Service**

[事实] Chesky and his co-founders discussed Airbnb.org before Airbnb itself launched, although the initiative did not take practical form until a host offered free accommodation after Hurricane Sandy.

[事实] Airbnb.org subsequently provided housing after disasters and displacement, including stays for 20,000 Afghan refugees, 100,000 Ukrainian refugees, and 70,000 free nights connected to the Los Angeles fires.

[事实] The nonprofit also supports people traveling for medical care and victims of domestic violence.

[事实] Airbnb plans to commit approximately half a billion dollars toward providing one million free nights per year over the next five years.

[事实] Chesky presents housing as Airbnb’s distinctive capability and says doing good in the world can also be good business.

[65:40] **Creating for Life Rather Than for Applause**

[事实] Chesky cites figures such as Leonardo da Vinci, Walt Disney, Steve Jobs, and Frank Lloyd Wright as people who continued creating near the ends of their lives.

[事实] He hopes to remain involved with meaningful creative work for as long as he is capable, while also being surrounded by family.

[事实] He says he no longer works for money or adulation but because he enjoys building and regards Airbnb as an unusually large creative canvas.

[事实] Chesky attributes receiving his initial opportunity partly to luck, while attributing the skills developed afterward to sustained training and hard work.

[事实] He wants to remain childlike rather than childish: continuing to explore, learn, make new friends, connect with people, and create throughout his life.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s greatest value lies in its unusually candid account of how a successful business model can conceal managerial weakness. Chesky does not present Airbnb’s growth as proof that every earlier decision was correct; instead, he separates market success from organizational excellence.

[推测] Its strongest practical material concerns conflict avoidance, skip-level communication, project review rhythms, and the distinction between empowerment and autonomy. These ideas are especially relevant to founders whose companies have outgrown informal management.

[推测] The discussion is necessarily centered on Chesky’s own interpretation. His admiration for founder-led, detail-oriented management receives limited challenge, and the episode offers less evidence about how Airbnb employees experienced the transition or how the model might work without an unusually powerful founder.

[推测] The episode is best suited to founders, executives, managers, and listeners interested in the psychological costs of ambition. Its broader message is that durable leadership depends not only on decisive organizational control, but also on humility, trusted relationships, practical flexibility, and a purpose that can survive the fading rush of success.
