# America's Holy Wars

## 概览

This episode examines how religion has shaped American war, diplomacy, expansion, and self-understanding from early colonial New England through the late 19th century. The central question is not whether America is uniquely religious, but why religion has remained unusually consistent in American political and military rhetoric compared with other modern great powers.

The discussion argues that American history contains a recurring tension: religious ideas could justify conquest, expansion, and violence, but they also produced pacifism, reform, abolitionism, and anxiety about moral corruption. That tension runs from Puritan New England and westward expansion to the Civil War and the annexation of the Philippines.

A major conclusion is that religion should not be treated as a mere cover for material interests. In cases such as William McKinley and the Philippines, Andrew Preston argues that religious belief helped shape both the justification for empire and the way Americans imagined their mission abroad.

## 分段落总结

[00:15] **Pequot War and the Origins of American Ambivalence**

[事实] The episode opens with the 1637 attack on the Pequot, in which colonists and Native allies killed hundreds of Pequot men, women, and children.
[事实] The hosts frame this episode as an early example of American tension between horror at bloodshed and religious justification for violence.
[事实] Some settlers defended the slaughter by appealing to scripture and claiming divine warrant for their actions.
[推测] The opening example is used to establish the episode's larger theme: American violence has often been accompanied by religious moral anxiety.

[02:22] **Why American Crusades Are a Useful Theme**

[事实] Dominic explains that American political rhetoric appears unusually religious from a 21st-century European perspective.
[事实] The discussion connects the topic to George W. Bush's description of the war on terror as a "crusade" and to earlier podcast discussions of Vietnam.
[事实] Guest Andrew Preston is introduced as a historian of American religion and foreign policy.
[推测] The term "crusades" is being used broadly to describe religiously charged missions for war, peace, reform, or expansion, rather than medieval crusading alone.

[03:54] **Puritan Inheritance and the City on a Hill**

[事实] Preston says some ideas in American war and diplomacy can be traced back to the Pilgrims and Puritans, even if they later disappear and reappear.
[事实] John Winthrop's idea of a community watched by the world later became embedded in American political culture as the "city on a hill."
[事实] Preston rejects the claim that America is wholly unique, but says religion has been unusually consistent in shaping American behavior as a great power.
[推测] The Puritan inheritance matters less as a direct blueprint than as a reservoir of language and assumptions repeatedly revived in later periods.

[06:39] **Expansion, Conversion, and Pacifist Tension**

[事实] The speakers discuss the tension between seeing Native peoples as recipients of Christian teaching and seeing them as enemies to be removed.
[事实] Preston says religious ideas supplied ideological fuel for expansion, but land hunger and settlement also mattered.
[事实] He argues that American crusading could mean aggression and conquest, but also Christian pacifism, reform, and a desire to help or convert others.
[事实] Quakers are presented as important founders of American pacifism and as a reformist conscience within American expansion.
[推测] The episode treats American religion as internally divided, not simply as a force for violence or restraint.

[09:04] **Puritans, Spanish Catholics, and Early Colonial War**

[事实] Dominic asks how English Protestant settlers differed from Spanish and Portuguese Catholic colonizers.
[事实] Preston says English settlers believed they were different and superior to the Spanish, and initially cared more about land and settlement than conversion.
[事实] Conflicts with Indigenous groups such as the Pequot and Narragansett escalated into extremely violent wars.
[事实] Preston says Europeans brought violence with them, while Native Americans also had existing traditions of warfare.
[推测] Religious identity intensified conflicts that were also driven by land, power, and settlement.

[13:42] **French and Indian Wars and Manifest Destiny**

[事实] Preston explains that Americans call the Seven Years' War the French and Indian War, reflecting colonial conflict against Catholic French forces and their Indigenous allies.
[事实] He says 18th-century wars in North America partly carried European-style religious dimensions.
[事实] In the 19th century, older expansionist ideas fused into the ideology of Manifest Destiny.
[事实] Manifest Destiny cast expansion as both a right and a responsibility to spread civilization.
[推测] The language of civilization and barbarism helped Americans morally frame territorial growth as obligation rather than conquest.

[15:48] **Christian Anxiety, Slavery, and Universalism**

[事实] Tom raises the Christian anxiety that power, conquest, and greatness may be morally corrupting.
[事实] Preston agrees that abolitionism emerged from this tradition, especially as slavery expanded in the first half of the 19th century.
[事实] Preston notes that abolitionism did not always map onto pacifism; John L. O'Sullivan, who coined Manifest Destiny, considered himself a Christian pacifist.
[事实] The speakers connect American revolutionary language of universal rights with Christian universalism and Enlightenment values.
[推测] The episode suggests that American universalism blends Christian and secular vocabularies rather than belonging neatly to one category.

[20:01] **Religion and the American Revolution**

[事实] Dominic asks whether the American Revolution resembled the English Civil War in its fears of tyranny, absolutism, and Catholic power.
[事实] Preston says those ideas were present, though not always logically consistent, especially among slaveholders who described themselves as enslaved by Britain.
[事实] He says religion helped intensify the ideological claims behind the Revolution, but it was less prevalent than in the English Civil War because many founders were not conventionally religious.
[推测] The Revolution is presented as a case where religious assumptions remained influential even when leaders did not use overtly religious language as much as earlier Protestants had.

[22:17] **Civil War as Humanitarian Intervention**

[事实] After the break, the discussion turns to the American Civil War as the most brutal war fought on North American soil.
[事实] Preston argues that the Civil War became America's first war of humanitarian intervention.
[事实] He says most white northerners were anti-slavery at the war's outbreak for political and economic reasons rather than moral abolitionist ones.
[事实] As the war developed, Lincoln and others fused preserving the Union with ending slavery as a moral crusade.
[事实] Later American wars, including the Spanish-American War and the First World War, consciously used the Civil War as a model for righteous humanitarian war.

[24:44] **Lincoln's Religious Language**

[事实] Preston says Lincoln is difficult to pin down religiously and showed limited evidence of religious rhetoric before becoming president.
[事实] During the Civil War, biblical cadence, scriptural imagery, and religious language became central to Lincoln's speeches.
[事实] Frederick Douglass regarded Lincoln's second inaugural address as resembling a great sermon.
[事实] Tom suggests that American culture was so saturated in Christian assumptions that even non-conventional believers reflected them.
[推测] Lincoln's example shows how civil religion can shape political speech without requiring straightforward personal orthodoxy.

[26:43] **Protestant America and Civil Religion**

[事实] The speakers discuss whether different denominations had distinct foreign-policy views in 19th-century America.
[事实] Preston says there was an American civil religion, but until World War II the United States was fundamentally a Protestant nation.
[事实] Catholics, Jews, Mormons, and other groups had to conform to a Protestant norm.
[事实] Preston cites Franklin Roosevelt's observation that Jews and Catholics lived in the United States at the sufferance of Protestants, while also noting Roosevelt's pluralism in appointments.
[推测] The discussion frames Protestantism as the dominant cultural grammar of American public life rather than merely one denomination among others.

[29:18] **Mexico, Cuba, and the Philippines**

[事实] The speakers note that American wars against Mexico, Spain, Cuba, and the Philippines involved Catholic powers or Catholic populations.
[事实] Preston says religion did not single-handedly cause these wars, but it shaped how they were fought and justified.
[事实] He describes the Mexican-American War as a major land grab.
[事实] The Spanish-American War began over Cuba, while the Philippines became an American possession after the U.S. destroyed the Spanish fleet at Manila.
[事实] McKinley rejected Filipino independence and later said he prayed for guidance before deciding to take, educate, uplift, civilize, and Christianize the Philippines.

[32:18] **McKinley, Protestant Empire, and Historical Interpretation**

[事实] Preston argues that historians often dismissed McKinley's religious explanation as false, but he says McKinley's devout Methodist faith makes it plausible.
[事实] He explains that McKinley knew Filipinos were Catholic; by "Christianize," he meant making them proper Protestants.
[事实] Kipling's "White Man's Burden" is discussed as a response to American annexation of the Philippines.
[事实] Preston says historians too easily write religion out of imperial history by reducing motives to markets, strategy, or geopolitics.
[推测] The episode's method is to restore religion as one genuine motive among several, without denying material interests.

[36:23] **America's Uneasy Relationship with Empire**

[事实] Tom contrasts British willingness to call itself an empire with American reluctance to acknowledge imperial power.
[事实] Preston says American denial of empire is factually false, especially in light of the Philippines.
[事实] He notes that in 1899 and 1900 Americans openly debated empire, with imperialists and anti-imperialists using those labels.
[事实] The United States then fought a brutal war to suppress Filipino resistance after Filipinos objected to being dominated by their former allies.
[推测] American anti-imperial self-image made empire harder to remember and easier to disavow.

[40:04] **Forgetting Empire and Fighting Other Empires**

[事实] Preston says Americans did not consciously put empire down so much as lose interest in it.
[事实] Few American settlers or officials wanted to go to places such as the Philippines, which became a sideshow.
[事实] The unhappy Philippine experience reduced appetite for further territorial seizure.
[事实] Later American foreign policy was shaped by wars against empires: Nazi, Japanese, and Soviet.
[事实] Ronald Reagan's 1983 phrase "evil empire" is presented as part of this anti-imperial tradition.
[推测] By defining enemies as empires, America strengthened its own belief that it was not imperial.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode is valuable because it treats religion neither as a cynical excuse nor as the sole cause of American war. Its strength is showing how religious belief, civil religion, Protestant culture, moral anxiety, and material interests interacted across several centuries.

[推测] A highlight is Andrew Preston's insistence that historians should take religious motives seriously on their own terms, especially in the case of McKinley and the Philippines. The discussion also benefits from connecting colonial violence, abolitionism, Manifest Destiny, the Civil War, and anti-imperial rhetoric into one long-running argument.

[推测] The limitation is that this part of the discussion stops before the 20th-century cases promised at the end, including the First World War, the post-9/11 world, and Trump-era politics. It is therefore best suited to listeners interested in the early and 19th-century foundations of American foreign-policy language rather than a complete modern survey.
