# The Fines: What Remains After 9/11

## Overview

This episode investigates “the fines,” hundreds of thousands of tons of dirt and dust left after material from the World Trade Center was sifted at New York City’s Fresh Kills landfill. Although officials considered the fines an unusable residue, some victims’ families believed they contained human remains and therefore deserved a dignified burial.

The story follows Diane and Kurt Horning, whose son Matthew died in the North Tower. Their campaign to move the fines exposes a conflict between forensic evidence, administrative decisions, burial rights, and the emotional significance families attach to even the smallest possible trace of the dead.

The episode ultimately asks when a body becomes dust—and who has the authority to decide. Scientific distinctions between remains and ordinary matter may be necessary, but they cannot fully resolve the cultural and emotional obligations people feel toward those they have lost.

## 分段落总结

[01:14] **A mystery beneath Freshkills Park**

[事实] Freshkills Park occupies land that was once the world’s largest landfill, closed in the 1990s under Mayor Rudy Giuliani.

[事实] The landfill reopened after September 11, 2001, to receive and sort material from the destroyed World Trade Center.

[事实] One section of the present-day park remains inaccessible because material from the recovery operation is buried there.

[推测] The park’s transformation from landfill to green space cannot erase the unresolved question of whether it is also a burial place.

[02:52] **An unprecedented promise to identify the dead**

[事实] Chief Medical Examiner Charles Hirsch was injured at Ground Zero but organized his office to handle the expected casualties.

[事实] Hirsch realized that the towers’ collapse had pulverized both buildings and bodies, meaning investigators would encounter thousands of fragments rather than intact bodies.

[事实] He promised that authorities would not rest until all recovered remains were identified.

[事实] DNA techniques developed through work on degraded remains from the Balkan Wars had become available just as 9/11 occurred.

[推测] Hirsch’s promise offered hope but also created expectations that science could identify and return every physical trace of every victim.

[06:15] **Matthew Horning and the meaning of personal remains**

[事实] Matthew Horning was a 26-year-old database administrator working on the 95th floor of the North Tower.

[事实] His parents described him as funny, modest, close to his family, interested in fantasy writing, and newly enrolled in a writing class scheduled for September 12.

[事实] Some of Matthew’s possessions, including his wallet and its contents, were recovered at Fresh Kills.

[事实] His mother, Diane, saw building fragments, a fire hose, cloth, and shoes during visits to the site, despite being told that everything larger than a quarter inch had been sifted out.

[推测] The recovered belongings made Fresh Kills personally significant to the Hornings and deepened their suspicion that the search had missed material connected to their son.

[10:16] **The recovery operation at Fresh Kills**

[事实] Fresh Kills reopened in the early hours of September 12, 2001, and became an active crime scene and the central sorting site for World Trade Center material.

[事实] Its city ownership, security, marine access, and approximately 170 acres of flat working space made it suitable for the operation.

[事实] Workers initially searched by hand and later used machines that separated pieces down to a quarter inch; material passing through the screens was classified as dirt, dust, or leftovers.

[事实] Human remains were sent to the medical examiner, while personal effects such as wallets, identification cards, and watches were also recovered.

[事实] Workers operated around the clock under traumatic conditions, often encountering human remains without having been trained for such work.

[13:41] **The creation of “the fines”**

[事实] By July 2002, officials considered the Fresh Kills operation complete after sorting approximately 1.5 million tons of material.

[事实] “The fines” was the name given to the unidentified dirt and dust that passed through the quarter-inch sifters.

[事实] The fines were deposited in a cordoned-off area of roughly 45 acres on the western side of the landfill.

[事实] City officials did not regard them as garbage, but they also concluded that nothing more could be salvaged from them.

[推测] Calling the material “fines” provided a technical category that separated it administratively from recognizable human remains, even though that distinction did not satisfy grieving families.

[14:56] **The Hornings organize for a proper burial**

[事实] Diane Horning believed the city had treated her son like garbage by leaving possible remains in a landfill.

[事实] The Hornings helped form WTC Families for a Proper Burial, which demanded that the fines be moved to a more dignified location.

[事实] The group met regularly, attended planning meetings, protested, and spoke directly with public officials, including Mayor Michael Bloomberg.

[事实] When Bloomberg said burial did not matter to him because he planned to donate his body to science, the Hornings replied that this was his choice and that families also deserved a choice.

[推测] Their campaign reframed the dispute from a technical cleanup decision into a question of family agency and respect for the dead.

[17:29] **Missed remains and unfulfilled assurances**

[事实] Years after the official Ground Zero recovery ended, additional human remains were discovered around the site, including on the Deutsche Bank building and in a manhole.

[事实] These discoveries produced more than a thousand bone fragments and other remains, demonstrating that some areas had been missed.

[事实] A New Jersey bill proposed moving the fines into containers at Ground Zero, but it did not advance.

[事实] Estimates placed the fines between 360,000 and 480,000 tons—roughly a football field in length and 200 feet high.

[事实] Officials made assurances that the material would not be forgotten, but the Hornings were repeatedly referred among different agencies without obtaining the result they sought.

[推测] Later discoveries near Ground Zero strengthened the families’ belief that the Fresh Kills search could also have been incomplete.

[20:52] **Why the treatment of the dead matters**

[事实] Cultural historian Thomas Laqueur recounts how the ancient philosopher Diogenes argued that his corpse could simply be discarded because, once dead, he would be unable to care what happened to it.

[事实] Laqueur observes that human societies have nevertheless consistently treated dead bodies as meaningful and used air, fire, water, or earth to dispose of them deliberately.

[事实] Religious relics, churchyard burial, cemeteries, cremation, open caskets, and scattered ashes all reflect the importance people assign to the location and treatment of the dead.

[推测] The historical discussion shows why a materially logical argument—dust is only dust—cannot settle a dispute rooted in ritual, memory, and human dignity.

[23:23] **The city refuses to move the fines**

[事实] The families’ preferred solution was to move the fines to Ground Zero and incorporate them into the memorial; alternatives included Governors Island or a Fresh Kills site not located above garbage.

[事实] In 2004, the city explicitly decided that the fines would remain at Fresh Kills.

[事实] Former landfill superintendent Dennis Diggins said officials treated the fines as another product of the processed material and never seriously considered moving them elsewhere.

[事实] WTC Families for a Proper Burial sued the city in 2005, alleging violations of due process and New York burial law.

[事实] The judge ruled that the plaintiffs could not establish a constitutionally protected right to an “unidentifiable mass of dirt,” particularly because they could not prove that specific relatives’ remains were present in it.

[推测] The legal system required individual identification where the families’ claim depended on collective uncertainty, making their central concern extremely difficult to prove.

[26:21] **Identification continues, but closure remains elusive**

[事实] Nearly 3,000 people died in the attacks, and more than 20,000 fragments of human remains were recovered.

[事实] Some families received repeated notifications as additional fragments of their relatives were identified.

[事实] More than 1,000 victims still had no identified remains at the time described in the episode.

[事实] In 2025, improved forensic methods enabled scientists to identify three additional 9/11 victims.

[事实] The Hornings received and buried several small fragments of Matthew, but they did not consider those fragments sufficient.

[推测] Continuing advances in DNA analysis preserve hope while also preventing the identification process from reaching a clear endpoint.

[27:31] **A daily symbol of an unfinished fight**

[事实] Diane and Kurt wear memorial pins every day and have vowed to continue until Matthew is no longer in what they call “the dump.”

[事实] The fines remain at Fresh Kills, where the city is developing a park and planning a 9/11 memorial.

[事实] The Hornings say they will never visit that memorial.

[推测] The planned memorial cannot provide reconciliation for families who believe its underlying location embodies the original harm.

[28:19] **The boundary between a body and dust**

[事实] Laqueur described taking dirt from a flowerbed where his father’s ashes had been scattered and placing it on his grandfather’s grave in Germany.

[事实] He acknowledged that the dirt was physically mixed with fertilizer and that his scientifically minded father would have considered the gesture irrational.

[事实] Despite that knowledge, Laqueur found the act emotionally meaningful because it felt as though he had reunited his father and grandfather.

[事实] The episode concludes that people continue to care for the dead even when they understand that a fragment or handful of earth has no unique material quality.

[推测] The fines occupy a transitional category: they can be simultaneously ordinary dust and the possible remains of human beings, depending on whether they are viewed materially, legally, or emotionally.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s chief strength is its careful presentation of a dispute with no emotionally neutral solution. It gives substantial attention both to the enormous, respectful recovery effort at Fresh Kills and to the families who experienced its final classification system as a profound betrayal.

Its most effective move is connecting forensic procedure and litigation to the broader history of caring for the dead. The discussion of Diogenes and Laqueur’s father shows that the meaning of remains does not depend solely on whether science can distinguish them from ordinary matter.

[推测] The episode’s limitation is that the city’s position is represented primarily through operational and legal explanations; it offers less detail about the full policy deliberations behind the decision not to relocate the fines. That leaves some practical and financial dimensions of the dispute underexplored.

[推测] This episode will be especially valuable to listeners interested in 9/11’s aftermath, forensic identification, public memory, burial ethics, and the tension between institutional categories and private grief.
