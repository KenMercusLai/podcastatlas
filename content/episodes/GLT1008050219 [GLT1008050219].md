# Prussia, Germany, and the Birth of a Nation

## 概览

This episode discusses Prussia, the creation of Germany, and the long shadow cast by German history from the 19th century to the world wars. Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook speak with historian Katja Hoyer, whose book *Blood and Iron* focuses on Kaiser-era Germany.

The central argument is that Germany was neither simply an artificial invention nor an inevitable nation-state. Hoyer presents German unity as something imagined by liberals, philosophers, middle classes, and ordinary people, but brought into being in 1871 through Bismarck’s wars and political audacity.

The conversation repeatedly returns to the tension between Prussia’s militarism and its overlooked liberal, tolerant, intellectual traditions. It also explores how later Nazi propaganda, British wartime rhetoric, and the trauma of the Second World War shaped modern perceptions of Prussia and Germany.

## 分段落总结

[00:48] **The Episode’s Scope: Prussia and Modern Germany**

[事实] The hosts introduce the subject as Prussia, Germany, and the birth of the nation that would become the most powerful European state of the 20th century.

[事实] Dominic argues that the most interesting period is from roughly 1870 to the Second World War, rather than the earliest history of Prussia.

[事实] Katja Hoyer is introduced as the guest, a German-born historian based in Britain and author of *Blood and Iron*.

[02:49] **What Prussia Was**

[事实] Hoyer explains that Prussia is difficult to define because its territory was historically scattered and ethnically mixed.

[事实] She locates its origins in the Baltic region and what is now northern Poland, while Brandenburg was in what is now northern Germany.

[事实] The name came from the original Prussian peoples, who were conquered by the Teutonic Knights; later Germanic settlers were not the same as the original Prussians.

[事实] Prussia became a kingdom in 1701, but even Frederick the Great had to encourage settlement to create a more cohesive German-populated territory.

[06:27] **Was Germany Real or Invented?**

[事实] Hoyer says that liberal and democratic Germans in the 19th century often did see Germany as real, especially after the wars against Napoleon.

[事实] The idea of Germany was partly grounded in language, summarized by the notion that Germany existed wherever the German tongue was heard.

[事实] At the same time, rulers and elites in Prussia, Bavaria, the Rhineland, Hamburg, and other states often had strong interests in preserving their separate powers.

[推测] The episode presents German identity as both imagined from below and politically resisted from above before 1871.

[08:39] **The Holy Roman Empire and the Second Reich**

[事实] The hosts connect the absence of an earlier German nation-state to the long history of German lands within the Holy Roman Empire.

[事实] Hoyer says the Second Reich tried to create continuity with the Holy Roman Empire but was much more centralized and nation-state-like.

[事实] She contrasts the Holy Roman Empire’s loose structure with post-1871 Germany, where there was a stronger expectation that member states would pull together.

[10:27] **Blood, Iron, and the Problem of 1871**

[事实] Hoyer rejects a deterministic Sonderweg theory that Germany was doomed from the beginning.

[事实] She still argues that the 1871 German state had an inherent problem because its creation was based on “blood and iron.”

[事实] Bismarck himself said as late as 1868 that German unification was unlikely within the century, only three years before he achieved it.

[事实] Hoyer says conquest was necessary at that point to bring southern states such as Bavaria and Baden into the new German state.

[12:42] **Philosophers, Nationalism, and Economic Interest**

[事实] Tom suggests that Germany also imagined itself through philosophy, Romantic nationalism, Kant, Hegel, the Brothers Grimm, forests, soil, and culture.

[事实] Hoyer agrees and identifies a dualism between soldiers and philosophers, or between elite statecraft and liberal-national ideas.

[事实] She says middle-class support for unification was also tied to economic interests.

[推测] The discussion implies that German unification would probably have happened eventually, but Bismarck’s wars determined its timing and form.

[14:55] **When Ordinary People Became German**

[事实] Dominic asks when ordinary Germans began to identify as Germans rather than primarily as Bavarians, Württembergers, Prussians, or others.

[事实] Hoyer traces this process to the Napoleonic Wars, especially calls by Frederick William III for Prussians and then Germans to unite.

[事实] The Battle of Leipzig and volunteer forces helped create a sense that Germans could act together against Napoleon.

[事实] The 1840 French scare produced demonstrations across German lands in defense of the Rhine, showing ordinary people identifying with a broader German cause.

[18:38] **Austria’s Ambiguous Place**

[事实] Dominic asks how Austria fits into German identity, noting that Hitler was Austrian.

[事实] Hoyer says Hitler was aware of this and trained himself to speak with a strong Bavarian accent.

[事实] Austria was difficult to include because the Austro-Hungarian Empire contained many non-German peoples, languages, and cultures.

[事实] The rivalry between Austria and Prussia could not easily have been solved within a single German unit.

[21:29] **Prussia as Modern Upstart**

[事实] Tom asks whether older German regions, such as cities along the Rhine, saw Prussia as an upstart.

[事实] Hoyer says Bismarck himself used the metaphor of Prussia as a modern frigate compared with Austria as an older, grander, slower ship about to sink.

[事实] She argues that Prussia used its relative lack of ancient prestige to present itself as modern, industrialized, and efficient.

[23:04] **Industrial Power and the Rhineland**

[事实] Germany’s rapid industrial catch-up worried Britain, especially in areas such as steel, coal, military production, and naval expansion.

[事实] Hoyer says Prussia’s acquisition of the Rhineland in 1815 gave it coal, iron, and other resources that later helped make it an industrial superpower.

[事实] She rejects the idea that Britain was to blame for later German catastrophes through the post-Napoleonic settlement.

[事实] At the time, Prussia appeared to be a useful continental counterweight to French power.

[25:43] **Prussian Militarism and Its Limits**

[事实] Hoyer says Prussian militarism was real but culturally different from militarism elsewhere.

[事实] She links it to the Landwehr and volunteer units of the Napoleonic Wars, which embedded military service more deeply in society.

[事实] Prussia’s vulnerable location in central Europe and memories of devastation, including the Thirty Years’ War and Napoleonic Wars, encouraged a belief that the people had to defend the state.

[事实] Hoyer also stresses that Prussia was liberal and tolerant in important ways, especially in religious tolerance and cultural life.

[29:52] **Prussia, Nazism, and Historical Memory**

[事实] Tom asks whether Prussia would be remembered differently without the catastrophe of Nazi Germany.

[事实] Hoyer says modern views partly fall into the trap of Nazi propaganda, because Hitler deliberately drew lines of continuity from the Third Reich back to the Second Reich and earlier Prussia.

[事实] She cites the Day of Potsdam as an example of Hitler publicly using Prussian imagery and Hindenburg’s approval to claim legitimacy.

[事实] Hoyer notes the irony that Prussia had already been largely abolished through the 1932 Prussian coup before its formal abolition in 1947.

[33:03] **Prussian Duty and Opposition to Hitler**

[事实] Tom notes that military opposition to Hitler also had Prussian elements.

[事实] Hoyer says Prussian officers’ sense of obedience and duty contributed both to loyalty to the state and to later opposition.

[事实] She connects this dual legacy to both the devastation of the Second World War and the July Plot of 1944.

[推测] The conversation presents Prussian military ethics as morally ambiguous rather than simply authoritarian or heroic.

[34:08] **Germany and Responsibility for the First World War**

[事实] Dominic asks whether Germany bears major responsibility for the First World War.

[事实] Hoyer does not say Germany deliberately caused a world war, but argues that German leaders saw conflict as useful for managing internal divisions.

[事实] She says Germany faced parliamentary stagnation, swelling trade unions, and a sense of internal crisis around 1912 to 1914.

[事实] Hoyer argues that Wilhelm and the military wanted a war-like crisis, but expected something more limited, like 1870 or a Balkan conflict.

[37:21] **Wilhelm II’s Responsibility and Character**

[事实] Hoyer agrees that Wilhelm II can be seen as a somewhat pitiful or comic figure, but says this does not absolve him of responsibility.

[事实] As head of state and commander-in-chief, ultimate responsibility rested with him.

[事实] She says Wilhelm lacked the intellectual capacity and patience to understand the wider picture.

[事实] She also highlights his fascination with military technology, ships, and new weapons as a psychological factor in his attitude toward war.

[39:26] **Bismarck and the Great Man Question**

[事实] The hosts ask whether Bismarck illustrates the great man theory of history.

[事实] Hoyer says Bismarck is one of the strongest possible examples of that theory because it is hard to see events unfolding the same way without him.

[事实] She describes how Bismarck defied Parliament over army reform and governed for years without proper parliamentary budget approval.

[事实] She argues that his audacity in provoking France was crucial to the Franco-Prussian War and German unification in 1871.

[44:14] **Germany’s Relationship with Its Past**

[事实] Dominic asks whether ordinary Germans still view their past through trauma and guilt.

[事实] Hoyer describes the Second World War as a black hole in German history that sucks in everything before and after it.

[事实] She says German education repeatedly returns to Nazism, while events such as the 150th anniversary of 1871 receive little public commemoration.

[事实] She says figures such as Bismarck and Wilhelm II remain difficult for Germans to handle because any commemoration requires interpretation in light of later wars.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value lies in how it complicates familiar images of Prussia and Germany. Rather than reducing Prussia to militarism or Germany to a straight road toward Nazism, it shows the mixture of war, liberal nationalism, philosophy, economic interest, statecraft, and memory that shaped German unification.

A major strength is Katja Hoyer’s ability to explain big historical processes through clear contrasts: Prussia and Austria, soldiers and philosophers, nationalism from below and power politics from above, Prussian tolerance and Prussian militarism. The discussion is strongest when it resists simple blame narratives.

Its limitation is that several huge topics, especially Weimar Germany, colonial ambitions, and the detailed causes of the First World War, are only touched briefly. [推测] Listeners wanting a full chronological history of Prussia may find the episode more thematic than comprehensive.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in European history, nationalism, Bismarck, and the historical roots of modern Germany, especially those who want to understand Prussia without viewing it only through the lens of the Second World War.
