# Crazy Horse After Little Bighorn: Surrender, Betrayal, and Sitting Bull’s Exile

## 概览

This episode follows the aftermath of the Battle of the Little Bighorn. The hosts argue that the Lakota and their allies won a stunning battlefield victory, but that the victory triggered a far more forceful American military and political response.

The discussion centers on how U.S. authorities used Custer’s defeat to intensify the reservation policy, seize the Black Hills, divide Native leaders, and recruit Native auxiliaries against holdouts such as Sitting Bull and Crazy Horse.

Crazy Horse’s story becomes the emotional core of the episode: his failed attempt to find spiritual guidance, surrender at Fort Robinson, worsening isolation, mistranslation and suspicion, and eventual killing while resisting imprisonment. The episode closes by following Sitting Bull to Canada, then back into U.S. custody and eventual return to Standing Rock.

## 分段落总结

[01:35] **Lucille Clifton and Crazy Horse as a Symbol**

[事实] The episode opens with lines from Lucille Clifton’s poem “The Death of Crazy Horse.”

[事实] The hosts say Crazy Horse had become a legend of resistance to white America, especially in the 1970s and 1980s.

[事实] They connect Crazy Horse, Wounded Knee, and the broader fate of the Plains Indians as linked symbols in the story.

[推测] The poem is used to frame the episode less as a battlefield narrative and more as a tragedy about memory, resistance, and dispossession.

[03:53] **Victory at Little Bighorn Becomes Disaster**

[事实] The hosts return to the immediate aftermath of Little Bighorn, describing it as a great victory for the Lakota and their allies.

[事实] They say the Native forces soon split up after moving about 150 miles away, partly because there were not enough buffalo to feed everyone.

[事实] The hosts argue the Lakota had no viable strategic endgame against the scale of American power.

[推测] The split after Little Bighorn appears as an understandable but fatal decision because it made sustained resistance even harder.

[06:01] **The U.S. Military Response Hardens**

[事实] Sherman and Sheridan used Custer’s defeat to demand more soldiers, more forts, and military control over Indian agencies.

[事实] Congress provided more money, recruited new volunteers known as “Custer Avengers,” and gave some agencies to military control.

[事实] The hosts say the U.S. effectively weaponized Custer’s defeat to ignore previous treaties and escalate the campaign.

[推测] Little Bighorn gave the army the political leverage it had long wanted.

[07:57] **Grant’s New Terms and the Loss of the Black Hills**

[事实] In August 1876, President Grant signed a law offering more rations to reservation Indians while requiring them to give up roaming, hunting, and claims to unceded lands.

[事实] The Black Hills and a third of the Great Sioux Reservation were lost under this settlement.

[事实] Red Cloud and Spotted Tail signed despite objecting that food was being used as blackmail.

[推测] The hosts present this as the point where land loss and the destruction of a way of life become inseparable.

[10:31] **Divide-and-Rule on the Reservations**

[事实] U.S. authorities deposed Red Cloud as the leading chief and elevated Spotted Tail.

[事实] Spotted Tail’s followers were allowed to keep their guns, while Red Cloud’s followers had to give theirs up.

[事实] Reservation Lakotas and warriors from several tribes were recruited to fight against Sitting Bull, Crazy Horse, and other holdouts.

[推测] The policy deliberately turned Native divisions into a tool of U.S. military control.

[12:30] **Nelson Miles and the Winter Pursuit**

[事实] Sheridan brought in Nelson A. Miles, whom the hosts describe as ambitious, effective, ruthless, and better equipped than earlier commanders.

[事实] Miles led a winter campaign and pursued Sitting Bull across the plains.

[事实] On 20 October 1876, Miles overtook Sitting Bull’s encampment in southern Montana and held talks with him.

[推测] Miles represents a more methodical version of the aggressive frontier commander Custer had wanted to be.

[14:15] **Negotiating with Sitting Bull**

[事实] Miles told Sitting Bull that he had to return to the reservation and could no longer roam.

[事实] Sitting Bull refused, saying he was not made to be an agency Indian.

[事实] The hosts note that U.S. officers could pursue ruthless policy while still showing some respect for Native leaders.

[推测] The episode complicates a simple moral division by emphasizing distrust of politicians alongside some mutual recognition between soldiers and Native leaders.

[17:04] **Hunger, Winter, and Fragmentation**

[事实] Miles observed that Sitting Bull seemed depressed and that many of his people wanted peace.

[事实] As winter came, game became scarce, morale declined, and some Native people surrendered.

[事实] Miles and Crook continued relentless pursuit through snow and blizzards.

[推测] Hunger and cold are presented as more decisive than battlefield defeat in breaking the holdouts’ resistance.

[18:31] **The Northern Cheyenne and the Destruction of Heritage**

[事实] Crook caught a group of Northern Cheyenne at the Dull Knife Fight in November, and their village was destroyed.

[事实] The hosts say the village contained war bonnets, buffalo hides, holy relics, and much of their heritage.

[事实] After surrendering, the Northern Cheyenne were sent to a reservation in Oklahoma, where they were badly treated.

[推测] The destruction of material culture is presented as an attack on memory and identity as well as on military capacity.

[21:37] **Wolf Mountain and Sitting Bull’s Move to Canada**

[事实] By early 1877, Sitting Bull and Crazy Horse were the main remaining leaders still outside reservation control.

[事实] In January 1877, Miles confronted Crazy Horse’s band at the Battle of Wolf Mountain in extreme winter conditions.

[事实] Crazy Horse’s attack failed, and the hosts say his “medicine” or momentum seemed gone.

[事实] Sitting Bull decided to go to Canada, which he called the grandmother’s land.

[推测] Wolf Mountain marks the collapse of the heroic momentum created by Little Bighorn.

[23:37] **Crazy Horse’s Vision Quest**

[事实] Crazy Horse did not follow Sitting Bull to Canada and instead became quieter while his followers argued.

[事实] On 5 March 1877, he and his wife packed their tipi and went into the wilderness for a vision quest.

[事实] After fasting and praying, he received no guidance and did not pretend otherwise.

[推测] His refusal to invent a vision reinforces the hosts’ view of him as a man of integrity.

[26:29] **Surrender at Fort Robinson**

[事实] Crazy Horse returned to find that his people had decided to give up because they lacked clothing and saw no point in continuing.

[事实] On 6 May 1877, Crazy Horse rode into Fort Robinson and surrendered.

[事实] Lieutenant William Philo Clark received him, smoked a peace pipe with him, and communicated through sign language.

[事实] Crazy Horse had expected his own agency west of the Black Hills and permission for a buffalo hunt, but the hosts say those promises were not going to be honored.

[推测] The surrender is framed as a turning point where formal peace depended on promises the U.S. government was unlikely to keep.

[30:03] **Restlessness, Isolation, and Omens**

[事实] Crazy Horse refused an invitation to go to Washington to meet President Rutherford B. Hayes.

[事实] Officers described him as wanting to do the right thing but being unsure what that meant.

[事实] Other Native figures whispered against him, mocked him, and treated him as a rival.

[事实] Crazy Horse saw a dead eagle and later dreamed that he died surrounded by enemies, but not from a bullet.

[推测] The episode portrays Crazy Horse as psychologically trapped between surrender, suspicion, and the loss of his old world.

[32:30] **The Nez Perce Mistranslation**

[事实] Crook wanted Crazy Horse to help the U.S. Army in a campaign against the Nez Perce.

[事实] Crazy Horse questioned why he was being asked to fight again after being told the fighting was over.

[事实] Interpreter Frank Grouard translated Crazy Horse as saying he would fight until every white man was killed, though the hosts say Crazy Horse had meant the northern enemy being discussed.

[推测] The hosts suggest Grouard may have deliberately betrayed Crazy Horse rather than merely mistranslated him.

[35:43] **Panic, Red Cloud, and Flight**

[事实] After the mistranslation, American officers began to fear that Crazy Horse had gone mad and might break out.

[事实] Sheridan told Crook to resolve the situation.

[事实] Red Cloud told Crook that Crazy Horse should be killed, but Crook decided he would arrest him instead.

[事实] On 4 September, Crazy Horse fled Fort Robinson and went to the Spotted Tail agency.

[推测] The crisis shows how rumor, rivalry, and official fear combined to make Crazy Horse’s position impossible.

[37:00] **Humiliation at Spotted Tail Agency**

[事实] Crazy Horse asked Spotted Tail, his uncle, for a home at the agency.

[事实] Spotted Tail humiliated him publicly and insisted that he himself was the chief there.

[事实] Lieutenant Jesse Lee described Crazy Horse as frightened, trembling, and uncertain whether to trust anyone.

[事实] Crazy Horse told officers he wanted no trouble, wanted to leave Red Cloud, and felt his brain was in a whirl.

[推测] The episode presents this as Crazy Horse’s social and political isolation reaching its lowest point.

[39:17] **Return to Fort Robinson and the Arrest Plan**

[事实] Crazy Horse returned to Fort Robinson the next day with Lieutenant Lee escorting him.

[事实] Lee noticed Red Cloud’s warriors, some in war paint, and many cavalrymen with rifles.

[事实] Colonel Bradley told Lee that orders from Sheridan required Crazy Horse to be taken to Chicago and then to an island off Florida.

[推测] For Crazy Horse, being removed far from the plains would have represented a form of living death.

[40:54] **The Killing of Crazy Horse**

[事实] Little Big Man and Captain Kennington led Crazy Horse toward the guardhouse.

[事实] Crazy Horse saw prisoners shackled inside and said he did not want to go in because it was where prisoners were kept.

[事实] He tried to break free, pulled a hidden knife, and cut Little Big Man’s arm.

[事实] A private bayoneted Crazy Horse in the back, piercing his kidney and lung, and he was carried into an office.

[推测] His death fulfilled the earlier dream in the sense that he died violently but was not shot.

[42:38] **The Floor, the Cot, and His Last Words**

[事实] Crazy Horse refused to lie on a cot and asked to be put on the floor.

[事实] He lingered for hours and said he did not know why he had been stabbed.

[事实] He said the white men were not to blame and that Indians had betrayed him.

[事实] He died just before midnight, with the hosts giving his last words as wanting only to be left alone.

[推测] The hosts treat his choice of the floor as an act of final dignity and refusal to become an object of white pity.

[45:19] **Sitting Bull in Canada**

[事实] Sitting Bull went to Canada and got on well with the Mounties.

[事实] The hosts say he contrasted Canada favorably with the United States.

[事实] While he was away, Americans told false stories about him, including claims that he knew French, studied Napoleon, or was a white West Point graduate named Bison McLean.

[事实] Canada did not really want him, buffalo were becoming scarce there too, and local Native peoples competed with him for depleted resources.

[推测] Canada offered temporary refuge but not a sustainable future.

[48:00] **Sitting Bull’s Surrender and Captivity**

[事实] By early 1881, only the weak and elderly remained with Sitting Bull, and they survived on limited food and handouts.

[事实] Sitting Bull returned to America and surrendered in July 1881.

[事实] He gave his rifle to one of his sons to surrender and spoke about wanting the boy to learn how to live among Americans.

[事实] Instead of being sent to Standing Rock as promised, he was taken on the steamboat General Sherman to Fort Randall as a prisoner of war.

[推测] The hosts present his surrender as another example of promises being broken.

[49:55] **Fort Randall, Release, and the Next Chapter**

[事实] At Fort Randall, Sitting Bull drew pictograms of battles, fights, and buffalo hunts.

[事实] The hosts cite an estimate that the U.S. government spent about a million dollars for each Indian killed during the Indian Wars.

[事实] In 1883, Sitting Bull was released and traveled by steamboat toward Standing Rock.

[事实] At stops along the Missouri, people treated him as a celebrity, sought his autograph, and paid him.

[事实] He arrived at Standing Rock in May 1883, and the hosts preview future topics including the Ghost Dance, Wounded Knee, and Buffalo Bill.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its tight focus on the tragic aftermath of victory: Little Bighorn is not treated as the climax of Native resistance, but as the trigger for a harsher and more systematic American response.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the accounts of Crazy Horse’s surrender, isolation, mistranslation, and death. The hosts combine military chronology with attention to symbolism, especially the refusal to lie on the cot.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode relies heavily on dramatic narration and quoted descriptions, so listeners looking for a more source-critical or Native-language perspective may want additional context.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in the Plains Wars, Crazy Horse and Sitting Bull, the politics of reservation policy, and history told through character-driven tragedy.
