# Whey Protein vs. Bone Broth and Collagen Protein

## 概览

This AMA excerpt centers on whether bone broth or collagen protein offers a distinct health advantage over whey protein. The main conclusion is context-dependent: whey protein is presented as the stronger choice for muscle protein synthesis, strength, recovery, and high-quality dietary protein intake, largely because of its leucine content and amino acid profile.

Bone broth and collagen protein are framed as less ideal for muscle-building goals but potentially useful for skin health and appearance. Huberman cites evidence that collagen intake may support skin elasticity and appearance, especially around 15 grams per day over two weeks or more.

The discussion also adds practical caveats: total daily protein should still come mostly from whole foods when possible, collagen should count toward total protein intake but not be treated as equivalent to whey for muscle goals, and some people may find whey or high-leucine foods worsen acne.

[事实] The AMA question asks whether bone broth or collagen protein has a distinct health advantage compared with whey protein.

[事实] Huberman says not all proteins are equal; they differ in bioavailability and amino acid content.

[事实] He identifies leucine as an important essential amino acid that must come from food.

[事实] Whey protein, beef, chicken, and eggs are described as relatively high in leucine compared with collagen protein and some bone broths.

[05:09] **Whey Protein for Muscle Protein Synthesis**

[事实] Huberman says whey protein is superior when the goal is muscle growth, strength, muscle repair, or muscle protein synthesis.

[事实] He explains that leucine-rich dietary protein can support muscle protein synthesis even without intense exercise.

[事实] He says high-quality, bioavailable, leucine-rich protein is especially useful when doing resistance training near or to failure.

[推测] The practical takeaway is that whey is the better protein choice when the main goal is training adaptation or preserving lean tissue.

[07:06] **Bone Broth and Collagen for Skin Health**

[事实] Huberman says bone broth and collagen protein are not without value.

[事实] He says some data show regular collagen protein intake can improve skin elasticity and appearance.

[事实] He describes these effects as not dramatic, but statistically significant in some studies compared with control conditions.

[事实] He says amino acids in bone broth and collagen protein have been shown to support skin elasticity and appearance at about 15 grams per day over roughly two weeks or more.

[08:52] **Why Whey Does Not Replace Collagen for Skin**

[事实] Huberman says whey protein has not been shown, at least in studies he is aware of, to directly improve skin health and appearance in the same way as collagen or bone broth.

[事实] He says whey can still support recovery, muscle protein synthesis, immune health, and general vigor.

[事实] He notes that other high-leucine proteins can also support muscle protein synthesis.

[推测] The distinction is not that one protein is universally better, but that each has a different strongest use case.

[10:04] **Daily Protein Targets and Food Sources**

[事实] Huberman says many people seeking muscle protein synthesis, exercise recovery, and general health may do well with about one gram of quality protein per pound of lean body weight or desired body weight.

[事实] He says a looser version is about one gram of quality protein per pound of body weight per day.

[事实] He suggests getting roughly 60-70% of protein from whole foods such as lean meats, chicken, eggs, fish, or suitable vegetarian combinations.

[事实] He says the remaining 30%, or up to 50% if needed, can come from protein powders or bars, with whey as an excellent option.

[13:21] **How Collagen Counts Toward Total Protein**

[事实] Huberman says bone broth and collagen protein count toward total daily protein intake.

[事实] He cautions that collagen protein does not scale well when evaluated as a high-quality, bioavailable, high-leucine protein source.

[事实] He says people interested in skin health and appearance could add 15 grams of collagen powder per day or consume bone broth.

[14:22] **Whey, Leucine, mTOR, and Acne**

[事实] Huberman says high leucine intake has been shown to contribute to acne in some people.

[事实] He says whey protein has been evaluated for its tendency to increase acne in some people.

[事实] He links this possible effect to leucine, insulin-related effects, and the mTOR pathway.

[事实] He does not say whey protein always causes acne; instead, he suggests people with acne try removing whey for days or weeks to see whether symptoms improve.

[16:46] **Individual Testing and Hormonal Context**

[事实] Huberman says people should become “scientists of themselves” by testing what works and observing their own responses.

[事实] He says people should avoid a whey protein brand if it causes stomach discomfort.

[事实] He says men with acne concerns can try removing whey or replacing it with casein or another high-quality protein.
