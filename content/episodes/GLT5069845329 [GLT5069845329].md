# Francis Drake: Pirate, Privateer, and Circumnavigator

## 概览

This episode traces Francis Drake from late Victorian patriotic myth to the far messier sixteenth-century figure behind it: a Devon sailor, fierce Protestant, slaver, pirate, strategist, and global celebrity.

The hosts argue that Drake’s importance was not only that he robbed Spain, but that he exposed the vulnerability of Spain’s Atlantic and Pacific systems. His raids gave English seamen confidence that Spain’s imperial monopoly could be challenged.

The narrative moves from Drake’s origins and early slave-trading voyages with John Hawkins, through the disaster at San Juan de Ulua and the raid on Nombre de Dios, to the 1577 voyage of the Golden Hind. It ends with Drake back in Plymouth after circumnavigating the world, rich and famous but unsure whether Elizabeth I will protect or punish him.

## 分段落总结

[02:31] **Drake as national myth and contested memory**

[事实] The episode opens with Henry Newbolt’s 1897 poem “Drake’s Drum,” linking Drake to late Victorian patriotism, empire, and naval confidence.
[事实] Drake is presented as an English hero celebrated for the Armada, global exploration, and circumnavigation, but also as a figure condemned for involvement in the slave trade.
[推测] The opening frames Drake as both a patriotic legend and a morally unstable historical subject.

[05:41] **English hero, Spanish villain**

[事实] The hosts note that parts of Sir Francis Drake Boulevard and the Sir Francis Drake Hotel in San Francisco have been renamed.
[事实] In Spain, Drake was hated as “El Draque,” associated with piracy and predation, and later anti-British rhetoric in Argentina also used Drake as a symbol of English piracy.
[推测] Drake’s reputation depends heavily on whether he is viewed from an English, Spanish, or postcolonial perspective.

[08:23] **Drake in the Tudor Cold War**

[事实] The episode places Drake within the wider conflict between Elizabethan England and Philip II’s Spain, after earlier episodes on spies, Jesuits, and priest hunters.
[事实] Drake is described as rising from nowhere to become a famous and charismatic figure who helped England imagine itself as a future sea power.
[事实] The hosts also say he was the first English commander to ally with a black military power.

[09:35] **Devon origins and Protestant formation**

[事实] Drake was born around 1540 near Tavistock in Devon, the grandson of a yeoman farmer and the son of Edmund Drake.
[事实] Edmund Drake fled Devon after legal trouble and anti-Protestant unrest, reinventing himself in Kent as a preacher.
[事实] Francis Drake grew up with intense Protestant convictions and was apprenticed to a captain, who later left him his ship.

[13:31] **John Hawkins and the slave trade**

[事实] Drake’s relative John Hawkins was involved in Atlantic slaving and launched what the hosts call the first English slaving voyage in 1562.
[事实] Drake definitely joined Hawkins’s third slaving expedition in 1567 and was given command first of a captured Portuguese ship and then of the Judith.
[事实] The hosts describe the expedition’s atmosphere as a mixture of Protestantism, patriotism, and greed.

[16:02] **San Juan de Ulua and the birth of vengeance**

[事实] In 1568 Hawkins’s fleet was blown to San Juan de Ulua near Veracruz, where a Spanish attack sank four English ships, killed more than 300 sailors, and took 114 prisoners.
[事实] Drake and Hawkins escaped, but Hawkins suffered major financial losses and there were rumors that Drake had behaved cowardly.
[推测] The disaster deepened Drake’s humiliation and helped turn anti-Spanish resentment into a personal mission.

[20:39] **From failed trader to pirate strategist**

[事实] Elizabeth’s government would not openly support Drake after San Juan de Ulua because it feared war with Spain and because his earlier actions were close to piracy.
[事实] Drake concluded that Spanish defenses in the Indies were weak and that piracy was more profitable than trying to enter Iberian-controlled trade.
[事实] The hosts state that without government sanction, Drake was legally a pirate rather than a privateer.

[24:37] **Nombre de Dios and the silver route**

[事实] Drake’s 1571 raid returned three times the profit Hawkins had made from his slaving voyages.
[事实] Drake identified Nombre de Dios in Panama as a vulnerable port through which silver from Potosi and other treasure moved toward Spain.
[事实] The town was poorly fortified and protected mainly by isolation and Spanish assumptions that outsiders would not attack it.

[29:04] **The first raid on Nombre de Dios**

[事实] In 1572 Drake sailed from Plymouth with two ships, three flat-pack pinnaces, about 100 men, and provisions for around a year.
[事实] His night attack on Nombre de Dios nearly reached the treasure house, but rain ruined the English gunpowder and crossbows, while Drake was wounded in the leg.
[推测] Drake’s warning that he would return was part bravado and part psychological warfare.

[31:39] **Diego and the Cimarrones**

[事实] Diego, an African man enslaved by the Spanish, defected to the English during the Nombre de Dios raid and became close to Drake.
[事实] Diego introduced Drake to the Cimarrones, escaped African outlaws in Panama who were skilled fighters and wanted revenge on the Spanish.
[事实] Spanish officials recognized the alliance between Drake’s Englishmen and the Cimarrones as a serious threat.

[34:01] **Seeing the Pacific and seizing the mule train**

[事实] In early 1573 Drake climbed a tree platform in Panama and saw both the Atlantic and the Pacific, then prayed to sail an English ship in the Pacific.
[事实] A first ambush of the treasure mule train failed when one of Drake’s men acted too early after drinking.
[事实] On 1 April, a second ambush succeeded and took 30 tons of gold and silver, so much that half had to be buried.

[36:57] **Profit, alliance, and contradiction**

[事实] Drake shared the haul with his allies, including the Cimarrones, and returned with extraordinary wealth and valuable intelligence about Spanish routes.
[事实] The hosts stress the contradiction that Drake had participated in slaving but also allied with black fighters and offered praise for them.
[推测] The episode presents Drake’s conduct as driven by profit, Protestant conviction, revenge, and appetite for adventure rather than by a consistent moral principle.

[39:53] **The 1577 expedition begins**

[事实] On 15 November 1577, Drake left Plymouth Sound with five small ships, led by the Pelican.
[事实] The Pelican was about 80 feet long, carried 18 guns, and had been built to Drake’s requirements.
[事实] Drake sailed with relatives, Diego, religious books, navigation manuals, and an account of Magellan’s voyage.

[43:32] **Cover story and court connections**

[事实] Officially, the expedition was said to be trading with Alexandria and Constantinople, but the ships carried weapons and military supplies.
[事实] Thomas Doughty, a gentleman and secretary to Sir Christopher Hatton, joined the voyage.
[事实] Hatton and Francis Walsingham were connected to the expedition, and Walsingham arranged a private meeting between Drake and Elizabeth.

[46:03] **Elizabeth’s deniable authorization**

[事实] According to Drake’s later account, Elizabeth said she wanted revenge on the King of Spain for injuries she had received.
[事实] Drake’s plan was to pass through the Strait of Magellan, attack Spanish shipping on the Pacific coast of the Americas, map the coast, and return the same way.
[推测] Elizabeth’s refusal to put the authorization in writing gave Drake room to act while preserving royal deniability.

[50:32] **Doughty, class, and mutiny**

[事实] Tension grew between Drake, a non-gentleman sea captain, and Doughty, a gentleman with court connections.
[事实] Drake tried giving Doughty commands, including the Pelican, but relations worsened after accusations, mutinous remarks, and Drake’s suspicion of witchcraft.
[事实] At San Julien, the crew found the remains of a mutineer hanged by Magellan, which shaped the mood around Doughty’s fate.

[53:48] **The execution of Thomas Doughty**

[事实] Drake put Doughty on trial for treason and incitement to mutiny on 30 June.
[事实] Doughty asked to see Drake’s commission from the Queen, but Drake had no written commission.
[事实] Doughty was found guilty, shared a final meal with Drake, and was then beheaded.
[推测] The hosts treat the execution as brutal but effective in establishing Drake’s command.

[57:04] **The Golden Hind and the Strait of Magellan**

[事实] Before entering the Strait of Magellan, Drake saluted Elizabeth, held a church service, and renamed the Pelican the Golden Hind.
[事实] The Golden Hind was the crest of Sir Christopher Hatton, whose secretary Drake had just executed.
[事实] Drake passed through the Strait in 15 days, faster than Magellan’s 37 days.

[60:59] **Alone in the Pacific**

[事实] After the Strait, storms lasted on and off for almost two months, sinking one ship and driving another back toward England.
[事实] The Golden Hind became the only English ship left in the Pacific.
[事实] Off Chile, Indigenous islanders mistook the English for Spanish; two sailors were killed, Drake and Diego were wounded, and Diego later died.

[61:30] **The great Pacific prize**

[事实] Drake captured the Spanish treasure ship Nuestra Senora de la Concepcion.
[事实] The haul included 14 chests of silver reals, 80 pounds of gold, and 26 tons of silver bars.
[事实] The hosts say Drake treated the captured Spanish captain chivalrously and offered enslaved people found aboard freedom and sailors’ wages.

[63:36] **North to New Albion**

[事实] Spanish authorities expected Drake to return via Panama or the Strait of Magellan, so he instead sailed north looking for a northern passage.
[事实] By June 1579 he had reached almost as far as present-day Canada before turning back south to the Bay Area.
[事实] Drake met the Miwok, was given a feathered crown, and claimed the land for Elizabeth as New Albion.

[66:02] **Across the Pacific and through the Indies**

[事实] Drake left California on 24 July after taking charts from captured Spanish ships and stocking provisions such as seabirds and seals.
[事实] The Golden Hind sailed 68 days without sighting land before reaching Palau on 30 September.
[事实] In the Spice Islands, a local sultan at war with the Portuguese gave Drake six tons of cloves and a ring for Elizabeth.

[67:51] **Return to England**

[事实] On 10 January, the Golden Hind ran aground on a reef but was freed after efforts to lighten the ship and a change in wind.
[事实] Drake rounded the Cape of Good Hope, reached Sierra Leone on 22 July, and completed a 9,700-mile stretch without resupply, scurvy deaths, or dehydration deaths.
[事实] On 26 September, the Golden Hind returned to Plymouth after 1,046 days, completing the first English and second recorded circumnavigation of the globe.

[70:07] **Treasure, risk, and cliffhanger**

[事实] Drake returned with treasure worth more than Elizabeth’s annual revenue.
[事实] Before entering Plymouth, he asked a fisherman whether Elizabeth was still alive and well.
[事实] He stayed aboard because Plymouth had plague and because Philip II, now also King of Portugal, was demanding that Elizabeth punish him for piracy.
[推测] The episode ends by making Elizabeth’s response the key unresolved political question.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in how it combines adventure narrative with geopolitics, reputation, religion, class, slavery, and maritime strategy. It does not reduce Drake to either hero or villain.

[推测] Its strongest thread is the contrast between Drake’s English myth and the Spanish view of him as a pirate. The discussion of Diego and the Cimarrones also adds complexity to a story often told mainly as an English naval triumph.

[推测] A limitation is that Indigenous peoples and enslaved Africans mostly appear through their relationship to Drake’s story, rather than as fully developed subjects in their own right. The episode is best suited to listeners who enjoy narrative history and Tudor imperial rivalry rather than a narrowly academic treatment.
