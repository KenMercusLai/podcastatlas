# Evita’s Body and the Afterlife of Peronism

## 概览

This episode focuses on what happened after Eva “Evita” Perón’s death: the embalming of her body, the political power attached to it, and the strange ways her corpse became entangled with Argentina’s myths, coups, exile politics, and violence.

The hosts argue that Evita’s body was not merely a relic but a political object. Peronists treated her as a quasi-sacred presence, anti-Peronist regimes tried to erase or hide her, and later factions invoked her image as Argentina descended into instability.

The discussion follows Juan Perón’s decline after Evita, his exile, the rise of Isabel Perón and José López Rega, the return of Peronism in the 1970s, and the eventual burial of Evita in Recoleta Cemetery after decades of concealment, ritual, and political struggle.

## 分段落总结

[00:25] **The Episode’s Focus**

[事实] The episode opens with Juan Domingo Perón’s memory of seeing Evita’s embalmed body about a year after her death in 1952.
[事实] The hosts frame the episode as the story of Evita’s afterlife, especially the fate of her mummified body.
[事实] They note that myths, propaganda, fiction, and documented history overlap heavily in this story.

[03:03] **Dr. Ara and the Embalming**

[事实] Dr. Pedro Ara took charge of Evita’s body in a secret laboratory on the third floor of the CGT trade union headquarters.
[事实] The body had been lying in state for too long, so Ara carried out a long preservation process.
[事实] After about a year, Ara told the government the body was stable, kept under glass, and that only he held the key.
[推测] The comparison to Lenin’s preserved body suggests the hosts see Evita’s embalming as part of a broader politics of revolutionary or authoritarian relics.

[04:50] **The Monument and the Cult of Evita**

[事实] A huge tomb was planned for Evita, modeled partly on grand national monuments and involving an enormous statue.
[事实] One proposal involved embalming four additional bodies to stand as symbolic figures around the monument.
[事实] The cult of Evita continued outside the CGT building, with torchlit processions on the 22nd of each month.
[事实] Peronist media and supporters promoted stories of Evita’s face appearing in the moon or stars, and there were demands for her canonization.

[08:50] **Perón Without Evita**

[事实] The hosts describe Perón after Evita’s death as lonely and politically weakened.
[事实] Evita had appealed to constituencies and performed emotional political work that Perón could not easily replace.
[事实] Without her, Peronism became more formal, ponderous, and dependent on ordinary political choices.
[推测] The hosts imply that Evita’s absence made Perón’s regime less flexible and less charismatic.

[10:08] **The Student Union and Nelly Rivas**

[事实] Perón supported the creation of a Union of Secondary School Students and allowed students to use the grounds of his Olivos residence.
[事实] The hosts describe schoolgirls exercising near Perón’s bedroom window and say he invited some of them to lunch.
[事实] Nelly Rivas, aged 14, became close to Perón, who was around 58.
[事实] The hosts say later testimony indicated that Perón and Nelly had a physical relationship.

[13:24] **Economic Crisis and Political Violence**

[事实] Argentina’s trade balance worsened, exports no longer brought in enough money, and the country relied on foreign currency it lacked.
[事实] The hosts connect this period to the beginning of very high inflation in later decades.
[事实] After bombs went off at a Peronist rally in 1953, Perón used violent rhetoric and crowds attacked opposition offices and the Jockey Club.
[事实] Police began using cattle prods on political opponents, which the hosts call an ominous development.

[15:58] **Perón Versus the Catholic Church**

[事实] Perón entered a major confrontation with the Catholic Church.
[事实] The hosts say historians disagree on the causes, including whether youth organizations and education were central to the conflict.
[事实] Perón expelled two Catholic priests, and the Vatican excommunicated him.
[推测] Evita may have helped mediate the regime’s Catholic symbolism if she had still been alive.

[17:56] **The 1955 Coup and Perón’s Fall**

[事实] In September 1955, army units revolted in Córdoba after an earlier failed coup had involved bombing Buenos Aires.
[事实] The hosts say Perón could possibly have fought back but chose not to.
[事实] Ernesto Sabato’s account is used to show that elites celebrated Perón’s fall while poorer supporters mourned him.
[事实] The new authorities banned Peronist symbols, organizations, and even public mentions of Perón and Evita.

[20:22] **Exile, Nelly, and Isabel**

[事实] Perón first went to Paraguay, where he wrote affectionately to Nelly Rivas, then moved on to Panama.
[事实] In Panama, he became involved with María Estela Martínez, a cabaret dancer who called herself Isabel.
[事实] Isabel had an interest in spiritualism and the occult from her youth.
[事实] Nelly later tried to reach Perón with valuables and poodles, was stopped near Paraguay, and was sent to reform school.

[22:15] **The Army Finds Evita’s Body**

[事实] After the coup, army officers found Evita’s body in the CGT laboratory.
[事实] Francisco Manrique described the body as waxy, artificial-looking, and mannequin-like.
[事实] The army hoped the body might be fake, cut off one finger, and used an X-ray to confirm it was real.
[推测] The body’s artificial appearance helped intensify the uncertainty and unease around it.

[23:47] **The Failed Secret Burial**

[事实] Military intelligence chief Moori Koenig planned to bury Evita secretly in Chacarita Cemetery.
[事实] The coffin lid was supposed to be welded shut, but the welders did not arrive.
[事实] The body was driven around Buenos Aires in a truck and parked in unused places.
[事实] Koenig reportedly found flowers and a candle near the truck, making him fear either betrayal or something supernatural.

[26:57] **The Vatican Arrangement**

[事实] Koenig eventually hid the body above or near his military office and became increasingly unstable.
[事实] President Aramburu arranged through Catholic channels for the body to be removed.
[事实] An Italian priest arrived, took the coffin, and left Aramburu a sealed envelope with the body’s destination.
[事实] After that, Evita’s body disappeared from public knowledge.

[28:44] **Evita as a Radical Icon**

[事实] By the late 1960s, Argentina faced inflation, hunger, unrest, and unstable military rule.
[事实] Students and radicals, influenced by left-wing movements and the Cuban Revolution, adopted Evita as an icon.
[事实] Groups such as the Montoneros used Evita’s memory, and slogans in Buenos Aires proclaimed that Evita lived.
[事实] Peronists remained angry that her body had vanished and that she had no public grave.

[31:48] **Isabel as the New Evita**

[事实] Perón lived in Madrid, married Isabel, and used her as a messenger to underground Peronist groups in Argentina.
[事实] Isabel was not politically experienced, but Peronist supporters treated her with reverence.
[事实] The hosts say she was increasingly cast as a replacement Evita.
[推测] Isabel’s youth, theatrical background, and marriage to Perón made the comparison politically useful.

[33:49] **José López Rega, the Warlock**

[事实] José López Rega attached himself to Isabel and joined Perón’s household in Madrid.
[事实] He was obsessed with occultism and wrote books on spiritualism and astrology.
[事实] Perón’s circle nicknamed him “the warlock.”
[事实] After Perón became ill, López Rega and Isabel became indispensable to him and gained influence.

[35:36] **Lanusse Opens the Door to Peronism**

[事实] In 1971, military president Alejandro Agustín Lanusse decided Argentina could not stabilize without reincorporating Peronists into politics.
[事实] Restrictions on Peronist memory and Evita’s image were relaxed.
[事实] V. S. Naipaul observed Evita’s pictures everywhere in Argentina in 1972.
[事实] Lanusse opened the sealed envelope that revealed the body’s location.

[37:30] **Evita’s Body Is Found in Milan**

[事实] The envelope led Argentine agents to a cemetery in Milan.
[事实] Evita had been buried under the false identity Maria Maggi de Magistris.
[事实] An Argentine intelligence colonel impersonated a relative, obtained permission to exhume the body, and transported it by van through Europe to Spain.
[事实] The body arrived at Perón’s Madrid home, where Perón, Isabel, López Rega, and Dr. Ara were waiting.

[39:36] **The Body in Perón’s House**

[事实] Perón recognized the body as Evita’s, and Dr. Ara examined it.
[事实] The hosts say Isabel cleaned the body and arranged its hair.
[事实] Evita’s family wanted a proper burial, but Perón kept the body in an upstairs bedroom.
[事实] Reports claimed López Rega performed rituals with Isabel and Evita’s coffin to transfer Evita’s spiritual essence into Isabel.

[41:13] **Perón’s Return and the Ezeiza Violence**

[事实] Perón returned briefly to Argentina with Isabel at the end of 1972, and Isabel appeared with an enlarged photograph of Evita.
[事实] In 1973, Peronists were allowed to run a presidential candidate, Héctor Cámpora, who won.
[事实] López Rega became minister of social welfare.
[事实] When Perón made his major return at Ezeiza Airport, rival left- and right-wing Peronist groups opened fire, and many people were killed.

[44:02] **Perón, Isabel, and López Rega in Power**

[事实] Perón ran again for president with Isabel as his vice-presidential candidate.
[事实] He won a landslide in 1973 despite being elderly and seriously ill.
[事实] López Rega remained powerful, lived with Perón and Isabel, and controlled a major budget.
[事实] The hosts say López Rega funded right-wing anti-communist death squads known as the AAA.

[46:13] **Perón Dies and Isabel Takes Office**

[事实] Perón had another heart attack on July 1, 1974.
[事实] López Rega reportedly tried to save him through occult gestures or incantations.
[事实] Perón died, and Isabel became president of Argentina.
[事实] At that point, inflation was around 60%, and terrorist violence was out of control.

[46:45] **The Body Returns to Argentina**

[事实] The Montoneros stole President Aramburu’s body and demanded Evita’s body in return.
[事实] One month later, Evita’s body was flown back from Madrid to Buenos Aires.
[事实] López Rega planned a huge pantheon for Evita, but another military coup overtook the project.
[事实] López Rega was forced out, fled abroad, was later arrested, and died in prison before trial.

[49:04] **Dirty War and Final Burial**

[事实] Isabel was overthrown in March 1976 and held under house arrest for five years before being sent back to Madrid.
[事实] The military dictatorship launched the Dirty War, killing between 20,000 and 30,000 people according to the hosts.
[事实] Evita’s body was eventually placed in the Duarte family vault in Recoleta Cemetery in 1976.
[事实] The hosts describe stories of trapdoors, a deep compartment, and a single key held by Evita’s sister.

[51:23] **Closing Reflections**

[事实] The hosts compare Evita’s body to Alexander the Great’s body as a politically powerful corpse.
[事实] They describe the story as unusually strange, almost like a horror film.
[事实] They connect Evita’s politics to tango, radio melodrama, and popular culture.
[推测] The episode suggests Argentina’s political culture in this period cannot be understood separately from myth, performance, ritual, and spectacle.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s main value is its ability to connect a bizarre posthumous story to serious political history. Evita’s corpse is treated not as a macabre curiosity alone, but as a symbol through which Peronists, anti-Peronists, soldiers, radicals, priests, and occultists fought over legitimacy.

Its strongest moments are the recurring links between theatricality and power: embalming, processions, secret burials, false identities, chants, ritual, exile, and political violence all become part of the same narrative. The hosts also make clear that Peronism’s emotional force outlived both Evita and Perón himself.

The limitation is that some episodes in the body’s afterlife come through rumor, novels, memoirs, or later accounts, so the boundary between documented history and legend is not always firm. The hosts often flag this uncertainty, but the story’s strangeness makes some claims hard to verify from the transcript alone.

This episode is best suited to listeners interested in political myth, Latin American history, authoritarianism, and the way bodies and symbols can acquire power after death. [推测] It may be less useful for listeners seeking a strictly institutional account of Argentine politics, because the emphasis is on narrative, personality, and symbolism.
