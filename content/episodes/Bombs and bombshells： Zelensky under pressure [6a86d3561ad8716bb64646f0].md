# Bombs and bombshells: Zelensky under pressure

## 概览

This episode examines pressure on Volodymyr Zelensky from two directions: Russia’s intensified attacks on Ukraine and growing political strain around his own circle. Oliver Carroll describes a moment of shrinking room for manoeuvre, with corruption investigations, calls for elections and worsening economic risks converging.

The episode then turns to China’s mental-health crisis. Sarah Wu explains that demand for care is rising as stigma eases, but shortages, weak regulation and high costs still make treatment difficult to access.

The final segment considers Paw Patrol as a cultural phenomenon. Andrew Miller weighs ideological critiques of the children’s show against a more psychological reading: its appeal may lie in giving children a fantasy of solving adult problems.

[01:19] **Episode Setup**

[事实] Jason Palmer introduces The Intelligence and previews stories on China’s mental-health push and the politics of Paw Patrol.

[事实] The episode also opens with Ukraine, focusing on Russian strikes, Zelensky’s political pressure and investigations near the president’s circle.

[01:55] **Kyiv Attacks and Zelensky’s Bad Week**

[事实] The transcript describes another night of fear and destruction in Kyiv, with more than a dozen killed in recent Russian strikes.

[事实] Ukraine is said to be running short of interceptors as Russian attacks become more dangerous.

[事实] Oliver Carroll says Zelensky faced pressure after former defence minister Mikhail Fyodorov called for a presidential election and hinted at corruption among those close to the president.

[推测] The segment frames Zelensky’s vulnerability as both military and political.

[03:23] **Fyodorov, Dismissal and Corruption Pressure**

[事实] Carroll says Fyodorov was dismissed in July after a dispute involving the defence minister and top general Alexander Sirsky.

[事实] Fyodorov had recently been one of Zelensky’s loyal team members and had previously used similar election language in support of Zelensky’s re-election.

[事实] Carroll describes Fyodorov as a symptom of growing chaos, lack of direction and a shrinking presidential circle.

[事实] Anti-corruption detectives revealed an operation called Forrest Gump that allegedly involved presidential staff in money laundering to provide bail for officials charged in earlier investigations.

[05:14] **Election Calls Face Resistance**

[事实] Fyodorov has directly called for a presidential election.

[事实] People around Fyodorov’s team were surprised by the lukewarm response, even among sympathetic figures.

[事实] Polling shows Ukrainians are against elections during the war because voting could disrupt the war effort.

[事实] Martial law forbids elections during wartime, and changing that would require parliamentary support.

[推测] Elections may become more discussable if bombardment and economic hardship worsen, but the transcript presents major legal and practical barriers.

[06:47] **The War Shifts Toward the Air**

[事实] Carroll says Ukraine has slowed the Russian advance on the front lines and made limited counterattacks.

[事实] He says the war’s center of gravity has moved to the air.

[事实] Ukraine is using drones against Russian oil, gas and defence targets, while Russia is using more destructive ballistic missiles.

[推测] Ukraine’s shortage of interceptors is becoming a central constraint on its ability to protect cities and industry.

[07:36] **Exports and Civilian Business Under Attack**

[事实] Russia has begun targeting Ukrainian civilian business, including warehouses and residential areas.

[事实] Carroll says Russia has moved from targeting port infrastructure to targeting ships themselves.

[事实] This has reduced shippers’ willingness to take risk and left ports commercially dead.

[事实] The disruption is estimated to cost Ukraine about 0.6% of GDP this year.

[08:27] **Economic Fallout for Ukraine**

[事实] Carroll says the economic outlook is poor and that worse is likely to come.

[事实] Ukraine has 50 million tons of harvest that need to be moved, but road transport would be difficult and expensive.

[事实] If companies cannot sell the harvest, expected losses could reach 10 to 12 billion, just under 5% of GDP.

[推测] Export disruption, military pressure and corruption politics together narrow Zelensky’s options.

[10:33] **China’s Mental-Health Burden**

[事实] Mental-health treatment in China is described as difficult because of stigma, shortages of qualified professionals and high costs.

[事实] Sarah Wu says China’s rapid social and economic change has left many people struggling.

[事实] A Lancet paper estimated 191 million cases of mental disorders in China in 2023, about one in seven people.

[事实] Anxiety and depression are the most common illnesses, while eating disorders have grown fastest.

[12:31] **Stigma, Lockdowns and Online Help**

[事实] Wu says stigma remains stubborn, but the pandemic marked a turning point.

[事实] Strict lockdowns led to spikes in depression and anxiety, making more people see mental health as a personal or family issue.

[事实] Many people sought counselling for the first time, and because they could not leave home, the boom moved online.

[推测] Demand appears to have expanded faster than China’s professional mental-health system could mature.

[13:33] **Government Plans and Stability Concerns**

[事实] The government plans a nationwide system of psychological support and crisis intervention by 2030.

[事实] The plan includes more drug-based treatment, psychotherapy, school counselling rooms, county-level psychiatric services and more professionals.

[事实] Free hotlines have been set up nationwide, with nearly a million calls in the first half of this year.

[事实] Wu links government urgency partly to social stability concerns after a pilot crash in Beijing and violent attacks described as revenge against society.

[推测] Beijing’s response combines public-health goals with social-control priorities.

[15:49] **Regulation, Cost and Public Understanding**

[事实] Wu says counselling is a free-for-all because there are no official licensing requirements.

[事实] Reports of fraud and malpractice are common, though some online platforms and professional associations are strengthening standards.

[事实] Therapy in Beijing can cost 500 to 1,500 yuan, and counselling is mostly paid out of pocket.

[事实] A Shanghai Mental Health Center doctor says patients often expect counselling to work like medicine, but it is a slow process.

[推测] China’s challenge is not only expanding services, but also making them affordable, regulated and better understood.

[18:47] **Paw Patrol as a Cultural Force**

[事实] Andrew Miller describes Paw Patrol as a major franchise with more than a dozen TV seasons, a toy empire and three theatrical film releases.

[事实] The newest film discussed is Paw Patrol: The Dino Movie.

[事实] The show is set in Adventure Bay, where a 10-year-old boy leads heroic puppies responding to mild emergencies.

[事实] Miller calls the show formulaic, with repeated catchphrases and earworm music.

[20:41] **Political Readings of Paw Patrol**

[事实] Miller says the show can superficially seem left-wing because its villain disdains elections and the law, while the pups protect the environment and promote teamwork.

[事实] Critics argue that Chase, a police pup, makes the show pro-police propaganda.

[事实] Critics also argue that the pups are unaccountable freelancers, making the show a sly advert for privatizing state services.

[推测] Miller treats these critiques skeptically while still accepting that children’s media can transmit values.

[22:23] **Values and Childhood Wish Fulfillment**

[事实] Miller says it would be mistaken to dismiss the debate entirely because stories for children impart values and lessons.

[事实] He notes that dictators try to control stories told to the young.

[事实] He suggests psychoanalysis may explain Paw Patrol better than political ideology.

[事实] He says many children want to heal their parents or take on parental roles, and Paw Patrol may fulfill a wish to solve adults’ problems.

[推测] The show’s appeal may come less from ideology than from giving children a fantasy of competence in an anxious adult world.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is its range: it moves from Ukraine’s war and political strain to China’s mental-health system and then to a lighter but pointed cultural essay. The Ukraine segment is the strongest hard-news section because it connects battlefield dynamics, export disruption, corruption investigations and election pressure.

The China segment is useful for understanding mental health as both a medical and governance issue. Its strongest material is the contrast between ambitious national plans and the practical gaps in licensing, affordability and local funding.

The Paw Patrol segment adds texture by showing how even children’s entertainment can become a debate about politics, values and anxiety. [推测] The episode is best suited to listeners who want a compact Economist-style briefing rather than a deep dive into one subject.
