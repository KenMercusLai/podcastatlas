# Will Ahmed on WHOOP: Wearables, Health Data, AI Coaching, and the Future of Preventive Health

## 概览

WHOOP founder and CEO Will Ahmed explains how the company evolved from a $500 performance wearable for elite athletes into a subscription health platform approaching four million members. He argues that subscriptions changed WHOOP’s incentives: instead of forcing annual hardware upgrades, the company focuses on sustained engagement, software improvements, and measurable behavior change.

The conversation explores WHOOP’s core metrics—sleep, recovery, strain, heart-rate variability, and stress—and how continuous monitoring can reveal deviations from an individual baseline. Host Jason Calacanis shares examples in which abnormal readings preceded COVID-19 and lung inflammation, while Ahmed describes members whose unusual physiological signals led them to seek medical testing.

Ahmed also discusses WHOOP’s large physiological dataset, AI coach, journal, blood testing, GLP-1 support, and efforts to infer health risks from combined data. The discussion then turns to FDA regulation, data privacy, competitive positioning, athlete partnerships, and the broader challenge of distinguishing wellness insights from regulated medical diagnoses.

## 分段落总结

[01:06] **WHOOP’s Growth and Current Scale**

[事实] WHOOP builds wearable technology intended to improve performance and health, and Ahmed says he has been building the company since its founding in 2012.

[事实] It took roughly 12 years to reach one million members, followed by much faster growth to two million, three million, and an expected four million by year-end.

[事实] WHOOP plans range from approximately $15 to $30 per month, depending on the membership tier.

[03:12] **Why WHOOP Adopted a Subscription Model**

[事实] WHOOP initially launched its consumer product for a one-time price of $500, but strong retention was accompanied by weak sales.

[事实] The company lowered the upfront barrier and shifted to subscriptions, reasoning that members who loved and continued using the product could generate revenue over time.

[事实] The transition created growth in 2018 and 2019 and made WHOOP a recurring-revenue company rather than a conventional hardware business.

[事实] Ahmed says subscriptions made the company more focused on existing members, engagement, health outcomes, rapid innovation, and the overall member experience.

[事实] WHOOP now releases new hardware when it represents a meaningful improvement while adding features such as ECG, AFib detection, blood testing, WHOOP Age, and blood-pressure insights to the ongoing membership.

[07:09] **Sleep, Recovery, Strain, and the Daily User Experience**

[事实] WHOOP’s three main pillars are sleep, recovery, and strain.

[事实] Its sleep analysis includes sleep score, schedule consistency, time asleep, restorative sleep, slow-wave sleep, REM sleep, and circadian rhythm.

[事实] The recovery score is presented on a zero-to-100 scale using red, yellow, and green zones.

[事实] Calacanis says these measurements help him manage sleep and readiness while traveling across time zones and preparing for podcasts or speaking engagements.

[09:50] **Health Monitoring and Early Warning Signals**

[事实] WHOOP’s health monitor tracks respiratory rate, resting heart rate, skin temperature, blood oxygen, and heart-rate variability.

[事实] Calacanis recounts seeing an elevated respiratory rate and skin temperature before experiencing chest pain; hospital testing later identified lung inflammation affecting the area around his chest and heart, rather than a heart attack.

[事实] He also describes abnormal WHOOP readings preceding a positive COVID-19 test, which prompted him to test and seek treatment early.

[事实] Ahmed says members regularly report similar experiences, including a baseball player whose abnormal sleeping heart rate led to testing and a diagnosis of stage-four Hodgkin lymphoma.

[事实] Ahmed also describes a long-term member whose heart-rate variability abruptly dropped from about 100 to 50 before testing revealed testicular cancer.

[推测] These stories illustrate the potential value of personalized baseline deviations as prompts to seek professional evaluation, but the transcript does not establish that WHOOP can diagnose these conditions.

[14:14] **Heart-Rate Variability as a Measure of Recovery**

[事实] Ahmed defines heart-rate variability, or HRV, as variation in the time between successive heartbeats.

[事实] Higher HRV generally reflects healthier regulation between sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous-system activity.

[事实] WHOOP measures HRV continuously and uses it in stress monitoring and recovery scoring, comparing each member’s readings with their personal baseline.

[事实] Ahmed says HRV generally declines with age and that maintaining a stable level over many years may indicate good fitness and longevity.

[事实] Research into HRV helped inspire WHOOP because Ahmed saw its potential while studying at Harvard, yet found that it was not then practical to measure continuously.

[17:21] **Continuous Wear and a Large Physiological Dataset**

[事实] Continuous data collection was a founding principle of WHOOP and influenced features such as a screenless design, a battery that lasts up to 14 days, and a charger that can be attached while the device remains on the body.

[事实] Ahmed says the average member wears WHOOP for 23.6 hours per day.

[事实] WHOOP has accumulated more than 50 billion hours of continuous physiological data, which Ahmed describes as one of the world’s largest health datasets.

[事实] The company is using open-source models to build internal foundation models trained on its data.

[19:50] **AI Models and Predicting Biomarker Abnormalities**

[事实] WHOOP is combining wearable signals with blood tests, advanced labs, and uploaded medical records.

[事实] Ahmed says the company is seeing early indications that physiological signals may help predict when a biomarker is outside its normal range without directly measuring that biomarker.

[事实] He gives elevated cortisol as an example of a possible prediction that could prompt a targeted laboratory test.

[推测] If validated, this approach could make health testing more selective and personalized, although Ahmed presents it as an emerging capability rather than an established diagnostic product.

[21:04] **The AI Coach and Personalized Travel Guidance**

[事实] Calacanis says WHOOP’s AI coach has become more capable at interpreting product issues, time-zone changes, missing sleep records, and travel schedules.

[事实] WHOOP created a dedicated travel team because travel and jet lag are common member use cases.

[事实] The AI coach can recommend when to sleep during a flight, when to avoid caffeine, and how to adapt after arrival.

[事实] Ahmed defines WHOOP’s target audience broadly as aspirational people, including athletes and anyone seeking more energy, better performance, or a longer life.

[事实] About 80% of members interact with the AI, according to the figures discussed in the episode.

[23:37] **The WHOOP Journal and Behavioral Data**

[事实] More than half of WHOOP members use the journal every day.

[事实] The journal includes more than 100 behaviors, lifestyle choices, supplements, and drugs, including alcohol use, hydration, travel, meditation, mood, and stress.

[事实] WHOOP correlates logged behaviors with later recovery outcomes to show members which habits are associated with positive or negative changes.

[事实] Ahmed says these self-reported observations add an important data layer alongside sensor readings, laboratory results, and medical records.

[推测] The journal’s usefulness depends on consistent and accurate self-reporting, and observed correlations may reflect other unrecorded behaviors rather than direct causation.

[25:35] **Personal Behavioral Insights and the Limits of Correlation**

[事实] Calacanis reports that warm baths correlate with a seven-percentage-point improvement in his recovery score.

[事实] Ahmed says alcohol commonly has a strongly negative association with recovery; his own data shows a negative nine-percentage-point relationship.

[事实] WHOOP’s population data indicates that eating within three hours of bedtime is detrimental to sleep and recovery because the body remains occupied with digestion.

[事实] Calacanis finds that high-stress time is negatively associated with his recovery, while a consistent bedtime, caffeine, creatine, and morning sunlight show positive associations in his data.

[事实] The speakers acknowledge that a result such as Calacanis’s negative vitamin D correlation does not prove causation and could reflect travel or another confounding factor.

[29:18] **Designing Stress and Health Scores**

[事实] WHOOP’s stress score primarily uses HRV while incorporating additional sensor data.

[事实] Ahmed says producing a useful score requires combining credible medical research, large-scale consumer data, and interface design.

[事实] The recovery and Healthspan screens are among the most frequently screenshotted areas of the WHOOP app.

[事实] WHOOP can import activities from other services and use recorded goals and activity history to help create training plans.

[32:04] **GLP-1 Tracking, Muscle Preservation, and Coaching**

[事实] Calacanis says he lost 43 pounds using a GLP-1 medication and now takes a maintenance dose.

[事实] WHOOP’s GLP-1 feature tracks medication use while encouraging steps, strength training, hydration, and protein consumption.

[事实] Ahmed says a major concern with GLP-1-associated weight loss is that people may lose substantial muscle as well as fat and become frail.

[事实] He says WHOOP therefore encourages activity, resistance training, and appropriate protein intake, and notes anecdotal reports of creatine use among people taking GLP-1 medications.

[推测] The feature positions WHOOP as a behavior-support platform around medical treatment, but the transcript does not provide clinical outcome data demonstrating its effectiveness.

[35:05] **FDA Regulation and Blood-Pressure Insights**

[事实] WHOOP recruited about 70,000 members for a stress, sleep, and blood-pressure study in which participants uploaded cuff readings over several weeks.

[事实] The resulting dataset was used alongside physiological signals to train a wrist-based blood-pressure algorithm.

[事实] Ahmed distinguishes wellness products from products making medical diagnoses: heart-rate monitoring for exercise or sleep can be wellness, whereas AFib detection or heart-attack prediction requires regulation.

[事实] WHOOP launched blood-pressure insights as a wellness feature, while the FDA initially argued that it should be regulated and sent the company a warning letter.

[事实] Ahmed says the FDA subsequently updated its guidance to recognize possible wellness applications for blood-pressure technology, and WHOOP’s feature remains available.

[事实] WHOOP also follows regulated pathways where appropriate; its wrist-based ECG feature received FDA 510(k) clearance.

[41:20] **Build, Partner, or Acquire—and Protecting Member Data**

[事实] WHOOP evaluates whether to build products internally, partner with another company, or make acquisitions.

[事实] For blood-pressure cuffs, WHOOP chose integration because the technology is relatively commoditized and can calibrate the cuffless feature already built into WHOOP.

[事实] Ahmed says the company ultimately wants its wearable to replace the need for routine cuff measurements.

[事实] WHOOP states that it does not sell member data and that the paid membership—not the member’s data—is the product.

[推测] The subscription model may align WHOOP’s commercial incentives more closely with member trust than an advertising-supported model, though the conversation does not independently audit its privacy practices.

[43:24] **Competition and the Screenless-Wearable Market**

[事实] Ahmed calls Apple Watch the world’s most successful smartwatch and recognizes Apple’s strength in hardware and software.

[事实] He differentiates WHOOP from multifunction smartwatches by emphasizing its screenless, continuous-wear design and focus on health and performance.

[事实] Ahmed observes that there were more wearable competitors a decade ago, when companies such as Jawbone, Fitbit, Nike, Adidas, and Under Armour were active in the category.

[推测] WHOOP’s specialization and accumulated longitudinal data appear to be central competitive advantages, but the discussion does not provide a systematic comparison of measurement accuracy across products.

[44:39] **Rapid-Fire Health and Performance Preferences**

[事实] Ahmed chooses Andrew Huberman for podcasting reach while crediting Bryan Johnson with expanding people’s imagination about what health metrics to measure.

[事实] He favors cold showers for accessibility, traditional hot saunas over red-light therapy, and strength training over spending most exercise time on an elliptical.

[事实] Ahmed identifies muscle mass as an important longevity measure.

[事实] He recommends an exact wake time for someone running a technology company, although recovery- or sleep-based alarms may suit vacations.

[事实] He personally drinks coffee immediately rather than waiting 90 minutes and says recovery is the WHOOP metric he watches most closely.

[47:31] **LeBron James and Authentic Athlete Adoption**

[事实] Ahmed announces LeBron James as a global WHOOP ambassador after James had used the product for 11 years without a formal relationship.

[事实] James was among the first 100 people to wear WHOOP, which gave the young company early validation.

[事实] WHOOP’s ambassador roster also includes Cristiano Ronaldo, Patrick Mahomes, Lewis Hamilton, Aryna Sabalenka, and Nelly Korda.

[事实] Ahmed says early-stage companies should be cautious about paying people to use a product because authentic adoption is especially important before large-scale marketing.

[事实] Several elite athletes later invested in WHOOP after using and valuing the product, and some relationships expanded into paid rights to use their names, images, and likenesses.

[50:36] **Wearables in Professional Competition**

[事实] Ahmed says the NBA is still considering whether players may wear devices such as WHOOP during games.

[事实] At the Australian Open, some players were asked to remove WHOOP devices even though the International Tennis Federation had approved their use in matches.

[事实] Ahmed says other major tennis tournaments subsequently clarified that wearables could be worn.

[事实] Both speakers acknowledge that sports organizations have legitimate reasons to review what athletes may wear during competition.

[52:18] **Late-Stage Investing and Secondary-Market Risks**

[事实] Calacanis describes expanding his investment syndicate from early-stage deals into late-stage opportunities involving companies such as Zipline, ICON, and Vast.

[事实] Ahmed advises investors arranging special-purpose vehicles or secondary transactions to communicate with the company’s CFO and obtain the company’s approval.

[事实] The speakers warn that some secondary brokers claim access to shares they do not control in order to manufacture demand.

[事实] Calacanis says he has tested such claims by asking for transaction details as a board member, only for the supposed sellers to stop responding.

[推测] This closing section is only loosely connected to the episode’s main health-technology discussion, but it offers practical context on financing successful private companies.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest contribution is its clear explanation of how continuous physiological data, personal baselines, self-reported behavior, and AI coaching can work together. Ahmed connects WHOOP’s product choices to its business model, showing how subscription economics influence hardware cycles, engagement priorities, and privacy claims.

[推测] The personal stories make the potential of wearables vivid, particularly when abnormal readings encourage users to seek medical attention. Their limitation is that they are anecdotes rather than controlled evidence, so listeners should not interpret them as proof that WHOOP can diagnose cancer, infection, or cardiovascular disease.

[推测] The discussion is highly enthusiastic and frequently promotional, with limited scrutiny of accuracy, false alarms, privacy implementation, or clinical validation. Some health recommendations and product claims would benefit from independent evidence and medical context.

[推测] The episode is best suited to founders, wearable users, health-technology investors, athletes, and people interested in quantified-self tools. It is especially useful as a case study in product design and recurring-revenue strategy, provided listeners distinguish wellness guidance and correlations from medical diagnosis.
