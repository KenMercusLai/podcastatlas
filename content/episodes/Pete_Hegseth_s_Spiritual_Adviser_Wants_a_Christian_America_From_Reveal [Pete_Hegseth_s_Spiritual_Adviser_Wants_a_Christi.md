# Pete Hegseth’s Spiritual Adviser Wants a Christian America

## 概览

This episode investigates the growing influence of pastor Doug Wilson, co-founder of the Communion of Reformed Evangelical Churches and a spiritual mentor admired by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Wilson advocates transforming the United States into an explicitly Christian nation, beginning with a concentrated religious community in Moscow, Idaho.

Through interviews with Wilson, his family, church members, local residents, and former members, reporter Kiera Butler examines how this worldview shapes politics, family life, education, gender roles, and attitudes toward LGBTQ people and non-Christians. The reporting contrasts the order, certainty, hospitality, and community experienced by believers with allegations of abuse, rigid hierarchies, social exclusion, and deep local divisions.

The episode also scrutinizes the role of media attention. Critical coverage can expose Wilson’s movement while simultaneously expanding his audience, attracting new followers, and strengthening the alternative religious economy surrounding his church.

Its closing stories present two opposing responses to the same community: one former member found freedom by leaving its black-and-white worldview, while another returned because its rules and certainty gave her stability and relief.

## 分段落总结

[00:09] **Introducing the investigation**

[事实] The episode is presented as a Reveal investigation into the rise of Christian nationalism and the effort to understand people across political and religious divisions.

[事实] It opens with Doug Wilson leading a prayer service in the Pentagon’s main auditorium, where Pete Hegseth publicly thanks him for his leadership and mentorship.

[事实] Hegseth belongs to a church in the denomination Wilson co-founded, whose teachings the program describes as patriarchal and strongly anti-gay.

[推测] The Pentagon scene establishes why Wilson’s beliefs matter beyond one congregation: they now have proximity to national military power.

[03:53] **Doug Wilson’s expanding Christian project**

[事实] Wilson maintains a large online presence, publishes frequently, and promotes positions against abortion, same-sex marriage, and contemporary understandings of gender.

[事实] His denomination is opening churches around the country, while his main church in Moscow, Idaho, has reportedly doubled in size.

[事实] Wilson’s stated goal is to turn Moscow into a Christian town and ultimately make America a Christian nation.

[事实] He says Moscow contains a visible but still small “prototype community,” while national progress is larger but less visible.

[05:31] **The Kirkers’ growing presence in Moscow**

[事实] Moscow is portrayed as a conventional college town with progressive cultural markers alongside a growing population of members of Wilson’s church, known as Kirkers.

[事实] Wilson says Kirkers constitute roughly 10 percent of the town’s population.

[事实] Church-linked institutions and businesses include churches, New Saint Andrews College, Logos School, Canon Press, and housing developments for incoming families.

[推测] This network functions as more than a congregation: it is an emerging social, educational, commercial, and residential ecosystem.

[09:25] **Wilson’s ideal Christian nation**

[事实] Wilson says he wants to achieve his political objectives only through democratic means.

[事实] His priorities include outlawing abortion, overturning the Supreme Court ruling permitting same-sex marriage, eliminating no-fault divorce, and changing education.

[事实] He favors treating the family as the basic civic unit, with a household head generally voting on behalf of the family.

[事实] In his ideal system, non-Christians could be citizens and vote but could not hold public office; Muslims could pray in mosques but would not be allowed a public call to prayer from a minaret.

[推测] Although Wilson invokes democratic methods, his desired outcome would give Christianity formal supremacy and restrict equal political participation by religious minorities.

[12:39] **Family discipline and prescribed gender roles**

[事实] Church member Emily describes raising five children within a highly structured Christian household and says spanking may be appropriate for repeated “hard-heartedness” at certain ages.

[事实] Logos School, the church-run classical Christian school attended by her children, also uses corporal punishment.

[事实] Wilson teaches that boys should be tough and not effeminate, while Emily says boys and girls have different God-given roles.

[事实] Emily views submission to her husband as beneficial, rejects feminism, and argues that women damage themselves and their relationships when they attempt to assume male authority.

[推测] The family model presented here offers adherents clarity and shared purpose while enforcing sharply unequal expectations based on sex.

[17:08] **Certainty, hospitality, and serious allegations**

[事实] Wilson and his wife Nancy welcome the reporter into their home, where several members of their extended family live on the same property.

[事实] Nancy says Reformed theology transformed a gray and confusing world into one governed by clear truths and God’s sovereign control.

[事实] The reporter asks Wilson about allegations involving grooming and sexual abuse connected to Logos School; Wilson says the church fired an offending teacher and contacted police.

[事实] Nancy objects to media attention on church scandals, condemns transgender people, and has written that marital rape does not exist because a husband cannot trespass in his “own garden.”

[推测] The juxtaposition of generous hospitality with severe teachings and abuse allegations illustrates why the community resists simple characterization.

[21:05] **How the Wilsons see outsiders**

[事实] When the reporter asks what the Wilsons think of her as a feminist, non-Christian career woman in an egalitarian marriage, they call her pleasant but say the enemy has darkened her mind.

[事实] Wilson says nonbelievers are not the enemy but “the terrain” over which a spiritual war is being fought.

[事实] After attending a restrained, text-focused church service, the reporter publishes an article connecting the denomination’s beliefs to Hegseth’s ideas about masculinity and a divinely ordained military.

[事实] Some people outside the church believe the article was too gentle, while Kirkers argue that it misunderstood them and judged their beliefs through mainstream cultural assumptions.

[推测] The exchange reveals a fundamental barrier to mutual understanding: each side interprets moral disagreement through a different account of truth and authority.

[26:02] **A second visit and a clash of worldviews**

[事实] The Kirkers invite the reporting team back to Moscow, and Emily’s family hosts them for dinner to discuss the earlier article.

[事实] Emily objects to portraying Wilson as meticulously building an empire and says he has simply followed the tasks God placed before him.

[事实] She argues that no journalist is worldview-neutral and that the reporter should examine the moral foundations behind her own judgments.

[事实] Kirkers describe comfortable homes, large families, and close community as the “fruits” of living according to biblical values.

[推测] The conversation shifts the dispute from whether anyone is biased to which worldview should possess moral authority.

[31:14] **Transgender lives and competing definitions of flourishing**

[事实] The reporter offers the example of a happy transgender man and his family to challenge the claim that only the Kirkers’ beliefs produce good lives.

[事实] Emily and Nathaniel reject the person’s male identity and point to suicide rates among transgender people as evidence that transitioning does not bring happiness.

[事实] The reporters counter that discrimination may contribute to those suicide rates, while Nathaniel rejects the implication that religious disagreement makes Christians responsible.

[事实] Nathaniel also frames transgender access to bathrooms as a threat to his daughters.

[推测] The exchange shows that even the meaning of evidence is contested: one side reads suffering as proof against transgender identity, while the other points to social hostility as a cause.

[33:40] **Division, backlash, and the transformation of Moscow**

[事实] Kirkers report boycotts, vandalism, doxxing, insults, and broken personal relationships arising from local opposition to their beliefs.

[事实] Other Moscow residents say the Kirkers patronize their own businesses, avoid the local interfaith council, and are building a separate community rather than integrating into the town.

[事实] Residents identify protests against pandemic mask mandates as a turning point that made more people view Wilson’s church as an aggressor seeking to change Moscow.

[事实] National coverage of those protests also attracted new followers and contributed to an influx of Kirker families.

[推测] Conflict itself appears to advance Wilson’s project by reinforcing group identity, generating publicity, and drawing sympathetic newcomers.

[37:17] **The amplification dilemma**

[事实] Wilson says the Mother Jones article reflected a different worldview and contained inaccuracies, though he did not consider it a “hit piece.”

[事实] He and Nancy object to language and imagery they believe made the church look cult-like or made Wilson appear sinister.

[事实] Wilson says media attention has been “astoundingly beneficial” to his movement despite the abusive messages it sometimes generates.

[事实] The reporter worries that covering extremist views can amplify them, but also notes that Wilson can no longer easily be dismissed as a fringe figure.

[推测] The episode identifies a central journalistic dilemma: scrutiny may inform the public while also supplying a movement with attention, legitimacy, and recruitment material.

[40:37] **Canon Press as a media and commercial engine**

[事实] Wilson’s annual “No Quarter November” uses provocative videos and culture-war imagery to attract attention and promote Canon Press products.

[事实] Canon Press is a for-profit company selling books, curricula, audiobooks, and podcasts about Christian life; Wilson says it earns millions of dollars.

[事实] Its Moscow operation includes event space, professional studios, multiple podcasts, and staff producing and editing media.

[事实] A representative says employees are expected to align with the organization’s mission.

[推测] Canon Press converts ideological controversy into both cultural influence and commercial revenue, giving Wilson an independent mechanism for expanding his reach.

[44:08] **Cy Whitling’s departure from the church**

[事实] Cy Whitling grew up in a prominent Kirker family and learned that boys should prepare to fight for a Christian America against its enemies.

[事实] Journalism and documentary classes exposed him to human complexity that did not fit the church’s black-and-white worldview.

[事实] After leaving Moscow, forming a relationship with a non-Christian woman, and separating from the church, he lost contact with his family.

[事实] Cy says he is bisexual and considers his current life more nuanced, difficult, and fulfilling than the life prescribed for him in Moscow.

[推测] His story illustrates the potentially severe relational cost of leaving a community in which faith, family, occupation, and identity are tightly interconnected.

[48:14] **Linda’s return to certainty**

[事实] Linda left the church, became a left-wing activist, and worked on queer and housing issues in New York and Los Angeles.

[事实] After having a child and experiencing a difficult breakup, she returned to Moscow and concluded that biblical rules provided the order she needed.

[事实] Linda says her return freed her from depression and anxiety, comparing religious discipline to the tension that allows a violin string to make music.

[事实] The episode contrasts Cy’s freedom in nuance and complexity with Linda’s comfort in the black-and-white boundaries of God’s plan.

[推测] Their divergent experiences prevent the story from reducing either departure or return to a single psychological or moral explanation.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s chief strength is its sustained access. Rather than examining Christian nationalism only through speeches or policy statements, it enters homes, schools, businesses, worship services, and difficult conversations, showing how an ideology becomes an everyday social system.

Its use of contrasting voices is especially effective. Wilson and current members describe certainty, hospitality, discipline, and flourishing, while former members and other residents reveal estrangement, exclusion, and civic conflict. The paired stories of Cy and Linda provide a particularly strong ending because neither experience cancels the other.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode’s emphasis on personal encounters leaves some institutional questions only partially developed, including the full scale of the denomination, independent evidence concerning reported abuse, and the precise policy channels connecting Wilson’s teachings to Hegseth’s decisions.

[推测] The episode is especially valuable for listeners interested in Christian nationalism, conservative media networks, religion and gender, community polarization, and the ethical problem of reporting on influential extremist movements without inadvertently promoting them.
