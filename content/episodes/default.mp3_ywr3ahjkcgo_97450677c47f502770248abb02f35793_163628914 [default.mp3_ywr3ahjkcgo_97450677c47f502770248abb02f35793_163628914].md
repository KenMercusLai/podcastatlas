# Tomorrow Today with Shekhar Natarajan: Shalpa Rimpoche on Peace, Karma, Dzogchen, and AI

## 概览

This episode centers on a long conversation with His Eminence Shalpa Rimpoche about exile, Tibetan Buddhist training, compassion, karma, lineage, enlightenment, stillness, death, and the possibility of building a global culture of peace.

A major thread is the guest’s life story: his Tibetan family’s displacement, his childhood recognition as a reincarnate child, his rigorous spiritual education, his study in Varanasi, his relationship with Chhatral Rinpoche, and his later move to the United States to teach.

The later discussion turns toward present-day humanity: distraction, meaning scarcity, AI, blockchain, democracy, war, and the Universal Peace Sanctuary in Lumbini. The guest argues that peace must be grounded in unconditional love, personal clarity, and “power over mind,” while the host links these ideas to “Angelic Intelligence” and AI alignment.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it combines biography, Buddhist philosophy, and contemporary technology ethics in one conversation. The strongest parts are the guest’s concrete stories: his father’s escape, his childhood discipline, the fish release during his parents’ illness, and his relationship with Chhatral Rinpoche.

The discussion is most coherent when it explains recurring concepts through lived examples: karma as cause and effect, compassion as action, Dzogchen as completeness, stillness as non-conditioning, and breathing fully as practical presence rather than a mere technique.

The episode is less precise when it moves into large-scale claims about AI, blockchain, peace economies, and thousand-year institutions. Those ideas are presented with conviction, but the transcript gives limited operational detail beyond fundraising, transparency, peace clubs, and the Universal Peace Sanctuary’s planned facilities.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in spirituality, Tibetan Buddhist thought, peace-building, and AI ethics from a philosophical rather than technical perspective.
