# Scheduling Fitness with the Foundational Fitness Protocol

## 概览

This AMA excerpt focuses on how to schedule fitness so that resistance training and cardiovascular work happen often enough to support immediate and long-term health.

Huberman presents his foundational fitness protocol as a practical scaffold: three resistance sessions, three cardiovascular sessions, and one full rest day each week. He emphasizes that the exact days can shift, but the weekly targets give structure.

The main conclusion is that consistency matters more than perfect rigidity. Workouts can move earlier or later in the week, sessions can occasionally be combined, and a workout can sometimes be skipped when sleep, illness, stress, travel, or family life should take priority.

[03:50] **Weekly Structure of the Protocol**

[事实] The basic weekly structure is three resistance training sessions, three cardiovascular training sessions, and one complete rest day.
[事实] Huberman says none of the workouts is especially long, with the longest usually around 60 minutes and perhaps 75 minutes.
[事实] His day one is Monday leg training, including quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, and tibialis work.

[04:36] **Rest and Midweek Training**

[事实] The day after leg training is a complete day off, with optional deliberate heat or cold exposure.
[事实] The following training day can be a 20-30 minute cardiovascular session slightly faster than zone two.
[事实] If the legs are not recovered, he may instead do torso resistance training.

[05:19] **Torso Training**

[事实] The torso session includes pushing and pulling movements such as overhead press, dips, bench press, rows, pull-ups, or chin-ups.
[事实] He says the session should be tailored to avoid aggravating existing injuries or causing new ones.
[推测] Recovery and injury avoidance are treated as more important than following a bodybuilding-style split.

[06:06] **VO2 Max and Small Body Parts**

[事实] The high-intensity interval or VO2 max session is short, usually eight to 15 minutes total.
[事实] Huberman often uses an air dyne bike with about 20 seconds hard, 10 seconds rest, repeated for about eight cycles.
[事实] The small-body-parts session includes biceps, triceps, calves, neck, and abdominal work, usually lasting about 45-60 minutes.

[07:22] **Long Sunday Cardio**

[事实] Huberman’s Sunday session is long outdoor movement, often a ruck, hike, or weighted walk.
[事实] He describes using a weighted backpack, water jug, weight vest, or other practical loading options.
[事实] He does not measure heart rate during these workouts and values the outdoor, cardiovascular, and social benefits.

[09:18] **Building Flexibility Into the Week**

[事实] Huberman says the protocol is an ideal scaffold, but travel or life demands may require shifting workouts forward or backward by one day.
[事实] He typically does not skip leg day unless he is sick, extremely sleep deprived, or dealing with major stress.
[推测] The weekly plan is meant to guide decisions rather than function as a rigid rule.

[10:52] **Moving Leg Day and Managing Recovery**

[事实] If he moves leg day to Sunday, he may also do long slow distance cardio if he is well rested, well fed, not fighting illness, and has time.
[事实] If he trains legs hard on Tuesday, he often takes the next day completely off.
[事实] When he does resistance training two days in a row, he makes sure the third day is a complete rest day.

[事实] If he falls behind on cardiovascular training, he may combine a 20-30 minute jog with VO2 max work at the end.
[事实] His weekly target is to cover legs, torso, arms or smaller body parts, long slow distance cardio, moderate cardio, and high-intensity cardio.

[14:59] **Consistency and Skipping Workouts**

[事实] Huberman says people should adjust schedules according to life demands and individual fitness needs.
[事实] He says occasionally skipping a workout will not crater an entire fitness program.
[事实] He recommends aiming to complete about 85-95% of scheduled workouts.

[17:13] **Workout Timing, Caffeine, and Entrainment**

[事实] Huberman prefers morning workouts and often hydrates, takes electrolytes, and uses caffeine before training.

[事实] Huberman usually prioritizes sleep, especially when fatigued, run down, or exposed to colds and flu.
