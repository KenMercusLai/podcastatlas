# Manco’s Revolt and the Siege of Cusco

## 概览

This episode follows the Inca emperor Manco as he moves from Spanish-backed puppet ruler to leader of a major uprising against the conquistadors in Cusco.

The hosts argue that Spanish greed, sexual violence, encomienda expansion, factional rivalry, and the humiliation of Manco made collaboration impossible. Manco’s revolt nearly destroys the Spanish position, but Spanish cavalry, steel weapons, reinforcements, and Indigenous allies keep the conquest alive.

The episode ends with the siege of Cusco becoming a stalemate, Almagro returning from Chile to seize Cusco, and Manco withdrawing to Vilcabamba to establish an Inca state in exile.

## 分段落总结

[00:44] **Manco’s indictment of Spanish rule**

[事实] The episode opens with Manco denouncing the Spaniards as greedy, violent occupiers who plunder temples, exploit women, and seek to enslave the Incas.

[事实] The hosts identify this as Manco addressing a secret gathering in Cusco in autumn 1535.

[推测] The opening frames the revolt as a response to Spanish abuses while also noting that Manco idealizes earlier Inca rule.

[03:32] **The episode’s setup**

[事实] The hosts recap that Pizarro’s men had captured and killed Atahualpa, taken a huge ransom, entered Cusco, and installed Manco as puppet emperor.

[事实] This episode focuses on Manco’s uprising and a street-by-street battle for control of Cusco.

[事实] The hosts warn that the story will include large-scale death, torture, and reprisals.

[04:21] **Manco’s early legitimacy**

[事实] Spanish chroniclers portrayed Manco as noble and even heroic, though little is known about him before Pizarro made him ruler.

[事实] Manco was a brother of Huascar, had fled Atahualpa’s faction, and was probably in his late teens when he met Pizarro.

[事实] He was initially welcomed in Cusco and reported by Spanish observers to be a successful ruler.

[推测] Manco likely saw the Spaniards as a small group of useful military allies rather than as a permanent colonial force.

[07:45] **Restoration and fracture lines**

[事实] Manco built a new palace in Cusco, and the Spaniards did not initially push Christianisation as aggressively as in Mexico.

[事实] A priest, Cristobal de Molina, described an Inca ploughing festival in April 1535 with respect and detail.

[事实] The hosts identify three growing fracture lines: between Spaniards and Incas, among Incas, and among Spaniards.

[推测] The ritual scene created an illusion that order had returned after the Inca civil war.

[09:38] **Encomiendas and occupation**

[事实] Spaniards increasingly looted gold and silver, treated priests with contempt, and received landed estates called encomiendas.

[事实] New Spanish arrivals wanted gold, land, and labor, while Pizarro ignored royal requests to stop distributing land he did not own.

[事实] Reports from towns and villages suggested that local people no longer saw Spaniards as liberators but as occupiers.

[11:00] **Women, alliance, and coercion**

[事实] Spanish conquistadors commonly took local women as wives, mistresses, or concubines.

[事实] Pizarro had two children with Atahualpa’s half-sister Kispe Cusi, called Ines by the Spaniards.

[事实] The hosts stress that Inca women could be powerful political actors, but the transcript also describes coercion and sexual violence.

[推测] These relationships could create alliances, but they were shaped by conquest and unequal power.

[14:32] **Spanish factionalism and Manco’s dilemma**

[事实] Manco’s position weakened as some Inca elites questioned whether he was merely the Spaniards’ tool.

[事实] The feud between Francisco Pizarro and Diego de Almagro worsened after disputes over titles, ransom shares, and control of Cusco.

[事实] In Cusco, Almagro’s men clashed with the Pizarro faction led by Juan and Gonzalo Pizarro.

[16:18] **The Pizarro brothers humiliate Manco**

[事实] Juan Pizarro kidnapped Manco’s young unmarried sister Inquilcoya.

[事实] Gonzalo Pizarro pursued Cura Ocllo, Manco’s wife and sister, and threatened the high priest when challenged.

[事实] Manco tried offering silver and even disguising another woman to protect Cura Ocllo, but Gonzalo was not fooled.

[推测] The hosts suggest the brothers’ behavior may have combined desire, contempt, and the political value of high-born Inca women.

[20:43] **Almagro’s Chile expedition backfires**

[事实] Francisco Pizarro tried to reduce conflict by sending Almagro south to Chile with funding and the hope of finding new wealth.

[事实] Manco supplied 12,000 men under Vilac Umu and Paulo.

[事实] The expedition suffered badly on the Altiplano, and native porters were forced into brutal chain gangs.

[事实] Vilac Umu deserted in southern Bolivia, returned to Cusco, and urged Manco to resist Spanish domination.

[23:38] **Manco’s failed escape and public abuse**

[事实] In November 1535, Manco summoned a meeting, condemned Spanish greed and abuse, and called for resistance.

[事实] He tried to leave Cusco secretly, but a servant betrayed him to the Pizarro brothers.

[事实] The Spaniards captured him, chained him, beat him, spat and urinated on him, robbed him, abused his wives, and burned his eyelashes while demanding gold.

[事实] News of his treatment helped trigger uprisings, followed by severe Spanish reprisals.

[27:43] **The successful escape plan**

[事实] Hernando Pizarro returned from Spain in January 1536 and treated Manco with more courtesy than his brothers had.

[事实] Manco and Vilac Umu secretly prepared weapons, stockpiled food, and sent messages across the empire using khipus.

[事实] Manco escaped by telling Hernando he would retrieve a giant golden statue for him.

[推测] Manco’s plan worked because it exploited the Spaniards’ greed.

[30:18] **The Inca army gathers**

[事实] At Lares in the Sacred Valley, Manco found that chiefs had gathered about 100,000 people, armed and fed for the campaign.

[事实] The hosts describe this mobilisation as a final display of Inca organisational power.

[事实] Hernando had fewer than 200 Spaniards and about 500 Indigenous auxiliaries in Cusco.

[推测] This was Manco’s strongest chance to overturn Spanish rule in Peru.

[32:47] **The assault on Cusco**

[事实] On 6 May 1536, Manco ordered the assault on Cusco.

[事实] Inca forces broke through defences, entered the alleys and central square, and used heated sling stones wrapped in cotton to set thatched roofs on fire.

[事实] Smoke, flames, and noise left the Spanish defenders in chaos and fearing death.

[35:46] **Spanish survival inside the burning city**

[事实] The Spaniards retreated into two buildings at the edge of the central square.

[事实] Later Spanish stories credited the Virgin Mary with saving the buildings, but the hosts prefer the account that African slaves used buckets of water on the roofs.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that the conflict involved Spaniards, Inca factions, and other Indigenous groups on both sides.

[38:06] **The fight for Sacsayhuaman**

[事实] Hernando decided the Spaniards had to retake Sacsayhuaman, the citadel overlooking Cusco.

[事实] Juan Pizarro led 50 horsemen uphill through missiles and barricades.

[事实] A stone struck Juan Pizarro in the head after he had gone without a helmet because of an earlier wound.

[事实] Juan died after dictating a will that denied support to Inquilcoya and her child.

[43:05] **The citadel falls**

[事实] Gonzalo and Hernando continued the assault using scaling ladders.

[事实] Titucusi Gualpa defended the citadel fiercely, killed Incas who tried to surrender, and leapt to his death when the fortress was lost.

[事实] Hernando’s men killed about 1,500 people after taking the fortress.

[事实] The fall of Sacsayhuaman damaged Inca morale.

[45:33] **Stalemate and the limits of the revolt**

[事实] The hosts identify Spanish armor, steel weapons, cavalry, and Indigenous allies as major reasons the Spaniards survived.

[事实] Manco’s army was largely made up of peasants and farmers, many with families, and by August many drifted back to their fields.

[事实] Quiso Yupanqui destroyed several Spanish relief columns in the Andes by ambushing them with rocks and boulders.

[事实] Francisco Pizarro in Lima sent desperate letters after hearing nothing from Cusco for months.

[49:14] **Lima is threatened and saved**

[事实] Conta Huacho, Pizarro’s mother-in-law and a former queen of a native people, raised an army to help Lima.

[事实] Some Spaniards blamed the Pizarros for causing the rebellion through exploitation and torture.

[事实] Quiso Yupanqui marched on Lima, but Spanish cavalry defeated his forces on flat ground and killed him with many commanders.

[事实] Spanish reprisals after Lima was saved included mutilating prisoners.

[52:46] **Reinforcements favor the Spaniards**

[事实] In Cusco, the Spaniards remained surrounded but faced fewer attacks and began raiding for food and prisoners.

[事实] By the end of 1536, the siege had become a stalemate: the Spaniards could not get out, and the Incas could not get in.

[事实] The stalemate favored the Spaniards because reinforcements and new weapons continued arriving by sea.

[54:06] **Almagro returns and complicates the war**

[事实] In April 1537, Almagro returned from Chile dejected after finding no gold or silver.

[事实] He saw the siege as an opportunity to ally with Manco, end the war, and outshine the Pizarros.

[事实] Three forces now faced one another around Cusco: Hernando inside the city, Manco outside, and Almagro outside.

[57:05] **Manco tests Almagro**

[事实] Almagro sent Ruy Diaz to Manco at Ollantaytambo with promises of pardon and punishment for the Pizarros.

[事实] Hernando warned Manco not to trust Almagro.

[事实] Manco demanded that Almagro prove loyalty by executing four Spanish scouts, but Almagro refused.

[事实] Manco concluded that Spaniards would ultimately stand together and attacked Almagro unsuccessfully.

[58:47] **Ruy Diaz and the guava episode**

[事实] Ruy Diaz was stripped, smeared with mixtures, made to drink chicha, tied to a post, pelted with guava fruit, shaved, and had his hair cut.

[事实] The hosts treat this episode as strange and comparatively mild next to the other violence in the series.

[推测] The shaving and alteration of appearance may have had symbolic meaning, but the hosts say they cannot confidently explain it.

[61:00] **Manco withdraws to Vilcabamba**

[事实] Manco’s options narrowed because he could not capture Cusco and had lost any chance of agreement with Almagro.

[事实] He withdrew from Ollantaytambo, abandoned the Andean highlands, and moved through the Sacred Valley toward the jungle with idols and relics.

[事实] On 18 April, Almagro entered Cusco as conqueror, burned Pizarro houses, imprisoned Hernando and Gonzalo, and took control of the city.

[事实] Almagro installed Manco’s half-brother Paulo as a rival puppet Inca in July 1537.

[事实] Manco established headquarters at Vitcos in Vilcabamba, forming an Inca state in exile beyond easy Spanish reach.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strength is its clear explanation of why Manco’s revolt was not a sudden outburst but the result of cumulative Spanish abuse, political miscalculation, and collapsing trust.

[推测] It is especially valuable for listeners interested in conquest as a multi-sided civil and imperial conflict rather than a simple Spaniards-versus-Incas story.

[推测] The main limitation is that the discussion relies heavily on Spanish and later chroniclers, and the hosts themselves note that some speeches and interpretations may be shaped by classical literary conventions.

[推测] This episode suits listeners who want a dramatic but source-aware account of the fall of the Incas, including military detail, factional politics, and the brutal human cost of conquest.
