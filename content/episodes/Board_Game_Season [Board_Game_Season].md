# The Farming Game: How a Board Game Saved a Family Farm

## Overview

This episode tells the story of George and Anne Rohrbacher, who abandoned conventional careers in the 1970s to become farmers in Washington State. Their early success gave way to drought, crushing debt, and the wider American farm crisis, leaving their growing family at risk of losing everything.

George transformed their hardships into *The Farming Game*, a deliberately punishing simulation of agricultural life. The game saved their farm, taught generations of students about farming economics, and gave struggling rural families a way to discuss—and sometimes laugh about—their troubles.

The story ultimately presents farming as a model of life: preparation matters, but chance cannot be controlled. Its final lesson is that uncertain games are best played with other people, because companionship can create a form of victory even amid loss.

## 分段落总结

[00:12] **Games as Pocket Philosophies**

[事实] The host describes games as an art form that shapes what players want and how they may pursue it.

[事实] Every game therefore offers a particular way of understanding the world.

[事实] This episode follows a family that created a board game not merely to teach lessons, but to save its own livelihood.

[02:05] **A Frying Pan and an Instant Connection**

[事实] George Rohrbacher met Anne in 1969 after asking to borrow a frying pan so he could cook bacon for a BLT.

[事实] Their attraction was immediate, and within a week they decided to become engaged despite Anne’s parents’ reservations.

[事实] They married in the summer of 1970 after George graduated from the University of Colorado.

[08:03] **Going Back to the Land**

[事实] George and Anne traveled through 37 states over ten months and became fascinated with the 1970s back-to-the-land movement.

[事实] Unlike earlier homesteading movements driven largely by economic necessity, this movement emphasized self-sufficiency, environmentalism, sustainable communities, and reconnection with nature.

[事实] They accepted work as caretakers of a run-down duck-hunting club in Washington’s fertile Yakima Valley, where George learned farming and ranching from local farmers.

[12:34] **From First Farm to Breaks Ranch**

[事实] The couple purchased a 27-acre farm, worked long hours, expanded their operation, and had their first son, Blake.

[事实] A period of agricultural prosperity, rising land values, and readily available credit encouraged American farmers to expand.

[事实] George and Anne bought a remote thousand-acre property called Breaks Ranch even after its house burned down, then built a geodesic dome and moved into its unfinished basement.

[13:32] **The Policies Behind Agricultural Expansion**

[事实] Agriculture Secretary Earl Butz promoted larger, more industrialized farms and urged farmers to “get big or get out.”

[事实] Strong Soviet demand for American grain raised crop prices and farm incomes, while farmers borrowed heavily to purchase land, machinery, fertilizers, and other inputs.

[事实] The drive for maximum productivity contributed to the decline of older practices such as crop rotation and agricultural diversity.

[19:32] **Drought, Debt, and a Farm in Crisis**

[事实] In 1977, Breaks Ranch received only four inches of rain instead of its usual 25 to 30, sharply reducing its wheat yield and leaving too little hay for the cattle.

[事实] Another severe drought in 1979 caused the crop to fail while the family owed more in annual interest than it could earn.

[事实] Anne was pregnant with their third child, and the couple believed they might have to admit defeat and abandon the farm.

[21:24] **A Board Game Conceived in the Field**

[事实] While baling hay alone at 3 a.m. on July 7, 1979, George realized that his desperate situation could become the subject of a board game.

[事实] He drew the first board on scrap sheetrock, using its four sides to represent the seasons and incorporating farmers, bank debt, and the Yakima Valley.

[事实] George believed the game could provide an escape from the family’s financial crisis, although Anne initially thought the idea sounded irrational.

[24:26] **Turning Real Farming into Game Mechanics**

[事实] *The Farming Game* begins with inherited acreage, $5,000 of debt, and a $50,000 line of credit; its goal is to earn enough from farming to leave an off-farm job.

[事实] Players confront harvest uncertainty, operating expenses, additional borrowing, and possible bankruptcy.

[事实] The game’s misfortunes—including machinery failures, embargoes, wage garnishment, and the Mount St. Helens eruption—were based on events experienced by the Rohrbachers or their friends.

[事实] George and Anne repeatedly tested the game and adjusted its harsh economics so that success remained possible.

[26:20] **Betting the Ranch on Production**

[事实] The couple had no experience manufacturing games and discovered that different specialists were needed for the board, backing, money, dice, and acreage pieces.

[事实] Their family performed some assembly work at home, including cutting play money and bundling it with rubber bands.

[事实] In September 1979, they sold half their cattle, borrowed more money, and ordered 10,000 copies in hopes of reaching the Christmas market.

[29:06] **A Breakthrough in Sales**

[事实] George advertised in agricultural magazines and contacted radio broadcaster Paul Harvey, whose program was especially popular among farmers.

[事实] The producer could not independently confirm the Harvey broadcast, and George and Anne did not hear it themselves.

[事实] According to the couple and retailers, Harvey mentioned the game, after which demand rose sharply.

[事实] Within six weeks, they sold 7,000 of the initial 10,000 copies, while their daughter Laura was born amid the rush to package orders.

[30:54] **Finding the Game’s Core Audience**

[事实] George and Anne formed the Weekend Farmer Company, and George traveled to agricultural shows and small-town stores in a van carrying as many as 1,200 games.

[事实] Word of mouth among dedicated players helped sustain sales, while an 800 number routed customer orders directly into the family home.

[事实] The children grew up around the business and eventually helped answer calls and process orders.

[32:49] **The American Farm Crisis**

[事实] High interest rates, falling farm income, and banks calling in loans pushed heavily indebted farmers into a nationwide crisis during the early 1980s.

[事实] Family farm income fell by more than 80 percent within a few years, and the USDA estimated in 1981 that roughly half of American farmers would eventually be driven from their land.

[事实] Farm failures also damaged rural businesses and communities, while Black farmers lost land at ten times the rate of white farmers.

[事实] Farmers experienced the loss not simply as unemployment but as the destruction of family history, identity, and inherited responsibility.

[35:31] **A Game Becomes an Advocacy and Teaching Tool**

[事实] Mental-health programs used *The Farming Game* to help farmers relieve stress and discuss their difficulties.

[事实] An agricultural women’s advocacy group distributed 535 copies to members of Congress to explain the farm crisis, although the game did not directly produce legislation.

[事实] According to George, more than 3,000 North American classrooms used the game to teach agricultural economics.

[事实] The game later traveled to Russia with support from the U.S. Agency for International Development to illustrate privatized farming, and it eventually sold hundreds of thousands of copies.

[37:46] **The Game Saves the Rohrbacher Farm**

[事实] Revenue from the game allowed George and Anne to keep Breaks Ranch operating while many other family farms disappeared.

[事实] Their home doubled as the company’s workplace, and their children grew up among order forms, boxes, dice, play money, and constantly ringing telephones.

[事实] The farm also gave the children a life immersed in changing seasons, wildlife, snow, creeks, and waterfalls—the life their parents had risked everything to preserve.

[40:28] **Relief and Connection Around the Table**

[事实] Farm families remained the game’s largest audience, particularly during years when rural America was under severe strain.

[事实] The game allowed players to turn crop failures and financial misfortune into shared experiences that could provoke laughter rather than despair.

[事实] At an agricultural exposition, a farmer told George that regular Sunday games had helped reconnect him with his two sons, one of whom had recently been jailed.

[事实] The farmer thanked George and said the game had saved his boys.

[43:28] **A Counter-Cyclical Legacy**

[事实] The game has remained in print since 1979 and tended to sell best during difficult periods, including the financial crisis and the COVID pandemic.

[事实] Its success separated the Rohrbachers’ fate from that of many small family farmers displaced during the 1980s crisis.

[事实] The episode notes that nearly 40 percent of Americans lived on farms at the beginning of the twentieth century, compared with about one percent today.

[事实] Although George and Anne intended to make a board game, the episode characterizes their creation as a record—and eulogy—for a disappearing way of life.

[44:30] **Playing the Story in Miniature**

[事实] The producer played *The Farming Game* with George, Anne, and members of their family in Colorado.

[事实] George quickly went bankrupt in his own game, reflecting Anne’s observation that he tends either to win big or lose big.

[事实] Their contrasting styles—George the optimistic dreamer and Anne the cautious pragmatist—recreated the dynamics of their life together.

[46:11] **Farming as a Model of Life**

[事实] The producer concludes that agricultural knowledge is only part of the game’s meaning; it also explores risk, uncertainty, care, and human partnership.

[事实] George says farmers must be optimists or they would never plant seeds.

[事实] Farming shows that people can prepare but cannot control every outcome, and that cared-for things may grow through both good and bad years.

[事实] The episode’s final conclusion is that games of chance are best played with someone else, because shared experience makes it possible to win even when one loses.
