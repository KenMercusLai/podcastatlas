# Late July Snacks: Nicole Bernard Dawes, Crackers, Cookies, and the Tortilla Chip Pivot

## 概览

This episode follows Nicole Bernard Dawes, founder of Late July Snacks, from her childhood inside a natural-food and potato-chip family business to her own long attempt to build a better organic snack brand.

The central business problem is that Late July launched with strong distribution but without a product that moved fast enough. Organic crackers earned early excitement, sandwich cookies were beloved but hard to make profitable, and the company faced a severe 2009 crisis after Nicole’s father died and a major loan was called.

The turning point was tortilla chips. They matched Nicole’s taste-first organic mission, worked for allergy-conscious families, fit a much larger snack category, and ultimately transformed Late July into a fast-growing brand that later became part of Campbell’s through Snyder’s Lance.

## 分段落总结

[00:08] **Early national orders did not equal demand**

[事实] Late July left its 2003 trade-show debut with major orders and national distribution.
[事实] Nicole says some retailers were excited about organic snacks before their shoppers understood organic.
[事实] About two months after the show, sales “disappeared completely.”
[推测] The opening frames the episode as a lesson in the gap between retailer enthusiasm and repeat consumer demand.

[03:02] **Cape Cod Chips shaped Nicole’s product worldview**

[事实] Nicole grew up on Cape Cod; her mother ran a health food store and her father Steve founded Cape Cod potato chips.
[事实] Steve was inspired by an article about old-fashioned chips and opened a small Hyannis storefront.
[事实] The chip company nearly failed, but a car crash into the storefront brought newspaper attention and insurance money that helped it survive.
[推测] Nicole’s later belief in better-tasting natural snacks came from seeing her father prove that “healthier” did not have to mean inferior.

[08:45] **Cape Cod was sold, bought back, and sold again**

[事实] Steve sold Cape Cod Chips to Anheuser-Busch in 1985.
[事实] Around 1995-1996, he bought the brand back for under $3 million after Anheuser-Busch divested its snack division.
[事实] Nicole joined the rebuilt company, worked in marketing and innovation, and developed a reduced-fat chip.
[事实] Steve later sold Cape Cod again to Lance for about $30 million, which Nicole says devastated her.
[推测] These sales taught Nicole that investors, founders, and family members can have very different timelines.

[16:36] **Late July began as a search for a scalable organic snack**

[事实] After Cape Cod’s second sale, Nicole wanted to build her own food business.
[事实] She first experimented with an organic snack mix, but her father warned it would be expensive and hard to scale.
[事实] While pregnant, she noticed that natural-food-store cracker aisles still felt outdated compared with mainstream snack categories.
[事实] Late July launched as an organic cracker brand alongside the emergence of the USDA Organic Seal.
[推测] The brand combined Nicole’s Cape Cod summer imagery with a practical bet on a large, underdeveloped organic category.

[21:59] **Bootstrapping exposed early organic supply-chain limits**

[事实] Nicole used a small amount of money from the Cape Cod sale, hired a designer, used a co-packer, and was initially the only employee.
[事实] She brought her father out of retirement to help, and they did not take salaries for years.
[事实] Organic suppliers for ingredients such as flour and cheese were scarce.
[事实] Late July launched at Natural Products Expo East in Baltimore and signed Whole Foods nationally.
[推测] The USDA Organic Seal created credibility, but the ingredient and retail systems around organic packaged snacks were still immature.

[26:34] **Crackers moved too slowly**

[事实] Nicole’s husband Peter joined the company, bringing a film, marketing, and creative background.
[事实] Late July realized crackers had much lower purchase velocity than potato chips.
[事实] Nicole says people might finish a bag of chips quickly, while crackers are more occasional.
[事实] The company was small, under-marketed, and using outside manufacturing.
[推测] The product tasted good, but the category did not generate the speed a national brand needed.

[31:38] **Cookies deepened the profit problem**

[事实] Late July did not yet have a hero product and was not growing as fast as hoped.
[事实] The company tried sandwich cookies made with premium organic ingredients such as real dark chocolate and Madagascar vanilla.
[事实] Nicole says the cookies were very difficult to make profitable, especially on promotion.
[事实] In 2007, Snyder’s Lance bought about 20% of Late July and provided access to a Georgia manufacturing facility.
[推测] The strategic investment solved some operational problems but also foreshadowed a likely future acquisition path.

[35:14] **The 2009 crisis nearly broke the company**

[事实] Around 2008, Late July had about $8 million in sales, including about $2 million from cookies that needed to be discontinued.
[事实] Nicole’s father was diagnosed with terminal cancer and died in March 2009.
[事实] A bank called a roughly $3.5 million equipment loan into default under a death-of-a-member clause.
[事实] Nicole’s youngest son was also found to have a life-threatening peanut allergy, while the company’s top product was a peanut butter cracker.
[推测] The crisis combined grief, family risk, financing risk, and product-strategy risk at once.

[40:12] **Mission-aligned help bought Late July time**

[事实] Nicole convinced the bank to give the company more time.
[事实] At Expo East, she told Late July’s story to Meg Hirschberg, who introduced her to Gary Hirschberg of Stonyfield.
[事实] Gary invested, and Nicole was introduced to RSF Finance, which agreed to take over the debt as a loan.
[事实] Nicole says entrepreneurs with debt should consider key person insurance.
[推测] Candor and mission alignment helped Late July find financing when conventional capital was scarce.

[45:01] **Tortilla chips became the decisive pivot**

[事实] Nicole and Peter wanted Late July to become a broader snack company.
[事实] Tortilla chips fit the brand because they could be organic, naturally gluten-free, naturally nut-free, and inclusive.
[事实] Nicole says if tortilla chips had not worked, the company likely would have failed.
[事实] The line launched in 2010 after the company hired a manufacturing person to reduce strain on Peter.
[推测] Tortilla chips were both a market pivot and the product Nicole felt she had wanted since childhood.

[51:40] **Stop & Shop unlocked conventional distribution**

[事实] Whole Foods gave the tortilla chips a chance, but launched them alongside Kettle’s Tia’s, which received more display support.
[事实] Late July needed conventional distribution, and a distributor agreed to help if Stop & Shop accepted the product.
[事实] Nicole’s major sales call with Stop & Shop ended with the buyer saying yes in the room.
[事实] She calls that the moment that made Late July an overnight success seven years in the making.
[推测] The Stop & Shop win turned the product from a hopeful launch into a scalable retail strategy.

[56:06] **The hero product changed Late July’s scale**

[事实] Nicole says the tortilla chips tasted excellent and arrived when few comparable organic, healthy options existed.
[事实] Late July went from roughly $8 million in sales and barely holding on to being on track for $100 million within about three years.
[事实] The company stayed lean, with about 27 employees around the time it reached roughly $100 million in sales.
[事实] A Late July product won a taste test over Tostitos.
[推测] The brand’s value came from taste, positioning, and distribution rather than owning factories.

[59:16] **Selling control was painful but practical**

[事实] Around 2014, Snyder’s Lance increased its ownership from about 19% to 80%.
[事实] Nicole says the decision helped early investors, including her mother, get liquidity after years of risk.
[事实] Gary Hirschberg helped structure the deal so Nicole and Peter still effectively controlled the board.
[事实] Campbell’s later acquired or merged with Snyder’s Lance in 2018, bringing Late July into Campbell’s portfolio.
[推测] Nicole came to understand her father’s earlier sale decisions more fully once she had to balance her own wishes against investors’ needs.

[63:11] **The exit turned on contract language and emotion**

[事实] Late July had a clause allowing Nicole’s side to buy it back if Snyder’s sold, but the transaction was treated as a merger rather than a sale.
[事实] Nicole says this showed how critical contractual language is.
[事实] She chose not to spend years in litigation and says Campbell’s has protected and grown the brand.
[事实] Her final Late July chapter felt heavy and sad at the Newport Folk Festival, where the brand was a sponsor and where her parents had met decades earlier.
[推测] For Nicole, losing Late July was not just a financial event; it ended a family-linked chapter of identity and work.

[66:05] **Nixie carried the mission into beverages**

[事实] Nicole started Nixie after noticing that the beverage aisle had few organic options and many products with sugar or plastic packaging.
[事实] Nixie uses organic flavors in sparkling water and later sparkling soda, with no calories and no sugar.
[事实] Nicole self-funded Nixie at first so she could understand velocity and bestsellers before considering investors.
[事实] COVID hit just as Nixie was entering stores, but Nicole says every brand was dealing with that disruption at the same time.
[推测] Nixie applies Late July’s core playbook to a harder category: lead with taste, then build loyalty through mission.

[73:16] **Taste, work, and luck are Nicole’s closing lessons**

[事实] Nicole says taste is everything because consumers only buy a product again if they like it.
[事实] She says mission builds connection, but taste remains the most important factor.
[事实] She is motivated by food, customers’ everyday moments, employees’ growth, and giving programs tied to employee-chosen charities.
[事实] She says she was lucky to be born into an entrepreneurial family, but survival required tenacity, hard work, and not giving up.
[推测] The episode’s final argument is that luck may open a door, but execution determines whether a company lasts.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode is valuable for food and consumer-packaged-goods founders because it makes category velocity, trade-show hype, co-manufacturing, strategic investment, and retail distribution feel concrete rather than abstract.

[推测] Its strongest moments are the candid crisis stories: the failed early national launch, the unprofitable cookies, the bank default after Steve’s death, and the Stop & Shop sales call that changed the company’s trajectory.

[推测] The limitation is that the transcript gives less detail on margins, exact deal terms, and Nixie’s current scale, so listeners looking for a financial teardown will only get part of the picture. The episode is best suited for founders, brand builders, retail operators, and listeners interested in family businesses and mission-driven consumer brands.
