# Tomorrow Today: Andy Stalman on Branding, AI, and Human Value

## 概览

This episode features Shekhar Natarajan speaking with Andy Stalman, introduced as “Mr. Branding,” about the meaning of brands, how brands are built, and why protecting a brand now includes culture, leadership, IP, AI, and human trust.

The conversation follows Stalman’s life from Buenos Aires to Spain, his early career across media, Lacoste, airports, and entrepreneurship, then moves into his framework for branding: identity, values, purpose, consistency, sensory experience, and “brand doing.”

A major thread is that AI does not replace the need for brand clarity. Instead, both Stalman and Shekhar argue that AI exposes organizational gaps: misaligned values, weak internal culture, privacy risks, and leaders who optimize short-term results while neglecting meaning, engagement, and trust.

## 分段落总结

[01:28] **Opening And Conversation Scope**

[事实] Shekhar introduces Andy Stalman as “Mr. Branding” and says he wants to discuss what a brand is, how to create and protect one, and what it takes to build a beautiful brand.
[事实] Shekhar also frames the episode around Stalman’s personal journey, including suffering, challenge, and inspiration.

[02:38] **Argentina, Crisis, And Global Perspective**

[事实] Stalman says he was born in Buenos Aires, Argentina, a country he describes as vibrant but repeatedly caught in crisis.
[事实] He argues that living through crisis creates creativity, resilience, and innovation.
[事实] Moving from Argentina to Barcelona and Madrid gave him a balance between young and old cultures, south and north, instability and stability.
[推测] His branding worldview is shaped by seeing culture, history, and economic conditions across very different contexts.

[05:58] **Family, Values, And Roots**

[事实] Stalman says his wife is Argentinian and that he has three children: Felipe, a daughter studying law and business administration, and Max.
[事实] He says his children are different from one another but connected by values such as respect, responsibility, and being good people.
[事实] His grandparents came from Poland, Ukraine, Spain, and the UK, and he describes his wider family as global.
[事实] He describes his father as hardworking, intelligent, and originally from a poor family in Patagonia, and his mother as extroverted and creative.

[09:43] **Early Work Ethic And First Career Steps**

[事实] Stalman says many Argentinians start working soon after high school, often graduating university with several years of work experience.
[事实] He links this work ethic to immigrant family values around effort, sacrifice, education, ethics, and respect.
[事实] He says working while studying helped shape his character for later challenges.
[推测] He presents early work not as hardship alone, but as preparation for entrepreneurship and leadership.

[13:07] **Media, Lacoste, Airports, And Learning By Doing**

[事实] Stalman worked in media and says he was part of a small team that launched the first 24-hour Spanish-language news channel in the world.
[事实] At Lacoste, he was marketing director for the Southern Cone and worked on keeping the brand valuable while making it relevant to younger generations.
[事实] At Aeropuertos Argentina 2000, he became marketing and commercial director at age 27 or 28 and helped shift revenue toward off-air income such as shops, retail, and food and beverage.
[事实] He says the airport team was young, passionate, and innovative, and that one person he hired later became the company’s CEO.

[18:42] **Leaving Argentina During Crisis**

[事实] Stalman moved to Barcelona in September 2002 with his wife and older son.
[事实] He describes Argentina’s 2001 crisis, including the “corralito,” when people could not access money held in banks.
[事实] He explains the 1990s peso-dollar convertibility system and says the apparent prosperity collapsed when there were not enough dollars to support it.
[事实] He says Argentina has strong talent in areas such as technology, culture, agriculture, design, and business, but chaos often drowns out innovation.

[25:46] **From Employee To Entrepreneur**

[事实] Stalman says Lacoste taught him to protect brand legacy, values, positioning, quality, aspiration, sport, and lifestyle.
[事实] He says Aeropuertos Argentina 2000 taught him how global and connected business can be, and how to challenge the status quo.
[事实] Moving to Spain also meant ending 15 years of working for others and beginning to work for himself.
[事实] He says a steady salary, company car, phone, and benefits can feel safe, but at that stage he wanted meaning, purpose, and another way to connect business.

[28:44] **Building A Branding Business Across Markets**

[事实] Stalman started a company in Spain with Australian designer Ken Cato.
[事实] He says the company quickly expanded its view from Spain to Europe, and then received interest from Latin American markets including Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Argentina, Brazil, and Chile.
[事实] He says that in 2002, getting known was difficult because there were no smartphones and public visibility depended heavily on media such as Financial Times or Forbes.

[31:04] **The Birth Of Totem**

[事实] Stalman says internet, social media, technology, and omnichannel change created a wave that required rethinking the business.
[事实] After leaving his earlier company, he had a personal brand but no corporate brand.
[事实] He co-founded Totem with Graham, a British partner living in Australia.
[事实] The intended name was initially Tribe, but it was unavailable; Totem became the registered name and Stalman later judged it easier to pronounce and stronger globally.

[35:01] **Totem Methodology And Brand Identity**

[事实] Totem is described as a privately owned branding company doing end-to-end projects for country brands, city brands, hotels, airlines, consumer goods, technology, agriculture, and institutions.
[事实] Stalman says most companies can answer what they do, how they do it, when they do it, and where they do it, but struggle with why they do it and who they are.
[事实] He connects brand with identity, personality, purpose, values, value proposition, promise, talent, leadership, and impact.
[事实] He says a brand is not only what people see, but also what they feel, hear, smell, touch, taste, and experience through technology.

[38:00] **Consistency, Culture, And Organizational Pain**

[事实] Shekhar says large brands often have a gap between what they say and what they do, and between internal and external communication.
[事实] Stalman agrees that inconsistency, different internal and external messages, and misalignment between leadership and teams are organizational pain points.
[事实] Stalman says many leaders lack the humility to admit these problems.
[推测] The discussion treats branding problems as operational and cultural problems, not just marketing problems.

[40:09] **CEO Incentives And Brand Responsibility**

[事实] Stalman says many CEOs work toward bonuses, revenue, and profit rather than engagement, commitment, relationships, or culture.
[事实] He argues that admired leaders pursue results but not at any cost.
[事实] He says values are not words on a wall; they solve dilemmas, answer questions, and create impact.
[事实] He says Gen Z workers seek purpose and meaning, not only salary.

[42:24] **AI As A Mirror Of The Brand**

[事实] Stalman says brands and AI are mirrors of the organization.
[事实] He argues that adding AI to a bad brand exposes the weakness, while adding AI to a strong brand can amplify its strengths.
[事实] He says AI needs to know the organization to help it act consistently.
[事实] He says a company with 10,000 employees may produce 10,000 answers if it has not aligned people behind DNA, culture, values, and purpose.

[43:49] **Actionable Values And Brand Behavior**

[事实] Shekhar uses Amazon as an example of a company whose value statements help resolve dilemmas and guide decisions.
[事实] He contrasts actionable values with vague phrases such as “think global, act local.”
[事实] Shekhar says values decay as they move down organizational layers if they are not reinforced clearly.
[事实] Stalman says this returns to why Totem matters: brands should connect people through values, causes, culture, principles, and philosophy.

[48:39] **From Branding To Brand Doing**

[事实] Stalman introduces “brand doing” as an evolution of branding.
[事实] He says the brand is what it does, not what it says.
[事实] He says “10 grams of doing is better than 100 grams of saying.”
[事实] He identifies Amazon as an example of consistency in what a brand represents, solves, and delivers.

[50:18] **Collaboration And Unresolved Deliberation**

[事实] Shekhar says collaboration cannot just be written on a wall because humans are competitive and collaboration must be designed into processes.
[事实] He says most company problems are unresolved deliberations.
[事实] He argues that unresolved deliberations create paralysis, bureaucracy, lethargy, and delayed decisions.
[推测] This extends the brand discussion into management systems: values only matter if they change how decisions are made.

[52:14] **Angelic Intelligence And AI Alignment**

[事实] Shekhar explains “angelic intelligence” as a concept where technology acts like a guiding force that amplifies human beauty and nudges people in the right direction.
[事实] He says alignment in AI will be a major problem and that nature often realigns through catastrophe.
[事实] He says he wants people to understand what angelic intelligence means, why it exists, and what its purpose is.
[推测] Shekhar is positioning angelic intelligence as a human-centered alternative to purely efficiency-driven AI.

[56:01] **Human Layer Before Technology Hype**

[事实] Stalman says Shekhar’s approach is interesting because it brings a human layer through technology rather than the other way around.
[事实] He says the name helps people understand something unseen that guides them through turbulent times.
[事实] He says the hard part will be helping people reconnect with their essence because the current hype is AI, productivity, automation, and efficiency.
[事实] He says love, beauty, and human beings should be central but are not currently the hype.

[58:06] **Sports, AI, And Love**

[事实] Stalman says that at a 2023 AI conference for sports business, his keynote was titled around the idea that the only safe bet for sports in the future is not AI, but love.
[事实] He says a FIFA vice president told him the point was true because technology changes, while love transcends time and space.
[事实] He argues that people may love Messi, but they usually use or need technology rather than love it.
[推测] Stalman uses sports to show that emotional attachment can be more durable than technological advantage.

[60:22] **Technology, Slavery, And Basic Execution**

[事实] Stalman says technology should ease human duties and create free time for reading, studying, playing, or asking new questions.
[事实] He says many people have become slaves of technology instead.
[事实] He asks how organizations can talk about superintelligence if they still cannot answer the phone or reply to an Instagram DM.
[推测] His critique is that AI ambition is hollow when basic customer and organizational behaviors are broken.

[61:48] **AI Agents, Leakage, And Brand Risk**

[事实] Shekhar describes a study involving thousands of Twitter posts generated by bots and says agents began to mimic users’ tone over time.
[事实] He says the study showed agents could leak information and mentions a 36% leakage figure.
[事实] He argues that highly knowledgeable AI systems become vulnerable points in an ecosystem, especially if a CEO or CFO’s personal model is hacked.
[事实] He asks how brands can be protected in the world of AI.

[65:21] **Protecting Brand Value In An AI World**

[事实] Stalman says companies are working on homemade AI models to avoid leaks into outside models.
[事实] He says brand protection now depends on having enough brand value to survive reputation, privacy, or theft crises.
[事实] Shekhar uses Coca-Cola as an example where the difference between production cost and sale price comes from brand value.
[事实] Stalman says Zara’s brand value recently became larger than Nike’s and that Zara contributes significantly to Inditex’s company value.

[69:44] **Brand Value, IP, And CEO Mindset**

[事实] Stalman says protecting the brand means protecting both brand value and company value.
[事实] He says studies suggest brand value can represent around 35% to 40% of value for S&P 500 companies.
[事实] He argues that branding should be judged as valuable or not valuable, not simply cheap or expensive.
[事实] He says registration and IP protection are mandatory, but not sufficient in an AI context.

[71:13] **Employee Alignment And The CEO As Brand Guardian**

[事实] Stalman says 85% of employees worldwide are not engaged with the company and brand they work for.
[事实] He says alignment must start from the head of the company.
[事实] He argues that the president or CEO, not only the CMO, must act as brand guardian.
[事实] He says brand value includes engagement, commitment, delivery, and value creation, not only revenue or balance sheet value.

[72:47] **Brand Value Trends And Leadership**

[事实] Shekhar asks whether brand value has gone up or down when technology hype is stripped out.
[事实] Stalman says most brand values have gone up, although some brands such as Nike are going through difficult times.
[事实] He says technology has been the hype for the past quarter century.
[事实] He argues that mature companies should not depend on a leader shining brighter than the brand itself.

[77:24] **S-Curves, Innovation, Alphabet, And Zara**

[事实] Shekhar says companies need to know where they are on the S-curve and when to start innovating again.
[事实] He uses Steve Jobs and Apple as an example of repeatedly pulling the company into new product curves.
[事实] Stalman says Alphabet faced pressure from AI and LLMs, but used Gemini and AI to create a new curve from the top of the existing one.
[事实] Stalman says Zara was late to online selling, but its online shop became the number one shop in the network.

[80:44] **Rapid Fire On Brands And Platforms**

[事实] Stalman names Ryanair as an underrated brand because people criticize it without understanding the strategy behind its low-cost model.
[事实] He describes Apple in one word as “slaves,” saying once people enter the Apple ecosystem they cannot leave.
[事实] He says the biggest lie in brand strategy is “we are customer centric.”
[事实] He says most CMOs do not understand the importance of branding.

[83:19] **AI, Social Networks, And LinkedIn**

[事实] Stalman says AI’s effect on branding is positive because technology is here to stay and brands must figure it out.
[事实] He predicts that probably 80% of current social networks will be irrelevant in five years.
[事实] He says LinkedIn gets positioning right because it is top of mind for professional networking and jobs, even if the network itself is average.

[84:27] **Place, Personal Brand, And Love**

[事实] When asked Buenos Aires or Barcelona, Stalman says he does not have a divided heart but two hearts: one for Spain and one for Argentina.
[事实] When asked what part of his personal brand he would not change, he answers “Mr. Branding.”
[事实] He says the most powerful thing any brand can do is love.
[事实] He calls love the most powerful emotion in life and says it gives meaning, inspiration, and connection.

[85:55] **Suffering, Recovery, And Regret**

[事实] Stalman says people usually face several life moments that can break them.
[事实] He says the important thing is not whether one falls, but how one stands up afterward.
[事实] He says life has more wonderful moments than bad moments, even though bad moments can be deeply painful.
[事实] He says people near the end of life often regret not spending more time with family and friends, not being more loyal to what they felt, and not loving more.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is that it treats branding as a whole-company discipline rather than a marketing surface. Stalman repeatedly connects brand to culture, leadership, employee engagement, customer perception, and operational behavior.

[推测] The best moments come when branding and AI intersect: the discussion makes clear that AI can amplify identity, but it can also expose weak alignment, leak sensitive knowledge, and create new risks around trust and IP.

[推测] The episode is most useful for founders, brand leaders, CMOs, CEOs, and AI product leaders thinking about how values become behavior. Its limitation is that several claims and statistics are mentioned conversationally without detailed sourcing in the transcript.

[推测] The closing makes the conversation more personal: Stalman’s emphasis on love, resilience, and meaning reframes branding as a human commitment rather than only a commercial asset.
