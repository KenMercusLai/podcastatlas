# The July Crisis: Russia, France, and the Austrian Ultimatum

## 概览

This episode follows the July Crisis from the perspective of Russia and France as President Raymond Poincaré visits St Petersburg in July 1914, just before Austria-Hungary delivers its ultimatum to Serbia.

The central argument is that Russia’s stance was driven less by vague pan-Slav sentiment than by strategic anxiety over the Balkans, the Turkish Straits, grain exports, the Black Sea, and repeated recent humiliations by Austria-Hungary.

France, meanwhile, is presented as anxious, revanchist, and deeply committed to binding Russia into a common front against Germany. The episode stresses that both alliances behaved as if firmness would force the other side to back down.

By the end, Austria’s ultimatum has reached Serbia, Russia has signaled support to Belgrade, and Sazanov is pushing toward partial mobilization. The hosts frame the crisis as a chain of defensive calculations that rapidly becomes almost impossible to reverse.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Poincaré Arrives In St Petersburg**

[事实] The episode opens with Maurice Paléologue’s recollection of President Poincaré arriving in Russia on 20 July 1914.

[事实] The hosts describe the meeting between the French president and Tsar Nicholas II as a crucial moment because France and Russia were allied on Germany’s western and eastern flanks.

[事实] Austria-Hungary has already prepared its ultimatum to Serbia but is waiting for the French delegation to leave Russia before delivering it.

[推测] The timing makes the summit more dangerous because French and Russian leaders are consolidating their position without yet knowing the exact Austrian move.

[02:41] **Russia’s Strategic Anxiety**

[事实] Russia is described as a huge multi-ethnic empire of 164 million people, ruled by Nicholas II since 1894.

[事实] After defeat in the Russo-Japanese War and the 1905 revolution, Russia had turned attention back toward Europe, especially its alliance with France and the Balkans.

[事实] The hosts argue that Russia’s interest in the Balkans was not only about pan-Slav solidarity or Orthodoxy, but also about control of the Bosphorus and access from the Black Sea to the Mediterranean.

[事实] Russia’s economy depended heavily on Ukrainian grain exports, so closure of the Straits would be a major strategic and economic threat.

[推测] Russia saw Serbian survival and Balkan influence as part of a wider struggle to prevent hostile control over routes vital to its power.

[06:16] **Serbia, Past Crises, And The “Never Again” Mood**

[事实] Serbia had doubled in size after the Balkan Wars and had become Russia’s client in the region.

[事实] Russia and Serbia had previously backed down over Austria’s annexation of Bosnia and over Serbia’s attempt to gain access to the Adriatic through Albania.

[事实] Austria interpreted these retreats as proof that Russia would back down again, while Russia drew the opposite lesson and felt it could not retreat a third time.

[事实] Russia was modernizing quickly, investing in armaments, training, and railways, and its army was expected to become larger than Germany’s and Austria’s combined.

[推测] The crisis was sharpened by a clash between Austrian confidence and Russian fear of further loss of prestige.

[08:06] **Ottoman Dreadnoughts And The Straits**

[事实] The hosts cite Dominic Lieven’s book *Towards the Flame* as a major account of Russia’s role in the origins of the war.

[事实] Russian leaders feared not only Ottoman decline but also a possible Ottoman naval recovery.

[事实] The Ottoman Empire had ordered dreadnoughts from British firms, while Russia had none in the Black Sea.

[事实] Foreign minister Sergei Sazanov warned that Ottoman naval expansion could destroy Russia’s ambitions around the Straits.

[推测] This gave Russian decision-makers a sense that time was not on their side.

[09:22] **Sazanov’s Worldview**

[事实] Sergei Sazanov had been Russian foreign minister since 1910 and is described as anti-German, pro-Serb, nervous, polite, and personally decent by contemporary accounts.

[事实] He believed Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire were declining powers, while Serbia represented a younger force with a historical mission.

[事实] After the assassination of Franz Ferdinand, Sazanov developed a narrative that Austria was using the murder as a pretext against Serbia and that Germany was the real force behind Austria.

[事实] The hosts note that this interpretation of 1914 remains influential.

[推测] Sazanov’s assumptions made it harder for him to treat Austria’s grievance as genuine or limited.

[13:03] **Rumors Of The Ultimatum**

[事实] In mid-July, Russian officials heard rumors that Austria-Hungary was preparing an ultimatum.

[事实] Russia had intercepted telegrams suggesting Austria was sounding out Italy about its planned action.

[事实] Sazanov warned that if Austria acted against Serbia, there would be a “world inferno.”

[事实] He said Russia could not tolerate Austria moving against Serbia.

[推测] Russia was already emotionally and strategically committed before the ultimatum was formally delivered.

[14:02] **France’s Position After 1870**

[事实] France is described as smaller and less demographically powerful than Germany by 1914, despite retaining enormous cultural prestige.

[事实] The trauma of the Franco-Prussian War, the German proclamation at Versailles, and the loss of Alsace-Lorraine remained central to French politics.

[事实] Poincaré came from Lorraine, and his childhood had been shaped by German occupation and displacement.

[事实] France’s alliance with Russia, signed in 1894, had diplomatic, financial, and cultural dimensions.

[推测] French policy combined defensive fear of Germany with a revanchist hope of recovering lost territory.

[18:19] **French Militarism And The Russian Alliance**

[事实] The hosts discuss a growing prestige of war in pre-1914 French public culture.

[事实] Poincaré supported the three-year military service law, which raised French troop levels closer to Germany’s despite France’s smaller population.

[事实] France is described as the most militarized society in Western Europe per capita.

[事实] French leaders wanted to keep Russia closely bound through loans, railway financing, and strategic coordination.

[推测] France did not necessarily seek war immediately, but it wanted any future war to occur under conditions favorable to France and Russia.

[22:08] **French Public Opinion And The St Petersburg Visit**

[事实] French public attention after Sarajevo was partly absorbed by the Caillaux scandal, in which Henriette Caillaux had shot the editor of *Le Figaro*.

[事实] The hosts say there was relatively little French sympathy for Franz Ferdinand compared with Britain.

[事实] French press discussion of the Balkans tended to defend Serbia and criticize Austrian accusations.

[事实] Poincaré traveled to St Petersburg with prime minister René Viviani, who is described as less bellicose and increasingly stressed during the visit.

[推测] French domestic reaction gave Poincaré little reason to restrain Russia on Serbia’s behalf.

[24:50] **Poincaré’s Hard Line**

[事实] At a reception in St Petersburg, Poincaré challenged the Habsburg ambassador Count Szapáry and warned that Serbia had friends.

[事实] Szapáry reported being shocked by Poincaré’s confrontational tone.

[事实] Viviani was seen behaving erratically during the visit and was urged by Poincaré to compose himself.

[事实] Montenegrin princesses Anastasia and Milica encouraged talk of war, the recovery of Alsace-Lorraine, and meeting in Berlin.

[推测] The summit strengthened the impression in Russia that France would support a hard line.

[26:52] **The Alliance Is Cemented**

[事实] The summit reinforced Franco-Russian agreement that they should work to draw Britain closer to their side.

[事实] Poincaré and the Russians agreed that firmness was the right response if the Balkan crisis escalated.

[事实] A dinner aboard the French ship was a social disaster, with late service, poor weather, and an unpopular menu.

[事实] Viviani objected to making the communiqué too hard-line on the Balkans but was overruled.

[推测] Poincaré left St Petersburg believing Austria-Hungary would back down if faced with France and Russia together.

[29:01] **Austria Delivers The Ultimatum**

[事实] Austria-Hungary delivered its ultimatum to Serbia on Thursday, 23 July 1914, after Poincaré and Viviani had left Kronstadt.

[事实] The timing was intended to make Russian communication with the French ship more difficult.

[事实] Serbian prime minister Nikola Pašić was away campaigning, so the ultimatum was initially received by the finance minister.

[事实] Austria gave Serbia a 48-hour deadline and warned that failure to answer would break diplomatic relations.

[推测] Austria’s procedure was designed to create pressure while limiting the chance of coordinated French-Russian response.

[31:13] **The Ultimatum’s Demands**

[事实] Austria accused Serbia of tolerating societies and groups that promoted terrorism, outrages, and murders.

[事实] The hosts say Austria was not wrong that the assassins had links to Belgrade networks and help from Serbian frontier officials.

[事实] Austria had identified figures such as Major Tankosić and Milan Ciganović but had not uncovered the full Black Hand connection or Apis’s role.

[事实] The most inflammatory demands required Austro-Hungarian collaboration inside Serbia and participation in the investigation on Serbian soil.

[推测] The ultimatum mixed real grievances with demands that struck directly at Serbian sovereignty.

[33:32] **Serbia’s Shock And Pašić’s Return**

[事实] The hosts describe the ultimatum as a severe intrusion on Serbian sovereignty.

[事实] They note Winston Churchill later called it an exceptionally insolent document, while Christopher Clark compared it with NATO’s 1999 ultimatum over Kosovo.

[事实] Serbian officials were shocked, though some had suspected a harsh Austrian move might come.

[事实] Pašić initially moved as if to leave for Thessaloniki, but his train was stopped and he was ordered back to deal with the crisis.

[推测] Serbia’s leaders were caught between the danger of accepting humiliating terms and the danger of provoking war.

[35:45] **Serbia Looks To Russia**

[事实] On Friday, 24 July, Pašić met his cabinet at dawn while the 48-hour deadline continued to run.

[事实] Serbian ministers planned to ask for more time, though they expected Austria would refuse.

[事实] Historians differ on whether Serbia could have accepted the ultimatum; some evidence suggests some Serbian officials initially considered acceptance.

[事实] The hosts say Serbia’s calculation depended above all on whether Russia would support it.

[推测] Russia’s response became the decisive factor in whether the Balkan crisis would remain local or become European.

[37:18] **Sazanov Reacts**

[事实] When Sazanov read the ultimatum, he said it meant European war.

[事实] He telephoned Nicholas II, in what the hosts describe as one of the first times the Tsar used a telephone.

[事实] Nicholas initially doubted that Germany would risk a world war because Germany was prosperous and successful.

[事实] Sazanov insisted that Austria was acting aggressively and blamed Germany for encouraging the crisis.

[推测] The Tsar’s hesitation shows that not all Russian leaders immediately assumed Germany wanted war.

[39:10] **Paléologue’s Role**

[事实] Maurice Paléologue, the French ambassador in St Petersburg, is portrayed as theatrical, imaginative, unreliable, and very close to Poincaré from school.

[事实] Because Poincaré and Viviani were at sea, Paléologue became the key French intermediary with Russia.

[事实] Instead of urging caution, Paléologue encouraged Sazanov to take a hard line against Austria and Germany.

[事实] Sazanov then told Russia’s council of ministers that backing down would destroy Russia’s prestige and status as a great power.

[推测] Paléologue’s behavior helped reinforce Russian escalation at a critical moment.

[42:10] **Prestige, The Straits, And Repeated Concessions**

[事实] The hosts say Russia cared about Serbia because of the Straits, Balkan influence, previous concessions over Bosnia and Albania, and the fear of Ottoman naval strengthening.

[事实] They emphasize that Russia felt Austria was demanding yet another humiliation after Russia had already backed down twice in recent Balkan crises.

[事实] Austria and Russia were both using similar language about prestige, survival, and the need not to appear weak.

[推测] The crisis was driven less by simple aggression than by rival empires framing escalation as defensive necessity.

[43:51] **Communication Limits And Mobilization**

[事实] The hosts stress that leaders lacked modern instant communication and relied on ambassadors who added their own interpretations.

[事实] Both sides believed time was running out and assumed the other side was hostile and plotting.

[事实] Industrialized total warfare made the stakes feel existential, because defeat could threaten entire societies rather than merely cost territory.

[事实] Russia’s size and slower mobilization made its leaders anxious about falling behind.

[推测] Fear of being too late pushed Russia toward early military measures that other powers would interpret as escalation.

[45:20] **Russia Gives Serbia Its Own Blank Cheque**

[事实] On the night of 24-25 July, Russia sent Serbia a message saying it should not accept the ultimatum and that Russia would stand by it.

[事实] The hosts describe this as almost a Russian blank cheque to Serbia.

[事实] On 25 July, Russian ministers met with Nicholas II at Krasnoye Selo.

[事实] Sazanov urged partial mobilization in the western military districts, including Moscow, Kiev, Kazan, and Odessa, along with leave cancellation, reserve call-ups, and railway alerts.

[推测] Russia intended partial mobilization as both a defensive step and a signal to Austria, but it sharply raised the stakes for Germany as well.

[47:38] **Seven Days To War**

[事实] Russian mobilization was not yet a general mobilization or declaration of war.

[事实] The hosts say the move created a situation in which either Russia would have to call off mobilization or Austria would have to retreat from its ultimatum.

[事实] The Serbian reply was due at 6 p.m. on Saturday, 25 July.

[事实] The episode ends by noting that there are seven days left before world war begins, and that Britain will enter the story in the next episode.

[推测] The episode’s closing point is that the crisis has moved from diplomacy into a countdown shaped by military timetables and alliance credibility.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is its close attention to Russian and French motives, especially the argument that Russia’s Balkan policy was tied to the Straits, grain exports, naval anxiety, and prestige rather than only ethnic or religious solidarity.

Its strongest feature is the way it shows parallel reasoning on both sides: Austria, Russia, France, and Germany all appear to believe that firmness is defensive, necessary, and likely to make the other side retreat.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode is part of a series, so some references to Sarajevo, Austria, Germany, Serbia’s internal networks, and the next role of Britain depend on earlier or later episodes for full context.

[推测] It is best suited to listeners who want a narrative, personality-rich account of the July Crisis, with diplomatic detail and historiographical debate woven into a fast-moving story.
