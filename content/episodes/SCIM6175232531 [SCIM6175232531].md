# Dr. Shauna Swan on Endocrine Disruptors, Fertility Decline, and Practical Exposure Reduction

## 概览

This episode centers on whether chemicals in the environment, food, packaging, cosmetics, household products, and medical materials are affecting reproductive health. Dr. Shauna Swan argues that some endocrine-disrupting chemicals, especially phthalates, bisphenols, pesticides, PFAS, and related compounds, can alter hormone systems and are linked to measurable changes in fertility-related biology.

The discussion follows Swan’s research path from animal toxicology findings on “phthalate syndrome” to human studies measuring maternal phthalate exposure, infant anogenital distance, and later sperm-count associations. A major theme is that early pregnancy exposure can have lasting developmental effects, while some adult exposures may be reduced relatively quickly if intake stops.

The episode also moves from evidence to practical actions: avoiding fragrance-heavy products, reducing plastic contact with food and drink, not heating food in plastic, limiting canned foods, choosing glass/steel/ceramic where possible, considering organic produce when feasible, and replacing nonstick cookware. Swan emphasizes that policy change is difficult, so individual exposure reduction remains an important area of agency.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode Setup and Main Question**

[事实] Andrew Huberman introduces Dr. Shauna Swan as a professor of environmental medicine and public health at Mount Sinai School of Medicine.
[事实] The episode focuses on toxins and compounds in food, air, water, cosmetics, household items, and other environments that may affect reproductive biology.
[事实] Huberman frames the discussion around falling fertility rates, declining testosterone and sperm counts, increasing PCOS, and practical exposure-reduction steps.

[06:49] **Endocrine Disruptors as a Reproductive Health Problem**

[事实] Swan says there are environmental chemicals and agents that can affect reproductive health, depending on timing, dose, and exposed organism.
[事实] She focuses mainly on man-made chemicals that alter hormones, also called endocrine-disrupting or hormone-disrupting chemicals.
[事实] The discussion narrows toward testosterone, estrogen, masculinization, feminization, sperm, egg quality, and developmental biology.

[11:01] **How Swan Came to Study Phthalates**

[事实] Swan describes oral contraceptives as endocrine-disrupting chemicals because they are designed to alter reproductive hormones.
[事实] A CDC chemist, John Brock, encouraged her to study phthalates after CDC methods showed phthalate metabolites were present in many people, including women of reproductive age.
[事实] Brock also pointed her to National Toxicology Program animal work describing a “phthalate syndrome.”

[13:23] **Phthalate Syndrome in Animal Studies**

[事实] In NTP rat studies, pregnant mothers were fed different doses of phthalates.
[事实] Male offspring showed incompletely masculinized genital development, including smaller penis size, reduced testicular descent, and changes in the genital tract.
[事实] Swan says the critical exposure window in rats was narrow, around gestational days 9 to 12; exposure before or after that window did not produce the same effect.

[16:00] **Anogenital Distance as a Marker**

[事实] Swan highlights anogenital distance, the distance from anus to genitals, as a key measure that had long been used in animal studies but not previously as a toxicological measure in humans.
[事实] She says anogenital distance is normally sexually dimorphic, typically 50% to 100% longer in males than females.
[事实] The phthalate syndrome was notable because it attached a syndrome label to an environmental chemical class rather than to a pharmaceutical.

[20:11] **Translating the Animal Finding to Human Infants**

[事实] Swan had stored urine samples from pregnant women in a prior study and later used them to measure phthalate metabolites.
[事实] Her team invited families back so they could measure the genitals of their young children.
[事实] The researchers designed a careful exam for human infants, including repeated measurements and checks across examiners.

[26:16] **Maternal Phthalates and Shorter Male Anogenital Distance**

[事实] Swan reports that higher maternal levels of three anti-androgenic phthalates were associated with shorter anogenital distance in male infants.
[事实] She names DEHP, DBP, and BBzP as the most anti-androgenic, testosterone-lowering phthalates discussed in this context.
[事实] Huberman summarizes the result as male distributions becoming more female-like, and Swan agrees that this reflects feminization of the measure.

[31:02] **Replication in the TIDES Study**

[事实] Swan says she repeated the work in a second study called TIDES, the Infant Development and Environment Study.
[事实] In TIDES, urine was collected in each trimester and babies were examined at birth, making the design closer to the original rodent studies.
[事实] Swan says the second study again found the phthalate-related pattern.

[34:21] **Female Development, PCOS, and Fetal Androgen Exposure**

[事实] Swan says daughters of women diagnosed with PCOS had longer, more male-like anogenital distance.
[事实] Huberman clarifies that PCOS is associated with elevated androgens in adult women.
[事实] Swan describes anogenital distance as a readout of androgen exposure inside the womb during early pregnancy.

[40:20] **Anogenital Distance and Adult Sperm Count**

[事实] Swan says animal data suggest “AGD is forever,” meaning adjusted for body size, relative anogenital distance persists.
[事实] In a Rochester college-student study, men with longer anogenital distance had higher sperm counts.
[事实] Swan also cites work by Michael Eisenberg showing that men in infertility clinics who had fathered children had longer anogenital distance than men who had not but were trying.

[45:55] **When Sperm Count Matters for Fertility**

[事实] Swan describes Danish data showing that sperm concentration matters most when it is low.
[事实] She says below roughly 40 to 50 million sperm per milliliter, sperm count strongly affects probability of conception.
[事实] She says above roughly 75 to 100 million sperm per milliliter, additional sperm count matters much less for fertility probability.

[51:14] **Evidence for a Long-Term Sperm Count Decline**

[事实] Swan was asked by a National Academy of Sciences committee to evaluate a Danish paper claiming sperm count had dropped 50% in 50 years.
[事实] She initially doubted the finding and examined possible confounders such as counting methods, volunteer selection, obesity, and smoking.
[事实] After multivariable analysis and later repeated reviews, she concluded that sperm count was declining and that genetics could not explain such a rapid two-generation change.

[59:43] **Pesticides and Semen Quality**

[事实] Swan studied men from four U.S. cities using consistent equipment, centralized technician training, and quality control.
[事实] The sample came from partners of pregnant women, giving a defined parent population even though those men may have had somewhat higher semen quality because their partners were pregnant.
[事实] Men in central Missouri had about half as many moving sperm as men in Minneapolis.
[事实] Within Missouri, five pesticides were significantly higher in men with low sperm parameters, including motility and morphology.

[64:53] **Atrazine, Frogs, and Sexual Behavior**

[事实] Swan and Huberman discuss Tyrone Hayes’s work on atrazine exposure in frogs.
[事实] Swan says Hayes first studied frogs in more or less exposed wild environments and then exposed frogs in the lab.
[事实] She reports that a significant number of atrazine-exposed male frogs mounted other males.
[推测] The discussion uses this as an example of how endocrine-disrupting chemicals may affect not only reproductive anatomy but also neuroendocrine circuits and behavior.

[69:59] **Brain Development and Sex-Typical Play**

[事实] Swan says the brain, like the genitals, is sexually dimorphic.
[事实] Her team used a play-behavior questionnaire and found that higher prenatal exposure to anti-androgenic phthalates was associated with less male-typical play in boys around age four.
[事实] She emphasizes that these are statistical tendencies, not deterministic outcomes.
[事实] The analysis controlled for factors such as older sibling sex and parental attitudes toward same-sex play.

[75:31] **Major Everyday Sources of Exposure**

[事实] Swan identifies food-related exposure as a major area of concern: food itself, packaging, storage, and cooking utensils.
[事实] She says beverages are closely related because people repeatedly ingest from containers.
[事实] In a film-related intervention with infertile couples, her team inventoried products such as facial care, shampoo, cleaning supplies, laundry detergent, and food-storage practices.

[78:21] **Practical Product Swaps**

[事实] Swan says fragrance is a major source of phthalate exposure and advises avoiding products with fragrance.
[事实] She says this includes perfume, lotions, soaps, deodorants, laundry detergents, shampoo, and other scented products.
[事实] Her intervention also targeted plastic food-storage containers and nonstick pans because of PFAS chemicals.
[事实] She says participants responded positively to the changes, but the sperm and conception results were not ready to report.

[83:04] **Plastic, Cans, and BPA-Free Claims**

[事实] Swan says some exposure-reduction changes can save money, such as buying bulk foods or choosing unwrapped produce when available.
[事实] She says the primary source of BPA is the lining of cans.
[事实] She warns that “BPA-free” products may use related chemicals such as BPS or BPF, which she describes as harmful analogs.
[事实] She contrasts phthalates as chemicals that make plastic soft with BPA as a chemical that makes plastic hard.

[88:13] **Heating Plastic and Skin-Contact Products**

[事实] Swan says never to put plastic in a microwave.
[事实] She explains that plasticizers are added to plastic but are not chemically bound, so heat can increase migration into food or liquid.
[事实] She also warns about plastic bottles heated in cars.
[事实] She says phthalates are used in pesticides to increase absorption and in personal-care products to help hold scent and color.

[91:31] **Sunscreen and Product Screening**

[事实] Huberman distinguishes UV risk from concern about chemical sunscreen ingredients.
[事实] Swan recommends carefully checking sunscreen products and mentions Environmental Working Group consumer guides as one screening option.
[事实] She says she does not endorse specific products and tries to avoid conflicts from product recommendations.

[95:32] **Swan’s Own Exposure-Reduction Habits**

[事实] Swan says she distills drinking water at home and stores it in glass containers.
[事实] She tries to leave shoes at the door because dust can contain PFAS chemicals.
[事实] She checks face products, shops at farmers markets, and says she always buys organic when possible.
[事实] She is increasingly concerned about chemicals in clothing, especially workout wear, and also names building materials and furniture as harder exposure sources.

[100:32] **Regulation, REACH, and Medical Products**

[事实] Swan says Europe’s REACH policy requires showing a chemical is safe before market entry.
[事实] She contrasts that with the U.S. approach, where chemicals often enter the marketplace first and are studied later if concerns arise.
[事实] She says her phthalate research took about 10 years and $10 million, illustrating how slow chemical-by-chemical evaluation can be.
[事实] She cites a California bill restricting DEHP in IV bags but notes that evaluating replacement materials is also difficult.

[106:46] **Developmental Exposure and Adult Recovery Limits**

[事实] Swan says prenatal developmental effects cannot simply be reversed later.
[事实] She cites a Danish study in which maternal smoking during pregnancy was associated with a 50% reduction in male offspring sperm count.
[事实] She contrasts that with adult smoking, where stopping may allow sperm health to recover.
[事实] She says exposures can affect multiple generations because germ cells for the next generation are carried within the developing fetus.

[109:44] **The Broader Field and Plastics Treaty**

[事实] Swan says she is not alone; there is a large international field studying endocrine disruptors and plastics.
[事实] She mentions an international plastics treaty under negotiation and says many scientists, activists, citizens, and legislators are involved.
[事实] She says plastic use rose sharply starting around 1950 and remains a major challenge.
[事实] She cautions that plastic is important but not the only concern, since pesticides and other chemical classes also matter.

[113:40] **Fertility Rates and Population Structure**

[事实] Swan says global fertility rates have declined at roughly the same scale as sperm counts, about 50% in 50 years.
[事实] She identifies replacement fertility as about 2.1 children per woman.
[事实] She says many countries are below replacement, naming South Korea at 0.78 and Japan at 1.
[推测] The discussion connects declining fertility to future demographic strain, because fewer young people may be available to support a larger older population.

[115:45] **Heat, Cell Phones, and Reproductive Function**

[事实] Swan says she has not seen convincing evidence that electromagnetic radiation from cell phones or similar exposures affects pregnancy or fertility.
[事实] Huberman says prior evidence suggests possible heat effects from cell phones kept near the body, rather than direct EMF effects.
[事实] Swan says heat is related to fertility and sperm count, including seasonal patterns in warm months and climates.
[事实] Huberman adds that sitting, obesity-related heat, and scrotal temperature are linked to lower sperm count.

[118:04] **Egg Quality, Menopause, and Fertility Explanations**

[事实] Swan says there is growing evidence linking some chemicals to earlier menopause or premature ovarian failure, though she does not quote specific papers.
[事实] She says fertility decline is often attributed to social factors such as delayed childbearing, contraception, women’s education, and workforce participation.
[事实] She argues that non-human species fertility decline cannot be explained by delayed childbearing, contraception, rent, or other human social factors.

[122:05] **Assisted Reproduction and Future Technologies**

[事实] Huberman discusses assisted reproduction, including ICSI and three-party IVF for mitochondrial disease.
[事实] Swan mentions gamete genesis, where skin cells may be used to produce sperm or egg cells.
[事实] She says future technologies may help address declining ordinary conception.
[事实] She cautions that if adverse outcomes are observed in assisted-reproduction offspring, it can be hard to separate effects of the technology from the infertility factors that led parents to seek treatment.

[126:12] **Listener Questions: Receipts, Thyroid, Detox, and Cookware**

[事实] Swan says receipts are a concern because chemicals such as BPA can be absorbed through the body, and she suggests electronic receipts.
[事实] She says endocrine disruptors can affect the thyroid system and that there is substantial evidence of adverse effects.
[事实] She says water-soluble chemicals such as phthalates and bisphenols can leave the body within hours if intake stops.
[事实] She says PFAS and some pesticides can persist much longer, depending on chemical structure and whether they are fat-soluble.
[事实] When asked to choose between nonstick pans and seasoned iron pans, she says iron is the clear choice.

[132:07] **Closing Framing**

[事实] Huberman thanks Swan for her long-term work on endocrine disruptors, fertility, and public education.
[事实] He emphasizes that the topic is emotionally loaded because it concerns exposures from products people buy and use every day.
[事实] He closes by framing the episode as both a warning about reproductive health risks and a source of practical agency.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it does not treat “environmental toxins” as a vague wellness category. The strongest sections trace a clear evidence chain: animal toxicology, human biomarker design, replicated phthalate findings, sperm-count epidemiology, and practical exposure changes.

Its main strength is Swan’s caution. She repeatedly distinguishes what she has studied from what she has not, avoids naming commercial products, and flags unpublished or preliminary intervention data. That makes the episode more useful than a simple list of things to fear.

The limitation is that the conversation is broad and sometimes moves quickly across chemistry, fertility, behavior, policy, and assisted reproduction. Listeners looking for a precise product-by-product protocol will not get one here, and some topics are discussed as plausible concerns rather than settled findings.

[推测] The episode is best suited for people planning pregnancy, concerned about fertility, choosing household products, or trying to understand endocrine disruptors without relying only on headlines. It is also useful for clinicians, researchers, and policy-minded listeners who want a public-facing overview of how environmental exposure research is built.
