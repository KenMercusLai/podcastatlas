# Ten Unobvious Churches and the Histories They Hold

## 概览

This episode asks what churches can tell us about the past. It opens with T.S. Eliot’s *Little Gidding* and frames churches as places where layers of history, faith, architecture, art, memory and local life remain unusually tangible.

The guest, Rachel Morley of the Friends of Friendless Churches, chooses ten less obvious churches rather than famous cathedrals or standard heritage-list favourites. Her selections move through stories of daredevils, saints, Welsh nonconformity, war memorials, Saxon architecture, science, turkeys, folk magic, wall paintings and literature.

The central conclusion is that even small, remote or overlooked churches can contain dense histories. The episode argues that churches matter not only as religious buildings but as shared archives of local experience, national politics, artistic achievement and communal memory.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Churches as historical contact points**

[事实] The episode opens with lines from T.S. Eliot’s *Little Gidding*, which Tom connects to his recent visit to the church at Little Gidding.
[事实] Tom and Dominic describe churches as intimate ways to encounter history, where generations have stood, prayed and taken the place seriously.
[事实] They say Christianity, the Reformation and de-Christianisation profoundly shaped English, British and Irish history.
[推测] The opening frames churches as living historical evidence rather than simply old buildings.

[03:03] **Rachel Morley and the rules of the list**

[事实] Rachel Morley is introduced as director of the Friends of Friendless Churches and Dominic’s sister-in-law.
[事实] The list is not a simple “best churches” ranking; Rachel says she wants choices that tell a story and avoid the usual famous examples.
[事实] She notes that many public suggestions were cathedrals, even though the prompt was for churches.
[推测] Her approach deliberately shifts attention from canonical landmarks to overlooked buildings with strong stories.

[10:33] **St Mary the Virgin, Shrewsbury**

[事实] Rachel’s first choice is St Mary the Virgin in Shrewsbury, a red sandstone church with a large spire, Norman elements, a 15th-century carved timber roof and notable stained glass.
[事实] She tells the story of Robert Cadman, an 18th-century steeplejack and rope-slider who died in 1739 when his rope snapped during a performance from the church spire.
[事实] The church has an almost complete Jesse window from about 1330 and a major collection of 15th- and 16th-century continental stained glass.
[推测] The Cadman story shows how churches can preserve local drama and spectacle alongside art and architecture.

[18:15] **St Beuno’s, Clynnog Fawr**

[事实] Rachel moves to North Wales and discusses St Beuno’s at Clynnog Fawr in Gwynedd, describing it as a large 15th-century church on the site of Beuno’s monastery.
[事实] She says Beuno was associated with sick cattle and children, and with healing traditions linked to a holy well and sleeping on the saint’s grave.
[事实] She recounts legends in which Beuno brings people back from the dead, including a story involving a pupil later known as “Iron Eyebrow.”
[推测] This section highlights the blend of saintly biography, pilgrimage practice and folk legend around medieval churches.

[24:04] **St Brothen’s, Llanfrothen**

[事实] Rachel’s third church is St Brothen’s in Llanfrothen, North Wales, a church cared for by the Friends of Friendless Churches.
[事实] She explains that Robert Roberts, a nonconformist quarryman who died in 1888, wanted to be buried beside his daughter but was refused by the vicar.
[事实] David Lloyd George advised the family to defy the authorities, broke open the churchyard gates and helped bury Roberts by candlelight; the resulting case helped launch Lloyd George’s career.
[推测] Rachel presents the church as a local site tied to broader conflicts over Welsh nonconformity and disestablishment.

[28:14] **Gladstone at Hawarden**

[事实] Rachel briefly recommends St Deiniol’s at Hawarden in Flintshire for listeners interested in prime ministers.
[事实] Gladstone was married there in 1839, and the church includes a memorial chapel with a Burne-Jones nativity window.
[推测] This detour reinforces the episode’s theme that political history is often embedded in parish churches.

[31:16] **War chapels and Sandham Memorial Chapel**

[事实] Rachel mentions the Italian Chapel on Orkney and a Welsh prisoner-of-war chapel as examples of wartime religious spaces made from modest materials.
[事实] Her main fourth choice is Sandham Memorial Chapel in Hampshire, whose interior was painted by Stanley Spencer between 1920 and 1926.
[事实] The chapel honours the forgotten dead of the First World War, especially Lieutenant Harry Sandham, who returned from war but later died after contracting malaria.
[事实] Rachel describes Spencer’s resurrection scene, where soldiers rise from graves and carry their crosses toward Christ.
[推测] The chapel’s power comes from its focus on ordinary wartime labour and neglected forms of loss.

[37:22] **St Nicholas, Worth**

[事实] Rachel’s fifth choice is St Nicholas at Worth in Sussex, described as the largest surviving Saxon cruciform church on its original foundations.
[事实] Robert Whitehead, inventor of the torpedo, is buried in the churchyard.
[事实] Rachel links Whitehead’s family to the von Trapp story, because his granddaughter married Georg von Trapp before the later events associated with *The Sound of Music*.
[推测] The church’s appeal lies in the unexpected collision of Saxon architecture, military technology and popular musical history.

[39:56] **Time, space and St Gregory’s, Kirkdale**

[事实] Rachel introduces a “time and space” sequence, mentioning Francis Godwin near St John the Baptist, Whitbourne, and Isaac Newton connections at Market Overton.
[事实] Her sixth main church is St Gregory’s, Kirkdale in Yorkshire, known for a preserved Saxon sundial.
[事实] The sundial inscription refers to Orm son of Gamal, King Edward and Earl Tostig, allowing it to be dated to around 1055-1065.
[事实] Rachel says the sundial survived because it was plastered over for about 700 years and was discovered in 1771.
[推测] This section uses church fabric to connect local inscriptions with scientific curiosity, Saxon chronology and national history.

[45:43] **St Andrew’s, Boynton**

[事实] Rachel’s seventh church is St Andrew’s, Boynton in Yorkshire, where turkeys appear throughout the building.
[事实] She says William Strickland of Boynton brought the first turkeys to Britain in 1526, bred them, became wealthy and adopted the turkey as a family symbol.
[事实] The church includes turkey imagery in features such as a lectern, windows and tombs, along with a Georgian interior and a Norman tub font.
[推测] The church turns an apparently comic detail into a story about exploration, status, wealth and family identity.

[48:09] **Charms, skeletons and witchcraft**

[事实] Rachel introduces churches connected with superstition and magic, including a crying knight effigy in Gwynedd, a painted skeleton in Powys and an “abracadabra” charm for Elizabeth Lloyd.
[事实] She discusses Lilias Adie, a Scottish woman who died in prison after a witchcraft confession probably made under torture.
[事实] She contrasts Adie’s treatment with the eccentric Shropshire figure Mad Jack Mytton, who is buried in a crypt despite his extreme behaviour.
[推测] This passage shows how churches preserve traces of folk belief, fear, gendered judgement and later fascination with the supernatural.

[51:54] **Eynhallow, Orkney**

[事实] Rachel’s eighth church is on Eynhallow in Orkney, an island associated with stories of magic, “thin folk” and protection through salt and the sign of the cross.
[事实] She says the island’s soil was believed to repel undesirable things, including rats and mice.
[事实] The church is now a ruin; it was repurposed as a dwelling around the 16th century and discovered by chance in 1851.
[事实] Rachel notes that the site is under-researched and difficult to access, with visits possible only one day a year.
[推测] Eynhallow represents the extreme edge of the episode’s theme: a church as archaeology, folklore and sacred landscape at once.

[53:53] **Wall paintings and St Cadoc’s, Llancarfan**

[事实] Rachel mentions St Botolph’s, Hardham in West Sussex, and St Lawrence, Broughton in Buckinghamshire, as churches with important wall paintings.
[事实] Her ninth main choice is a church in the Vale of Glamorgan identified through its wall paintings, including St George and the dragon, the Seven Deadly Sins and the Seven Acts of Mercy.
[事实] The paintings were discovered by chance in 2008 after a builder noticed red paint during repair work.
[事实] Rachel especially praises the vivid depiction of gluttony, where a demon forces drink into a man with a swollen stomach.
[推测] The discovery illustrates how major medieval art can remain hidden in ordinary-looking churches.

[58:20] **St Michael and All Angels, Hathersage**

[事实] Rachel’s tenth choice is St Michael and All Angels in Hathersage, a 14th-century church filled with references to the Eyre family.
[事实] She says Charlotte Bronte visited Hathersage in 1854 and drew on the church and village for *Jane Eyre*.
[事实] The churchyard is also linked to the legend of Little John, Robin Hood’s companion, after a very large thigh bone was reportedly discovered in 1784.
[推测] The church’s value here is literary and legendary as much as architectural.

[60:30] **An Irish bonus: Turners Cross**

[事实] Rachel adds an Irish choice after noting that Ireland had not originally been included in Dominic’s brief.
[事实] She chooses the church at Turners Cross in Cork, where she grew up, describing it as the first concrete church in Ireland and possibly the first modernist church in Ireland.
[事实] The church was designed by Barry Byrne, a student of Frank Lloyd Wright, and features a large Christ figure with open arms.
[推测] The bonus choice broadens the episode’s scope from medieval and early modern churches to modernist church architecture.

[62:50] **Friends of Friendless Churches**

[事实] Rachel explains that the Friends of Friendless Churches was founded in 1957 by Ivor Bulmer Thomas and others, including T.S. Eliot, John Betjeman, John Piper, Lady Mander and Roy Jenkins.
[事实] The charity protects churches that are no longer used for worship and works across England and Wales.
[事实] Rachel says it currently has 60 churches in its care and operates on a small grant.
[推测] The closing argument is that churches should remain accessible public inheritances rather than being lost, neglected or sold privately.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strength is Rachel Morley’s ability to make each church memorable through a concrete story: a falling rope-slider, a disputed burial, resurrected war dead, a turkey dynasty, hidden wall paintings or a literary afterlife.

[推测] Its limitation is pace. Because the list is dense and often interrupted by jokes or digressions, some churches receive only a compressed treatment, and listeners may want images or notes alongside the audio.

[推测] This episode is especially suited to listeners interested in British and Irish history, church architecture, folklore, local history, medieval art and unexpected connections between small places and large historical themes.
