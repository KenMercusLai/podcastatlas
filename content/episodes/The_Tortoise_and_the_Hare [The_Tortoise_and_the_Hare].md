# The Tortoise and the Hare

## Overview

This episode challenges the legal profession’s reliance on the LSAT and elite law-school credentials. Malcolm Gladwell argues that the system rewards “hares”—people who solve difficult problems quickly—even though important legal work often requires “tortoises” who read slowly, think deeply, and work carefully.

Judge Jeffrey Sutton provides the central counterexample. Despite attending Ohio State rather than an elite “T14” law school, he became one of Justice Antonin Scalia’s most highly regarded clerks. His career exposes a contradiction between the traits used to select aspiring lawyers and those needed to excel in demanding legal roles.

The episode also examines how strict LSAT time limits preserve a useful ranking curve while disadvantaging slower thinkers. Data from law firms further suggests that law-school prestige does not reliably predict professional success.

Gladwell ultimately proposes a “don’t ask, don’t tell” rule for educational credentials: employers should neither ask where applicants studied nor allow applicants to disclose it. Hiring would then focus on ability, experience, and fit rather than institutional prestige.

## 分段落总结

[00:57] **Scalia’s answer to an aspiring lawyer**

[事实] At a 2009 speech, an American University law student asks Justice Antonin Scalia how talented, hardworking students without elite credentials can become exceptionally successful in law.

[事实] Scalia says he generally selects Supreme Court clerks from the hardest law schools to enter because he cannot afford a hiring mistake.

[事实] He assumes those schools admit the “best and brightest,” even if they do not teach particularly well.

[推测] His answer treats admission to an elite institution as a substitute for directly evaluating an applicant’s capabilities.

[05:28] **Jeffrey Sutton contradicts the prestige rule**

[事实] Scalia identifies Jeffrey Sutton as one of the finest clerks he ever had, while admitting that he would not originally have hired Sutton because Sutton attended Ohio State.

[事实] Sutton had applied to Ohio State and Michigan, but enrolled at Ohio State after Michigan rejected him.

[事实] Sutton later became a federal appellate judge and was praised for his legal scholarship and Supreme Court advocacy.

[推测] Sutton’s success undermines Scalia’s claim that elite-school attendance is a dependable filter for exceptional legal talent.

[08:17] **The LSAT selects hares over tortoises**

[事实] The LSAT requires candidates to complete sections of roughly 20–25 questions within 35 minutes.

[事实] Gladwell characterizes it as a test of solving difficult problems quickly rather than merely solving them correctly.

[事实] He calls Sutton a “tortoise”: a brilliant person whose abilities may not have been fully captured by a speed-focused admissions test.

[推测] A lower LSAT score may reflect a slower cognitive style rather than weaker reasoning ability.

[10:37] **Supreme Court work rewards slow, careful thought**

[事实] Former Supreme Court clerk Tali Farhadian describes reading petitions slowly, with a pencil, because the details and arguments are intricate.

[事实] She says Supreme Court cases are difficult precisely because their answers are not obvious and lower courts may disagree about them.

[事实] Clerks may spend months researching and analyzing assigned cases.

[事实] Sutton says law favors the tortoise and that graduates of elite schools must learn to operate in “tortoise mode.”

[推测] The qualities demanded by Supreme Court clerkships conflict with the speed emphasized by the admissions system that feeds candidates into those positions.

[14:02] **The contradiction in legal gatekeeping**

[事实] Gladwell summarizes the contradiction: Scalia excludes students from a slow, careful job because they did not excel on a test of speed.

[事实] At the same time, Scalia acknowledges that one of his best clerks did not follow the elite-school path produced by that test.

[事实] The audience nevertheless applauds Scalia’s remarks.

[推测] The response illustrates how deeply prestige-based assumptions have been normalized, including among people disadvantaged by them.

[15:05] **How the LSAT is engineered**

[事实] Gladwell visits the Law School Admission Council, where experts explain that developing a single LSAT question can take 36 months.

[事实] Questions are repeatedly tested to ensure that they present the intended kind of difficulty.

[事实] One reading-comprehension question was rejected after similarly high-performing men and women answered it at substantially different rates.

[事实] The council removed the question even though it could not determine the cause of the disparity.

[推测] The LSAT’s designers are highly attentive to measurable group bias within individual questions, but this does not resolve broader concerns about the test’s timed structure.

[17:36] **Why every section has a strict time limit**

[事实] LSAT representatives argue that efficiency and handling substantial reading and analysis within limited time are relevant to law school.

[事实] Gladwell objects that fixed section limits prevent candidates from transferring unused time from their stronger areas to weaker ones.

[事实] The representatives say uniform timing is part of standardization and advise candidates to find strategies that maximize their score under those conditions.

[事实] Gladwell argues that the system disadvantages slow readers even while carefully screening questions for other forms of unfairness.

[推测] The disagreement reflects competing definitions of efficiency: uniform speed under fixed conditions versus flexible allocation of time according to individual strengths.

[23:18] **More time would disrupt the admissions hierarchy**

[事实] Gladwell argues that giving candidates more time would allow some tortoises to catch up with hares.

[事实] Test-preparation experts estimate that extending the LSAT to 125% of its existing duration could reduce time pressure for many slower candidates.

[事实] They say higher scores among tortoises would enlarge the pool above elite schools’ usual score thresholds and weaken the existing bell curve.

[事实] According to the episode, law-school admissions decisions are driven roughly 70% by LSAT scores and 30% by grades, leaving little room for other factors.

[推测] The strict timing may be valuable partly because it separates applicants conveniently, not because it perfectly measures the full range of abilities required in law.

[25:45] **Law needs both cognitive styles**

[事实] Gladwell rejects simply replacing a speed-focused test with an untimed but much harder test, because that would privilege tortoises over hares.

[事实] He notes that some legal work, such as completing an extremely complicated transaction within 48 hours, genuinely benefits from fast readers and thinkers.

[事实] He concludes that the profession needs both hares and tortoises.

[推测] A fair selection system should recognize different forms of excellence instead of defining merit through a single cognitive style.

[26:10] **Artificial scarcity at elite American schools**

[事实] Gladwell says 53,000 applicants compete for approximately 4,500 annual places at the top 14 American law schools.

[事实] He contrasts this scarcity with large Canadian universities that retain elite reputations while enrolling far more undergraduates.

[事实] He cites undergraduate enrollments of 70,890 at the University of Toronto, 52,711 at the University of British Columbia, and 27,601 at McGill.

[事实] His proposed Canadian solution is straightforward: make prestigious schools larger.

[推测] Expanding capacity could reduce the stakes attached to marginal test-score differences and weaken prestige-driven sorting.

[28:11] **A “Moneyball” analysis of legal hiring**

[事实] Evan Parker analyzes successful lawyers and works backward to determine which applicant characteristics predict performance.

[事实] He finds that firms possess extensive candidate information but often fall back on shortcuts and select people resembling existing partners.

[事实] Parker calls conventional interview ratings essentially a coin flip as predictors of performance; higher ratings were negatively associated with retention in the data discussed.

[事实] In some firms, blue-collar work experience combined with later academic success is a meaningful positive predictor.

[推测] Evidence-based hiring may identify valuable traits that conventional prestige and interview judgments overlook.

[31:36] **Law-school prestige fails as a predictor**

[事实] Parker says the law school a person attended does not meaningfully predict success at a law firm.

[事实] He says T14 attendance repeatedly appears as an insignificant factor in his analyses.

[事实] His study of rainmakers found many successful lawyers who attended little-known schools or earned their degrees through night school.

[事实] Gladwell concludes that the chain connecting LSAT performance, elite law-school admission, and elite-firm hiring rests on an unsupported assumption.

[推测] Educational pedigree persists partly as a status signal even when it offers employers little practical information about future performance.

[34:22] **The “don’t ask, don’t tell” proposal**

[事实] Gladwell proposes that employers should not ask where candidates studied and candidates should not disclose their educational institutions.

[事实] People could still attend Harvard or another elite school, but employers could not use the institution’s name as a hiring shortcut or display it as a status marker.

[事实] He extends the proposal beyond law to hiring generally.

[事实] He argues that students should choose schools according to educational fit and happiness rather than anticipated brand value in the workplace.

[推测] Removing institutional names would force employers to evaluate skills, experience, and suitability more directly.

[35:33] **An alternative answer Scalia could have given**

[事实] Gladwell imagines Scalia saying that he values people who think deeply, read slowly, and work persistently on consequential problems.

[事实] He presents Sutton as the model of such a candidate: a “giant tortoise.”

[事实] In this imagined answer, school name and performance on 25 questions in 35 minutes would not determine access to a clerkship.

[推测] Gladwell frames meritocratic gatekeeping as an active responsibility to assess genuine fit rather than inherit convenient institutional rankings.

[36:43] **The LSAT results**

[事实] Gladwell and his assistant Camille Baptista reveal that they received the same LSAT score.

[事实] The numerical score is withheld.

[事实] Gladwell describes the tie as balancing his experience against Camille’s speed and newer academic skills.

[推测] The tied result serves as a comic conclusion that reinforces the episode’s warning against assigning too much meaning to a single score.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest contribution is its clear distinction between speed and depth. By connecting the LSAT’s timing rules with the actual work of Supreme Court clerks, it shows that a selection mechanism can be technically sophisticated while still measuring only part of what matters.

Sutton’s career and Parker’s hiring data give the argument more weight than a purely theoretical critique of standardized testing. The episode also avoids claiming that speed has no value; its more balanced conclusion is that legal work requires both hares and tortoises.

[推测] The proposed ban on disclosing educational institutions is intentionally provocative and may be difficult to implement, while the transcript does not explore possible enforcement problems or situations in which particular training programs provide relevant information.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for students, educators, recruiters, and employers interested in standardized testing, institutional prestige, and evidence-based hiring. Its broader lesson is that a meritocracy becomes less credible when its gatekeepers confuse convenient proxies with demonstrated ability.
