# The Mennonite National Anthem

## 概览

This episode concludes the series on Ancel Keys’s Minnesota Starvation Experiment through the life of volunteer Lester Glick. His diaries document extreme physical deterioration, tuberculosis, social isolation, and psychological changes that persisted long after the experiment ended.

The program explains why the study remains a scientific gold standard: its meticulous records offer rare evidence about starvation and recovery, including the body’s tendency to restore fat faster than muscle. Modern researchers still rely on these data because comparable controlled experiments would be ethically and practically difficult to conduct.

Glick’s Mennonite faith—especially the call in Matthew 25 to feed the hungry—motivated his participation. Although the experiment permanently altered his health and ended his plans for medical school, he transformed his suffering into a lifetime of social-work education, practical generosity, and service to others.

## 分段落总结

[01:12] **Lester Glick’s Physical Collapse**

[事实] A July 1945 diary entry describes Glick as emaciated, weak, discouraged, losing hair, and barely able to walk after months of starvation.

[事实] Near the experiment’s end, doctors discovered a tuberculosis lesion in his left lung and prescribed six months in a sanatorium.

[事实] At the experiment’s final supper, the other volunteers moved away from him because they feared tuberculosis, leaving him isolated.

[03:30] **The Experiment’s Lifelong Aftereffects**

[事实] Glick’s children, Chris and Byron, say the experiment permanently disrupted his relationship with food and his body.

[事实] He repeatedly gained and lost large amounts of weight, felt continuously hungry, and kept candy under his car seat so food would always be nearby.

[事实] Byron compares his father’s emotional scars to those experienced by combat veterans and says Glick continued paying the price for his convictions throughout his life.

[08:16] **The Scientific Problem of Recovery from Starvation**

[事实] Physiologist Abdul Dulloo explains that after severe weight loss, the body tends to restore fat faster than lean tissue such as muscle.

[事实] Recovering patients may regain more fat than they lost, particularly abdominal fat, while muscle mass and function recover less readily.

[事实] These questions matter for people recovering from malnutrition, cancer treatment, eating disorders, and obesity.

[事实] The episode states that 85–90 percent of people who lose weight eventually regain it.

[11:08] **Why Starvation Is So Difficult to Study**

[事实] Useful research requires strict control over what participants eat, how much they eat, and when they eat for many months.

[事实] Experiments involving captive or otherwise vulnerable populations would be unethical, while voluntary participants must possess extraordinary discipline.

[事实] In a later NIH calorie-restriction study, 143 adults aimed to cut caloric intake by 25 percent for two years but achieved an average reduction of only 12 percent.

[13:44] **The Minnesota Experiment as a Scientific Gold Standard**

[事实] Ancel Keys kept comprehensive records covering six months of starvation and the volunteers’ subsequent recovery.

[事实] Dulloo calls the Minnesota study the gold standard for understanding how normal-weight human bodies respond to starvation.

[事实] Given an imaginary ability to remove ethical and practical constraints, Dulloo would repeat the experiment using modern tools to monitor activity, muscle, immune function, and other changes.

[事实] He characterizes the volunteers’ uncoerced participation as an enormous contribution to humanity.

[16:20] **Faith and the Decision to Volunteer**

[事实] Glick was a 23-year-old Mennonite conscientious objector when the United States entered World War II.

[事实] While working in a tuberculosis ward at a Michigan state mental hospital, he encountered a recruitment notice asking, “Will you starve that they be better fed?”

[事实] Historian Dwayne Stoltzfuss connects that appeal to Matthew 25 and the Mennonite calling to leave one’s comfort zone and serve people in need.

[事实] A Mennonite pastor describes Matthew 25—welcoming strangers, clothing the naked, and feeding the hungry—as the “national anthem” of the Mennonite church.

[19:04] **Hunger as Total Deprivation**

[事实] Glick moved to Minneapolis and joined the experiment without knowing how profoundly it would affect him.

[事实] His diaries record anger over reduced bread allowances, growing antisocial behavior, and withdrawal from the other volunteers.

[事实] Stoltzfuss says malnutrition separated Glick from the community-oriented identity formed by his family and church.

[事实] Glick came to understand hunger as more than a calorie deficit: it was an overwhelming deprivation of physical, emotional, and social sustenance.

[21:46] **A New Calling in Social Work**

[事实] Tuberculosis prevented Glick from pursuing his original plan to attend medical school.

[事实] He instead earned graduate degrees in social work and sought to teach a practice grounded in loving kindness rather than merely arranging services.

[事实] He established social-work programs at Goshen College, Syracuse University, and the University of Southern Mississippi.

[事实] His memoir lists 38 adult addresses, reflecting a life spent moving around the country in pursuit of his work.

[23:36] **Teaching and Practicing Empathy**

[事实] Glick required his family to eat only limited servings of white rice every Wednesday so his children could experience hunger and develop empathy.

[事实] His children did not fully understand the exercise at the time, but they recognized its importance to him and continued it until leaving home.

[事实] In retirement, Glick grew vegetables for neighbors and worried about their food supply when illness left him unable to maintain his garden.

[25:24] **Reconsidering the Experiment’s Moral Legacy**

[事实] Gladwell questions why the scientific community remains uneasy about the Minnesota experiment and why people insist it should never be repeated.

[事实] He argues that critics focus too heavily on what volunteers sacrificed at the time and insufficiently on the later benefits produced by their suffering.

[推测] The episode presents voluntary sacrifice as ethically significant not only because participants consented, but because their actions generated knowledge and service extending far beyond themselves.

[27:08] **Service After Starvation**

[事实] Many participants pursued humanitarian or religious work after the experiment, including livestock relief in Europe, refugee assistance, missionary service, and food distribution.

[事实] Glick devoted himself to establishing social-work schools and no longer separated spiritual deprivation from physical hunger.

[事实] His diary describes chewing pencil wood and experiencing disturbing food cravings during the starvation phase.

[推测] Their later careers suggest that firsthand hunger transformed scientific volunteers into lifelong witnesses to the moral urgency of feeding and caring for others.

[29:08] **Cinnamon Rolls as a Form of Sustenance**

[事实] After the experiment, Glick became fascinated with cinnamon rolls and carried their picture in his wallet until his death.

[事实] He made thousands of deep-fried cinnamon rolls and delighted in sharing them at church and with other people.

[事实] Byron interprets this practice as a small-scale expression of his father’s lifelong effort to give people sustenance and bring them somewhere unexpectedly good.

[推测] The cinnamon rolls became both a residue of Glick’s trauma and a means of converting his enduring hunger into generosity.

[31:08] **Matthew 25 and Glick’s Final Legacy**

[事实] Byron reads Matthew 25 from a family Bible passed from his grandfather to his father and then to him.

[事实] The passage commands believers to feed the hungry, give drink to the thirsty, welcome strangers, clothe the naked, visit the sick, and attend to prisoners.

[事实] It concludes that serving “the least” of one’s brethren is equivalent to serving Christ.

[推测] Ending with this passage frames Glick’s life as a literal enactment of the Mennonite calling that first led him into the experiment.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its integration of scientific history, religious conviction, and personal biography. Glick is presented neither merely as an experimental subject nor solely as a victim, but as someone whose suffering produced both lasting damage and an intensified commitment to service.

Its use of diary entries and interviews with Glick’s children gives the experiment an emotional and bodily reality that scientific summaries cannot convey. The discussion of fat and muscle recovery also clearly demonstrates why data gathered in the 1940s remain relevant to modern medicine and nutrition research.

[推测] The episode’s defense of the experiment may feel incomplete to listeners seeking a fuller examination of informed consent, research safeguards, or the limits of using future benefits to justify severe harm. Its argument is deliberately sympathetic to voluntary sacrifice and gives less attention to possible objections.

[推测] This episode is especially valuable for listeners interested in medical ethics, nutrition science, pacifist religious traditions, social work, and stories about turning trauma into care for others.
