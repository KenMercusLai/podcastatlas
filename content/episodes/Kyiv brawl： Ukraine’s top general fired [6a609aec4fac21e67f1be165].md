# Kyiv brawl: Ukraine’s top general fired

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* begins with Ukraine’s domestic political rupture during wartime: President Volodymyr Zelensky’s dismissal of defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov, followed by the firing of top general Oleksandr Sersky after public protests.

The discussion frames the fight as more than a personnel dispute. It contrasts Fedorov’s reformist, technology-focused approach with Sersky’s more rigid military culture, then asks whether the new commander, Mykhailo Drapati, can preserve momentum in Ukraine’s armed forces.

The episode then turns to rising police killings in America, focusing on Albuquerque and New Mexico, before closing with a lighter but culturally revealing report on Japan’s “incident properties,” homes associated with deaths that many tenants avoid despite steep discounts.

## 分段落总结

[01:12] **Ukraine’s wartime political clash**

[事实] The episode says Ukraine and Russia have recently intensified attacks on each other, including a major ballistic attack on Kyiv and Ukrainian strikes in Crimea and Russian cities.
[事实] Zelensky fired top general Oleksandr Sersky after public protests that also called for the return of dismissed defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov.
[事实] Shashank Joshi says the dispute concerns the ethos of Ukraine’s military and the nature of its war effort.
[推测] The personnel battle is presented as a proxy fight over how Ukraine should modernize and sustain its war strategy.

[03:30] **Fedorov’s reforms and conflict with Sersky**

[事实] Fedorov had previously served as minister of digital transformation and was described as young and well regarded by Ukraine’s allies.
[事实] He helped scale up Ukraine’s use of drones and changed procurement incentives so effective brigades could receive more drones and ammunition.
[事实] Fedorov clashed with Sersky over brigade organization, resource allocation, and the treatment of technologically innovative units.
[事实] Sersky is described in the transcript as a divisive, Soviet-style, rigid disciplinarian with crude tactics.
[推测] The conflict reflected a broader clash between a reformist technological model and a more traditional command culture.

[05:17] **Why Sersky’s dismissal does not settle the issue**

[事实] Joshi says Fedorov remains dismissed and has not regained his old job.
[事实] Zelensky has considered other candidates for defence minister and has offered Fedorov other roles overseeing procurement or technology.
[事实] Fedorov is described as having leverage because of street protests and because Sersky, his rival, has been removed.
[推测] Fedorov may push to regain the defence ministry rather than accept a narrower role.

[05:50] **Drapati’s appointment and military reform**

[事实] Sersky’s successor is named as Mykhailo Drapati, who had been responsible for Ukrainian defences in the northeast.
[事实] Joshi says Drapati is likely to be received well in the army and is viewed as part of a new generation.
[事实] Drapati publicly said the armed forces needed new technology, needed to protect people, and that commanders had to take responsibility.
[推测] Those statements are interpreted as a coded rejection of Sersky’s higher-casualty approach.

[06:56] **Ukraine’s unresolved war pressures**

[事实] Joshi says changes to army leadership begin to address how Ukraine’s army is run.
[事实] He says Ukraine’s ground forces have blunted Russian advances and imposed a high price on Russia.
[事实] Ukraine is also facing Russian missile and drone attacks, shortages of Patriot interceptors, and pressure on Black Sea exports.
[推测] Leadership reform may help Ukraine’s battlefield performance, but it cannot solve air defence shortages or economic pressure by itself.

[08:13] **Zelensky’s political damage**

[事实] Joshi says Zelensky has come out of the episode politically bruised.
[事实] He compares the moment with a previous backlash over raids targeting Ukraine’s anti-corruption bodies.
[事实] Fedorov publicly criticized the sitting army chief and described a dysfunctional culture of lies, poor organization, and lack of responsibility.
[事实] Joshi says Ukraine’s allies in Europe and the United States liked Fedorov and shared many of his concerns about Sersky.
[推测] Zelensky’s handling of the reshuffle may weaken confidence in his political judgment among both Ukrainians and allies.

[11:18] **Rising police killings in America**

[事实] The second report focuses on Albuquerque, where the rate of police killings is described as among the highest in the United States.
[事实] Lawyer Laura Ives describes a case involving an elderly man with a small pocket knife who was hit with a pepper ball and died.
[事实] Daniel Knowles reports that police killings are rising nationally even as murder rates are falling.
[事实] The episode says one in ten Americans killed violently are killed by police.
[推测] The report challenges the idea that falling violent crime automatically means fewer deadly encounters with police.

[12:01] **Albuquerque as a case study**

[事实] Knowles visits the site where Jose Armas, a 23-year-old man threatening suicide, was shot dead by police on May 26th.
[事实] Armas’s family had called emergency services hoping social workers would arrive, but police came instead.
[事实] The episode says Albuquerque police have shot and killed 116 people since 2013.
[事实] ACLU representative Daniel Williams says New Mexico is frequently a national leader in police killings.
[推测] The segment presents mental-health crisis response as a central weakness in the current system.

[13:21] **Mental health, drugs, and failed reform**

[事实] The figures cited come from Mapping Police Violence.
[事实] The episode compares Albuquerque with Boston, saying Boston had far fewer killings despite a larger population.
[事实] A 2014 Justice Department report said officers too often used deadly force against people posing minimal threat.
[事实] New Mexico’s mental-health cuts and rising overdose deaths are presented as worsening factors.
[推测] Police are being asked to handle social and medical crises that enforcement alone cannot resolve.

[15:06] **Consent decrees, accountability, and police culture**

[事实] Albuquerque’s police department spent 11 years under a federal consent decree that ended last year.
[事实] The decree introduced policies such as restrictions on when officers could draw guns.
[事实] Alfred Matthewsons says the decree did not accomplish its primary purpose of reducing shootings and identifies culture as the reason.
[事实] The episode notes that barely a dozen police officers are criminally charged each year for particularly egregious killings.
[推测] Policy changes without deeper cultural change may be insufficient to reduce fatal police encounters.

[16:01] **Risk, tactics, and future backlash**

[事实] The episode says it can be hard to fault officers at the moment of some shootings because America is heavily armed.
[事实] Chicago’s reduction in police killings is linked to restrictions on foot chases.
[事实] Laura Ives argues officers should be willing to wait when a human life is at stake.
[事实] The report says Albuquerque’s consent decree did change policing through tasers and body cameras, but police may have become more risk averse.
[推测] If fatal encounters keep rising and official accounts are contradicted by witnesses, public protest could return.

[17:19] **Japan’s incident properties**

[事实] Moeka Iida visits an apartment where an isolated elderly woman died and went unnoticed for days.
[事实] The apartment is an example of a jikobukken, or “incident property,” where someone has died in a tragic or brutal way.
[事实] Comedian Matsubara Tanishi has lived in such homes for over a decade and written memoirs about the experience.
[事实] Rising living and housing costs are making these properties less unappealing to some tenants.
[推测] Economic pressure may be weakening cultural taboos around stigmatized housing.

[19:00] **Why these homes lose value**

[事实] Iida explains that ancestral worship and Buddhist mourning rituals shape attitudes toward death in Japan.
[事实] Deaths that deviate from the norm, including murder, suicide, or unnoticed death, are seen negatively and associated with bad luck.
[事实] Incident properties may rent or sell for 10% to 50% below normal value.
[事实] Natural deaths tend to bring smaller discounts, while murders can bring discounts as high as 50%.
[推测] The size of the discount reflects both stigma and the perceived severity of the previous death.

[20:09] **A culture and business around haunted housing**

[事实] Japan has a website that maps jikobukken across the country with icons and descriptions of the deaths.
[事实] Some YouTubers visit these homes looking for scares.
[事实] Some companies dispatch Buddhist monks to perform cleansing rituals.
[事实] A company called Kachimodo investigates properties overnight with devices such as thermal cameras and can issue a certificate saying no paranormal activity was found.
[推测] The market around these homes monetizes both fear and reassurance.

[21:49] **Would the reporter live in one?**

[事实] Iida says she would like to think she is rational and unaffected by such things.
[事实] She says knowing about a brutal murder or suicide in her living space might have a psychological impact.
[事实] She has not checked whether her own home is a jikobukken and prefers not to know.
[推测] Her answer suggests that even people who doubt the supernatural may still be influenced by emotional discomfort and social stigma.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strength is its range: it moves from Ukraine’s wartime leadership crisis to American police violence and then to Japanese housing taboos without treating any topic as isolated trivia. Each segment uses a specific case to open onto a larger institutional or cultural question.

The Ukraine section is the most politically consequential, especially because it links battlefield effectiveness with internal military culture and Zelensky’s judgment. The police segment is valuable for its data-driven contrast between falling murders and rising police killings.

The Japan segment offers a quieter ending, but it adds texture by showing how housing markets, death rituals, superstition, and inflation interact. [推测] Its limitation is that the topic is treated more as a cultural feature than as a broader housing-policy issue.

[推测] This episode is well suited for listeners who want concise international analysis with on-the-ground reporting, but less suited for those seeking deep technical detail on Ukraine’s military command structure or American policing data.
