# Stars in Their AIs: Tech Is Changing Hollywood

## 概览

This episode examines how generative AI is moving into Hollywood, from synthetic performers such as Tilly Norwood to AI-assisted stunts, effects, script development, planning, shot generation and editing.

The discussion presents Hollywood as divided: some directors and studios see AI as a way to expand creative possibilities and reduce costs, while others fear job losses, aesthetic decline and a flood of low-quality AI-generated content.

The episode then shifts to two broader segments: the weakening of Obamacare after pandemic-era subsidies expired, and the unexpected popularity of American-style line dancing in France.

## 分段落总结

[01:46] **AI-generated performers enter the frame**

[事实] The episode opens with a scene of a woman being arrested on a London street, then reveals that the woman, camera crew and production setup do not exist because the scene was generated with AI.
[事实] The performer is identified as Tilly Norwood, an AI-generated actor.
[事实] The hosts frame AI tools as increasingly relevant to scripts, stunts and special effects in Hollywood.
[推测] The opening uses Tilly Norwood as a concrete example of why AI feels both technically impressive and unsettling for the film industry.

[02:44] **Hollywood’s divided reaction to AI**

[事实] Some people in Hollywood publicly embrace AI because they see potential for creativity and cost reduction.
[事实] Others oppose it because of job threats and the risk of large amounts of low-quality “AI slop.”
[事实] The episode says navigating AI’s impact on Hollywood is currently controversial.
[推测] The central conflict is less about whether AI will arrive and more about how openly, ethically and extensively it will be used.

[03:23] **Creating a Tilly Norwood scene in minutes**

[事实] Tilly Norwood’s creator, Eileen van der Velden, guided the reporter through making an AI-generated clip from scratch.
[事实] The London street scene took about four minutes to create.
[事实] The clip was described as not perfect and needing more takes to reach film-like quality.
[事实] A comparable live-action scene filmed on a London street would have taken about half a day and cost more than £5,000.
[推测] The example shows why AI is attractive to producers even before it fully matches traditional filmmaking quality.

[04:48] **AI is not replacing stars, but it is being used quietly**

[事实] The episode says there is no evidence that AI is currently good or creative enough to replace human actors on the red carpet.
[事实] Netflix co-chief executive Ted Sarandos is cited as saying great films still require great artists.
[事实] The film business is described as having a “don’t ask, don’t tell” approach, with many using AI without advertising it.
[推测] AI adoption may be more advanced behind the scenes than public discussion suggests.

[05:25] **AI as a production tool for dangerous or costly scenes**

[事实] Director John Irwin used AI in his film Young Washington to create a raging torrent in a 50-foot trench.
[事实] He said this allowed filming on location while sparing actors from a potentially dangerous stunt.
[事实] The reporter says Irwin found AI creatively addictive.
[事实] Netflix said it used AI in about 300 titles this year.
[推测] The most acceptable early uses of AI may be practical production tasks that reduce risk, time or cost without replacing performers outright.

[06:22] **The backlash against AI in film**

[事实] The 2023 Hollywood strikes were strongly motivated by fears that AI would be used to produce scripts and create digital replicas of actors.
[事实] Director Cain Parsons, who directed Backrooms, said he would make AI disappear if he could.
[事实] At an Amazon MGM event in May, AI use was announced for some animated series, including Punky Duck.
[事实] Two days later, Punky Duck’s director publicly left the project and said he and his family had received death threats.
[推测] The backlash shows that AI is not only a labor issue but also an emotional and cultural flashpoint for creators and fans.

[07:39] **Big tech pushes AI deeper into entertainment**

[事实] Google and China’s ByteDance are making video-generation AI models used around the industry.
[事实] Netflix acquired Interpositive, a company led by Ben Affleck, for AI production.
[事实] Google DeepMind invested about $75 million in A24.
[事实] Amazon is involved through its cloud computing business, Amazon MGM Studios and Prime Video.
[推测] Major technology companies are helping make AI adoption structurally difficult for Hollywood to avoid.

[08:54] **AI’s momentum and Hollywood’s cost problem**

[事实] AI is described as quietly playing a role in script development, production planning, shot generation and editing.
[事实] The episode says Hollywood urgently needs to cut costs, and AI can help with that.
[事实] Studios are experimenting with generative AI to turn library content into vertical video suitable for smartphones.
[事实] Disney turned High School Musical into 52 micro-episodes for TikTok.
[推测] AI may become important not only for production savings but also for repackaging existing intellectual property into new formats.

[09:51] **AI as another controversial Hollywood technology shift**

[事实] The episode compares AI controversy with earlier resistance to digital footage in the 1990s.
[事实] George Lucas is cited as comparing opponents of generative AI to people who objected to cars because they were more dangerous than horses and carts.
[事实] Lucas said there is nothing to be done about AI because it is the future.
[推测] The episode leans toward the view that AI adoption is inevitable, even if its final role remains contested.

[12:03] **Obamacare after the subsidy fight**

[事实] The episode says the US government shut down for 43 days last year, the longest shutdown of non-essential government services in the country’s history.
[事实] The main dispute was over Obamacare subsidies: Democrats wanted to extend them, while Republicans argued they were too expensive.
[事实] A temporary solution ended the impasse, but the subsidies expired.
[推测] The segment frames Obamacare as weakened not by formal repeal but by the withdrawal of financial support.

[13:02] **What Obamacare was designed to solve**

[事实] The Affordable Care Act passed in 2010 to make health insurance more affordable and accessible.
[事实] It aimed to help people who could not get coverage through employers, government programs or family employment.
[事实] Republicans opposed it as too much government intervention, while some on the left thought it did not go far enough.
[推测] Obamacare’s political vulnerability comes partly from trying to patch gaps in the existing system rather than replacing it entirely.

[13:53] **Enrollment rose after pandemic-era subsidies**

[事实] Obamacare expanded coverage through Medicaid expansion and insurance exchanges.
[事实] The individual mandate was functionally scrapped by Republicans in 2019.
[事实] Congress greatly increased subsidies during the COVID pandemic in 2021.
[事实] Enrollment through the exchanges nearly doubled by 2025, bringing America close to near-universal health coverage.
[推测] The surge in enrollment suggests affordability, rather than lack of demand, was a major barrier.

[15:16] **Coverage falls and costs rise**

[事实] At the end of last year, 22 million people were buying insurance through the exchanges.
[事实] That number had fallen to around 19 million and was likely to fall by another 2 million by year-end.
[事实] Average premiums rose nearly 60%, or about $180 per month.
[事实] Average deductibles rose nearly 40% to almost $4,000.
[推测] As healthier people exit the market, the remaining pool becomes more expensive to insure.

[16:08] **Death spiral fears**

[事实] The episode explains that insurance can become more expensive as healthier people leave, leaving sicker customers behind.
[事实] Economists call this dynamic a “death spiral.”
[事实] Experts predict 10 million fewer Americans will buy insurance through the marketplaces by 2028 compared with 2025.
[事实] Original Obamacare subsidies remain even though the enhanced subsidies expired.
[推测] The market may shrink sharply but not collapse completely because some subsidy support still exists.

[17:07] **The uninsured burden shifts to patients and hospitals**

[事实] Hospitals are seeing people who lost exchange coverage go uninsured rather than get insurance elsewhere.
[事实] Evidence from Obamacare research shows uninsured people are more likely to delay needed treatment, including medicine and surgery.
[事实] Hospitals must still provide emergency care regardless of ability to pay.
[事实] In May, unpaid hospital care was up 16% compared with the same time last year.
[推测] The costs of reduced insurance coverage are likely to spread beyond individuals to hospitals and the wider healthcare system.

[18:18] **France’s enthusiasm for line dancing**

[事实] The episode says August village festivals in rural France often feature line dancing.
[事实] Line dancing is described as choreographed group dancing in lines or rows, with synchronized steps and no physical contact with a partner.
[事实] Line dancing became popular in America in the 1970s and saw a resurgence in the 1990s after “Achy Breaky Heart.”
[推测] The French enthusiasm is presented as surprising because it contrasts with France’s self-image of highbrow culture.

[19:40] **A growing French line-dance subculture**

[事实] The Francophone Federation of Country Dance and Line Dance lists more than 500 clubs across France.
[事实] In a poll, nearly one in ten adults said they had taken part in line dancing.
[事实] The southern town of Salon-de-Provence hosted the French Line Dance Open in May.
[推测] The activity has moved beyond casual village entertainment into a structured hobby and competitive scene.

[20:12] **American culture’s pull in France**

[事实] The episode says France has long had a fascination with American culture outside Paris and major cities.
[事实] Nine of the top ten box-office hits in France last year were American.
[事实] Disneyland Paris is Europe’s only Disney theme park outpost and offers line dancing classes.
[事实] Modern country pop has helped bring new recruits to line dancing.
[推测] Line dancing’s popularity reflects a broader French appetite for American mass culture, especially in social and rural settings.

[21:09] **Line dancing as shared summer pleasure**

[事实] Sophie Pedder attended village festivals in France during a summer marked by forest fires, drought and heat wave.
[事实] She describes people across generations enjoying line dancing in the streets on summer evenings.
[推测] The segment ends on a lighter note, treating line dancing as a form of communal release during a difficult season.

## 播客点评/总结

The strongest part of the episode is the Hollywood AI segment, which grounds a broad industry debate in specific examples: Tilly Norwood, John Irwin’s AI-assisted stunt work, Netflix’s reported AI use and major technology-company investments.

The Obamacare segment is more policy-focused and data-heavy, using enrollment, premium and deductible numbers to explain how subsidy changes can reshape an insurance market without formally repealing the law.

The final line-dancing segment works as a cultural contrast, shifting from high-stakes technology and healthcare debates to a lighter observation about American cultural influence in rural France.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who want a concise current-affairs briefing across technology, policy and culture rather than a deep dive into only one subject.
