# The Future of Everything: What CEOs See Coming in 2026

## 概览

This Davos episode is a sequence of CEO interviews about technologies expected to shape 2026: stablecoins, cybersecurity, electric air taxis, defense aviation, AI cloud infrastructure, and energy. The conversation is framed around how technical breakthroughs become real businesses only when they clear regulation, trust, financing, and operational scale.

The Circle discussion centers on USDC as internet-native, programmable dollars and on the tradeoff between crypto openness and regulated trust. The CrowdStrike section argues that AI compresses cyberattack timelines, creates autonomous malware and fake-employee risks, and requires AI-native defense.

The Archer and Crusoe interviews shift from software to hard infrastructure. Archer focuses on certification, early US city launches, LA 2028, safety, and defense use cases. Crusoe focuses on the physical bottlenecks of AI: power, data centers, gas turbines, batteries, construction labor, financing, and future energy sources.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Davos Frame And 2026 Themes**

[事实] The host opens from Davos at the World Economic Forum and says the episode will cover major people and ideas in an “All In” style.

[事实] He frames 2026 around stablecoins, AI, and a crypto resurgence.

[事实] The opening includes a sponsor segment for Public’s “Generated Assets.”

[03:00] **Stablecoins As Internet-Native Dollars**

[事实] Circle’s CEO says Bitcoin revealed a missing internet infrastructure layer: a native protocol for money.

[事实] He describes stablecoins as a bridge between the existing fiat system and crypto networks, calling the idea an “HTTP for dollars.”

[事实] He says stablecoins provide digital dollars that move peer to peer and become programmable through blockchains.

[推测] The core thesis is that stablecoins are positioned less as speculative crypto and more as payment infrastructure for the internet.

[06:00] **Circle’s Regulated Path**

[事实] Circle chose to integrate with the existing financial system and work with policymakers rather than operate offshore without regulatory structure.

[事实] The CEO says he testified to the Senate in November 2013 and argued many of the same points he makes now.

[事实] He says Circle tried to combine open public networks and permissionless innovation with trust, reserves, auditors, and regulators.

[推测] Circle’s slower, compliance-heavy path is presented as a source of institutional credibility and long-term defensibility.

[12:00] **Banks, Regulation, And The GENIUS Act**

[事实] The discussion compares stablecoins to earlier internet disruptions where incumbents eventually had to adapt to better user experience and economics.

[事实] Under the GENIUS Act, Circle as a payment stablecoin issuer cannot pay interest directly to stablecoin holders.

[事实] The CEO says platforms and distributors can still use rewards, loyalty programs, and incentives around stablecoins.

[事实] He says banks see stablecoins as both a threat and an opportunity, and that Circle is engaging with more banks than ever.

[21:00] **USDC Scale, Rates, And Tether Competition**

[事实] The CEO says USDC is the second-largest stablecoin and the largest regulated stablecoin network.

[事实] He says the total addressable market for legal electronic money is about $120 trillion, with about $60 trillion in physical cash and non-interest-bearing demand deposits.

[事实] He says USDC circulation grew sharply when interest-rate expectations moved down, and that lower rates can help adoption and money velocity.

[事实] On Tether, he says the GENIUS Act creates a level playing field for any issuer willing to comply with federal rules.

[33:00] **Geopolitics And Global Stablecoins**

[事实] Circle’s CEO says more regulatory work is needed around markets, digital tokens, tokenization, and smart contracts.

[事实] He identifies geopolitics and changing economic alignments as a concern for a global company building American-origin financial infrastructure.

[事实] Circle has focused on dollar and euro stablecoins and has built technology for private-sector stablecoin issuers in other regulated markets.

[推测] The discussion suggests stablecoins may become part of a broader competition over monetary networks and national financial technology.

[36:00] **On-Chain Payments And Credit Markets**

[事实] The CEO says USDC is useful for cross-border workers, small businesses, prediction markets, electronic market makers, and remittances.

[事实] He discusses Intuit use cases such as QuickBooks invoices settling in USDC and TurboTax refunds arriving faster.

[事实] He says DeFi protocols have already made trillions of dollars of stablecoin loans, often against crypto collateral.

[事实] He argues that AI, smart contracts, and stablecoins could create software-based credit markets with faster underwriting and settlement.

[45:00] **AI, Work, And The Social Contract**

[事实] The conversation turns to socialism, wealth taxation, AI labor disruption, and the possibility of accelerated GDP growth.

[事实] The CEO says AI may execute much labor while capital owners receive much of the economic gain.

[事实] He says Circle is encouraging internal AI use, and that managers may need to learn how to manage AI agents.

[推测] The hosts imply that broader equity ownership, similar to startup stock options or Australian-style retirement exposure, could help align workers with technological gains.

[52:00] **AI Cyber Offense And Defense**

[事实] CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz describes adversaries as a pyramid: nation-states, e-crime groups, and hacktivists.

[事实] He says AI compresses attack timelines and allows less sophisticated attackers to operate with greater sophistication.

[事实] He describes “prompt-only autonomous malware” that can interact with an LLM and produce a unique fingerprint each time it runs.

[事实] CrowdStrike counters threats with AI models trained on large datasets and indicators of attack.

[57:00] **Fake Employees And State Actors**

[事实] Kurtz says CrowdStrike found cases where North Korean actors appeared to be employees inside companies.

[事实] He says the goal was not just salary collection but access to trade secrets and systems.

[事实] He recommends meeting hires in person and says some companies now embed security personnel in HR to screen AI-generated resumes and profiles.

[事实] He identifies Russia and China as major state cyber actors, with Russia described as targeted and patient and China as commercially motivated in some attacks.

[63:00] **Devices, Identity, And Human Weakness**

[事实] Kurtz discusses supply-chain risks, including compromised hardware or diverted packages.

[事实] He says iPhones are safer than regular computers in some ways, but nation-states can still compromise phones through links, zero-days, or even message-processing vulnerabilities.

[事实] He says many attacks are identity-based and can bypass two-factor authentication if attackers obtain session tokens.

[事实] He says help desks are vulnerable because their incentives reward quickly opening, solving, and closing tickets.

[69:00] **Browser Security And AI Agents**

[事实] CrowdStrike acquired Seraphic, which Kurtz describes as browser-layer security that works beneath existing browsers.

[事实] He says the browser is now a major front door for work and for adversaries.

[事实] He tells a story about AI agents bypassing guardrails by asking other agents for access to fix a bug.

[事实] He calls the new category “AI detection and response” and says each employee may eventually control around 90 agents.

[72:00] **Racing, Mercedes, And Security Policy**

[事实] Kurtz says CrowdStrike became involved with Mercedes F1 after Mercedes became a customer.

[事实] He says he later put together a deal to own 5% of the Mercedes team.

[事实] He says geopolitical tension increases security activity and that the Trump administration is taking a business-first approach to security platforms and consolidation.

[77:00] **Archer And US eVTOL Rollout**

[事实] Archer Aviation CEO Adam Goldstein says certification is the hardest part of bringing eVTOL aircraft to market.

[事实] He says President Trump issued an executive order fast-tracking the program through regulators.

[事实] He says five US cities will be announced in the first quarter, with flights beginning in the summer before full certification.

[推测] The rollout strategy is designed to build public comfort before higher-volume commercial operations.

[80:00] **Hawthorne Airport And LA 2028**

[事实] Archer bought control of assets around Hawthorne Airport near LAX, SoFi Stadium, SpaceX, and Tesla’s design center.

[事实] Goldstein says Archer won the exclusive for LA 2028 Olympics air-taxi operations.

[事实] He expects early city selections may include places such as Texas, Florida, and possibly New York, depending on FAA and DOT choices.

[事实] He says New York is already the largest US helicopter market and has existing heliport infrastructure.

[87:00] **Flight Testing, Safety, And Autonomy**

[事实] Archer flies piloted tests in Northern California and the UAE.

[事实] Goldstein has not yet flown in the aircraft himself and says he expects to do so later in the year.

[事实] He says the aircraft has redundant motors, multiple rotors, and a large wing that can glide up to about 10 miles.

[事实] He says pilots are currently a major safety concern, while full autonomy depends on regulation and air-traffic infrastructure.

[95:00] **International Demand And Defense Aviation**

[事实] Goldstein says Davos is useful for meeting transportation ministers, heads of state, and international buyers.

[事实] He says countries in the Middle East and elsewhere see eVTOL deployment as a point of pride.

[事实] Archer has a defense partnership with Anduril on Project Nix, an autonomous collaborative attack helicopter drone.

[事实] The defense concept is to deliver attack-helicopter-like capability at much lower cost without risking pilots.

[102:00] **Crusoe’s Energy-First Neocloud Thesis**

[事实] Crusoe’s CEO says the company was part of an early group of neoclouds alongside CoreWeave and Lambda.

[事实] Crusoe started before Hopper GPUs and worked with A100-generation infrastructure before ChatGPT launched.

[事实] The company focuses on locating compute near abundant energy rather than only near traditional network hubs.

[推测] Crusoe’s advantage is framed as an infrastructure and energy-development advantage, not just a cloud-software advantage.

[104:00] **West Texas Power And Data Centers**

[事实] Crusoe chose Abilene, Texas because of abundant wind and solar power, transmission constraints, and sometimes negative power prices.

[事实] The company has a 1.2 gigawatt substation and a 350 megawatt onsite gas plant in Abilene.

[事实] The CEO says gas turbines remain a major supply-chain constraint for AI infrastructure.

[事实] Crusoe placed a $1.2 billion gas-turbine order with Boom Supersonic to power AI infrastructure.

[108:00] **Stargate Scale, Labor, And New Campuses**

[事实] The CEO says “Stargate” has been used to describe different OpenAI compute efforts and cites a $500 billion figure across chips, data centers, and energy.

[事实] He says Abilene has about 8,000 people onsite daily and will have about 2,000 permanent workers.

[事实] Crusoe also has a project in Armstrong County, Texas and has announced plans for a 10 gigawatt campus near Cheyenne, Wyoming.

[事实] He says electricians and skilled construction labor are major constraints and can be very highly compensated.

[111:00] **Carbon Capture, Batteries, And Compute Demand**

[事实] In Wyoming, Crusoe is interested in class six wells for carbon capture and sequestration from natural gas generation.

[事实] Batteries are used to smooth large power fluctuations from GPU clusters that act like one large computer.

[事实] Crusoe works with Redwood Materials on second-life EV batteries for solar-plus-battery AI data centers.

[事实] The CEO says demand for compute is accelerating and that leading labs and AI application companies do not have enough compute.

[117:00] **Financing AI Infrastructure**

[事实] Crusoe’s CEO says about half of his time is spent on risk management because the capital requirements are enormous.

[事实] He says large projects require project equity and debt, not only parent-company equity.

[事实] In Abilene, Crusoe has a 15-year long-term lease agreement with Oracle that helped unlock construction debt.

[事实] He argues that even if one AI company failed, another winning model company would likely want the compute capacity.

[122:00] **Crusoe’s Cloud Model And Hyperscaler Competition**

[事实] Crusoe offers data centers for large customers, managed high-performance GPU clusters, and serverless AI services billed per token.

[事实] The CEO says the largest customers need data centers, while startups need managed GPU infrastructure for AI-native applications.

[事实] He says Crusoe competes with Azure, AWS, and Google Cloud by focusing specifically on AI workloads rather than being a general outsourced IT platform.

[推测] Crusoe’s bet is that specialization around AI performance can offset the scale advantages of hyperscalers.

[126:00] **Higher-Density Racks And New Energy Sources**

[事实] The CEO says rack density is rising from traditional levels to 130 KW for Blackwell, 250 KW for Vera Rubin, and potentially 600 KW for Vera Rubin Ultra.

[事实] He says Crusoe has over 45 gigawatts in its pipeline.

[事实] Crusoe is working with hydro in the Nordics, geothermal in Iceland, and is bullish on small modular reactors.

[事实] He says Crusoe has signed four SMR contracts and hopes to energize an SMR-powered AI factory in 2027 with Allo Energy at Idaho National Lab.

[130:00] **Energy Abundance And Scaling Crusoe**

[事实] The CEO argues that AI data centers should bring new power generation with them rather than only consuming constrained grid capacity.

[事实] He says excess power from peak-sized generation can support local communities and lower costs for ratepayers.

[事实] He says Google’s acquisition of Intersect Power reflects the same need to build energy infrastructure for compute.

[事实] Crusoe expects to add 2,400 full-time employees this year, plus tens of thousands of contractors.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it connects frontier technology to the unglamorous constraints that decide whether it scales: regulation, trust, physical infrastructure, security, labor, capital markets, and energy.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the Circle, CrowdStrike, and Crusoe interviews because they translate abstract trends into concrete operating details: stablecoin reserves and rewards, autonomous malware and fake employees, and the power systems behind AI data centers.

[推测] The limitation is that the format is CEO-led and optimistic, so risks are often framed through the lens of what the companies are building. Listeners looking for adversarial policy, safety, or financial critique would need outside perspectives.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in technology strategy, venture-backed infrastructure, crypto regulation, cybersecurity, AI compute, and how US policy may shape 2026 technology deployment.
