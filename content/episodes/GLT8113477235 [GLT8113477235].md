# Joan of Arc: Trial, Death, and Rehabilitation

## 概览

This episode concludes the series on Joan of Arc by focusing on her captivity, trial, execution, and later rehabilitation. The hosts frame the trial not simply as an English show trial, but as a politically charged ecclesiastical inquisition conducted with unusual procedural care.

The central tension is that the English and their Burgundian allies needed Joan condemned in order to undermine Charles VII’s legitimacy, especially his coronation at Reims. Yet Joan’s testimony, especially about her voices and her refusal to abandon male clothing, made her both compelling and vulnerable.

The episode follows Joan from Rouen Castle to her public recantation, relapse, execution on 30 May 1431, and posthumous retrial. It ends by showing how France’s later victories made it possible for Charles VII to reclaim Joan as a national heroine.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **English Propaganda Against Joan**

[事实] The episode opens with a proclamation issued on 3 January 1431 in the name of Henry VI, accusing Joan of dressing as a man, deceiving people, committing violence, and claiming divine authority.

[事实] The proclamation presents Joan as a threat to divine and canon law and authorizes her transfer to Bishop Pierre Cauchon for examination.

[推测] The wording shows that Joan’s trial was intended from the start to damage not only her reputation but also the political legitimacy of Charles VII.

[01:41] **Henry VI’s Weak Position In France**

[事实] Henry VI was nine years old and had arrived in France in 1429, but his appearance in Rouen failed to impress local crowds.

[事实] The Duke of Bedford wanted Henry crowned in France to counter Charles VII’s coronation at Reims, but Reims was controlled by Charles and Paris was considered unstable.

[推测] Henry’s youth and lack of charisma made the English regime more dependent on symbolic victories such as Joan’s condemnation.

[05:04] **Joan As A Political Opportunity**

[事实] Joan had been sold to the English by a Burgundian nobleman and arrived in Rouen on 23 December 1430.

[事实] Richard Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick, took charge of her imprisonment and understood the need to make her trial an international spectacle.

[事实] Joan was held in a cold tower cell, shackled by the ankles, watched by English guards, and denied unauthorized communication.

[推测] The severity of her imprisonment reflected fear of escape and the political importance attached to controlling her.

[08:21] **Why The Church Court Was Used**

[事实] Bedford and Warwick are described as genuinely believing Joan might represent satanic power.

[事实] The proceedings were outsourced to ecclesiastical authorities, specifically Bishop Cauchon, because the case concerned suspected heresy.

[事实] The trial was an inquisition rather than a modern courtroom trial with jury and defense counsel.

[推测] Using a church court gave the English cause greater religious authority while still serving political aims.

[10:07] **The Charges And Trial Preparation**

[事实] Cauchon indicted Joan on 9 January as a woman of notorious reputation and focused on her alleged false revelations and her wearing of male clothing.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that Cauchon followed inquisitorial procedure carefully and spent about six weeks preparing before the trial opened.

[事实] Investigators were sent to Domremy, Joan was examined again to confirm her virginity, and 131 lawyers, theologians, priests, and abbots eventually participated.

[事实] Almost all participants were French and aligned with the Burgundian or Lancastrian side.

[推测] The trial’s procedural seriousness did not remove its political bias, but it complicates the idea that it was merely a crude show trial.

[15:50] **The Trial Begins**

[事实] Joan appeared before the tribunal on 21 February in Rouen Castle, still wearing male clothing and with cropped hair.

[事实] She faced learned male clerics and theologians despite being an illiterate teenage peasant without legal representation.

[事实] The interrogations were disorienting, with many questioners, shifting topics, and no stable defense structure.

[事实] Joan initially refused to swear an unrestricted oath, saying some matters, especially her revelations from God, might be beyond what she could disclose.

[推测] Her insistence on limiting what she would answer shows both caution and a strong sense that her voices were sacred obligations.

[20:08] **Joan’s Voices Under Interrogation**

[事实] Across repeated sessions, Joan was pressured to speak about her voices and gradually revealed more.

[事实] She said she had heard the voice three times the previous day and that it told her to answer boldly because God would help her.

[事实] She identified the voices as those of St Catherine, St Margaret, and St Michael.

[推测] The hosts suggest that Joan may have connected the voices to specific saints only under the pressure of interrogation, though this is presented as a historian’s interpretation.

[25:11] **The Nature Of Joan’s Religious Experience**

[事实] Joan described her voices as real, external, heavenly presences rather than as thoughts coming from within herself.

[事实] Her answers were often concrete and matter-of-fact, such as laughing at the idea that St Margaret would speak English.

[事实] The inquisitors struggled to categorize her experiences because accepting them as heavenly would undermine the Lancastrian regime, while calling them satanic raised difficult theological questions.

[推测] Joan’s testimony appears powerful because it combines conviction, simplicity, and resistance to abstract theological framing.

[29:02] **The Sign To Charles VII And Male Clothing**

[事实] Joan eventually described the sign given to Charles VII as an angel bringing him a crown of pure gold, accompanied by other angels and saints.

[事实] Some theologians considered this claim false, misleading, and damaging to the dignity of angels.

[事实] Her male clothing became a central issue because she said her voices wanted her to wear it, placing private revelation above church teaching.

[事实] On 23 May, twelve charges were presented to Joan, and she refused to renounce her words and deeds.

[推测] The clothing issue mattered because it gave the court a clearer route to declaring her disobedient and heretical.

[33:06] **Public Condemnation And Sudden Recantation**

[事实] Joan was taken by tumbril to a cemetery in Rouen where thousands had gathered around a large stage.

[事实] Cauchon pronounced that she was a heretic to be handed to secular authority, and preparations for burning were already in place.

[事实] At the last moment, Joan unexpectedly declared that she would submit to the church and renounce her visions and revelations.

[推测] Her recantation likely shocked the court because the proceedings had seemed to be moving directly toward execution.

[36:45] **Life Imprisonment And Regret**

[事实] Cardinal Beaufort advised that Joan’s public submission meant she could not be handed over for execution at that moment.

[事实] Joan marked an abjuration document acknowledging that she had been deceived by evil spirits.

[事实] Cauchon sentenced her to perpetual imprisonment on bread and water, after which she was returned to her cell, dressed in women’s clothing, and shaved bald.

[推测] Joan may have expected release and then realized she had traded her identity and loyalty to her voices for lifelong imprisonment.

[41:11] **Relapse And Fatal Reply**

[事实] Four days after her recantation, Joan put male clothing back on.

[事实] She said she had been tricked about the clothing oath, implied that men around her made women’s clothing unsafe, and said she would rather die than remain imprisoned for life.

[事实] When asked whether she had heard the voices of St Catherine and St Margaret since her recantation, she answered yes.

[事实] A clerk later marked this answer as a fatal reply.

[推测] Her return to the voices and male clothing made it impossible for the court to treat her as a repentant penitent.

[43:50] **Execution In Rouen**

[事实] On 29 May, Cauchon’s experts unanimously judged Joan unregenerate, excommunicate, and fit to be handed to secular power.

[事实] On 30 May 1431, Joan was taken to the market square in Rouen wearing male clothes and a cap identifying her as a relapsed heretic and idolater.

[事实] She was bound to the stake, asked for a crucifix, cried out “Jesus” through the smoke, and her ashes were later dumped into the Seine.

[事实] Later stories claimed that witnesses wept and that her heart would not burn, though the hosts note listeners may judge those stories for themselves.

[推测] The execution became more morally powerful because the account emphasizes Joan’s fear, piety, and youth rather than triumphal martyrdom.

[47:50] **Silence, French Recovery, And Charles VII’s Reassessment**

[事实] Six and a half months after Joan’s death, Henry VI was crowned as Henry II of France in Notre-Dame.

[事实] Charles VII did not immediately turn Joan into a martyr and kept largely silent about her because she had been condemned as a heretic.

[事实] France’s fortunes improved: Burgundy reconciled with Charles in 1435, Charles entered Paris in 1436, and he began conquering Normandy in 1449.

[事实] In February 1450, after entering Rouen, Charles finally spoke publicly about Joan and initiated inquiries into her trial.

[推测] Charles’s renewed interest in Joan was tied to his stronger political position and the need to legitimize his own coronation.

[52:04] **Retrial And Joan’s Legacy**

[事实] A series of inquiries led to an official retrial, reviewing Joan’s life and death.

[事实] By the time of the retrial, Joan’s predictions that Orléans would be relieved, Charles would be crowned, and the English would be driven from France had effectively come true.

[事实] In 1453, the Battle of Castillon marked the effective end of the Hundred Years’ War, leaving England with only Calais in France.

[事实] On 7 July 1456, judges in Rouen declared Joan’s original conviction invalid and condemned it as corrupt, deceitful, slanderous, fraudulent, and malicious.

[推测] The rehabilitation transformed Joan from condemned heretic into a foundational heroine of French national memory.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s main value lies in its careful balance between narrative drama and historical complication. It does not reduce Joan’s trial to a simple English frame-up, but it also makes clear that theology, law, propaganda, and dynastic politics were inseparable.

A major strength is the attention given to Joan’s own voice under interrogation. The discussion highlights her intelligence, vulnerability, conviction, and fear without turning her into either a flawless saint or a cynical fraud.

[推测] The episode is especially suitable for listeners interested in medieval politics, religious courts, the Hundred Years’ War, and the making of national myths. Its limitation is that it relies heavily on the hosts’ interpretation of the trial record and selected historians, so listeners wanting a full legal history of the proceedings would need further reading.
