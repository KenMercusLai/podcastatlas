# Why Innocent People Confess—and Why We Believe Them

## 概览

People generally assume that individuals act in their own self-interest. This makes false confessions seem almost inconceivable: why would an innocent person voluntarily claim responsibility for a crime?

Psychologist Saul Kassin explains that confessions can result from authority, isolation, exhaustion, misinformation, promises, threats, and gradual psychological pressure. He distinguishes voluntary, compliant, and internalized false confessions, using historical cases, laboratory studies, and wrongful convictions to show how each can arise.

The episode also examines the extraordinary power of confession evidence. Jurors may disregard contradictory testimony, DNA exclusions, and other exculpatory evidence once a suspect has confessed. A false confession can even reshape witnesses’ memories and contaminate subsequent evidence.

Kassin argues that interrogations should be recorded from beginning to end, investigators should use open-ended questions without disclosing crime facts, and authorities should actively test alternative explanations rather than assuming their initial theory is correct.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **The Puzzle of Acting Against Self-Interest**

[事实] People commonly assume that individuals and groups behave in ways that advance their own interests.

[事实] This assumption makes it easy to understand why a guilty person would claim innocence, but difficult to imagine why an innocent person would confess.

[推测] The episode frames false confession as a challenge to ordinary intuitions about rational behavior.

[02:27] **A Childhood Accusation Shapes Saul Kassin’s Work**

[事实] In sixth grade, Saul Kassin received an F after his teacher accused him of plagiarizing a book report about Mickey Mantle.

[事实] Kassin said he had written the report himself but felt too helpless to defend himself effectively; his mother later demanded evidence, and the teacher changed his grade after finding none.

[事实] Kassin became a psychologist who studied how people interpret others’ actions, with particular interest in jury decision-making.

[推测] His childhood experience gave him a lasting personal understanding of the psychological paralysis that can follow a confident accusation from an authority figure.

[04:53] **Why Jurors Treat Confessions as Conclusive**

[事实] In Kassin’s early mock-jury studies, cases containing confessions produced unanimous guilty verdicts.

[事实] Participants explicitly cited the confession as the basis for their decisions, leading Kassin initially to remove confession cases because they created no variation in verdicts.

[事实] He later recognized that this apparent methodological nuisance revealed the unusually powerful influence of confession evidence.

[推测] A confession can function as a cognitive shortcut that ends further scrutiny of guilt or innocence.

[06:43] **Interrogation Tactics and the Milgram Parallel**

[事实] After reading a widely used interrogation manual, Kassin found that investigators were advised to begin with a confident accusation of guilt and could imply they possessed substantial evidence even when they did not.

[事实] The manual described a gradual process of obtaining increasingly incriminating admissions until the suspect produced a complete narrative confession.

[事实] Kassin compared this escalation to Stanley Milgram’s obedience experiments, in which 65% of participants continued to the highest indicated shock level under pressure from an authority figure.

[事实] Kassin concluded that a police detective with real power over a suspect’s future could exert even greater influence than a laboratory experimenter.

[11:19] **The Missing Science of Confessions**

[事实] Kassin identified two troubling observations: juries readily believe confessions, while police are trained to obtain them through powerful psychological techniques.

[事实] He found that confessions had received little scientific study because guilt seemed self-evident whenever someone admitted wrongdoing.

[事实] An early case described by psychologist Hugo Münsterberg involved a cognitively vulnerable suspect who was interrogated for hours, gave inaccurate details, was convicted within a week, and was later executed.

[推测] Treating confessions as inherently truthful prevented researchers and courts from examining the conditions under which they were produced.

[13:38] **Voluntary and Compliant False Confessions**

[事实] Voluntary false confessions occur without direct police pressure and may be motivated by protecting another person, seeking attention, or psychological disturbance.

[事实] After Charles Lindbergh’s infant son was kidnapped, roughly 200 people falsely volunteered confessions; police rejected them when they could not provide independently verifiable crime details.

[事实] Compliant false confessors know they are innocent but confess to escape immediate stress or obtain an expected short-term benefit.

[事实] During the Salem witch trials, some accused people confessed because those who refused could be executed.

[推测] Under acute pressure, ending an immediate ordeal can feel more urgent than avoiding consequences that seem distant or uncertain.

[18:34] **The Central Park Jogger Case**

[事实] Five Black or Latino teenagers aged 14 to 16 confessed after being questioned for hours about the 1989 assault and rape of a jogger in New York’s Central Park.

[事实] Detectives conducted the interrogations off camera and recorded only the later confessions delivered to prosecutors.

[事实] The recorded statements contained some accurate details and expressions of remorse, but they also contradicted one another.

[事实] All five teenagers were convicted, and their confessions remained persuasive despite the absence of supporting evidence and DNA results excluding them as the source of the recovered semen.

[24:13] **Contaminated Confessions and Exoneration**

[事实] Kassin later learned that detectives had shown Corey Wise pictures of the victim and had taken some suspects to the crime scene before their recorded statements.

[事实] Kassin argued that this supplied information could explain why apparently accurate crime details appeared in the confessions.

[事实] Serial rapist Matias Reyes later confessed, and his DNA matched the crime-scene samples; the teenagers’ convictions were overturned in 2002.

[事实] The teenagers said they endured interrogations lasting 14 to 30 hours and believed that minimizing their roles and cooperating would allow them to go home, although detectives disputed claims about threats and promises.

[推测] Recording only the final statement concealed the process through which investigators may have shaped both the content of the confessions and the suspects’ expectations.

[29:43] **When Innocent People Internalize Guilt**

[事实] Internalized false confessions occur when suspects come to believe, at least temporarily, that they may have committed an act they do not remember.

[事实] Seventeen-year-old Peter Reilly initially denied killing his mother but was falsely told that he had failed a polygraph test and that the test revealed facts he might not remember.

[事实] Reilly moved from saying that he “must have” committed the crime to producing a full confession and was convicted.

[事实] A later prosecutor found evidence that Reilly was elsewhere at the time of death, and his conviction was vacated.

[推测] False scientific authority can make a vulnerable person distrust autobiographical memory and replace it with an externally supplied explanation.

[34:00] **Wrongful Convictions Also Endanger Public Safety**

[事实] After Reilly’s conviction was vacated, police did not meaningfully reinvestigate alternative suspects in his mother’s killing.

[事实] Kassin said that DNA exoneration cases sometimes identify actual perpetrators who committed additional violent crimes while innocent people remained imprisoned.

[事实] He argued that obtaining a confession from an innocent person and closing the case fails both the accused and public safety.

[推测] False confessions create two victims of institutional error: the wrongly convicted person and the public left exposed to the actual offender.

[37:19] **Police Confidence Exceeds Lie-Detection Accuracy**

[事实] In one experiment, prison inmates confessed to one crime they had committed and one they had not committed.

[事实] Observers distinguished true from false confessions with an average accuracy of 54%, approximately the level expected from chance.

[事实] Police observers were less accurate than college students because they were more likely to believe false confessions, yet the police expressed greater confidence.

[推测] Training and experience may increase confidence without correcting mistaken beliefs about how deception looks.

[38:49] **Suggestibility, Authority, and Isolation**

[事实] Research by psychologist Gísli Guðjónsson showed that leading questions and negative feedback can cause people to alter their recollections.

[事实] Children were more suggestible than adults, and interrogators could unintentionally introduce inaccurate information into the memories of suspects or witnesses.

[事实] Kassin described isolation as a standard starting point for interrogation: suspects are separated from family, friends, phones, and other sources of support or information.

[事实] Isolation prevents suspects from receiving emotional support and from checking investigators’ claims about evidence against an external reality.

[推测] Interrogation questions are not neutral information-gathering tools; their wording and context can help create the accounts they appear merely to uncover.

[43:19] **Exhaustion and the Duration of Interrogation**

[事实] Most interrogations last approximately one or two hours, but documented wrongful-conviction interrogations averaged about 16 hours and sometimes continued for 24 hours or longer.

[事实] The United States has no general time limit on interrogations.

[事实] Over time, suspects may be deprived of sleep, food, bathroom access, and social support.

[事实] Kassin treats an interrogation lasting 16 hours as an immediate indicator that the resulting confession carries elevated risk.

[推测] Prolonged interrogation can shift a suspect’s goal from protecting long-term interests to satisfying immediate physical and psychological needs.

[44:47] **Laboratory Evidence of False Confessions**

[事实] In Kassin’s computer experiment, volunteers were falsely accused of pressing a forbidden key that caused a crash.

[事实] When another apparent participant falsely claimed to have witnessed the act, substantially more volunteers signed confessions, and some came to believe they might actually have pressed the key.

[事实] A second experiment found that more than half of witnesses became unable to confirm another person’s continuous presence after merely being told that the person had confessed to leaving the room and stealing money.

[事实] In Innocence Project cases involving false confessions, almost 80% also contained at least one additional evidentiary error, usually arising after the confession.

[推测] Confessions do not simply join the evidence already available; they can alter how witnesses, informants, alibi providers, and forensic examiners interpret that evidence.

[48:44] **False Confession Tactics in the Workplace**

[事实] AutoZone employee Joaquin Robles was accused after $820 appeared to be missing and was interrogated for hours by a loss-prevention manager.

[事实] After being threatened with police involvement, Robles signed a dictated confession and agreed to repay the money through payroll deductions because he believed doing so would preserve his job.

[事实] AutoZone terminated him, and the company later discovered that the money had never been stolen; the apparent loss resulted from a clerical error.

[推测] The same pressures that produce false criminal confessions can operate in workplaces whenever an authority figure controls a valued outcome such as continued employment.

[51:05] **Reforming Investigations**

[事实] Kassin recommends recording interrogations in full so judges, juries, and prosecutors can evaluate how a confession was produced.

[事实] He supports evidence-based interviewing that relies on open-ended questions and avoids communicating undisclosed crime details to suspects.

[事实] This approach allows investigators to determine whether accurate details originated independently with the confessor.

[事实] The host and Kassin conclude that investigators should think more like scientists by remaining skeptical of their initial hypotheses and actively testing whether those hypotheses could be wrong.

[推测] The central reform is not eliminating all pressure, but replacing guilt-presumptive interrogation with a transparent process designed to discover reliable information.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s principal strength is its combination of personal experience, historical cases, major wrongful convictions, and controlled experiments. Together, these sources make the counterintuitive phenomenon of false confession psychologically understandable without suggesting that every confession is unreliable.

Its discussion of the Central Park case is especially valuable because it shows both sides of the evidentiary problem: why the recorded statements looked persuasive and how contamination, contradiction, prolonged questioning, and DNA evidence ultimately undermined them. The episode also broadens the issue beyond individual injustice by connecting wrongful convictions to unsolved crimes and reduced public safety.

A limitation is that several disputed interrogation details cannot be resolved because the original questioning was not fully recorded. The workplace example also illustrates the wider relevance of these mechanisms, but the episode offers less systematic evidence about how frequently false confessions occur outside criminal justice.

[推测] This episode is particularly useful for jurors, investigators, lawyers, managers, psychology students, and anyone inclined to treat confident admissions or authoritative accusations as conclusive proof.
