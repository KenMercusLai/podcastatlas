# AMA: Resveratrol, NAD, and Longevity Supplements

## 概览

This Ask Me Anything episode begins with a listener question about whether resveratrol can extend lifespan. Andrew Huberman answers that, as of the discussion in the transcript, he is not aware of strong direct evidence that resveratrol increases human lifespan.

The episode then expands into a broader review of popular longevity-related supplements and drugs, including grape seed extract, NR, NMN, NAD infusions, metformin, berberine, and rapamycin. Huberman distinguishes between tools he uses for subjective vitality or other health reasons and tools he does not use because the evidence for lifespan extension is not strong enough for him.

A central conclusion is that supplements and drugs should be treated as secondary to fundamentals: sleep, exercise, nutrition, sunlight, stress control, and social connection. Huberman repeatedly emphasizes consulting a board-certified physician before adding or removing supplements or prescription drugs.

[02:12] **Resveratrol evidence**

[事实] Huberman says resveratrol received attention because it was believed to affect cellular pathways that might extend cellular and organismal lifespan.
[事实] He says that by 2023, much of that thinking has been “debunked.”
[事实] He says resveratrol may have some positive effects on cell function, but he is not aware of direct evidence that it increases lifespan.
[推测] His position is skeptical but not closed; he invites recent, controlled evidence if listeners know of it.

[03:39] **Grape seed extract and vascular function**

[事实] Huberman says he takes 400 to 800 milligrams of grape seed extract, usually with a meal, as part of his daily supplementation stack.
[事实] He says he takes it mainly for vascular function, blood flow, and related effects, not to extend lifespan.
[事实] He says he would not place grape seed extract in his top five or top ten supplements.
[推测] He treats grape seed extract as a low-cost, low-risk “insurance policy,” rather than a core longevity intervention.

[06:01] **NAD, NR, and NMN**

[事实] Huberman describes NAD as a cellular pathway related to cellular energetics and mitochondrial function.
[事实] He says NR and NMN are considered by many to be precursors to NAD.
[事实] He says people try to increase NAD through NR supplementation, NMN supplementation, and direct NAD administration.
[事实] He says increasing NAD may not clearly or likely increase lifespan in humans at this point.

[08:21] **Huberman’s NR and NMN routine**

[事实] Huberman says he takes 500 milligrams of NR daily and one to two grams of sublingual NMN.
[事实] He says he takes NR and NMN to increase NAD and because he subjectively experiences sustained mental and physical energy.
[事实] He says he does not take NR or NMN with the expectation that they will increase lifespan.
[事实] He says he has no current financial relationship with companies selling NR or NMN.
[事实] He says NMN became unavailable as a conventional supplement after an FDA-related ruling connected to its filing as an experimental drug.

[13:22] **NAD infusions**

[事实] Huberman says he has tried intravenous NAD infusions twice.
[事实] He says he used 500 to 1,000 milligrams and found the experience very uncomfortable, including nausea and pressure-like sensations.
[事实] He says he felt better after the infusions, including improved sleep and vigor after illness.
[事实] He says he is not aware of clinical trials on NAD infusions for vigor and says people should consult their physician.
[推测] He sees NAD infusion as a possible occasional recovery tool for himself, not a regular longevity protocol.

[18:00] **Supplements are not the foundation**

[事实] Huberman says supplementation is not the foundation of mental health, physical health, or performance.
[事实] He says light exposure, movement, nutrition, stress modulation, and social relationships should be addressed before considering supplements.
[推测] This frames the episode’s supplement discussion as secondary to behavioral health fundamentals.

[18:41] **Metformin, berberine, and mTOR**

[事实] Huberman says he does not take metformin.
[事实] He says he also does not take berberine because it gives him severe headaches and lowers blood glucose.
[事实] He explains that metformin and berberine are discussed in longevity circles because of effects on blood glucose and mTOR-related pathways.
[事实] He says he does not see sufficient evidence to support taking metformin for increasing lifespan.
[事实] He says fasting may reduce mTOR, but he does not think there is direct evidence in humans that this increases lifespan.

[21:18] **Rapamycin**

[事实] Huberman says rapamycin is discussed because mTOR stands for mammalian target of rapamycin.
[事实] He says rapamycin is used largely as an anti-cancer drug and is being studied for lifespan effects.
[事实] He mentions research interest in rapamycin across species, including dogs.
[事实] He says he does not currently take rapamycin because the evidence does not justify it for him relative to its potential side effects.
[推测] His stance is cautious rather than dismissive, since he says future data could change his view.

[23:49] **Foundational longevity behaviors**

[事实] Huberman says quality sleep is fundamental to a quality and long life.
[事实] He says no supplement or drug for longevity comes close to the health-metric improvements associated with quality sleep and sufficient exercise.
[事实] He recommends 180 to 220 minutes of zone two cardio per week, VO2 max work at least once per week, and resistance training.
[事实] He says resistance training helps maintain muscle size, strength, and nerve-to-muscle connectivity.
[事实] He also emphasizes nutrition, social connection, stress control, morning sunlight, and circadian rhythm.

[26:34] **Medical caution and closing**

[事实] Huberman says people should consult a board-certified physician before adding or removing supplements or prescription drugs.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its clear separation between evidence for lifespan extension and personal subjective effects. Huberman openly says he uses some tools for energy or vascular reasons while not claiming they extend lifespan.

[推测] A strong point is the repeated emphasis on basics: sleep, exercise, nutrition, sunlight, stress control, and relationships. This keeps the discussion from becoming a supplement shopping list.

[推测] The main limitation is that the excerpt summarizes evidence at a high level rather than walking through specific studies in detail. Listeners wanting paper-by-paper analysis would need additional sources.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in longevity supplements who want a cautious, practical framework for separating promising mechanisms, personal experimentation, and proven health behaviors.
