# Mindsets, Placebo Effects, and Leveraging Stress

## 概览

This episode features Andrew Huberman’s conversation with Dr. Aliyah Crum about how mindsets shape motivation, perception, behavior, and physiology. Crum defines mindsets as core assumptions about a domain, such as stress, food, exercise, illness, or side effects.

The central claim is that mindset is not merely “positive thinking.” The transcript describes studies showing that beliefs about what people eat, whether their work counts as exercise, and whether stress is harmful or enhancing can change measurable outcomes such as ghrelin response, blood pressure, stress symptoms, work performance, cortisol, and DHEA.

The discussion moves from classic growth mindset research to Crum’s work on placebo, nocebo, nutrition, exercise, and stress. The most actionable takeaway is a three-step approach to stress: acknowledge it, welcome it as a sign that something important is at stake, and use the stress response to pursue the goal that matters.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Introduction and Guest Context**

[事实] Huberman introduces the episode as part of Huberman Lab Essentials, focused on actionable science-based tools for mental health, physical health, and performance.
[事实] The conversation is with Dr. Aliyah Crum, whose work centers on the science of mindsets.

[00:24] **What a Mindset Is**

[事实] Crum defines mindsets as core beliefs or assumptions about a domain that orient expectations, explanations, and goals.
[事实] She uses stress as an example: people may view stress as enhancing and useful, or as debilitating and harmful.
[事实] These beliefs shape what people expect under stress, how they explain events, and what they are motivated to do.

[01:33] **Examples Beyond Growth Mindset**

[事实] Huberman references Carol Dweck’s growth mindset work, especially the idea that intelligence or ability can be developed rather than fixed.
[事实] Crum explains that mindsets simplify complex reality, but they are consequential because they shape motivation.
[事实] Her lab has studied mindsets about stress, food, exercise, illness, symptoms, and side effects.
[事实] Crum says mindsets can influence not only motivation but also physiological mechanisms by changing what the body prioritizes and prepares to do.

[05:01] **The Milkshake Study Setup**

[事实] Crum describes a Yale study asking whether beliefs about food can change physiological response while objective nutrients remain constant.
[事实] The study drew on placebo-effect research, where beliefs about a treatment can produce measurable physiological effects even without an active drug.
[事实] Participants were told they were tasting milkshakes with different nutrient profiles, while researchers measured physiological responses.

[07:03] **Milkshake Study Findings**

[事实] The same participants consumed the same actual milkshake at two time points, but were told it was either a high-calorie indulgent shake or a low-calorie sensible shake.
[事实] The actual shake was about 300 calories, despite labels suggesting roughly 620 calories or a diet shake.
[事实] Researchers measured ghrelin, described as a hunger hormone that normally drops after calorie intake.
[事实] When participants believed they had consumed the indulgent shake, ghrelin dropped at about three times the rate compared with when they believed it was sensible.

[09:15] **Eating Mindset and Satiety**

[事实] Crum initially expected that believing food was healthy would be the better mindset, but the study suggested the opposite for ghrelin response.
[事实] When participants thought they were eating sensibly, their bodies left them physiologically less satiated.
[推测] For people trying to maintain or lose weight, the transcript suggests that feeling one is eating enough and eating indulgently may support more adaptive satiety signals than feeling deprived.

[10:20] **Sponsor Break: Function**

[事实] Huberman describes Function as a service offering over 100 lab tests and physician-informed interpretation.
[事实] He says his own testing showed elevated mercury and that subsequent changes helped reduce it.
[事实] This section is a sponsor message rather than part of the scientific discussion.

[11:59] **Diet Beliefs and Social Context**

[事实] Huberman asks whether mindset effects might help explain why people in different diet communities report strong benefits from their chosen approach.
[事实] Crum says both the actual diet and beliefs about the diet matter.
[事实] She emphasizes that social and cultural contexts shape mindsets.
[事实] She cautions against framing outcomes as either entirely mental or entirely physical.

[13:33] **Nocebo Effects and Symptoms**

[事实] Huberman mentions psychogenic fever research, where believing one is sick can produce a real increase in body temperature.
[事实] Crum identifies this as related to the nocebo effect, where negative beliefs cause negative consequences.
[事实] She says people are more likely to experience side effects when they are told about those side effects.

[14:28] **Hotel Housekeeper Exercise Study**

[事实] Crum describes a study of hotel housekeepers who were physically active at work but often did not view their work as exercise.
[事实] Some housekeepers reported getting zero exercise, despite doing physically demanding work all day.
[事实] Participants were randomized, and one group was informed that their work met exercise guidelines and could produce health benefits.

[15:45] **Exercise Mindset Outcomes**

[事实] Four weeks later, the informed group showed health improvements despite reporting no behavioral changes.
[事实] Crum says they lost weight and reduced systolic blood pressure by about ten points on average.
[事实] She argues that public health messages focused only on telling people what exercise they need may fail to motivate behavior.
[推测] The study implies that recognizing existing physical activity as meaningful exercise may change the body’s response to that activity.

[17:13] **Rethinking Stress**

[事实] Crum says public health messages have often portrayed stress as harmful to health, productivity, relationships, fertility, and cognition.
[事实] She argues that the broader stress literature is more complex and does not show stress as uniformly debilitating.
[事实] She cites evidence that stress can narrow focus, increase attention, speed information processing, support physiological toughening, and sometimes contribute to post-traumatic growth.

[20:01] **Stressors Versus Stress Mindset**

[事实] Crum distinguishes between appraising a specific stressor as a challenge or threat and holding a broader mindset about the nature of stress.
[事实] Prior work showed that viewing stressors as challenges rather than threats can produce more adaptive brain and body responses.
[事实] Her lab studied whether seeing stress itself as enhancing or debilitating affects outcomes.

[21:13] **UBS Stress Mindset Study**

[事实] Crum’s team studied employees at UBS after the 2008 financial crisis, during a period of layoffs and high pressure.
[事实] Employees were randomized to see no videos, videos presenting stress as debilitating, or videos presenting stress as potentially enhancing.
[事实] Nine minutes of videos over a week changed stress mindsets.
[事实] Those who watched the enhancing videos reported fewer stress-related symptoms such as back aches, muscle tension, insomnia, and racing heart, and also reported better work performance.

[23:48] **What “Stress Is Enhancing” Does and Does Not Mean**

[事实] Crum clarifies that a stress-is-enhancing mindset does not mean the stressor itself is good.
[事实] She gives examples such as cancer diagnosis and poverty as stressors that are not good.
[事实] The claim is that the experience of stress associated with challenge or adversity can sometimes lead to enhanced cognition, health, performance, or well-being.
[事实] Crum says this mindset changes motivation, affect, and potentially physiology.

[26:21] **Sponsor Breaks: AG1 and Carbon**

[事实] Huberman presents sponsor messages for AG1 and Carbon.
[事实] AG1 is described as a vitamin, mineral, probiotic drink with prebiotics and adaptogens.
[事实] Carbon is described as a nutrition coaching app that adapts based on user results.

[29:20] **Hormones, Stress, and Subconscious Pathways**

[事实] Huberman references Duncan French’s work suggesting that a strong adrenaline response, such as from a first skydive, can raise testosterone in the short term.
[事实] He notes that adrenaline is epinephrine and is biochemically derived from dopamine.
[事实] Crum describes mindsets as a portal between conscious and subconscious processes.
[事实] She says mindsets operate like default settings that influence whether the body prepares to protect itself or grow.

[32:27] **Three Steps to Leverage Stress**

[事实] Crum says people often ask how to manage or cope with stress, but the more useful question is how to leverage or work with it.
[事实] She defines stress neutrally as the effect of experiencing or anticipating adversity in goal-related efforts.
[事实] She says people only stress about things they care about.
[事实] Her three-step approach is to acknowledge stress, welcome it because it signals something important, and utilize the stress response to pursue the goal.

[34:40] **Stress as a Built-In Response**

[事实] Huberman says stress can narrow visual attention, sharpen time perception, and speed information processing.
[事实] He notes that the stress response is generic and does not need to be trained in the same way as relaxation.
[事实] Crum encourages listeners to examine their own mindsets, identify which ones serve them, and adopt more useful, adaptive, and empowering ones.

[35:38] **Crum’s Personal Framework**

[事实] Crum says her guiding principle is that mindsets matter.
[事实] She learned this partly through athletics, especially gymnastics, where visualization affected performance.
[事实] She also mentions her father’s background as a martial artist and meditation teacher.
[事实] She says science has done relatively little to deliberately leverage human mental resources, including the placebo effect.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The strongest value of this episode is that it treats mindset as a measurable psychological and physiological variable rather than as vague self-help language. The milkshake, hotel worker, and UBS studies give the discussion concrete evidence and make the claims easier to evaluate.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for listeners interested in behavioral science, stress, nutrition psychology, exercise adherence, placebo effects, and practical mental frameworks. The “acknowledge, welcome, utilize” model is the clearest actionable tool.

[推测] A limitation is that the transcript presents selected studies and mechanisms without deeply discussing study limitations, effect sizes beyond a few examples, or replication boundaries. Listeners should treat the ideas as promising tools rather than universal guarantees.

[推测] Overall, the episode argues persuasively that what people believe about stress, food, exercise, and symptoms can change how they behave and how their bodies respond, while still emphasizing that mindset interacts with reality rather than replacing it.
