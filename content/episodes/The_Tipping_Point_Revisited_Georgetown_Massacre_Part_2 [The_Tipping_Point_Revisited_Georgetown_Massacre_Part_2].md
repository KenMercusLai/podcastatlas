# The Tipping Point Revisited: Georgetown Massacre, Part 2

## 概览

This episode concludes the story of *U.S. v. Khoury*, in which Ayman Khoury was prosecuted after paying $180,000 to Georgetown tennis coach Gordie Ernst to secure his daughter Catherine’s admission. After four damaging days, the defense had exposed Georgetown’s blurred boundaries between fundraising and admissions but still needed evidence that directly undermined the government’s theory.

The decisive turn came when Catherine unexpectedly testified. Prosecutors alleged that she and her father had planned to fake a shoulder injury so she could avoid playing tennis. Her testimony and contemporaneous text messages instead showed that she abandoned tennis after her parents’ breakup shattered her relationship with her father, who had taught her the sport.

The defense transformed those private messages into the emotional center of its closing argument. The jury acquitted Ayman Khoury on every count. The episode then reveals the document kept from the jury: a Georgetown fundraising report that assessed the Khoury family’s potential giving capacity at $1–5 million, further complicating the university’s portrayal as a straightforward victim.

## 分段落总结

[00:41] **A Surprise Witness Arrives**

[事实] On the fifth day of the trial, the defense informed the judge that an unexpected witness had arrived and that it intended to introduce text messages she wrote in May 2015.

[事实] The witness was Catherine Khoury, the daughter whose Georgetown admission was at the center of the case.

[事实] The prosecutors appeared shocked because they had not expected Catherine or her messages to become part of the trial.

[02:04] **The Defense Still Needs a Knockout Punch**

[事实] Ayman Khoury had admitted giving $180,000 in cash to Georgetown tennis coach Gordie Ernst to help his daughter gain admission.

[事实] The defense’s earlier examination of Georgetown showed that admissions and fundraising were closely intertwined, but this only placed Khoury’s conduct within a broader compromised system.

[事实] Roy Black said Catherine’s appearance and testimony became the trial’s decisive “knockout punch.”

[推测] Exposing institutional hypocrisy could create doubt, but the defense still needed evidence that directly contradicted the prosecution’s account of Khoury’s intent.

[05:34] **The Salesforce Document Is Kept from the Jury**

[事实] Roy Black repeatedly asked Georgetown witnesses about Salesforce and the university’s investigation of prospective students’ family wealth.

[事实] When Black attempted to display a related document, prosecutors objected and the judge sustained the objection.

[事实] Trial rules required a witness to authenticate the document, but the Georgetown witnesses would not do so.

[事实] Unable to present the document through those witnesses, the defense turned to Catherine and her text messages.

[08:47] **The Prosecution Keeps Catherine an Abstraction**

[事实] The prosecution told jurors that Catherine’s grades, test scores, and tennis ability would be discussed, while emphasizing that she herself was not the person on trial.

[事实] The government used her perceived shortcomings to support its case without calling her to explain her own actions.

[事实] Black described Catherine as poised and authentic and said the prosecution failed to alter its theory after hearing her testimony.

[推测] Catherine’s physical presence made it harder for jurors to treat her merely as evidence in a scheme devised by others.

[11:25] **The Government’s Fake-Injury Theory**

[事实] Timothy Donovan testified that Ayman Khoury said Catherine did not intend to play at Georgetown and would rely on an old shoulder injury as an excuse.

[事实] Donovan understood the injury to be illegitimate and part of a plan allowing Catherine not to play.

[事实] Prosecutors therefore portrayed Ernst, Donovan, and Ayman Khoury as conspiring to deceive Georgetown, while also alleging that father and daughter planned to fake an injury.

[事实] The government granted Donovan immunity, and his account supplied a central theory of its case.

[事实] The defense argued that prosecutors adopted this theory without ever speaking to Catherine.

[16:28] **Catherine Establishes Her Own Story**

[事实] Howard Srebnick conducted Catherine’s direct examination, beginning with simple questions before addressing tennis and her admission.

[事实] Catherine said her father taught her tennis when she was about five or six and remained her primary coach; playing together was one of their favorite shared activities.

[事实] She described herself as the sixth player on an eight-person high-school team and acknowledged that she initially believed she was not good enough for Georgetown tennis.

[事实] After coach Gordie Ernst encouraged her to train, she took the sport more seriously and expected to practice with Georgetown’s team while redshirting for a year.

[事实] Catherine believed her acceptance resulted from her own effort and ability.

[21:30] **The Family Breakup Explains Why She Quit**

[事实] Catherine learned in May 2015, after her Georgetown acceptance, that her parents were divorcing.

[事实] She testified that she felt betrayed by her father, lost respect for him, and expressed her anger through tears, harsh words, and text messages.

[事实] The messages called her father pathetic and selfish and said she no longer wanted to associate with him.

[事实] Catherine explained that tennis reminded her of her father and became too painful to continue, so she did not join Georgetown’s team.

[事实] She testified that her withdrawal from tennis had nothing to do with a shoulder injury and that she hoped she might love the sport again someday.

[26:39] **The Defense Turns the Messages into Its Closing Argument**

[事实] Gladwell argues that prosecutors first demeaned Catherine’s tennis ability, then criticized her privileged admission, and finally accused her of helping fake an injury.

[事实] In closing, Black stood near Ayman Khoury and read Catherine’s angry messages aloud while looking at him.

[事实] Khoury, members of the defense team, and some jurors cried during the presentation.

[事实] Black argued that the messages showed Catherine was not under her father’s control and would not have cooperated with a fabricated injury scheme.

[推测] The defense converted the case from an abstract admissions conspiracy into a recognizable account of family rupture, making the prosecution’s theory emotionally and psychologically less credible.

[30:45] **Acquittal on All Counts**

[事实] Black’s closing argument emphasized that the prosecution’s theory failed because prosecutors did not know about Catherine’s messages.

[事实] The jury acquitted Ayman Khoury on every count.

[事实] After the verdict, a juror encountered Khoury outside the courtroom and told him to take care of his daughter.

[推测] Gladwell interprets the verdict as reflecting the jury’s inability to draw a morally convincing distinction between Khoury’s conduct and Georgetown’s own practices.

[32:18] **What the Hidden Salesforce Report Contained**

[事实] Georgetown maintained a Salesforce database that tracked families’ estimated wealth, donation potential, institutional ties, and contacts with fundraisers.

[事实] Athletic connections and having a child at the university were described as indicators of future giving.

[事实] After Catherine’s admission but before she arrived on campus, the admissions officer handling her application asked the development office for information about Ayman Khoury’s giving potential.

[事实] Georgetown’s internal estimate placed the Khoury family’s giving capacity between $1 million and $5 million.

[事实] The defense possessed the report and mentioned its existence, but the jury never saw it because no witness authenticated it.

[推测] The report weakened a simple victim-perpetrator narrative by showing that Georgetown itself viewed the family as a major fundraising prospect.

[35:09] **Georgetown Acknowledges the Document Afterward**

[事实] After the acquittal, Black publicly discussed the Salesforce report and its $1–5 million estimate.

[事实] Georgetown’s lawyers then complained that he had discussed a confidential university document.

[事实] The defense highlighted the contradiction between Georgetown’s reluctance to acknowledge the report during trial and its assertion of ownership afterward.

[事实] Black responded to the complaint with the words “SORE LOSER.”

[36:31] **The Broader Question Behind the Case**

[事实] Gladwell connects the trial to a broader question explored in *Revenge of the Tipping Point*: why tennis and other college sports have become staging grounds for the ambitions of prosecutors, elite universities, wealthy parents, and their children.

[事实] He previews another federal case involving cash payments to an Ivy League basketball coach and the same defense lawyers.

[推测] The episode treats the Khoury case as more than a dispute about one payment, using it to question the social value and institutional power assigned to elite college athletics.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its courtroom storytelling. It carefully establishes the prosecution’s theory, shows how Catherine’s testimony contradicted it, and explains why contemporaneous messages carried more force than a later denial alone.

It also offers a valuable examination of institutional inconsistency. Georgetown appeared as the alleged victim of an admissions scheme while simultaneously evaluating the Khoury family as a potential source of millions of dollars in donations.

[推测] The account is openly sympathetic to Khoury’s defense and strongly critical of both prosecutors and Georgetown. Because the episode relies heavily on interviews with the defense team and notes that the U.S. Attorney’s Office declined to participate, listeners should distinguish the documented courtroom events from Gladwell’s more forceful moral conclusions.

[推测] This episode will be especially useful to listeners interested in trial strategy, elite-college admissions, fundraising ethics, and the emotional narratives that can shape how juries interpret disputed intent.
