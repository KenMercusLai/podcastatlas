# The Rif War: Morocco, Colonialism, and the Road to the Spanish Civil War

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* focuses on Morocco through the Rif War, a little-known but highly consequential colonial conflict centered on the Rif Mountains in northern Morocco during the early 20th century, especially the 1920s.

The hosts frame the war as both a late colonial war and a preview of later 20th-century conflicts. The discussion connects Spanish imperial humiliation, Moroccan Berber resistance, anti-colonial Islam-inflected nationalism, chemical weapons, aerial bombing, amphibious landings, and the emergence of figures later central to the Spanish Civil War.

A central figure is Mohammed Abdel Krim, who moves from Spanish-linked collaborator and Islamic judge to leader of the Rif resistance. Opposing him are Spanish military leaders whose failures, brutality, and later mythologies helped shape the Spanish Army of Africa and the political culture that fed into Franco’s rise.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Introducing Morocco and the Rif War**

[事实] The hosts introduce the episode as part of their World Cup history series and choose Morocco as the subject.

[事实] Dominic identifies the Rif War as an important but little-known conflict, especially in the English-speaking world.

[事实] The war involved figures such as Franco and Marshal Pétain, American airmen, chemical weapons, colonial armies, and Moroccan tribesmen.

[推测] The episode presents the Rif War as a compact case study in how older imperial warfare fed into modern forms of violence.

[02:15] **A War That Prefigures the 20th Century**

[事实] The hosts describe the Rif War as both the last Victorian-style colonial war and a precursor to the Spanish Civil War, the Second World War, Vietnam, Afghanistan, and other later conflicts.

[事实] Morocco is described as the only North African country not incorporated into the Ottoman Empire.

[事实] The Alawi dynasty is said to have been established in the 1630s.

[推测] The hosts use the war to argue that colonial frontiers often became testing grounds for military and political methods later used elsewhere.

[03:18] **Morocco, European Pressure, and the Rif Mountains**

[事实] By the end of the 19th century, France had established a protectorate in Morocco, while Spain retained enclaves at Ceuta and Melilla.

[事实] The Rif Mountains lie between Ceuta and Melilla on Morocco’s Mediterranean coast.

[事实] The people of the Rif are described as Berbers, or historically Numidians or Mauritanians in Roman terms.

[事实] The mountains were forested, inaccessible, and rarely visited by Europeans.

[06:02] **Spanish Imperial Humiliation and the Africanist Lobby**

[事实] Spain had lost much of its empire, including Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines, especially after the Spanish-American War.

[事实] King Alfonso XIII and those around him supported an “Africanist” lobby that saw North Africa as Spain’s remaining imperial destiny.

[事实] The Catholic Church in Spain framed expansion into Morocco in crusading and Reconquista terms.

[推测] Spanish expansion in Morocco is presented as partly driven by wounded national pride after defeats by the United States.

[07:30] **Mining Interests and the Escalation of Conflict**

[事实] The Rif Mountains contained high-grade iron ore that could be extracted through open-pit mining.

[事实] The Spanish crown granted mining concessions to businessmen close to the royal family.

[事实] In 1909, local Berbers attacked Spanish workers building a railway bridge intended to open access to the mines.

[事实] Spain responded by mobilizing 40,000 troops and sending them into Morocco.

[推测] The hosts treat this as the moment when Spain became deeply committed to an escalating colonial conflict.

[09:19] **Opposition Inside Spain and the 1912 Protectorate**

[事实] Many people in Spain opposed the Moroccan campaign, fearing disaster.

[事实] In 1909, reserve call-ups triggered strikes and riots, including the “Tragic Week” uprising in Barcelona.

[事实] In 1912, France formally granted Spain a protectorate along Morocco’s northern coast.

[事实] Britain supported a Spanish buffer between Gibraltar and French territory, while European powers also wanted to prevent German involvement.

[11:03] **Mohammed Abdel Krim’s Background**

[事实] Mohammed Abdel Krim came from the Rif Mountains and belonged to a prominent family.

[事实] His father was literate, educated, and recognized by the Sultan as an Islamic judge.

[事实] Abdel Krim studied Islamic law and classical Arabic in Fez.

[事实] Through his father’s links to Spain, Abdel Krim taught in Melilla and later worked with Spanish authorities.

[13:00] **From Spanish Collaborator to Resistance Leader**

[事实] Abdel Krim wrote Arabic newspaper articles praising European civilization and Spanish modernization.

[事实] He worked with the Native Affairs Office and became chief Islamic judge in Melilla around 1913.

[事实] During the First World War, the Spanish jailed him briefly after discovering links between his family and the Germans.

[事实] Although he regained his judgeship, the imprisonment humiliated him in the eyes of his community.

[推测] The episode suggests his previous collaboration gave him the education, contacts, and administrative experience needed to become an effective anti-colonial leader.

[16:03] **Islam, Nationalism, and Tribal Unity**

[事实] By around 1920, the Abdel Krim family had severed links with Spanish authorities.

[事实] Abdel Krim became a leading figure of resistance against Spanish intrusion into the Rif.

[事实] The hosts discuss Islam as a unifying force among Berber tribes that had often been divided by local feuds.

[推测] Abdel Krim’s movement is framed as combining local defense, revenge, anti-colonial politics, and Islamic legitimacy.

[18:23] **The Spanish Army’s Weaknesses**

[事实] The Spanish army in North Africa is described as badly led, corrupt, poorly supplied, and largely composed of poor illiterate conscripts.

[事实] Soldiers were given old or malfunctioning rifles and survived on poor rations.

[事实] Some soldiers reportedly bartered rifles in Berber villages for fresh vegetables.

[事实] Venereal disease was said to be widespread because the army was accompanied by many prostitutes.

[推测] The hosts present Spain’s military failure as institutional, not merely tactical.

[21:15] **General Fernández Silvestre and Overconfidence**

[事实] General Manuel Fernández Silvestre commanded Spanish forces in Ceuta and Melilla.

[事实] He had served in Cuba during the Spanish-American War and wanted to avenge Spain’s humiliation.

[事实] He dismissed Abdel Krim’s warning not to advance into the mountains.

[事实] Silvestre reportedly said the only way to succeed in Morocco was to cut off the heads of all the Moors.

[推测] His contempt for Abdel Krim and the Rif fighters foreshadows the catastrophe at Annual.

[23:18] **The Disaster at Annual**

[事实] Silvestre sent Spanish forces into the mountains, where they became separated in small forts called blockhouses.

[事实] Communications failed, relief expeditions were destroyed, and soldiers in isolated forts ran out of supplies.

[事实] In July 1921, Silvestre ordered a retreat from Annual toward the coast.

[事实] Rif fighters shot down retreating Spanish troops from the hills, killing around 13,000 people.

[事实] Silvestre remained behind and reportedly shot himself.

[27:33] **Spain’s Political Crisis After Annual**

[事实] After Annual, Spain regarded the defeat as one of the most humiliating in its history.

[事实] King Alfonso XIII was told of the disaster while playing golf in the south of France and responded dismissively.

[事实] Spain raised a new army, but strikes, mutinies, flag-burning, and sympathy for the Rif appeared inside Spain.

[事实] General Miguel Primo de Rivera seized power in a coup partly connected to fears of an investigation into Annual.

[推测] The disaster helped undermine parliamentary politics and strengthened military authoritarianism in Spain.

[31:10] **The Republic of the Rif**

[事实] After Spain pulled back to Ceuta and Melilla, Abdel Krim became the unchallenged master of the Rif Mountains.

[事实] He created the unrecognized Republic of the Rif.

[事实] The Republic built roads, formed a small bureaucracy, and instituted an Islamic legal code.

[事实] The hosts say the state did not last long enough for its institutions to fully embed.

[推测] External enemies, especially Spain and France, helped Abdel Krim hold together otherwise divided groups.

[32:42] **The Army of Africa, the Foreign Legion, and Franco**

[事实] Spain began relying on Moroccan troops known as regulares, with Spanish officers.

[事实] These forces became part of the Army of Africa later associated with Franco.

[事实] Spain also developed its own Foreign Legion, led by Lieutenant-Colonel José Millán Astray.

[事实] Millán Astray was wounded in Morocco, wore an eyepatch, and was known as the “glorious mutilated one.”

[事实] Francisco Franco became Millán Astray’s important subordinate and organizer.

[35:00] **Crusading Ideology and Franco’s Mythology**

[事实] The Spanish Foreign Legion was steeped in Reconquista and Christian crusading imagery.

[事实] Franco had fought in Morocco for years and had once survived a bullet wound in the abdomen near Ceuta.

[事实] His Moroccan troops reportedly believed he was protected by God.

[推测] The hosts suggest this idea of Franco as chosen or protected fed into later nationalist mythology during the Spanish Civil War.

[37:16] **Chemical Weapons in the Rif War**

[事实] Chemical weapons had been used in the First World War and were outlawed by the 1925 Geneva Protocol.

[事实] Spain signed the protocol but still intended to use chemical weapons in Morocco.

[事实] The hosts list phosgene, diphosgene, nitrochloroform, and mustard gas as chemicals used.

[事实] French intelligence and German companies helped Spain obtain weapons and systems.

[推测] The war shows European powers cooperating to preserve colonial authority despite legal and moral constraints.

[39:16] **France Enters the War**

[事实] Abdel Krim sent forces into the French protectorate after French authorities began limiting his supplies.

[事实] Rif forces attacked French positions and killed or wounded about 6,000 French soldiers.

[事实] France responded by bringing in Marshal Pétain, then celebrated as the hero of Verdun.

[事实] In summer 1925, French and Spanish leaders agreed to crush Abdel Krim.

[推测] Attacking French positions is presented as Abdel Krim’s major strategic mistake.

[41:03] **American Pilots and Civilian Bombing**

[事实] Charles Sweeney, an American First World War pilot, organized American volunteers to fight for the French.

[事实] The volunteers had several bombers and carried out raids, including a bombing of Chefchaouen.

[事实] The hosts describe this as one of the first aerial bombardments of a civilian town.

[事实] Sweeney justified the intervention as defending “white man’s civilisation.”

[推测] The episode connects the Rif bombing to later attacks on Guernica, Coventry, and Hamburg.

[44:11] **The Alhucemas Landing**

[事实] The French and Spanish planned an amphibious landing at Alhucemas Bay.

[事实] On September 8, 1925, 20,000 French troops marched north while 18,000 Spanish troops landed by sea.

[事实] The operation involved landing craft, artillery, tanks, air support from more than 100 planes, naval bombardment, and an aircraft carrier.

[事实] Primo de Rivera was supreme commander, while José Sanjurjo commanded on the ground.

[事实] Franco led the first wave of troops ashore.

[推测] The hosts present Alhucemas as a forerunner of later large-scale amphibious operations such as D-Day.

[47:00] **Abdel Krim’s Defeat and Exile**

[事实] After French and Spanish forces advanced through the mountains and occupied his home village, Abdel Krim realized he could not defeat both powers.

[事实] He surrendered to the French rather than the Spanish in 1926.

[事实] The French took him and his family to Réunion, where they lived in comfortable exile for about 20 years.

[事实] In 1947, while traveling toward France for health reasons, he was taken off the ship in Egypt by Moroccan nationalists and hidden in Cairo.

[事实] He refused to return to Morocco while French and Spanish enclaves remained there and died in Cairo in 1963.

[49:24] **Casualties and Historical Significance**

[事实] Spain lost about 43,000 troops killed, missing, and wounded.

[事实] France lost about 18,000.

[事实] Berber losses are uncertain, perhaps 10,000 or 20,000.

[事实] The hosts ask whether the Rif War should be seen as the last colonial war, the first anti-colonial war, or an ancestor of the Spanish Civil War and World War II.

[推测] The war’s significance lies partly in its ambiguity: it belongs to both the age of empire and the age of modern ideological war.

[50:03] **The Rif War and the Spanish Civil War**

[事实] The war was marked by massacres, torture, beheadings, and extreme violence.

[事实] Spanish Africanist officers developed a reactionary mindset shaped by humiliation, distrust of politicians, and belief in extreme violence.

[事实] Emilio Mola, José Sanjurjo, and Franco all had important links to the Rif War or the Army of Africa.

[事实] Franco became commander of the Army of Africa and later the face of the nationalist rebellion after other senior figures died.

[推测] The hosts argue that the Spanish Civil War can be traced in significant ways back to Morocco in the 1910s and 1920s.

[52:05] **Closing Reflections on Moroccan History**

[事实] The hosts describe the episode as grim but useful background for future episodes on the Spanish Civil War.

[事实] They discuss possibly returning to Moroccan subjects such as Marrakesh, Fez, or Tangier.

[事实] Tangier is described as a former League of Nations protectorate, a neutral city, a literary center, and an important site in gay culture.

[推测] The closing suggests Morocco offers many further historical angles beyond the Rif War.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s main value is that it takes a relatively obscure conflict and shows why it matters far beyond Moroccan or Spanish history. The hosts connect the Rif War to colonial violence, anti-colonial resistance, aerial bombardment, chemical warfare, and the formation of the Spanish military culture that later produced Franco’s regime.

A major strength is the vivid narrative built around Abdel Krim, Fernández Silvestre, Primo de Rivera, Pétain, Millán Astray, Sanjurjo, and Franco. The episode is especially effective at showing how personalities, imperial ambitions, military incompetence, and ideology interacted.

Its limitation is that the Moroccan and Berber perspective is largely mediated through the hosts’ narrative rather than explored through many direct local sources. Some casualty figures and bombing effects are explicitly uncertain in the discussion.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in imperial history, Morocco, the origins of the Spanish Civil War, and the way colonial wars anticipated later forms of modern warfare.
