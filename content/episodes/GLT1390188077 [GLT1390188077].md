# The Rolling Stones in the Late 1960s: Redlands, Brian Jones, Hyde Park, and Altamont

## 概览

This episode traces the Rolling Stones from the Redlands drugs raid in February 1967 through Brian Jones’s death, the Hyde Park memorial concert, and the violence at Altamont in December 1969.

The hosts argue that the Stones became a symbolic battlefield for wider anxieties about drugs, class, youth culture, fame, and Britain’s changing social order. The Redlands case was legally minor in some respects, but it became national news because the band already functioned as “folk devils.”

The second half turns darker, focusing on Brian Jones’s decline and death, then on the Stones’ role in the shift from 1960s pop to more serious, adult, album-oriented rock. Altamont is presented as a symbolic endpoint of the decade, though the hosts suggest the optimism of the 1960s had already faded before that concert.

## 分段落总结

[00:33] **Redlands Raid Sets the Scene**

[事实] Keith Richards later described a police knock at the door of his country house, Redlands, on the evening of 12 February 1967.

[事实] The hosts identify the officer as Chief Inspector Gordon Dynley of the West Sussex Constabulary, arriving to search the house.

[事实] The episode frames the raid as the start of one of the most famous trials in post-war British history.

[01:58] **Episode Roadmap**

[事实] The discussion focuses on the Rolling Stones in the last three years of the 1960s.

[事实] The hosts plan to cover the Redlands drugs case, Brian Jones’s fate, Hyde Park in 1969, and Altamont later that year.

[推测] The episode treats the Stones less as a simple rock band story and more as a lens on cultural conflict in late-1960s Britain and America.

[03:09] **1967 Britain and the Stones at a Crossroads**

[事实] By 1967, Harold Wilson’s Labour government was struggling economically, and the national mood was more anxious than in the early 1960s.

[事实] The Stones had major British and American hits, but the hosts say their blues-edged style seemed out of step with psychedelia.

[事实] Their album *Between the Buttons* is described as more folky and psychedelic than earlier records.

[推测] The Stones were trying to adapt to a cultural moment increasingly shaped by the Beatles, sitars, backwards recording, and psychedelic experimentation.

[05:06] **Celebrity, Class, and Country Houses**

[事实] Mick Jagger, Keith Richards, and Brian Jones had become figures recognised even by people not deeply interested in their music.

[事实] The band members bought country houses, including Richards’s Redlands, Jagger’s Stargroves, Watts’s Peckham’s, Jones’s Cotchford Farm, and Wyman’s Geding Hall.

[事实] The hosts stress the incongruity between the Stones’ rebellious image and their attraction to country-house status, history, and aristocratic circles.

[推测] The houses made the Stones more provocative to critics because they appeared to be occupying spaces associated with traditional English respectability.

[09:02] **The Redlands Weekend**

[事实] On 11 February 1967, Jagger and Richards drove to Redlands with friends after working at Olympic Sound Studios.

[事实] Guests included Marianne Faithfull, Robert Fraser, Christopher Gibbs, and other countercultural hangers-on.

[事实] The hosts say the weekend was not a bacchanal: the group had smoked cannabis but also walked, talked, took photographs, and went to the beach.

[10:24] **News of the World and the Police Tip-Off**

[事实] The police raid followed a tip-off from the *News of the World*.

[事实] The newspaper already had a conflict with Jagger because it had falsely accused him of taking drugs openly in a London club after confusing him with Brian Jones.

[事实] The police obtained a search warrant from magistrates in Chichester and arrived with 18 uniformed officers.

[推测] The hosts present the newspaper’s role as partly retaliatory, driven by its desire to find damaging material on Jagger.

[11:57] **What the Police Found**

[事实] The police and guests were described as mostly polite during the raid.

[事实] Marianne Faithfull had been bathing and wrapped herself in a fur rug, later feeding salacious but inaccurate press coverage.

[事实] Police found amphetamines in Jagger’s green velvet jacket and found marijuana, amphetamines, and heroin tablets on Robert Fraser.

[事实] Jagger and Fraser were charged with drug possession, while Richards was charged with allowing cannabis smoking on his property.

[15:47] **Drugs as a Symbolic Panic**

[事实] The hosts explain that drugs had not been a major public issue in Britain when the Stones were growing up.

[事实] By the mid-1960s, cannabis convictions and registered teenage addiction figures were rising, while travel made importing drugs easier.

[事实] Drugs became linked in the media to music, youth culture, San Francisco counterculture, and celebrity influence.

[推测] The Redlands case became huge because it connected anxieties about drugs with broader fears over family, affluence, immigration, and cultural change.

[19:36] **The Stones’ Image Versus Reality**

[事实] The hosts argue that the Stones were not yet especially heavy drug users compared with their later reputation.

[事实] Bill Wyman and Charlie Watts are described as unusually straight by rock standards.

[事实] Jagger joined the Country Gentlemen’s Association, Richards collected books on sea battles, and Watts was loyal to his mother and wife.

[推测] Newspapers preferred the image of the Stones as decadent symbols because it was more dramatic than their quieter, more conventional habits.

[21:03] **Trial, Class, and Punishment**

[事实] Judge Leslie Block also lived in a 16th-century Sussex farmhouse and had served in the Royal Navy during the Second World War.

[事实] Block later said he and fellow magistrates had tried to cut the Stones down to size.

[事实] At trial, Robert Fraser pleaded guilty, Jagger’s defence about doctor-advised amphetamines failed, and Richards’s case focused on drug use at his house.

[事实] Jagger received three months in prison, Fraser six months, and Richards twelve months.

[推测] The hosts suggest Richards’s sentence may have reflected his attitude in court and the symbolic offence of using a respectable English house for countercultural behaviour.

[25:18] **Appeal and Establishment Reaction**

[事实] Jagger and Richards were released on bail pending appeal, while Fraser remained imprisoned because heroin had been found on him.

[事实] The hosts reject the idea that the Wilson government plotted against the Stones, while allowing that newspapers, police, and magistrates were ready to seize an opportunity.

[事实] Establishment newspapers, including *The Times*, criticised the harsh treatment of the Stones.

[事实] William Rees-Mogg’s famous *Times* editorial argued that Jagger should not be punished for public prejudice against the band’s image.

[29:01] **Public Opinion and Aftermath**

[事实] Contemporary surveys cited in the episode found many young people thought Jagger deserved prison.

[事实] Richards’s conviction was overturned on appeal, while Jagger’s conviction stood but his prison sentence was quashed.

[事实] Jagger appeared on a special *World in Action* programme and said he was not a rebel and respected the wartime generation.

[推测] The public split complicates the idea of a simple youth-versus-establishment conflict.

[31:49] **Robert Fraser as a Darker Casualty**

[事实] Robert Fraser’s sentence was not overturned, and he spent four months in Wormwood Scrubs working in the prison kitchens.

[事实] Jagger and Richards wrote affectionate letters to Fraser while he was imprisoned.

[事实] The hosts say Fraser later became a serious heroin addict, his gallery failed, he went to India, and he died of AIDS in 1986.

[推测] Fraser’s life is used as an example of the darker, destructive side of the 1960s.

[33:13] **Brian Jones and Hyde Park**

[事实] The episode introduces Mick Jagger reading Shelley’s *Adonais* at the Hyde Park concert on 5 July 1969 after Brian Jones’s death.

[事实] Hyde Park drew at least a quarter of a million people.

[事实] Brian Jones is identified as the founder of the Rolling Stones, the person who placed the advert and initially helped organise the band.

[推测] The hosts treat Hyde Park’s fame as inseparable from Jones’s death and the band’s transition into a new phase.

[35:14] **Brian Jones’s Marginalisation**

[事实] Jones was described as fragile, demanding, flaky, and difficult to work with.

[事实] He refused to write original songs because he mainly wanted to play blues.

[事实] By 1963, he had been marginalised by Jagger, Richards, and manager Andrew Loog Oldham.

[事实] Other Stones and associates later gave sharply negative assessments of his personality.

[37:17] **Anita Pallenberg and Jones’s Decline**

[事实] Jones’s drinking, pill use, and mistreatment of girlfriends are described as severe.

[事实] He began a destructive relationship with Anita Pallenberg, a multilingual West German model from a wealthy artistic family.

[事实] During a trip to Morocco with Richards and Pallenberg, Richards and Pallenberg began an affair after Jones fell ill in Toulouse.

[事实] Jones returned to Britain devastated, saying they had taken his music, his band, and his love.

[推测] The affair with Pallenberg intensified Jones’s isolation from the band.

[40:08] **Police Raids and Musical Reboot**

[事实] Jones’s flat was repeatedly raided by police in 1967 and 1968.

[事实] The hosts argue courts were often relatively sympathetic to Jones because psychiatrists and therapists warned that prison could destroy him.

[事实] The Stones tried to recover from *Their Satanic Majesties Request* by returning to blues-rooted material on *Beggars Banquet*.

[事实] Brian made little contribution to *Beggars Banquet* and was largely absent during work on *Let It Bleed*.

[43:38] **Jones Is Pushed Out**

[事实] The Stones needed to tour America because of major tax and legal bills.

[事实] Jones’s drug convictions made his entry to the United States doubtful, and the band also believed he could no longer perform reliably.

[事实] On 8 June 1969, Jagger, Richards, and Charlie Watts went to Cotchford Farm to tell Jones he was out of the band.

[事实] The public explanation was “musical differences.”

[45:33] **Brian Jones’s Death**

[事实] On 2 July 1969, Jones spent the evening by his swimming pool with girlfriend Anna Wohlin and builder Frank Thorogood.

[事实] They were drinking vodka and taking amphetamines before swimming around midnight.

[事实] After others left the pool and returned, Jones was found dead, floating in the water.

[事实] The pathologist and coroner concluded that he drowned while affected by drink and drugs.

[46:44] **Murder Theories Around Jones**

[事实] The hosts discuss later suspicions that Jones was murdered, including claims involving Frank Thorogood.

[事实] Anna Wohlin’s book is mentioned as presenting the allegation that Jones was murdered.

[事实] Charlie Watts dismissed the murder theory, saying many people might have wanted to harm Jones but that it did not happen.

[推测] The episode treats the murder claims as intriguing but not proven.

[49:03] **Hyde Park as Memorial and Spectacle**

[事实] The Stones had already planned the Hyde Park concert to introduce Mick Taylor as Jones’s replacement.

[事实] After Jones’s death, Charlie Watts suggested turning the concert into a memorial.

[事实] The crowd was peaceful, the weather was good, and police were described as relaxed.

[事实] Fifty Hells Angels were hired to protect equipment, but there was no major trouble.

[50:28] **Jagger’s Performance and the Concert’s Reception**

[事实] Jagger appeared in makeup and a white frilly outfit designed by Mr Fish.

[事实] He read Shelley nervously and imperfectly before the performance.

[事实] The released butterflies had suffocated in their boxes.

[事实] The hosts say the concert itself was musically poor but succeeded as a public spectacle.

[推测] Hyde Park mattered more as an image of the late 1960s than as a musical triumph.

[53:45] **Tax Trouble and the American Tour**

[事实] The Stones’ adviser Prince Rupert Loewenstein told them they had been badly advised on taxes and needed to leave Britain to avoid financial disaster.

[事实] They went to America in October 1969 for their first tour there since summer 1966.

[事实] The band noticed that America felt darker after riots, Vietnam, and Nixon’s election.

[事实] Audiences screamed less and listened more seriously to the music.

[55:39] **From Pop Group to Rock Band**

[事实] The hosts argue that between 1967 and 1969, rock music became understood as more serious, adult, and album-oriented.

[事实] Musicians such as Clapton, Page, and Beck are cited as examples of guitar virtuosos who wanted listeners to take the music seriously.

[事实] The singles market declined while albums became more important and profitable.

[推测] The distinction between “pop” and “rock” is presented partly as a cultural and commercial construction.

[58:42] **Altamont Is Planned as a Free Concert**

[事实] Because of criticism over high ticket prices, the Stones decided to end their American tour with a free concert near San Francisco.

[事实] The event moved to Altamont Raceway, about 60 miles from San Francisco, after permits proved difficult.

[事实] The Grateful Dead’s management helped organise security by hiring local Hells Angels, who were paid with beer.

[推测] The poor location, weak planning, and volatile security arrangements helped create the conditions for disaster.

[60:10] **Violence at Altamont**

[事实] Around 300,000 people came to Altamont on a grey day, and many became drunk or wasted quickly.

[事实] Hells Angels carried weapons such as billiard cues and motorbike chains.

[事实] The Stones stopped “Sympathy for the Devil” because of fighting near the stage.

[事实] Meredith Hunter, an 18-year-old Black man, was stabbed repeatedly by a Hells Angel after apparently pulling a revolver.

[61:29] **Altamont as the End of the 1960s**

[事实] The Stones finished their set without knowing how serious the violence was, then left by helicopter.

[事实] Meredith Hunter was not the only fatality: two fans were run over by a car, and another drowned in a drainage ditch.

[事实] Rock writer Ralph Gleeson is quoted as contrasting Woodstock with Altamont as the end of a phase of youth culture.

[推测] The hosts argue Altamont became symbolic partly because the Stones’ music had already soundtracked the fading of 1960s optimism.

[63:46] **The Stones’ Legacy**

[事实] After Altamont, the Stones continued into a major run of albums including *Sticky Fingers* and *Exile on Main Street*.

[事实] The hosts argue that the Stones helped define the template of the rock band: sex, drugs, rebellion, darkness, death, and satanic imagery.

[事实] They compare the Stones’ lasting symbolic status with the Beatles’ cultural importance.

[推测] The Stones endure because their music and image captured wider cultural tensions, not only because of their songs.

[64:47] **Jagger’s Later Self-Assessment**

[事实] Mick Jagger said in 1976 that people overestimated the Rolling Stones and that they were not as good as people thought.

[事实] The hosts note that punk was emerging around that time, making the Stones’ former youth-rebellion image more complicated.

[推测] Jagger’s comment may reflect awareness that the band had become partly a parody of the youthful anarchy they once embodied.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value lies in connecting familiar Rolling Stones stories to larger historical questions: class resentment, tabloid culture, drug panic, legal authority, and the changing market for popular music. The strongest sections are those that show how small incidents became symbolic because the Stones already carried a charged public image.

The discussion is especially effective on contradiction: the Stones as rebellious folk devils who also loved country houses, old war films, and establishment markers; the “establishment” as both punitive and protective; and Altamont as both a real tragedy and a mythic cultural marker.

[推测] Its main limitation is that it relies heavily on interpretive framing and biographical anecdote, so listeners wanting a close musical analysis of the albums may find the cultural history more prominent than the music itself.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in 1960s Britain and America, the history of rock culture, celebrity scandal, and the way popular music can become a proxy for broader social change.
