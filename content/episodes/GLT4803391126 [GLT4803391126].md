# The French Revolution: From Hailstorms to the Tennis Court Oath

## 概览

This episode traces how France moved from environmental disaster and financial crisis in 1788 to open constitutional confrontation by June and July 1789. A devastating hailstorm, drought, failed harvests, rising bread prices, and an exceptionally severe winter intensified existing pressure on the monarchy.

The hosts argue that the crisis was not simply a story of an indifferent old regime collapsing overnight. The monarchy and elites did attempt relief and reform, but they were overwhelmed by debt, public expectations, institutional confusion, and the politicizing force of pamphlets, rumors, and spectacle.

The discussion follows the calling of the Estates General, the rise of the Third Estate, the influence of figures such as Necker, Sieyes, Mirabeau, Mounier, Robespierre, and Louis XVI’s inability to impose a clear settlement. It ends with Necker’s dismissal and the approach of 14 July 1789, setting up the fall of the Bastille.

## 分段落总结

[00:44] **The 1788 storm and the crisis of nature**

[事实] The episode opens with Robert Darnton’s description of the hailstorm that hit France on 13 July 1788, destroying crops, vineyards, roofs, animals, and even killing peasants.

[事实] The storm was followed by drought and a hot summer, and farmers wrote to Versailles asking for help.

[事实] Relief efforts did exist: the Archbishop of Paris launched charity work, the king gave one million livres, and a lottery raised 12 million livres.

[推测] The natural disasters mattered because they struck at the worst possible moment, compounding an already severe political and financial crisis.

[04:22] **Necker returns as France’s hoped-for savior**

[事实] After Brienne’s fall on 25 August, the monarchy turned to Jean-Jacques Necker, a Swiss Protestant banker widely seen as capable of restoring financial order.

[事实] Necker believed he could borrow money at better rates and keep the monarchy afloat until the Estates General met.

[事实] His arrival calmed the stock exchange temporarily.

[推测] Necker’s popularity rested partly on his outsider status and reputation for sober financial competence.

[08:00] **Bread prices, rumor, and winter misery**

[事实] Bread was central to survival for much of the French population; a family of four needed two four-pound loaves a day.

[事实] A loaf rose from eight sous to twelve sous by October and later peaked around fifteen sous on 14 July 1789.

[事实] The winter of 1788-1789 was described as exceptionally long and cold, with frozen rivers, blocked roads, and snow across France.

[推测] Scarcity fed conspiracy theories that ministers or grain hoarders were profiting from hunger.

[12:00] **Rural hardship and urban unemployment**

[事实] Many peasants were sharecroppers or smallholders who owed dues to seigneurs and risked eviction when bad weather ruined their ability to pay.

[事实] People moved into cities seeking relief, but urban unemployment was already high.

[事实] About one fifth of Paris’s population was unemployed or receiving relief during the winter of 1788.

[推测] The hosts frame this as the convergence of financial crisis, political crisis, cultural radicalism, and social desperation.

[15:08] **The Estates General and the question of representation**

[事实] Louis XVI had called the Estates General, but there was little institutional memory because it had not met since 1614-1615.

[事实] Reformers wanted the Third Estate’s numbers doubled and wanted all estates to sit together so majority voting could prevail.

[事实] The First Estate was the clergy, the Second Estate the nobility, and the Third Estate everyone else.

[推测] The unresolved voting procedure made the Estates General a constitutional crisis before it even opened.

[16:51] **Sieyes and the Third Estate as the nation**

[事实] Emmanuel Sieyes published What Is the Third Estate? in January 1789.

[事实] Sieyes argued that the Third Estate was “everything,” had been “nothing” in the political order, and demanded to become “something.”

[事实] He claimed the Third Estate formed a complete nation and that nobles and clergy should abandon privileges if they wanted to join it.

[推测] The language of disease used against privilege signaled a menacing radicalization of political rhetoric.

[19:01] **Necker’s compromise and the elections**

[事实] Necker and the king agreed that the Third Estate would have about 600 deputies, compared with about 300 each for clergy and nobility.

[事实] Reforming clergy or nobles could be elected as representatives of the Third Estate.

[事实] Necker did not decide whether the three estates would sit together or separately.

[事实] The king invited grievances, producing about 25,000 cahiers from across France.

[21:24] **Raised expectations and growing disorder**

[事实] The grievance process gave people an unprecedented chance to express complaints about taxes, seigneurial rights, food, prices, and government.

[事实] The hosts say people wanted contradictory things: more freedom, lower prices, less government interference, and more paternal relief.

[事实] By March, food riots were reported from northern France to Avignon and Toulouse.

[推测] The grievance process may have intensified disappointment because no eighteenth-century government could satisfy the expectations it raised.

[24:28] **The Reveillon riot in Paris**

[事实] A rumor spread that wallpaper manufacturer Reveillon had said workers should live on fifteen sous a day.

[事实] Reveillon had actually been a successful and paternalistic employer who paid workers well and supported them during the cold winter.

[事实] On 28 April 1789, thousands attacked and destroyed his house and workshop; guards later fired on the crowd.

[事实] Around 300 people may have been killed, making it the bloodiest day of the Revolution until 1792 according to the episode.

[29:40] **Paris becomes militarized**

[事实] After the Reveillon riot, troops patrolled Paris, including German and Hungarian detachments serving in the French army.

[事实] Foreign troops in Paris unsettled ordinary Parisians and fed rumors of coups or insurrection.

[事实] The Estates General opened at Versailles in early May.

[推测] Military order deepened fear rather than restoring confidence.

[32:15] **Opening ceremony, hierarchy, and bad optics**

[事实] At Versailles, clothing and ceremony became politically significant: nobles wore elaborate dress while the Third Estate was required to wear plain black coats.

[事实] The 4 May procession placed monarchy, clergy, nobility, and the Third Estate in a visible hierarchy.

[事实] Necker had wanted the Estates General in Paris, away from court ritual, but it was held at Versailles.

[推测] The ceremony reinforced precisely the hierarchy that many reformers wanted to challenge.

[36:11] **Mirabeau, Robespierre, and the Third Estate deputies**

[事实] Mirabeau, though a nobleman, was elected for the Third Estate from Aix-en-Provence and became a dramatic champion of the people.

[事实] The Third Estate included Sieyes, Mounier from Grenoble, and a young lawyer from Arras named Maximilien Robespierre.

[事实] Of the 600 Third Estate deputies, 180 were lawyers and 220 were judges or magistrates.

[推测] The Third Estate’s deputies were not mainly poor commoners but articulate, ambitious, upwardly mobile men.

[40:24] **The first session and Necker’s disappointment**

[事实] On 5 May, Louis XVI gave a speech warning against too much talk of innovation.

[事实] A confusing episode over hats revealed the tension between court protocol and the Third Estate.

[事实] Necker delivered a long speech on finance, delegating much of it to another official, and many listeners found it boring and disappointing.

[推测] The session failed to provide the symbolic or practical leadership many had expected.

[42:23] **The verification stalemate and politics as theatre**

[事实] On 6 May, Mirabeau objected to the estates verifying credentials separately because that would concede separate chambers.

[事实] The stalemate continued for weeks, with spectators traveling from Paris to watch debates in Versailles.

[事实] Arthur Young observed that spectators clapped, cheered, and booed as though politics were theatre.

[推测] The Revolution was becoming both a political crisis and a mediated public spectacle.

[47:36] **From ultimatum to National Assembly**

[事实] By late May and early June, reports from the countryside were bleak, and posters in Paris warned of insurrection if nothing changed.

[事实] On 10 June, Sieyes proposed an ultimatum: the other estates should join, or the Third Estate would act alone.

[事实] On 13 June, parish priests began crossing over to the Third Estate.

[事实] On 17 June, the deputies declared themselves the National Assembly.

[50:49] **Sovereignty, grief, and Louis XVI’s indecision**

[事实] The National Assembly declared existing taxation illegal unless authorized by itself, while temporarily allowing taxes to continue.

[事实] Louis XVI’s seven-year-old son died of tuberculosis on 4 June, devastating Louis and Marie Antoinette.

[事实] Necker advised compromise, while Marie Antoinette and Louis’s brothers favored shutting the process down.

[推测] Louis’s personal grief and habitual indecision weakened the monarchy’s response at a decisive moment.

[53:39] **The Tennis Court Oath**

[事实] On 20 June, the Third Estate found its meeting hall closed while workers prepared it for a royal session.

[事实] Dr. Guillotin suggested using a nearby real tennis court owned by a friend.

[事实] In the tennis court, the deputies swore not to separate until they had written a new constitution for France.

[推测] The oath turned exclusion from a meeting hall into a powerful act of revolutionary self-definition.

[57:15] **The royal session and Mirabeau’s defiance**

[事实] On 23 June, the Third Estate was made to wait in the rain and enter through a side door, while clergy and nobility were seated first.

[事实] Louis offered reforms, including representative approval of taxes and regular Estates General meetings, but rejected the National Assembly.

[事实] After Louis left, the Third Estate refused to move, and Mirabeau declared they would leave only at bayonet point.

[事实] Louis declined to enforce his order and said they could stay.

[61:27] **Louis yields, but the crisis continues**

[事实] On 27 June, Louis ordered the clergy and nobility to join the National Assembly.

[事实] Crowds cheered the king and queen at Versailles, while Marie Antoinette appeared visibly grief-stricken and brought out her children.

[事实] Arthur Young thought the revolution seemed complete, while the Marquis de Ferriere warned of bloodshed and disorder.

[推测] The monarchy appeared to have conceded without regaining control.

[63:38] **Troops, radicalization, and Necker’s dismissal**

[事实] Rumors spread that German and Swiss troops serving the French king were moving toward the capital.

[事实] Paris grew more radical, and there were reports of soldiers disobeying officers and crowds attacking suspected police informants.

[事实] Louis’s advisers debated whether to use troops, leave Versailles, or move toward the frontier.

[事实] On the night of 11 July, Necker was dismissed and exiled from France, setting up the events of 14 July 1789.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in showing the French Revolution as an accumulating crisis rather than a sudden explosion. Weather, bread prices, debt, institutional confusion, public opinion, rumor, and theatrical politics all build toward July 1789.

[事实] A strong feature is the use of vivid characters and scenes: Necker as the financial savior, Sieyes as the theorist of the Third Estate, Mirabeau as the dramatic orator, and Louis XVI as a grieving and indecisive monarch.

[推测] The main limitation is that the episode is consciously narrative and character-driven, so listeners wanting a systematic economic or legal breakdown of the Estates General may need supplementary material.

[推测] This episode is especially suitable for listeners who want to understand why the Bastille mattered before the Bastille appears: it explains the pressure, symbolism, and fear already present by 11 July 1789.
