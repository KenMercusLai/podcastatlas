# Nelson: Scandal, Malta, and the Road to Copenhagen

## 概览

This episode follows Horatio Nelson from his controversial return from the Mediterranean to the brink of the Battle of Copenhagen. The central tension is that Nelson remains Britain’s great naval hero after the Nile, but his affair with Emma Hamilton, his treatment of Fanny Nelson, and his uneasy relations with senior officers have begun to tarnish his reputation.

The hosts debate whether Nelson’s time in Palermo was a dereliction of duty or a strategically defensible position tied to Naples, Sicily, and the blockade of Malta. They emphasize that while contemporaries accused him of vanity, distraction, and scandalous conduct, his role in securing Malta and maintaining British naval power was still significant.

The second half widens into European strategy. Britain’s blockade of France is threatened by the League of Armed Neutrality, led by Russia and joined by Denmark, Sweden, and Prussia. Nelson, desperate to escape domestic misery and return to action, pushes the cautious Admiral Hyde Parker toward an attack on Copenhagen.

## 分段落总结

[02:41] **A Hero’s Return With a Scandal Attached**

[事实] The episode opens with a scene from Dominic Sandbrook’s book in which Nelson arrives in London on 8 November 1800 after more than two years away.

[事实] Fanny Nelson and Nelson’s father Edmund are waiting for him, but Nelson arrives with Emma Hamilton, who is pregnant by him, and her husband Sir William Hamilton.

[推测] The hosts use this scene to frame the episode as both naval history and domestic melodrama.

[05:16] **Where the Mediterranean Story Resumes**

[事实] By late summer 1799, the Parthenopean Republic in Naples has been crushed and King Ferdinand and Queen Maria Carolina are back on their thrones.

[事实] Nelson is temporary commander-in-chief in the Mediterranean while waiting for Admiral Lord Keith to return.

[事实] The Admiralty’s major strategic priority is retaking Malta, where a French garrison remains after Napoleon’s expedition to Egypt.

[07:03] **Palermo: Distraction or Strategy**

[事实] Nelson bases himself in Palermo with the Hamiltons, prompting criticism that he has lost sight of his duty and prefers Emma Hamilton’s company to military action.

[事实] Tom argues that Palermo is a reasonable base because Nelson must protect Naples and Sicily while coordinating the blockade of Malta.

[推测] The episode presents Nelson’s conduct as morally messy but not simply militarily negligent.

[10:22] **Contemporary Criticism of Nelson and Emma**

[事实] Lord and Lady Elgin criticize Nelson and the Hamiltons, and Thomas Troubridge warns Nelson about nocturnal parties, gambling, gossip, and Lady Hamilton’s reputation.

[事实] Some of Nelson’s friends and colleagues believe his focus and public dignity have declined.

[推测] The criticism reflects both concern about Nelson’s conduct and broader British snobbery toward Emma, Italy, and the Neapolitan court.

[12:33] **Lord Keith and the Strategic Clash**

[事实] Lord Keith disapproves of Nelson’s relationship with Emma Hamilton and is frustrated by Nelson’s refusal to obey orders cleanly.

[事实] The hosts argue that Keith and Nelson also disagree over strategy: Keith focuses more on Menorca, Spain, and Gibraltar, while Nelson sees Italy and Malta as central.

[推测] Personal scandal makes the conflict more visible, but the deeper disagreement is about command style and naval priorities.

[17:00] **Disobedience and the Malta Blockade**

[事实] In early 1800, Keith orders Nelson toward Menorca, but Nelson ignores him and fights a decisive action connected to the Malta blockade.

[事实] On 18 February, Nelson captures the French ship Les Généreux, one of the ships that had escaped destruction at the Battle of the Nile.

[事实] The hosts describe this as typical Nelson: he disobeys orders, trusts his instincts, and produces results.

[18:35] **Was Nelson Underperforming**

[事实] John Sugden is quoted as highly critical of Nelson during this period, describing him as becoming an impediment rather than an asset.

[事实] Tom counters with Sugden’s own view that no one fought harder for Malta than Nelson.

[事实] Nelson also suffers illness and chest pains, and he resents Keith for not congratulating him after Les Généreux and for redirecting prize money.

[20:24] **Leaving the Mediterranean**

[事实] By mid-1800 Nelson repeatedly asks to go home, and Keith eventually allows him to leave.

[事实] Keith offers a frigate, but Nelson and the Hamiltons instead travel overland through Europe, partly because Emma does not want to travel by sea and wants to accompany Maria Carolina toward Vienna.

[事实] The hosts say this overland return is widely seen as undignified for the victor of the Nile.

[22:40] **Malta Falls to Britain**

[事实] Malta surrenders on 5 September 1800 shortly after Nelson has departed.

[事实] Britain keeps Malta rather than handing it to King Ferdinand.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that Malta becomes a major British strategic possession for more than a century.

[24:33] **Celebrity, Expense, and Ridicule in Europe**

[事实] Nelson and Emma are treated as celebrities as they pass through Europe, including Vienna, where Nelson meets Joseph Haydn and has his portrait painted.

[事实] The trip costs about £3,000, which the hosts compare to about £5 million in modern income terms.

[事实] British aristocrats increasingly mock Emma as vulgar and ridicule Nelson’s taste for public praise.

[28:02] **Return to England and Fanny’s Anxiety**

[事实] Nelson returns by packet boat to Yarmouth and is greeted by huge crowds as a national hero.

[事实] Fanny has become anxious because Nelson’s letters have become fewer, shorter, and colder.

[事实] A confusion over where Nelson and Fanny are meant to meet worsens the emotional awkwardness of his return.

[31:02] **Public Fame and Private Humiliation**

[事实] Nelson is mobbed in London and becomes politically important enough to meet William Pitt and Henry Addington.

[事实] Fanny, Nelson, Emma, and Sir William are repeatedly forced into public social situations together.

[事实] Fanny is seen in tears at dinner parties and faints during a performance at Drury Lane.

[推测] Nelson’s public celebrity makes the private crisis harder for everyone, especially Fanny.

[33:08] **Christmas With the Hamiltons**

[事实] Nelson refuses to spend Christmas with Fanny and his father, choosing instead to go with the Hamiltons to Salisbury and Fonthill.

[事实] William Beckford hosts an extravagant Christmas at Fonthill Abbey.

[事实] Emma, eight months pregnant, performs her “attitudes” during the festivities.

[35:02] **The End of the Marriage**

[事实] On 9 January, after Nelson mentions “dear Lady Hamilton,” Fanny says she is tired of hearing about her and demands that Nelson choose either Emma or herself.

[事实] Nelson replies that he loves Fanny sincerely but cannot forget his obligations to Lady Hamilton or speak of her without affection and admiration.

[推测] The hosts treat this breakfast confrontation as the effective end of Nelson and Fanny’s marriage.

[38:29] **The Baltic Crisis**

[事实] The second half explains that the War of the Second Coalition is breaking apart as Austria and Russia prepare to leave the war against France.

[事实] Britain’s control of the seas and blockade of France become even more important because Britain is increasingly isolated.

[事实] Denmark’s policy of armed neutrality threatens Britain because Danish warships help merchant ships challenge the blockade.

[40:34] **Tsar Paul and Armed Neutrality**

[事实] Tsar Paul I of Russia is described as strange, authoritarian, and hostile to French revolutionary symbols.

[事实] Paul becomes angry when Britain captures and keeps Malta, because he is obsessed with the Knights of St John and Malta’s status.

[事实] In December 1800, Russia, Denmark, Sweden, and Prussia form the League of Armed Neutrality to break Britain’s blockade and trade with France.

[42:56] **Why Britain Sees Denmark as an Existential Problem**

[事实] The hosts argue that Baltic supplies such as timber, hemp, and iron could help France rebuild its navy.

[事实] Andrew Lambert is quoted to the effect that accepting this situation would mean Britain abandoning great power status and accepting French dominance in Europe.

[事实] George III tells Parliament that Britain’s honor, independence, maritime interests, and security are at stake.

[44:17] **Nelson Returns to Naval Service**

[事实] The Admiralty decides to send a fleet into the Baltic with orders to capture or destroy the Danish fleet first.

[事实] Nelson is eager to return to sea, but he is appointed deputy rather than commander.

[推测] The hosts suggest that his scandal-damaged reputation may explain why he is not given sole command.

[46:19] **Horatia and Secret Parenthood**

[事实] Around 29 January 1801, Emma gives birth to Nelson’s daughter, Horatia.

[事实] Nelson and Emma do not publicly acknowledge the child and use coded names in letters.

[事实] Horatia is placed with Mrs Gibson, who acts as her guardian.

[推测] Horatia’s birth strengthens Nelson’s bond with Emma because Fanny had not given him a child and Nelson loved children.

[49:13] **Jealousy and Cruelty**

[事实] Nelson becomes intensely jealous when Emma plans to dine with the Prince of Wales.

[事实] Emma is also jealous of Nelson’s interactions with other women, and Nelson writes dismissive descriptions of women he meets to reassure her.

[事实] Nelson sends Fanny a harsh letter saying he will pay her an allowance but wants no nursing or attention from her.

[事实] The hosts say both Nelson and Emma behave badly toward Fanny.

[53:08] **Fanny’s Long-Term Position**

[事实] Fanny cannot realistically move on with another partner while still Nelson’s abandoned wife.

[事实] The hosts note that Fanny will later receive financial benefits such as Nelson’s pension, while Emma lacks that stability.

[推测] The episode frames Fanny’s story as socially and emotionally tragic even though she is financially more protected than Emma.

[54:13] **Hyde Parker’s Delays**

[事实] On 5 March, Nelson goes to Yarmouth, where Admiral Hyde Parker’s fleet is waiting.

[事实] Parker is 61, cautious, and newly married to a much younger bride.

[事实] The hosts say Parker delays departure with balls and parties while Nelson is impatient for action.

[55:20] **A Distracted Nelson Heads Toward War**

[事实] Even as the fleet sails northeast, Nelson continues obsessing over Emma and the Prince of Wales in his letters.

[事实] The hosts find it remarkable that Nelson can focus enough to perform effectively during the Copenhagen campaign.

[事实] On 14 March, the Admiralty orders the fleet to force the strait, annihilate the Danish fleet and dockyards at Copenhagen, and potentially continue toward Kronstadt.

[57:00] **Approaching Denmark**

[事实] On 19 March, the fleet reaches the northern tip of Jutland and learns that Britain’s negotiators have been expelled from Denmark.

[事实] This news confirms that war with Denmark is now unavoidable.

[事实] By 22 March, the British fleet approaches Helsingør, or Elsinore.

[58:00] **Copenhagen’s Defences**

[事实] Parker hesitates because Copenhagen is heavily defended and difficult to attack.

[事实] The harbor is shallow, the middle ground shoal complicates navigation, and the Danish defenses include major batteries, forts, warships, hulks, and floating gun platforms.

[推测] The hosts present the attack as so dangerous that only someone like Nelson would push for it.

[59:14] **Nelson Forces the Issue**

[事实] Nelson writes Parker a forceful letter arguing that no time should be lost and that England’s honor depends on the fleet’s success.

[事实] Parker agrees to attack but tells Nelson that he must personally lead the assault.

[事实] The episode ends before the Battle of Copenhagen begins, setting up the next installment.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is its balance between intimate biography and grand strategy. It shows Nelson not only as a battlefield genius, but also as vain, emotionally volatile, socially exposed, and capable of real cruelty.

[推测] A major highlight is the argument over Malta. The hosts complicate the easy story that Emma simply distracted Nelson, showing that his Mediterranean record can be read as strategically effective even while his personal conduct damaged his standing.

[推测] The limitation is that the episode ends on a deliberate cliffhanger before Copenhagen itself, so listeners looking for the battle narrative will need the next episode.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in naval history, the Napoleonic Wars, Nelson’s private life, and the way personal scandal can intersect with military reputation and state strategy.
