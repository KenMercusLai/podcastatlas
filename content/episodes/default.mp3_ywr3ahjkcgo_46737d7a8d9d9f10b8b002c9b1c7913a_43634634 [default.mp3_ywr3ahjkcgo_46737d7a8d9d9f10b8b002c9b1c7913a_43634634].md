# Preparing Young People—and Women—for an AI-Driven Future

## 概览

Technovation CEO Tara Chklovski discusses how early, hands-on entrepreneurship education can equip young people to navigate uncertainty, solve meaningful problems, and participate in the AI economy. Drawing on two decades of work, she argues that the program’s real product is not an app but the identity transformation experienced by each student.

The conversation highlights projects created by girls worldwide, including an AI-assisted mine-safety system, a platform preserving grandparents’ stories, and a menstrual-product access tool developed by deaf students in Nigeria. These examples illustrate how lived experience can reveal overlooked problems and lead to locally relevant technology.

Chklovski warns that women may be disproportionately left behind by AI because social norms leave them less time to explore frontier technologies. Her response is active participation: women and young people should learn the tools, build alternatives, and bring their own perspectives into technology rather than remaining solely in the role of critics.

The discussion also covers Technovation’s goal of reaching 25 million young people, its corporate-funded mentorship model, wealth inequality, AI safety, and the future of education. Chklovski expects schools to change slowly, with much educational innovation emerging from nonprofits operating between industry and government.

## 分段落总结

[00:59] **From Aerospace Engineering to Education**

[事实] Tara Chklovski grew up in Meerut, India, in a relatively poor family that nevertheless had access to education.

[事实] She moved to the United States for a master’s degree in aerospace engineering but later left her PhD program to work on broader social problems.

[事实] She identified education as a lever capable of changing life trajectories that are otherwise strongly shaped by birthplace and circumstance.

[事实] She founded a nonprofit 20 years ago to cultivate courage, entrepreneurship, and innovation among people who were not traditionally viewed as innovators.

[03:39] **The Technovation Model and Its Focus on Girls**

[事实] Technovation developed a youth accelerator model that introduces students—especially girls—to the difficult, uncertain process of entrepreneurship.

[事实] Chklovski said longitudinal data collected after roughly 12 to 14 years showed that the program could produce a lasting shift in participants’ identities.

[事实] She focused on girls because women remain underrepresented in positions of power, influence, and impact.

[推测] The model treats entrepreneurial practice as a developmental experience, not merely a route to launching successful products.

[04:59] **Why Women Risk Being Left Behind by AI**

[事实] Chklovski acknowledged that women have gained greater visibility through legal changes and stronger role models.

[事实] She nevertheless argued that women could become the largest group left behind by AI because social norms are changing more slowly than technology.

[事实] She said women often have less time to experiment with frontier technologies, making them later adopters and more likely to fear rather than build with those tools.

[事实] Technovation has a community of approximately 300,000 adult women who have experienced entrepreneurship education.

[06:17] **Sponsor Message: AI-Assisted Meeting Notes**

[事实] The host described Granola as an AI-powered notepad that converts meetings into structured notes, decisions, and action items.

[事实] He said the product reduced his post-meeting note-processing time from several hours to minutes or seconds.

[08:03] **Youth Innovation Rooted in Lived Experience**

[事实] The latest Technovation summit brought finalists to Bengaluru, the first host city outside Silicon Valley, after approximately 33,000 girls participated worldwide.

[事实] A team from a mining town in Kazakhstan built hardware and on-site AI to analyze vibration, humidity, and heat, aiming to distinguish genuine mine-collapse threats from frequent false alarms.

[事实] A young team in India created a Tamil-language, WhatsApp-native application called “Wisdom Tree” to collect grandparents’ stories and connect families across countries.

[事实] Deaf girls from Nigeria developed a system helping girls with disabilities obtain menstrual products, using AI to predict demand and manage supplies across clinics.

[推测] These projects suggest that students’ direct exposure to local problems can produce technology use cases overlooked by distant institutions or mainstream product teams.

[13:00] **The Student—not the App—is the Product**

[事实] Chklovski said winning is not the program’s primary objective because even participants who do not reach the finals can undergo a life-changing experience.

[事实] She reported that roughly 70% of participants enter technology or STEM careers and may increase their earning capacity by approximately three to six times.

[事实] She estimated that Technovation has contributed about $200 million in increased income for participating women.

[事实] Approximately 35% to 40% of alumni start businesses, while many others work in technology companies or assume leadership roles.

[事实] Chklovski summarized the program’s philosophy by saying that the product is not the app but the girl.

[14:41] **Rethinking the Case for Women in Technology**

[事实] Chklovski said recent policy changes were difficult for organizations serving only women and challenged her assumption that everyone agreed about the need for more women in technology.

[事实] She argued that speaking only within ideologically aligned communities prevents the creation of new ideas and solutions.

[事实] In her view, demonstrating women’s collective economic impact may be more persuasive than relying exclusively on equality and justice arguments.

[事实] She encouraged employers to evaluate participants by what they have built—their courage, systems thinking, and ability to handle uncertainty—rather than focusing on gender.

[17:43] **Opening the Program to Boys**

[事实] Technovation is opening its program to boys because Chklovski believes all young people need an entrepreneurial mindset and the ability to work through uncertainty.

[事实] The organization plans to train educators and mentors so that girls are not displaced or overlooked as participation broadens.

[事实] Chklovski argued that schools are not designed to teach the mindset required to solve today’s hardest, open-ended problems.

[推测] Expanding participation represents a shift from a gender-exclusive intervention toward a broader educational model while retaining safeguards for girls.

[18:40] **AI, Entry-Level Work, and Urgent Reskilling**

[事实] Chklovski expressed urgency about reskilling because large companies are hiring fewer young people and some entry-level knowledge-work tasks can already be performed by AI.

[事实] She said young people need to become curious, brave lifelong learners who have experience building real things.

[事实] Technovation began teaching AI in 2016 and has accumulated about a decade of impact data, which Chklovski contrasted with newer AI curricula.

[事实] She wants the program adopted by schools and supported through teacher training so students encounter it before entering the workforce.

[事实] Her advice to young people is to become problem solvers, cultivate self-awareness, learn independently, and surround themselves with strong role models.

[21:45] **Why the Goal Is 25 Million Learners**

[事实] Chklovski began modeling what it would take to offer a Technovation-like experience to the world’s approximately 800 million teenagers.

[事实] She concluded that reaching 25 million young people could create a critical mass and network effects within individual countries.

[事实] Technovation is pursuing this goal through initiatives including a UNICEF partnership and the AI Forward Alliance.

[事实] She acknowledged that the organization remains far from its target but argued that ambitious goals can accelerate once partnerships and technology align.

[推测] The target is intended as a systems-change threshold rather than a claim that Technovation alone can directly serve every teenager.

[24:27] **Corporate Funding and the Mentorship System**

[事实] Approximately 90% of Technovation’s funding comes from corporations that see value in developing the future workforce.

[事实] Corporate employees can volunteer as mentors without a technical background, typically committing about two hours per week for three months.

[事实] Mentors are matched with students according to factors such as time zone, language, and interests.

[事实] The curriculum draws on Eric Ries’s lean startup methodology and teaches problem identification, user interviews, rapid testing, iteration, marketing, and product-market fit.

[事实] About 33,000 students participated in the discussed season, and their projects followed a three-month schedule with a firm April deadline.

[27:30] **Young People as Early Signals of Emerging Problems**

[事实] The host proposed creating a series that would share stories from Technovation participants, similar to his planned coverage of “AI for Good” projects.

[事实] Chklovski recalled that teenagers proposed an app-blocking tool in 2013 because they already saw smartphones as addictive and harmful to concentration.

[事实] She also cited teams that independently created concepts resembling Uber and Pinterest around 2010.

[事实] Technovation plans to use AI to analyze its large body of student projects and identify patterns in the problems young people are noticing.

[事实] Mental health and alternatives to conventional social media are recurring areas of student interest.

[30:42] **AI Safety Through Participation and Building**

[事实] Chklovski expressed discomfort with framing AI safety solely from the position of a critic.

[事实] She argued that people concerned about unsafe technology should participate in building better alternatives and bring their distinct perspectives into product development.

[事实] She still considers AI risks real, follows alignment issues closely, and has changed how she grants access to certain tools or systems.

[事实] She advised innovators to understand both the capabilities and dangers of rapidly changing AI tools rather than refusing to use them.

[推测] Her position combines caution with agency: safety concerns should inform construction and experimentation, not lead only to withdrawal.

[32:29] **Avoiding Ideological Tribes and Big-Tech Fatalism**

[事实] Chklovski said binary camps around AI, sustainability, and diversity can feel safe but do not serve the wider public well.

[事实] She encouraged people to diversify their information sources and remain open to views outside their own communities.

[事实] She argued that major technology companies cannot solve every important problem and that many opportunities remain for smaller organizations and entrepreneurs.

[事实] She cited a figure from an Andreessen Horowitz podcast claiming that only about 4.5% of people pay for AI, using it to illustrate the gap between Silicon Valley discourse and worldwide adoption.

[推测] The discussion challenges the perception that a handful of AI companies have already determined the technology’s entire future.

[35:13] **Human Suffering, Inequality, and the Limits of Abundance Narratives**

[事实] Chklovski identified reducing human suffering as a major unresolved challenge, citing billions without healthcare and hundreds of millions living in extreme poverty.

[事实] She rejected the assumption that AI-driven abundance will automatically eliminate deprivation.

[事实] She argued that sufficient wealth already exists, but people in power are often unwilling to share wealth and influence.

[事实] The host cited a comparison claiming that what Mukesh Ambani earns in one hour would take an average Indian 431 years to earn.

[推测] The exchange frames poverty less as a problem of technological scarcity than as one of distribution, empathy, and political power.

[37:12] **Training Courage and Expanding Participation**

[事实] Chklovski said word of mouth may be more important than money for reaching 25 million people because technology can reduce delivery costs.

[事实] She observed that people often choose low-effort entertainment over demanding opportunities for learning and self-improvement.

[事实] She argued that courage can be trained through teamwork, mentorship, personal values, and building something that helps another person.

[事实] Technovation recruits roughly 18,000 to 20,000 mentors each year and continues to seek additional mentors and students.

[事实] She invited listeners to mentor participants and spread awareness of the program.

[39:32] **Why Schools May Change Slowly**

[事实] Chklovski described three functions of schools: childcare, civic or governmental socialization, and preparation for the future.

[事实] She argued that the first two functions create institutional inertia because schools retain social value even when they do not adequately prepare students for future work.

[事实] She expects learning formats to change through tools such as YouTube and AI chatbots, while traditional schools themselves may not look dramatically different.

[事实] She said learners still need systems that motivate them to pursue difficult goals rather than defaulting to familiar or easy behavior.

[事实] Chklovski expects nonprofits operating between industry and government to play a major role in educational innovation, as they did in the coding-education movement.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest contribution is its connection between AI readiness and lived, project-based experience. The student examples make the argument concrete: young people can use advanced technology to address mine safety, disability access, family memory, and other needs they understand firsthand.

Chklovski also offers a distinctive perspective on inclusion. Rather than presenting women only as people requiring protection from technological disruption, she emphasizes their role as builders, leaders, and economic actors who should help determine how AI develops.

[推测] Some of the numerical claims are presented conversationally without sources or methodological detail in the transcript, so listeners should treat them as discussion points rather than independently verified findings. The interview also moves quickly across education, inequality, AI safety, and public policy, leaving several arguments only partially developed.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for educators, nonprofit leaders, corporate mentors, parents, and people interested in practical AI education. Its central message is clear: preparation for an uncertain future depends less on memorizing fixed answers than on developing courage, judgment, adaptability, and the experience of building solutions for real people.
