# Running Hot

## 概览

This episode of *Revisionist History* questions one of emergency response’s most familiar features: the routine use of lights and sirens. Starting with the relentless sirens outside producer Ben Naddaff-Hafrey’s Brooklyn apartment, it asks whether their extreme noise and associated risks are justified by meaningful improvements in patient outcomes.

Research presented in the episode shows that lights-and-sirens driving substantially increases crash risk while usually saving only seconds or a few minutes. Those minutes matter in a narrow set of cases—such as cardiac arrest, choking, or respiratory arrest—but most emergency calls do not lead to potentially lifesaving interventions.

The episode traces today’s “everything is urgent” mindset partly to the 1970s television series *Emergency!*, which helped popularize paramedicine while depicting virtually every call as time-critical. It concludes that sirens remain valuable in genuine emergencies but are used far more often than the evidence supports.

## 分段落总结

[00:11] **Sirens as a “spandrel”**

[事实] Malcolm Gladwell introduces a spandrel as something that persists alongside functional features even though it has no necessary function of its own.

[事实] The episode investigates whether the routine use of emergency sirens is a similarly unquestioned feature of modern life.

[推测] The framing suggests that sirens may once have fit emergency services better than they fit today’s more developed medical-response system.

[02:07] **A Brooklyn neighborhood saturated with sirens**

[事实] Ben and his wife Julia counted 24 sirens between 9 a.m. and 10 p.m. on one day at their apartment near a busy fire station.

[事实] Their count excluded nighttime sirens, including occasions when emergency vehicles appeared to use them despite nearly empty roads.

[事实] Sirens can produce between 110 and more than 130 decibels; the Federal Q2B fire siren reaches 123 decibels at 100 feet.

[事实] A neighborhood dog regularly howls when the sirens sound, becoming part of Ben’s attempt to document the problem.

[06:57] **A two-part campaign against unnecessary noise**

[事实] Ben’s first plan is to establish that local sirens are dangerously and unnecessarily loud; his second is to find the howling dog as a sympathetic symbol.

[事实] Environmental psychologist Arline Bronzaft explains that noise can impede learning and is linked to mental-health effects, stress, poor sleep, and increased heart-disease risk.

[事实] Bronzaft notes that European sirens are less intrusive and asks for evidence that quieter sirens cause more deaths.

[推测] Noise evidence alone may be insufficient to change policy because sirens are culturally associated with safety and lifesaving speed.

[11:28] **Berrien County challenges routine lights and sirens**

[事实] Jonathan Bayer, an EMS medical director in Berrien County, Michigan, argues that lights and sirens have little demonstrated benefit and substantial documented risk.

[事实] The county’s call-classification system labeled about half of its cases as priority one, sometimes elevating complaints such as dental pain because of overly broad screening questions.

[事实] A cited 2019 peer-reviewed study found that using lights and sirens increased crash risk by more than 50 percent, with an even higher risk during patient transport.

[事实] Studies discussed in the episode found average time savings ranging from 42 seconds to 3 minutes and 48 seconds—about 1.5 minutes in cities and slightly over 3.5 minutes in rural areas.

[15:53] **Treating sirens like a high-risk medical therapy**

[事实] Bayer identifies cardiac arrest as a case where every minute can matter, because each minute without CPR is associated with roughly a 10 percent increase in mortality and reduced survivable brain function.

[事实] He asks crews to treat lights and sirens like a medical therapy: use them only when the expected benefit outweighs the risk.

[事实] Berrien County retained priority-one responses for conditions such as cardiac arrest, choking, and respiratory arrest while downgrading cases where a few minutes were unlikely to change outcomes.

[事实] The revised coding eliminated about half of the county’s lights-and-sirens responses.

[17:56] **Reduced siren use without worse triage outcomes**

[事实] Berrien County compared ambulance classifications with the priorities hospitals assigned when patients arrived.

[事实] The rate at which a patient initially treated as lower priority was later classified as priority one did not increase under the new system.

[事实] Paramedics supported the change because they already believed many of their calls were not genuinely time-critical.

[推测] The county’s results indicate that carefully designed dispatch criteria can reduce risky driving without depriving patients of appropriate care.

[19:03] **Public backlash and the expectation of urgency**

[事实] Residents and local media initially described the reform as slower ambulance service, prompting accusations that the hospital was endangering patients.

[事实] Bayer defended the policy with research showing that lights and sirens offer small time savings while significantly increasing crash risk.

[事实] After a press conference explaining the evidence, complaints disappeared within six weeks.

[事实] Berrien County subsequently had Michigan’s lowest EMS rate of lights-and-sirens use.

[推测] The backlash reveals that people often interpret visible speed and noise as proof of adequate care, even when outcome data does not support that assumption.

[24:01] **How television shaped modern emergency response**

[事实] The NBC series *Emergency!*, launched in 1972, dramatized paramedics at a time when the United States had only 12 paramedic units.

[事实] The program was developed with James Page, described as a father of modern EMS, and was explicitly intended to build support for paramedicine.

[事实] It reached roughly 30 million viewers, influenced future paramedics and lawmakers, and coincided with 46 states legalizing paramedicine during its early years.

[事实] The show depicted nearly every call as life-threatening and used lights and sirens for every response, reinforcing the idea that emergency care is always a race against time.

[推测] A valuable campaign for the creation of paramedic services also established cultural expectations that persisted after emergency medicine became more capable and widely available.

[31:01] **A fire-service model applied beyond fires**

[事实] Early EMS units were commonly embedded in fire departments, whose operating assumptions reflected the genuine urgency of fires that can spread if not contained.

[事实] By 2023, fewer than 4 percent of the calls firefighters answered were actually fires; most involved EMS or rescue.

[事实] The episode argues that emergency-service practices have not fully adapted to this change in mission.

[推测] Lights-and-sirens routines may be an institutional inheritance from firefighting rather than a response calibrated to the medical needs of most present-day calls.

[32:16] **National data reveals extensive overuse**

[事实] Jeff Jarvis analyzed 7.5 million emergency records, including 5.9 million non-routine responses, from the national ESO dataset.

[事实] Lights and sirens were used en route to approximately 86 percent of calls, yet only 6.9 percent resulted in an intervention classified—even generously—as potentially lifesaving.

[事实] Dispatch systems use scripted questions and graded determinants, from highly life-threatening “echo” calls to low-risk “omega” calls; evaluations find these systems imperfect but reasonably accurate.

[事实] Only 0.67 percent of “eye problem” calls led to a potentially lifesaving intervention, although all such calls in the analyzed data received lights-and-sirens responses.

[推测] These figures support the episode’s estimate that lights and sirens may be used 80 to 90 percent more often than necessary in the United States.

[35:14] **An emergency system that has outgrown its origin story**

[事实] Since the era depicted by *Emergency!*, the United States has gained more paramedics, doctors, emergency rooms, improved emergency medicine, and better 911 call assessment.

[事实] Emergency-response television continues to rely on a narrative in which help is distant, every case is urgent, and responders arrive with lights and sirens.

[事实] Jarvis describes “alarm fatigue”: when everything is treated as an alarm, people become desensitized and alarms lose effectiveness.

[推测] The symbolic image of emergency response has remained fixed in the 1970s even as the actual system’s capacity and needs have changed.

[37:04] **Safer responses with minimal delay**

[事实] Jarvis reduced lights-and-sirens use by about one-third, producing a median response-time increase of only six seconds.

[事实] He and Ben hypothesize that reserving sirens for genuine emergencies could make the public react more strongly when they do sound, though they acknowledge that this requires testing.

[事实] The episode links overuse to collisions, community noise, responder burnout, and hearing damage.

[事实] In 2015, 1,500 firefighters sued Federal Signal, alleging that its sirens caused widespread hearing loss.

[推测] Selective use could improve both safety and the warning value of sirens, but the latter benefit remains an unproven hypothesis.

[39:15] **The howling dog complicates the campaign**

[事实] Ben eventually identifies the dog as Davy, a yellow Labrador living one street away.

[事实] Davy’s owners report that he does not appear distressed by sirens; his tail often wags, and he has encouraged other dogs to howl with him.

[事实] One theory offered is that dogs interpret a siren as a separated animal calling to its pack and howl in response.

[事实] Ben concludes that Davy cannot serve as the suffering mascot of his anti-siren campaign because the dog appears to enjoy them.

[推测] Davy’s instinctive howl mirrors the episode’s central theme: a behavior can persist because it is inherited or customary, not because it remains well suited to present circumstances.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest contribution is its reframing of lights and sirens as a high-risk intervention rather than an automatic part of emergency response. The combination of national data, a county-level policy change, and an operational case study makes the argument more concrete than a discussion focused only on noise pollution.

Its historical analysis is also valuable. By connecting emergency-response habits to firefighting traditions and *Emergency!*, the episode shows how media and institutional history can turn a contingent practice into something the public regards as indispensable.

[推测] The episode is persuasive about overuse but less complete on implementation details, such as the full range of triage errors, regional differences, and how revised dispatch standards should be governed. Its causal account of television’s influence is illuminating, though cultural expectations likely have additional sources.

This episode will be especially useful to EMS professionals, dispatch-system designers, public-health policymakers, urban-noise advocates, and listeners interested in how familiar institutions persist after their original circumstances have changed.
