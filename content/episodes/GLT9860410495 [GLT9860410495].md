# Chicago 1968: Protest, Police, and the Democratic Convention

## 概览

This episode follows the Democratic National Convention in Chicago in August 1968, placing it within the wider crisis of that year: Lyndon Johnson’s withdrawal, Robert Kennedy’s assassination, the Vietnam War, anti-war protest, racial unrest, and the Democratic Party’s internal collapse.

The hosts contrast the countercultural theatrics of the Yippies with the hard-edged authority of Mayor Richard J. Daley and the Chicago police. A central theme is how language intended as playful provocation by activists was interpreted by city authorities as a real threat, helping produce an atmosphere primed for violence.

The episode’s conclusion is that the convention became a political disaster for the Democrats. Hubert Humphrey won the nomination, but television images of police violence, party disunity, tear gas, and street chaos helped confirm public impressions that the Democrats were the party of disorder, while many Americans still sided with the police over the protesters.

## 分段落总结

[00:14] **The Yippie Manifesto and the Coming Clash**

[事实] The episode opens with the Yippies’ 1968 manifesto calling people to Chicago to confront the Democratic convention, described as the “National Death Party.”
[事实] The hosts frame the convention as one of the most dramatic weeks in modern American political history, involving riot police, anti-war protesters, and Democratic leaders.
[事实] The Yippies are presented as practicing “the politics of play,” not merely conventional protest.
[推测] The opening suggests that spectacle, provocation, and media visibility will be as important to the story as formal party politics.

[04:05] **The Democratic Race After Johnson and Kennedy**

[事实] Lyndon Johnson withdrew from the presidential race on March 31, leaving Eugene McCarthy, Hubert Humphrey, and Robert Kennedy as key Democratic contenders.
[事实] Kennedy narrowly won California on June 4 and was assassinated in his moment of victory.
[事实] McCarthy did not inherit the anti-war mantle cleanly; Kennedy supporters remained bitter, and McCarthy seemed exhausted and disengaged afterward.
[事实] Humphrey became the likely nominee by midsummer, despite being compromised by his loyalty to Johnson’s Vietnam policy.
[推测] Humphrey’s personal decency and liberal record were politically overshadowed by his association with the war.

[07:35] **Chicago and Mayor Richard J. Daley**

[事实] Chicago is described as the second-largest city in the United States at the time, a powerful Democratic machine city, and highly segregated in practice.
[事实] Mayor Richard J. Daley controlled Chicago politics and was closely aligned with Lyndon Johnson.
[事实] Daley wanted the convention in Chicago for prestige and economic benefit, and he hoped it might even help draft Johnson back into the race.
[事实] Daley and Johnson are portrayed as temperamentally similar, hawkish, forceful political figures.
[推测] Daley’s need to project order in his own city made compromise with protesters especially unlikely.

[11:38] **The MOBE and Anti-War Mobilization**

[事实] The National Mobilization Committee to End the War in Vietnam, known as the MOBE, was the leading peace group planning protests in Chicago.
[事实] Dave Dellinger is described as a pacifist figure rooted in older Protestant, Quaker, and conscientious-objector traditions.
[事实] The MOBE aimed to bring 100,000 people to Chicago to “confront the war makers.”
[事实] The protesters focused on the Democratic convention rather than the Republican convention because Democrats held power and were supposed to be closer to their side.
[推测] The episode draws a parallel between anti-war protests against Democrats in 1968 and later protests aimed at Democratic leaders over Gaza.

[13:34] **Abbie Hoffman, Jerry Rubin, and Yippie Theatre**

[事实] The Yippies were led by figures including Abbie Hoffman and Jerry Rubin, both activists from Jewish backgrounds with university and movement experience.
[事实] Hoffman is presented as someone who understood television and public spectacle, believing modern revolutionaries should head for the TV studio rather than the factory.
[事实] The Yippie style involved happenings, sit-ins, psychedelic imagery, hippie dress, and theatrical acts such as trying to levitate the Pentagon.
[事实] Hoffman leaned toward playful counterculture, while Rubin was more confrontational and spoke openly about provoking repression.
[推测] The hosts suggest the Yippies underestimated how literally hostile authorities might take their language.

[19:16] **Planning Breakdowns and Warnings From Chicago**

[事实] After Johnson withdrew and Kennedy was assassinated, the Yippies revived their plan for a Chicago festival and protest.
[事实] They needed permits to sleep in parks, use stages, and hold demonstrations, but Chicago authorities delayed or refused cooperation.
[事实] Chicago-based activists warned that the organization was chaotic and that police violence was likely.
[事实] Hoffman and Rubin dismissed these local warnings, creating tension between New York and Chicago activists.
[事实] Attempts to contact the Blackstone Rangers alarmed Chicago police, even though the gang showed no real interest in the Yippies.
[推测] Local organizers appear to have understood the danger more clearly than the national countercultural figures.

[22:33] **Threats, Demands, and Police Preparations**

[事实] By late August, the Chicago Underground warned people not to come, saying the police might riot and Chicago could become a “festival of blood.”
[事实] Hoffman announced Yippie demands including legalizing marijuana, abolishing money, ending imperialism, and “make love not war.”
[事实] The MOBE worried that the Yippies were irresponsible and narcissistic, while its own slogan “confront the war makers” also implied possible confrontation.
[事实] The Chicago police were issued riot helmets, tear gas, shotguns, and other equipment in anticipation of serious disorder.
[推测] Both sides contributed to an atmosphere in which performance, fear, and preparation for violence reinforced one another.

[24:33] **The Chicago Police Perspective**

[事实] The hosts discuss David Farber’s work for giving attention to the Chicago police’s point of view.
[事实] The police force had been modernized, and one in four Chicago officers were Black, a detail the hosts say is often left out of the usual image.
[事实] Police morale was low amid rising crime, perceived lack of support, and pay that lagged behind other working-class jobs.
[事实] Many officers resented privileged college protesters who seemed to disrespect authority and support causes they did not understand.
[事实] After riots following Martin Luther King’s assassination, Daley criticized police weakness and advocated shooting arsonists to kill and looters to maim.
[推测] The police response cannot be explained only as simple brutality; it also reflected class resentment, fear, and political pressure from Daley.

[30:32] **The Geography and First Yippie Actions**

[事实] The convention was held at the amphitheater near the stockyards, south of downtown, while hotels and campaign headquarters were around Michigan Avenue and the Loop.
[事实] The amphitheater was heavily secured with barbed wire, roadblocks, armed police, and a no-fly zone.
[事实] The Yippies gathered in Lincoln Park, where only a few hundred people appeared at first, far fewer than expected.
[事实] Yippie flyers called for people to “break in” to Democratic hotels, which they saw as humorous but police could read as threatening.
[事实] The Yippies staged a mock presidential nomination for a pig called Pigasus, and Jerry Rubin was arrested after giving a nomination speech.
[推测] The Yippies’ jokes worked inside their own culture but often backfired when seen by police, officials, or mainstream viewers.

[35:17] **Sunday Night in Lincoln Park**

[事实] On Saturday, August 24, a confrontation over clearing Lincoln Park by 11 p.m. was defused when Allen Ginsberg chanted and led people out.
[事实] On Sunday, August 25, the MOBE marched to the Hilton Hotel, while the Yippies’ planned “Festival of Life” drew only three or four thousand people instead of 100,000.
[事实] Only one band, MC5, turned up, and disputes over electricity and staging increased tension.
[事实] Around midnight, police discipline broke and officers began clubbing people, using mace and tear gas, attacking reporters, photographers, and medical volunteers.
[事实] Jerry Rubin reacted by saying the violence was “fantastic” because it matched his desire for escalation.
[推测] The first major violence validated the fears of local organizers and transformed the convention week into a repeated cycle of confrontation.

[40:25] **The Convention Opens Amid Violence**

[事实] The Democratic convention opened on Monday, with Daley still hoping Johnson might be nominated and preparing “We love LBJ” signs.
[事实] Daley told the convention he would not tolerate people who sought to destroy institutions and values.
[事实] Fighting resumed in Lincoln Park that evening, with police wearing gas masks, using gas and smoke grenades, removing badges, and beating demonstrators.
[事实] The hosts say women were especially targeted, possibly because they were less likely to fight back and because of sexual aggression.
[事实] Police also smashed cameras and damaged cars with McCarthy bumper stickers.
[推测] Once police began removing badges, the normal boundaries of accountability appeared to collapse.

[42:04] **Vietnam, Bobby Seale, and Escalating Fear**

[事实] On Tuesday, August 27, Johnson celebrated his birthday in Texas while the convention argued over Vietnam policy and party procedures.
[事实] News organizations complained to Daley that reporters were being beaten, but this made him more determined to maintain a hard line.
[事实] Bobby Seale of the Black Panthers addressed activists in Lincoln Park and used rhetoric about armed self-defense against police.
[事实] The police also deployed a tear-gas machine powered by a garbage truck.
[事实] Tuesday was quieter partly because many people were tired and injured from previous nights.
[推测] Seale’s rhetoric may have been performative, but in the tense context of 1968 it plausibly intensified police fears.

[44:54] **Wednesday’s Grant Park Rally**

[事实] On Wednesday morning, police arrested Abbie Hoffman at breakfast and charged him with disorderly conduct over an obscene word on his forehead.
[事实] The MOBE leaders were not arrested and held a rally in Grant Park with about 15,000 people.
[事实] The crowd included Yippies, MOBE peace campaigners, pacifists, and McCarthy volunteers.
[事实] Violence escalated after a teenage boy climbed a flagpole and began lowering the American flag, prompting police to beat him and the crowd to intervene.
[事实] Police beat MOBE organizer Rennie Davis severely, and Tom Hayden then gave a speech urging protesters to take the disruption into the streets.
[推测] The flag incident mattered because it symbolized, for police and many Americans, the protesters’ perceived unpatriotism.

[47:21] **The Crowd Is Trapped and Tear-Gassed**

[事实] Around 6,000 people were blocked in Grant Park, with many bridges guarded by National Guardsmen with machine guns.
[事实] Police moved into the trapped crowd with clubs and tear gas, creating panic, vomiting, crying, and temporary blinding.
[事实] Tear gas drifted west toward the Democratic hotels on Michigan Avenue.
[事实] The crowd eventually found an unguarded bridge at Jackson Drive and crossed toward Michigan Avenue.
[事实] Ralph Abernathy’s mule train from the Poor People’s Campaign appeared in the middle of the chaos, symbolizing poverty in America.
[推测] The collision of anti-war protest, civil-rights symbolism, police force, and party politics made the scene unusually surreal and politically damaging.

[50:27] **The Michigan Avenue Police Riot**

[事实] At about 8 p.m., with demonstrators surrounded near Balbo Drive and Michigan Avenue under the Hilton Hotel, police charged into the crowd.
[事实] Officers used clubs, fists, and mace; some protesters fought back with sticks and rocks.
[事实] Reinforcing police were seen chanting “kill, kill, kill” as they entered the confrontation.
[事实] Demonstrators were pushed through glass windows, and police continued attacking amid shattered glass.
[事实] The violence unfolded under television lights and in front of cameras already present for the convention.
[推测] The location made the violence impossible to contain politically because delegates, journalists, and national viewers could all witness it.

[52:20] **Candidates Watch the Violence**

[事实] Norman Mailer watched the police violence from his hotel and described the attack in vivid literary terms.
[事实] Eugene McCarthy watched from the 23rd floor and compared the scene first to Bruegel and then to battles from the Second Punic War.
[事实] McCarthy’s campaign staff saw his historical analogies as evidence of detachment, though he later visited wounded protesters.
[事实] Humphrey was affected by tear gas entering through the air conditioning and had to wash it out in the shower.
[推测] The contrast between McCarthy’s aloof observation and Humphrey’s physical exposure to tear gas captures the strange mixture of tragedy and grotesque comedy in the episode.

[54:39] **Humphrey Wins as the Convention Implodes**

[事实] The nomination battle came down to Hubert Humphrey, Eugene McCarthy, and George McGovern, who represented Kennedy delegates.
[事实] Television footage of police beating demonstrators appeared around 9:30 p.m., shocking delegates.
[事实] Senator Abraham Ribicoff, while nominating McGovern, said that under McGovern there would not be “Gestapo tactics” in Chicago’s streets.
[事实] Mayor Daley reacted furiously and shouted abuse at Ribicoff from the convention floor.
[事实] Humphrey won on the first ballot by a large margin.
[推测] Humphrey’s formal victory was immediately overshadowed by the images and emotions of violence.

[56:48] **Humphrey’s Failed Moment and TV Framing**

[事实] After winning, Humphrey kissed the television image of his wife while photographers recorded the moment.
[事实] The hosts describe the gesture as sweet but badly out of step with the police violence happening below.
[事实] NBC later intercut footage of Humphrey supporters celebrating with images of protesters being beaten and gassed, even though the scenes were not simultaneous.
[事实] Daley told Walter Cronkite that the police had been fighting terrorists and claimed there was intelligence about assassination plots, which the hosts say was not true.
[推测] Television editing intensified the political damage by making Democratic celebration and police violence appear morally linked.

[59:02] **Public Reaction and the Limits of Countercultural Protest**

[事实] McCarthy later spoke to demonstrators in the park, called them “the government in exile,” and read them a poem by Robert Lowell.
[事实] Humphrey’s acceptance speech began with sadness over the violence and included a prayer-like appeal for harmony.
[事实] The convention confirmed suspicions that Democrats were chaotic, disunited, and associated with disorder.
[事实] Public opinion largely sided with the police: Daley received many more supportive letters than critical ones, and polls showed few Americans thought police were too harsh.
[事实] The hosts argue that many Americans saw long-haired protesters waving Viet Cong flags or lowering American flags as unpatriotic.
[推测] The episode treats Chicago 1968 as a warning that protest movements must think carefully about how they appear to the broader public.

[64:18] **The Chicago Seven and the Road to Nixon**

[事实] A grand jury charged seven people with using interstate commerce with intent to incite a riot; they became known as the Chicago Seven.
[事实] The defendants included Abbie Hoffman, Jerry Rubin, Bobby Seale, Dave Dellinger, Tom Hayden, and Rennie Davis, though Seale’s case was later declared a mistrial.
[事实] Their 1969 trial became a political showpiece; they were acquitted of conspiracy but convicted of other offenses, which were later reversed on appeal.
[事实] Humphrey left Chicago in despair, trapped by loyalty to Johnson and the Vietnam War.
[事实] The episode ends by pointing toward Richard Nixon as the great political beneficiary of the 1968 crisis.
[推测] The convention’s disorder helped create the conditions for Nixon’s law-and-order appeal.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest value is its refusal to reduce Chicago 1968 to a simple morality play. It presents the brutality of the police clearly, but also spends time explaining why officers, Daley, and many ordinary Americans interpreted the protesters as dangerous, privileged, unpatriotic, or contemptuous.

A major highlight is the attention to political theatre: Yippie jokes, mock nominations, television lighting, Norman Mailer’s literary descriptions, Humphrey’s awkward sentimentality, and Ribicoff’s confrontation with Daley all become part of the historical argument. The episode shows how media spectacle shaped both protest strategy and political fallout.

Its limitation is that the discussion is driven mainly by political actors, public images, and elite commentary; the voices of ordinary demonstrators, Chicago residents, and injured people are less directly present in the transcript. [推测] Listeners looking for a deeper social-history account of Black Chicago, rank-and-file activists, or police organization may need supplementary material.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in 1960s American politics, protest strategy, media history, and the roots of modern “law and order” politics. It is also useful for understanding how a party can win a nomination fight while losing the larger public narrative.
