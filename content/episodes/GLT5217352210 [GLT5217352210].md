# The Invention of Modern Holidays: From Battlefields to Package Tours

## 概览

This episode traces how modern tourism changed across the 20th century, beginning with First World War travel and moving through interwar walking clubs, camping, sunbathing, holiday camps, postwar seaside resorts, package flights, and the rise of Mediterranean mass tourism.

A central theme is continuity: holidays keep being sold not only as fun, but as morally, socially, or physically improving. The hosts connect battlefield pilgrimages, cooperative travel clubs, Butlins, and package tours to older traditions of guided travel, class performance, health, and self-improvement.

The episode also argues that the major rupture comes later, with deregulated air travel and the internet. Before that, tourism remains shaped by middlemen: travel clubs, agents, reps, brochures, camps, and organised group experiences.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Tourism During and After the First World War**

[事实] Thomas Cook evacuated 6,000 tourists stranded in Europe in 1914, but still sent groups to unaffected parts of France, especially the Côte d'Azur.
[事实] The hosts discuss how, after the First World War, some people travelled to cemeteries and battlefields to pay respects, while others visited battle sites in a more voyeuristic way.
[推测] The episode frames battlefield tourism as morally ambiguous because remembrance, curiosity, and spectacle could overlap.

[03:40] **Interwar Travel Clubs and High-Minded Holidays**

[事实] In the 1920s, many British workers still lacked statutory holidays, but some people travelled through cooperatives, trade unions, and organised travel clubs.
[事实] The Polytechnic Touring Association, later part of Lunn Poly, organised earnest group outings connected to education and self-improvement.
[事实] The hosts describe groups such as the Holiday Fellowship and Workers Travel Association as associated with folk songs, literary discussion, walking tours, and moral seriousness.
[推测] These holidays carried forward the Victorian idea that travel should improve character, not simply provide leisure.

[06:20] **Camping, Radicalism, and Back-to-Nature Culture**

[事实] Camping is presented as part of a nonconformist, politically radical, cooperative, back-to-nature world.
[事实] The hosts link early camping culture with fresh air, walking, sandals, folk songs, philanthropy, and utopian conversation.
[推测] Camping is treated less as a simple outdoor pastime than as a cultural statement about health, simplicity, and social ideals.

[08:26] **Sunbathing and the New Cult of the Sun**

[事实] Sunbathing is described as a new fashion by the early 20th century, after long periods in which paleness had been valued as an elite marker.
[事实] The episode cites Norman Douglas and D.H. Lawrence to show how sun, nudity, paganism, and physical liberation became linked in interwar culture.
[事实] Eugène Schueller of L'Oreal created Ombre Solaire in 1935, described as the first sun protection cream.
[事实] The link between sunburn and skin cancer was not discovered until 1956.
[推测] The shift toward tanning suggests a broader change in class signals: leisure, travel, and exposed skin became signs of modernity and status.

[12:10] **Mallorca and the Interwar Mediterranean**

[事实] Mallorca already attracted significant tourism before the Second World War: 20,000 visitors a year in 1930, rising by 1935 to 40,000 hotel guests and 50,000 cruise visitors annually.
[事实] Robert Graves is mentioned as a famous figure associated with Mallorca, while Patrick Leigh Fermor and the Durrells are linked to similar bohemian Mediterranean patterns.
[推测] The hosts suggest that writers and bohemian settlers both followed and helped advertise the appeal of Mediterranean destinations.

[13:33] **Paid Holidays and Domestic Mass Leisure**

[事实] The Holidays with Pay Act arrived in 1938 and effectively gave many workers a week's holiday.
[事实] Most people in this period still went to British seaside resorts rather than foreign beaches.
[事实] Blackpool was attracting 17 million visitors a year by the 1940s.
[事实] The Lake District is described as more middle-class, connected with Wordsworth, Coleridge, Beatrix Potter, and the National Trust.
[推测] Paid holidays widened access to leisure, but class still shaped where people went and what kind of holiday counted as desirable.

[15:38] **Holiday Camps and Billy Butlin**

[事实] Holiday camps had existed in tent-based forms from the early 1900s, but Billy Butlin became the figure most associated with their mass popularity.
[事实] Butlin, born in South Africa, came to Britain, became a fairground entrepreneur, and opened his first camp at Skegness in 1936.
[事实] A week at Butlin's cost about three pounds and included accommodation, food, and activities.
[事实] Butlin introduced entertainers, later known as redcoats, after noticing that guests were hesitant to join activities.
[推测] Butlin's success depended on turning leisure into a managed collective experience, with organised fun replacing the older high-minded group holiday.

[20:05] **Tourism to Nazi Germany**

[事实] The Nazis offered tours of Dachau while presenting it as a model camp.
[事实] Some cooperative and progressive travel groups were still advertising walking tours in Germany, Bavaria, and the Rhineland in 1939.
[事实] These tours were promoted as ways to form friendships across race, creed, and class and to encourage understanding and peace.
[推测] The hosts treat these trips as troubling in hindsight, while also acknowledging that some travellers believed international contact could help prevent war.

[24:22] **Postwar Seaside Resorts, Butlins, and Caravans**

[事实] After the Second World War, British mass tourism initially centred on seaside resorts and holiday camps rather than foreign package holidays.
[事实] The late 1940s and early 1950s are described as the high point of British seaside resorts.
[事实] Butlin opened further camps in the 1940s and 1950s, including Ayr, Mosney, Bognor Regis, and Minehead.
[事实] Caravanning became a major success in the 1950s and 1960s as more people owned cars.
[推测] Postwar leisure combined new mobility with older habits of domestic mass entertainment.

[28:00] **Vladimir Raitz and the Package Holiday**

[事实] Vladimir Raitz, a White Russian emigre who became a Reuters journalist, is presented as the pioneer of the overseas package flight.
[事实] Raitz founded Horizon after seeing an opportunity in Corsica, where a friend was running a water polo club.
[事实] Because of aviation regulations, his early trips were permitted only for students and teachers.
[事实] He advertised in Teacher's World, the Nursing Mirror, and the New Statesman, offering a Corsica trip for 32 pounds with flight, canvas accommodation, food, wine, sun, and beach.
[推测] Raitz updated Thomas Cook's guided-tour model for the age of charter flights.

[30:52] **Horizon, Travel Reps, and the Bikini**

[事实] Horizon expanded to camps in Majorca and the Costa Brava and developed clubs and travel reps.
[事实] Vladimir Raitz interviewed Alan Clark for a Horizon travel rep job and turned him down.
[事实] The bikini was invented by Louis Reard in 1946 and unveiled at the Piscine Molitor in Paris by Micheline Bernardini.
[事实] Reard named it after Bikini Atoll, where the United States had recently conducted an atomic test.
[推测] The bikini story shows how postwar leisure, sexuality, fashion, and atomic-age imagery became intertwined.

[34:05] **Spain, Benidorm, and Mediterranean Mass Tourism**

[事实] By 1971, British tourists were taking four million holidays abroad.
[事实] Spain under Franco promoted tourism to bring in hard currency and stimulate its economy.
[事实] Benidorm had fewer than 3,000 residents in 1957, but by 1960 it received 30,000 British and German visitors every August.
[事实] By 1960 Benidorm had 300 new buildings, 30 high-rise blocks of flats, 34 hotels, and four cinemas.
[推测] The Spanish coast became a mass-tourism landscape partly because cheap package travel matched Franco-era economic needs with British demand for sun.

[35:34] **Carry On Abroad and the Risks of Package Tourism**

[事实] The hosts discuss Carry On Abroad as a cultural mirror of early package tourism.
[事实] In the film, British tourists arrive at a supposedly Mediterranean paradise where the hotel is unfinished and basic services fail.
[事实] Dominic recalls a 1980s family holiday to Crete where the booked hotel was still a building site.
[事实] Early travel television such as the BBC's Holiday programme and ITV's Wish You Were Here partly held travel companies to account.
[推测] Package tourism was exciting but also risky because customers depended on brochures, reps, and weak regulation.

[38:52] **Foreign Food, Declining Resorts, and the End of the Old Holiday Camp**

[事实] Some early British tourists were suspicious of foreign food; the episode mentions Derbyshire miners taking their own beer and tinned food to Italy.
[事实] By the 1970s, British seaside resorts and holiday camps were in serious decline.
[事实] Butlins struggled with violence, drunkenness, and rowdy young visitors, and banned single young men to restore a family image.
[事实] Package tours to Spain were cheap enough for working-class people with steady wages.
[推测] The appeal of guaranteed sun abroad accelerated the decline of older domestic holiday institutions.

[40:38] **The Internet Break and the End of the Travel Agent Age**

[事实] Dominic argues that the 1970s package-tour world still belonged to the tradition of Thomas Cook because it relied on middlemen, reps, brochures, and organised group travel.
[事实] He identifies the internet and 1990s air-travel changes as the major break in modern tourism.
[事实] Thomas Cook Group entered compulsory liquidation in 2019, although Thomas Cook Holidays continued as a successor business.
[推测] Digital booking weakened the old gatekeeper model by letting travellers compare, book, and customise trips directly.

[43:02] **Continuities: Class, Improvement, Health, and Fear of Boredom**

[事实] The hosts identify class as a constant theme in tourism from the 17th century onward.
[事实] They argue that holiday choices still reveal class position because modern travellers have so many options.
[事实] They also highlight the enduring idea that holidays should broaden the mind, improve health, or be good for the traveller.
[事实] The fear of boredom is presented as another continuity, from grand tourists to Butlins guests.
[推测] The episode's broader conclusion is that modern holidays are never just escape; they are also statements about taste, morality, health, and identity.

[46:14] **Closing: Corfu and Historical Holiday Fantasies**

[事实] Tom says he is going to Corfu at the end of September to play cricket and then stay for two more weeks.
[事实] The hosts joke about a retro holiday agency offering historical experiences such as the Grand Tour, drinking seawater at Brighton, German spa treatments, Kaiser-era trips, and Butlins.
[事实] They say the next episode will return to the ancient world to ask whether anything like holidays or tourism existed there.
[推测] The ending uses humour to connect the series' historical examples with present-day travel habits.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it treats tourism as social history rather than a light lifestyle topic. It shows how holidays reveal class, morality, technology, state policy, consumer risk, and changing ideas of health and pleasure.

A major strength is the long continuity the hosts draw from pilgrimage and the Grand Tour to Thomas Cook, Butlins, Horizon, and internet booking. The episode is especially strong on British tourism, package holidays, and the comic absurdity of organised leisure.

[推测] Its main limitation is that the story is strongly British-centred, with France, Spain, Germany, Corsica, and Mallorca largely discussed through British travellers' experiences. Listeners wanting a broader global history of tourism may find the scope narrower than the title might imply.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in social history, modern Britain, travel culture, and the hidden class codes behind apparently ordinary leisure choices.
