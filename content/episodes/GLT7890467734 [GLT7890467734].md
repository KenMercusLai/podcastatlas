# The Star-Spangled Banner: Fort McHenry, Slavery, and America’s Anthem

## 概览

Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook open a World Cup-themed series on national anthems by focusing on “The Star-Spangled Banner,” chosen because the United States is one of the 2026 host nations and because the anthem has an unusually contested history.

The episode traces the song from the War of 1812 and the British attack on Fort McHenry to Francis Scott Key’s poem, its reuse of a familiar English melody, and its eventual adoption as the United States national anthem in 1931.

The central argument is that the anthem is not a simple patriotic artifact. The hosts emphasize its military origins, its musical difficulty, its history of parody and reinterpretation, and the unresolved controversy over whether its third verse refers to enslaved African Americans who fled to the British.

## 分段落总结

[02:43] **Opening The Anthem Series**

[事实] The hosts introduce a series tied to the 2026 Football/Soccer World Cup, focusing on six competing nations whose anthems have significant historical backstories.

[事实] They begin with “The Star-Spangled Banner” because the United States is a host nation, the anthem is prominent in sport, and it is famously difficult to sing.

[事实] The hosts preview later discussion of controversial performances and protests involving the anthem, including Colin Kaepernick’s kneeling protest.

[05:04] **The War Of 1812 Context**

[事实] The song’s historical setting is the British attack on Fort McHenry near Baltimore in September 1814, during the War of 1812.

[事实] The hosts describe the war as obscure in Britain and often skimmed over in American memory, partly because it ended without a decisive victory.

[事实] They list causes of the war: British trade restrictions, impressment of American sailors, American ambitions toward Canada, conflict with Native American allies of Britain, and hopes for national unity.

[08:02] **Canada, Chesapeake Bay, And Washington**

[事实] Early American invasions of Upper and Lower Canada failed, while British forces occupied parts of Michigan and Maine.

[事实] The hosts say the War of 1812 became a foundational moment in Canadian identity.

[事实] In 1813 and especially 1814, the British Chesapeake Bay campaign targeted American coastal towns and commerce.

[事实] After Napoleon’s first abdication, Britain had more resources to direct against the United States, leading to the occupation of Washington and the burning of the Capitol and White House.

[10:50] **Dr Beans And Francis Scott Key**

[事实] After Washington, British forces headed toward Baltimore, a major American city and harbor.

[事实] Dr. William Beans was arrested by the British after trying to stop British troops from taking goods in Maryland.

[事实] Francis Scott Key, a Maryland lawyer from a slave-owning family, was asked to help negotiate Beans’s release.

[事实] Key went under a flag of truce, found the British fleet, and was allowed aboard HMS Tonnant.

[14:07] **Key Watches The Attack**

[事实] British officers agreed to release Beans, but Key and his companions had overheard plans for the attack on Baltimore and were detained until the operation ended.

[事实] At dawn on September 13, 1814, British ships began bombarding Fort McHenry.

[事实] The attack included Congreve rockets and bomb vessels, producing the imagery later remembered as rockets and bombs in the anthem.

[事实] The bombardment lasted roughly 24 hours and involved hundreds of rockets and many shells, but caused limited damage to the fort’s defenses.

[18:18] **The Flag Over Fort McHenry**

[事实] At dawn on September 14, Major George Armistead ordered a large American flag raised over Fort McHenry.

[事实] The flag had 15 stars and 15 stripes and measured 42 by 30 feet.

[事实] It had been made by Mary Pickersgill with help from her daughter, nieces, and a servant.

[事实] A smaller storm flag had flown during the bombardment, while the large flag was raised afterward.

[推测] Key’s sense of dramatic triumph was shaped by what he could see from a distance, even though the fort may never have been in serious danger from the British bombardment.

[20:51] **From Poem To Printed Ballad**

[事实] After the British withdrew, Key wrote a poem rather than an anthem.

[事实] The hosts reject the common story that he wrote it on the back of an envelope, citing the historian Mark Clague’s point that envelopes were not commonly used in 1814.

[事实] Key finished the poem at the Indian Queen Hotel in Baltimore.

[事实] His brother-in-law helped get it printed, and the Baltimore Patriot published it under the title “The Defence of Fort McHenry.”

[23:15] **A Familiar English Tune**

[事实] The poem functioned as a broadside ballad: new words written to fit a familiar tune.

[事实] The tune was “The Anacreontic Song,” written in England in 1775 by John Stafford Smith.

[事实] The hosts say the tune was not simply a bawdy drinking song; it was associated with the Anacreontic Club, a London musical society.

[事实] Versions of the tune were already popular in the United States and had been used for abolitionist and political songs.

[事实] Key had previously written another patriotic ballad to the same melody.

[29:21] **Becoming The Star-Spangled Banner**

[事实] By October 1814, the poem had been reprinted in newspapers from New England to Georgia.

[事实] A Baltimore music shop owner, Thomas Carr, helped give it the title “The Star-Spangled Banner.”

[事实] At this stage, there was no suggestion that it would become the national anthem; that would not happen for more than a century.

[事实] The hosts introduce the later accusation that the song glorifies slavery.

[32:04] **The Third Verse Controversy**

[事实] In 2017, the NAACP petitioned Congress to replace “The Star-Spangled Banner” as the national anthem, calling it racist and anti-Black.

[事实] The controversy centers on the third verse’s reference to the “hireling and slave.”

[事实] Mark Clague argues that those words likely referred to British subjects, mercenaries, and collaborators, using Revolutionary-era language about submission to monarchy.

[事实] The hosts compare this ambiguity to “Rule, Britannia!,” where “slaves” refers to political subjection rather than chattel slavery.

[推测] The phrase can carry more than one historical meaning because American rhetoric about liberty and slavery was already deeply conflicted.

[36:19] **Escaped Slaves And British Strategy**

[事实] The hosts explain that British commanders in the Chesapeake relied heavily on escaped enslaved people for local intelligence.

[事实] Admiral George Coburn promised that runaway slaves would be welcomed by the British and not returned.

[事实] Male runaways were armed, given red uniforms, and organized into the Corps of Colonial Marines.

[事实] White American slaveholders were alarmed by the prospect of armed Black men fighting with the British.

[事实] The hosts cite the irony that, while Key wrote about the “land of the free,” enslaved African Americans were trying to reach British ships because they were more likely to find freedom there.

[41:05] **After The War**

[事实] The War of 1812 ended in 1815 essentially as a draw.

[事实] Coburn did not return the roughly 6,000 escaped slaves who had reached British protection.

[事实] Many went to Canada, while some Colonial Marines settled in Trinidad in villages where they and their descendants were called “Americans.”

[推测] This context makes it plausible that Key’s third verse was at least partly aimed at enslaved people who fled to the British.

[42:25] **Francis Scott Key And Slavery**

[事实] Key owned enslaved people, buying his first in 1800 and owning eight when he died in 1843.

[事实] As a lawyer, he sometimes represented slaveholders seeking the return of enslaved people, but he also freed some enslaved people and represented some who sought freedom.

[事实] He supported the American Colonization Society, which aimed to send free Black Americans to Africa and later contributed to the foundation of Liberia.

[事实] As district attorney for Washington, D.C., Key strongly opposed abolitionism and prosecuted anti-slavery activists.

[推测] The hosts present Key as morally and politically ambiguous at best, and as someone many Black Americans would understandably find hard to celebrate.

[47:18] **Popularity, Parody, And Institutional Use**

[事实] The song remained popular after the War of 1812 and was sung at public celebrations.

[事实] People changed the words for political and social causes, including temperance campaigns.

[事实] Both Union and Confederate supporters claimed the song during the Civil War.

[事实] By the late nineteenth century, the U.S. Naval Academy and army posts were using it in formal flag ceremonies.

[事实] The Fort McHenry flag itself became a relic, though the hosts note that some of its damage came from family members giving pieces away as souvenirs.

[51:09] **The Fight To Make It The Anthem**

[事实] In 1916, Woodrow Wilson ordered the song played at military occasions and commissioned a standard arrangement.

[事实] The United States still had no official national anthem after the First World War.

[事实] Congressman John Charles Linthicum repeatedly introduced bills to make “The Star-Spangled Banner” the anthem.

[事实] Critics objected that the melody was British, the lyrics were militaristic, and the song was too difficult to sing.

[事实] The song requires a range of 19 semitones, which the hosts say is difficult even for some professional singers.

[55:25] **Rivals And Official Adoption**

[事实] Rival candidates included “Hail Columbia” and “America the Beautiful.”

[事实] “America the Beautiful” was popular because it praised the American landscape and was easier to sing.

[事实] “The Star-Spangled Banner” won partly because of its military associations.

[事实] The Veterans of Foreign Wars organized a petition with five million names, and Herbert Hoover signed the anthem into law in 1931.

[57:02] **Modern Performance Controversies**

[事实] José Feliciano’s 1968 World Series performance was criticized as disrespectful because he gave the anthem a Latin jazz and folk-influenced interpretation.

[事实] Jimi Hendrix performed a famous guitar version at Woodstock in 1969, one of many versions he played in the late 1960s.

[事实] The anthem was also central to the 1968 Olympic protest by Tommie Smith and John Carlos.

[事实] In 2005, Sacha Baron Cohen, performing as Borat, caused controversy by parodying patriotic song conventions at a rodeo in Virginia.

[推测] These examples show that disputes over the anthem often concern who is allowed to reinterpret national symbols and how reverently they must be treated.

[60:16] **Kaepernick, Black Lives Matter, And Key’s Legacy**

[事实] In 2016, San Francisco 49ers quarterback Colin Kaepernick knelt during the anthem to protest police brutality toward African Americans.

[事实] Kaepernick became a free agent after that season and was not signed by another team; his lawsuit against the NFL was settled out of court.

[事实] Kneeling became a wider symbolic gesture after George Floyd was killed in 2020.

[事实] In 2020, activists toppled a Francis Scott Key statue in Golden Gate Park, and it was later replaced with 350 black steel figures representing Africans on the first slave ship to Virginia in 1619.

[62:03] **Ambiguity And Alternative Anthems**

[事实] The hosts conclude that it is impossible to know exactly what Key had in mind when he wrote the disputed line.

[事实] Dominic says his personal view is that the text probably was aimed at enslaved people trying to escape and help the British.

[事实] The hosts also argue that art can be separated from the artist.

[事实] Abolitionists wrote their own version of the anthem in 1844 in The Liberator, turning the song’s language against slavery.

[推测] The episode’s broader conclusion is that national anthems often preserve competing and uncomfortable stories rather than a single settled meaning.

[64:13] **Series Preview**

[事实] The hosts preview the next episode on “God Save the King,” connecting it to similar issues of anthem history and controversy.

[事实] They also mention upcoming episodes on the German, Dutch, Brazilian, and South African national anthems.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it treats a familiar anthem as a historical object rather than a fixed patriotic symbol. It connects battlefield events, print culture, slavery, sport, protest, and public memory into one coherent story.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the discussion of the War of 1812 context and the careful treatment of the “hireling and slave” controversy. The hosts avoid presenting the slavery interpretation as proven, but they give enough context to show why the issue cannot be dismissed.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in American history, national symbols, sports rituals, and the politics of memory. Its limitation is that the comic British perspective sometimes frames serious subjects with irony, which may not suit listeners looking for a more solemn treatment.
