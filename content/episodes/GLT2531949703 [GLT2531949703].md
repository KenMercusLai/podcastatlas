# The Mystery of the Somerton Man

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* uses Australia as part of a World Cup-themed series, but turns away from football to one of Adelaide’s most famous mysteries: the Somerton Man. The hosts frame the case as a real-life 1940s detective story, full of clues that seem almost too literary to be true.

The core narrative follows the discovery of an unidentified man on Somerton Park Beach in December 1948, the puzzling objects found on and near him, and the later discovery of a tiny printed phrase from the *Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam*. The case opens onto theories involving poison, espionage, a secret love affair, and coded messages.

The episode’s main conclusion is that recent DNA work identified the man, according to the presenters, as Carl Webb, also known as Charles Webb. But the identification does not solve the deeper questions: why he was at the beach, why his clothing labels were removed, what killed him, and what connection, if any, he had to Jessica Thompson.

## 分段落总结

[00:40] **Australia, Adelaide, and the chosen mystery**

[事实] The hosts introduce the episode as part of a World Cup-themed series focused on Australia.

[事实] Tom chooses the mystery of the Somerton Man rather than a conventional football topic.

[事实] Adelaide is described as both the “City of Churches” and a city with an extraordinary history of murders.

[推测] The opening frames the case as history, true crime, and detective-fiction atmosphere at the same time.

[04:22] **The body on Somerton Park Beach**

[事实] The story begins on November 30, 1948, at Somerton Park Beach near Adelaide.

[事实] Witnesses see a man lying with his head against the seawall; he appears to move, but does not swat away mosquitoes around him.

[事实] On the morning of December 1, two jockeys find the same man still lying there and discover that he is dead.

[事实] A cigarette is found fallen from his mouth onto his jacket lapel, though accounts differ on whether it was unsmoked or half-smoked.

[05:56] **Missing identity and strange belongings**

[事实] Police find an unused rail ticket from Adelaide, a used bus ticket, a U.S.-made comb, Juicy Fruit gum, and cheap cigarettes containing more expensive cigarettes.

[事实] Every label on the clothing he is wearing has been removed, and he has no wallet or other identifying material.

[事实] The man is described as around 40 to 45, good-looking, well-built, with short slightly greying hair and no calluses on his hands.

[事实] His shoes are very highly polished despite his being found on a beach.

[事实] His toes and leg muscles suggest to the examiner that he may have worn high heels or ballet slippers, and possibly been a ballet dancer.

[07:47] **Autopsy, poison, and the suitcase**

[事实] The autopsy finds a healthy heart, an enlarged spleen, and excess blood in the liver, kidneys, and stomach.

[事实] The presenters say these findings suggest poison, but no specific poison is identified and there is no sign of violence.

[事实] The estimated time of death is 2 a.m., between the evening sightings and the morning discovery.

[事实] Police find a suitcase at Adelaide’s main station, linked to him by U.S.-made thread matching a repair in his trousers.

[事实] Most suitcase clothing labels are removed, but “T Keane” or “Keane” appears on a tie, laundry bag, and vest; searches for a missing T Keane produce no result.

[12:22] **The “Tammam Shad” clue**

[事实] A pathologist later finds a tiny tightly rolled piece of paper hidden deep in one of the man’s pockets.

[事实] The paper contains the printed words “Tammam Shad,” which the hosts say is Persian meaning “it is done” or “it is finished.”

[事实] The phrase comes from the *Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam*, in Edward Fitzgerald’s translation.

[事实] Police ask the public to check copies of the book, and a man comes forward with a copy that had been thrown into his car near Somerton Park Beach.

[事实] The relevant words are missing from that copy, matching the paper found on the dead man.

[14:27] **Code, phone numbers, and Jessica Thompson**

[事实] Police find five lines of apparently random letters in the back of the book, one crossed out and rewritten, but codebreakers cannot crack them.

[事实] The book also contains two phone numbers: one for a local bank and one for Jessica Thompson, a nurse living about 400 meters from where the body was found.

[事实] Jessica Thompson denies knowing the dead man, but a policeman says she appeared extremely shaken when shown the body or plaster cast.

[事实] Thompson says the *Rubaiyat* is her favorite book and that she once gave a copy to Alf Boxel, an Australian Army lieutenant she met while nursing in Sydney.

[事实] Police initially think the dead man may be Alf Boxel, but Boxel is later found alive.

[16:26] **Spy theories and Cold War atmosphere**

[事实] One theory discussed is that the Somerton Man was a spy.

[事实] The hosts connect this theory to 1948 Cold War tensions and to the Woomera test range northwest of Adelaide, where Anglo-Australian V2-related work was taking place.

[事实] Alf Boxel may have been involved in intelligence, according to the episode.

[事实] Jessica Thompson’s daughter later said her mother spoke Russian and was interested in communism.

[推测] The spy theories grow from suggestive context rather than hard evidence, which the hosts explicitly say is lacking.

[18:30] **The doomed-love theory and Robin Thompson**

[事实] Another theory is that the case involved a doomed love affair connected to Jessica Thompson.

[事实] At the time the body was found, Jessica was involved with Prosper Thompson but was not yet married to him.

[事实] Jessica had a young son named Robin, apparently born around 1947, who was later raised as Prosper’s son.

[事实] Robin Thompson later became a ballet dancer, which interested Derek Abbott because the Somerton Man’s body had suggested ballet-like legs.

[事实] Abbott’s theory was that Robin was the son of Jessica Thompson and the Somerton Man, supported by claims about ears, teeth, ballet, and an unknown father.

[21:04] **Derek Abbott, Rachel, and DNA**

[事实] Derek Abbott, a University of Adelaide physicist, becomes deeply involved in investigating the Somerton Man.

[事实] Abbott concludes that DNA is needed from the Somerton Man and from Robin or Robin’s descendants.

[事实] Robin had died, but his daughter Rachel was alive; Abbott meets Rachel and they become engaged within a day.

[事实] Abbott and Rachel marry, have three children, and Rachel jokes that he married her for her DNA.

[事实] After authorities refuse exhumation, Abbott extracts hairs from the Somerton Man’s death mask and works with American genealogist Colleen Fitzpatrick.

[23:16] **DNA identification as Carl Webb**

[事实] The DNA results announced on July 26 show that Robin was not the Somerton Man’s son.

[事实] The presenters say the DNA work identifies the Somerton Man as Carl Webb, also known as Charles Webb.

[事实] Webb is described as the sixth child of an Australian-German couple from Melbourne.

[事实] Webb’s brother-in-law was named Thomas Keane, linking him to the “T Keane” clothing clue.

[事实] Webb had left his wife in April 1947, had no later record before the death, and his wife began divorce proceedings in 1951 on grounds of desertion.

[24:41] **Poetry, horses, and unresolved clues**

[事实] The hosts say Webb enjoyed poetry, which may explain the *Rubaiyat* connection.

[事实] They also say Webb enjoyed betting on horses.

[推测] Derek Abbott’s theory is that the mysterious letters may have been horse names or betting notes rather than espionage code.

[事实] The episode says many questions remain: why the labels were removed, why the cigarettes were arranged strangely, and why his body suggested ballet-like legs.

[事实] The presenters say no firm connection between Webb and Jessica Thompson is known.

[26:12] **Why the mystery still matters**

[事实] The hosts argue that people were drawn to grand theories: spy plots, American agents, European refugees, and espionage.

[事实] The DNA identification points instead toward an Australian man from Melbourne whose death seems to have gone unnoticed.

[事实] The hosts compare this to Golden Age detective fiction, where elaborate clues can lead to a more ordinary solution.

[推测] The episode uses the case to show how political anxieties, especially Cold War fears, can shape the stories people build around unresolved deaths.

[事实] The episode ends by emphasizing that the identification is a major development but many parts of the history remain unwritten.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its tight storytelling: it turns a complex unsolved case into a clear sequence of clues, theories, and revisions without losing the sense of mystery.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the way it links true crime to literary genre, showing how the Somerton Man case seems to echo Golden Age detective fiction while also attracting Cold War conspiracy theories.

[推测] Its limitation is that many claims remain unresolved inside the transcript itself, especially the cause of death, Jessica Thompson’s role, and the meaning of the letters in the *Rubaiyat*.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in historical mysteries, detective fiction, Cold War atmosphere, and cases where modern DNA evidence answers one question while leaving several others open.
