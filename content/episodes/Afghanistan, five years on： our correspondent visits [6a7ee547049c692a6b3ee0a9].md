# Afghanistan, Five Years On; Colombia’s Quake; Nirmal Purja Remembered

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* looks first at Afghanistan five years after the Taliban returned to power, asking whether the country has gained peace at the cost of prosperity, rights and international connection.

The reporting from Kabul, Herat and Kandahar describes a country where violence has fallen and cities feel calmer, but poverty, hunger, unemployment, restrictions on women and girls, and Taliban hardline rule shape everyday life.

The episode then turns to Colombia after a major earthquake, focusing on uneven disaster response and the contrast between richer regions and neglected areas such as Chocó. It closes with an obituary of Nirmal Purja, the Nepali mountaineer remembered for his speed, endurance and controversial high-altitude methods.

## 分段落总结

[01:47] **Afghanistan After America’s Longest War**

[事实] The episode frames Afghanistan as the place where America fought for two decades after the September 11 attacks and then withdrew as its proxy Afghan government collapsed.

[事实] The Taliban returned to power five years before this episode, raising the question of what has happened to ordinary Afghans left behind.

[推测] The segment presents Afghanistan as a case where military defeat, state collapse and humanitarian consequences remain tightly connected.

[02:27] **Kabul’s Uneasy Normality**

[事实] Tom Sass reports from Kabul that the city has busy streets, commerce, traffic, growth and a degree of peace.

[事实] He also says music has been banned in cafes, dress codes have been imposed, and Taliban security officials are visible near ministries.

[推测] Kabul’s apparent calm is portrayed as fragile and constrained by ideological policing.

[03:41] **Safety, Control and Poverty**

[事实] Tom spent about ten days in Afghanistan, visiting Kabul, Herat and Kandahar and taking a roughly 1,000-kilometre road trip.

[事实] He says violence has fallen sharply, the Taliban has a tight grip on power, and checkpoints around big cities are relatively light touch.

[事实] He also reports heavily armed men, vice-and-virtue police, widespread poverty, hunger and unemployment.

[推测] The reporting suggests that security has improved, but it has not translated into broad material well-being.

[04:54] **Hardliners Consolidate Power**

[事实] Tom says power has consolidated around Taliban hardliners led by the Amir, Hibatullah Akhundzada, based in Kandahar.

[事实] The Amir has taken control of revenue sources including mining contracts and has built a separate loyal force thought to number about 8,000 fighters.

[事实] More moderate Taliban figures have been too weak or scared to challenge this direction, and some who did challenge it were expelled.

[推测] The Taliban’s internal balance appears to have moved away from gradual modernisation and toward centralised hardline rule.

[06:17] **Daily Life Under Economic Pressure**

[事实] In Surubi, east of Kabul, villagers described food insecurity, and one elder said they had peace but empty pockets.

[事实] Healthcare is in crisis after humanitarian aid cuts, linked both to Western governments reducing support and Taliban policies.

[事实] Almost six million Afghan refugees have been forcibly returned from Iran and Pakistan, many arriving with nothing.

[推测] The country’s humanitarian burden is being intensified by both international isolation and regional refugee returns.

[07:24] **Women, Girls and Fear of Speaking Openly**

[事实] Women and girls have been removed from many parts of public life, banned from many workplaces and universities, and girls are banned from secondary school.

[事实] Most Afghans Tom spoke with were willing to talk only anonymously because they feared consequences.

[事实] In Kabul, he visited a madrasa being used as a workaround for secondary-age girls to receive some schooling.

[事实] The girls he interviewed were glad to study but said many friends could not afford the school and that their future opportunities were very limited.

[08:43] **Afghanistan’s Foreign Relations**

[事实] Russia is described as the only country that has recognised the Taliban government.

[事实] Countries including India, Turkey, China, Gulf states and Iran have established diplomatic relations and work with the Taliban on issues such as trade.

[事实] China is involved in trying to develop mining, though Tom says the Taliban has been difficult to work with.

[推测] Regional governments are engaging pragmatically even without broad formal recognition.

[09:13] **Isolation and Western Policy**

[事实] Tom says Taliban hardliners under the Amir are not very interested in deep engagement with the outside world.

[事实] Western policy has relied on coercion: withholding recognition, reducing aid, maintaining sanctions and freezing Afghan central-bank reserves.

[事实] Tom says this strategy has not moderated the Taliban regime.

[事实] The EU has invited the Taliban to discuss migration, Germany is talking about returning Afghan refugees, and America and others cooperate with the Taliban on intelligence and counterterrorism.

[推测] The West appears to be shifting from isolation toward reluctant long-term engagement.

[10:48] **A Bleak Future for Afghanistan**

[事实] Tom describes the near-term outlook as bleak.

[事实] One Afghan schoolgirl told him she did not think things would be okay and did not hope for any future.

[事实] Tom argues that outside countries should keep engaging, keep humanitarian aid flowing and consider reforming sanctions that now deter private investment.

[推测] The most realistic path presented is slow, pragmatic engagement rather than rapid political change.

[13:21] **Colombia’s Earthquake**

[事实] The episode says a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck Colombia four days earlier, and the crucial 72-hour rescue window had passed.

[事实] Hundreds of people had died and many more were missing.

[事实] In Quibdó, a nurse described caring for a mother and 23-day-old baby after a wall collapsed on the mother’s back.

[事实] The hospital lacked basic resources, including adequate morgue space.

[15:44] **Government Response and Public Anger**

[事实] Colombia’s new president, Abelardo de la Esprilla, had been sworn in three days before the earthquake and assumed personal command of the response.

[事实] He visited Quibdó on the first day after the earthquake.

[事实] People criticised the response in Cali, where some rescuers were volunteers using bike helmets and hammers rather than trained professionals with proper equipment.

[事实] Aftershocks brought down more buildings, adding to anger and danger.

[17:16] **A Tale of Two Colombias**

[事实] Colombia’s geography makes aid delivery difficult in remote Chocó, where roads are limited and landslides block access.

[事实] The response was better in richer areas such as Cali and the coffee region.

[事实] In Chocó, armed groups already extort poor communities, and residents repeatedly said they had been abandoned.

[推测] The earthquake exposed pre-existing inequality between Colombia’s better-served regions and its poorer, more isolated areas.

[20:05] **Nirmal Purja’s Late Start in Mountaineering**

[事实] Nirmal Purja was born within sight of Dhaulagiri in Nepal but moved away from the mountains at age five.

[事实] He began to realise his talent for altitude climbing around age 30 while crossing the mountain he had seen as a child.

[事实] Anne Rowe describes him as discovering that he could keep going powerfully at altitude.

[21:03] **Project Possible**

[事实] Purja made it his life’s purpose to spend as much time as possible on the fourteen peaks above 8,000 metres.

[事实] His Project Possible aimed to climb all fourteen as quickly as possible.

[事实] He completed them in six months and six days, compared with a previous record of more than seven years.

[推测] The achievement is presented as a radical compression of what elite mountaineering had previously considered possible.

[21:38] **Criticism and Rescue Ethos**

[事实] Critics objected to Purja’s use of bottled oxygen, fixed ropes and helicopters between base camps.

[事实] Purja said bottled oxygen helped him rescue climbers who had been abandoned after running out of oxygen.

[事实] He rescued many people and attributed his refusal to leave people behind to his army ethos.

[事实] He also climbed without bottled oxygen to prove he could do so.

[23:01] **Meaning Beyond Ego**

[事实] When critics said Project Possible was about ego, Purja replied that it was not about him or his name.

[事实] He said his feat was meant to show people that they could overcome the mountains and obstacles in their own lives.

[推测] The obituary frames his ambition as both self-dramatising and inspirational.

[23:40] **Fitness, Gurkha Training and Sherpa Recognition**

[事实] Purja trained to become a Gurkha, as his father and three elder brothers had done.

[事实] He pushed himself through extreme physical training, including midnight runs and heavy cross-country military exercises.

[事实] He wanted Sherpas to receive more credit for their crucial role in Western climbers’ ascents.

[推测] His military background shaped both his physical endurance and his attack-minded approach to mountains.

[24:49] **Talking to Mountains and Death on Broad Peak**

[事实] Purja was not especially involved in Sherpa religious ceremonies before climbs, but he would speak to mountains alone and ask permission to climb.

[事实] On July 30, while attempting Broad Peak, he encountered a huge avalanche and did not survive.

[事实] He had been close to completing his third round of the fourteen highest peaks.

[推测] The obituary closes by portraying the mountain, rather than Purja’s willpower, as having the final word.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest section is the Afghanistan reporting, because it combines ground-level observation with political analysis: Kabul’s calm streets, Taliban moral enforcement, poverty, girls’ restricted education and the failure of Western coercion are connected into one argument.

The Colombia segment is shorter but effective in showing how a natural disaster becomes a test of state capacity and regional inequality. Its most valuable detail is the contrast between Bogotá’s normality and devastated poorer regions.

The obituary of Nirmal Purja offers a vivid portrait of an extreme athlete whose achievements were both admired and criticised. [推测] It will appeal most to listeners interested in geopolitics, humanitarian crises and character-driven obituaries rather than a single-topic episode.
