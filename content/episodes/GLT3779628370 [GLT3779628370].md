# The French Revolution: Republic, Valmy, and the Road to Louis XVI's Trial

## 概览

This episode covers September to December 1792, when the National Convention abolishes the French monarchy while Paris fears a Prussian assault. The hosts frame 21 September 1792 as a seismic moment, but also stress that the Republic is not yet clearly defined.

The military story runs alongside the political one. At Valmy, Dumouriez and Kellermann halt the Prussian advance, a result celebrated as a republican triumph even though the episode describes it as closer to survival than outright victory.

The Convention then becomes a battlefield of its own. The Girondins, the Plain, and the Montagnards clash over power, violence, and legitimacy, while suspicion turns dissent into possible treason.

The episode ends by setting up Louis XVI's trial. His imprisonment, the legal debate over trying him, Saint-Just's case for execution, and the discovery of the iron safe all move the story toward the king's fate.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **The Monarchy Is Abolished**

[事实] Jérôme Pétion, president of the National Convention on 21 September 1792, announces the abolition of the monarchy.
[事实] The hosts describe this as a huge moment in French and European history after centuries of French kingship.
[推测] The episode treats abolition as a beginning rather than a settled outcome, because the Republic still has to define itself.

[02:04] **Paris Prepares for War**

[事实] Paris is shown preparing for attack with bells, cannons, volunteers, patriotic songs, and searches for lead to make musket shot.
[事实] The new National Convention is elected after the storming of the Tuileries and has broad powers to remake France.
[事实] Turnout is low because of war, harvest pressures, confusion, intimidation, and public oral voting.

[05:39] **A Radical Convention Takes Shape**

[事实] In Paris, voting coincides with the September Massacres and later takes place at the Jacobin Club after moderates and royalists are purged.
[事实] Paris elects figures including Robespierre, Danton, Desmoulins, Santerre, Marat, and Philippe Égalité.
[事实] Many deputies are young professionals, with younger members described as more militant and excitable.

[08:50] **Year One of the Republic**

[事实] Collot d'Herbois urges immediate abolition of monarchy without a referendum, and Bishop Henri Grégoire denounces kingship.
[事实] The vote becomes unanimous once deputies see where opinion is moving.
[事实] State documents are to be dated from “Year One of the French Republic.”
[推测] The absence of a clear formal proclamation leaves a symbolic gap that later makes Louis XVI's fate more important.

[11:31] **Valmy Stops the Prussians**

[事实] Dumouriez is ordered to block the Prussian advance instead of pursuing his preferred campaign in Belgium.
[事实] Brunswick hesitates to march on Paris because Dumouriez could cut his supply lines, and the Prussian army is tired, hungry, wet, and sick.
[事实] Kellermann reinforces Dumouriez, and French regulars with improved lightweight artillery face the Prussians at Valmy on 20 September.

[14:21] **A Draw Becomes a Victory**

[事实] At Valmy, artillery fires for hours through mist before Prussian infantry advance.
[事实] Kellermann rallies the French with “Vive la Nation,” and the hosts say there is no evidence the troops sang “La Marseillaise” there.
[事实] Brunswick calls off the attack; the French have not decisively won, but they have survived and stopped the Prussians.
[推测] Because collapse had been expected, the limited battlefield result becomes politically transformative.

[20:54] **The Convention Splits into Factions**

[事实] The Convention is divided among the Girondins, the Plain or Marsh, and the Montagnards.
[事实] The Girondins draw strength from provincial cities, while the Montagnards are more Parisian and dominate the Jacobin Club.
[事实] Robespierre, Danton, and Marat are major Montagnard names, but the hosts stress that they do not function as a unified triumvirate.
[推测] The factions resemble shifting camps more than modern political parties.

[25:06] **Violence and the Leftward Ratchet**

[事实] The Girondins are portrayed as salon-oriented republicans, while the Montagnards appeal more directly to the Parisian people.
[事实] The Montagnards are more accepting of revolutionary violence, especially after the September Massacres.
[事实] The hosts argue that revolutionaries can be outflanked to their left, making yesterday's radicals look like today's reactionaries.
[推测] This dynamic makes moderation dangerous because hesitation can be framed as betrayal.

[30:20] **The Girondins Fail to Destroy the Mountain**

[事实] On 22 September, Girondin speakers attack the Montagnards as anarchists, murderers, levelers, and would-be dictators.
[事实] Marat responds theatrically, including by threatening to shoot himself if condemned.
[事实] A later attack by Louvet accuses Robespierre of seeking dictatorship, but Robespierre successfully defends revolutionary violence as necessary.
[推测] The Girondins weaken themselves by attacking without removing their opponents.

[37:03] **France Goes on the Offensive**

[事实] After Valmy, Brunswick withdraws across the Rhine, leaving the western bank exposed.
[事实] French armies advance toward Mainz, Worms, Frankfurt, the Swiss Federation, Savoy, Nice, and Belgium.
[事实] Dumouriez defeats the Austrians at Jemappes, enters Brussels, and moves toward Liège, Antwerp, and the Dutch border.
[推测] The episode links these victories to a new wave of patriotic republican fervor.

[40:29] **The Revolution Becomes Universal**

[事实] Deputies debate whether France should stop at natural borders or carry liberty beyond them.
[事实] The Convention votes to assist peoples who want to recover liberty and later says French armies will export republican reforms.
[事实] These reforms include abolishing feudalism, attacking Catholic Church privileges, and creating republican systems.
[推测] The hosts present this as a new kind of ideological war, not just a conventional war for territory.

[45:01] **Louis XVI in the Temple**

[事实] Louis XVI, Marie Antoinette, Élisabeth, and the children are imprisoned in the Temple.
[事实] They read, homeschool the children, color maps, play badminton, and hear Louis read Roman history.
[事实] Their restrictions are severe: Marie Antoinette cannot sew, Louis cannot shave, and guards write threatening graffiti.
[推测] The domestic routine contrasts with the growing political danger around the royal family.

[47:57] **The Legal Case Against Louis**

[事实] The Convention debates whether Louis can be tried, since the old constitution specified removal from office as the penalty for royal wrongdoing.
[事实] Jean-Baptiste Mailhe argues that natural law overrides the constitution and requires punishment.
[事实] Some deputies want to leave Louis alone, most support a trial, and some Montagnards want execution without trial.
[推测] The appeal to natural law allows revolutionaries to bypass constitutional limits in the name of a higher principle.

[49:32] **Saint-Just and the Iron Safe**

[事实] Saint-Just argues that Louis is guilty because he was a king, not merely because of particular actions.
[事实] He says a virtuous republic cannot allow a former king to live.
[事实] On 20 November, Roland discovers Louis's secret iron safe in the Tuileries, containing papers that show contacts with Austrians and counter-revolutionary groups.
[事实] The Convention later orders Louis Capet to appear before it, and Robespierre says Louis must die so the homeland can live.
[推测] The episode frames the king's death as the symbolic baptism of the Republic.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it connects battlefield events, constitutional uncertainty, factional politics, and revolutionary ideology into one narrative. Valmy is not treated as isolated military history, but as part of the Republic's emotional and political birth.

A major strength is the explanation of how terms such as “people,” “virtue,” “nature,” “public safety,” and “treason” become weapons inside the Convention. The hosts also make clear why Louis XVI's continued existence becomes a political problem for the new regime.

[推测] The main limitation is density: the discussion assumes listeners can follow many people, factions, and earlier episodes. It is best suited to listeners interested in revolutionary legitimacy and political violence, rather than those wanting a simple standalone account of one battle or one trial.
