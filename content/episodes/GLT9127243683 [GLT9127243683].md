# Baghdad: The Abbasid Revolution and the City of Peace

## 概览

This episode traces how the Abbasids overthrew the Umayyad caliphate and shifted the political and cultural center of Islam eastward. The discussion begins with the instability of the late Umayyad world, especially the diversity and tensions across Syria, Iraq, Iran, and Khorasan.

The hosts focus on Abu Muslim’s role in mobilizing Khorasan, where Islamic and Zoroastrian expectations of sacred rule overlapped. They then follow the Abbasid victory, the brutal destruction of the Umayyads, and the rise of Al-Mansur.

The core conclusion is that Baghdad was not merely a new capital but a deliberate statement of Abbasid power. Its location, circular design, Persian influences, commercial promise, and symbolic absorption of older civilizations made it the “crossroads of the universe” and prepared the way for the Islamic Golden Age.

## 分段落总结

[00:40] **Al-Mansur’s Vision for Baghdad**

[事实] The episode opens with Al-Mansur declaring that the site between the Tigris and Euphrates would become a global crossroads for trade from the south, north, east, Syria, Egypt, and beyond.
[事实] He says he will build the city as his capital and residence for himself and his heirs.
[推测] The opening frames Baghdad as both a practical commercial hub and an ideological project meant to embody Abbasid legitimacy.

[02:50] **The Umayyad World Before Collapse**

[事实] The hosts recap the previous episode’s portrait of the Umayyad caliphate as a vast Islamic empire stretching from the Straits of Gibraltar toward the borders of China.
[事实] They describe the Umayyads as facing defeats, sectarian uprisings, religious movements, and a growing class of Islamic lawyers challenging their authority.
[事实] The late Umayyad caliph Marwan had become unpopular in Syria and retreated to Harran.
[推测] The Abbasid rise succeeded partly because it exploited an empire already strained by internal religious, political, and regional tensions.

[04:23] **Diversity and Religious Mixture in the Caliphate**

[事实] The caliphate is described as highly diverse, with Harran still containing pagans who worshipped Babylonian gods and passed themselves off as Sabaeans.
[事实] Khorasan is presented as a vast region including parts of modern Turkmenistan, Afghanistan, eastern Iran, and Uzbekistan, where Zoroastrianism remained strong.
[事实] Zoroastrian and Muslim traditions in Khorasan were said to be fusing and blurring.
[推测] This religious mixture made Khorasan fertile ground for revolutionary claims about sacred rulers and divine justice.

[07:13] **Khorasan and the Abbasid Revolution**

[事实] The rebellion against the Umayyads begins not in Iraq, where resentment was strong and the Abbasids were waiting, but in Khorasan.
[事实] In 745, Behiferid appears in Khorasan dressed in green, carrying a Persian book, claiming to be an agent of light and also using a title associated with Muhammad.
[事实] Behiferid attracts Iranian peasants but is captured and hanged outside a mosque in 749.
[推测] Behiferid’s movement shows how revolutionary religious language in the region could blend Zoroastrian and Islamic expectations.

[09:04] **Abu Muslim’s Enigmatic Role**

[事实] Abu Muslim, a mysterious and charismatic figure, suppresses Behiferid while leading a movement that resembles him in some ways.
[事实] His identity is unclear, with uncertainty over whether he was Arab or Persian, aristocrat or former slave.
[事实] He acts as an agent of the Abbasids and presents them as a divinely appointed family suited to world rule.
[推测] Abu Muslim’s ambiguous identity helped him appeal across social and ethnic boundaries in Khorasan.

[12:27] **The Black Banners and Persian Memory**

[事实] Abu Muslim unfurls the black banners of the Abbasids near Merv, where the last Sasanian king had died while fleeing the Arabs.
[事实] The hosts say this location evoked Persian memories, even though Abu Muslim was not trying to restore the Sasanian empire.
[事实] By 748 he captures Merv, and in 749 his forces move through Iran and into Iraq.
[推测] The Abbasids successfully redirected older Persian loyalties toward their own Islamic dynastic claim.

[13:53] **The Abbasids Take Power**

[事实] Abu Muslim defeats an Umayyad force at Isfahan, secures the Iranian plateau, crosses into Mesopotamia, and captures Kufa.
[事实] On 28 November, Abu al-Abbas, great-grandson of Muhammad’s uncle, is proclaimed caliph in Kufa.
[事实] Abu al-Abbas takes the throne name As-Saffah, meaning “the Shedder of Blood.”
[推测] The throne name signals that Abbasid legitimacy would be enforced through extreme violence as well as sacred lineage.

[15:57] **The Defeat of Marwan and the Umayyads**

[事实] Marwan gathers what forces he can and meets the Abbasids near the Great Zab on 25 January 750.
[事实] The Umayyads outnumber the Abbasids, but Abbasid pikemen successfully resist their cavalry.
[事实] Marwan flees through Harran, Damascus, Jerusalem, and Egypt before being killed; his head is brought to As-Saffah.
[事实] As-Saffah hunts down Umayyads, has surviving members butchered, and orders Umayyad tombs desecrated.
[推测] The Abbasid victory was not only a dynastic transfer but an attempted annihilation of Umayyad memory and legitimacy.

[19:27] **Al-Mansur Succeeds As Caliph**

[事实] As-Saffah rules for four years and dies of smallpox in 753.
[事实] He is succeeded by his elder brother Abu Jaffar, who takes the throne name Al-Mansur, “the Victorious One.”
[事实] Al-Mansur is described as tall, lean, regal, pious, hostile to drink and music, and very brutal.
[推测] Al-Mansur combined religious seriousness, administrative discipline, and ruthless political instincts.

[21:49] **Brutality and Bureaucracy Under Al-Mansur**

[事实] Justin Marozzi is cited as saying Al-Mansur’s executions were so numerous that one wonders how he found time for other affairs of state.
[事实] The hosts recount a likely legendary story in which Al-Mansur leaves a storeroom filled with labeled bodies of descendants of Ali.
[事实] Al-Mansur persecutes the family of Ali to secure Abbasid dynastic rule.
[推测] The story is likely false but reflects the reputation his reign generated: bureaucratic, controlled, and terrifying.

[23:51] **The Abbasids Betray Revolutionary Expectations**

[事实] The Abbasid revolution had promised justice, equity, and the realization of Qur’anic teachings on earth.
[事实] Disillusioned supporters soon saw that the Abbasids behaved like the old rulers they had replaced.
[事实] Al-Mansur is nevertheless described as a highly impressive ruler who managed the economy effectively.
[推测] The episode presents Abbasid rule as a revolution that preserved much of the logic of imperial power.

[24:57] **Choosing a New Capital**

[事实] Al-Mansur believes his dynasty and the caliphate need a firm capital.
[事实] Damascus is rejected because it is associated with the Umayyads and lies too far west from the Abbasid Iranian power base.
[事实] Kufa is rejected because it is full of sectarian tensions, while Harran is associated with pagans and Marwan.
[事实] Al-Mansur chooses to build a new capital in Mesopotamia and settles on a village called Baghdad, a Persian name meaning “gift from God.”
[推测] Baghdad’s location solved strategic, symbolic, and political problems at once.

[27:36] **Building the Round City**

[事实] Al-Mansur arrives in spring 762, and the foundation date is chosen by two royal astrologers, around 30 July or 2 August.
[事实] He lays the first brick, and the first stage takes four years to complete.
[事实] The city is officially named Madinat As-Salam, “the City of Peace.”
[事实] The original city is described as a perfect circle with a moat, two massive walls, four gates, arcades, bazaars, a central mosque, and a palace.
[推测] The circular plan turned Baghdad into an architectural statement of order, centrality, and universal rule.

[30:00] **Commerce, Security, and Urban Growth**

[事实] The four gates lead toward the city center and are associated with spectacular architectural features.
[事实] Bazaars initially stand within the city but are later moved outside to Kark after a Byzantine emissary warns that bazaars breed mobs.
[事实] Kark becomes the commercial and industrial hub, and the western part of Baghdad is still nicknamed Kark.
[事实] Soldiers from Khorasan receive land around the city, and people from across the world begin moving to Baghdad.
[推测] Baghdad rapidly outgrew its planned core because political power and commercial opportunity drew migrants and wealth.

[31:18] **Palace, Mosque, and Caliphal Authority**

[事实] The central enclosure contains a mosque and the Palace of the Golden Gate.
[事实] The palace has a large green dome over the main audience chamber, echoing Umayyad palace traditions on a greater scale.
[事实] Stories later claimed that a horseman statue on the dome pointed toward the next enemy of the caliph.
[事实] Only Al-Mansur was allowed to ride a horse inside the central enclosure.
[推测] The city’s center expressed a fusion of piety, royal spectacle, and strict hierarchy.

[34:06] **Persian Roots of Abbasid Baghdad**

[事实] Later Baghdadis praised the round city as unique, but the hosts note that Sasanian cities had also used circular forms.
[事实] The designers and architects of Baghdad were probably Persian.
[事实] The mosque’s design is said to draw on Persian architectural examples reaching back to Persepolis.
[事实] The combination of palace and sacred structure echoed both Muhammad’s Medina and Sasanian royal cities.
[推测] Baghdad shows Islam’s center of gravity shifting from a Roman-facing world to a Persian-facing one.

[36:31] **From Roman Orientation to Persian Orientation**

[事实] The hosts contrast the Umayyads’ Roman and Byzantine associations with the Abbasids’ Persian orientation.
[事实] The Dome of the Rock and the Great Mosque of Damascus are discussed as examples of Umayyad engagement with Byzantine and Roman models.
[事实] The Abbasid foundation of Baghdad marks a move away from that Roman model and toward Persian cultural and ideological influence.
[推测] This shift helped define what later audiences think of as the medieval Islamic world.

[37:34] **Baghdad as the Heir to Older Civilizations**

[事实] Baghdad lies near Babylon, Seleucia, and Ctesiphon, and Ctesiphon supplied raw materials for its construction.
[事实] Scholars in Baghdad recognized the significance of its placement in a region associated with the birth of civilization.
[事实] The city welcomed Zoroastrians, converted Jews, Christian physicians, and eventually the Eastern Patriarch.
[事实] Pre-Islamic symbolic materials and stories were absorbed into the fabric of the Islamic city.
[推测] Baghdad presented Islam as the culmination and absorber of earlier civilizations rather than a clean break from them.

[40:20] **The Crossroads of the Universe**

[事实] By Al-Mansur’s death during pilgrimage to Mecca in 775, Baghdad was accepted as the place to be.
[事实] The hosts conclude that Al-Mansur’s boast had been fulfilled: Baghdad became a global crossroads.
[事实] They describe it as a meeting point of past and future, traditions, and understandings of the divine.
[事实] The episode ends by setting up the next installment on Baghdad’s golden age, including arts, philosophy, mathematics, science, the Arabian Nights, and Harun al-Rashid.
[推测] The episode treats Baghdad’s foundation as the necessary precondition for the later Islamic Golden Age.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s value lies in connecting military history, religious ferment, dynastic legitimacy, urban planning, and cultural geography into one narrative. It does not present Baghdad simply as a city that became rich, but as a deliberate Abbasid answer to the problems of ruling a vast and diverse empire.

A major strength is the way the hosts explain the Persian dimension of early Abbasid power. Abu Muslim, Khorasan, Zoroastrian memory, Sasanian urban models, and Baghdad’s location all become part of a larger argument about Islam’s changing orientation.

The main limitation is that the episode moves quickly across complex sectarian and regional contexts, so listeners without background in the Umayyads, Abbasids, Shia claims, or Zoroastrianism may need the previous episode or outside reading. Some vivid anecdotes are explicitly treated as probably legendary, so they are best understood as evidence of reputation rather than confirmed fact.

This episode is especially suited to listeners interested in early Islamic history, the making of medieval Baghdad, and the transition from conquest empire to imperial civilization.
