# Romans Against Jews: The Jewish Revolt Begins

## 概览

This episode frames the Jewish revolt against Rome as a story whose later religious significance can easily distort how the events are understood. The hosts argue that the fall of Jerusalem in AD 70 mattered enormously for Jews and Christians, especially because of the destruction of the Temple, but they also caution against treating the revolt only as a timeless religious clash.

The discussion works backward from Jerusalem’s destruction to the long history of Judean identity: the Babylonian destruction of the first Temple, the return under Persian rule, the rebuilding of the Temple, and the role of scripture in preserving a distinct peoplehood. It then moves through Hellenistic rule, Judean expansion, Roman conquest, Herod’s reign, and Rome’s uncertain methods of governing Judea.

The episode’s central explanation for the revolt combines taxation, cultural resistance, messianic expectation, Roman administrative pressure, and local factionalism. By the end, the revolt has begun, Rome has suffered a humiliating defeat at Beth-Horon, Vespasian has been sent to restore Roman control, and the story pauses as Vespasian becomes emperor in AD 69.

## 分段落总结

[00:33] **Jesus, Jerusalem, and the Stakes of the Revolt**

[事实] The episode opens with a passage attributed to Jesus predicting Jerusalem surrounded by armies, flight from Judea, great distress, captivity, and Jerusalem being trampled by Gentiles.
[事实] The hosts identify the Jewish revolt and the Roman crushing of Jerusalem as the subject of the episode.
[事实] Tom describes the story as thrilling, terrible, and blood-soaked, and links it as a kind of sequel to the earlier episode on the crucifixion.
[事实] The destruction of Jerusalem is presented as an event recognized by contemporaries as highly significant.

[02:35] **The Temple and Later Significance**

[事实] The hosts discuss the claim by B. F. Westcott that the fall of Jerusalem to Rome in AD 70 was among the most significant national events in world history.
[事实] Tom says the destruction had immense significance for both Christians and Jews.
[事实] The incineration of the Temple is described as the climactic event of the war and as continuing to reverberate into the present.
[推测] The episode’s interpretive problem is that later Jewish and Christian meanings can make the revolt appear more exceptional than it may have seemed as a Roman provincial rebellion.

[05:16] **Babylon, Return, and Judean Identity**

[事实] The first Temple built by Solomon was destroyed by the Babylonians in the sixth century.
[事实] The people of Judah were taken to Babylon and later allowed by Cyrus of Persia to return to Judah and rebuild the Temple in Jerusalem.
[事实] The speakers say the people of Judah preserved their identity through scripture, Torah, histories, teachings, and prophecies.
[事实] This body of texts, later known as the Bible, helped them trace their origins back before the Babylonian sack of Jerusalem.

[06:36] **Why “Judeans” Matters**

[事实] Tom says these people did not originally call themselves Jews in the later medieval or modern sense.
[事实] In the Greek world they were known as Judeoi, which the hosts say is better understood as Judeans.
[事实] Judeans lived not only in Judea but also in Babylon, Alexandria, and later Rome while still looking to Jerusalem as their mother city.
[推测] The term “Jews” can make the conflict sound like a religious war, while “Judeans” highlights its character as a provincial revolt inside the Roman world.

[08:28] **Judea Between Greek Powers**

[事实] After Alexander’s empire broke apart, Judea lay between the Ptolemies in Egypt and the Seleucids in Syria.
[事实] As those empires declined, the Judeans established a kind of independence.
[事实] Tom describes the Judeans as expansionist, conquering Idumea and obliging Idumeans to become Judeans in worship and law.
[事实] The Judeans also fought Samaria, whose people shared the law of Moses but did not regard Jerusalem as holy.

[10:47] **Pompey and the Roman Takeover**

[事实] Pompey the Great captured Jerusalem in 63 BC.
[事实] Pompey entered the Holy of Holies, where only the Judean high priest was normally allowed to go on one day of the year.
[事实] He found nothing there, was impressed, and allowed the Judeans to continue worshipping as they wished in exchange for tribute.
[事实] Tom says Pompey effectively equated the Judean god with Jupiter, the supreme Roman god.

[11:44] **Herod as Roman Client King**

[事实] Antony and Cleopatra installed Herod, an Idumean and Judean, as king.
[事实] Herod survived the fall of Antony and Cleopatra by persuading Octavian, the future Augustus, that he would now be loyal to him.
[事实] Herod is described as brutal but effective, and as a major builder.
[事实] He greatly developed the Temple, founded Caesarea on the coast, and built palaces at Masada.

[13:42] **Rome’s Uncertain Rule After Herod**

[事实] Herod probably died in 4 BC, after which Augustus divided his kingdom among his sons.
[事实] The arrangement did not work well, and direct Roman rule was imposed on Judea itself.
[事实] Tom says it is contested whether Judea was a separate province or a subdivision of Syria; he leans toward the latter.
[事实] Rome repeatedly shifted between puppet kings, priestly influence, and direct rule by prefects or procurators.

[15:00] **Pontius Pilate and Roman Divide-and-Rule**

[事实] Pontius Pilate was sent by Tiberius and stayed in Judea for a long time.
[事实] Tom describes Pilate as effective and basically pro-Judean.
[事实] Pilate built an aqueduct, respected Temple traditions, and removed an eagle after Judeans objected.
[事实] The Romans are described as backing Jerusalem and the Judeans as the local big power amid regional ethnic and cultural divisions.

[16:51] **Mid-Episode Promotion**

[事实] Dominic interrupts to mention that The Rest is History has often been asked to cover the Spanish Civil War.
[事实] He says plans are underway to cover it, hopefully in 2027.
[事实] He promotes a Book Club episode on Hemingway’s For Whom the Bell Tolls, including discussion of the Spanish Civil War, fascism, idealism, foreign intellectuals, and Hemingway’s time in Madrid.

[18:20] **Resentment of Roman Rule**

[事实] Dominic asks whether Judean resentment of Rome was exceptional or similar to resentment felt by other conquered peoples.
[事实] Tom says the key practical reason for resentment was heavy taxation.
[事实] He also says Judeans saw Greek culture as attractive, appealing, and dangerous.
[事实] Archaeological evidence from Galilee is presented as suggesting Judean households consciously rejected Greek or Roman-style pottery.
[推测] Cultural resistance, taxation, apocalyptic expectation, and talk of messiahs together formed the background to the revolt, though Tom questions whether they fully explain it.

[20:52] **The Revolt of AD 66**

[事实] In spring AD 66, Judea exploded into revolt.
[事实] Nero had sent Gessius Florus to extract as much money as possible because Rome needed funds after the great fire two years earlier.
[事实] A revolt broke out in Jerusalem, and the Roman garrison there was slaughtered.
[事实] This act bound the people of Jerusalem to rebellion against Rome.

[22:02] **Gallus’s Failed Expedition**

[事实] In autumn AD 66, the Syrian governor Gaius Cestius Gallus led a large army with legions toward Jerusalem.
[事实] Gallus attacked from the north, broke through the outer wall, but then withdrew rather than press through the remaining walls.
[事实] His army was ambushed in the Beth-Horon Pass and wiped out.
[事实] Tom calls this the worst Roman military disaster since the annihilation of Varus’s legions in the Teutoburg Forest.

[23:46] **Why Rome Had to Retaliate**

[事实] The hosts compare the revolt with German resistance to Rome.
[事实] Tom says Roman reprisals in Germany had been devastating and near-genocidal in scale.
[事实] Judea was strategically important, rich, and a crucial hinge between Syria and Egypt.
[事实] Roman prestige meant there was no alternative, from a Roman perspective, except to crush the rebels.

[24:51] **A Divided Judean Rebellion**

[事实] Tom says the Judean rebels hated each other and were divided by region, class, and priestly status.
[事实] Many Judeans sided with Rome.
[事实] Herod Agrippa warned against revolt and urged people to consider the power of Rome.
[事实] Agrippa and his sister Berenice are described as devout Judeans committed to the Temple but also loyal servants of Rome.
[推测] The revolt was not a unified national uprising but a fragmented conflict involving both anti-Roman rebels and pro-Roman Judeans.

[26:43] **Vespasian Enters the War**

[事实] Nero appointed Vespasian to command battle-hardened legions against the revolt.
[事实] Tom says Nero chose Vespasian partly because his relatively humble background made him seem less threatening.
[事实] Vespasian is described as a proficient soldier, popular with his men, and effective.
[事实] He moved into Galilee and took it easily, with the main resistance at Jotapata.

[27:48] **Yusuf at Jotapata**

[事实] Jotapata was commanded by Yusuf, son of Matthew, a nobleman of priestly background.
[事实] After the Romans stormed the town, Yusuf later told a story that the defenders agreed to kill one another in sequence rather than surrender.
[事实] Yusuf and one other man were left, and they decided to surrender.
[事实] Yusuf was brought in chains before Vespasian and told him that he was destined to become Caesar.

[28:51] **Nero’s Fall and Vespasian’s Prophecy**

[事实] In summer AD 68, Nero was toppled and committed suicide.
[事实] The Roman Empire fell into civil war, and AD 69 became the Year of Four Emperors.
[事实] Vespasian emerged from that crisis as Caesar.
[事实] Yusuf’s chains were removed, and he was encouraged to publicize that he had predicted Vespasian’s rise.
[事实] Flavian propaganda cited Judean prophecy that a king would come from Judea and rule the world, applying it to Vespasian.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s main value is that it slows down a famous catastrophe and treats it as history before treating it as theology. Rather than beginning with AD 70 as an inevitable sacred rupture, it reconstructs the political, administrative, fiscal, and cultural pressures that led to revolt.

A strong feature is the attention to terminology. The distinction between “Jews” and “Judeans” is not just semantic; it supports the episode’s larger argument that the conflict can be misread if modern religious categories are projected backward too quickly.

The limitation is that this installment stops just as the war reaches its most dramatic phase: Vespasian’s rise, Jerusalem’s siege, the Temple’s destruction, and Masada are left for the next episode. [推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who want context and interpretation before battlefield narrative, rather than a standalone account of the full revolt.
