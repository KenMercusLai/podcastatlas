# Joan of Arc: From Reims to Rouen

## 概览

This episode follows Joan of Arc after the relief of Orléans, showing how her reputation turned from battlefield inspiration into a force capable of reshaping French politics.

The hosts trace Joan’s charisma through the Loire campaign, the march to Reims, and Charles VII’s coronation, arguing that she shattered English morale and made Charles’s legitimacy visible to Christendom.

The final movement shows the reversal: Paris fails, Joan’s role at court becomes uncertain, she goes freelance, is captured at Compiègne, and is handed toward the English-controlled machinery that will put her on trial.

## 分段落总结

[01:29] **Guy de Laval Sees Joan**

[事实] The episode opens with Guy de Laval’s June 8, 1429 letter describing Joan in white armor, riding with armed men and inspiring intense devotion.
[事实] The letter presents Joan as both martial and sacred, asking priests and church people to pray for the army.
[推测] The hosts use this scene to frame Joan as a figure whose image mattered almost as much as her military advice.

[03:25] **Orléans and the French Captains**

[事实] Orléans had been under English siege for six months before it was relieved.
[事实] French captains such as the Bastard of Orléans, La Hire, and Gilles de Rais played roles in the victory.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that Joan, not the veteran warlords, became the glamorous embodiment of the “miracle of Orléans.”

[06:24] **Chivalric Performance and Image**

[事实] Joan is described as having transformed from a peasant girl into a figure resembling a knight from chivalric romance.
[事实] The hosts connect her armor, banner, courage, and public display to the romances she likely heard while growing up.
[推测] Joan’s power is presented less as calculated propaganda than as an instinctive ability to inhabit a role others already found inspiring.

[09:21] **Gender, Virginity, and Holiness**

[事实] Joan did not claim to be male and consistently called herself “Joan the Maid.”
[事实] The hosts argue that she combined martial traits associated with men and spiritual authority associated with female virginity.
[事实] Testimony from her squire Jean d’Aulon is used to show that companions emphasized her attractiveness without sexual desire.
[推测] Her exceptionality came from standing outside ordinary gender expectations while still grounding her identity in holiness.

[13:43] **Orléans as Proof**

[事实] Some people were skeptical of Joan, but Orléans served as proof for many on the French side.
[事实] Joan did not generally claim to perform miracles beyond the success of her mission at Orléans.
[事实] The hosts say she focused on the mission given by her voices: relieve Orléans, crown Charles at Reims, and drive out the English.

[16:38] **Building the Reims Campaign**

[事实] Charles was still widely called the Dauphin because he had not yet been crowned at Reims.
[事实] After Orléans, around 5,000 men gathered by early June despite Charles’s empty treasury.
[事实] The Duke of Alençon was appointed commander, while Charles instructed him to act according to Joan’s advice.
[推测] Joan’s reputation temporarily solved a practical military problem by drawing unpaid support to Charles’s cause.

[19:02] **Loire Victories**

[事实] At Jargeau, French artillery and assault broke English resistance, and the Duke of Suffolk was captured.
[事实] A week later, John Talbot was captured in open battle, and around 2,000 Englishmen were killed.
[事实] These defeats damaged the English reputation for battlefield invincibility built since Agincourt.

[21:54] **The Risky Road to Reims**

[事实] Joan urged Charles to move roughly 130 miles north toward Reims through hostile territory without time for a proper supply train or artillery.
[事实] At Troyes, Joan said her voices promised entry within four days.
[事实] The town surrendered after seeing preparations for assault, including ditches filled with brushwood.
[推测] The hosts treat Troyes as a case where Joan’s mystique helped collapse Burgundian-aligned resistance before a battle began.

[24:49] **Charisma Versus Strategic Reality**

[事实] Dominic argues that France’s population and resources meant England was always overextended in the long run.
[事实] Tom accepts the structural point but stresses that Joan changed French morale and exposed the fragility of English and Burgundian control.
[推测] Joan’s historical importance lies in accelerating a reversal that may have been strategically likely but politically stalled.

[29:37] **Charles VII Crowned at Reims**

[事实] Charles entered Reims on July 16, 1429, and was crowned the next morning, July 17.
[事实] The holy oil from Saint-Rémy was used, though Charlemagne’s regalia were unavailable and a makeshift crown was produced.
[事实] Joan stood beside Charles with her white banner, then knelt and said God’s will was done.
[事实] Charles later rewarded Joan’s village with tax exemption and ennobled Joan and her family.

[35:26] **Joan Appeals to Burgundy**

[事实] On the day of the coronation, Joan wrote to Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, calling for peace with Charles VII.
[事实] She urged Christian reconciliation and suggested war should instead be directed against non-French enemies.
[事实] Philip had recently renewed the Anglo-Burgundian treaty but also opened negotiations with Charles.
[推测] Joan appears to have viewed Burgundy’s resistance as a spiritual error more than a conventional political calculation.

[39:49] **Bedford Blames Joan**

[事实] The Duke of Bedford faced a worsening English position: Orléans lost, major captains captured, Champagne weakened, and Charles crowned.
[事实] Bedford blamed Joan and regarded her as a witch whose influence had damaged English rule.
[事实] The hosts stress that English and Burgundian figures seem to have sincerely believed this, not merely used it cynically.
[推测] Blaming Joan also helped them avoid admitting deeper weaknesses in the English regime.

[42:58] **The Paris Failure**

[事实] In early September 1429, Joan and Alençon came before Paris, and Joan believed the city could be taken.
[事实] Charles VII was skeptical and did not fully support the attack.
[事实] On September 8, the assault failed; many French men were left dead or dying, Joan’s standard-bearer was hit, and Joan was wounded in the thigh.
[事实] The attack’s timing on the Virgin Mary’s birthday was later seen as damaging to Joan’s image of holiness.

[47:23] **A Mission Completed, a Role Unclear**

[事实] After Paris, Joan remained famous across Europe, but her position at Charles’s court became less secure.
[事实] She had already accomplished the two great tasks she had promised: Orléans and Reims.
[事实] Joan wanted to continue war in Normandy, while Charles faced money, strategy, and political limits.
[推测] The episode presents a widening gap between Joan’s chivalric vision and Charles’s administrative reality.

[49:28] **Joan Goes Freelance**

[事实] By March 1430, Joan raised a company of about 200 men, including her brother Pierre.
[事实] She moved through conflict zones around Paris, supporting French actions against Burgundians.
[事实] Some could see this as resembling the free companies that had plagued France.
[推测] Joan would likely have understood herself as continuing the war Charles and his captains should have fought.

[50:47] **Compiègne and Capture**

[事实] On May 23, 1430, Joan arrived at Compiègne, which was under Burgundian siege after switching allegiance to Charles.
[事实] Her voices told her to attack and capture the Duke of Burgundy.
[事实] The sortie failed, the retreat sounded, and Joan was trapped outside the town when the gates closed.
[事实] Burgundian soldiers pulled her from her horse, and she surrendered to a Burgundian captain.

[53:26] **Ransom and Abandonment**

[事实] Joan passed into the hands of John of Luxembourg, who could profit by ransoming or selling her.
[事实] Charles VII did not ransom her and avoided public comment on her capture.
[事实] The Archbishop of Reims presented Joan’s capture as punishment for pride after she had gone beyond her mission.
[推测] Charles may have seen Joan’s capture as politically dangerous and easier to leave unresolved.

[56:41] **Bedford and Cauchon Prepare a Trial**

[事实] Bedford wanted to turn Joan’s capture into a propaganda victory against Charles VII’s coronation.
[事实] Pierre Cauchon, Bishop of Beauvais, proposed that the English buy Joan and hand her to the church for trial.
[事实] Since Joan had been captured within Cauchon’s bishopric, he claimed jurisdiction.
[事实] The intended charges included heresy, sorcery, and demonic influence.

[60:14] **Toward Rouen**

[事实] When Joan learned she would be sold to the English, she threw herself from a 60-foot tower and survived with serious injuries.
[事实] Joan was formally delivered to the English in September 1430.
[事实] On December 23, 1430, she was brought in chains to Rouen under heavy guard.
[事实] The episode ends by noting that Joan had less than half a year to live.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its balance between narrative drama and political analysis: Joan is treated as a charismatic religious figure, a military catalyst, and a problem for every regime that tries to use her.

[事实] The hosts repeatedly test miraculous explanations against material realities such as money, manpower, English overextension, Burgundian politics, and Charles VII’s caution.

[推测] Its limitation is that some claims about motives, especially Charles’s relief at Joan’s capture or Joan’s enjoyment of command, are interpretive rather than directly provable from the events discussed.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in the Hundred Years’ War, medieval religion, political legitimacy, propaganda, and the making and unmaking of heroic reputations.
