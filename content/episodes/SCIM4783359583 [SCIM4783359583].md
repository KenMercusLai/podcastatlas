# Why Colds and Flu Rise in Winter

## 概览

This Huberman Lab AMA segment answers why colds and flus become more common in winter. The main answer is not a single cause: shorter days, colder temperatures, more indoor proximity, and drier heated air all contribute to greater transmission and susceptibility.

The discussion moves from environmental factors to behavioral and physiological defenses. Huberman emphasizes that close contact with sneezing or coughing people increases risk, and that nasal breathing plus sufficiently humidified air may help preserve the nasal passages as a first line of defense.

The episode also previews a future deeper discussion on colds, flus, cold exposure, deliberate breathing, and immune responses. Promotional material at the beginning and end explains the premium AMA channel and its role in funding human research on mental health, physical health, and performance.

[01:48] **Why Winter Has More Colds And Flus**

[事实] The first question asks whether more winter colds and flus are caused by temperature, indoor time, or whether the pattern is a myth.

[事实] Huberman states that research clearly shows greater prevalence of colds and flus in winter months.

[事实] He explains that winter generally means shorter days, longer nights, less sunlight, and colder temperatures in most parts of the world.

[推测] The answer frames winter illness as a combined environmental and behavioral problem rather than a simple effect of cold air alone.

[03:52] **Indoor Time And Physical Proximity**

[事实] Huberman identifies increased indoor time as one reason cold and flu transmission rises during colder, shorter days.

[事实] He says closer physical proximity increases the probability of catching a cold or flu from someone who is sneezing or coughing.

[事实] He describes studies examining transmission at different distances, including one foot, three feet, and six feet.

[事实] He adds that skin contact, saliva exchange, shared contact, and insufficient handwashing after wiping the nose or face can increase transmission.

[06:32] **Contagiousness During Symptoms**

[事实] Huberman warns against claims that someone who is sneezing or coughing from a cold or flu is “not contagious.”

[事实] He says the data indicate that when people are sneezing and coughing because of a cold or flu, they are still contagious.

[推测] This point is used to support practical distancing and hygiene decisions when someone is visibly symptomatic.

[07:49] **Heated Indoor Air And Dry Nasal Passages**

[事实] Huberman says heated indoor rooms in winter tend to have drier air.

[事实] He explains that both cold outdoor air and heated indoor air can contribute to dryness, especially in the nasal passages.

[事实] He identifies the nasal passages as a primary defense site against viral infections such as colds and flus, as well as bacterial and fungal infections.

[09:43] **Nasal Breathing As A Defense**

[事实] Huberman says nasal breathing is generally preferable when a person is not eating, speaking, or exercising hard enough to require mouth breathing.

[事实] He explains that nose hairs, mucus, and the nasal microbiome help trap or defend against incoming viruses, bacteria, and fungal threats.

[事实] He says maintaining the mucosal lining of the nasal passages is important for keeping this defense intact.

[11:46] **Humidifying Air**

[事实] Huberman suggests that people who spend a lot of time indoors and suffer from colds and flus may benefit from using an air humidifier.

[事实] He especially emphasizes keeping air sufficiently humidified at night while sleeping.

[事实] He says humidification will not stop a sick person from transmitting illness, but may help keep the nasal passages and respiratory pathways healthier and more resistant.

[推测] The practical takeaway is to treat indoor air quality as part of winter illness prevention, not only as a comfort issue.

[13:28] **Sleep, Severity, And Immune Nuance**

[事实] Huberman says people are exposed to viral, fungal, and bacterial threats throughout the day, and the immune system counters them through multiple mechanisms.

[事实] He notes uncertainty around why a mild sniffle sometimes does not become a severe cold or flu.

[事实] He says lack of sleep can make sickness feel much worse and can shift symptoms from mild toward more severe.

[事实] He also says some cold and flu strains are inherently milder than others.

[14:12] **Mouth Breathing And Illness Risk**

[事实] Huberman says people who deliberately breathe through the nose tend to get fewer colds and flus, while mouth breathers tend to get more.

[事实] He cites a paper titled “Association of Oral Breathing with Dental Malocclusions and General Health in Children.”

[事实] He says the paper links oral breathing with higher prevalence of allergies and greater likelihood of getting sick and taking medication for sickness.

[事实] He says nasal breathing is generally better for health, aesthetics, and dental health than mouth breathing when nasal breathing is possible.

[15:57] **Distance, Hygiene, And Sick Etiquette**

[事实] Huberman recommends paying attention to whether people nearby are coughing or sneezing indoors.

[事实] He suggests keeping distance from symptomatic people and encouraging handwashing after they wipe their nose.

[事实] He says he considers it impolite for someone who knows they are sick to attend an event and get others sick.

[推测] The discussion connects infection prevention with social responsibility, not just individual self-protection.

[17:04] **Outdoor Winter Exercise And Rehydrating Airways**

[事实] Huberman says proximity still matters outdoors, and someone sneezing next to you outside can still transmit a cold or flu.

[事实] He says indoor transmission at the same distance may have a slightly higher probability than outdoor transmission.

[事实] He recommends trying to nasal breathe outdoors during winter exercise or conversation unless mouth breathing is necessary.

[事实] He says if cold outdoor air dries the nasal or oral passages, a hot shower, steam room, or humidifier may help rehydrate them afterward.

[19:21] **Core Temperature, Fever, And Cold Exposure**

[事实] Huberman says there appears to be a relationship between core body temperature and susceptibility to cold and flu.

[事实] He explains that fever is an immune adaptation intended to heat up and destroy viruses.

[事实] He cautions that very high fever can be dangerous to the brain and body.

[事实] He raises the question of whether cold plunges or cold showers are appropriate during winter or when someone is coming down with a cold.
