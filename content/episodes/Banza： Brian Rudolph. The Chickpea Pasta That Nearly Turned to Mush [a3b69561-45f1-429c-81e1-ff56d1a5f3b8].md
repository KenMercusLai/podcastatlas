# Banza: The Chickpea Pasta That Nearly Turned to Mush

## 概览

Brian Rudolph explains how a personal sensitivity to wheat led him to experiment with chickpea flour and eventually create Banza, a high-protein, high-fiber, gluten-free pasta brand. With no culinary or manufacturing background, he developed the earliest recipes in his apartment before recruiting his older brother Scott as co-founder.

The company gained early funding, television exposure, and major retail orders before it had mastered large-scale production. Its first manufacturing partner produced pasta that dissolved into mush, forcing the founders to devise emergency cooking instructions and manually relabel 20,000 boxes just to survive the launch.

Banza subsequently improved its product, built a distinctive orange brand, expanded manufacturing capacity, and grew into tens of thousands of stores. Its most serious later setback came from a viral report alleging high glyphosate levels, which immediately reduced sales and exposed weaknesses in the company’s crisis response.

Brian concludes that Banza’s progress resulted from deliberately creating more opportunities for luck: entering competitions, appearing on television, contacting creators, and repeatedly placing the company where unexpected breakthroughs could happen.

## 分段落总结

[01:17] **A healthier alternative to conventional pasta**

[事实] Banza was introduced as the first commercially available pasta made from chickpeas, which provide protein and fiber while being naturally gluten-free.

[事实] Brian began experimenting in 2013 because wheat appeared to aggravate his recurring sinus inflammation, not because he initially intended to capitalize on the growing demand for protein.

[事实] Five years after launching, Banza had become the fastest-growing pasta brand in the United States; by the time of the interview, it was sold in more than 25,000 stores.

[03:37] **Learning entrepreneurship in Detroit**

[事实] After college, Brian joined Venture for America and moved to Detroit, where he became the first employee at a technology startup called Quickly.

[事实] His work ranged from customer service to making approximately 60 cold contacts per day, giving him broad exposure to startup operations.

[事实] Before focusing on food, he considered several other business concepts but questioned whether they would hold his interest for a decade.

[05:37] **A health problem redirects his attention to food**

[事实] Brian had previously regarded food mainly as fuel and did not consider himself a skilled cook.

[事实] Following repeated sinus infections and courses of antibiotics and steroids, he tried an elimination diet at his father’s suggestion.

[事实] After avoiding wheat for about a month, he reintroduced it through beer and experienced severe nasal congestion, reinforcing his belief that wheat contributed to his symptoms.

[事实] His physician could not confirm the connection but advised him to continue the diet if it helped.

[08:02] **Choosing chickpeas as the foundation of a business**

[事实] Brian experimented with protein-rich breads, ice creams, and alternative pastas because he was dissatisfied with the gluten-free pasta then available.

[事实] A conversation about whether he could remain committed to an idea for ten years prompted him to focus on food.

[事实] Inspired by Chobani’s transformation of the yogurt category, he set a goal of doing something comparable in pasta.

[事实] Chickpeas appealed to him because they offered protein, fiber, antioxidants, and environmental advantages relative to many other protein sources.

[10:22] **Developing pasta in an apartment kitchen**

[事实] Brian bought chickpea flour online and tested combinations involving eggs, chia, tapioca, and xanthan gum to compensate for the absence of gluten.

[事实] His first chickpea, egg, and chia dough was sticky and produced poor pasta; removing chia improved it, but he continued searching for a vegan formulation that more people could eat.

[事实] He initially rolled the dough with a wine bottle and cut it with a knife before spending about $30 on a hand-cranked pasta machine.

[事实] He routinely woke at 5 a.m. to make pasta before work, and his boss’s assessment that one batch was “okay” encouraged him to continue.

[13:42] **Recruiting a co-founder and establishing the brand**

[事实] Brian asked his older brother Scott, whom he had initially approached for advice, to become his co-founder.

[事实] The name Banza emerged from wordplay involving “garbanzo” and sounded sufficiently similar to “pasta” to suggest a new product category.

[事实] A crowdfunding campaign raised $17,000 and helped the company obtain another $10,000 from Venture for America.

[事实] Banza subsequently received an invitation to appear on the CNBC program Restaurant Startup before it had begun selling its product.

[16:47] **Television exposure opens the first retail doors**

[事实] Brian and Scott accepted the television opportunity because it offered rapid feedback from chefs and investors, even though a poor appearance could have damaged the company.

[事实] Investor and restaurateur Joe Bastianich backed the concept with $75,000 and offered Banza access to Eataly.

[事实] A separate pitch competition led to an introduction to Meijer’s gluten-free buyer, despite Banza losing the competition itself.

[事实] Brian arrived at the Meijer meeting with pasta in a bag and no finished packaging, expecting advice rather than an order; the buyer instead requested a chain-wide launch in 190 stores.

[20:30] **Scaling production turns the pasta into mush**

[事实] A small Michigan manufacturer helped Banza develop the product but could produce only about 150–200 pounds per hour.

[事实] The founders moved production to a much larger gluten-free pasta manufacturer in California that had experience with rice and corn but not beans.

[事实] At the larger facility, production increased to roughly 1,000 pounds per hour, but pasta that looked acceptable while dry became mush when boiled.

[事实] Multiple failed runs of approximately 8,000–15,000 pounds consumed much of the company’s limited capital.

[事实] Exhausted, dehydrated, and unable to sleep properly, Brian suffered a kidney stone during what the founders believed might be their final production attempt.

[26:46] **An emergency workaround saves the initial order**

[事实] Brian discovered that plant operators thought they were processing corn, revealing serious communication failures at the manufacturing facility.

[事实] With money running out and a distributor warning that an unfulfilled order could end Banza’s retail relationship, the company felt compelled to ship approximately 20,000 boxes.

[事实] The founders found that steeping the pasta in hot water with the heat turned off kept it from falling apart, although Brian still considered the product disappointing.

[事实] Because the packaging had already been printed, workers manually applied stickers instructing customers to “steep it like tea.”

[事实] After fulfilling the order, Banza returned to the smaller Michigan manufacturer, where shorter production runs made experimentation and adjustment easier.

[30:58] **Publicity and nutritional differentiation build demand**

[事实] Restaurant Startup aired as Banza entered 192 stores, generating enough interest to crash the company’s website.

[事实] The program communicated Banza’s core claims: more protein and fiber than conventional pasta, along with gluten-free and vegan positioning.

[事实] The product still had a noticeable chickpea flavor, especially with lighter sauces, but red sauce often made the experience closer to conventional pasta.

[事实] Customers accepted some early product flaws because Banza addressed an unmet need for nutrient-dense pasta.

[34:17] **Packaging turns an unfamiliar product into a visible brand**

[事实] Farmers-market feedback showed that leading with “chickpeas” or prominently emphasizing “gluten-free” could discourage shoppers who assumed the product was not for them.

[事实] Banza instead emphasized protein and fiber to present the pasta as broadly beneficial rather than narrowly restrictive.

[事实] Brian chose orange partly because it was his favorite color and partly because it sharply contrasted with the blue packaging common throughout the pasta aisle.

[事实] The distinctive orange box helped Banza stand out and became central to the brand’s identity.

[36:19] **Manufacturing capacity and retail expansion**

[事实] By 2015, Banza’s small manufacturing partner was operating near capacity but was unwilling to invest in another production line.

[事实] A manager who had previously expelled Brian from the California plant later proposed starting his own facility; despite their difficult history, Brian reopened the relationship.

[事实] Banza raised its first seed round and partnered with that operator to establish additional manufacturing capacity after other options proved inadequate.

[事实] Retail displays repeatedly lifted sales to higher baselines, helping the company demonstrate momentum to investors.

[39:13] **Target and social media accelerate national growth**

[事实] Target initially tested Banza in a few hundred stores, and the company closely monitored and supported the rollout.

[事实] Banza used social media early, contacted creators who posted appealing recipes, and sent them products to try.

[事实] The company eventually claimed more social-media followers than any other pasta brand, which also strengthened its conversations with retailers.

[事实] By 2022, Banza was in approximately 21,000 stores and had expanded into pizza, macaroni and cheese, and rice products.

[41:54] **A viral glyphosate report creates a crisis**

[事实] In 2024, the advocacy organization Moms Across America published test results alleging high glyphosate levels in Banza pasta without first contacting the company.

[事实] Brian learned of the report through a Google alert and immediately anticipated serious reputational harm.

[事实] Banza was surprised because most of its supply chain was traceable and the farmers it knew were not applying glyphosate.

[事实] Influencers amplified the allegation, and Brian said later versions circulating online became even more extreme than the original claim.

[45:36] **Fear, falling sales, and delayed answers**

[事实] Banza’s sales fell 30% from one week to the next after the report spread.

[事实] Consumers, including people who regularly served Banza to their children, contacted Brian in distress and struggled to contextualize the alleged exposure.

[事实] The episode affected Brian physically and emotionally: he described shaking when new videos appeared, walking continuously from stress, and losing about ten pounds.

[事实] Banza quickly posted that it was investigating, but independent laboratory results were repeatedly delayed and took approximately three weeks.

[48:25] **Testing and supply-chain changes**

[事实] Banza’s published testing found glyphosate levels 98% lower than those claimed in the original report, though the result was not zero.

[事实] Brian said the detected level was below the Environmental Working Group’s risk benchmark, which he characterized as highly conservative.

[事实] The company moved to ensure that it knew every farmer in its supply chain or, where direct traceability was unavailable, used extensive testing to verify growing practices.

[事实] Banza achieved its intended supply-chain compliance within a few weeks.

[49:58] **Using transparency to rebuild trust**

[事实] Banza worked with the Detox Project on certifications covering glyphosate and a standardized panel of more than 400 pesticides.

[事实] The program required public test results, and Banza added a QR code to its packaging through which customers could access those results.

[事实] Brian acknowledged that pesticide drift from neighboring fields can occur and committed the company to publishing positive findings, investigating their causes, and correcting them.

[事实] He concluded that Banza should have communicated faster and more visibly while its investigation was underway, even before definitive test results arrived.

[52:07] **Lessons from crisis communication**

[事实] Brian’s advice to other companies is to communicate extensively, provide an earnest assessment, explain what they care about, and state what they intend to do.

[事实] Recovery was uneven: sales in some accounts became stronger than before, while natural-food retailers recovered much more slowly.

[事实] Brian chose to assume positive intent on the part of Moms Across America, believing its team was attempting to protect consumers.

[事实] He personally monitors online references to Banza and glyphosate and responds with additional context, despite finding the work exhausting.

[54:51] **Refocusing on the core product**

[事实] The host stated that Banza had become the fifth-largest pasta brand in the United States.

[事实] The company expanded into wheat-based protein pasta, frozen pizza, protein waffles, macaroni and cheese, and other products.

[事实] Despite this diversification and growing competition from established and emerging brands, Brian identified chickpea pasta as Banza’s most important product and core focus.

[推测] His emphasis on doing less suggests that disciplined focus has become more important as the company’s product opportunities have multiplied.

[56:26] **Creating opportunities for luck**

[事实] Brian credited both favorable market trends and deliberate effort for Banza’s growth.

[事实] He described the company’s approach as doing everything possible to increase the likelihood of luck, including accepting uncertain opportunities such as reality television.

[事实] His final lesson was that founders cannot control tailwinds, but they can repeatedly place themselves in situations where a tailwind is more likely to appear.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode is particularly valuable as a candid account of bringing a novel food product from a home kitchen to industrial production. Its strongest material concerns the gap between a workable prototype and a scalable product: machinery, operator knowledge, run size, cash constraints, and retailer deadlines all mattered as much as the recipe itself.

The story also shows how differentiation can compensate temporarily for an imperfect product. Banza combined a clear nutritional benefit with memorable packaging, strong media exposure, and persistent outreach, giving early customers reasons to tolerate shortcomings while the company improved its pasta.

The glyphosate discussion adds a useful second dimension: operational success does not protect a consumer brand from a sudden trust crisis. Brian openly describes Banza’s slow initial communication, the emotional impact of public accusations, and the long-term work required to rebuild credibility through traceability, testing, and published results.

[推测] The account is primarily presented from the founder’s perspective and does not independently assess the competing laboratory methods or health claims. It is therefore most useful as a case study in entrepreneurship, manufacturing, branding, and crisis management rather than as a definitive scientific evaluation of pesticide risk.
