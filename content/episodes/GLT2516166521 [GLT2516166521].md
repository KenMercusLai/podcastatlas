# King Arthur: The Once and Future King

## 概览

This episode explores the enduring mystery of King Arthur’s death and promised return. Taking Sir Thomas Malory’s *Le Morte Darthur* as its narrative centre, the hosts trace Arthur’s final battle with Mordred, the return of Excalibur to the lake, and the wounded king’s voyage to Avalon.

The discussion then moves from legend to textual history. Early Welsh sources mention Arthur and Medraut falling at Camlann, while Geoffrey of Monmouth supplied many elements that later became canonical, including Mordred’s betrayal and Avalon as Arthur’s destination.

The episode also examines how Arthurian mythology served political purposes. Glastonbury Abbey claimed to discover Arthur’s grave, while Edward I used the supposed remains to appropriate Arthur as a model for English rule over a unified Britain.

Finally, Malory’s story is placed against the Wars of the Roses. His depiction of a kingdom destroyed by betrayal, shifting loyalties and civil war is presented as a reflection on the political violence of fifteenth-century England.

## 分段落总结

[02:01] **The Once and Future King**

[事实] The episode opens with Malory’s ambiguous account of Arthur’s fate: some say he died, while others believe he was taken elsewhere and will return.

[事实] Arthur belongs to a broader tradition of rulers expected to awaken in a national crisis, alongside Frederick Barbarossa, Constantine XI and King Sebastian of Portugal.

[事实] By the Middle Ages, Arthur’s fame extended across Christendom and inspired literature, sculpture and tournaments in which participants dressed as Knights of the Round Table.

[推测] Arthur’s exceptional longevity as a legend stems partly from the unresolved tension between his apparent death and the hope of his return.

[11:39] **Arthur as a Medieval Moral and Literary Ideal**

[事实] In Jacques de Longuyon’s list of the Nine Worthies, Arthur appears first among the three exemplary Christian heroes.

[事实] William Caxton published Malory’s *Le Morte Darthur* after introducing the printing press to England, helping establish a unified Arthurian narrative for English-speaking readers.

[事实] The hosts connect Arthurian motifs to later popular stories, including *Harry Potter*, *Star Wars* and *The Lord of the Rings*.

[事实] Arthurian literature circulated in numerous European languages and was more widely discussed abroad than in England, according to Caxton.

[推测] The cultural importance of Arthur lies less in whether a historical Arthur existed than in the extraordinary influence of the legends built around him.

[19:25] **Excalibur, the Lady of the Lake and Morgan le Fay**

[事实] In Malory’s account, Arthur proves his legitimacy by drawing the sword from the stone and later receives Excalibur from the Lady of the Lake.

[事实] The Lady also gives Arthur a magical scabbard that prevents its wearer from losing blood, making it even more valuable than the sword.

[事实] Morgan le Fay steals Excalibur and its scabbard, gives them to her lover Accolon, and later throws the recovered scabbard into a lake.

[事实] Arthur retains Excalibur but loses the protection that could have prevented him from bleeding to death.

[推测] The loss of the scabbard establishes Arthur’s mortality early in the story and foreshadows his eventual fatal wound.

[24:27] **Mordred’s Birth and Betrayal**

[事实] Arthur unknowingly sleeps with his half-sister Morgause, and Merlin foretells that their son Mordred will destroy Arthur and his knights.

[事实] Arthur responds by sending children born on May Day to sea, but Mordred alone survives the resulting shipwreck.

[事实] As an adult, Mordred is accepted at Arthur’s court and becomes a Knight of the Round Table despite Merlin’s warning.

[事实] While Arthur campaigns in France, Mordred falsely reports his death, summons Parliament and has himself crowned king.

[事实] A peace meeting on Salisbury Plain collapses when a knight draws his sword against an adder, prompting both armies to attack.

[29:54] **The Last Battle and the Return of Excalibur**

[事实] Malory describes the final battle as lasting all day and leaving only Arthur, Lucan, Bedivere and Mordred alive among the principal figures.

[事实] Arthur kills Mordred with a spear, but Mordred forces himself farther along the weapon and deals Arthur a mortal wound.

[事实] Arthur orders Bedivere to throw Excalibur into a lake; Bedivere twice conceals the sword before finally obeying.

[事实] A hand rises from the water, catches and brandishes Excalibur, then disappears beneath the surface.

[事实] A barge carrying mysterious women—including Morgan le Fay and the Lady of the Lake—takes Arthur toward Avalon to have his wound treated.

[推测] Malory’s refusal to explain Morgan’s changed role or Arthur’s ultimate condition preserves the supernatural mystery of the ending.

[41:20] **Camlann in the Earliest Welsh Tradition**

[事实] The mid-tenth-century *Annales Cambriae* records that Arthur and Medraut fell at the Battle of Camlann in 537.

[事实] The entry does not identify the combatants’ relationship, explain who won, or specify where Camlann was located.

[事实] Later Welsh poetry contains early indications of rivalry between Arthur and Medraut.

[事实] Proposed locations for Camlann include Slaughter Bridge in Cornwall, Cadbury Castle in Somerset, Camlan in Gwynedd and Camboglanna near Hadrian’s Wall.

[推测] The available evidence is too sparse and ambiguous to establish the battlefield’s location or even confirm the historical event behind the tradition.

[44:26] **Geoffrey of Monmouth Creates an Arthurian World**

[事实] Geoffrey of Monmouth’s twelfth-century *History of the Kings of Britain* offered the first full-length account of Arthur’s reign and became an exceptional medieval bestseller.

[事实] Geoffrey made Mordred Arthur’s nephew, depicted him as regent during Arthur’s continental campaign, and developed the story of his seizure of the throne.

[事实] Geoffrey identified the last battle with a location beside the River Camel in Cornwall.

[事实] He also introduced Avalon as the island to which the mortally wounded Arthur was carried for treatment.

[事实] Geoffrey never states whether Arthur was healed or where Avalon was located.

[推测] Geoffrey’s strategic ambiguities allowed later writers and institutions to expand the legend while claiming connections to it.

[50:08] **Glastonbury Claims Arthur’s Grave**

[事实] A destructive fire at Glastonbury Abbey in 1184 eliminated an important pilgrimage site and created an urgent need for rebuilding funds.

[事实] In 1191, the monks claimed to discover the bones of an enormous man and a lead cross identifying the burial as that of King Arthur in Avalon.

[事实] The discovery allowed Glastonbury to identify itself with Avalon and promote the abbey as the resting place of Europe’s most famous legendary king.

[事实] The monks also claimed that bones found beside Arthur belonged to Queen Guinevere.

[推测] The timing and financial benefits strongly suggest that the alleged discovery served the abbey’s institutional and economic interests.

[53:49] **Edward I’s Political Appropriation of Arthur**

[事实] In 1278, Edward I and Eleanor of Castile presided over the reinterment of the supposed remains of Arthur and Guinevere at Glastonbury Abbey.

[事实] Edward could portray himself as heir to an ancient king said to have ruled a united Britain rather than merely as a descendant of Norman conquerors.

[事实] This ceremony occurred amid Edward’s campaigns against Llywelyn ap Gruffudd and before the formal annexation of Wales.

[事实] Displaying Arthur’s body also challenged Welsh, Cornish and Breton traditions that Arthur remained alive and would return.

[事实] Later English kings continued to use Arthurian imagery, Round Table tournaments and chivalric orders to support royal ambitions.

[推测] By possessing and reburying Arthur’s supposed remains, Edward simultaneously neutralised a symbol of Welsh resistance and absorbed that symbol into English royal ideology.

[60:14] **Malory and the Wars of the Roses**

[事实] Malory completed *Le Morte Darthur* around 1469–1470, during renewed instability in the Wars of the Roses.

[事实] The author identifies himself as a prisoner, and the prevailing scholarly view associates him with Sir Thomas Malory of Newbold Revel.

[事实] Malory was imprisoned under both Lancastrian and Yorkist governments and was specifically excluded from several general pardons issued by Edward IV.

[事实] His account gives Mordred distinctly fifteenth-century institutions and weapons, including Parliament, the Tower of London and artillery.

[事实] Malory condemns the English for fickle loyalties and describes wounded survivors being murdered and robbed after the final battle.

[推测] Malory’s ruined Round Table functions as a commentary on the coups, betrayals and shifting allegiances of his own lifetime.

[67:03] **Why the Hope of Arthur’s Return Endures**

[事实] Malory presents the fall of Arthur’s realm as the result not only of Mordred’s betrayal but also of wider political instability and changeable public loyalties.

[事实] Despite the claimed discovery of Arthur’s grave and his appropriation by English monarchs, Malory preserves the possibility that Arthur may return.

[事实] The episode concludes by previewing further discussions of Lancelot, Guinevere, Merlin and the possible historical Arthur.

[推测] In an age of civil war, the idea of a just king returning to restore order would have carried particular emotional and political force.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s chief strength is its movement between literary storytelling, medieval source criticism and political history. Malory’s dramatic narrative is presented vividly, but the hosts repeatedly distinguish it from the sparse and ambiguous evidence of the earlier Welsh tradition.

Another highlight is the explanation of how legends become instruments of power. The discussions of Glastonbury Abbey and Edward I show that arguments about Arthur’s death, burial and nationality carried financial and imperial consequences.

[推测] The conversational humour makes a complicated body of medieval material approachable, although frequent digressions and modern comparisons occasionally interrupt the historical argument. Some listeners may also want a clearer separation between what Geoffrey invented, what Malory inherited and what the earliest sources can genuinely support.

This episode is especially suitable for listeners interested in Arthurian literature, medieval Britain, political mythology and the transformation of cultural memory. Its central conclusion is persuasive: the uncertainty surrounding Arthur is not a weakness in the legend but one of the main reasons it has endured.
