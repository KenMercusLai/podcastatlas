# Omission accomplished: why the Iran-war cycle spins on

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* opens with the renewed America-Iran conflict and the failure of a June memorandum of understanding to produce either a durable truce or a broader settlement. The discussion argues that the Hormuz dispute shows a larger weakness in Donald Trump’s Middle East diplomacy: vague term sheets leave core conflicts unresolved.

The second segment examines how Chinese companies are pushing AI services abroad, especially driverless taxis. The episode frames overseas expansion as both a business opportunity and a strategic route for China to shape future technical standards.

The final segment remembers Wally Funk, an aviation pioneer who passed astronaut tests in the early space age but was excluded from the official astronaut corps because she was a woman. At 82, she finally reached space aboard Blue Origin, fulfilling a lifelong ambition.

## 分段落总结

[01:23] **America, Iran and the Strait of Hormuz**

[事实] The host says the Strait of Hormuz has moved between open and closed status, with Gulf states trying to broker a temporary reopening.
[事实] Greg Carlstrom says Oman proposed a formal truce of about ten days and a joint regional consortium to manage Hormuz without mandatory tolls.
[事实] Iran rejected the proposal and demanded complete control over inbound traffic into the Persian Gulf.
[推测] The failed Hormuz talks are presented as a test case for why narrower compromises are proving difficult before larger diplomatic issues are even reached.

[04:13] **The June MOU is already fraying**

[事实] Carlstrom says the MOU stated that America and Iran were permanently ending their war, but fighting resumed after about three weeks.
[事实] The two sides are disputing frozen Iranian funds, with Iran expecting immediate release and America insisting on phased access after compliance.
[事实] The MOU set a 60-day period, ending August 16th, to negotiate a comprehensive agreement on Iran’s nuclear programme, sanctions relief and other long-standing issues.
[事实] Carlstrom says high-level face-to-face talks have barely begun and that a broader agreement in the remaining time is not realistic.

[05:52] **Why the conflict cycle keeps spinning**

[事实] Carlstrom says diplomacy is not working and neither America nor Iran has a decisive way to end the conflict.
[事实] He says further American airstrikes could degrade Iran’s ballistic missile capability but would not stop drones, short-range missiles or force capitulation.
[事实] He says continued economic pressure from blockading Iranian ports may affect Iran, but only over months, and Tehran has endured long economic pain before.
[事实] Iran could escalate attacks on Gulf states, other theatres and the global economy, but Carlstrom doubts that would force America to accept Iran’s preferred terms.
[推测] The segment’s central conclusion is that both sides need compromise, but neither currently appears willing to make one.

[08:03] **A broader pattern in Trump’s Middle East diplomacy**

[事实] Carlstrom describes the MOU as 14 points across a page and a half, with little substance.
[事实] He compares it with unresolved agreements involving Saudi Arabia and the Houthis in Yemen, Gaza, and Lebanon.
[事实] He says the Houthis have announced a blockade of Saudi shipping in the Red Sea and attacked Saudi airports and energy infrastructure.
[事实] He says Gaza and Lebanon agreements also leave major questions unanswered, including Hamas disarmament, Israeli withdrawal and Hezbollah’s status.
[推测] The episode argues that vague agreements may create the appearance of diplomatic progress while postponing the hard details that determine whether conflict actually ends.

[11:42] **China’s driverless taxis go abroad**

[事实] Don Weinland says that a driverless cab outside China or the United States is likely to use Chinese technology.
[事实] He says Baidu is involved in a Switzerland project due to launch early next year, and similar services may appear in Singapore, Slovakia and Saudi Arabia.
[事实] He says Chinese firms are moving overseas quickly in AI services, while American rivals such as Waymo are more focused on the American market.
[推测] Driverless taxis are used as the clearest example of China exporting AI as a service rather than simply exporting manufactured goods.

[13:24] **Why Chinese AI may be worth more overseas**

[事实] Weinland says China has too many cab drivers, making driverless taxi services harder to turn into profitable businesses at home.
[事实] He compares a 10km cab ride in Wuhan at about 23 yuan, or $3.40, with a similar ride in Switzerland costing $54.
[事实] He says overseas markets with higher fares and fewer drivers create a stronger value proposition for Chinese driverless taxi companies.
[推测] The economic logic is that Chinese AI services can earn more abroad than in China’s lower-cost, more crowded domestic market.

[16:01] **Exports, disruption and standards**

[事实] Weinland says China’s government is happy to see more exports, though services exports and AI services exports remain small.
[事实] He says exporting AI services could let China gain benefits from AI while pushing some labour-market disruption abroad.
[事实] He says first movers can shape technical standards where countries have not yet developed rules for technologies such as driverless taxis.
[事实] He warns that if Chinese standards are adopted first, later American entrants may have to conform to them.
[推测] The strategic concern is that China could influence the global architecture of AI services before American firms expand internationally at scale.

[19:30] **Wally Funk’s lifelong drive to fly**

[事实] Ann Wroe describes Wally Funk as someone who rarely obeyed instructions when they conflicted with what she wanted to do.
[事实] Funk wanted to fly from childhood, jumped from a barn roof with a Superman cape at five, had her first flight at nine, and earned a pilot’s licence by 17.
[事实] At college she performed well in aviation activities including rolls, turns, spins and races.
[推测] The portrait emphasizes Funk’s persistence and independence as central to her aviation career.

[20:36] **Excluded from the astronaut corps**

[事实] Funk joined Dr William Randolph Lovelace’s programme testing women for space flight at age 21.
[事实] The women took the same tests as men, including invasive medical tests and a sensory-deprivation tank.
[事实] Funk stayed in the tank for 10 hours and 35 minutes, far longer than most people, and Wroe says she did better overall than the men.
[事实] Funk and the other women did not become astronauts because men in the official space programme got “cold feet about girls.”
[推测] The segment frames Funk’s exclusion as a result of gender barriers rather than lack of ability.

[22:21] **Aviation work after the setback**

[事实] Funk worked for the Federal Aviation Administration, instructing pilots and inspecting airplanes.
[事实] She later joined the National Transportation Safety Board to investigate air crashes, sometimes reaching crash sites by mule or by abseiling off cliffs.
[事实] Wroe says Funk was the first woman in both of those jobs.
[事实] Funk also set up a flight school in Taos, New Mexico.
[推测] Her later career shows that being blocked from space did not end her role as a pioneer in aviation.

[23:26] **Reaching space at 82**

[事实] Funk continued pursuing space-related experiences, including centrifuge training in California and training at the Yuri Gagarin Training Center in Moscow.
[事实] Jeff Bezos invited her to fly as his guest on a Blue Origin rocket when she was 82.
[事实] The trip lasted ten minutes and reached 62 miles above Earth.
[事实] Funk became the oldest person to go into space at the time, and Wroe says she remains the oldest woman to have gone to space.
[推测] The episode presents the Blue Origin flight as a late but meaningful vindication of Funk’s lifelong ambition.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is strongest when it connects immediate news to broader patterns. The Iran segment does not just recount fighting and negotiations; it uses the failed MOU to explain why thin diplomatic frameworks can collapse when core terms remain unresolved.

The China AI segment is valuable because it links business incentives, domestic labour-market pressures and geopolitical standards-setting in one compact discussion. Its limitation is that it focuses mostly on driverless taxis, so wider AI-service exports are mentioned more briefly.

The Wally Funk obituary gives the episode a human closing note, contrasting institutional exclusion with individual persistence. [推测] The episode is especially suited to listeners who want concise international analysis with a mix of geopolitics, technology and biographical storytelling.
