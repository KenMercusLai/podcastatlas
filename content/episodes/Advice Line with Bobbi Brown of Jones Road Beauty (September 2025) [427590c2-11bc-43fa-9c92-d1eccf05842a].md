# Advice Line with Bobbi Brown of Jones Road Beauty

## 概览

Guy Raz is joined by Bobbi Brown, founder of Bobbi Brown Cosmetics and Jones Road Beauty, to advise three founders on scaling without losing what makes their businesses work.

The discussion centers on scrappy growth, founder-led storytelling, product focus, and the risk of expanding too quickly into expensive channels. Brown repeatedly emphasizes testing small, protecting what already works, and using authentic customer connection before chasing scale.

The callers include a cabbage-based pain relief balm, a custom phone case brand powered by live social selling, and a natural mouth rinse originally positioned for coffee drinkers. Across all three, the advice is to widen awareness while preserving trust, community, and product clarity.

[00:19] **Archive Episode Introduction**

[事实] Guy Raz says the team is taking a break and has selected a favorite episode from the archives.
[事实] The show invites founders to call or send a voice memo with a one-minute message about their business challenge.

[01:38] **Bobbi Brown Returns To The Show**

[事实] Guy introduces Bobbi Brown as the founder of Bobbi Brown Cosmetics and Jones Road Beauty.
[事实] He recalls her original 2018 episode, including her role in popularizing a more understated, natural makeup look.
[事实] Brown says she launched Jones Road in 2020 on the day her 25-year non-compete expired.
[事实] Brown says she wanted to improve clean makeup textures and felt constrained inside a large corporation.

[04:54] **Starting Again And Staying Scrappy**

[事实] Brown says launching Jones Road was not optional for her because she believed the products were simple, better, and something people would love.
[事实] She considered starting with something as small as “the best brown eyeshadow” on Etsy before realizing there was another way to build the company.
[事实] Brown says she did things differently the second time, avoided unnecessary spending, and liked being in charge.
[推测] Her advice frames entrepreneurship less as a fixed playbook and more as a process of testing, conserving resources, and staying close to the product.

[06:13] **Making A Trend Last**

[事实] Guy asks how founders can turn something that might look like a fad into something with longevity.
[事实] Brown says founders must keep paying attention to the product, make small or large fixes when needed, and ensure it remains the best thing on the market.
[事实] She says repeat purchases, word of mouth, and occasional new products all matter.
[推测] Brown’s view is that staying power comes from product quality and customer behavior, not from simply riding a trend.

[08:04] **Cab Dab: Scaling Beyond One Doctor’s Trust**

[事实] Mark Sokolowski from Chicago introduces Cabbage Labs, maker of Cab Dab, a muscle and joint rub made with cabbage leaf extract.
[事实] Mark says he is an orthopedic surgeon and learned from patients who used cabbage leaf wraps for pain and inflammation.
[事实] He says the business has been profitable since day one, has sold or distributed 30,000 tubes, and has another 10,000 tubes ordered.
[事实] His question is how to scale beyond personally giving the product to patients.

[11:24] **Advice For Cab Dab: PR, Samples, And Local Evangelism**

[事实] Brown compares Mark’s free product sampling to her early practice of giving away lipsticks.
[事实] She recommends getting more people to know the story through PR, local media, social content, and a resourceful marketing hire.
[事实] Brown says Mark should stop giving away full-size tubes and instead create small samples with a card and discount.
[事实] Guy suggests exploring trusted distribution channels such as chiropractors, medical communities, conferences, and free trials.
[推测] The core advice is to preserve the high-trust trial experience while making it cheaper and repeatable.

[14:49] **Balancing A Medical Career And A Side Business**

[事实] Guy notes that Mark is a busy orthopedic surgeon and asks how he should balance the business with his medical work.
[事实] Brown says Mark can go at a comfortable pace, use help during the workday, and check in with that person before or after his medical schedule.
[事实] She says there is no rush and that growth will happen as more people learn about the product.
[推测] The hosts do not push Mark to leave medicine; they suggest building systems around his limited time.

[18:58] **Abby Rose: A Young Founder With A Live-Selling Brand**

[事实] Abby Rosalier from Dallas introduces Abby Rose, an online custom phone case business.
[事实] She says she is 23, started the business at 16, and had earlier small businesses as a child.
[事实] Abby says the business grew during COVID, has had 50% growth in the past year, and has done more than $2 million in total sales.
[事实] She says the company is family-run, with her sister hand-making cases and other relatives involved.
[事实] Abby says TikTok is the strongest channel, with about 400,000 followers, and Instagram has about 111,000 followers.

[23:04] **Advice For Abby Rose: Do Not Chase Explosive Growth Blindly**

[事实] Abby asks whether to double down on live social selling or expand into wholesale and paid advertising.
[事实] Brown cautions that not every business needs to grow like a rocket ship and says explosive viral moments can be hard to repeat.
[事实] Brown suggests examining what is working, what is not working, and what other channels such as YouTube, Substack, giveaways, and collaborations might add.
[事实] Brown warns that retail can sound appealing but brings major expense and operational complications.

[24:44] **Founder Dependency And Expanding The Faces Of The Brand**

[事实] Abby says sales slow down when she is not online because customers want to see orders live and interact with the brand.
[事实] Brown suggests experimenting with bringing in Abby’s sister, family members, friends, or other guests on the brand’s account.
[事实] Guy suggests training other sales representatives on commission so the brand is not limited to Abby herself.
[事实] Brown encourages Abby to try one experiment rather than create a large formal strategy immediately.
[推测] The advice treats Abby’s personal presence as an asset that needs to become a repeatable format, not a bottleneck.

[28:00] **Community, Customers, And User Participation**

[事实] Abby says she thought her customers were mostly high school and college students but has learned many are stay-at-home moms seeking community.
[事实] Brown encourages Abby to keep building that community.
[事实] Guy suggests user-generated content, such as design contests where winners create a case live with Abby.
[事实] Brown says Abby needs to show people why her phone cases are cooler, more fun, and better.

[31:31] **Marketing Across Age Groups**

[事实] Guy and Brown discuss how products sometimes take off with unexpected demographics.
[事实] Brown says Jones Road initially attracted women over 40 and later more millennials.
[事实] Brown says she does not market to separate age groups; she markets to women who want to look natural and pretty.
[推测] The implied lesson is to focus messaging on customer desire and identity rather than rigid demographic categories.

[32:46] **Tannen Oral Care: From Coffee Mouth Rinse To Wellness Brand**

[事实] Henry Davis from Charlotte introduces Tannen Oral Care, maker of a post-coffee mouth rinse.
[事实] Henry says the product came from noticing the unpleasant moment after coffee and learning that brushing immediately after coffee can affect softened enamel.
[事实] He says he and his co-founder researched mouthwash ingredients, spoke with dentists, used a chemistry set, and worked with his high school chemistry teacher.
[事实] Henry asks how to position Tannen as a legacy oral care brand with staying power beyond local recognition in Charlotte.

[事实] Brown says she likes the brand’s Instagram and vibe but questions whether coffee-focused packaging could turn away non-coffee drinkers.
[事实] Henry says they set out to make a coffee mouthwash but believe they created a strong natural mouthwash more broadly.
[事实] Brown suggests repositioning around uses beyond coffee, including wine, tea, stomach issues, and general mouth freshness.
[事实] Guy says the current positioning is cool but very niche and may limit growth.
[推测] The hosts see coffee as a useful origin story but not necessarily the best long-term category definition.

[事实] Brown says the packaging should make ingredients and benefits clearer, comparing the idea to RXBAR’s simple ingredient presentation.

[42:26] **Finding A Tribe Before Expanding**

[事实] Guy says Tannen has both an opportunity and a challenge: it should not appeal only to coffee drinkers, but it must clearly show how it benefits people.
[事实] He suggests starting with a specific tribe, such as young men, gym users, dentist office customers, or wellness communities.
[事实] Henry says the company has been leaning into social wellness spaces such as cold plunges and saunas in Charlotte.
[事实] Brown later advises young founders to test small channels, such as one dentist office or one gym, before committing heavily.
[推测] The strategic recommendation is to build strong adoption in one credible community, then expand from there.

[44:31] **Final Founder Advice From Bobbi Brown**

[事实] Brown says founders should avoid jumping into big moves and instead dip a toe in to test ideas.
[事实] She says founders should grow the business first rather than assume more funding will solve the problem.
[事实] Brown advises Tannen to study competition in clean and dentist-founded mouthwash and position itself differently.
[事实] When asked what advice she would give her younger self, Brown says she should learn to breathe, shut off her brain sometimes, and recover.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable for founders who already have early traction but are unsure how to scale without damaging what works. The strongest throughline is Brown’s practical bias toward low-cost experiments, word of mouth, community, and clear product benefits.

A highlight is how specific the advice becomes for each caller: smaller samples and local PR for Cab Dab, distributed live-selling hosts for Abby Rose, and broader wellness positioning for Tannen. The conversation avoids generic startup advice and stays close to each founder’s actual constraint.

[推测] The main limitation is that the episode is advisory rather than deeply diagnostic, so topics like margins, operations, regulatory risk, retail economics, and customer acquisition costs are only touched at a high level.

[推测] This episode is best suited for consumer-product founders, creator-led brands, and early-stage operators deciding whether to expand channels, hire help, or reposition beyond an initial niche.
