# Ireland, Home Rule, and the Road to Crisis

## 概览

This episode continues the history of Ireland, Britain, and empire after the failed 1798 rebellion and the Act of Union. The central question is why the United Kingdom of Britain and Ireland did not produce political settlement, despite Catholic emancipation, imperial integration, migration, military service, and commercial ties.

The discussion argues that the Irish Famine was a decisive rupture: it reshaped population, memory, emigration, and nationalist politics, while British policy during the crisis carried deep moral and political culpability by the standards of its own time.

The episode then traces how revolutionary republicanism remained a minority current while constitutional Home Rule became the main nationalist strategy. By the early twentieth century, John Redmond and the Irish Parliamentary Party brought Home Rule close to enactment, but fierce Ulster unionist opposition meant Britain and Ireland were moving toward a civil-war crisis before the First World War intervened.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Wolf Tone, Union, and the Unfinished Irish Question**

[事实] The episode opens with Wolf Tone’s words about breaking the connection with England, asserting Irish independence, and replacing Protestant, Catholic, and dissenter identities with the common name of Irishman.

[事实] The hosts and Paul Rouse return to the GPO in Dublin, continuing from the previous episode’s discussion of the 1798 rebellion, republicanism, romanticism, revolution, religion, and the Act of Union.

[事实] Ireland became part of the United Kingdom of Britain and Ireland after the failed uprising, but the episode asks why that settlement did not fully work.

[01:43] **Catholic Emancipation and Daniel O’Connell**

[事实] Paul Rouse says the Act of Union contained a major disconnect because Catholic emancipation was expected but then rejected, despite William Pitt’s support.

[事实] George III refused Catholic emancipation, connecting the issue to his coronation oath.

[事实] Daniel O’Connell founded the Catholic Association in 1823, organized politically through Irish counties, and forced the issue by winning election in County Clare.

[事实] The Catholic Emancipation Bill passed in 1829 under the Duke of Wellington, allowing Catholics to sit in Westminster.

[03:08] **County Identity, Wellington, and Irishness Within Empire**

[事实] O’Connell is described as “the liberator,” born in Kerry and later one of the leading barristers and politicians of his day.

[事实] County identity is presented as fundamental in Ireland, tied to politics, local government, and the Gaelic Athletic Association.

[事实] The Duke of Wellington is discussed as an Irish-born figure who became synonymous with British military victory and imperial identity.

[推测] The discussion uses O’Connell and Wellington to show that nineteenth-century Irish identity could pull toward both nationalist mobilization and imperial service.

[05:42] **Monuments and Mixed Loyalties in Nineteenth-Century Ireland**

[事实] Dublin’s Phoenix Park still has a major Wellington monument, while Nelson’s Pillar outside the GPO was later blown up.

[事实] Paul Rouse says nineteenth-century Ireland contained people loyal to empire, including some Catholics, while much of the population remained rural and extremely poor.

[事实] Many Irish people joined the British Army, though Rouse cautions that this often reflected poverty as much as imperial identification.

[推测] The episode presents Irish participation in empire as real but socially uneven, shaped by class, poverty, opportunity, and allegiance.

[07:31] **Population Growth and the Famine’s Centrality**

[事实] Ireland’s population grew from about 3 million in 1750 to about 8.4 million on the eve of the famine in 1845.

[事实] Rouse says modern Ireland cannot be understood without understanding the famine’s cultural, economic, political, and demographic consequences.

[事实] About three million people depended on the potato for more than 90% of their calories before the blight.

[事实] Potato blight destroyed about half the 1845 crop, the entire crop failed in 1846, and normal harvest did not return until 1849.

[10:52] **Death, Emigration, and British Responsibility**

[事实] Around one million people died from starvation and illness during the famine years, and more than one million emigrated.

[事实] Irish emigrants went to places including Liverpool, London, America, and Canada; coffin ships and sites such as Grosse Île and Ellis Island became important in Irish memory.

[事实] Many Irish emigrants to America carried the conviction that Britain was responsible for the famine.

[事实] Rouse frames culpability by the standards of the mid-nineteenth century rather than by later values.

[11:53] **Relief, Soup Kitchens, and Ideology**

[事实] The British government initially relied on public works schemes, which employed about 700,000 people at their height in spring 1847.

[事实] Public works were replaced in 1847 by soup kitchens feeding about three million people daily, but the government withdrew from them by October 1847.

[事实] Rouse says the crisis was widely known through newspapers, charities, Quaker relief, donations from Britain and America, and reports from Dublin Castle.

[事实] The Choctaw donated money despite their own recent experience of the Trail of Tears.

[13:56] **Trevelyan, Malthusian Thinking, and Extermination Language**

[事实] Charles Trevelyan, the chief famine administrator, expressed the idea that “permanent good” could come from “transient evil.”

[事实] Rouse connects some famine policy thinking to providential and Malthusian ideas about population and sustainability.

[事实] The Lord Lieutenant in Ireland wrote to the British prime minister that no other European legislature would disregard such suffering and persist in what he called a policy of extermination.

[事实] Rouse also notes that Irish farmers and businessmen exported food during the famine, warning against an overly simple narrative.

[16:11] **Anti-Irish Prejudice and Popular Representation**

[事实] Rouse says British ruling-class disdain for working people was general, but Ireland’s separateness and centuries of stereotypes mattered.

[事实] The discussion cites Punch cartoons and later British tabloid imagery as examples of simianized, savage representations of Irish people.

[事实] Rouse says famine history is not only about large numbers but also about individual deaths on roadsides and on coffin ships.

[推测] The episode suggests anti-Irish prejudice worsened the political and moral failure of British responses to the famine.

[18:21] **Famine Memory, Diaspora, and Limited Rebellion**

[事实] The famine created an Irish diaspora that could not be reconciled to the United Kingdom.

[事实] The hosts note that republican nationalism remained a minority pursuit after the famine, while majority Irish nationalism generally sought Home Rule rather than immediate republican separation.

[事实] A Young Ireland rebellion in 1848 drew on romantic nationalist ideas but had little support during the famine.

[推测] The discussion suggests mass poverty, exhaustion, imperial power, and practical survival all limited revolutionary possibilities.

[20:11] **Population Collapse and the Revolutionary Tradition**

[事实] Ireland was the only Western European country whose population continued to decline for a hundred years after the famine.

[事实] Rouse says Ireland went from about 8 million to 4 million while Britain grew dramatically across the nineteenth century.

[事实] The Irish Republican Brotherhood was founded in Dublin and New York, reflecting the importance of the Irish diaspora.

[事实] About eight million people left Ireland between 1801 and 1921.

[21:54] **Migration, Commerce, and the Union’s Everyday Ties**

[事实] Irish migration to Britain was significant because Ireland and Britain were part of a single kingdom.

[事实] Rouse describes ties of kin, commerce, culture, education, jobs, money, and a common market between Britain and Ireland from the 1820s onward.

[事实] Ireland remained profoundly rural outside industrializing Belfast and became deeply dependent on exporting agricultural products, including live cattle, to Britain.

[推测] These connections made the Union more than a constitutional arrangement, even for many people who also had grievances.

[23:33] **Failed Fenian Rebellion and the Turn Toward Home Rule**

[事实] The Irish Republican Brotherhood staged a rebellion in 1867, but it failed badly and was riddled with informers.

[事实] Afterward, the movement resolved to await the decision of the Irish nation before inaugurating war against England.

[事实] The hosts frame this as a contrast between revolutionary aspiration and the practical need for a chance of success.

[事实] Political energy increasingly moved toward the campaign for Home Rule.

[24:56] **Home Rule Enters the Center of Politics**

[事实] After the break, the episode quotes 1066 and All That on Gladstone and the Irish question.

[事实] Home Rule is presented as a topic with seismic consequences for the United Kingdom, not only for Ireland.

[事实] Rouse says the Irish Parliamentary Party became powerful when organized around electoral dynamics at Westminster.

[事实] In the 1870s, the Home Rule Association became the Irish Parliamentary Party and dominated the Irish representation of around 100 MPs.

[27:26] **What Home Rule Meant**

[事实] Home Rule meant a limited Irish parliament under the monarchy, not full independence.

[事实] Powers over defense and taxation would not be extensive, though the exact form shifted over time.

[事实] Nationalists often saw Home Rule as a stepping stone.

[事实] Rouse warns against imagining a hard division between constitutional Home Rulers and revolutionary separatists, because some Irish Parliamentary Party figures were also Fenians.

[28:52] **Ulster Unionism and Nationalist Blind Spots**

[事实] Rouse says Irish nationalism repeatedly underestimated how deeply Ulster unionists and loyalists rejected an independent or Home Rule Ireland.

[事实] Wolf Tone and later Patrick Pearse both emphasized unity across Protestant, Catholic, and dissenter lines.

[事实] Rouse says Irish nationalists found it almost impossible to imagine Ireland as anything other than a united island.

[事实] There was no serious talk of partition on the island before the 1880s.

[30:48] **Land, Landlords, and the Land War**

[事实] Rouse says Ireland’s land was largely owned by landlords rather than by those who farmed it.

[事实] From 1879 to 1881, the Land War was led by Michael Davitt and focused on tenant farmers’ desire to own their land.

[事实] The word “boycott” comes from Captain Boycott and rent resistance during this conflict.

[事实] Rouse notes that some landlords were progressive or charitable, while others were absentee landlords who took rents without giving much back.

[32:25] **Land Reform, Violence, and Gladstone’s Home Rule Conversion**

[事实] The first major Land Act came in 1881 amid agrarian violence, anti-eviction campaigns, arson, and murder.

[事实] Gladstone became committed to Home Rule partly because he accepted Ireland as different and saw the land question as requiring a different settlement.

[事实] The first Home Rule attempt in 1885-1886 failed in the House of Commons and split the Liberal Party.

[事实] A later attempt passed the Commons in the 1890s but was blocked by the House of Lords.

[34:15] **The Lords, Conservative Power, and Parnell**

[事实] The House of Lords was overwhelmingly conservative and contained many peers with Irish land interests.

[事实] Charles Stewart Parnell led the Irish Parliamentary Party and worked with Gladstone for Home Rule.

[事实] Parnell used land agitation and blurred the line between parliamentary nationalism and Fenian republicanism.

[事实] In 1882, the Phoenix Park murders shocked Britain and Ireland and contributed to the development of Special Branch.

[36:30] **Parnell’s Scandal and Fall**

[事实] Parnell was undermined first by forgeries claiming he supported violence and then by the Catherine O’Shea scandal.

[事实] Parnell and Catherine O’Shea had lived essentially together and had two daughters, but the matter became public through a dispute involving Captain O’Shea.

[事实] Opponents of Home Rule used the scandal to split the Irish Parliamentary Party.

[事实] Parnell’s funeral became a major nationalist spectacle, with hurling sticks draped in black crepe.

[38:22] **Redmond, the Liberals, and the Return of Home Rule**

[事实] Two Home Rule bills had failed before the twentieth century.

[事实] The Liberals won a landslide in 1906 and did not initially depend on the Irish Parliamentary Party.

[事实] After two later elections, the Liberals and Conservatives were close in seats, making John Redmond and the Irish Parliamentary Party kingmakers.

[事实] Redmond had stayed loyal to Parnell after the split and later reunited the Irish Parliamentary Party.

[40:10] **Land Purchase and Redmond’s Place in History**

[事实] The Wyndham Act of 1903 and later acts introduced major land purchase reforms, transferring landlord-held land to tenant farmers.

[事实] Rouse says John Redmond has been largely written out of Irish nationalist history despite getting Home Rule onto the statute books.

[事实] Redmond also played a major role in enabling the Parliament Act of 1911, which limited the House of Lords’ ability to block legislation.

[推测] The episode implies that British historical memory also underplays Redmond’s importance.

[42:00] **Britain’s Constitutional Crisis and the Third Home Rule Bill**

[事实] Rouse says Britain, not only Ireland, was being pulled toward civil war by the Irish crisis and the decisions of British politicians.

[事实] The Liberal government’s welfare and budget policies produced intense conflict with the House of Lords and the Conservatives.

[事实] Conservative leader Andrew Bonar Law described the Liberal government as a revolutionary committee that had seized despotic power.

[事实] Asquith and Redmond pursued the third Home Rule Bill once the Lords’ veto had been limited.

[43:20] **The Parliament Act and the Legislative Path to Home Rule**

[事实] The House of Lords accepted limits on its veto because the king signaled he would create Liberal peers if necessary.

[事实] The Parliament Act reduced the Lords’ ability to block Home Rule.

[事实] Redmond supported the People’s Budget and the Parliament Act despite measures that were unpopular with parts of his Irish base.

[事实] The Home Rule Bill was introduced in April 1912 and had to be reintroduced in the same form before it could become law.

[45:38] **Ulster Resistance and the Approach to Civil War**

[事实] By 1912, Home Rule appeared legislatively possible, but many people in Ireland, especially in Ulster, vehemently opposed it.

[事实] Rouse says Ulster unionists linked their prosperity and freedom to remaining part of the United Kingdom.

[事实] He says both Liberals and the Irish Parliamentary Party failed to grasp the depth of unionist feeling.

[事实] The episode ends by saying Britain and Ireland were plausibly heading toward civil war before events in Sarajevo changed the political context.

## 播客点评/总结

[事实] The episode’s main value is that it connects Irish political history to British constitutional history, showing that Catholic emancipation, famine policy, land reform, Home Rule, the Lords, and Ulster unionism were part of one intertwined crisis.

[事实] A major strength is Paul Rouse’s insistence on complication: he rejects simple cartoons of landlords, famine responsibility, Irish loyalty, and nationalist politics while still identifying British moral and political culpability.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for listeners who know the Easter Rising or Irish independence story but are less familiar with the nineteenth-century background that made 1912-1916 so volatile.

[推测] Its limitation is that Ulster unionism is mainly introduced as the next episode’s subject, so this installment deliberately leaves some of the most important opposition to Home Rule for later discussion.
