# Sitting Bull and the Road to Little Bighorn

## 概览

This episode shifts the Custer series toward Sitting Bull, using an 1882 interview attributed to him as a starting point while immediately questioning how reliable that English-language rendering can be.

The discussion traces how the Lakota world was being squeezed by settler migration, military forts, railroads, and the collapse of bison-centered life. It connects Red Cloud’s War, the Fetterman fight, the 1868 treaty, and the Great Sioux Reservation to the emergence of Sitting Bull as the leading anti-reservation figure.

The episode’s main conclusion is that Sitting Bull’s authority came from several sources at once: warrior prestige, spiritual power, personal charisma, and an uncompromising refusal to accept Washington’s terms. It ends by bringing Custer back into the story through the Northern Pacific survey expedition on the Yellowstone River.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Sitting Bull’s Voice and Its Reliability**

[事实] The episode opens with a passage attributed to Sitting Bull, presenting him as defending his people’s land, freedom, and right to resist invasion.

[事实] The hosts say the passage comes from an 1882 interview with Canadian journalist James Creelman, when Sitting Bull was at Fort Randall, six years after Little Bighorn.

[推测] Because Sitting Bull spoke little English, the exact wording is likely mediated through translation, journalism, performance, or embellishment.

[02:14] **Sitting Bull as Charming and Terrifying**

[事实] The hosts describe Sitting Bull as both charming and good-humored, but also frightening as a warrior.

[事实] They contrast him with Crazy Horse, who is portrayed as more solitary and unconventional.

[推测] The episode frames Sitting Bull as easier to read socially than Crazy Horse, but no less committed to violence and resistance when required.

[05:35] **Recap: Lakota Pressure and the Plains World**

[事实] The hosts recap the previous episode’s themes: shifting alliances, migration, war, horses, guns, settler expansion, trails, railroads, and pressure on the bison economy.

[事实] They explain that the Dakota/Lakota had moved from woodland life toward a Plains culture and had taken the Powder River country from the Crow.

[推测] The episode presents Native Plains societies as historically dynamic rather than timeless or static.

[07:04] **The Fetterman Fight**

[事实] Crazy Horse helped lure Captain William Fetterman and around 80 American soldiers out of Fort Kearney into an ambush.

[事实] All the American soldiers were killed, and the bodies were mutilated afterward.

[事实] The hosts note that Americans often called it a massacre, while Lakota perspectives could frame it as a major victory.

[推测] The discussion uses the fight to show how labels such as “massacre” depend heavily on political and cultural viewpoint.

[08:42] **Imperial Parallels and American Shock**

[事实] The hosts compare the Fetterman fight to British imperial frontier disasters such as those in Afghanistan, the Northwest Frontier, and Isandlwana.

[事实] They argue that the conflict had the structure and assumptions of an imperial project, even if Americans did not always describe it that way.

[事实] The fight elevated Crazy Horse’s reputation and increased the Lakota’s image as a formidable enemy on the Plains.

[11:07] **Revenge, Restraint, and Sympathy for the Lakota**

[事实] General Sherman called for severe revenge against the Sioux, using language of extermination.

[事实] Despite that, the American government closed the Bozeman Trail and forts rather than immediately launching massive reprisals.

[事实] The hosts say this was partly because the U.S. Army was also occupied with Reconstruction in the former Confederacy.

[事实] They also note that some East Coast Americans, including Senator James Doolittle and paleontologist Charles Othniel Marsh, blamed lawless white aggression and sympathized with the Lakota.

[14:39] **The 1868 Peace Commission and Social Darwinism**

[事实] Andrew Johnson’s peace commission criticized the hypocrisy of praising white resistance while condemning Native resistance.

[事实] The commission still accepted Manifest Destiny, but argued for more honest and sensitive dealings with Plains peoples.

[事实] The hosts connect this period to Darwinian and social-Darwinist thinking, in which sympathy for Native peoples could coexist with belief that “civilization” would inevitably displace them.

[推测] The episode suggests that scientific and racial ideas helped Americans morally rationalize expansion they already intended to pursue.

[17:43] **The Great Sioux Reservation and Red Cloud’s Choice**

[事实] The 1868 treaty created the Great Sioux Reservation, including much of modern South Dakota west of the Missouri.

[事实] Territory farther west was treated ambiguously as “unceded Sioux territory,” with unclear rules about white settlement and army access.

[事实] Red Cloud visited Washington, met Ulysses S. Grant, and returned believing accommodation through the reservation system was the realistic course.

[事实] Most Lakota, along with many Cheyenne and Arapaho, followed Red Cloud onto the reservation.

[20:00] **Sitting Bull Refuses the Reservation**

[事实] Several groups and leaders, including Crazy Horse and Sitting Bull, rejected the reservation path.

[事实] Sitting Bull was probably born in 1831, was Hunkpapa, and was first known as Jumping Badger before being nicknamed Slow.

[事实] His father, Returns Again, was a respected lodge head with healing, dreams, and visions associated with him.

[事实] Sitting Bull received his famous name after counting his first coup at age 14, when he knocked a Crow enemy from his horse with a stick.

[26:10] **Name, Status, and the Sacred World**

[事实] After the young Sitting Bull’s first coup, his father gave him the name Sitting Bull and took the name Jumping Bull for himself.

[事实] The hosts stress that Lakota spiritual life cannot be separated neatly into a modern category called “religion.”

[事实] They describe a world in which animals, visions, and supernatural power are woven into ordinary life.

[推测] This helps explain why stories of buffaloes and eagles communicating with people are treated in the episode as central to Lakota authority, not as ornamental folklore.

[29:14] **The 1856 Crow Duel**

[事实] Sitting Bull sang a war song while charging a Crow chieftain in 1856.

[事实] The Crow fighter shot through Sitting Bull’s shield and wounded his foot, but Sitting Bull shot him and then stabbed him in the heart.

[事实] The hosts compare the scene to Homeric and Norse heroic traditions.

[推测] The episode uses this duel to show why Sitting Bull could become a legendary warrior within Lakota memory and white historical imagination alike.

[32:02] **Warrior Societies and Spiritual Authority**

[事实] By the 1860s, Sitting Bull was a celebrated warrior with a horned headdress and many war honors.

[事实] He belonged to and held office in multiple warrior fraternities.

[事实] The hosts argue that his spiritual authority, not just his fighting record, made him exceptional.

[事实] They identify Wakan Tanka as the Great Mystery or Great Spirit at the center of the Lakota sacred world.

[33:03] **The Sun Dance and Visionary Power**

[事实] The Sun Dance is described as a ritual involving pain, endurance, gifts, dancing, and visions.

[事实] Sitting Bull endured rituals including the removal of pieces of flesh from his arms and other forms of bodily suffering.

[事实] The hosts say his scars testified to courage and sacred power.

[推测] The episode treats physical suffering as a source of public legitimacy because it demonstrated access to visions and “medicine.”

[38:39] **Sitting Bull’s Personality and Celebrity**

[事实] Later stories portrayed Sitting Bull as a holy man who communicated with animals and could predict the future.

[事实] The hosts say his powers of prediction will matter in the buildup to Little Bighorn.

[事实] They also describe him as funny, musical, a good mimic, affectionate, and someone who suffered bouts of depression.

[事实] After Little Bighorn, he became an international celebrity, and his autograph was highly prized.

[41:04] **A Leader of Anti-Reservation Resistance**

[事实] Sitting Bull rejected compromise with Washington and criticized Red Cloud’s post-Washington stance.

[事实] He argued that reservation supplies such as bacon, hardtack, sugar, and coffee were not worth becoming dependent on the Americans.

[事实] The hosts note that some Lakota moved between reservation life in winter and freer hunting or raiding in summer, so the divide was not absolute.

[事实] In 1869, anti-reservation Lakota leaders acclaimed Sitting Bull as a war chief and leader of the Sioux nation.

[44:00] **Railroads Bring Custer Back**

[事实] In the early 1870s, Sitting Bull’s raids remained relatively small-scale, but railroad expansion brought new pressure.

[事实] The Northern Pacific Railroad planned a route through Lakota lands.

[事实] Sitting Bull and Crazy Horse opposed survey parties in 1872.

[事实] In 1873, a new Yellowstone River survey expedition arrived with a U.S. Army escort led by George Armstrong Custer.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is its refusal to flatten Sitting Bull into a simple resistance icon. It shows him as a warrior, holy man, political leader, performer, celebrity, and human being with humor and vulnerability.

[推测] A key strength is the broader framing: the hosts connect Lakota politics to Reconstruction, imperial frontier warfare, treaty ambiguity, railroads, and social Darwinism. That makes the road to Little Bighorn feel structural rather than accidental.

[推测] The main limitation is that some vivid stories are clearly filtered through later accounts, translation, and legend. The hosts acknowledge this, especially with the Creelman interview and the heroic stories around Sitting Bull.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners who want narrative history with personality, context, and debate, rather than a narrow military chronology of Custer and Little Bighorn.
