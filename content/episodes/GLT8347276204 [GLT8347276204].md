# Anglo-Iranian Relations: Admiration, Oil, Betrayal, and Football

## 概览

This episode uses England’s World Cup match against Iran as a frame for a much longer history of Anglo-Iranian relations. The discussion argues that the relationship was never simply hostile: for much of the 19th century, many Iranian reformers admired Britain’s law, education, press, parliamentary politics, industry, and constitutional model.

The central tension is that admiration created unusually high expectations. Ali Ansari presents Britain as an aspirational model for Iranian reformers, but British policy repeatedly disappointed them, especially when strategic interests, Russia, India, and later oil took priority over professed political ideals.

The episode’s second half traces how oil, wartime occupation, the 1951-53 nationalisation crisis, and the 1953 coup reshaped Iranian views of Britain. It ends by connecting older images of Britain as the “old fox” or “evil state” to modern football, public opinion, protests, and the continuing appeal of English culture and language.

## 分段落总结

[00:33] **The World Cup as a historical entry point**

[事实] The episode opens with a 1923 Persian-language description of Britain as distinctive, conservative, politically stable, and marked by both freedom of thought and prejudice.

[事实] The hosts connect the quotation to England playing Iran in the World Cup and introduce the topic as the long relationship between England or Britain and Iran.

[事实] Ali Ansari, Professor of History at St Andrews and an Iranian exile, joins the discussion.

[推测] The football match functions less as the main subject than as a contemporary hook for exploring a deeper history of admiration, rivalry, and suspicion.

[03:01] **Football, revolution, and Iranian identity**

[事实] The hosts recall Iran’s 1998 World Cup match against the United States, including the Iranian players carrying flowers and Iran winning the match.

[事实] They discuss how, after the Iranian Revolution, the Islamic Revolutionary Council criticised football because players and supporters could behave aggressively or improperly.

[事实] Ali Ansari says Iran continued playing football partly because Ayatollah Khomeini liked the sport and his son played it to a high standard.

[推测] Football appears as a recurring space where politics, nationalism, and public feeling overlap without being fully controlled by ideology.

[03:57] **Ali Ansari’s personal background**

[事实] Ansari says he was sent to Britain for boarding school just before the Iranian Revolution.

[事实] He connects that decision to an Iranian view of Britain as a place where citizens, or English gentlemen, could be formed.

[推测] His personal story reinforces the episode’s larger point that Britain was not only feared or resented in Iran, but also treated as culturally prestigious.

[04:37] **Early mutual awareness between Persians and English people**

[事实] Ansari says English awareness of Persia came through classical sources, the Bible, and inherited cultural ideas.

[事实] He says Iranians tended to talk about Britain as essentially English, often conflating Britain and England.

[事实] He describes the relationship as more complex and nuanced than the mythology of “perfidious Albion” allows.

[事实] Early Iranian references to Britain treated it mainly as an island and trading place.

[06:13] **From Mongol-era contact to East India Company links**

[事实] Ansari identifies an early formal contact when Edward I sent an ambassador to the Ilkhans, the Mongol rulers of Iran, seeking help connected to the crusades.

[事实] More sustained links developed in the 16th and 17th centuries through trade and the East India Company.

[事实] Persia became more prominent in the English imagination as Britain expanded its interests in India.

[推测] The relationship became practically important when Persia was tied to trade, empire, and routes connected to India.

[07:56] **Persianate India and British understanding of Iran**

[事实] Ansari stresses that Mughal India was part of the Persianate world and used Persian as a language of administration.

[事实] British officials travelling from India to Iran often knew Persian and understood local political etiquette and cultural idioms.

[事实] Britain viewed Iran partly through the lens of defending India, first from Afghanistan and Central Asia, then increasingly from Russia.

[推测] British knowledge of Persianate culture gave Britain influence in Iran, but also tied the relationship to imperial security rather than Iranian priorities.

[09:19] **Iran between Britain and Russia**

[事实] Ansari describes Iran emerging from the 18th century after intense political turmoil and facing two modernising imperial powers: British India and the Russian Empire.

[事实] He says Iranians feared Russia because Russia could send troops into Iran quickly, while Britain was a naval power less inclined to do so.

[事实] Britain was not yet seen as a satanic figure in the Iranian imagination.

[推测] Britain’s distance and softer methods made it more appealing than Russia, even while it remained an imperial power.

[10:54] **Britain as an aspirational reform model**

[事实] Ansari says British officials presented Britain’s success as the result of discipline, law, and education after earlier political disorder.

[事实] Reforming Iranians found British political ideals attractive and expected Britain to live up to them.

[事实] Ansari says this pattern later resembled Iranian expectations of America in the 20th century.

[推测] The roots of later disappointment lay in the gap between Britain’s self-presentation and its conduct abroad.

[13:22] **Persian princes in Britain**

[事实] Ansari discusses Persian princes who visited England in 1835 and observed British society.

[事实] They were impressed by liberty, freedom, newspapers, economic development, industry, the Navy, and the Thames Tunnel.

[事实] The hosts quote their explanation of British politics, including their descriptions of Tories and Whigs.

[推测] The visitors interpreted British power as a product of institutions, consultation, industry, and public life rather than only military strength.

[15:55] **Whiggism, persuasion, and ideological intimacy**

[事实] Ansari says the Persian princes absorbed a strongly Whig account of British politics, influenced by figures such as Palmerston.

[事实] He contrasts Russian reliance on brute force with British efforts to win influence through persuasion and emulation.

[事实] Ansari argues that the problem in the relationship was partly its ideological intimacy: Iranians found the British model extremely attractive.

[推测] The closer the intellectual attraction, the greater the later sense of betrayal when British policy failed to match British ideals.

[17:15] **Freemasonry and the Constitutional Revolution**

[事实] Ansari says many Iranians, including some religious figures, joined Masonic lodges in the 19th century.

[事实] He describes Freemasonry then as iconoclastic, anti-superstition, and linked to new political realities.

[事实] He says the 1906 Iranian Constitutional Revolution was strongly modelled on the British parliamentary system and was described by some leaders as the “spiritual child of Great Britain.”

[事实] Ansari says Iranian constitutionalists pleaded for British support, but Britain became less willing to annoy Russia.

[19:13] **Admiration turning into disappointment**

[事实] The hosts summarise the 19th century as a period of Iranian admiration for Britain, especially among intellectuals.

[事实] Ansari says this admiration created huge scope for disappointment when Britain failed to support Iranian reformers.

[事实] He gives the example of an Iranian prince criticising Britain for opposing the slave trade internationally while not helping Iran against slave raiding in Central Asia.

[推测] British inconsistency became politically damaging because Iranians judged Britain by the standards Britain had encouraged them to admire.

[20:27] **War, concessions, and the road to oil**

[事实] Ansari notes the brief Anglo-Persian War of the 1850s over Afghanistan.

[事实] He says the Treaty of Paris after the war was lenient compared with what Russia might have imposed.

[事实] British economic concessions later entered Iran, partly justified by some as a way to catalyse reform.

[事实] Ansari identifies the development of the oil industry as the next major phase in Anglo-Iranian relations.

[22:23] **The D’Arcy concession and the discovery of oil**

[事实] Ansari says an oil concession was granted to an Australian-British entrepreneur named Knox D’Arcy for exploration and exploitation in southern Iran.

[事实] He says it took seven years, large spending, and near abandonment before oil was struck in 1908.

[事实] The 1907 Anglo-Russian Convention divided Iran into spheres of influence but did not focus on the oil concession.

[推测] Oil’s importance was not fully obvious at the moment of the concession, but it soon transformed Britain’s view of Iran.

[25:08] **Oil turns Iran into a strategic asset**

[事实] Ansari says Churchill’s decision as First Lord of the Admiralty to switch the Royal Navy from coal to oil made Iranian oil strategically important.

[事实] He says Iran shifted from being mainly a buffer state in the Great Game to being a strategic asset for Britain.

[事实] Britain played a role both in the creation of the modern Iranian state through constitutionalism and in the creation of Iran’s first modern industry through Abadan and the oil refinery.

[事实] Ansari says the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company overshadowed other industrial development in Iran.

[26:32] **Nationalism and resentment over concessions**

[事实] Ansari says the oil industry coincided with the rise of nationalist thought in Iran.

[事实] He says British ideas of progress and national development helped encourage Iranian nationalism.

[事实] He identifies two major British concessions: the Imperial Bank of Persia, with fiscal rights including issuing banknotes, and the oil company.

[事实] He says Iran’s oil royalties were based on profits, while Iranians were not allowed to inspect the company’s accounts.

[28:35] **Oil profits and wartime value**

[事实] Ansari says the British government benefited from the oil arrangement through preferential Royal Navy rates and corporation tax.

[事实] He says Iranian irritation grew because Britain benefited from oil in ways Iran did not fully share.

[事实] During the Second World War, Iranian oil became pivotal for supplying British forces at preferential rates.

[推测] The oil arrangement made resentment structural because it connected Iranian sovereignty, foreign profit, and British strategic power.

[29:00] **Iran in the First World War**

[事实] Iran was technically neutral in the First World War.

[事实] The hosts and Ansari discuss British, Russian, and Ottoman activity in Iran during the war.

[事实] Ansari says the war caused major socioeconomic disruption and famine in western Iran.

[事实] He says current “perfidious Albion” narratives blame Britain for the famine, but he identifies internal turmoil, the Ottomans, and the Russians as the chief culprits, with Britain entering later.

[30:15] **The Second World War occupation**

[事实] Ansari says the Soviets and British occupied Iran in 1941, with Americans arriving after 1942.

[事实] The Allies used Iran as a bridge to supply the Soviet Union.

[事实] He says this was probably the first time many ordinary Iranians encountered ordinary British soldiers.

[事实] Political life opened up during the occupation, making the atmosphere febrile and bringing renewed attention to foreign concessions.

[31:47] **Post-war power shifts and the oil nationalisation crisis**

[事实] Ansari says the Allies agreed to leave Iran six months after the war, but Stalin kept Soviet forces in northern Iran.

[事实] After Iran prevented a Soviet oil concession in the north, Iranian politicians asked why they could not revisit the British oil concession in the south.

[事实] This led into the 1951-53 oil nationalisation crisis under Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh.

[事实] Ansari says Mossadegh’s nationalisation reflected, in part, the fact that Britain’s own Labour government was nationalising industries.

[33:17] **The Anglo-Iranian Oil Company as a central problem**

[事实] Ansari says Ernest Bevin understood that Britain would struggle to lecture others about national assets while nationalising industries at home.

[事实] Ansari identifies the board of the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company as the “villain” because it resisted reconsidering its relationship with Iran.

[事实] He says the Foreign Office often urged the company to make concessions because the world had changed.

[事实] He also says Anglo-American relations in the Middle East involved competition, not simply a united Western front against Iran.

[35:20] **The 1953 coup**

[事实] The hosts discuss the 1953 overthrow of Mossadegh under the Pahlavi dynasty.

[事实] Ansari says the coup was essentially devised by the British, but it required approval from the Eisenhower administration.

[事实] Because British diplomatic relations had ended and the British embassy was closed, the operation had to be run effectively out of the American embassy.

[事实] Ansari says Iran closed the British embassy because of fears that Britain was interfering and planning a coup.

[37:08] **British political skill and Iranian suspicion**

[事实] Ansari says Iranian elites had a “schizophrenic” view of Britain: they admired British politics but also saw the British as cunning.

[事实] He says Iranians often viewed the British as people who could do politics better than Iranians themselves.

[推测] The coup strengthened an older image of Britain as politically subtle, manipulative, and dangerous.

[38:00] **The 1979 Revolution and conspiracy views**

[事实] Ansari says Iranian monarchists have claimed that the Iranian Revolution was spun by the British Secret Service.

[事实] He mentions jokes that Ayatollah Khomeini was “made in England” or that “Margaret Thatcher” sounded like “my great teacher.”

[事实] He says political factions across Iran tend to see Britain as central to the issue.

[推测] The jokes illustrate how suspicion of Britain crossed ideological lines rather than belonging only to one faction.

[39:03] **The American embassy crisis**

[事实] Ansari says there were early attacks on both the British and American embassies after the revolution.

[事实] The American embassy was seized on November 4, 1979.

[事实] Ansari links the seizure to fears shaped by 1953, the idea of the embassy as a nest of spies, and the Shah receiving medical treatment in America.

[事实] He says many Iranians have since reflected negatively on the seizure, and only revolutionary diehards now view it positively.

[40:06] **The Shah and Britain**

[事实] The hosts note that the Shah visited the United Kingdom and Queen Elizabeth II visited Iran.

[事实] Ansari says the Shah was fluent in French and English and saw himself as a strong nationalist.

[事实] Ansari says the Shah criticised Britain in interviews, but revolutionary consciousness treated him as a British and American lackey because of the 1953 coup.

[事实] Ansari recalls his mother interpreting David Owen’s parliamentary support for the Shah as a sign that the Shah was finished.

[41:28] **Rushdie and Britain returning to the firing line**

[事实] The hosts identify Valentine’s Day 1989 as the moment when Ayatollah Khomeini issued the fatwa against Salman Rushdie over The Satanic Verses.

[事实] Ansari says this put Britain back into the firing line after the 1980s rivalry between Iran and America.

[事实] He says many Iranians of a certain generation or political outlook still regard Britain as the source of evil or original sin.

[事实] He says hardline circles in Iran describe Britain as the “evil state.”

[42:15] **Britain, America, and paradoxical attraction**

[事实] Ansari says some Iranians view Britain as “America’s brain.”

[事实] He says younger generations have a different view.

[事实] He notes that suspicion of Britain has not stopped many Iranians from going to Britain for education or medical treatment.

[事实] He says a common distinction is hatred for the British state, not the British people.

[43:19] **England, America, Wales, and the modern World Cup mood**

[事实] The hosts note that Iran was drawn in a World Cup group with England, America, and Wales.

[事实] Ansari says official political circles might try to stir nationalist feeling against “the little Satan.”

[事实] He says most people would probably not treat the England match ideologically and would be interested because of English football and the Premier League.

[事实] He gives an election example in which voters ignored official warnings about supposed British-backed candidates.

[44:51] **Footballers, protests, and public opinion**

[事实] The hosts mention Iranian footballers supporting the campaign against the headscarf.

[事实] Ansari says sporting celebrities who previously stayed neutral have come out in support of protesters.

[事实] He says the government finds them hard to handle because they are immensely popular.

[事实] He says football is effectively Iran’s national sport, even though wrestling is often described that way.

[推测] Football gives prominent Iranians a public platform that can complicate the government’s control of political messaging.

[46:03] **English language and cultural influence**

[事实] Ansari says Iranians increasingly learn English through films, songs, and other secondhand sources.

[事实] He describes English as Iran’s second language and says people learn it to get ahead.

[事实] He says Iranians are often impressively fluent in English despite isolation from the outside world.

[事实] He recounts an 1809 anecdote about a Persian ambassador in Britain learning English soon after arriving.

[47:57] **The “old fox” and the tragedy of disappointed friends**

[事实] The hosts discuss the image of Britain as the “old fox,” including a poem by Pishavari and a 2016 poster showing a fox and a Union Jack waistcoat.

[事实] Ansari says the poster described Britain as an eternal enemy of Iran, but he says that is historically untrue.

[事实] He says other regional histories often represented Persians themselves as cunning, and that Iranians transferred that image onto the British because Britain did it better.

[事实] Ansari concludes that British politics was attractive to Iranians, but British policy disappointed them.

[事实] He says some British diplomats privately admitted that Britain was letting down its friends.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is its refusal to reduce Anglo-Iranian relations to simple hostility. It shows that suspicion of Britain grew out of a long history in which many Iranians first admired British constitutionalism, education, law, newspapers, industry, and political culture.

A major strength is the way the discussion connects high politics with cultural memory: football, language learning, Freemasonry, newspapers, oil concessions, wartime occupation, the 1953 coup, and the Rushdie fatwa are all treated as parts of the same historical relationship.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for listeners who want context for modern Iranian attitudes toward Britain and America, but it is less focused on detailed Iranian domestic politics beyond the points needed to explain Anglo-Iranian relations.

[推测] Its clearest takeaway is that Britain’s reputation in Iran was damaged not simply by hostility, but by disappointed admiration: Britain encouraged ideals that many Iranian reformers embraced, then often failed to support them when imperial or strategic interests intervened.
