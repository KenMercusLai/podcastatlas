# England and Denmark: Rivalry, Kinship, and the Long Road to Wembley

## 概览

This episode uses the England-Denmark Euro 2020 semi-final at Wembley as a hook for a rapid history of Anglo-Danish relations, beginning with Viking attacks on Anglo-Saxon England and ending with Danish sailors in wartime Newcastle. The hosts frame the fixture as one of England’s oldest rivalries, but also as a story of deep kinship and repeated cultural overlap.

The first half focuses on the Viking Age: Lindisfarne, the Danelaw, Alfred, St Edmund, Guthrum, Aethelflaed, Athelstan, Aethelred, Danegeld, and Cnut. The central argument is that Danish attacks did not simply threaten England; they helped create England as a unified political kingdom.

The second half jumps to early modern and modern episodes: Anne of Denmark’s marriage to James VI/I, Christian IV’s visit, Charles I’s failed attempt to secure Danish help, Nelson and Copenhagen, Hans Christian Andersen’s disastrous stay with Dickens, Schleswig-Holstein, and the Danish wartime presence in Newcastle. The conclusion softens the rivalry by emphasizing shared memory, alliance, and friendship.

## 分段落总结

[00:25] **Wilton and the Oldest Rivalry**

[事实] The episode opens with a 1003 Viking attack on Wilton, a symbolically important Anglo-Saxon royal and religious site.

[事实] The Anglo-Saxon commander is described as pretending to be ill, vomiting, losing control, and fleeing, after which the English army also runs away.

[事实] The hosts connect this scene jokingly to England facing Denmark at Wembley and to the fear of English collapse in a high-pressure football match.

[02:27] **Danish Invasion and the Making of England**

[事实] The hosts argue that England can be seen as a kingdom formed in response to Danish invasion.

[事实] Danish attacks destroyed or overwhelmed several Anglo-Saxon kingdoms, including Northumbria, Mercia, and East Anglia, while Wessex held out.

[事实] Alfred, Edward, Aethelflaed, and Athelstan are presented as key figures in the fightback that turned Wessex into the kingdom of England.

[03:41] **Who Were the Danes?**

[事实] The episode explains that early medieval labels such as Danes, Northmen, Jutes, Goths, and Vikings are slippery and often overlapping.

[事实] In the English context, “Danes” often refers broadly to Scandinavian raiders and settlers who invaded England and occupied the Danelaw.

[事实] The hosts also note that Viking activity helped form not only England, but also a more unified kingdom of Denmark.

[05:55] **Beowulf and Older Danish Connections**

[事实] The hosts discuss Beowulf as the greatest Old English epic, while noting that it is set among Scandinavians rather than in England.

[事实] Beowulf visits Hrothgar, king of the Danes, at Heorot, a hall later linked by the hosts to Tolkien’s imagined world.

[事实] The episode uses Beowulf to argue that English-Danish cultural links predate the Viking attacks on Lindisfarne.

[07:02] **Kinship, Settlement, and Place Names**

[事实] The hosts describe Anglo-Danish history as both rivalry and kinship, citing Nelson’s later description of the Danes as brothers of Englishmen.

[事实] They mention English place names ending in forms such as “-thwaite” and “-thorpe” as evidence of Danish settlement, especially in northern and eastern England.

[推测] The football rivalry is framed as a “family row” because the historical and cultural boundaries between English and Danish identities were never completely separate.

[09:05] **The Great Army and St Edmund**

[事实] The Great Northern Army is described as destroying Northumbria, occupying York, and moving into East Anglia.

[事实] St Edmund, king of the East Angles, is described as being captured by the Danes, refusing to abandon Christianity, and being killed with arrows.

[事实] The hosts recount the story of Edmund’s severed head being guarded by a wolf and later becoming a major English saint.

[事实] St Edmund is presented as a former patron saint of England and later also honored by Christianized Danes.

[12:06] **Guthrum, Alfred, and the Danelaw**

[事实] Guthrum and other Danish leaders are discussed as part of the great Viking assault on Wessex.

[事实] Alfred flees to Athelney after the Danes attack Chippenham during Christmas, then returns, defeats them, and makes a treaty with Guthrum.

[事实] Guthrum becomes Christian, takes the name Athelstan, and receives territory later known as the Danelaw.

[事实] Alfred strengthens Wessex by building fortified market towns along a defensive line.

[14:41] **Aethelflaed, Athelstan, and Unification**

[事实] Aethelflaed, Alfred’s daughter, and Edward, Alfred’s son, go on the offensive against Danish power.

[事实] They conquer territory south of the Humber, and Alfred’s grandson Athelstan later takes Northumbria from the Danes.

[事实] England becomes unusually unified, and under Edgar develops centralized control over silver and currency.

[推测] The hosts present the Danish threat as a catalyst for unusually strong English royal institutions.

[16:35] **Tettenhall and Cultural Difference**

[事实] The hosts discuss the Battle of Tettenhall or Wednesfield, linked to the future site of Wolverhampton.

[事实] Aethelflaed is credited with provoking Danish retaliation by seizing relics from Lincolnshire, after which Danish forces are defeated.

[事实] The hosts ask whether pagan Danes and Christian Anglo-Saxons were deeply different or culturally close.

[事实] The answer given is that difference was strongest before Danish conversion, but Danish settlers adapted quickly to English Christianity and West Saxon influence.

[19:24] **Assimilation in the Danelaw**

[事实] A coin owned by one host is used as an example of Danish imitation of West Saxon currency and Danish pilgrimage to Rome.

[事实] Anglo-Saxon moralists are said to have criticized Danish habits, including eyeliner, shaved necks, and bathing every Saturday.

[事实] Danish identity in the Danelaw is described as fading over generations while leaving traces in place names and local culture.

[21:40] **Aethelred, Danegeld, and the Danish Return**

[事实] Under Aethelred the Unready, Danish attacks return in large numbers.

[事实] Aethelred tries to pay the Danes off through Danegeld, but the hosts emphasize that this attracts more raiders.

[事实] Aethelred flees to Normandy, returns, dies, and is succeeded by Edmund Ironside, who fights Cnut to a standstill.

[23:32] **Bluetooth, Svein, and Cnut’s North Sea Power**

[事实] Harold Bluetooth is described as the first Christian Danish king and as the namesake of Bluetooth technology.

[事实] Svein Forkbeard, Harold’s son, is identified as the leader of the Wilton attack that opened the episode.

[事实] Cnut and his family are presented as Christian rulers who were still militarily terrifying and part of a powerful North Sea empire.

[24:39] **Cnut and the Tide**

[事实] The hosts correct the common story of Cnut and the waves, explaining that he is not portrayed as foolish but as demonstrating the limits of human power before God.

[事实] Cnut is described as initially harsh and rapacious in England, including imposing an extreme tax demand.

[事实] He later becomes a model of Christian kingship and is described as anglicized.

[26:54] **The Last Danish Invasion Attempt**

[事实] After the Norman Conquest, a Danish king named Svein is invited by northern rebels and occupies York in 1069.

[事实] William the Conqueror advances, the rebels flee, and the Danes move to East Anglia before being paid Danegeld to leave.

[事实] The hosts present this as the final serious Danish invasion attempt, after which Viking-style raiding fades.

[28:36] **The Football Frame Returns**

[事实] The second half explicitly identifies the episode as an Anglo-Danish history tied to the England-Denmark Euro 2020 semi-final, played in 2021 at Wembley.

[事实] The hosts jokingly score the first historical half as Denmark leading because of Viking successes.

[事实] They then leap forward from the medieval period to the Stuart era.

[29:36] **Anne of Denmark and James VI/I**

[事实] Anne of Denmark marries James VI of Scotland, later James I of England, when she is fourteen.

[事实] Their relationship deteriorates partly over the custody of their son Henry.

[事实] Anne stages a hunger strike after James wants to remove ladies-in-waiting whose brothers had been involved in the Gowrie conspiracy.

[事实] The hosts recount a strange story in which Anne kills James’s favorite hound with a crossbow and receives an expensive jewel from him afterward.

[35:17] **Hamlet, Macbeth, and Christian IV**

[事实] The hosts discuss Shakespearean Anglo-Danish links, including Hamlet being performed before James and Anne at Hampton Court.

[事实] James visits Elsinore, the setting associated with Hamlet, when he goes to collect Anne.

[事实] Storms during James and Anne’s North Sea crossing are linked to James’s obsession with witchcraft and to Macbeth.

[事实] Christian IV, Anne’s brother, visits London in 1606, and a court entertainment reportedly becomes chaotic because of heavy drinking.

[38:49] **Charles I and Danish Newcastle**

[事实] Charles I, who was half Danish through Anne, asks his uncle Christian IV for military aid in 1642.

[事实] In return, the Danes reportedly wanted Orkney, Shetland, and control of Newcastle in pawn.

[事实] The hosts describe this as commercially motivated because Newcastle was known for coal and salt.

[事实] Danish help does not become decisive, and the plan fails.

[40:16] **Nelson and the Battle of Copenhagen**

[事实] In 1801, during the Napoleonic Wars, Britain is trying to enforce a blockade against Baltic powers including Denmark.

[事实] Nelson attacks Copenhagen harbor on 2 April 1801, capturing and sinking Danish ships.

[事实] The famous story that Nelson put a telescope to his blind eye and said he did not see the signal to withdraw is described as probably apocryphal.

[42:32] **The 1807 Bombardment of Copenhagen**

[事实] The hosts describe the 1807 British attack on Copenhagen as a preemptive strike driven by fears that Denmark might join France.

[事实] British forces land troops and bombard Copenhagen, including with Congreve rockets.

[事实] The destruction of the Church of Our Lady is discussed, with British gunners said to have used church spires for range practice.

[推测] The episode treats 1807 as one of the darker and less remembered British episodes in Anglo-Danish relations.

[45:38] **Hans Christian Andersen and Dickens**

[事实] Hans Christian Andersen meets Charles Dickens in 1847 and later develops an intense admiration for him.

[事实] In 1857 Andersen visits Dickens at Gad’s Hill and stays for five weeks despite expectations of a much shorter visit.

[事实] The hosts describe Andersen as a difficult guest who demands shaving help, monopolizes Dickens, weeps dramatically at a performance, and reacts badly to criticism.

[事实] Dickens and his family later complain about Andersen, with Dickens’s daughter calling him a “bony bore.”

[49:36] **Schleswig-Holstein and British Non-Intervention**

[事实] The hosts describe Schleswig-Holstein as a famously complex diplomatic problem involving Denmark, German territories, and the London Protocol.

[事实] Denmark tries to integrate Schleswig-Holstein more closely, prompting Prussian and Austrian invasion.

[事实] Britain debates intervention, but does not act to save Denmark.

[事实] The episode links the conflict to Bismarck’s path toward German unification.

[53:28] **Danish Sailors in Newcastle During World War II**

[事实] A memorial in Newcastle Cathedral commemorates Danish seamen who died between 1939 and 1945.

[事实] After Germany attacked Denmark in April 1940, Danish ships that could escape sailed to Britain and operated under Allied flags with Danish crews.

[事实] Newcastle becomes the home of the Danish fleet in exile, with Danish clubs, a Danish church, and a harbor area called the Danish Pool.

[事实] The hosts connect this warmly to Christian IV’s earlier unrealized desire for Newcastle.

[55:18] **Rivalry, Friendship, and the Closing Note**

[事实] The hosts close by saying that Anglo-Danish history is not only rivalry, but also a record of good, rich, and productive relations.

[事实] They return to the football frame and joke that another bonus episode may depend on whether England defeats Denmark.

[推测] The closing message is that historical rivalry can coexist with admiration, shared culture, and political solidarity.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in making a football fixture feel historically dense without losing momentum. It uses jokes, literary references, and military history to show how England and Denmark have repeatedly shaped each other.

[推测] Its strongest section is the Viking Age discussion, because it turns the familiar invasion narrative into a broader argument about the creation of England, the Danelaw, conversion, and assimilation.

[推测] The main limitation is that the pace is extremely fast, especially after the medieval period. Several later episodes, such as Charles I’s Danish negotiations, the 1807 bombardment, and Schleswig-Holstein, are treated as vivid snapshots rather than full explanations.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who enjoy conversational history, football-adjacent cultural framing, and Anglo-Saxon or British imperial history, rather than listeners looking for a systematic diplomatic history of Denmark and England.
