# I Am Superman: Patty Jenkins’ Unmade Prison-Dog Fairy Tale

## Overview

Patty Jenkins discusses *I Am Superman*, an unproduced R-rated film about Bandit, a fighting pit bull condemned to die, and an incarcerated man who is given responsibility for rehabilitating him. The story uses their uneasy bond to examine whether people and animals labeled “bad” are ever truly allowed a second chance.

Jenkins envisioned an unconventional production built around an authentic canine performance: a small crew, a long shooting schedule, hidden digital cameras, and potentially a real prison where selected inmates could train as crew members. The central challenge was not merely making a dog act, but capturing a full emotional journey without reducing the film to a conventional sentimental redemption story.

The discussion then broadens into Hollywood’s aversion to unfamiliar narratives. Jenkins argues that the industry welcomed her as a director of other people’s projects while repeatedly distrusting her original ideas, even after the success of *Monster* and *Wonder Woman*. She connects this pattern to sexism, backward-looking commercial logic, and a narrow understanding of what women artists can create.

In its final section, the conversation becomes deeply personal. Jenkins explains how her father’s death when she was seven, followed by another traumatic loss, shaped her familiarity with darkness, romantic longing, and misunderstood people. She sees filmmaking partly as a way to take control of narratives in a world where real events can be arbitrary and devastating.

## 分段落总结

[00:33] **Bandit’s violent fairy-tale opening**

[事实] The proposed film opens with narration introducing Bandit, a fighting pit bull who once dreamed of becoming a loved family dog.

[事实] After years of abuse, Bandit attacks and kills his trainer, establishing both the film’s R-rated tone and the depth of the animal’s trauma.

[事实] Jenkins describes the project as a fairy tale whose lead character is the dog, with an ambitious dramatic role for a male actor.

[推测] The opening deliberately asks viewers to reconsider an apparently monstrous act by revealing the suffering that produced it.

[03:01] **The prison-dog program and the possibility of second chances**

[事实] Jenkins developed the story after researching programs in which incarcerated people rehabilitate dogs considered unadoptable.

[事实] She says the programs give prisoners—people whom society often wants only to punish—a chance to become necessary and heroic to another living being.

[事实] Jenkins states that participants in these programs have recidivism rates that fall to almost zero.

[事实] The film is titled *I Am Superman* because the incarcerated man paired with Bandit renames the dog Superman.

[推测] Pairing rejected dogs with rejected people allows the film to challenge the idea that either group can be permanently defined by its past.

[06:50] **A man and a dog trapped by other people’s expectations**

[事实] The human protagonist has returned to prison after being accused of another crime and enters the dog program despite disliking dogs.

[事实] Bandit was supposed to be euthanized but is accidentally admitted to the program, leaving two supposedly terrible figures forced to work together.

[事实] The man begins to hope he can change his life, but that hope collapses when the prison shuts down the program and sends Bandit away to be killed.

[事实] Jenkins says the prison administration and institutional corruption sabotage the program, so the story does not follow a straightforward feel-good trajectory.

[推测] The project’s real antagonist is the surrounding system, which refuses to recognize that the man and dog may already be capable of change.

[11:42] **Jenkins’ lifelong connection to pit bulls**

[事实] Jenkins describes herself as a fanatical dog lover who owns a pit bull and a French bulldog and has kept pit bulls throughout her life.

[事实] As a child, she spent summers with her grandparents in Mississippi, where she was surrounded by roughly twenty or thirty pit bulls.

[事实] She regards the breed as intelligent, independent, emotional, and steadfast, while acknowledging that pit bulls can be dangerous and capable of inflicting serious harm.

[推测] Her familiarity with the breed informed Bandit as a complex protagonist rather than a simple emblem of innocence or menace.

[13:18] **Designing an authentic performance for a dog**

[事实] Jenkins wanted Bandit to deliver such a strong performance that people might discuss whether the dog deserved an Oscar.

[事实] The dog’s emotional progression would need to move through hostility, curiosity, apprehension, affection, and an eventual loss of control.

[事实] Jenkins proposed using a very small crew over many shooting days, waiting patiently for genuine expressions rather than relying only on conventional animal cues.

[事实] She was willing to shoot digitally and conceal multiple cameras so the dog could interact with the actor without being distracted by a large production apparatus.

[事实] She also considered filming in a real prison and training selected inmates to work as members of the crew, while recognizing that lockdowns would complicate production.

[15:57] **The demanding lead role and Ryan Gosling’s involvement**

[事实] Jenkins says the lead actor would need to love dogs, embrace an unorthodox process, and learn how to work with the animal throughout production.

[事实] At times, the actor might need to provoke an off-camera response from the dog rather than perform the scene conventionally.

[事实] Jenkins discussed the role with Ryan Gosling around 2005–2006, and they considered making the film at different points.

[事实] According to Jenkins, other actors often wanted the prisoner to behave more violently so that the character could follow a familiar redemption arc.

[推测] The role’s difficulty came from asking an actor to portray an already-changed man whose apparent threat exists largely in other people’s assumptions.

[18:06] **Why Hollywood resisted the story**

[事实] Jenkins repeatedly rejected suggestions that the prisoner should stab someone at the beginning and then earn redemption.

[事实] She says that such a change would contradict her view that prisons are largely warehouses for sad, trapped human beings rather than collections of romanticized super-criminals.

[事实] Industry figures also told her that an R-rated dog movie could not work, while she argued that adults as well as children are drawn to films about dogs.

[事实] Although readers praised the screenplay, Jenkins could not secure approximately five million dollars to make it as her second feature.

[推测] The qualities that made the project difficult to finance—its tonal ambiguity, adult rating, and rejection of familiar prison mythology—were also what Jenkins believed would make it distinctive.

[20:33] **Sexism, risk, and Hollywood’s backward-looking logic**

[事实] Jenkins says she does not understand why financiers would not take a relatively inexpensive chance on her after her Oscar-winning first film, *Monster*.

[事实] She believes sexism may have contributed to the industry treating her success as a one-off while offering her work on projects conceived by others.

[事实] Jenkins argues that Hollywood evaluates future success through precedents created by a narrow range of voices, which structurally disadvantages unfamiliar stories and diverse perspectives.

[事实] She recalls that one executive predicted nobody would want to see *Monster*, yet the film later earned more than eighty million dollars.

[事实] Afterward, executives asked whether she had more female-serial-killer projects, reducing the film’s success to its surface subject rather than its execution.

[推测] The discussion portrays discrimination less as open rejection of proven talent than as a persistent refusal to grant that talent broad creative authority.

[26:21] **Success produces the wrong lessons**

[事实] Jenkins says the industry similarly interpreted *Wonder Woman* primarily as evidence that audiences wanted women directing women’s stories.

[事实] She felt this overlooked the film itself, its hero’s journey, and the successful execution of an unconventional project.

[事实] Jenkins says highly successful women privately acknowledge that major achievements do not necessarily lead to trust in their next original idea.

[事实] She believes genuine acceptance of diverse creative voices remains far away because decision-making power is still concentrated among people with the same established interests.

[推测] Jenkins’ frustration is not simply about access to directing jobs, but about the difference between being hired as a skilled executor and being trusted as an artist.

[28:29] **The personal origins of Jenkins’ attraction to darkness**

[事实] Jenkins grew up in an Air Force family, lived in Thailand during the Vietnam War, and experienced the surrounding danger and loss at an early age.

[事实] Her father died in a plane crash when she was seven, an event she identifies as the defining experience of her life.

[事实] Moving between places and knowing people from very different social backgrounds made her curious about lives unlike her own.

[事实] She says she has a particular tenderness for people who live dangerously because she has watched circumstances shape them and seen how easily they can be misunderstood.

[推测] Her work’s recurring interest in damaged outsiders arises from both personal grief and a resistance to judging people without examining what formed them.

[32:33] **Grief, Monster, and controlling the narrative**

[事实] Jenkins says adults underestimated how completely she understood her father’s death and how profoundly it destroyed her sense of safety.

[事实] She describes becoming suicidal after the loss and learning very young that terrible events can occur without warning or remedy.

[事实] Jenkins believes she would not be the same director had her father lived and says *Monster* was directly connected to his death.

[事实] She interprets *Monster* as a rejection of reassuring myths that love, belief, and effort guarantee a good outcome.

[事实] Her original motivation for becoming a filmmaker was to control stories and create outcomes that real life could not promise.

[推测] Filmmaking became a means of transforming helplessness before tragedy into authorship, without denying how dark reality can be.

[37:20] **Loss, romanticism, and Jenkins’ range as an artist**

[事实] Jenkins recalls that after her father died, a beloved older boy who had lived with her family was shot and killed.

[事实] She says these losses made her deeply romantic about love, longing, and absence, even as they intensified her familiarity with darkness.

[事实] Jenkins once identified primarily with the rebellious person who made *Monster*, but later recognized that she is also the person who made *Wonder Woman*.

[推测] The contrast between those films reflects not a contradiction but a creative sensibility in which darkness and idealism grow from the same experiences of loss.

[39:09] **How the film might still be made**

[事实] If Jenkins revives *I Am Superman*, she does not expect to pitch it widely in the conventional way.

[事实] She would prefer to assemble financing herself, retain control, and create enough space to execute the film without excessive notes from fearful decision-makers.

[事实] She says experience has taught her to structure a project so that it has the best chance to succeed rather than hope Hollywood will suddenly understand it.

[事实] Jenkins remains undecided about making the film and promises that, if she never does, she will return to reveal the rest of the story.

[推测] Independent financing would not merely solve a budget problem; it would protect the project’s unconventional moral and production choices.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode succeeds on two levels: it offers a vivid account of an unusually ambitious unmade film, then uses that film as a lens on punishment, creative risk, and institutional distrust. Jenkins provides enough plot and production detail to make *I Am Superman* feel tangible without disclosing its ending.

Its strongest material comes from the parallels among Bandit, the incarcerated protagonist, and Jenkins’ own career. Each is judged through an established label, while the deeper person—or animal, or artist—struggles to be recognized. The practical discussion of eliciting a natural canine performance also gives the episode unusual value for listeners interested in directing and production.

[推测] The episode’s main limitation is that its broader claims about prisons, recidivism, and gender discrimination are presented principally through personal experience and conversation rather than independently examined evidence. It is therefore more persuasive as an artistic and autobiographical argument than as a comprehensive analysis of those systems.

[推测] This episode will especially appeal to filmmakers, screenwriters, dog lovers, and listeners interested in unrealized Hollywood projects, unconventional storytelling, women’s creative authority, or the relationship between personal trauma and artistic vision.
