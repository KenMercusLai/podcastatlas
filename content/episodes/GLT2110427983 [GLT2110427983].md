# Henry V: From Harfleur to Agincourt

## 概览

This episode follows Henry V after his landing in France, beginning with the campaign against Harfleur and ending just before the Battle of Agincourt. The central question is how a campaign that began as a calculated strike on a vulnerable port became a dangerous inland march toward confrontation with a much larger French army.

The hosts emphasize that Henry’s position was both ideological and practical: he claimed to be acting as the rightful king of France, so he tried to impose discipline and limit plunder, but he also needed a visible success that would justify the enormous cost of the expedition. Harfleur was captured, yet the siege took too long, damaged the town, and weakened the English army through disease and attrition.

The second half shifts to the march from Harfleur to Calais. Henry’s army had limited food, depended heavily on archers, and had to cross the Somme while the French tried to block the obvious routes. The episode frames the approach to Agincourt as a contest of logistics, morale, political division, and calculated risk rather than as a simple prelude to a famous battle.

## 分段落总结

[01:35] **Shakespearean Opening and Campaign Recap**

[事实] The episode opens with a passage from Shakespeare’s *Henry V* describing the English fleet sailing toward France.

[事实] The hosts recap Henry V as a young, austere, serious, and intimidating war leader who has crossed the Channel to attack Harfleur.

[事实] Harfleur is described as Henry’s first target on the French mainland and as a possible springboard for a wider campaign to recover Normandy.

[推测] The Shakespearean opening frames the campaign as dramatic and national in tone, while the discussion quickly shifts to the practical risks of timing, logistics, and siege warfare.

[03:20] **Landing at Harfleur**

[事实] Henry lands near Harfleur in mid-August, later than ideal for a campaign that needs rapid progress before autumn.

[事实] John Holland, aged nineteen, scouts the landing area and reports that it is clear.

[事实] The English landing takes three days, using shallow barges to bring men and horses onto the rocky beach.

[事实] The French do not oppose the landing because they are taken by surprise and cannot assemble the resources in time.

[05:13] **Encircling a Vulnerable Port**

[事实] Henry invests Harfleur from the western side, while his brother, the Duke of Clarence, takes forces around to occupy the eastern heights.

[事实] Clarence’s route is difficult because of the river, mudflats, flooding, marshland, and tidal conditions around Harfleur.

[事实] By 19 August, Harfleur is effectively sealed off, with English forces controlling access around the town.

[事实] Harfleur’s defenses are weak: its walls are old and unrepaired, provisions are limited, and the garrison may have been very small.

[07:45] **Henry’s Rules of War**

[事实] Henry does not unfurl his banner on landing, which the hosts interpret as a sign that he is not authorizing general looting and pillage.

[事实] He issues ordinances forbidding attacks on churches, priests, women, crops, and buildings.

[事实] The hosts say Henry enforces these rules harshly, including punishing English soldiers who had committed violence before the expedition sailed.

[推测] Henry’s restraint is presented as both religious duty and political strategy, because he wants to appear as the rightful ruler of France rather than merely an English invader.

[10:08] **Harfleur Under Siege**

[事实] Harfleur is described as the first Norman town to be pulverized by artillery.

[事实] Henry’s cannon blast breaches in the walls, but the defenders use rubble to patch the gaps.

[事实] The siege lasts longer than Henry expected and causes more damage than he wanted to a town he hoped to turn into an English stronghold.

[事实] Disease spreads in the English camp, helped by marshy conditions, bad food, and poor sanitary circumstances.

[事实] Clarence and the Earl of Arundel fall sick, and the hosts use this to stress the difficulty of supplying and maintaining an army in France.

[13:26] **Harfleur Surrenders**

[事实] Harfleur asks for a three-week truce on 15 September, but Clarence allows only three days.

[事实] The garrison agrees to surrender on 22 September if no French relief force arrives.

[事实] No relief comes, and Harfleur’s leaders walk out to Henry’s tent with the keys of the town.

[事实] Henry keeps them waiting on their knees and imposes harsh terms: the garrison become prisoners, the old and infirm are expelled, and women and children may stay or leave.

[推测] The surrender gives Henry a victory, but not the quick and clean success he needed for a larger conquest of Normandy.

[15:04] **French Humiliation and Blame**

[事实] The loss of Harfleur is treated as a humiliation for France, especially because no effective relief attempt was made.

[事实] The Duke of Alençon is blamed and dismissed as commander in Normandy.

[事实] He is replaced by Marshal Boucicaut, described as one of the most famous soldiers in Christendom.

[事实] Boucicaut’s career includes fighting at Nicopolis, helping defend Constantinople, and founding a chivalric order.

[17:17] **The French Muster**

[事实] A royal proclamation in late August calls for a general summons across France to drive back the English.

[事实] The Dauphin comes to Normandy as captain general, but the hosts describe him as more of a figurehead because of inexperience and poor health.

[事实] The real military leadership is attributed to Boucicaut and Charles d’Albret, the Constable of France.

[事实] The French commanders hope to gather a large enough force before engaging Henry, but Harfleur falls before they can relieve it.

[19:36] **Henry Refuses to Go Home**

[事实] After Harfleur, Henry has only a damaged port to show for the cost of the expedition, while disease has weakened his army.

[事实] Clarence and Arundel are invalided home, and Arundel dies shortly after returning to England.

[事实] Henry challenges the Dauphin to a duel, but the Dauphin does not accept.

[事实] Henry decides to march from Harfleur to Calais rather than return directly to England.

[推测] The hosts suggest Henry may also want a battle, partly to test and demonstrate that God supports his claim to France.

[25:29] **Warnings from Calais**

[事实] William Bardolph, Lieutenant Governor of Calais, writes to the Duke of Bedford on 7 October, warning that Henry is likely to be fought within fifteen days.

[事实] Bardolph reports claims that very large French forces are assembling, though the hosts say these numbers are exaggerated.

[事实] The letter is used to show that Henry’s march is widely understood as a major gamble.

[推测] Even if the reported numbers are unreliable, the warning captures the strategic reality that the French have a much larger force available.

[27:01] **March Logistics**

[事实] Henry aims to march from Harfleur to Calais, a route he expects to be about 150 miles and to take eight days.

[事实] He leaves the sick behind, takes horses, leaves the cannon, and carries large quantities of arrows.

[事实] The army carries eight days of food because Henry does not want his men plundering French civilians.

[事实] The hosts connect Henry’s concern about arrows to his memory of Shrewsbury, where running out of arrows had doomed Hotspur’s side.

[28:48] **The Somme Problem**

[事实] To reach Calais, Henry must cross the River Somme, where an army is vulnerable during passage.

[事实] The French strategy is to block obvious crossings and force Henry inland, away from Calais and beyond his food supply.

[事实] The key ford at Blanchetaque is fortified by d’Albret with men and stakes.

[事实] Other French forces destroy bridges and causeways along the Somme to make crossing harder.

[31:11] **Turning Inland**

[事实] Henry captures a prisoner who tells him that Blanchetaque is defended.

[事实] After questioning the prisoner and consulting his commanders, Henry decides not to risk the crossing.

[事实] The English turn inland along the Somme rather than return to Harfleur.

[推测] The decision shows Henry’s determination to avoid public humiliation, even though it pushes his army deeper into danger.

[32:44] **Discipline Under Strain**

[事实] As the English march inland, hunger and anxiety grow because the original eight-day plan is failing.

[事实] Henry continues to enforce his ordinances and restricts dealings with the French to selected officers.

[事实] A soldier who steals a silver fitting from a church is publicly hanged.

[事实] English soldiers are ordered to wear the cross of St George so they can be identified.

[事实] The hosts cite French chroniclers who acknowledge English discipline and contrast it with French looting during the campaign.

[36:59] **Crossing the Somme**

[事实] On 19 October, the English learn of two possible fords ahead.

[事实] The approaches are marshy and the causeways have been damaged, but the far bank is not guarded.

[事实] Archers cross first with their bows and quivers held above their heads, then take defensive positions on the far bank.

[事实] The causeways are repaired enough for men to cross three abreast, and the army and baggage train use separate crossings.

[事实] French horsemen observe the crossing but do not have enough force to attack.

[39:33] **French Concentration**

[事实] Once the English cross the Somme, the French no longer need to guard the whole river and can concentrate to block the road to Calais.

[事实] The English are tired, wet, and hungry after the difficult march.

[事实] A French council of war on 19 October decides that if the English cross, the French should summon as many men as possible and give battle.

[推测] The Somme crossing boosts English morale briefly, but strategically it also moves them toward the battlefield the French are preparing.

[41:26] **French Politics and Absences**

[事实] The French king and Dauphin are not sent into battle because of the risk of capture and ransom.

[事实] The Dauphin remains behind partly to guard Paris against possible Burgundian moves.

[事实] The Duke of Orléans joins the army as a royal representative.

[事实] John the Fearless, Duke of Burgundy, and his son Philip do not appear at Agincourt, though some Burgundian relatives and retainers do.

[推测] The hosts suggest John the Fearless may be deliberately sitting out the battle in the hope that an Armagnac defeat would benefit him politically.

[45:35] **Challenge to Battle**

[事实] On 20 October, the French send a herald to Henry formally challenging him to battle.

[事实] Henry replies that there is no need to choose a day or place because the French can find him in the open fields.

[事实] Henry orders men entitled to coats of arms to wear them, signaling readiness for combat.

[事实] The English continue marching toward Calais despite expecting the French to attack.

[46:30] **The Road to Agincourt**

[事实] The English notice churned-up roads showing that a large French force has moved ahead of them.

[事实] They endure several days of grim weather, low food supplies, and difficult terrain.

[事实] By 24 October, they are about forty miles from Calais.

[事实] Henry’s scouts report that large numbers of French troops are gathering beyond the River Ternoise.

[事实] The English cross the river, reach a ridge, and see the French ranks blocking the way near a village identified as Agincourt.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it treats Agincourt not as an isolated miracle battle, but as the result of siege delay, disease, food planning, river crossings, morale, and French political fragmentation.

[事实] The hosts use a mix of Shakespeare, letters, eyewitness testimony, chronicles, and modern historical interpretation to build the narrative.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode ends just before the battle itself and postpones the contested question of army sizes to the next installment, so listeners wanting the tactical explanation of Agincourt will need the follow-up.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in medieval warfare, Henry V, the Hundred Years’ War, and the operational lead-up to famous battles rather than only battlefield drama.
