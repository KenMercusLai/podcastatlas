# Evita and Perón: The Affair That Became a Political Myth

## 概览

This episode traces how Eva Duarte, already a rising radio actress by 1944, became entangled personally and politically with Colonel Juan Domingo Perón. It begins with her voice in a propaganda-style radio drama praising an unnamed “soldier of the people,” clearly pointing to Perón.

The hosts place Perón inside Argentina’s unstable politics: the corrupt “infamous decade,” the 1943 military coup, the army’s admiration for German and Italian models, and Perón’s strategic turn toward labour politics. They argue that Peronism was less a fixed ideology than a theatrical, emotional, improvised political style.

The episode’s central tension is Eva’s transformation. At first she appears mainly as Perón’s girlfriend and a show-business figure, but she increasingly becomes a vehicle for his message and later a mythic bridge between Perón and the poor. The climax is 17 October 1945, when workers flood Buenos Aires to demand Perón’s release, an event later mythologised around Eva despite little evidence that she organised it.

## 分段落总结

[01:13] **A Radio Voice Introduces the Political Theme**

[事实] The episode opens with a 14 August 1944 Argentine radio program dramatizing poverty, tuberculosis, illiteracy and undernourishment across Argentina.

[事实] Eva Duarte plays “the woman” and closes the program by praising a “soldier of the people” who brings dignity to work and social justice.

[事实] The hosts identify the unnamed figure as Colonel Juan Domingo Perón, already the most significant political figure in Argentina in 1944.

[推测] The opening positions Eva’s public voice as already serving Perón’s political myth before she becomes Eva Perón.

[03:42] **Eva’s Rise from Actress to Symbol**

[事实] By early 1944 Eva has moved from the rural Pampa to Buenos Aires and, after poverty and struggle, become an established radio actress.

[事实] She has moved from playing maids and secretaries to playing famous women such as Elizabeth I, Catherine the Great, Lady Hamilton and Madame Chiang Kai-shek.

[事实] The hosts describe a tension in Eva’s story: she becomes remembered as a strong woman, yet her political persona is deeply tied to self-abnegation before Perón.

[推测] Her acting career gave her the tools for political performance, even before she had an independent political role.

[05:42] **Argentina Before Perón**

[事实] Eva had to obtain military permission for her “Heroines of History” radio work because Argentina had experienced a military coup in 1943.

[事实] The hosts describe Argentine politics as chaotic, with the 1930s remembered as the “infamous decade” of corrupt oligarchic rule.

[事实] Young military officers resented conservative landowners, British ownership of railways, and foreign control of the economy.

[事实] The 1943 coup swept away the existing government, and Juan Perón emerged as the major figure among the officers.

[08:00] **Juan Perón’s Background and Personality**

[事实] Perón was born in 1895, about twenty years older than Eva, and came from Buenos Aires province before being sent to boarding school in Buenos Aires.

[事实] He was athletic, liked individual sports and fencing, and did not especially like football or team sports.

[事实] The hosts describe him as tall, burly, charming, humorous, easy-going and personally popular, contrasting him with more rigid fascist figures.

[事实] His first wife, Aurelia Tizon, a schoolteacher and guitarist, died of uterine cancer in 1938.

[推测] The hosts suggest Perón’s relative lack of vanity and ability to laugh at himself helped make him politically attractive.

[11:11] **A Soldier Who Rarely Saw War**

[事实] Perón rose through the Argentine military in the 1930s, when Argentina was not fighting major wars.

[事实] He worked as a history professor at the Military Academy and studied campaigns such as the Russo-Japanese War, the Franco-Prussian War and the First World War.

[事实] The hosts say he probably never fired a shot in anger and emotionally seemed to dislike violence.

[推测] Argentina’s ceremonial military culture may have shaped Perón’s taste for uniforms, spectacle and martial imagery without battlefield experience.

[12:58] **Perón and Fascism**

[事实] In 1939 Perón was sent to Italy as a military attaché and specialised in alpine troops.

[事实] He admired the order and efficiency of Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy, and he looked up to Mussolini.

[事实] The hosts say he was attracted to corporatism, where capital and labour were united behind a single leader.

[事实] He was appalled by post-Civil War Spain and later discouraged anti-Semitic measures among his own followers.

[推测] The episode presents Perón as fascist-influenced in style and institutional imagination, but not simply an ideological Nazi or Franco-style fascist.

[16:38] **Peronism as Style, Emotion and Improvisation**

[事实] American reports in 1943 described Perón as energetic, affable, decisive, humorous and capable of violence.

[事实] The OSS predicted he could become Argentina’s president through a managed democracy with legal appearance but authoritarian reality.

[事实] The hosts describe Peronism as rooted in rallies, ritual, spectacle, flags and intense sentimentality.

[事实] They cite the idea that “there was no one Perón,” but rather a series of Peróns adapting to circumstance.

[推测] Peronism’s flexibility helps explain why later left-wing and right-wing Peronists could both claim the same tradition.

[19:30] **Perón Builds a Labour Base**

[事实] After 1943 Perón became number two in the War Ministry and requested control of the Department of Labour.

[事实] He calculated that many recently arrived, underrepresented workers in Buenos Aires could become a loyal base if he founded unions, met leaders and delivered benefits.

[事实] A Chilean journalist predicted that if Perón succeeded with labour, he could become Argentina’s great strongman or caudillo.

[推测] Perón’s labour strategy turned an unglamorous ministry into the foundation of mass power.

[20:46] **The San Juan Earthquake and the Meeting with Eva**

[事实] In January 1944 a major earthquake in San Juan killed at least 10,000 people.

[事实] Perón organised relief efforts and arranged for actors, actresses and singers to collect money in Buenos Aires.

[事实] Eva attended the Luna Park gala connected to the relief campaign, where Perón later claimed they first met.

[事实] The hosts say many people doubt she made an impression on him earlier that day, but the gala is where the relationship begins in the story.

[推测] Disaster relief gave Perón a public stage and gave Eva access to the political world she would soon enter.

[22:59] **Eva Replaces Perón’s Young Girlfriend**

[事实] Perón already had a very young girlfriend, probably called Maria Ines, whom he referred to as “the Piranha.”

[事实] Eva reportedly removed the girlfriend’s belongings by truck and sent her back to Mendoza.

[事实] Eva and Perón were publicly seen together on 3 February and from then on were a couple.

[事实] Some army colleagues disliked Eva, calling her common, brash and unsuitable.

[推测] The story shows Eva as impetuous and forceful, while Perón appears calmer and more calculating.

[27:03] **Mistress, Actress and Believer**

[事实] After the break, the hosts say Perón did not initially view Eva as a political asset, but mainly as his mistress.

[事实] She was present during meetings in his apartment but usually made coffee and did not speak much.

[事实] She continued her show-business career and benefited from being connected to Perón.

[事实] Eva was strongly drawn to Perón’s message about lifting up the working classes and the “grasitas,” people like those from her own background.

[推测] Her personal history made Perón’s labour rhetoric feel directly addressed to her.

[31:00] **Eva Starts Becoming the Message**

[事实] In interviews Eva still gave Hollywood-style answers, claiming interests such as riding horses, speaking French, reading Tolstoy and admiring Plutarch.

[事实] The hosts say Plutarch was Perón’s favourite author and doubt that Eva had really read him.

[事实] In 1945 Eva secured scarce film stock to make La Pródiga, about a woman known as the mother of the poor who sacrifices herself through good works.

[推测] Choosing La Pródiga suggests Eva was beginning to identify with a public role of charity, sacrifice and mediation between power and the poor.

[33:51] **Postwar Pressure and Spruille Braden**

[事实] By 1945 the Second World War was ending, and many Argentine military figures had sympathies toward Germany and Italy.

[事实] American ambassador Spruille Braden denounced Perón as a Nazi and encouraged liberal politicians to move against the military regime.

[事实] Perón responded by speaking of foreign reactionaries and plutocrats trying to bring him down.

[推测] Braden’s opposition helped Perón frame himself as a nationalist defender against foreign and elite interference.

[36:11] **A Patronage Dispute Triggers Perón’s Fall**

[事实] On 9 October, a new communications secretary was appointed who was an old contact of Eva’s.

[事实] The job had been promised to an army officer, and other officers saw the appointment as proof that Eva and her circle were gaining influence.

[事实] Eva’s brother Juan had already been given a role as Perón’s secretary and was widely disliked.

[事实] Perón was then forced to resign after complicated internal manoeuvring.

[推测] Anti-Eva resentment combined misogyny, class prejudice and real anxiety about patronage networks.

[37:38] **Resignation, Arrest and the Myth of Eva’s Rescue**

[事实] Perón resigned with an emotional speech at the Labour Secretariat, promising he would remain with the workers “until forever.”

[事实] He was later arrested and taken to an island in the River Plate delta.

[事实] The hosts reject the musical’s version in which Eva rouses the streets to save him.

[事实] They say there is no evidence Eva organised the mobilisation and that she lacked the contacts to do so at that stage.

[推测] Both Peronist and anti-Peronist versions later exaggerated Eva’s role for different political reasons.

[40:35] **17 October 1945 and the Descamisados**

[事实] The CGT and union allies called for a general strike and a march on the Casa Rosada to demand Perón’s release.

[事实] Workers from the industrial belt around Buenos Aires filled the city centre, surprising the authorities.

[事实] Hundreds of thousands gathered outside the Casa Rosada, and many were poor workers from the edge of the city.

[事实] Because of the heat, people jumped into fountains and removed shirts, giving rise to the term descamisados, “the shirtless ones.”

[事实] Perón appeared on the balcony, the crowd cried, and no one died that day despite later mythmaking.

[推测] The event fused class visibility, street power and theatrical emotion into the founding ritual of Peronism.

[44:09] **Eva’s Later Place in the 17 October Myth**

[事实] Later mythic accounts placed Eva at the centre of 17 October, walking through factories and stirring crowds.

[事实] The hosts say there is no contemporary evidence she was even there, though one host guesses she might have been.

[事实] Perón, not Eva, gave the balcony speech and declared his desire to be loved by the crowd.

[事实] The crowd chanted “Tomorrow is Saint Perón’s day; let the bosses do the work,” and a general strike followed.

[推测] Eva’s later identification with 17 October and the descamisados helped her imagine herself as the mystical bridge between Perón and the people.

[47:55] **Marriage, Erasure and the Birth of Evita Perón**

[事实] Five days after 17 October, Eva and Perón married in a brief civil ceremony in their apartment.

[事实] The marriage record contained falsehoods: the place, date of birth, age and name were wrong.

[事实] Eva claimed the surname Duarte and a 1922 birth year rather than 1919, concealing her illegitimacy.

[事实] Someone later removed and replaced her birth certificate in Los Toldos with false details.

[事实] After the wedding, Eva destroyed publicity stills, photographers’ negatives and evidence of her acting career; La Pródiga was privately shown to her but never released.

[推测] The episode frames this as the death of Eva Duarte and the emergence of Evita Perón, the politician.

## 播客点评/总结

[事实] The episode’s strength is its careful separation of myth from evidence, especially around 17 October 1945 and Eva’s supposed role in mobilising the crowds.

[推测] Its biggest value is showing Peronism as a fusion of labour politics, nationalism, spectacle and emotional theatre rather than a tidy ideology.

[事实] The hosts spend considerable time on humour, analogies and side remarks, including comparisons with musicals, Roman history, Mussolini, Mosley and modern politicians.

[推测] This makes the episode accessible and lively, though listeners wanting a strictly linear political history may find some detours distracting.

[推测] The episode is especially useful for listeners interested in how personal biography becomes political myth, and how Eva’s acting career, class background and relationship with Perón fed into the creation of Evita.
