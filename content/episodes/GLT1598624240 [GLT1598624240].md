# The Charge of the Light Brigade: Heroism, Catastrophe, and the Battle of Balaclava

## Overview

The episode reconstructs the Battle of Balaclava on 25 October 1854, explaining why the small port was vital to British operations in Crimea and how the surrounding ridges and valleys shaped the fighting. The hosts emphasize that commanders on the Sapuni Heights could see the whole battlefield, while officers below had a dangerously limited view.

Before the famous charge, the 93rd Highlanders repelled Russian cavalry in the action remembered as the “Thin Red Line,” and the Heavy Brigade won an improbable uphill cavalry victory. Yet personal hostility, ambiguous orders, and failures of communication then turned a limited objective—preventing the Russians from removing captured British guns—into a frontal attack down the North Valley.

The Light Brigade reached and temporarily silenced the Russian battery, but at devastating cost. The episode presents the charge as both an extraordinary display of discipline and courage and a grave strategic blunder whose consequences worsened British supply problems during the siege of Sevastopol.

## Segmented Summary

[03:36] **Tennyson, War Reporting, and the Making of a Legend**

[事实] Alfred Lord Tennyson wrote his celebrated poem after reading William Howard Russell’s report in *The Times* about the Charge of the Light Brigade.

[事实] Russell was the first correspondent employed by a British newspaper to cover an entire military campaign on location.

[事实] The hosts argue that the episode is unusually rich in detailed contemporary evidence while also being transformed into legend through poetry.

[推测] Without Russell’s reporting and Tennyson’s poem, the charge might not have retained the same cultural prominence.

[05:21] **Why Balaclava Mattered**

[事实] Balaclava was the British army’s maritime lifeline, connecting the siege positions outside Sevastopol to Royal Navy ships and essential supplies.

[事实] By 24 October, a Russian force of roughly 20,000 infantry, 3,400 cavalry, and 78 guns had assembled near Chorgan.

[事实] The British were outnumbered by about five to one on the Balaclava battlefield, although the terrain complicated the Russian advance.

[事实] Losing Balaclava would have endangered the siege of Sevastopol and potentially the wider British war effort.

[07:32] **The Battlefield’s Decisive Geography**

[事实] The battlefield consisted of the South Valley, the Causeway Heights, the North Valley, and the Fedia Kine Heights, overlooked from the west by the much higher Sapuni Heights.

[事实] The strategically important Vorontsov Road ran along the Causeway Heights and connected Balaclava with Sevastopol.

[事实] Lord Raglan and William Howard Russell observed the battle from the Sapuni Heights, where they could see terrain and troop movements hidden from commanders in the valleys.

[推测] This unequal visibility was central to the later misunderstanding: Raglan issued orders based on information that Lucan could not see for himself.

[12:10] **The Defences of Balaclava**

[事实] Six earthwork redoubts defended the Causeway Heights, with approximately 1,000 Ottoman troops commanded by British artillery officers.

[事实] The 93rd Highlanders guarded the road toward Balaclava near Kadikoi, while the Light and Heavy Brigades were positioned farther northwest.

[事实] Lord Lucan commanded the cavalry, while his brother-in-law and bitter enemy Lord Cardigan commanded the Light Brigade.

[事实] Raglan had previously restrained the cavalry because disease had depleted both men and horses, although Lucan was widely blamed for their inactivity.

[17:41] **The Russian Assault on Canrobert Hill**

[事实] At around 6:00 a.m., artillery fire announced the beginning of the Russian attack on the easternmost and strongest redoubt at Canrobert Hill.

[事实] The Ottoman defenders resisted for approximately ninety minutes before the Russians broke into the position at about 7:30 a.m.

[事实] Perhaps one-third of the Ottoman garrison was killed, while the survivors retreated into the South Valley under pursuit by Cossacks.

[事实] The Russians then moved west along the Causeway Heights, capturing the remaining redoubts as British and French reinforcements began a descent that would take roughly two hours.

[22:17] **The Thin Red Line**

[事实] Shortly after 9:00 a.m., Russian cavalry descended toward Sir Colin Campbell’s heavily outnumbered 93rd Highlanders and the road to Balaclava.

[事实] Instead of forming the conventional infantry square, Campbell arranged his men in two lines to maximize the firepower of their new rifles.

[事实] Repeated volleys stopped the Russian cavalry and forced it to withdraw before reaching the Highlanders.

[事实] Russell described the Highlanders as a “thin red streak topped with a line of steel,” a phrase subsequently remembered as the “Thin Red Line.”

[24:59] **The Heavy Brigade’s Uphill Victory**

[事实] Another large body of Russian cavalry approached the Heavy Brigade, which responded by charging uphill under Sir James Scarlett.

[事实] Successive British formations, including the Scots Greys and Inniskillings, entered a fierce cavalry melee despite appearing to be swallowed by the larger Russian force.

[事实] After about ten minutes, the Russians broke and withdrew.

[事实] Cardigan’s Light Brigade was well placed to pursue the retreating cavalry, but Cardigan believed Lucan had ordered him not to attack.

[推测] The hostility and poor communication between Cardigan and Lucan helped squander an opportunity for a more decisive British victory.

[30:00] **Ambiguous Orders and the Captured Guns**

[事实] After the earlier British successes, Raglan ordered Lucan to advance and recover the redoubts on the Causeway Heights.

[事实] Lucan interpreted the ambiguously worded instruction as requiring him to wait for infantry reinforcements, contrary to Raglan’s intention.

[事实] When Raglan saw the Russians preparing to remove captured British guns, he issued a new order directing the cavalry to advance rapidly, follow the enemy, and prevent the guns from being carried away.

[事实] Captain Louis Nolan personally carried the order down the escarpment and delivered it to Lucan.

[事实] Lucan could not see the captured guns on the Causeway Heights and found the instruction incomprehensible.

[37:36] **The Fatal Misunderstanding**

[事实] When Lucan asked which enemy and which guns he was supposed to attack, Nolan gestured eastward and told him, “There, my Lord, is your enemy; there are your guns.”

[事实] From Lucan’s position, the only visible guns were the Russian battery more than a mile away at the far end of the North Valley.

[事实] Russian guns and riflemen also occupied heights on both sides, making a frontal advance into the valley an obvious death trap.

[事实] Cardigan pointed out the danger, but Lucan replied that Raglan demanded the attack and that they had no choice but to obey.

[推测] A more cooperative relationship between Lucan and Cardigan might have prompted them to question Nolan further and clarify the intended target.

[43:01] **The Light Brigade Advances**

[事实] Cardigan formed the Light Brigade in two lines, placed himself in front, and ordered it to advance at a controlled trot.

[事实] The cavalry maintained parade-ground discipline as Russian guns opened fire from ahead and from both flanks.

[事实] Nolan rode across Cardigan’s front, waving his sword and shouting something that could not be heard over the gunfire.

[事实] A shell fragment struck Nolan in the chest and killed him before he could explain his actions.

[推测] Nolan may have realized that the brigade was advancing toward the wrong guns and attempted to redirect it, but his intention cannot be confirmed.

[48:26] **Into the Valley of Death**

[事实] Cardigan continued the measured advance despite mounting casualties, repeatedly ordering his men to remain steady.

[事实] Observers on the heights reacted with horror and admiration; French general Pierre Bosquet characterized the action as magnificent but not war.

[事实] Lucan halted the exhausted Heavy Brigade after recognizing that continuing would sacrifice it as well.

[事实] The French Chasseurs d’Afrique cleared Russian troops and guns from the Fedia Kine Heights, reducing the danger faced by returning British survivors.

[事实] After about eight minutes of advance, the remaining Light Brigade accelerated into a gallop toward the Russian battery.

[52:12] **Breaking Through the Russian Battery**

[事实] A close-range Russian volley devastated the Light Brigade’s front line immediately before it reached the guns.

[事实] Cardigan was the first British rider into the battery, and the surviving cavalrymen succeeded in silencing its guns.

[事实] The Light Brigade also drove back Russian cavalry positioned beyond the battery.

[事实] Cardigan then turned back up the valley while much of the surviving brigade remained engaged near the guns.

[事实] Russian fire and the French action on the flank helped prevent Cardigan from being captured during his return.

[55:21] **The Human Cost of the Charge**

[事实] The entire action lasted only about twenty minutes.

[事实] Of more than 700 men who advanced, only 195 returned immediately, while approximately 500 horses were killed or captured.

[事实] The retreat route was filled with dead and wounded men, riderless horses, and survivors supporting one another.

[事实] Cardigan acknowledged that the attack had been a “mad-brained trick” but denied responsibility; one survivor nevertheless declared that the men were ready to go again.

[57:04] **Tactical Achievement, Strategic Disaster**

[事实] The Light Brigade reached the battery, disabled its guns, and frightened off Russian cavalry, thereby achieving a limited tactical success.

[事实] The charge nevertheless destroyed much of a cavalry force that Raglan had carefully preserved throughout the campaign.

[事实] Britain failed to regain the Causeway Heights and lost control of the paved Vorontsov Road between Balaclava and Sevastopol.

[事实] Supplies subsequently had to travel over rough, muddy routes, contributing to severe logistical difficulties during the winter siege.

[推测] The episode’s central paradox is that the charge accomplished an immediate battlefield objective while gravely weakening the wider British campaign.

[59:16] **Blame, Reputation, and Public Hero Worship**

[事实] Raglan blamed Lucan, who was recalled to Britain and denied the court-martial he requested to clear his name.

[事实] Lucan’s reputation later recovered, and he was appointed a field marshal in 1887.

[事实] Cardigan returned to Britain as a celebrated hero, received an enthusiastic welcome, and publicly blamed Lucan while emphasizing his own courage.

[事实] Cardigan later became widely unpopular again and died in 1868 after suffering a stroke and falling from his horse.

[事实] His charger Ronald outlived him by four years, and the horse’s preserved head remained at the family seat, Deene Park.

[64:01] **Balaclava’s Wider Consequences**

[事实] Raglan died in Crimea in June 1855, officially from dysentery, after becoming deeply conscious of public criticism in Britain.

[事实] The loss of the Vorontsov Road made the siege positions harder to supply during a brutal winter in which thousands of British soldiers died from disease and exposure.

[事实] Russell’s reporting helped establish the image of ordinary soldiers as “lions led by donkeys” and intensified demands for reform.

[事实] The failures of aristocratic commanders created a public appetite for a different kind of Crimean War hero, anticipating the rise of Florence Nightingale.

[推测] The charge became enduringly powerful because it could simultaneously support admiration for the soldiers and condemnation of the system that sacrificed them.

[69:03] **The Surviving Trumpet Call**

[事实] The main narrative concludes with a historical recording made on 2 August 1890 by Martin Leonard Landfried, a trumpeter who survived the charge.

[事实] The recording preserves a trumpet call associated with the day of the Charge of the Light Brigade.

[推测] Ending with the survivor’s trumpet shifts the episode from analysis back to commemoration, giving the soldiers themselves the symbolic final word.

## Podcast Commentary/Summary

The episode’s greatest strength is its careful reconstruction of the battlefield. Its explanation of the valleys, ridges, roads, and different lines of sight makes the fatal misunderstanding intelligible: the charge did not result from a single inexplicable decision but from geography, ambiguous language, hierarchy, rivalry, and urgency acting together.

The discussion also avoids reducing the participants to simple heroes or villains. Cardigan and Lucan are presented as personally courageous yet deeply flawed, while Raglan’s distant vantage point gives him superior information without guaranteeing effective command. The contrast between individual bravery and institutional failure gives the story much of its force.

A limitation is the hosts’ frequent comic treatment of class, personality, and minor characters, which occasionally interrupts the military narrative. Some claims about motives—especially Nolan’s final intention and the emotional causes of Raglan’s decline—remain necessarily uncertain.

The episode is particularly suitable for listeners interested in military history, leadership, battlefield communication, Victorian public culture, and the role of journalism and poetry in shaping historical memory.
