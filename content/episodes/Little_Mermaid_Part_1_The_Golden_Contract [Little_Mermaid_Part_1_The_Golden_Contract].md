# The Little Mermaid, Part 1: The Golden Contract

## 概览

This episode of *Revisionist History* asks what Disney’s *The Little Mermaid* teaches children about law, morality, and justice. Malcolm Gladwell draws on research by law professor Laura Beth Nielsen, who argues that the film presents Ariel’s exploitative contract with Ursula as legally binding and unbreakable—even though it involves a minor surrendering her voice and risking lifelong enslavement.

The discussion traces the story from Hans Christian Andersen’s tragic original to Disney’s optimistic adaptation. Andersen’s tale centers on exclusion, sacrifice, and unrequited love, while Disney recasts it as a struggle between good and evil in which romantic love triumphs.

The central criticism is not simply that Ursula’s contract is immoral, but that the film depicts law as a weapon controlled by villains and violence as the only effective remedy. Because fairy tales help children form early ideas about fairness and authority, the episode argues that such narrative choices deserve serious scrutiny.

## 分段落总结

[02:06] **A Parent Reconsiders a Familiar Disney Film**

[事实] Northwestern University law professor and social scientist Laura Beth Nielsen began analyzing Disney films after repeatedly watching them with her two young sons.

[事实] *The Little Mermaid* prompted her to think about the legal and moral messages embedded in children’s entertainment.

[事实] Gladwell introduces the episode as the first installment of a three-part attempt to determine whether Disney’s version of the story can be fixed.

[06:33] **Fairy Tales as Moral Frameworks**

[事实] Nielsen says that young children begin seeking explanations for rules and obligations at the same time they first encounter fairy tales.

[事实] The episode argues that fairy tales supply frameworks through which children understand legality, morality, fairness, and acceptable behavior.

[推测] This makes children’s films consequential forms of moral education rather than harmless entertainment requiring no analysis.

[08:23] **Challenging Disney’s Cultural Power**

[事实] Gladwell jokes about the risks of criticizing Disney and recounts the company’s prolonged lawsuit against underground cartoonist Dan O’Neill over an unauthorized Mickey Mouse parody.

[事实] Disney pursued O’Neill for years despite the parody’s small circulation and his lack of financial resources.

[事实] Gladwell says the podcast’s criticism comes from affection for *The Little Mermaid* and a desire to give Ariel a more just story.

[11:16] **Hans Christian Andersen’s Tragic Original**

[事实] Andersen’s mermaid wants to become human partly because humans possess immortal souls.

[事实] The sea witch turns her tail into legs, but every step causes terrible pain; she also cuts out the mermaid’s tongue, depriving her of speech and song.

[事实] The mermaid must win the prince’s love to remain human, but he marries another woman and she dissolves into sea foam.

[事实] Fairy-tale scholar Jack Zipes describes Andersen’s stories as narratives about transformation, outsiders seeking acceptance, and the author’s own suffering.

[14:48] **The Story’s Connection to Andersen’s Life**

[事实] Scholarship discussed in the episode connects Andersen’s tales to his insecurity, unhappiness, and frustrated longing for acceptance.

[事实] Shortly before writing *The Little Mermaid*, Andersen experienced an unrequited attachment to a young man who rejected him for a woman.

[事实] Gladwell links that experience to a story in which an outsider sacrifices her voice and identity for acceptance, only to be rejected for another woman.

[推测] The original tale can therefore be read as a deeply personal expression of exclusion and unreturned love.

[18:44] **Disney Reinvents the Mermaid**

[事实] In the 1980s, Disney’s animated-film business was struggling, and writer-director Ron Clements proposed adapting Andersen’s story after recognizing its cinematic potential.

[事实] Clements and co-director John Musker transformed the tragedy into a story of good versus evil and love overcoming obstacles.

[事实] They named the mermaid Ariel, turned the sea witch into Ursula, and introduced King Triton, Prince Eric, Sebastian, Flounder, and Scuttle.

[事实] The film became culturally influential and helped launch a celebrated period of Disney animation.

[事实] Some Danish interviewers criticized Clements for replacing Andersen’s tragic ending with a happy one.

[22:14] **Ariel’s Golden Contract**

[事实] In Disney’s version, Ariel signs a contract giving her three days to receive a kiss from Prince Eric; if she fails, she must become Ursula’s slave.

[事实] Ariel trades away her voice, and Ursula actively interferes with Ariel’s attempt to fulfill the agreement.

[事实] When King Triton tries to destroy the golden contract, Ursula declares it legal, binding, and completely unbreakable.

[事实] Nielsen argues that the agreement would plainly be unlawful because it involves a minor, the surrender of a body part, and an exploitative outcome.

[推测] The golden scroll visually equates formal documentation with absolute legitimacy, regardless of whether the bargain is just.

[24:32] **Why Children’s Narratives Merit Scrutiny**

[事实] Nielsen says law should embody justice and that a written instrument should not prevail over a just outcome merely because it exists.

[事实] Gladwell initially wondered whether academic analysis of an animated fairy tale was excessive, but concluded that stories told to children are important enough to examine closely.

[事实] The episode cites Meghan Markle’s use of *The Little Mermaid*—particularly Ariel’s loss and recovery of her voice—to describe her own experience within the British royal family.

[事实] Gladwell argues that Disney now tells many of society’s most influential fairy tales and accuses the company of becoming morally careless.

[27:44] **Violence and Torture in Family Entertainment**

[事实] In *Zootopia*, police officer Judy Hopps obtains information after a suspect is threatened with death, and the coercion produces an immediate confession.

[事实] Gladwell notes that empirical evidence does not support coercive force as the most effective way to obtain reliable information.

[事实] The episode also identifies scenes of coercion or torture in *Toy Story 3* and *The SpongeBob Movie*, where suffering is presented partly for humor.

[事实] Social scientist Casey DeLaHanty found that torture was frequently played for comedy in popular Hollywood films released between 2008 and 2017.

[推测] Repeated comic portrayals may normalize coercion by making it appear effective, routine, and morally insignificant.

[31:00] **Law as a Villain’s Weapon**

[事实] Nielsen studies how ordinary people develop practical ideas about liberty, fairness, justice, and the proper role of the state.

[事实] She argues that law should prevent conflict and represent what is right, rather than enforce an agreement involving bodily sacrifice and lifelong slavery.

[事实] In the film, even Triton’s immense magical power cannot overturn Ursula’s contract.

[事实] Ursula appears to control the legal order, while the protagonists never use arrest, trial, or any other formal legal process against her.

[推测] The film’s internal logic teaches that once an agreement is signed, procedural form can defeat justice even when the agreement is manifestly abusive.

[33:50] **Violence Replaces Justice**

[事实] Prince Eric ultimately resolves the conflict by impaling and killing Ursula with the bow of a ship.

[事实] Nielsen observes that Disney films often present law as ineffective, irrelevant, or controlled by bad actors, while violence solves the central problem.

[事实] Gladwell characterizes *The Little Mermaid* as a vigilante story in which extrajudicial killing replaces legal accountability.

[推测] The episode’s deepest objection is that the movie imagines no legitimate institution capable of correcting injustice, leaving force as the only path to liberation.

[35:37] **The Revisionist Project Continues**

[事实] Gladwell previews later episodes featuring literary interpretation, analysis of the characters, and a Hollywood screenwriter’s attempt to reimagine the film.

[事实] The broader project aims to reconstruct *The Little Mermaid* so that its moral and narrative problems receive a different resolution.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest contribution is its close reading of the golden contract. By focusing on a single familiar scene, it exposes the distinction between something being formally documented and being morally or legally valid. Nielsen’s expertise gives that critique a clear foundation in questions of justice, consent, and exploitation.

Its broader comparison with *Zootopia*, *Toy Story 3*, and *The SpongeBob Movie* usefully shows that the concern extends beyond one princess story. The episode invites parents and other viewers to notice how family entertainment depicts coercion, authority, and conflict resolution.

[推测] The analysis occasionally uses provocative analogies and comic exaggeration that may overstate how literally children absorb a film’s legal logic. The transcript does not provide evidence directly measuring the movie’s effects on children, so its claims about influence remain more interpretive than causal.

[推测] This episode is especially suitable for listeners interested in law and culture, media criticism, fairy-tale adaptation, parenting, or the hidden assumptions within familiar popular stories.
