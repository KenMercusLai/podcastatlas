# Peter the Great’s Final Years: Reform, Cruelty, and the Death of Alexis

## 概览

This episode closes the series on Peter the Great by examining the contradictions of his later reign: a ruler committed to modernization, order, science, and Russia’s rise as a great power, but also fascinated by grotesque spectacle, violent parody, and personal humiliation.

The central story is the tragedy of Peter’s son Alexis, whose temperament, religious conservatism, and attachment to old Moscow made him intolerable to his father. The episode follows Alexis from neglected heir to fugitive in Habsburg territory, then to forced return, show trial, torture, and death.

The hosts conclude that Peter’s reforms transformed Russia’s international status and made autocracy more durable, but that his violence, paranoia, and treatment of Alexis reveal the darker side of his achievement.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Weber’s Account of Peter’s Grotesque Court**

[事实] The episode opens with Friedrich Christian Weber’s description of a 1710 wedding feast in St. Petersburg where the couple and attendees were dwarfs.

[事实] The hosts say the scene reflected Peter’s dark sense of humor and his fascination with people outside ordinary physical norms, including both dwarfs and giants.

[推测] The opening anecdote frames Peter’s court as both theatrical and disturbing, not simply eccentric.

[03:02] **The Episode’s Focus**

[事实] The hosts recap that earlier episodes covered the Great Northern War, Charles XII, Poltava, and Russia’s rise among the great powers.

[事实] They state that this final episode will focus on Peter’s last years and especially the tragic story of his son Alexis.

[事实] Weber is used as a guide to how St. Petersburg and Russia appeared to Western visitors.

[04:17] **St. Petersburg as Building Site and Symbol**

[事实] Weber arrived in St. Petersburg in 1714 and found it still under construction.

[事实] Peter had effectively moved the capital there by 1712, before the city was finished.

[事实] Italian and French planners helped shape canals and a rational street network, giving St. Petersburg its “Venice of the North” reputation.

[推测] The city embodied Peter’s attempt to impose Enlightenment-style order on a harsh and costly environment.

[06:21] **Western Window, Non-Western Reality**

[事实] St. Petersburg was intended as Russia’s “window on the West,” but Weber was struck by its non-European qualities.

[事实] He noted rude guards, extravagant gold and silver clothing, heavy drinking, and the presence of envoys and goods from Central and East Asia.

[事实] The hosts describe Russia as appearing like a crossroads between Europe and Asia.

[推测] Peter’s westernization did not make Russia simply European; it created a hybrid court culture that could seem exotic and unsettling to Western observers.

[09:37] **Dwarfs, Giants, and Parody**

[事实] Dwarfs were common at European courts, but Peter’s fixation was described as unusually intense.

[事实] Peter kept dwarf performers, attended church with dwarf escorts, and enjoyed spectacles such as dwarfs emerging from pies.

[事实] He also kept the giant Nicolas Bourgeois and tried to arrange a marriage that might produce more giants.

[推测] The hosts suggest Peter’s grotesque spectacles may have mocked elite and Orthodox rituals while also undermining conventional dignity around the throne.

[12:34] **Zotov’s Mock Wedding and Ritual Inversion**

[事实] Weber witnessed the 1715 wedding of Nikita Zotov, Peter’s elderly former tutor and mock pope of the “all drunken synod.”

[事实] Peter arranged a deliberately grotesque ceremony using stammerers, infirm attendants, a very old priest, and a mock tsar carried by bears.

[事实] The hosts describe the event as both a real wedding and an elaborate parody.

[推测] This kind of performance makes Peter’s world difficult to interpret because official ritual, drunken theater, and cruelty blurred together.

[14:00] **Reform and War-Driven Modernization**

[事实] The hosts stress that Peter was not only a figure of chaos; the Great Northern War accelerated his modernization of Russia.

[事实] He invested in factories, textiles, iron and copper works, canals, a new tax system, a Senate, and government colleges modeled partly on Sweden.

[事实] The Table of Ranks created military, civilian, and court ladders of status.

[推测] These reforms pushed Russia toward a more service-based state, though not a true meritocracy.

[15:33] **Corruption, Autocracy, and Contradiction**

[事实] Russia under Peter remained corrupt and authoritarian.

[事实] Alexander Menshikov is presented as a highly corrupt favorite whom Peter repeatedly forgave.

[事实] Pavel Yagushinsky reportedly warned Peter that executing every official who stole would leave him ruling alone.

[推测] The hosts connect Peter’s grotesque humor and violence to the tension between his reforming ambitions and the realities of corruption and autocratic power.

[18:36] **Was Peter an Enlightened Despot?**

[事实] The hosts compare Peter with later enlightened despots such as Frederick the Great and Joseph II, and even with Napoleon as a modernizing autocrat.

[事实] Peter shared traits such as suspicion of organized religion, interest in science and geography, and enthusiasm for top-down modernization.

[事实] They also emphasize that Peter’s fascination with violence and grotesquery makes him highly unusual.

[推测] Peter can be placed in a broader tradition of modernizing autocracy, but he does not fit neatly into the category of enlightened despot.

[19:45] **Peter as European Celebrity and Collector of Curiosities**

[事实] Peter toured Western Europe again in 1716-1717, visited France, met Louis XV, and questioned people about science, theology, taxation, and policing.

[事实] Robert K. Massie’s biography is used to describe Peter’s restless energy, notebooks, chess, lathe work, sailing, and unusual habits.

[事实] In 1718 Peter demanded “freaks and monsters” for his Kunstkamera, which displayed preserved anatomical specimens and the skeleton of Nicolas Bourgeois.

[推测] Peter’s curiosity about science and anatomy sits close to a disturbing appetite for spectacle.

[22:47] **Peter’s Violence and the Introduction of Alexis**

[事实] The hosts describe Peter as extremely violent, including beating officials, punching friends, and sometimes drawing his sword at feasts.

[事实] They present this as crucial background for understanding him as a father.

[事实] Alexis was born in February 1690 to Peter and Eudokia, whom Peter later forced into a nunnery.

[推测] Alexis grew up marked by Peter’s contempt for his mother and by his father’s emotional absence.

[24:47] **Alexis’s Character and Fear of Peter**

[事实] Alexis was bright and studious as a child, but became anxious, melancholy, and terrified of his father.

[事实] He loved Moscow, Orthodox ritual, icons, chanting, and the old religious world Peter despised.

[事实] Priests and conservative relatives saw Alexis as a possible future champion of the old ways.

[事实] In Dresden, fear of failing Peter’s expectations over geometrical drawings led Alexis to try to injure his own hand.

[推测] Alexis’s personality and political associations made him appear to Peter not just weak, but potentially dangerous.

[26:39] **Marriage to Charlotte and Deepening Collapse**

[事实] Peter arranged Alexis’s marriage to Charlotte of Wolfenbüttel, and they married in 1711.

[事实] Alexis later drank heavily, abused Charlotte verbally, neglected her, and kept a Finnish mistress named Afrosenia.

[事实] Charlotte gave birth to a son in 1715, the future Peter II, and died nine days later of postpartum fever.

[事实] On the day of Charlotte’s funeral, Peter gave Alexis a harsh letter threatening to remove him from the succession.

[29:41] **Succession Crisis and Alexis’s Disappearance**

[事实] Alexis’s friends advised him to renounce the throne, and he wrote to Peter saying he was unfit to govern.

[事实] Peter replied that Alexis must either become worthy of the succession or become a monk.

[事实] When Alexis said he would become a monk, Peter hesitated and gave him six more months.

[事实] In 1716 Peter demanded a final answer, but Alexis disappeared after reaching Danzig.

[33:19] **Alexis Flees to Habsburg Protection**

[事实] Alexis appeared in Vienna on 10 November 1716, terrified and seeking protection.

[事实] Emperor Charles VI hid him first at Ehrenberg Castle in the Tyrol and later moved him secretly to Naples.

[事实] Afrosenia traveled with Alexis disguised as a boy and was pregnant.

[事实] Russian agents traced Alexis’s movements and eventually found him in Naples.

[37:47] **Tolstoy Brings Alexis Back**

[事实] Peter sent Count Pyotr Tolstoy to recover Alexis.

[事实] Tolstoy confronted Alexis in Naples with a letter from Peter promising pardon if he returned and threatening him if he refused.

[事实] Tolstoy bribed Afrosenia to persuade Alexis to return.

[事实] Alexis agreed to go back on the conditions that he could live quietly in the country and stay with Afrosenia.

[41:09] **Return, Interrogation, and Show Trials**

[事实] On 3 February 1718, Alexis was brought before Peter in the Kremlin and begged for pardon.

[事实] Peter demanded that Alexis name everyone he had spoken to about their relationship.

[事实] Alexis produced a large list, after which Peter ordered a broad roundup of relatives, clergy, courtiers, tutors, officers, and servants.

[推测] The hosts see this as connected to Peter’s long-standing paranoia about conspiracy, recalling earlier repression of the Streltsy and anticipating later Russian show trials.

[43:27] **Eudokia, Glebov, and the Search for Conspiracy**

[事实] Alexis’s mother Eudokia was among those implicated because she had corresponded with him.

[事实] Peter’s agents found that Eudokia had stopped living as a nun and had a relationship with Stepan Glebov, the captain of her guards.

[事实] Nuns were flogged, Eudokia was sent to a remote convent, and other accused people were beaten, exiled, mutilated, or executed.

[事实] Glebov was tortured and impaled, while no real conspiracy was found.

[推测] Peter’s response suggests that the absence of proof did not weaken his belief in hidden enemies.

[46:41] **Afrosenia’s Testimony Against Alexis**

[事实] Alexis remained under house arrest in St. Petersburg and repeatedly begged to see Afrosenia.

[事实] When questioned by Peter, Afrosenia said Alexis had complained about Peter, welcomed rumors of mutiny, and discussed undoing Peter’s reforms.

[事实] She claimed Alexis wanted to abandon St. Petersburg, return power to the church, and give up Peter’s foreign conquests.

[事实] Afrosenia was not punished.

[推测] Her testimony gave Peter the confirmation he wanted, whether or not it proved an organized conspiracy.

[48:55] **Trial, Torture, and Death of Alexis**

[事实] Alexis was imprisoned in the Peter and Paul Fortress, and his trial began on 14 June 1718.

[事实] Peter acted as prosecutor and accused Alexis of plotting with the Habsburg emperor, Russian rebels, and foreign forces.

[事实] Alexis confessed to a conspiracy that the hosts say historians generally do not believe existed.

[事实] He was lashed with the knout on 19 June and again five days later, then sentenced to death.

[51:23] **The Official Story and the Likely Reality**

[事实] The official story claimed Alexis died after a stroke, repentance, reconciliation with Peter, and last rites.

[事实] The fortress logbook instead shows that Peter, Tolstoy, Menshikov, and others had Alexis brought to the torture chamber for three hours on the morning he died.

[事实] The hosts say Alexis was effectively tortured to death by his own father.

[推测] The hosts treat this as one of the darkest episodes of Peter’s reign because it was prolonged and deliberate rather than a sudden act of rage.

[53:06] **Succession After Alexis**

[事实] After Alexis’s death, Peter had daughters Anna and Elizabeth, and Alexis’s young son Peter, but no obvious ideal successor.

[事实] Peter had his wife Catherine crowned empress in 1724, signaling that she would succeed him.

[事实] Catherine had begun life as Marta Skavronska, an illiterate Lithuanian peasant girl and prisoner.

[推测] Catherine’s elevation shows how completely Peter’s autocratic authority could override older expectations of dynastic order.

[54:00] **Peter’s Final Illness and Death**

[事实] In his early fifties, Peter was physically and emotionally declining, weighed down by corruption scandals and decades of drinking.

[事实] He suffered a severe urinary infection and bladder problems, requiring catheter treatment.

[事实] In January 1725 he wrote “leave everything to...” before collapsing and never waking.

[事实] He died the next morning with Catherine at his bedside.

[57:00] **Aftermath and Durability of Peter’s System**

[事实] Catherine succeeded Peter, while Menshikov became the real power behind the throne.

[事实] After Catherine came Alexis’s son Peter II, then Anna, then the infant Ivan, then Peter’s daughter Elizabeth.

[事实] The hosts note that Russia was ruled for decades by women, children, and weaker figures, yet remained a great power.

[推测] Peter’s transformation of the state made the autocracy durable even when individual rulers were unimpressive.

[58:18] **Peter’s Legacy**

[事实] The hosts describe the paradox that Peter westernized Russia while strengthening centralized autocracy.

[事实] Later Russian thinkers disputed his legacy: Slavophiles distrusted him, early communist historians downplayed him, while Stalin and Vladimir Putin invoked him.

[事实] Putin is said to have compared the war in Ukraine with the Great Northern War and Peter’s recovery of supposedly Russian lands.

[推测] Peter remains central to debates about Russia’s identity because he embodies both Westernizing ambition and coercive imperial power.

[60:13] **Final Judgment**

[事实] The hosts call Peter a titanic figure whose reign shaped geopolitics into the present.

[事实] They identify attractive qualities in him, including curiosity, energy, enthusiasm for novelty, and love of practical learning.

[事实] They also emphasize cruelty, paranoia, violence, and autocracy, especially in the deaths and tortures connected to the Streltsy and Alexis.

[推测] The episode’s final judgment is deliberately ambivalent: Peter was transformative, but the human cost and moral horror of his rule cannot be separated from his achievements.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it refuses to treat Peter the Great as either a simple modernizer or a simple monster. It shows how reform, curiosity, spectacle, and violence coexisted in one reign, and how Peter’s drive to westernize Russia could strengthen rather than soften autocratic rule.

Its strongest section is the long account of Alexis, which turns a dynastic dispute into a psychological and political tragedy. The hosts connect family trauma, religious conservatism, state paranoia, and show-trial logic without losing sight of the personal cruelty involved.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in Russian history, autocracy, political modernization, and morally complicated rulers. [推测] Its limitation is that the vivid storytelling and dark anecdotes dominate the episode, so listeners looking for a systematic institutional analysis of Peter’s reforms may need supplementary reading.
