# Reclaiming the Human Voice: Bionics, AI, and the Future of Communication

## Overview

This episode of *Tomorrow Today* features Conrad, an entrepreneur and researcher who lost his biological voice after treatment for laryngeal cancer. Drawing on his experience in speech technology, he developed a non-invasive electronic system that amplifies and reshapes electrolarynx speech, helping users communicate more clearly and expressively.

The conversation moves beyond speech intelligibility to a harder problem: preserving the timing, pitch, emotion, laughter, and other vocal signals that make communication feel human. Conrad argues that effective voice augmentation requires an appropriate division of labor—the user controls intention and emotion, while AI helps preserve a recognizable personal voice.

The discussion also examines technical and commercial challenges, including real-time latency, on-device inference, pricing, reimbursement, voice-data ownership, and authentication. It closes with a broader vision in which assistive voice devices become mainstream wearables, reducing the stigma experienced by people who depend on them.

## Podcast Commentary/Summary

The episode’s strongest contribution is its treatment of voice as more than intelligible words. By focusing on laughter, timing, pitch, affection, and conversational spontaneity, it shows why restoring communication is a deeper engineering challenge than producing understandable synthetic speech.

Conrad’s dual perspective as founder and product user gives the discussion unusual practical depth. The conversation connects personal experience with concrete questions about latency, hardware constraints, reimbursement, privacy, consent, and inclusive product design.
