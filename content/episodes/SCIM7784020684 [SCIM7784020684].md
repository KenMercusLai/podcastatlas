# Cold and Heat Exposure: Brown Fat, Metabolism, and Practical Protocols

## Overview

Andrew Huberman speaks with Dr. Susanna Søberg about how deliberate cold exposure affects the nervous system, brown fat, thermogenesis, glucose regulation, and metabolic health. They explain that an effective cold stimulus should feel uncomfortably cold and trigger a noticeable physiological response, but it does not need to involve extreme temperatures or prolonged immersion.

The discussion traces the body’s response from skin cold receptors to the hypothalamus, catecholamine release, brown-fat activation, blood-vessel constriction, and shivering. Repeated exposure can produce adaptation, including a reduced cold-shock response, more efficient heat generation, and improved tolerance of changing temperatures.

Søberg also describes research comparing experienced winter swimmers with controls. The winter swimmers produced less insulin and cleared glucose more rapidly. Their routine averaged about 11 minutes of cold-water exposure and 57 minutes of sauna per week, distributed across multiple sessions.

The central practical message is to use manageable doses and temperature transitions rather than pursuing extremes. Ending on cold forces the body to rewarm itself, while alternating between warm and cold may train cells and thermoregulatory systems to adapt more efficiently.

## 分段落总结

[00:37] **Defining an Effective Cold Stimulus**

[事实] Deliberate cold exposure should feel uncomfortably cold and produce a “cold shock” rather than feel easy or pleasant.

[事实] Cold shock activates the sympathetic nervous system and catecholamines and may initially cause rapid breathing, hyperventilation, or a gasping reflex.

[事实] With repeated exposure, the breathing response and perceived shock tend to diminish as the body adapts.

[推测] Perceived discomfort and breathing disruption may be more practical guides than prescribing one universal water temperature, because cold tolerance varies between individuals.

[02:14] **Cold Showers, Immersion, and Cold Air**

[事实] Cold water stimulates more skin cold receptors than cold air because it contacts a larger portion of the body more directly.

[事实] Full-body immersion therefore produces a faster and more potent autonomic response than walking outside in cold air while lightly dressed.

[事实] Cold showers, cold air, and immersion can all activate the sympathetic nervous system and brown fat, although their potency differs.

[事实] Brown fat generates heat, while skeletal muscles provide a second thermogenic mechanism through shivering.

[04:29] **How Cold Activates Brown Fat**

[事实] Skin cold receptors signal the hypothalamus, which participates in regulating body temperature.

[事实] Cold exposure rapidly increases noradrenaline, adrenaline, and, to a lesser extent, cortisol; noradrenaline then helps activate brown fat.

[事实] The discussion describes additional pathways linking cold receptors and shivering muscles to brown-fat activation.

[事实] Brown fat and skeletal muscles work together to preserve core temperature and protect against hypothermia.

[07:39] **Afterdrop, Shivering, and Hormetic Stress**

[事实] After leaving cold water, constricted blood vessels reopen, allowing blood to travel through still-cold peripheral tissues and return to the core at a lower temperature.

[事实] This “afterdrop” can reduce core temperature after exposure and trigger shivering.

[事实] Shivering activates muscles and brown fat, increasing thermogenesis; Søberg advises not fearing moderate shivering as long as exposure has not progressed toward excessive hypothermia.

[事实] Cold, heat, and exercise are presented as forms of hormetic stress that can strengthen cellular stress responses and improve resilience when appropriately dosed.

[推测] The comparison with exercise suggests that discomfort can be useful, but only within a recoverable range rather than as a reason to ignore signs of dangerous cooling.

[11:02] **Long-Term Adaptation and Metabolic Effects**

[事实] Repeated cold exposure can increase brown-fat activation and mitochondrial capacity, improve peripheral blood-vessel constriction, and reduce the magnitude of the stress response.

[事实] Søberg cites a winter-swimming study in which participants showed lower blood pressure, lower heart rate, and better insulin sensitivity after a season.

[事实] Her own study found better glucose balance among adapted winter swimmers.

[事实] Cold is described as the most potent environmental activator of brown fat, but even exposing a hand to cold water or changing skin temperature can stimulate it.

[推测] Some proposed links between brown fat, reduced inflammation, and long-term disease prevention remain mechanistic interpretations rather than direct proof that cold exposure prevents disease.

[14:48] **Milder Ways to Stimulate Brown Fat**

[事实] Brown fat can be stimulated through cold wind, cooling garments, cold showers, or sleeping in a cool room; a cold plunge is not the only option.

[事实] A cited study found that sleeping in a 19°C room for one month increased brown-fat activity and insulin sensitivity compared with warmer conditions.

[事实] Participants slept under duvets and wore clothing, suggesting that cooling exposed areas such as the face may have been sufficient to produce a response.

[事实] Brown fat consumes glucose and fatty acids to generate heat and may thereby help clear these fuels from the bloodstream.

[事实] In Søberg’s experiment, placing a hand in 4°C water for four minutes produced measurable brown-fat activation within minutes.

[20:49] **The Winter-Swimming and Sauna Study**

[事实] Søberg recruited experienced winter swimmers who had practiced for two to three seasons and typically remained in the water for one to two minutes per immersion.

[事实] Participants practiced two or three days per week, completing three cold immersions and two sauna sessions per visit.

[事实] They started and ended with cold water; sauna sessions lasted 10–15 minutes at approximately 80°C.

[事实] Outdoor water temperatures varied from roughly 15°C downward and reached approximately 2–4°C at the coldest point.

[事实] Cold-water immersion activates both the sympathetic response and a parasympathetic diving response that reduces oxygen consumption.

[24:45] **Insulin Sensitivity and Glucose Clearance**

[事实] Compared with matched controls, winter swimmers produced less insulin across the experimental days.

[事实] After consuming a glucose drink, winter swimmers cleared glucose from the bloodstream more rapidly and had lower glucose levels after two hours.

[事实] They therefore achieved better glucose clearance despite releasing less insulin, indicating greater insulin sensitivity.

[推测] The findings support a metabolic association with regular cold-and-heat practice, but the observational comparison cannot by itself establish that temperature exposure caused every difference between the groups.

[26:47] **Practical Weekly Doses and the Risk of Excess**

[事实] The winter swimmers accumulated an average of approximately 11 minutes of cold-water exposure per week across two or three visits.

[事实] Individual immersions generally lasted one to two minutes rather than being completed as one prolonged weekly session.

[事实] Their sauna exposure averaged 57 minutes per week, divided into 10–15-minute sessions.

[事实] Søberg emphasizes that people do not need extreme or prolonged exposure to obtain the observed associations.

[事实] She cautions that excessive stress can exhaust cells and may increase cardiovascular risk.

[29:47] **Inflammation and Modern Thermal Comfort**

[事实] Søberg’s study measured inflammatory markers including IL-6 and IL-10, as well as blood pressure.

[事实] She argues that appropriate cold and heat exposure may help lower chronic inflammation through adaptive stress responses.

[事实] The discussion links inflammation with type 2 diabetes and with neurological or mental-health conditions including depression, anxiety, and Alzheimer’s disease.

[事实] Søberg contrasts temperature exposure with modern indoor lifestyles that minimize thermal variation and physical movement.

[推测] The proposed connection between increased thermal comfort and rising obesity is a broad hypothesis presented in the conversation, not evidence that indoor temperature control is a primary cause of obesity.

[33:03] **The Søberg Principle: End on Cold**

[事实] Ending with cold requires the body to rewarm itself through continued activation of brown fat and skeletal muscles.

[事实] Søberg says this thermogenic activity can continue for hours after the exposure and can also sustain neurotransmitter activity associated with the cold response.

[事实] Repeated practice may increase mitochondrial efficiency and make it easier for a person to generate and retain warmth.

[事实] In her study, winter swimmers had warmer skin at rest and greater brown-fat activation than controls.

[事实] Their more vascular skin also released heat more readily, which may keep brown fat and muscles slightly active to maintain body temperature.

[35:23] **Temperature Change Matters More Than Extremes**

[事实] For someone with only two available days per week, Søberg supports using several brief cold exposures separated by periods out of the water.

[事实] She identifies repeated changes between temperatures as the stimulus that challenges cells to adapt.

[事实] The conversation emphasizes transitions from warm to cold and cold to warm rather than rigidly prescribing one exact temperature and duration.

[事实] Søberg states that water around 12°C can activate brown fat and that even air around 19°C has produced activation in studies.

[事实] She concludes that ice is not necessary for every session and recommends varying exposure rather than continually pursuing the coldest possible temperature.

[推测] A flexible, discomfort-based protocol may be easier to sustain than an extreme regimen while still providing a meaningful thermoregulatory stimulus.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main strength is its clear explanation of the physiology behind cold exposure. The discussion connects the immediate cold-shock response with vasoconstriction, afterdrop, shivering, brown-fat thermogenesis, and longer-term adaptation without reducing the subject to a single rule.

Its most useful practical conclusion is that consistency and temperature change matter more than maximal cold. Brief sessions distributed through the week, an optional contrast with sauna, and ending on cold form a comparatively accessible framework. The stated weekly figures—11 minutes of cold and 57 minutes of sauna—describe the studied participants’ average practice rather than a universally proven prescription.

[推测] The episode is particularly valuable for listeners interested in metabolism, winter swimming, sauna, or the mechanisms of thermal adaptation. It is less definitive as guidance for disease prevention because several broader health claims are mechanistic interpretations or associations rather than direct clinical outcomes.

[推测] The discussion would have benefited from more explicit coverage of contraindications, supervision, cardiovascular screening, and water safety. Listeners should therefore treat the protocol as a research-informed framework, not as individualized medical clearance for extreme cold or heat exposure.
