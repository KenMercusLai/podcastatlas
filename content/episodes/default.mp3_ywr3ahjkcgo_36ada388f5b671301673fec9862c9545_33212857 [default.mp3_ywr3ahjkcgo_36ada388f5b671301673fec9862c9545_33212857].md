# Can Economists Rebuild Trust?

## 概览

This episode examines why economists, once highly influential in policymaking, are facing sharper distrust from politicians, voters, executives, and the public.

The discussion moves from broad criticism of the economics profession to concrete cases: missed forecasts around the financial crisis, attacks on Bureau of Labor Statistics data, skepticism toward work-from-home research, and the mismatch between aggregate economic indicators and lived experience.

The episode's core conclusion is that economists cannot rebuild trust by simply insisting the numbers are right. They need better transparency, more humility about past mistakes, clearer communication, and credible commitments to improve.

[事实] The episode opens by saying it was a major week for economic data, including GDP, consumer spending, and jobs numbers.
[事实] The transcript states that President Trump fired the economist in charge of publishing the jobs report and accused her, without evidence, of faking the numbers.

[事实] The host and New York Times economics reporter Ben Castleman discuss an American Economic Association session about economics and politics.
[事实] The panel included Jason Furman, several economists with government experience, and Oren Cass, who runs a conservative think tank influential in Trump's world.
[事实] Cass criticized economists for confidence around free trade with China, missing the 2008 financial crisis, missing inflation, and failing to convince many Americans that their recommendations work.
[推测] The panel functions in the episode as a symbol of the profession being forced to defend its authority.

[04:09] **Economists Lose Policy Influence**

[事实] The episode says economics rose sharply in influence starting around the 1960s and became important in policymaking.
[事实] Ben Castleman says economists used to help shape health care, climate, and tax policy, but are now being pushed aside in some policy discussions.
[事实] The episode says distrust is limiting economists' influence in a real way, including under both Trump and Biden administrations.

[06:44] **Economists Rank Low in Public Trust**

[事实] A 2017 YouGov survey in Britain found that 84 percent of respondents trusted nurses, while only 25 percent trusted economists.
[事实] The episode says trust in weather forecasters was twice as high as trust in economists.
[事实] When economists repeated the survey in the United States, they got similar results.
[事实] The episode links part of the distrust to a long free-market streak in economics emphasizing free trade, deregulation, and privatization.

[08:16] **Diane on Forecasting Limits and the Financial Crisis**

[事实] Diane, KPMG's chief economist, describes her job as trying to see the future with a "crystal ball."
[事实] She says economists can address some long-term questions but often get asked short-term questions, such as what the stock market will do tomorrow.
[事实] Diane says she missed the housing bubble burst and the global financial crisis, and that missing such large events is painful.
[事实] She says trust in economists eroded after the crisis because people asked not only why economists failed to predict it, but why they failed to stop it.

[10:24] **BLS Data Becomes a Trust Flashpoint**

[事实] Diane recalls that after the financial crisis, Jack Welch questioned whether Bureau of Labor Statistics jobs data had been inflated for political reasons.
[事实] Diane rejects the idea of a conspiracy and describes BLS statisticians as focused on technical statistical issues rather than political spin.
[事实] Aaron Sojourner, a labor economist, says BLS statisticians are widely known for political neutrality and do not provide spin.
[事实] Aaron says claims that BLS data can be quietly fudged ignore the role of civil servants dedicated to reliability and accuracy.

[14:01] **The BLS Firing Raises the Stakes**

[事实] Aaron says President Trump has routinely called BLS data inaccurate or fake.
[事实] The episode says Trump called a large jobs-number revision "rigged," while Aaron says revisions are normal.
[事实] Aaron calls the firing of the agency head a huge escalation and says U.S. official statistics are a global gold standard.
[事实] Aaron says the event softens his faith in the numbers and could hurt people's ability to make informed decisions about their lives and pocketbooks.

[15:28] **Nick Bloom and Work-From-Home Evidence**

[事实] Nick Bloom, a Stanford economist, studies the advantages and disadvantages of working from home.
[事实] He says the evidence depends on the job: about half of jobs are better in person, around a third are typically best hybrid, and some, such as call centers, can be better remote.
[事实] Nick says some executives discount the evidence because their own careers succeeded in offices.
[事实] Nick suspects return-to-office mandates can sometimes reduce headcount without severance, while the episode notes that Amazon denied this was its motivation.

[18:27] **More Data, Listening, and Less Combat**

[事实] Nick summarizes his response to distrust as bringing more data, more data, and more data.
[事实] He cites a U.S. Census survey of 150,000 American businesses as an example of evidence he uses to shift debate.
[事实] Nick says strongly worded statements and calling people idiots do not persuade the other side.
[事实] He says listening, taking concerns seriously, responding to them, and then presenting evidence is more effective.

[19:40] **Aggregate Numbers Miss Lived Experience**

[事实] Diane says distrust changed how she works and made her more careful with aggregate data such as GDP and unemployment.
[事实] She says economists missed how much price levels mattered after the pandemic, even when inflation rates were slowing.
[事实] She says the monthly expense to buy a home was 87 percent higher than in 2019, while incomes were not.
[事实] Diane now looks more at sector-level job growth, care disruptions, internships, entry-level jobs, consumer sentiment by income, and conversations with people.

[23:19] **Ben Ho on Costly Signals and Apology**

[事实] Ben Ho is introduced as an economics professor and author of a book on trust.
[事实] He studies techniques from behavioral economics and game theory for effective apologies after mistakes.
[事实] Ben says the first step is acknowledging that people have lost trust in economists instead of becoming defensive.
[事实] He says rebuilding trust requires costly signals: actions that take time, money, or risk and are hard to fake.

[27:00] **Past, Present, and Future Trust Repair**

[事实] Ben Ho says economists should address the past by explaining mistakes, taking responsibility, and acknowledging oversimplified models or insufficient data.
[事实] The episode connects this to the replication crisis, where researchers check whether older results can be reproduced.
[事实] For the present, Ben says economists should be more transparent and explain today's data-driven methods more clearly.
[事实] For the future, he says empty apologies do not work, but promises to do better can rebuild trust if people can hold the speaker accountable.

[30:15] **Accountability Without Surrendering the Field**

[事实] Ben Castleman says economists gain credibility when they personally acknowledge what they got wrong and how their views changed.
[事实] He says economics is more credible than ever in the sense that it is more data-driven, nuanced, and attentive to complexity.
[事实] He also says economics and data are under attack and that sometimes there is nothing to apologize for.
[事实] The episode concludes that rebuilding economists' credibility with the public and policymakers will take time.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode's value lies in treating distrust not as a simple communication failure, but as a professional crisis shaped by forecasting errors, political attacks, and the gap between statistics and lived experience.

[推测] Its strongest moments come from economists admitting limits: Diane owning her miss on the financial crisis, Aaron becoming less certain after the BLS firing, and Ben Ho arguing that trust requires costly, accountable signals.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode is mostly told through economists and journalists; it includes public distrust as a theme, but gives fewer direct voices from ordinary voters or workers who no longer trust the field.

[推测] This episode is especially useful for listeners interested in economic policy, public trust in institutions, government statistics, and how experts should communicate uncertainty without abandoning evidence.
