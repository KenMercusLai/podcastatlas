# Planet Money Fixes the NBA's Tanking Problem

## 概览

This episode examines why NBA teams intentionally lose games late in the season and frames tanking as an incentives problem created by the draft system. The hosts argue that if league rules reward losing with better draft odds, rational teams will keep finding ways to lose strategically.

The discussion traces the NBA's decades-long attempts to reduce tanking, from the reverse-order draft to the lottery and later odds tweaks. Each reform reduces one problem while creating another: weaker tanking incentives can also reduce the ability of genuinely bad teams to improve.

The episode then evaluates three larger reforms: the draft wheel, the Gold Plan, and abolishing the draft altogether. The central conclusion is not that one fix is obviously perfect, but that every draft design involves trade-offs among competitive balance, fan experience, team incentives, player autonomy, and market power.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Tanking Season In The NBA**

[事实] The episode opens by describing a recurring NBA pattern in which some die-hard fans begin rooting against their favorite teams as winter turns to spring.

[事实] The hosts say NBA teams with the worst records get the best chance at top draft picks, so losing can become rational.

[事实] Fan clips describe frustration with teams benching major players or appearing not to try.

[推测] The episode uses fan anger to show that tanking is not just a technical league-design issue, but also a product-quality problem for spectators.

[02:25] **Adam Silver Calls It An Incentives Problem**

[事实] NBA Commissioner Adam Silver is quoted saying behavior this year is worse than in recent memory.

[事实] Silver says the league is considering possible remedies and looking for a better system to align incentives.

[事实] The hosts connect this directly to Planet Money's economic lens: rules create incentives, and changing behavior requires changing the rules.

[推测] The framing suggests the episode will treat tanking less as bad morals and more as predictable behavior under a flawed system.

[05:45] **Zach Lowe Explains The Draft Incentive**

[事实] Zach Lowe, host of The Zach Lowe Show and longtime NBA reporter, joins the episode as the main NBA expert.

[事实] Lowe says tanking basically comes down to incentives: teams try to lose to get a higher draft slot.

[事实] The hosts begin with the history of incentives the NBA has offered through the draft.

[06:19] **Reverse-Order Draft And The 1984 Rockets**

[事实] In the 1960s and 1970s, the NBA draft broadly used reverse order, with worse teams picking higher and better teams picking lower.

[事实] The worst team in each conference used to flip a coin for the number one pick.

[事实] In 1984, with Hakeem Olajuwon and Michael Jordan entering the draft, the Houston Rockets sat top players, lost 14 of their final 17 games, and ended up with the first overall pick.

[事实] The Rockets selected Olajuwon and later won two NBA championships with him.

[推测] The Rockets example shows why tanking can be ugly for the league but still highly rewarding for an individual franchise.

[08:39] **The Lottery Era And Its Trade-Offs**

[事实] After the Rockets episode, the NBA introduced a draft lottery for teams that missed the playoffs.

[事实] The lottery added randomness so that finishing dead last no longer guaranteed the best position.

[事实] Lowe says the randomization was meant to discourage obvious, deliberate bottoming out.

[事实] The hosts say the change made tanking less attractive but also made it harder for some genuinely bad teams to get better.

[10:12] **Decades Of Draft Tweaks**

[事实] In 1987, the NBA changed the system so only the first three picks were determined by lottery.

[事实] In 1990, the league weighted the lottery so worse teams had better odds of top picks.

[事实] In 1994, the league increased the odds for the worst teams, then in 1996 decreased them slightly for some of those teams.

[事实] In 2019, the NBA increased lottery picks from three to four and flattened the odds, reducing the worst team's chance at the number one pick from 25% to 14%.

[11:21] **Why Tanking Still Persists**

[事实] The hosts say several strong incoming college players made tanking worthwhile for many teams even under lower lottery odds.

[事实] Lowe says NBA teams may be more motivated to tank than teams in other sports because one great basketball player can be uniquely valuable.

[事实] The episode notes that basketball has only five players on the court for a team and the best players can control many possessions.

[12:41] **Proposal One: The Draft Wheel**

[事实] Lowe describes learning about the draft wheel from Boston Celtics executive Mike Zarin during NBA Summer League in Las Vegas.

[事实] The draft wheel has 30 spokes, one for each first-round pick, and teams move through all 30 positions in a predetermined order over 30 years.

[事实] Under the wheel, a team's record in a given season would not determine where it picks.

[事实] The design guarantees every team one top-six pick and one pick from seven through 12 every five years.

[推测] The wheel is the cleanest anti-tanking proposal because it removes the direct reward for losing.

[14:59] **Why Teams Resisted The Draft Wheel**

[事实] Lowe says teams resisted the wheel because bad teams could remain bad while waiting years for a better pick.

[事实] Under the current system, bad teams get draft help quickly; under the wheel, they receive whatever pick the schedule gives them.

[事实] Small-market teams worried that without bad-record lottery help, they would lose more ground to markets like Los Angeles, New York, or Miami in free agency.

[事实] The hosts say major changes would require a vote by all 30 teams and approval from three-fourths of them.

[推测] The wheel solves tanking at the cost of weakening the league's traditional promise of hope for the weakest teams.

[16:50] **Proposal Two: The Gold Plan**

[事实] The episode turns to the Professional Women's Hockey League and interviews Jaina Hefford, a Hockey Hall of Famer with five Olympic medals.

[事实] Hefford says the PWHL was built with a mindset of creativity and willingness to do things differently.

[事实] The PWHL adopted the Gold Plan to limit tanking while still giving weaker teams a route to better draft position.

[18:10] **How The Gold Plan Works**

[事实] Hefford says the Gold Plan was developed by Adam Gold.

[事实] Under the plan, once a team is eliminated from playoff contention, wins begin helping its draft position rather than hurting it.

[事实] The team that earns the most points after elimination gets the highest draft pick.

[事实] The hosts use Seattle Torrent and Vancouver GoldenEyes as an example of eliminated teams competing for draft position by winning.

[推测] The Gold Plan tries to make late-season games meaningful for fans even when their teams are out of playoff contention.

[20:32] **Gold Plan Trade-Offs For The NBA**

[事实] Hefford acknowledges that a very bad team could be eliminated early and still fail to earn the top pick, making improvement harder.

[事实] The hosts say it is still early for the PWHL and this is the first year the plan might dramatically affect draft order.

[事实] The hosts say the NBA has much more money at stake, and drafting the next LeBron James could be worth as much as a billion dollars to a team.

[事实] They note NBA teams could still game the Gold Plan by losing early in the season and trying harder only after elimination.

[推测] The Gold Plan may reduce obvious late-season tanking, but it does not fully eliminate strategic losing.

[24:11] **Proposal Three: Abolish The Draft**

[事实] The episode's third proposal is described as a more radical approach already being used in another U.S. sports league.

[事实] The hosts interview World Cup champion and women's soccer commentator Sam Uys.

[事实] The NWSL eliminated its draft in 2024.

[事实] Players entering the NWSL can negotiate directly with teams that are interested in them.

[25:31] **Open Competition For Young Players**

[事实] Without a draft, teams compete for young players through salary, development environment, facilities, coaching, culture, and fans.

[事实] The NWSL still has a salary cap, so the system is not full free-market anarchy.

[事实] Teams must decide how much a young player is worth to them and then pay accordingly.

[推测] This approach shifts competition away from losing games and toward making a club more attractive.

[26:23] **Why The NWSL Removed Its Draft**

[事实] Sam says the main reason for eliminating the draft was not fighting tanking.

[事实] One reason was competition from the UK's Women's Super League, where top teams could pay elite players.

[事实] Another reason was that the NWSL did not want very young players, including 16-year-olds, forced to move across the country because of a draft assignment.

[事实] Players under 18 had been exempt from the draft, which meant some of the best youngest talent was already skipping the draft process.

[27:42] **Early Results And Market Concerns**

[事实] Sam says one year is not much data but she does not think the NWSL's no-draft experiment has been a problem.

[事实] She says rookies did well anecdotally.

[事实] She argues smaller-market teams can still succeed by creating appealing environments even if they do not pay top dollar.

[事实] She also says wealthier and more committed owners may keep building stronger teams year after year.

[推测] Abolishing the draft may end tanking incentives while increasing concern about long-term inequality between richer and poorer clubs.

[28:55] **Player Autonomy Enters The Debate**

[事实] Sam says draft systems should also be considered from the players' perspective, not only the league, fans, and teams.

[事实] She points to the NWSL's history of abuse and differences among states in women's rights and LGBTQ+ friendliness.

[事实] She argues that player autonomy and treating players as people with a say in where they go can lead to better performance, fan engagement, and club relationships.

[推测] This reframes the draft not only as a competitive-balance tool but also as a labor and autonomy issue.

[29:51] **No Perfect Balance**

[事实] The hosts say designing a system means balancing the needs of the league, teams, players, and fans.

[事实] The NBA declined a recorded interview but told the show that fixing tanking incentives is on the league's radar.

[事实] The NBA Board of Governors was set to discuss multiple possible solutions later that month.

[事实] Erika says she would get rid of the draft, while Keith says he favors the Gold Plan.

[31:14] **Change Is Coming**

[事实] Zach Lowe says he is confident change is coming, though he does not expect the draft to be abolished or a 30-team lottery to arrive soon.

[事实] Lowe says he has long opposed eliminating the reverse-order draft because he wants bad teams to have hope.

[事实] After this year's tanking, he says he is more open to thinking about systems where everyone has to try every year.

[推测] The episode ends with the modest but pointed demand that teams should simply play to win.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode's strongest value is its clear use of economics to explain a familiar sports problem. Instead of treating tanking as a scandal caused by a few bad actors, it shows how predictable behavior emerges from rule design.

[推测] Its best sections are the comparisons across leagues: the NBA lottery, the PWHL's Gold Plan, and the NWSL's no-draft model each reveal a different way to balance incentives, fairness, and entertainment.

[推测] The main limitation is that none of the proposals can be fully evaluated from the transcript alone. The Gold Plan is still young in the PWHL, the NWSL no-draft experiment has only limited early evidence, and the NBA's actual next move remains unclear.

[推测] This episode is especially useful for listeners interested in sports economics, mechanism design, labor markets, and the hidden trade-offs behind rules that seem simple on the surface.
