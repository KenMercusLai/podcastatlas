# May 1968 in Paris: From Student Discontent to National Crisis

## 概览

This episode explains the first phase of May 1968 in Paris: how a seemingly limited student dispute in Nanterre escalated into barricades, police violence, the occupation of the Sorbonne, and then a nationwide strike wave.

The hosts argue that the uprising was not mainly driven by a clear philosophical program. Instead, they emphasize overcrowded universities, authoritarian institutions, police overreaction, generational resentment, and the theatrical pull of France’s revolutionary memory.

The discussion ends before the political climax, setting up the next episode on de Gaulle’s response, his temporary disappearance from Paris, and the longer legacy of 1968.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **A Paris Episode On May 1968**

[事实] The hosts record in Paris, near the Sorbonne, and introduce the episode as a discussion of the events of May 1968.

[事实] They describe the familiar imagery of students throwing cobblestones, barricades, tear gas, and revolutionary atmosphere in the Latin Quarter.

[推测] The opening frames May 1968 as both a dramatic historical crisis and a scene shaped by later mythmaking.

[02:08] **France Before The Explosion**

[事实] The hosts say France did not have a strong recent reputation for youth protest before 1968, despite older revolutionary traditions.

[事实] They mention the March 1968 Le Monde headline that described French public life as defined by boredom.

[事实] Charles de Gaulle is presented as the aging embodiment of France, confident that French youth were more disciplined than their counterparts abroad.

[04:12] **Prosperity, Decolonization, And Hidden Strains**

[事实] Postwar France had gone through traumatic decolonization, including Vietnam and Algeria, while also enjoying strong economic growth during the “Thirty Glorious Years.”

[事实] The hosts describe full employment, annual growth of about five percent, and confidence that younger people would be better off than their parents.

[事实] They also stress that many rural migrants and workers lived in bleak suburban developments, while central Paris remained beautiful partly because problems had been pushed outward.

[05:56] **The University System Breaks Under Expansion**

[事实] The French student population had doubled since 1960 and tripled since 1950.

[事实] The state responded with annexes, overspill campuses, temporary buildings, and rapidly recruited lecturers.

[事实] The hosts say many students experienced overcrowded lecture halls, poor teaching, little contact with professors, and high dropout rates.

[07:54] **Radical Influences And Anti-Authority Mood**

[事实] Vietnam, anti-Americanism, and the wider protest culture of the 1960s formed part of the background.

[事实] The hosts describe France as hierarchical, paternalistic, still strongly marked by Catholic culture, and deferential toward authority.

[推测] These conditions made rebellion especially attractive once a visible trigger appeared.

[10:51] **Philosophy Was Not The Main Driver**

[事实] The hosts discuss situationism, absurdist theater, existentialism, and Michel Foucault.

[事实] They argue that Foucault and related intellectual currents became more important to the memory of 1968 than to the actual outbreak of the events.

[事实] Foucault was in Tunis during the events and was reportedly asking friends what was happening.

[15:20] **Nanterre As The Trigger Point**

[事实] Nanterre, a new Sorbonne overspill campus northwest of Paris, is described as unfinished, isolated, poorly served by transport, and lacking promised facilities.

[事实] Students were also subject to strict rules separating men’s and women’s residences at night.

[事实] The hosts introduce Daniel Cohn-Bendit, “Danny the Red,” as a prominent Nanterre student activist from a German Jewish refugee family.

[17:02] **Police Memory And Cohn-Bendit’s Role**

[事实] The hosts connect student radicalism to memories of French police brutality, including killings of Algerian protesters and trade unionists in the early 1960s.

[事实] Maurice Papon is mentioned as a senior Paris police figure with a Vichy-era record.

[事实] Cohn-Bendit is described as more libertarian than communist, despite later being widely associated with the radical left.

[19:05] **From Sexual Rules To Student Occupation**

[事实] In January 1968, Cohn-Bendit challenged the youth and sports minister over sexual issues in youth policy.

[事实] On March 22, students including far-left groups, poets, musicians, and theater students occupied an administration building at Nanterre.

[事实] Their meeting attacked university bureaucracy and class discrimination and also raised the issue of access to opposite-sex residences.

[推测] The original grievances were concrete and campus-based, but they quickly became absorbed into a broader revolutionary language.

[21:56] **Administrative Overreaction Escalates The Crisis**

[事实] University authorities responded with disciplinary hearings and suspensions after the Nanterre occupation.

[事实] Protests grew through April, and on May 2 the rector shut down the Nanterre campus.

[事实] This moved the protest into central Paris, where Sorbonne students occupied a lecture theater and police arrested and beat hundreds of people.

[事实] The Sorbonne was then closed for the first time in roughly 700 years.

[24:18] **Barricades And The First Nights Of Violence**

[事实] By May 6, student and lecturer unions called major marches, and the authorities shut down large parts of central Paris.

[事实] The hosts say de Gaulle and his education minister initially treated the unrest partly as students avoiding exams.

[事实] Students began building barricades, tearing up paving stones, and throwing them at police.

[推测] The hosts interpret the barricades as a form of revolutionary role-playing that then helped make the crisis more real.

[27:37] **The Paris Commune As Historical Script**

[事实] The hosts compare the imagery of 1968 to the Paris Commune of 1871.

[事实] Protesters chanted in support of the Commune, and British reporters described the Latin Quarter as a scene of destruction and tear gas.

[事实] The hosts highlight a piece of graffiti saying the speaker had something to say but was not sure what.

[推测] The episode presents the protest movement as powerful but ideologically diffuse.

[30:08] **Against Authority, Old Men, And Postwar Myths**

[事实] The hosts say the protests became less about specific campus rules and more about authority, old men, de Gaulle, and inherited postwar hierarchies.

[事实] They connect chants such as “CRS, SS” to resentment of police violence and suspicions about collaborationist continuities.

[事实] De Gaulle is described as oscillating between conciliation and repression while the crisis widened.

[31:47] **May 10 And The Media Effect**

[事实] May 10 is identified as the major “night of the barricades,” with hundreds of arrests and smashed shop windows.

[事实] The hosts stress that cheap transistor radios let students follow events in real time.

[事实] Television spread images of the violence across France, shocking some viewers and inspiring others.

[事实] The unrest began to spread beyond Paris through occupations elsewhere in the country.

[34:06] **The Sorbonne Occupation And Worker Strikes**

[事实] De Gaulle and Prime Minister Georges Pompidou agreed to reopen the Sorbonne, but students occupied it.

[事实] The occupied Sorbonne became a site of happenings, committees, teach-ins, and revolutionary discussion.

[事实] Workers then entered the story, beginning with Sud Aviation and spreading to Renault plants and other workplaces.

[事实] By May 18, two million workers were on strike; by May 23, the hosts say ten million workers, about two-thirds of the French workforce, were striking.

[38:02] **Students And Workers Wanted Different Things**

[事实] Cohn-Bendit later said students and workers were never truly a united force.

[事实] The hosts distinguish student demands for radical life change from worker demands around wages, hours, conditions, and recognition.

[事实] The Communist Party initially distrusted the student revolt as bourgeois disorder.

[事实] The government later offered major concessions, including a 35 percent increase in the minimum wage, but workers at the Renault plant at Billancourt rejected the deal as insufficient.

[43:37] **Slogans, Theater, And The Unfinished Story**

[事实] The hosts discuss famous slogans including “It is forbidden to forbid,” “Under the paving stones, the beach,” and “Be realistic, demand the impossible.”

[事实] They present these slogans as central to the cultural memory of May 1968.

[事实] The episode closes by previewing a second part on de Gaulle’s dramatic response and the legacy of 1968.

[推测] The ending suggests that the political significance of the crisis cannot be understood from the student revolt alone.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its clear explanation of escalation: it shows how poor university conditions, rigid authority, police violence, media coverage, and worker unrest combined into a national crisis.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the contrast between myth and mechanism. The hosts acknowledge the romantic imagery of Parisian revolution while repeatedly asking what people actually wanted and how events moved from one stage to the next.

[推测] The main limitation is that this installment stops before de Gaulle’s decisive response, so the political outcome and legacy are only introduced rather than resolved.

[推测] It is especially suitable for listeners interested in modern France, student protest, labor history, and the way symbolic revolutionary performance can interact with real institutional breakdown.
