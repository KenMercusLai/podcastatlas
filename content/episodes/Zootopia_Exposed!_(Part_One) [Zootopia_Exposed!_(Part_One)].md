# Zootopia Exposed! (Part One): The Gary Goldman Mystery

## 概览

Malcolm Gladwell investigates screenwriter Gary Goldman’s claim that Disney’s original *Zootopia* closely resembled a concept he submitted to the company years earlier. Goldman’s proposal used the same title, featured an animal society, and questioned the promise that anyone can become anything.

After a seven-year copyright battle ended in Disney’s favor, Goldman discovered that *Zootopia 2* featured “Gary the Snake,” whose family’s invention was stolen by powerful “fat cats.” The sequel’s plot appeared to echo Goldman’s dispute with Disney in unusually specific ways.

The episode does not establish why these parallels exist. Instead, it presents competing possibilities—including coincidence, mockery, an encoded apology, or an idea that bypassed corporate oversight—and reserves its own theory for Part Two.

## 分段落总结

[01:14] **A Family Trip Becomes an Investigation**

[事实] Malcolm Gladwell took his four-and-a-half-year-old daughter to see *Zootopia 2*, which he describes as the highest-grossing animated film of all time.

[事实] He introduces the episode as a strange Hollywood story requiring two installments to unravel.

[推测] The playful opening frames a children’s movie as evidence in a much larger mystery about authorship and corporate behavior.

[02:42] **Angus Fletcher Brings an Urgent Story**

[事实] Narrative scholar Angus Fletcher contacted Gladwell on behalf of his friend Gary Goldman, saying the story contained too many layers for a single episode.

[事实] Goldman is an experienced Hollywood screenwriter whose credits and collaborators include major films and directors.

[事实] Fletcher and Goldman had previously appeared on *Revisionist History* to discuss an unrealized science-fiction project.

[04:55] **Goldman Recognizes His Ideas in Zootopia**

[事实] The first *Zootopia*, released in 2016, follows rabbit Judy Hopps and fox Nick Wilde through a mystery in an animal-only society.

[事实] Goldman told Fletcher that a written pitch he gave Disney contained language closely resembling the film’s “anybody can be anything” message.

[事实] Goldman’s proposed animal world was also called “Zootopia.”

[推测] The shared title, premise, and thematic wording form the central basis for Goldman’s suspicion that Disney used his material.

[07:03] **The Original Zootopia Concept**

[事实] Goldman says he devised the name and concept around 2000 while looking for a creative project his young sons could enjoy.

[事实] In 2009, while working on another Disney project, he gave a Disney executive an eight-page outline for *Looney*, about an animator who creates an animal world called Zootopia.

[事实] The proposal included Roscoe, a cynical and obnoxious hyena, and Mimi, an optimistic squirrel.

[事实] Goldman designed the world to challenge the comforting but unrealistic advice that children can become anything they want.

[推测] Fletcher views the concept’s strength as its ability to support an entire mythic universe rather than merely a single plot.

[11:22] **Disney Passes, Then Releases Zootopia**

[事实] Goldman says the Disney executive offered to send his proposal to the animation division, after which his agent reported that Disney had passed.

[事实] Six years later, Goldman’s sons saw a billboard for Disney’s *Zootopia* and thought its characters resembled those from his proposal.

[事实] Goldman believed Roscoe had become the cynical fox Nick Wilde and Mimi had become the optimistic rabbit Judy Hopps.

[事实] The law firm Quinn Emanuel represented Goldman in a copyright-infringement lawsuit against Disney.

[13:12] **A Seven-Year Legal Defeat**

[事实] The litigation lasted seven years and included depositions of major Disney personnel.

[事实] Goldman lost the initial case, an appeal, and a subsequent effort in state court.

[事实] The courts found that the similarities did not constitute copyright infringement and dismissed the case before deciding whether Disney had used Goldman’s pitch.

[事实] Gladwell cites an analysis of more than 50 writer-versus-studio copyright cases in which every plaintiff lost.

[推测] The episode argues that copyright doctrine protects specific expression more readily than underlying ideas, making claims like Goldman’s exceptionally difficult to prove.

[16:53] **The Arrival of Gary the Snake**

[事实] Work on *Zootopia 2* began while Goldman’s lawsuit was still active.

[事实] A central character in the sequel is a snake named Gary.

[事实] Goldman initially interpreted the name and species as a personal attack, while the sequel’s screenwriter publicly said he chose “Gary” because he found it funny and often used it for nice characters.

[事实] Goldman’s family remained deeply affected by the litigation; he recounts that his younger son once punched and damaged a door after an adverse ruling.

[推测] Gladwell describes the family’s shared emotional response as “transactive anger,” with different relatives carrying parts of the grievance.

[19:17] **Does the Character Resemble Goldman?**

[事实] Goldman watched the sequel with his wife and two adult sons before its official opening.

[事实] His wife, an artist, said Gary the Snake looked like him.

[事实] Gladwell describes both Goldman and the character as having expressive eyes, a gentle manner, and an understated personality.

[事实] Goldman says other people independently contacted him after the film’s release to note the resemblance.

[事实] Much of the movie takes place in Marsh Market, a setting compared to New Orleans; Goldman is from New Orleans and is Jewish.

[推测] Gladwell and Goldman’s lawyer interpret the character’s appearance, behavior, name, and regional setting as a composite allusion to Goldman, but the episode provides no confirmation from Disney.

[24:50] **The Sequel’s Intellectual-Property Plot**

[事实] *Zootopia 2* reveals that Gary the Snake’s great-grandmother, Agnes, invented weather walls that allowed different animals to live together.

[事实] The Lynxley family stole her plans and patent, took credit for the invention, and expelled reptiles from Zootopia to conceal the theft.

[事实] Judy Hopps, Nick Wilde, and Gary attempt to recover the patent, restore the Snake family’s reputation, and bring reptiles back to the city.

[事实] The episode emphasizes that the villains are literally powerful “fat cats” who profited from stolen intellectual property.

[推测] These plot elements create a striking allegorical parallel with Goldman’s allegation that Disney took his Zootopia concept.

[27:01] **An Apparent Allegory of the Lawsuit**

[事实] Gladwell summarizes the parallel: both stories involve a Louisiana-associated figure named Gary who claims that powerful actors stole the invention underlying Zootopia.

[事实] Fletcher characterizes the sequel as a Disney-created allegory of Goldman’s dispute with Disney over the first film.

[事实] Goldman says Disney spent more than $300 million producing and marketing a movie that appears to be about him and his lawsuit.

[推测] Possible interpretations raised in the discussion include mockery, a corporate statement, an apology, or an unauthorized creative act within Disney.

[28:53] **How Did the Story Pass Corporate Review?**

[事实] Gladwell, Fletcher, and Goldman question whether Disney’s legal department and senior leadership understood the potential interpretation of the sequel.

[事实] Goldman notes that Disney had already won the lawsuit and questions why the company would release a story inviting renewed speculation.

[事实] Fletcher suggests that the positive portrayal of Gary the Snake somehow passed through Disney’s legal and corporate systems.

[推测] The hosts liken this possibility to a heist in which creators concealed the story’s meaning from internal reviewers.

[30:27] **Sealed Records and Corporate Silence**

[事实] Goldman’s lawyer Deborah Drews says Disney obtained a protective order requiring proprietary, confidential, or trade-secret material to be filed under seal.

[事实] Gladwell emailed Disney CEO Bob Iger to ask whether the sequel was an encoded apology to Goldman.

[事实] Iger replied politely but said he could not comment on Zootopia and Goldman.

[事实] Other Hollywood executives either declined to speak on the record or refused to discuss Disney.

[推测] Gladwell presents Iger as potentially either the author of a gracious but unacknowledged gesture or the victim of a creative maneuver he could not publicly address.

[34:05] **The Mystery Is Left Open**

[事实] Gladwell reports that powerful Hollywood figures offered no public explanation for the similarities.

[事实] The episode ends without deciding who conceived the relevant plot, what Disney knew, or whether the parallels were intentional.

[事实] Part Two promises a theory about what happened behind closed doors at Disney.

[推测] By withholding its explanation, Part One turns an unresolved authorship dispute into a corporate mystery built around circumstantial evidence.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest feature is its carefully escalating structure. It begins with similarities between two creative concepts, moves through the failed lawsuit, and then reveals that the sequel itself is about stolen intellectual property. Interviews with Goldman, Fletcher, and Goldman’s lawyer give the dispute both emotional and legal dimensions.

Its central argument is compelling as a pattern of coincidences, but it remains unproven. The transcript contains no confirmation that Disney copied Goldman’s proposal, modeled Gary the Snake on him, or deliberately turned the lawsuit into an allegory. Several conclusions are explicitly presented as theories.

[推测] The episode will appeal most to listeners interested in Hollywood screenwriting, copyright disputes, corporate decision-making, and narrative analysis. Its cliffhanger makes Part One effective as investigative entertainment, although listeners seeking a definitive factual resolution will need to reserve judgment pending the promised second installment.
