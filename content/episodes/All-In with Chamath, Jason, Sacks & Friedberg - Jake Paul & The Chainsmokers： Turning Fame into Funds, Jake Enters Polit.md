# From Fame to Funds: Jake Paul and The Chainsmokers on Attention, Boxing, Music, and Venture Capital

## 概览

This episode examines how celebrity, audience reach, and creative careers can be converted into durable businesses. Jake Paul describes moving from Vine and YouTube into professional boxing, sports promotion, venture investing, philanthropy, and a possible future in politics.

The Chainsmokers explain how they built their music career through early internet-native promotion, then used their relationships, distribution skills, and brand experience to establish the venture firm Mantis. They stress that fame may open doors, but investment performance still depends on sustained work, sound judgment, and realized returns.

Across both conversations, attention is treated as valuable economic capital—but also as a source of distortion. The guests and hosts discuss inauthentic content, celebrity-investor skepticism, investment bias, liquidity, follow-on investing, and the danger of valuations rising without matching business performance.

## 分段落总结

[01:16] **Building an Audience Through Authentic Creation**

[事实] Jake Paul says he began creating content before becoming an influencer was considered a desirable career and was motivated by a genuine interest in entertaining people.

[事实] He attributes his audience growth partly to daily messages encouraging viewers to work hard, smile, and pursue their goals.

[事实] He argues that creators are most likely to succeed when they are genuinely entertaining, have a distinct perspective, and fill a recognizable niche.

[推测] His account suggests that durable attention begins with creative conviction and audience value rather than with a calculated attempt to manufacture virality.

[03:20] **Vine’s Collapse and the Fragility of Platforms**

[事实] Paul says the top Vine creators asked the platform to pay each of them one million dollars per year because their content generated its views and audience.

[事实] After Vine rejected the proposal, the creators shifted their activity to Facebook, YouTube, and Snapchat; Paul says Vine died within months.

[推测] The story illustrates the risk creators face when their livelihood depends on a platform that does not adequately share value with its core suppliers.

[04:30] **The Social Costs of the Attention Economy**

[事实] Paul says he grew up with both technology and offline social interaction, while the long-term effects of the “iPad kid” generation remain unclear.

[事实] He describes a “YouTuber disease” in which people make absurd statements or use clickbait simply to attract views and publicity.

[事实] He argues that one platform acting alone cannot solve harmful-content incentives because users and attention can migrate to competing services.

[推测] Meaningful reform would therefore require coordinated standards across platforms rather than isolated moderation decisions.

[06:39] **From Creator to Entrepreneur and Boxer**

[事实] Paul says he became interested in startups after visiting Silicon Valley and later created Team 10, a social-media label that helped numerous creators gain millions of followers.

[事实] His boxing career began after a challenge from two British brothers; following three months of training, he won his first fight by knockout in Manchester.

[事实] He then relocated to Puerto Rico to train with fewer distractions and used his existing audience to build interest in every stage of his boxing career.

[事实] Paul says his company, MVP, represents roughly 400 boxing and MMA fighters and has merged with the PFL.

[推测] His boxing strategy replicated the Team 10 model: use an established audience to develop both his own career and a wider roster of talent.

[10:30] **Competing With the UFC Through Fighter Economics**

[事实] Paul claims the UFC pays fighters roughly 15% of total revenue, compared with approximately 50% in other professional sports leagues.

[事实] He says dissatisfied fighters want better pay, sponsorship freedom, and opportunities to participate in major fights.

[事实] Paul presents higher fighter compensation and more favorable commercial terms as the basis for competing with the UFC.

[推测] The proposed opportunity is a classic challenger strategy: attract talent by giving participants a larger share of the economic value they create.

[11:51] **Attention as Venture-Capital Leverage**

[事实] Paul says he began angel investing at age 18, made many mistakes, and later formalized his activity through a fund.

[事实] He describes capital as a commodity and argues that founders value investors who can bring attention, branding expertise, and distribution.

[事实] Paul says he advised OpenAI that Sora could become a social-media application, participated in its development process, and licensed his name, image, and likeness for generated videos.

[事实] He rejects the “celebrity investor” label and says Anti Fund should be compared with traditional firms through DPI and IRR.

[推测] His investment thesis treats media reach as a form of operational support rather than merely promotional endorsement.

[12:40] **The Limits and Risks of Attention**

[事实] Paul warns that companies may prioritize appearing important over building genuinely valuable products.

[事实] He identifies inauthenticity as a major problem in online content, journalism, and commentary, where extreme opinions can generate views and revenue.

[事实] Anti Fund uses a barbell approach, investing both at the earliest stage and in later-stage companies with established founders and records.

[推测] Attention can accelerate a strong company, but it can also conceal weak fundamentals; the differentiating factor is whether visibility is connected to substantive execution.

[16:06] **Jake Paul’s Business Flywheel and Political Ambitions**

[事实] Paul describes fighting, investing, content creation, and business promotion as a mutually reinforcing flywheel.

[事实] He says his foundation has opened 40 gyms where children can box for free and also sponsors young athletes and sends them to events and tournaments.

[事实] Paul says his work has increased opportunities and pay for women boxers, who had previously been underpaid and rarely featured on fight cards.

[事实] When asked where he sees himself at age 40, he answers “politics,” describing it as a way to help people and create change.

[推测] He expects future political candidates to benefit from being social-media natives with direct access to large built-in audiences.

[21:34] **The Chainsmokers’ Formation and Creative Evolution**

[事实] Drew Taggart and Alex Pall met through a mutual connection after Pall’s previous Chainsmokers partnership ended.

[事实] They decided to form a band despite not previously knowing each other and remained partners and close friends over the following 14 years.

[事实] They describe balancing the recognizable sound expected by existing listeners with the desire to explore new creative territory.

[事实] They also observe renewed audience interest in nostalgic music, including renewed attention to the music of 2016.

[推测] Their longevity appears to depend on preserving a recognizable identity while allowing enough experimentation to keep the work creatively meaningful.

[25:05] **Breaking Through in a Crowded Music Business**

[事实] The guests state that approximately 300,000 songs are uploaded to Spotify each day, making the current music market extremely competitive.

[事实] Early in their career, they created remixes and promoted them through Hype Machine, whose rankings reflected blog coverage and user engagement.

[事实] Pall identified bloggers covered by the platform and sent them personalized messages instead of generic promotional emails.

[事实] This strategy helped The Chainsmokers secure roughly 30 number-one placements on Hype Machine while they developed their production skills and artistic identity.

[推测] Their early success combined creative work with systematic distribution, personalized outreach, and an understanding of how an emerging platform ranked content.

[27:47] **Labels, Direct Distribution, and Live-Performance Economics**

[事实] The Chainsmokers say artists now have more ways to distribute music, build communities, and communicate directly with fans.

[事实] They describe a difficult choice for emerging artists between accepting label funding and retaining control of their future work.

[事实] They say labels still provide value, but AI, streaming, YouTube, and shrinking attention spans have made the industry’s future uncertain.

[事实] Live performances are a major source of their income, and DJ shows have substantially better economics than arena tours involving a full band and high overhead.

[推测] Direct-to-fan models may increase artistic independence, but they also require artists to assume more financial risk and operational responsibility.

[30:03] **From Music Celebrity to Institutional Venture Capital**

[事实] The Chainsmokers initially entered venture investing with the advantages of a successful music brand, a marketing platform, and strong appeal to consumer companies.

[事实] They say relationships with founders and opportunities to contribute to companies became more compelling than treating investments as passive income.

[事实] Their firm, Mantis, focuses on seed and Series A investments across cybersecurity, AI, infrastructure, deep technology, and health technology.

[事实] Mantis does not seek lead positions and instead describes itself as a collaborative “sixth man” that contributes relationships, go-to-market assistance, and brand-building expertise.

[推测] The firm’s positioning acknowledges that celebrity-derived access is most credible when paired with a clearly defined supporting role and repeatable operational value.

[37:22] **Why Fame Is Not Enough for Venture Success**

[事实] The Chainsmokers advise celebrities to secure their personal finances before entering venture capital because the asset class is long-duration, illiquid, and difficult.

[事实] They say successful investing requires constant work, direct participation, and the ability to filter substantial noise to find exceptional companies.

[事实] Fame can help them contact founders and gain access, but it can also deter institutional investors who fear being criticized for backing a celebrity-branded fund.

[事实] They respond to skepticism by focusing on results and expecting that stronger numbers will improve later fundraising.

[推测] Celebrity status is neither a substitute for investment skill nor an unqualified advantage; it amplifies both access and reputational scrutiny.

[39:43] **Returns, Hype, and Non-Obvious Investments**

[事实] The speakers argue that consistent DPI can overcome doubts because realized returns are more persuasive than fame, reputation, or paper valuations.

[事实] The Chainsmokers distinguish between investing in companies likely to receive rapid valuation markups and backing founders or ideas with credible long-term potential.

[事实] Jason Calacanis recalls that many investors rejected Uber because they did not want exposure to a complicated real-world transportation business.

[事实] Chamath Palihapitiya says his prior experience at Facebook caused him to reject Robinhood’s growth signals, which he describes as a billion-dollar mistake.

[推测] Deep domain experience can become a liability when it hardens into assumptions that prevent an investor from recognizing a new business model.

[43:21] **Founder Quality and the “What If It Works?” Test**

[事实] The discussion identifies founder capability, determination, and execution as factors capable of cutting through investors’ prior biases.

[事实] Calacanis says the key question is what the world would look like if an unconventional company actually succeeded.

[事实] He says he has never sold a Robinhood share and bought additional shares when the public-market price was nine dollars.

[事实] Palihapitiya observes that some great investors entered an asset class without strong prior assumptions but possessed curiosity, networks, and critical-thinking ability.

[推测] Exceptional venture decisions may require enough knowledge to evaluate execution while retaining enough distance to imagine outcomes that industry insiders dismiss.

[45:07] **Follow-On Investing, Liquidity, and Concentration**

[事实] The Chainsmokers say Mantis recently achieved one of its first significant liquidity events through Underdog Fantasy.

[事实] They emphasize that paper performance is insufficient unless a fund eventually returns cash to its limited partners.

[事实] The discussion presents follow-on investing as the skill of concentrating capital in the portfolio’s strongest companies.

[事实] The guests are considering a growth fund, while the hosts describe special-purpose vehicles as another way to fund later-stage opportunities.

[推测] A young venture firm’s maturation depends not only on selecting companies but also on learning when to increase exposure and when to realize gains.

[49:28] **Secondary Markets and Signs of a Bubble**

[事实] The hosts describe a venture market in which companies remain private longer, allowing firms to buy and sell positions across different stages.

[事实] They propose redefining a “unicorn” by one billion dollars in revenue rather than by a paper valuation of one billion dollars.

[事实] The speakers identify transactions that apply two- or threefold markups without corresponding improvements in underlying performance as bubble behavior.

[事实] They suggest that such pricing can be a signal to sell at least part of a position.

[推测] The discussion favors operating results and liquidity over headline valuations as more reliable measures of investment success.

[51:41] **The Work Behind Long-Term Celebrity Businesses**

[事实] The Chainsmokers say they still perform and have maintained a Las Vegas presence for approximately a decade.

[事实] The closing discussion describes a recurring pattern in which public attention creates an opening, mastery builds credibility, and sustained work turns both into a durable business.

[事实] The guests say their differentiator is continuing to work after the initial excitement has faded.

[推测] The episode’s central lesson is that fame supplies leverage, but endurance comes from discipline, specialization, and years of largely invisible execution.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode is most valuable as a comparative study of two versions of celebrity entrepreneurship. Jake Paul presents an aggressive, vertically integrated flywheel linking content, combat sports, promotion, investing, and philanthropy, while The Chainsmokers offer a more collaborative model built around music, relationships, and a supporting role in venture capital.

[推测] Its strongest moments are the candid discussions of attention’s downside, celebrity-investor skepticism, realized returns, and the biases that caused experienced investors to overlook companies such as Uber and Robinhood. These examples give the conversation more substance than a conventional discussion of personal branding.

[推测] A limitation is that many claims about business performance, platform economics, portfolio quality, and industry impact are presented conversationally and are not independently examined within the transcript. The discussion also gives more attention to investor and celebrity perspectives than to those of artists, founders, fighters, or limited partners affected by these models.

[推测] The episode is especially suitable for creators, athletes, entertainers, founders, and early-stage investors interested in converting audience reach into lasting enterprise value. Its practical message is straightforward: access and attention can create opportunities, but only focused execution, credible partnerships, and cash returns can sustain them.
