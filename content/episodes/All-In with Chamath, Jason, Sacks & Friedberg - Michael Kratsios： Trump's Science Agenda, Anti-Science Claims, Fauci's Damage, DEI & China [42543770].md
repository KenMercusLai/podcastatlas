# Michael Kratsios: Trump's Science Agenda, Science Funding, Fauci, DEI, and China

## 概览

This episode is an interview with Michael Kratsios, Director of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy. The central question is whether the Trump administration is anti-science, and Kratsios argues that its goal is the opposite: to renew American science through the “Science, A New Golden Age” agenda.

The discussion moves from public distrust of science to funding priorities, NSF and NIH incentives, DEI-related grants, climate research, and the claim that American science has become less productive per dollar. Kratsios repeatedly says the administration wants to support scientists, not institutions, and to test new ways of funding higher-risk, higher-upside research.

The second half broadens into competition with China, the STEM talent pipeline, immigration, gifted education, COVID-era damage to trust, and implementation. Kratsios points to big goals in space, quantum computing, fusion, and AI-enabled science, while the interviewer presses on whether Congress, universities, and future administrations will sustain the changes.

## 分段落总结

[00:43] **OSTP's Role and the Anti-Science Question**

[事实] Kratsios says OSTP coordinates science and technology policy across agencies because the United States does not have one single science-and-technology agency.
[事实] He says he previously served as U.S. Chief Technology Officer in the first Trump administration.
[事实] Asked whether the administration is anti-science, he says it is not and cites the “Science, A New Golden Age” report as evidence of its priorities.

[02:36] **Science, Politics, and Public Perception**

[事实] The interviewer cites a Nature poll in which 86% of respondents supported Kamala Harris and 6% supported Donald Trump.
[事实] Kratsios says the scientific community has “lost its way” and that science has been deeply politicized over the last 15 to 20 years.
[事实] He says COVID marked a peak of dogma, where disagreeing with one prominent figure could get someone labeled anti-science.
[推测] The episode frames the administration’s science agenda as both a policy program and a reputational counterargument.

[04:10] **Scientific Method Applied to Science Policy**

[事实] The interviewer describes science as inquiry, experimentation, data collection, and continued questioning.
[事实] Kratsios agrees and says government should apply those same principles to science policy and research funding.
[事实] He says science advocates often focus only on whether the R&D budget rises, while ignoring whether roughly $200 billion in annual S&T funding is producing the best breakthroughs.
[推测] The conversation shifts the definition of “supporting science” from spending more money to measuring outcomes and incentives.

[05:49] **Funding Cuts, NSF, and DEI Grants**

[事实] The interviewer says the administration disrupted, froze, or terminated $3 billion in unspent funds on active grants in 2025.
[事实] Kratsios says Congress controls appropriations and distinguishes actual funding from proposed budgets.
[事实] He cites a Senate analysis associated with Ted Cruz claiming roughly 25% of NSF grants during the Biden administration went to DEI-related “science.”
[事实] Kratsios argues that forcing grant applicants to include DEI factors is politicization and not “gold standard science.”
[推测] The administration’s justification is less a rejection of research funding overall than an attempt to redirect funding away from topics it defines as political.

[08:18] **Climate Research and Emergency Framing**

[事实] The interviewer asks whether anthropogenic climate change is settled, whether it is a climate emergency, and why climate research dollars are being redirected.
[事实] Kratsios says Secretary Wright’s position is that the climate is changing and humans have contributed CO2 through fossil fuel use.
[事实] He says the data does not support calling climate change an emergency.
[事实] He focuses on RCP 8.5, saying it was an extreme scenario that shaped media coverage and funding and was later removed because it could not be scientifically substantiated.
[推测] The segment presents climate policy as a dispute over severity, modeling, and policy response rather than over whether humans affect climate at all.

[10:33] **Energy Abundance as Climate Strategy**

[事实] Kratsios says the administration wants to invest in technologies that create abundant energy for Americans, including fusion and nuclear energy.
[事实] He says the administration set a 2035 target for fusion and has been strongly supportive of nuclear energy.
[事实] The interviewer argues that China’s carbon output changes the global calculation and that energy restrictions can harm poorer communities.
[事实] Kratsios cites roughly two billion people using dirty cooking fuels and says the UN has reported about three million deaths per year from that.
[推测] The preferred solution in this discussion is technological substitution and affordability, not economic contraction or restrictive climate policy.

[13:16] **NIH Funding and Scientific Stagnation**

[事实] The interviewer says NIH funding rose from $14 billion in 1998 to $27 billion in 2003 and $47 billion in 2024.
[事实] He says there has not been a proportional increase in breakthrough treatments and references Eroom’s law as a decline in output per dollar.
[事实] Kratsios says the administration generally believes American science has stagnated.
[事实] He gives the loss of Concorde-era faster flight as an example of visible technological regression.
[推测] The stagnation argument is used to justify reforming the process of science, not simply changing the amount of funding.

[15:56] **Meta-Science and Golden Tickets**

[事实] Kratsios says government and Congressional incentives often reward bigger budgets, not honest evaluation of what works.
[事实] He says NSF and NIH will create meta-science units to experiment with different funding models and measure outcomes.
[事实] He describes current NSF peer review as merit-based but says it can favor safer, consensus-friendly proposals.
[事实] He says NSF will test “golden tickets,” where reviewers can unilaterally select some grants, a model he says was tested in Denmark and elsewhere.
[推测] This is the clearest example of importing portfolio-style risk tolerance into federal science funding.

[18:56] **Grant Duration and Risk-Taking**

[事实] The interviewer compares science funding to venture capital, where many failures may be acceptable if one success creates large returns.
[事实] Kratsios says most NSF grants are roughly 18 months, but not all research ideas fit that timeframe.
[事实] He says New Golden Age proposes shorter fast-track grants as well as longer three-, four-, or five-year grants.
[推测] Flexible grant length is presented as a way to make funding fit scientific uncertainty rather than administrative habit.

[20:18] **Funding Scientists, Not Just Projects**

[事实] The interviewer suggests funding outstanding individuals with more trust, money, and time, similar to backing great entrepreneurs.
[事实] Kratsios points to NSF Graduate Research Fellowships, saying roughly 2,600 are awarded to aspiring PhDs.
[事实] He says those fellowships are portable, allowing recipients to choose where to pursue their PhDs and making universities compete for them.
[推测] Kratsios supports the principle of backing people, though his example focuses mainly on early-career fellowships.

[22:06] **Big National Missions: Space, Quantum, Fusion, and Genesis**

[事实] The interviewer asks whether the U.S. can still organize major projects like Apollo, the Manhattan Project, and the Human Genome Project.
[事实] Kratsios says yes, but argues the U.S. has lost its way and needs to return to big, discrete national goals.
[事实] He lists goals including American boots on the moon in 2028, a nuclear reactor in space by 2028, first elements of a moon base by 2030, a scientifically relevant quantum computer by 2028, and fusion by 2035.
[事实] He calls the Genesis Mission the administration’s flagship project to double U.S. scientific output by applying AI to hard scientific problems.
[推测] The agenda tries to combine Apollo-style missions with private-sector participation and distributed scientific work.

[25:00] **What Government Should Fund**

[事实] The interviewer asks why government should fund areas like quantum and AI when private capital is already flowing heavily into AI.
[事实] Kratsios says government-funded research should refocus on basic, early-stage, pre-competitive discovery science.
[事实] He says the quantum goal is to create an instrument useful for scientific discovery, not merely a commercial product.
[事实] He says AI-for-science is about helping researchers use AI to accelerate basic research, not competing with private AI companies.
[推测] His boundary is that public money should fund enabling science and gaps that markets will not naturally cover.

[27:01] **Fragmented Agencies as a Feature**

[事实] The interviewer asks whether U.S. science agencies should be consolidated into one science and technology agency.
[事实] Kratsios says the distributed U.S. system is a feature, not a bug.
[事实] He contrasts it with China’s top-down system and says China has not achieved EUV lithography since U.S. export controls were imposed in 2019.
[事实] He says national-security R&D works better when people like DARPA are embedded close to their mission.
[推测] Kratsios favors White House coordination over structural consolidation.

[29:23] **Updating the Postwar Science Model**

[事实] The interviewer identifies four funding channels: government agencies, universities, industry, and independent nonprofit research institutes.
[事实] Kratsios says Vannevar Bush’s 1945 “Science, the Endless Frontier” shaped the postwar model of federal basic research, mainly through universities.
[事实] He says the R&D mix has shifted from roughly 70% government-funded and 30% private to roughly 70% private and 30% federal.
[事实] He argues the old model is no longer as relevant and that New Golden Age asks whether government is funding work others are not incentivized to do.
[推测] The key principle is additionality: federal dollars should fill gaps rather than preserve old channels by default.

[31:58] **Priorities and Universities**

[事实] Kratsios says elected officials, Congress, and the executive branch should have a thesis about what scientific priorities matter for the country.
[事实] He names AI, quantum, nuclear, and bio as areas the U.S. needs to win.
[事实] On universities, he says the administration is pro-scientist and wants to fund strong scientists wherever they are.
[事实] He says that includes universities, focused research organizations, and even independent researchers if they pass merit-based review.
[推测] The agenda weakens the privileged status of universities while trying to avoid being framed as anti-academic scientist.

[34:06] **The Science Race with China**

[事实] The interviewer says China’s R&D spending rose from $33 billion in 2000 to $670 billion in 2021, while U.S. spending rose roughly 3x.
[事实] He says China now publishes about 50% more papers than the U.S. in nearly every domain except some life sciences.
[事实] Kratsios says China understands scientific and technological leadership as foundational to economic and national security.
[事实] He says the U.S. advantage remains its venture ecosystem, government science funding, private industry, universities, and attractiveness to global talent.
[推测] The episode treats science leadership as geopolitical infrastructure, not only a public good for humanity.

[37:15] **China’s Playbook and Critical Minerals**

[事实] Asked what China gets right, Kratsios says China has copied IP, produced cheaper alternatives, dumped them into U.S. markets, pushed U.S. businesses out, and then squeezed the U.S.
[事实] He says China has acted or threatened action around critical minerals, making U.S. independence important.
[事实] The interviewer presses that China’s paper output may reflect real discovery gains and notes lower labor, automation, and supply-chain costs.
[推测] Kratsios gives a clearer critique of Chinese industrial strategy than a detailed explanation of China’s scientific productivity gains.

[38:50] **STEM Talent, Immigration, and Gifted Education**

[事实] Kratsios says the U.S. needs more Americans entering STEM fields.
[事实] He says the share of U.S.-born computer science PhD recipients has inverted from about 70% American and 30% foreign 30 years ago to the reverse today.
[事实] Asked about retaining foreign PhD students, he says the U.S. should encourage Americans in STEM and allow those who want to stay to pursue legal pathways.
[事实] He says NSF once supported gifted and talented students in middle and high school, and those programs were stopped.
[推测] The talent strategy prioritizes rebuilding the domestic pipeline while leaving skilled immigration reform relatively undefined.

[43:56] **Scientist Prestige, Fauci, and COVID Trust**

[事实] Kratsios says New Golden Age centers grants and fellowships around scientists themselves, not institutions or background.
[事实] The interviewer says postwar scientists were once public celebrities and asks whether that prestige was lost or led to figures like Fauci.
[事实] Kratsios says Fauci did more damage to science than anyone in modern history.
[事实] He criticizes COVID-era school masking and perceived inconsistencies between protest permissions and restrictions on hospital visits.
[推测] The discussion uses COVID as the main symbol of institutional science losing public trust.

[47:03] **Questioning and Anti-Science Labels**

[事实] The interviewer says COVID-era conclusions often lacked empiricism and that people who asked questions were shunned.
[事实] Kratsios agrees and says people who asked questions were criticized by the scientific community as anti-science.
[事实] He says similar labeling happens when people question parts of IPCC climate reports and again cites RCP 8.5.
[推测] Their implied standard for restoring trust is protecting dissent, separating skepticism from denial, and returning to inquiry.

[49:13] **Basic Research, Implementation, and Political Durability**

[事实] Kratsios says NIH director Jay Bhattacharya told him the median age of an intramural NIH researcher is 71, though Kratsios says he needs to dig further into that statistic.
[事实] He says he and OMB Director Russ Vought issued an annual R&D priorities memo that puts basic fundamental research on the first page.
[事实] He says the top five research agencies with more than $3 billion in R&D must report back within 90 days on implementation.
[事实] He cites NSF X Labs, focused research organizations, four-year PhDs with industry partnerships, and future work with Congress on appropriations.
[推测] The agenda’s durability depends on whether early agency programs show results quickly enough to make reversal politically costly.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value is that it gives a direct, policy-level explanation of the Trump administration’s science agenda from the official responsible for coordinating it. It is especially useful for understanding the administration’s own framing of funding cuts, DEI, climate research, NIH and NSF reform, and competition with China.

[推测] Its strongest sections are the concrete descriptions of proposed funding experiments: meta-science units, golden tickets, flexible grant durations, portable fellowships, and funding scientists outside traditional university channels. These give the conversation more substance than a generic political debate about whether science budgets should rise or fall.

[推测] Its limitation is that many contested claims are presented from the administration’s perspective without external verification inside the transcript, including claims about DEI grant shares, RCP 8.5, climate-emergency framing, and NIH researcher age. Readers looking for a balanced empirical assessment would need to compare those claims with independent data.

[推测] The episode is best suited for listeners interested in U.S. science policy, federal R&D funding, AI-for-science, China competition, and the politics of public trust in scientific institutions.
