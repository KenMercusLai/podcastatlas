# The Pain, Paralysis, and Promise of Ambivalence

## 概览

Ambivalence—feeling positive and negative emotions at the same time—is uncomfortable and can delay decisions. People who express it are often perceived as indecisive, less competent, or less suited to leadership.

Yet mixed emotions also provide useful information. They can improve judgment, openness to advice, creativity, cognitive flexibility, cooperation, and the ability to understand complex situations.

Psychologist Naomi Rothman explains that the benefits depend heavily on context. Ambivalence can be costly in competitive settings but productive where people share cooperative goals and tolerate uncertainty rather than rushing toward closure.

The episode concludes with a listener’s story about a stranger whose insistence on prompt medical follow-up led to the removal of melanoma and changed the listener’s life.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Friends, rivals, and the origins of ambivalence**

[事实] As a teenager, Naomi Rothman played alongside close friends on a travel soccer team but competed against many of them during the high-school season.

[事实] Loving the same people she was trying to defeat created an emotionally conflicted experience that stayed with her.

[事实] Rothman later became a psychologist studying how mixed emotions affect thought, behavior, and social perception.

[03:01] **Everyday ambivalence and delayed decisions**

[事实] Rothman felt torn about buying her daughter a popular toy because it was highly desired but expensive, potentially messy, and possibly short-lived entertainment.

[事实] Her hesitation allowed the toy to sell out and increase in price, showing how indecision can impose costs in time, money, or disappointment.

[事实] In an academic meeting she led, expressing mixed feelings made her appear less assertive and encouraged senior colleagues to take control.

[08:15] **Why people distrust ambivalent experts and leaders**

[事实] Rothman’s studies found that ambivalent people were judged as less dominant, confident, and assertive than happy, angry, or neutral people.

[事实] Participants sometimes took more money from an ambivalent negotiation partner, suggesting that perceived uncertainty can invite attempts to seize control.

[事实] Physicians described as conflicted were rated as less informed and lower-performing, and people were less willing to follow their advice.

[事实] Executives who expressed ambivalence during earnings calls faced greater analyst skepticism, with a stronger scrutiny penalty for women.

[事实] Political culture similarly rewards certainty, as illustrated by a 2004 attack advertisement portraying John Kerry’s changing positions as weakness.

[13:41] **The discomfort of mixed emotions**

[事实] Research drawing on dissonance theory describes ambivalence as an uncomfortable state of tension and conflict.

[事实] That discomfort often motivates people to simplify the situation, choose a side, and seek psychological closure.

[事实] Ambivalence can therefore produce paralysis, irritate others, and make a person appear less ready to lead.

[15:42] **The danger of suppressing warning signals**

[事实] Rothman and her husband ignored their unspoken doubts and bought a rural farmhouse in Bucks County that exceeded their budget.

[事实] The long commute, isolation, maintenance burdens, and difficulty keeping the family together made the house unsuitable.

[事实] They sold it four years later at a loss.

[事实] Rothman connected the experience to a desire for stability and closure that caused them to disregard negative information.

[19:03] **Mixed emotions can improve judgment**

[事实] In experiments, participants induced to feel ambivalent were more receptive to peer advice and made more accurate estimates than comparison groups.

[事实] Rothman proposed that multiple emotions may counterbalance the biases associated with any single emotion.

[事实] Emotions can function as information about the environment, while ambivalence may signal that a problem is contradictory and requires further exploration.

[事实] Research discussed in the episode suggests that a strong desire for closure reduces learning and exploration, whereas a weaker closure imperative permits more of both.

[23:41] **Creativity and cognitive flexibility**

[事实] Experiments using emotionally mixed film scenes found that ambivalence encouraged unusual associations in later creativity tasks.

[事实] Rothman’s research found that emotional ambivalence can broaden how people understand concepts and make information processing more cognitively flexible.

[事实] Suppressing ambivalence can prioritize decisiveness over accuracy and shut down complex thinking.

[26:02] **Cooperation changes the social effects of ambivalence**

[事实] In competitive negotiations, an ambivalent partner may be treated as weak and invite dominant behavior.

[事实] In negotiations framed around cooperative goals, however, an ambivalent partner prompted more effortful problem-solving and better outcomes.

[事实] Rothman concluded that shared goals and cooperative norms help groups harness ambivalence productively.

[事实] The same principle may explain why expressing uncertainty can hurt a political candidate during an election but help an elected leader deliberate with advisers.

[28:17] **The paradox of ambivalent leadership**

[事实] Across laboratory and real-world studies, ambivalent leaders sought more information from team members.

[事实] Their behavior encouraged team members to seek information from one another, producing better team performance than under less ambivalent leaders.

[事实] Ambivalent leaders were also perceived as more ethical and more likely to consider employees’ perspectives, even though people were less inclined to promote them.

[事实] Research discussed in the episode links mixed emotions with meaning-oriented well-being by helping people integrate positive and negative aspects of life.

[33:16] **Learning to value complexity**

[事实] Rothman’s father taught her to recognize contradiction and complexity through Shakespeare, especially the relationship between Lear and Cordelia.

[事实] His conversational style resisted premature certainty and relied on questions such as “what if,” “perhaps,” and “I wonder.”

[事实] A “paradox mindset” replaces either-or thinking with a both-and approach, allowing people to accept competing demands and see how each may support the other.

[事实] Rothman stressed that the idea that such a mindset can reliably unlock ambivalence’s benefits remains a hypothesis requiring further study.

[41:05] **Ambivalence as protection and honest self-examination**

[事实] People may deliberately cultivate mixed feelings under uncertainty to soften the emotional impact of possible rejection or failure.

[事实] Rothman used this strategy while dating her future spouse, but she believes it also helped both partners examine their compatibility more carefully.

[事实] When her mother underwent lung-cancer surgery, Rothman resisted offering only cheerful reassurance and openly acknowledged her own anxiety.

[事实] Her mother later wrote that the conversation made her feel deeply seen and understood, praising the importance of embracing ambivalence and delaying premature certainty.

[49:08] **A stranger’s warning that saved a life**

[事实] Listener Jackie Briggs met a woman she calls Angela at a health conference in 2006.

[事实] Angela, a nurse, noticed an irregular mark on Briggs’s arm and urged her not to delay seeing a dermatologist.

[事实] Briggs followed the advice, was diagnosed with melanoma, and had it surgically removed.

[事实] Briggs credited Angela with saving her life; she later left her demanding IT job and took up activities including mountain climbing, kayaking, hiking, and motorcycle riding.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s main strength is its refusal to classify ambivalence as simply good or bad. Personal stories, laboratory findings, workplace research, and examples from medicine and politics show why mixed emotions can simultaneously create social liabilities and cognitive advantages.

Its most practical insight is contextual: ambivalence becomes more useful when people can tolerate discomfort, resist premature closure, and establish cooperative norms. Honest uncertainty may improve judgment, but expressing it indiscriminately in competitive or status-conscious settings can carry real costs.

[推测] The discussion is especially valuable for leaders, negotiators, clinicians, and anyone facing decisions with competing values. Its main limitation is that several proposed mechanisms and applications remain under investigation, so the episode offers a framework for thinking rather than a universal prescription.
