# Saigon, 1965: Intelligence, Bias, and the Limits of Knowing the Enemy

## Overview

This episode examines the Viet Cong Motivation and Morale Project, a secret RAND Corporation study commissioned during the Vietnam War. Drawing on thousands of interviews with prisoners and defectors, the project sought to determine whether American bombing was breaking the enemy’s will.

The same evidence produced radically different conclusions. Leon Goure believed the Viet Cong were nearing collapse, Conrad Kellen concluded that the United States could never win, and interviewer Mai Elliott remained conflicted. Their divergent readings support the episode’s central argument: intelligence failures often arise not from a lack of information, but from the histories, loyalties, fears, and biases through which people interpret it.

## 分段落总结

[01:40] **Mai Elliott and the Secret RAND Study**

[事实] Mai Elliott recalls her life in Saigon during the early 1960s, when she believed her circumstances could hardly have been better.

[事实] RAND recruited Elliott to work on a secret Pentagon study led by Leon Goure, interviewing Viet Cong prisoners and defectors.

[事实] The episode follows Elliott, Goure, and Conrad Kellen through the largely forgotten Viet Cong Motivation and Morale Project.

[07:18] **Why More Intelligence Is Not Necessarily the Answer**

[事实] The episode frames the project as a case study in intelligence failure and asks why understanding an enemy’s thinking is so difficult.

[事实] It argues that governments commonly respond to surprise by demanding more spies, intercepted communications, satellite images, and other information.

[事实] The RAND project suggests that even extraordinarily detailed knowledge of an adversary may not resolve the underlying analytical problem.

[09:01] **A War Intended to Break the Enemy’s Will**

[事实] After Vietnam was divided in 1954, the communist North supported Viet Cong guerrillas operating against the American-backed South.

[事实] The United States attempted to persuade North Vietnam to abandon the struggle through bombing rather than by invading the North.

[事实] Because this strategy depended on breaking the enemy’s will, American officials needed some way to measure Viet Cong morale.

[事实] Few officials in Washington understood Vietnam or spoke Vietnamese, prompting the government to turn to RAND.

[11:21] **Leon Goure’s Background and Mission**

[事实] Leon Goure was born into a Russian Menshevik family that fled Russia, Germany, and France before reaching the United States.

[事实] His son describes him as deeply attached to America and convinced that it represented the family’s final refuge.

[事实] Goure secured substantial Pentagon funding to expand RAND’s interviews with captured guerrillas and defectors.

[推测] His refugee experience and strong American patriotism likely shaped his conviction that communism had to be resisted wherever it advanced.

[15:31] **Mai Elliott and the Interview Archive**

[事实] Elliott grew up in a privileged family in northern Vietnam, where her father served in the French colonial administration.

[事实] Her family fled south after the communist victory, abandoning its home and possessions because her father feared retaliation.

[事实] RAND’s Vietnamese teams conducted extended, generally nonconfrontational interviews in prisons and rural areas, then transcribed, translated, and edited them in Saigon.

[事实] The project ultimately produced approximately 62,000 pages of interviews, creating an unusually detailed portrait of the Viet Cong.

[19:10] **Evidence Used to Justify Escalation**

[事实] Goure emphasized a finding that belief in a Viet Cong victory had fallen from 65 percent of interviewees to 20 percent after a year of heavy American bombing.

[事实] He presented his conclusions to military leaders, the Pentagon, the White House, journalists, and intelligence officials.

[事实] During 1965 and 1966, as President Lyndon Johnson deepened American involvement, Goure’s analysis supplied a public and institutional justification for escalation.

[22:22] **Conrad Kellen Enters the Debate**

[事实] Conrad Kellen was a German Jewish refugee and Second World War intelligence veteran with experience in psychological warfare.

[事实] RAND hired him after he criticized American propaganda leaflets for asking ordinary soldiers to end a war they had neither begun nor controlled.

[事实] By late 1966, RAND was divided over Vietnam and concerned that Goure had bypassed its normal review procedures.

[事实] Kellen read roughly one thousand interviews and concluded that the Viet Cong were not collapsing and that the United States could not win.

[29:29] **One Archive, Three Interpretations**

[事实] Goure and Kellen reached opposite conclusions from the same body of evidence: Goure saw weakening morale, while Kellen saw enduring resolve.

[事实] Elliott’s direct encounter with a captured communist officer challenged her belief that communists were merely violent, inhuman thugs.

[事实] She found the officer principled and deeply committed, leaving the interview with more questions than answers.

[事实] Although the encounter complicated her views, her family’s vulnerability prevented her from immediately accepting its implications for the war.

[33:49] **Personal History as an Analytical Lens**

[事实] Elliott explains that evidence can intensify fear rather than change a person’s mind when family survival appears to be at stake.

[事实] Goure described himself as a “professional refugee” and regarded defending American national interests as sufficient reason to continue the struggle against communism.

[事实] Kellen’s own experience of war led him to focus on the Viet Cong’s determination and on the human reality concealed by abstract language such as “casualties.”

[推测] Goure’s need to believe in resistance, Elliott’s fear of communist victory, and Kellen’s exhaustion with war each provided a different interpretive lens for the same intelligence.

[事实] The episode concludes that intelligence failures occur because analysts are human beings whose judgments are shaped by personal history and bias.

[38:42] **Aftermath and Delayed Recognition**

[事实] RAND recalled Goure from Vietnam in April 1967 and encouraged him to leave the organization; he later joined the University of Miami.

[事实] After moving to the United States, Elliott eventually accepted that North Vietnam was determined and that the war was wrong and unwinnable.

[事实] Kellen warned early that the war could not be won, but his conclusion was not heeded until it was too late.

[事实] Kellen criticized institutions that called themselves scientific while recognizing as experts only those who confirmed their existing views.

[事实] The Second World War continued to haunt Kellen, and near the end of his life he dreamed that Nazis were coming to take him away.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its use of three intertwined biographies to make an abstract problem in intelligence analysis emotionally concrete. Rather than treating bias as a simple intellectual defect, it shows how displacement, trauma, loyalty, and fear can shape what even highly capable people perceive in the evidence before them.

Its central lesson extends beyond Vietnam: gathering more information cannot guarantee sound judgment when analysts and institutions reward interpretations that support their existing commitments. The contrast between Goure and Kellen is especially effective because neither lacked access to evidence or intellectual ability.

The episode is less concerned with evaluating the interview sample or research methodology in technical detail than with explaining interpretation. It is therefore best suited to listeners interested in intelligence, institutional decision-making, the Vietnam War, cognitive bias, and the human consequences of policy analysis.
