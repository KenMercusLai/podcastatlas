# Denmark: The Rescue of the Danish Jews

## 概览

This episode of *The Rest Is History* uses Denmark’s 2022 World Cup profile as a route into one of the Second World War’s rare “good news” stories: the rescue of Danish Jews from Nazi deportation in 1943.

The discussion follows Denmark from its rapid German occupation in April 1940 through the collapse of the earlier “cooperation, not collaboration” arrangement in 1943. It then explains how warnings from German official Georg Ferdinand Duckwitz, Danish political and civic networks, Sweden’s willingness to receive refugees, and the actions of ordinary Danes enabled thousands of Jews to escape across the Oresund.

The hosts stress that the rescue was not caused by one factor alone. Denmark’s civic culture, the small size and integration of its Jewish population, Sweden’s proximity, and the ambiguous conduct of German administrator Werner Best all contributed to the outcome.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **A Danish Good News Story**

[事实] The episode is part of a World Cup-themed series covering the 32 countries participating in Qatar.

[事实] Instead of focusing on Vikings, the hosts choose the rescue of the Danish Jews during the Second World War.

[推测] The episode is framed as unusual for history because it centers on collective courage and survival rather than catastrophe alone.

[01:53] **Germany Occupies Denmark**

[事实] Denmark was formally neutral when the Second World War began in September 1939.

[事实] Germany invaded Denmark on 9 April 1940, and Denmark capitulated within hours after limited resistance.

[事实] The hosts note that Denmark’s flat terrain, small army, and land border with Germany made prolonged resistance militarily unrealistic.

[04:13] **A Model Protectorate**

[事实] The Germans allowed Denmark to retain domestic institutions including its government, parliament, police, courts, and King Christian X.

[事实] Nazi leaders viewed Danes as fellow “Aryans” and wanted Denmark to function as a model protectorate.

[事实] Germany also wanted Danish food supplies, including dairy and bacon, and believed cooperation would keep production flowing.

[推测] This pragmatic German interest in stability later helped create space for Danish resistance and rescue activity.

[05:42] **King Christian X and Danish Jewish Citizens**

[事实] King Christian X remained in Denmark and rode through Copenhagen on horseback as a public symbol of national dignity and continuity.

[事实] The Danish Jewish population was small, roughly 6,000 people plus associated family members, and was described as highly assimilated.

[事实] The king and Danish politicians made clear they would not accept persecution of Danish Jews.

[事实] The hosts reject the myth that the king wore a yellow star, but say he did state that Danish Jews were Danish citizens and could not be touched.

[09:18] **The Telegram Crisis and Martial Law**

[事实] In October 1942, Hitler sent King Christian X a birthday telegram, and the king replied with a brief formal thank-you.

[事实] Hitler was offended by the curt reply and reacted by recalling the German ambassador and expelling the Danish ambassador.

[事实] Relations worsened through strikes and Danish refusal to cooperate, and in August 1943 Germany dissolved the Danish government and declared martial law.

[事实] After this, the Germans moved toward action against Danish Jews.

[11:48] **Georg Ferdinand Duckwitz Gives Warning**

[事实] Georg Ferdinand Duckwitz was a German embassy official in Copenhagen and had been a Nazi since 1932.

[事实] Werner Best told Duckwitz in early September 1943 that the Germans planned to round up and deport Danish Jews.

[事实] Duckwitz opposed the plan, went to Berlin to argue against it, and was ignored.

[事实] He later warned Danish politician Hans Hedtoft, giving the Danes a short window to act.

[14:40] **The Warning Spreads**

[事实] Hans Hedtoft informed Chief Rabbi Marcus Melchior, who told his Copenhagen congregation there would be no service and urged them to go into hiding.

[事实] Danish civil servants and others spread warnings, including by contacting people with Jewish-sounding names from telephone books.

[事实] Jewish families began disappearing into friends’ homes, summer houses, cellars, attics, and other hiding places.

[推测] The rescue depended less on a centralized organization than on rapid informal trust networks.

[16:27] **Niels Bohr, Greta Garbo, and Sweden**

[事实] Physicist Niels Bohr escaped early to Sweden.

[事实] Bohr sought assurance that Sweden would receive Danish Jewish refugees and gained access to King Gustav V with help from Greta Garbo.

[事实] On 2 October 1943, Swedish radio announced that Danish Jews would be welcome in Sweden.

[推测] Sweden’s public commitment made the escape route more credible and urgent.

[19:04] **Ordinary Danes Organize Escape Routes**

[事实] The hosts say there was no pre-existing mass organization to manage the rescue.

[事实] Danish Jews were hidden in churches, hospitals, hotels, seaside homes, and private houses.

[事实] Nearly 8,000 people, including family members, were moved toward the coast of Zealand.

[事实] Fishermen transported people across the Oresund by fishing boats, rowboats, and sometimes kayaks; some charged payment.

[22:08] **The Elsinore Sewing Club**

[事实] One rescue group was called the Elsinore Sewing Club.

[事实] Its members included a bookbinder, a police officer, a police clerk, and a local newspaper editor.

[事实] They gathered fuel, boats, and support to move people from Helsingor to Sweden.

[事实] One of their boats is displayed at the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum in Washington, D.C.

[24:11] **Friends of the Sound and Gilleleje**

[事实] The Friends of the Sound were organized around innkeepers in Sneksten and helped nearly a thousand people cross to Sweden.

[事实] Innkeeper Henry Christen was later arrested for resistance activity and died in a concentration camp before the end of the war.

[事实] In Gilleleje, about 80 Jews hidden in a church loft were captured, except for a boy who hid behind a gravestone.

[事实] Many more Jews were hidden successfully in Gilleleje and escaped after the Germans had gone.

[27:54] **Survival and Deportation Numbers**

[事实] In total, 7,743 people crossed the Oresund to Sweden.

[事实] About 580 Danish Jews failed to escape; some remained hidden in Denmark until the end of the war.

[事实] 464 were captured and sent to Theresienstadt.

[事实] More than 99% of Denmark’s Jewish population survived the war.

[28:46] **Why Denmark Was Different**

[事实] The hosts discuss why Denmark’s Jewish survival rate was so much higher than in places such as the Netherlands.

[事实] One explanation given is Denmark’s patriotic democratic civic culture, in which Jews were widely regarded as fellow Danes.

[事实] Other factors included Sweden’s proximity, Sweden’s willingness to accept refugees, and the relatively small size of Denmark’s Jewish population.

[推测] The episode rejects a single-cause explanation and presents the rescue as the result of moral culture, geography, timing, and logistics working together.

[31:03] **Werner Best’s Ambiguous Role**

[事实] Werner Best was the SS general overseeing Denmark.

[事实] Best prioritized keeping Danish agricultural supplies flowing to Germany and wanted to avoid sabotage and strikes.

[事实] During the roundup, he instructed German forces to knock rather than break down doors.

[事实] He also ordered German patrol boats into harbor during the crucial escape period, supposedly for repainting.

[推测] Best’s conduct is presented as morally ambiguous: not heroic, but opportunistically helpful because he valued Danish cooperation.

[34:43] **Duckwitz After the War**

[事实] Werner Best was sentenced to death by a Danish court, but his sentence was reduced to 12 years on appeal.

[事实] Georg Ferdinand Duckwitz later became West German ambassador to Denmark and India, and served under Willy Brandt.

[事实] In 1971, Duckwitz was named Righteous Among the Nations by Israel.

[事实] Yad Vashem commemorates King Christian X and the Danish resistance movement.

[35:49] **Final Reflections**

[事实] The hosts describe the rescue as a powerful reminder that people can act bravely and do good.

[事实] They say Denmark may come out especially well in their World Cup-themed historical series.

[推测] The episode’s emotional force comes from showing broad civic decency in a period usually remembered for mass cruelty.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s value lies in how clearly it turns a complex wartime rescue into a narrative about institutions, geography, individual initiative, and civic culture. It avoids treating the rescue as a simple miracle and instead shows how many conditions had to align.

Its strongest moments are the concrete stories: King Christian X’s symbolic rides, Rabbi Melchior’s warning, the Elsinore Sewing Club, the Sneksten innkeepers, and the scenes in Gilleleje. These details make the rescue feel human rather than abstract.

[推测] A limitation is that the format favors a dramatic and morally uplifting arc, so some wider debates about Danish cooperation with occupation authorities are only lightly explored.

[推测] This episode is especially suitable for listeners interested in Second World War history, Holocaust rescue stories, Scandinavian history, and examples of civic solidarity under occupation.
