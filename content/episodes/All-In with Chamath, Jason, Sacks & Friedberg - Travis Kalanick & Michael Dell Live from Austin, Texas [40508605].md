# All-In Live in Austin: Travis Kalanick, Michael Dell, Physical AI, and Invest America

## 概览

This live episode centers on two builder interviews: Travis Kalanick discussing his post-Uber company after years in stealth, and Michael Dell explaining Texas, AI infrastructure, enterprise adoption, and philanthropy.

Kalanick frames his work as “digitizing the physical world”: manufacturing, real estate, and logistics become the physical equivalents of CPU, storage, and network. He applies that framework first to food infrastructure, then to mining automation and specialized robots.

Dell’s segment shifts from Texas as a pro-growth environment to the AI data-center boom, Dell AI Factory deployments, open models, edge inference, and the organizational redesign needed for companies to get real ROI from AI.

The closing section brings in Brad Gerstner to discuss Invest America, Michael and Susan Dell’s $6.25 billion pledge, and a broader argument that visible ownership accounts for children could reconnect families to the upside of American capitalism.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Travis Kalanick returns from stealth**

[事实] Jason introduces Travis Kalanick as his favorite angel investment and says Kalanick spent about seven years building largely out of public view.

[事实] Kalanick says employees at a multi-thousand-person company listed “stealth” on LinkedIn, including salespeople and recruiters.

[事实] The company operated under different names across countries, including CloudKitchens in the U.S., Kitchen Valley in Korea, Namah in the Middle East, and other local names.

[推测] The long stealth period helped keep the company quiet while it expanded globally, but it also made normal hiring, sales, and public positioning harder.

[02:50] **The atoms-based computer**

[事实] Kalanick maps digital computing resources to physical-world equivalents: CPU manipulates bits, storage stores bits, and networks move bits; manufacturing manipulates atoms, real estate stores atoms, and logistics moves atoms.

[事实] He says City Storage Systems was intentionally boring as a name and represented digitized real estate in an “atoms-based computer.”

[事实] The first “computer” was food: manufacturing, real estate, and logistics for prepared meals.

[事实] His stated food mission is to make prepared-and-delivered meals efficient enough to approach grocery-store costs.

[推测] He is extending the Uber-style digitization thesis from transportation into vertically integrated physical infrastructure.

[06:00] **Food, mining, and specialized robots**

[事实] Kalanick says the broader company is called “Adams” in the transcript and describes its mission as physical automation to transform industries and move the world.

[事实] Beyond food, he says the company is doing physical mining automation, with a mission of making mines more productive to power earth’s industries.

[事实] He says the transport side includes a wheelbase for specialized robots, because robots that move and act in the physical world need mobility.

[事实] He says a transaction involving Pronto, a San Francisco company focused on automating mining equipment, was “inches from closing.”

[推测] The strategy favors specialized, economically useful robots over general humanoid robots.

[08:00] **Mining automation and remote operations**

[事实] Kalanick says automation can make existing mines more productive.

[事实] He says automation can also justify work in places that would otherwise be difficult because of labor footprint, safety issues, remoteness, or regulation.

[事实] The discussion links mining automation with remote monitoring and possible automated tunneling.

[推测] The implied thesis is that robotics can expand mineral access by reducing human and operational constraints.

[10:00] **Physical AI stack and autonomy**

[事实] Kalanick says the physical AI stack includes more than computation and models; it also includes land development, chemistry, and manufacturing.

[事实] He describes Tesla as “the Google of this era” for physical AI, while saying there are still many things to build.

[事实] On autonomous vehicles, he says Waymo is ahead as an existence proof but faces manufacturing, scale, urgency, and execution issues.

[事实] He says Tesla’s autonomy challenge is “hard mode times 100” and depends on when vision has a ChatGPT-like breakthrough.

[推测] He avoids picking a clear winner and frames autonomy as a timing and scaling problem rather than only a model-quality problem.

[13:45] **Vision-language-action and compression**

[事实] Kalanick says vision-language-action models and physical AI are active areas of research and implementation.

[事实] He compares the hoped-for moment in physical AI to the jump people felt from ChatGPT 3.5 to GPT-4.

[事实] He says language is an “epic compression” mechanism and notes that humans use far less energy than autonomous driving systems.

[事实] He imagines agents or safety systems using language-like communication to identify issues efficiently.

[推测] He sees compression, not just larger models, as important for making physical AI practical.

[16:20] **Austin move and California frustration**

[事实] Kalanick says he moved to Austin in December and had owned a place on Lake Austin since 2021.

[事实] He says he grew up in California and that leaving was emotionally difficult because it was home.

[事实] He says California is “getting weird,” while Austin feels like a place where people want to build the future.

[事实] He says he is setting up a team in Austin and jokes about having an office on the lake and jet-skiing to work.

[推测] The move is presented as a shift toward a builder-friendly environment, not only as a personal lifestyle choice.

[22:00] **California governance and public safety**

[事实] Friedberg raises the idea of a $500 million tech and business coalition to engage Sacramento and asks whether California can be turned around.

[事实] Kalanick says tech used to have a libertarian “leave me alone, I want to make stuff” posture, but that posture no longer works.

[事实] He says California ballot initiatives are powerful and identifies prosecutors who do not enforce crime as a focus area.

[事实] He says “truth and justice are the immune system for society” and ties social disorder to deterioration in those values.

[推测] The segment argues that tech leaders need direct political engagement rather than private frustration alone.

[25:40] **Robot hardware, materials, and capital**

[事实] Friedberg contrasts efficient biology with the high energy use of robots and asks about actuators, materials, and hardware layers.

[事实] Kalanick says he is focused on non-humanoid specialized robots, including food machines and mining equipment.

[事实] He agrees there is a large opportunity for venture capital and research in material science because hardware must catch up if software works.

[事实] On capital, he says capital is not a strategic weapon by itself, but at Uber it became a required competency because competitors could receive huge funding and quickly take market share.

[推测] Kalanick treats fundraising as strategic only when scale and financing are core competitive constraints.

[30:00] **Middle East capital and macro uncertainty**

[事实] Kalanick says his Middle East business was supposed to go public in January, but the Saudi market fell about 20% over roughly two months.

[事实] He says oil prices were part of the issue and that people in Saudi Arabia wanted oil prices to rise.

[事实] Asked whether war in the Middle East could reduce capital flows to the U.S., he says he does not know and is not calling investors during a war.

[事实] He says an optimistic view is that the situation may not last forever, comparing it to fears around tariffs that changed quickly.

[推测] He links long-term optimism to AI, physical AI, productivity gains, progress, and abundance.

[32:00] **China manufacturing and immigration**

[事实] Kalanick says China’s cities and manufacturing base have advanced dramatically over decades.

[事实] He says Shenzhen now feels like it is “one-upping Singapore” and points to Xiaomi and other hungry manufacturers.

[事实] He says the U.S. should step up its game while aiming for friendly, adult competition with China.

[事实] He says an employee in China with an American wife faces difficulty coming to work in the U.S., and he wants more normalcy for good migration.

[推测] His view combines respect for Chinese execution with a call for U.S. competitiveness and selective openness to talent.

[36:00] **Michael Dell on Texas**

[事实] Michael Dell says he started Dell in Austin with $1,000 in his University of Texas dorm room about 42 years earlier.

[事实] He says Dell expects about $140 billion in revenue this year.

[事实] Dell attributes Texas’s appeal to low taxes, a pro-growth environment, and a business-friendly climate.

[事实] He says Austin is close to being one of the ten largest U.S. cities, Texas may have four of the ten largest cities, one out of ten U.S. children is born in Texas, and Texas has more New York Stock Exchange companies than New York.

[推测] Dell presents Texas’s rise as the result of long-standing structural advantages rather than a sudden trend.

[40:00] **AI data centers and token demand**

[事实] Dell says AI infrastructure buildout requires many new data centers and large amounts of power.

[事实] He says Texas has advantages because it has power, land, and an ability to build, especially in less populated West Texas areas.

[事实] He says Dell introduced its first H100 server a couple of weeks before ChatGPT was announced.

[事实] He says Dell’s AI infrastructure business went from about $2 billion to $10 billion to $25 billion, and is expected to be about $50 billion this year.

[推测] Dell positions his company as a key infrastructure supplier for both hyperscale and enterprise AI.

[42:00] **Dell AI Factory and enterprise demand**

[事实] Dell says demand exceeds supply across hyperscalers, cloud service providers, sovereign AI, and enterprises.

[事实] He says more than 4,000 enterprises are using Dell AI Factory.

[事实] He says companies want to protect their data while using AI on it, bringing AI to where the data is.

[事实] He says early large customers were building their own models, using open-source models, or applying machine learning in areas like algorithmic trading.

[推测] Dell sees enterprise AI as a local and controlled infrastructure problem, not only a public-cloud service.

[43:50] **AI ROI requires organizational redesign**

[事实] The discussion says accelerated depreciation lets companies deduct 100% of data-center and infrastructure spending in the current year for a 10-year period.

[事实] Dell says his company sees AI use cases with 20% or greater productivity or efficiency improvement.

[事实] He says those gains do not happen by simply pressing a button; companies need to rethink outcomes, simplify processes, standardize tools, gather data, and apply technology.

[事实] He says this has to be done top-down because silos will not spontaneously improve themselves.

[推测] The main barrier to AI ROI is organizational redesign and leadership discipline, not just access to models.

[46:00] **Speed, culture, and AI-native competition**

[事实] Dell says he told his team three years earlier that a future competitor would be faster, more innovative, lower cost, and more successful unless Dell became that company.

[事实] He says the biggest benefit from AI transformation is speed.

[事实] He says Dell’s infrastructure business grew 73% last quarter and was guided to grow about 100% in the current quarter.

[事实] He estimates only 10% to 15% of large companies have really figured out AI, while others are fumbling or doing superficial projects.

[推测] The episode frames culture, leadership, and willingness to change as the decisive factors in AI adoption.

[49:00] **AI-native businesses and doing more**

[事实] Dell says AI-native businesses will disrupt industries.

[事实] He says incumbents have assets like brands, balance sheets, and customer relationships, but those are expiring-value assets if companies do not change quickly.

[事实] He cites Stripe’s view that the 2025 cohort of companies is growing about four times faster than the 2018 cohort because newer companies start with better tools.

[事实] Dell rejects the idea that AI mainly means doing the same things with fewer people; he expects people to do many more things and solve more problems.

[推测] The AI transition is presented as expansion of productive capacity rather than only labor substitution.

[52:00] **Where inference will live**

[事实] Dell says inference will happen across cloud, edge, devices, and embedded equipment.

[事实] He says wherever data is created, AI infrastructure and inference should be close to that data.

[事实] He says the lowest-cost token will be generated where the data is, including on phones, PCs, and embedded equipment.

[事实] Dell says the company has 10,000 customers embedding Dell products in their own products, including medical devices, security, hospitals, industrial plants, and other data-driven environments.

[推测] His infrastructure view favors a distributed AI market rather than a cloud-only architecture.

[54:20] **Open models, desktops, and agents**

[事实] Jason asks whether powerful desktops will return because users want to protect data and skills rather than send them to cloud providers.

[事实] Dell says not everyone may have a $10,000 desktop, but that would be good for Dell.

[事实] Dell says Dell Portal and Hugging Face qualify open models on many kinds of machines.

[事实] He mentions progress in open-source models, including Google Gemma, OpenAI open-source models, Nvidia Nemotron, and a thriving open-source ecosystem.

[事实] He says autonomous agents will be important inside companies and will need authentication, validation, controls, and security.

[推测] Open models create demand for both local compute and enterprise governance layers.

[57:40] **AGI uncertainty and AI-first operations**

[事实] Dell says he does not really know how to define or time AGI and superintelligence.

[事实] He says recent model releases feel like a quality threshold and that teams can now accomplish in days or weeks what previously took months.

[事实] Asked how to build an AI-first company, Dell says he focuses on running his own company.

[事实] He says Dell is thinking about reimagining tools and processes for 2027, 2028, and 2029, then accelerating toward that future.

[推测] Dell avoids abstract AGI speculation and emphasizes near-term operational transformation.

[60:00] **AI backlash and regulation**

[事实] The hosts discuss AI ranking as an unpopular term and joke that calling it linear algebra, matrix multiplication, calculus, or statistics might make it sound friendlier.

[事实] Dell says he is generally optimistic and believes AI can improve education, scientific discovery, healthcare, energy, and other unsolved problems.

[事实] He frames AI as amplification of human potential and capability.

[事实] The group questions how lawmakers can stop people from running open-source models at home and asking for medical or legal advice.

[推测] The discussion suggests broad software bans will be hard to enforce if AI models are widely available on personal devices.

[64:00] **Invest America and the Dell pledge**

[事实] Dell says Brad Gerstner introduced him to the Invest America idea in 2021.

[事实] Dell says discussions with the prior administration did not produce action, but the Invest America Act was eventually passed.

[事实] Dell says thousands of companies are joining and matching the government contribution.

[事实] Michael and Susan Dell announced $250 for 25 million children aged 2 to 10 in zip codes with median income of $150,000 or less, totaling $6.25 billion.

[事实] Dell says families have to sign up to claim the accounts and that more than 100,000 children per day are signing up.

[推测] The gift is framed as direct capital access for children rather than traditional programmatic philanthropy.

[66:00] **Brad Gerstner on the legislative push**

[事实] Gerstner says he worked on Invest America for four years and had discussed it on the All-In pod.

[事实] He says there was a narrow legislative window in April during tariff turmoil, with Senate drafting and bipartisan support already in place.

[事实] He says he and Dell sought an Oval Office meeting and that Trump seeks out ideas from business leaders.

[事实] Gerstner frames Invest America as not a red idea or blue idea, but a “red, white and blue” idea.

[推测] Gerstner presents the program as both social policy and a defense of the ownership society.

[68:00] **Philanthropy as a platform**

[事实] Dell says his foundation focuses mainly on children in urban poverty, while also doing some local Central Texas work.

[事实] Dell says Invest America looked like a platform for directly giving to the people his philanthropy targets.

[事实] He says they considered starting in Texas first, but decided to go bigger.

[事实] Gerstner and Dell say they chair the Invest America giving committee and have had conversations with potential donors.

[推测] They want the Dell pledge to catalyze a broader movement involving billionaires, companies, states, parents, and smaller donors.

[70:00] **Account mechanics and Social Security 2.0**

[事实] Gerstner says every child under 18 is eligible to claim an account.

[事实] He says every child born in America starting January 1, 2027 will automatically get a Trump account at birth tied to their Social Security card.

[事实] He says the $1,000 contribution must be reauthorized every four years, but the accounts themselves do not.

[事实] He says 3.7 million children per year will get accounts that can compound like a 401(k) from birth.

[事实] He estimates that over 15 years, $5 trillion could move into families’ pockets.

[推测] He frames the program as a market-based supplement or alternative to opaque government transfer systems.

[72:00] **Visible ownership and compounding**

[事实] Gerstner says four and a half million children have claimed accounts and that the trajectory is toward 10 million by July 4, 2026.

[事实] He says children and parents will see an app that resembles Robinhood, showing their $1,000 or $250 and breaking S&P 500 ownership into companies like Nvidia, Walmart, and Dell.

[事实] He says parents, friends, relatives, and companies can add money through Apple Pay, QR codes, birthdays, holidays, and other occasions.

[事实] He says Social Security is a sacred promise, but criticizes it as money going into a black hole without a visible named account.

[推测] The design is meant to make children feel that they are “in the game” by turning abstract saving into visible ownership.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The strongest value of this episode is its operator-level framing. Kalanick turns physical AI into an infrastructure stack, while Dell turns enterprise AI into a question of data location, speed, process redesign, and leadership.

[推测] The highlights are the concrete operating details: Kalanick’s stealth company structure, the atoms-based analogy, Dell’s AI infrastructure revenue ramp, the 4,000-plus AI Factory claim, and the specific terms of the Dell Invest America pledge.

[推测] The main limitation is that the live format leaves many political and policy claims lightly challenged. Assertions about California governance, China, Social Security, AI regulation, and Invest America’s projected impact are discussed energetically but not deeply stress-tested in the transcript.

[推测] This episode is best for listeners interested in physical AI, robotics, AI infrastructure, enterprise transformation, Texas as a business environment, and ownership-based public policy. It is less suited for listeners looking for a technical deep dive or adversarial policy debate.
