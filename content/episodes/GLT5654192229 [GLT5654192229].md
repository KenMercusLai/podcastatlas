# The Da Vinci Code, the Priory of Sion, and the Cathars

## 概览

This episode uses the 20th anniversary of *The Da Vinci Code* as a way into the longer history of medieval heresy, conspiracy theories, and the modern myth of the Cathars.

The hosts trace the novel’s central claims about Jesus, Mary Magdalene, the Merovingians, the Priory of Sion, and the Holy Grail back through *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail*, BBC documentaries, Rennes-le-Château legends, forged archive documents, and the self-mythologizing activities of Pierre Plantard.

A key conclusion is that the famous Priory of Sion story was not an ancient medieval secret but a modern construction, built from tourism, invented documents, fringe politics, treasure stories, and later popularized by bestselling books.

The episode ends by turning toward the Cathars themselves: the hosts explain the conventional view of Catharism as a powerful medieval dualist counter-church, then introduce the more recent scholarly challenge that the Cathars, as traditionally imagined, may not have existed in that form.

## 分段落总结

[00:40] **Opening With The Da Vinci Code**

[事实] The episode opens with a reading from the first scene of *The Da Vinci Code*, set in the Louvre with curator Jacques Saunière and an attacker.

[事实] The hosts note that the novel was published in 2003 and describe it as a major publishing phenomenon twenty years earlier.

[事实] They joke about Dan Brown’s prose, including his habit of introducing characters through status, age, and physical description.

[推测] The theatrical reading sets up the episode’s tone: amused, skeptical, but interested in why the book was so compelling.

[03:23] **Why The Da Vinci Code Was So Readable**

[事实] One host says he read *The Da Vinci Code* quickly and found it highly page-turning despite criticizing its prose and information-heavy passages.

[事实] The other host says he did not find it as gripping because he already knew where the story was going.

[事实] They identify *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail* as a major source for the theory that Jesus survived or had descendants through Mary Magdalene in southern France.

[推测] The discussion separates literary effectiveness from historical reliability: the book works as a thriller even while its claims are treated with skepticism.

[05:21] **Dan Brown’s Claimed “Facts”**

[事实] The hosts quote the novel’s introductory “facts” section, which claims the Priory of Sion was a real European secret society founded in 1099.

[事实] They say Dan Brown also claimed the descriptions of artwork, architecture, documents, and rituals in the novel were accurate.

[事实] The episode states that Dan Brown promoted the book by saying the historical background was “absolutely” or “99%” true.

[推测] These claims made the novel unusually influential because readers were invited to treat its conspiracy plot as historical revelation.

[06:24] **Dan Brown, Robert Langdon, And The Merovingians**

[事实] Dan Brown attended Phillips Exeter Academy, and the hosts describe it as one of America’s elite boarding schools.

[事实] The episode notes that *The Da Vinci Code* sold about 81 million copies and was outsold only by the latest Harry Potter book at the time.

[事实] The novel’s character Sir Leigh Teabing explains the theory that Jesus and Mary Magdalene had descendants who later intermarried with the Merovingian royal line.

[事实] Tom Holland explains that the Merovingians were the Frankish dynasty associated with Clovis, who is remembered as an early Catholic king in post-Roman Gaul.

[09:21] **What The Merovingians Really Were**

[事实] Clovis is described as a Frankish warlord who conquered large parts of Gaul after Roman imperial power collapsed there.

[事实] The hosts explain that Clovis adopted Catholic Christianity at a time when many other “barbarian” Christian rulers followed Arianism.

[事实] The Merovingians later lost practical power to the Carolingians, whose most famous member was Charlemagne.

[事实] The last Merovingian king, Childeric III, was deposed, tonsured, and placed in a monastery.

[推测] The genuine history of the Merovingians provides just enough strangeness and prestige for later conspiracy writers to turn them into a hidden sacred bloodline.

[11:46] **Dagobert II And The Priory Of Sion Claim**

[事实] The hosts summarize the conspiracy theory that Dagobert II secretly had a son and that his descendants survived into the present.

[事实] In the theory, this bloodline was guarded by secret organizations including the Templars, the Cathars, and especially the Priory of Sion.

[事实] The date 1099 is linked to the Crusaders’ capture of Jerusalem.

[事实] The hosts explicitly state that they do not believe the secret bloodline story is true.

[推测] The appeal of the theory lies less in evidence than in its promise of a hidden structure behind two thousand years of history.

[13:51] **The Cathars As A Medieval Source Of Mystery**

[事实] The hosts introduce the Cathars and Templars as groups often imagined to possess secret wisdom linked to the Holy Grail.

[事实] The Cathars are described as associated with the Languedoc in southern France and as supposedly destroyed by a papal crusade and later inquisitors.

[事实] A legend says that before the fortress of Montségur fell, someone escaped with a secret treasure or the Holy Grail.

[事实] The episode connects this legend to older occult interpretations, including Josephin Peladan’s early twentieth-century claims.

[推测] The Cathars matter to the story because they give modern conspiracy myths a medieval setting that feels plausible and dramatic.

[15:36] **From The Holy Blood And The Holy Grail To Dan Brown**

[事实] *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail* was published in 1982 and helped popularize the bloodline theory later used by Dan Brown.

[事实] The authors of that book sued Dan Brown for plagiarism but did not win.

[事实] Sir Leigh Teabing’s name is said to be an anagrammatic nod to Michael Baigent, one of the book’s authors.

[事实] The hosts say the book grew out of BBC documentaries by Henry Lincoln about Rennes-le-Château.

[推测] Dan Brown’s novel did not invent the conspiracy; it converted an existing fringe historical narrative into a global thriller.

[17:03] **Rennes-le-Château And The European Conspiracy**

[事实] Rennes-le-Château is described as a small village in Cathar country in southwestern France, near the Spanish border.

[事实] *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail* claimed that the Priory of Sion protected a Merovingian line going back to Jesus and ultimately to David.

[事实] The book also connected the Priory of Sion to a future “United States of Europe” or Holy European Empire ruled by a Merovingian monarch.

[推测] The hosts treat this political element as comic but also revealing of early 1980s anxieties about European integration.

[21:00] **The Dossiers Secrets**

[事实] After a break, the hosts discuss the “dossiers secrets d’Henri Lobineau,” documents supposedly found in the Bibliothèque nationale in Paris.

[事实] These documents allegedly contained parts of the history of the Priory of Sion and lists of its leaders.

[事实] Henry Lincoln is described as the key figure behind the BBC documentaries and later *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail*.

[事实] Lincoln had previously worked as an actor and scriptwriter, including on *Doctor Who*.

[推测] The archive setting gave the story an appearance of legitimacy, even though the episode later explains the documents were planted.

[24:03] **Henry Lincoln Discovers Rennes-le-Château**

[事实] In 1969, Henry Lincoln was on holiday in the Cévennes in southwestern France.

[事实] He read a paperback version of a book originally published as *L’Or de Rennes*, or *The Gold of Rennes*.

[事实] That book told him about Bérenger Saunière, a nineteenth-century priest at Rennes-le-Château who seemed to have acquired unexplained money.

[事实] The story claimed Saunière found secret documents proving the survival of a Merovingian bloodline guarded by the Cathars, Templars, and Priory of Sion.

[推测] Lincoln’s holiday reading became a crucial bridge between a local French legend and an international conspiracy narrative.

[27:46] **Gérard de Sède And The Cathar Obsession**

[事实] *The Gold of Rennes* was written by Gérard de Sède, a French writer born in 1921 from an aristocratic family.

[事实] De Sède had been involved with surrealism and later wrote many books, several of them focused on the Cathars and Occitania.

[事实] The hosts describe him as fascinated by the idea of a secret or occult society in southern France.

[推测] De Sède helped package regional medieval history, local mystery, and esoteric speculation into a form that later writers could reuse.

[29:29] **Pierre Plantard Enters The Story**

[事实] The hosts identify Pierre Plantard as the central figure behind the modern Priory of Sion myth.

[事实] Plantard was born in 1920 and was the son of a butler and a cook.

[事实] He became involved with far-right and ultra-nationalist groups in interwar France.

[事实] In 1940, after France fell, he wrote to Marshal Pétain offering to help fight what he called Masonic and Jewish conspiracy.

[推测] Plantard’s later claims about noble lineage appear connected to his desire to escape an ordinary social background and imagine himself as aristocratic or knightly.

[31:01] **Plantard’s Secret Societies**

[事实] Plantard founded an organization called Alpha Galates and produced a magazine with a chivalric youth theme.

[事实] After the war, he became a Freemason despite previously claiming to oppose Masonic influence.

[事实] He repeatedly tried to involve himself in political crises, including writing to General de Gaulle during the Algerian crisis.

[事实] He also claimed to be a clairvoyant.

[推测] The episode presents Plantard as someone drawn to status, secrecy, titles, and invented authority.

[32:28] **The Real Origin Of The Priory Of Sion**

[事实] Plantard founded the Priory of Sion in 1956 in Annemasse in Haute-Savoie.

[事实] The name “Sion” came from a hill near the town, not from ancient Jerusalem.

[事实] The organization’s registered mission sounded religious and charitable, but its actual activities included local school bus contracts and a housing dispute.

[事实] At this stage, the Priory of Sion was not guarding any bloodline of Christ.

[推测] The gap between the mundane reality and the later myth is one of the episode’s central comic revelations.

[34:26] **Inventing The Evidence**

[事实] In the early 1960s, Plantard rebooted the Priory of Sion as an esoteric chivalric fellowship.

[事实] He promoted the idea that Nostradamus had predicted a great monarch who would take the throne of France.

[事实] Plantard presented himself as connected to that monarchic destiny.

[事实] He and an associate created forged documents, including genealogies and lists of Priory leaders, and deposited them in the Bibliothèque nationale.

[推测] Plantard understood that invented claims needed archival-looking evidence, so he manufactured the paper trail that later writers would “discover.”

[36:23] **How The Forgery Became A Bestseller Myth**

[事实] Plantard contacted Gérard de Sède and encouraged him to connect Rennes-le-Château, the Merovingian bloodline, and the Priory of Sion.

[事实] Henry Lincoln later read de Sède’s book and found the planted documents in the Bibliothèque nationale.

[事实] Lincoln and his co-authors added the Jesus and Mary Magdalene element, which Plantard himself reportedly rejected as nonsense.

[事实] The hosts say Plantard did not become the person who made the most money from the myth; later writers did.

[推测] The conspiracy theory grew beyond its inventor’s control once other storytellers added stronger religious and historical stakes.

[38:00] **Noël Corbu And The Tourist Treasure Story**

[事实] Plantard’s Rennes-le-Château idea was influenced by another book about treasure hunting.

[事实] That book told the story of Noël Corbu, who bought property at Rennes-le-Château from Marie Dénarnaud, the former housekeeper of Bérenger Saunière.

[事实] Corbu ran a hotel and restaurant and wanted to attract visitors to a remote location.

[事实] He claimed Saunière had discovered treasure in his church, including treasure linked to Blanche of Castile and Saint Louis.

[推测] The episode frames Corbu’s claims as a commercial story designed to bring customers and treasure hunters to his restaurant.

[42:00] **Parchments, Gnostic Gospels, And Local Branding**

[事实] Corbu also claimed Saunière had found hidden manuscripts or Gnostic gospels.

[事实] The hosts connect this claim to the publicity around the Dead Sea Scrolls in the 1950s.

[事实] Corbu died in a car accident in 1968, which the hosts joke could be interpreted conspiratorially.

[事实] Rennes-le-Château later became strongly associated with Cathar, Templar, crystal, and esoteric tourism.

[推测] The village’s later branding shows how a local myth can become economically useful even when its factual basis is weak.

[43:47] **What Really Happened With Saunière**

[事实] Bérenger Saunière served as priest at Rennes-le-Château from 1885 to 1909.

[事实] He spent money renovating his church and adding sculptures, but the hosts say these came from a catalogue and were not inherently esoteric.

[事实] His money did not come from treasure or blackmailing the papacy.

[事实] The hosts say he made money by taking payments to say masses, more than he could actually perform, which led to his removal.

[推测] The ordinary explanation for Saunière’s money is much less exciting than the myth, which helped the myth persist.

[45:13] **The Full Chain Of The Myth**

[事实] The hosts summarize the chain: Saunière sold masses, Corbu turned the story into a treasure legend, Plantard created forged Merovingian documents, de Sède wrote about them, Lincoln developed the story, and Dan Brown popularized it globally.

[事实] The Jesus and Mary Magdalene element entered the story through *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail*.

[事实] Dan Brown later claimed the historical background of his novel was true.

[推测] The myth succeeded because each stage added a more dramatic layer to an initially mundane story.

[46:53] **Why The Cathars Made The Myth Plausible**

[事实] The hosts argue that the Da Vinci Code-style conspiracy resonated because people already associated the Languedoc with mystery, violence, heresy, and hidden knowledge.

[事实] They describe the Cathars as central to the region’s historical and tourist imagination.

[事实] Tom Holland says the modern myth reflects real medieval anxieties about conspiracies, accusations, and religious mysteries.

[推测] The Cathars gave modern fiction a ready-made atmosphere of persecution, secrecy, and suppressed truth.

[47:40] **The Popular And Academic Cathars**

[事实] The hosts mention Kate Mosse’s 2006 novel *Labyrinth*, which also features the Cathars and gives the story a feminist spin.

[事实] In that version, the Cathars are guardians of ancient wisdom, a rival church, and more open to women’s religious authority.

[事实] The hosts say this popular image overlapped with older academic histories that described the Cathars as a highly organized medieval heretical movement.

[事实] These histories often presented Cathars as dualists who believed matter was evil, spirit was good, and the earthly world was associated with the devil.

[推测] Popular novels and academic histories reinforced one another by making the Cathars seem like a coherent and ancient counter-church.

[49:59] **The Conventional History Of Catharism**

[事实] The hosts describe the conventional scholarly view that Cathar beliefs descended from ancient Gnostic traditions.

[事实] This view links Catharism to the Byzantine world and to Bogomil missionaries from Bulgaria.

[事实] The Catholic Church is said to have become alarmed by this supposed shadow church in southern France.

[事实] The Albigensian Crusade is described as the first crusade launched against supposed Christians within Western Christendom rather than against external non-Christian enemies.

[事实] The crusade and later inquisitorial campaigns are described as bloody, brutal, and extending into the fourteenth century.

[推测] The conventional story provides a tragic framework of spiritual freedom crushed by religious intolerance.

[52:23] **The New Challenge: Did The Cathars Exist As Imagined?**

[事实] Tom Holland quotes Mark Gregory Pegg’s *A Most Holy War*, which argues that the familiar story of the Cathars is not true.

[事实] Holland says Pegg is not alone and that other historians also argue the Cathars as conventionally understood did not exist.

[事实] The episode ends by setting up later installments on who the Cathars were, what they believed, and what happened in the Albigensian Crusade.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that the crusade itself definitely happened and that thousands were killed.

[推测] The cliffhanger reframes the episode: the issue is not only whether modern conspiracy theories are false, but whether long-standing academic narratives also need revision.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is its careful backward tracing of a famous modern conspiracy theory. Instead of simply saying *The Da Vinci Code* is wrong, the hosts show how its claims passed through earlier books, documentaries, local legends, forged documents, commercial incentives, and personal fantasy.

A major strength is the balance between humor and historical explanation. The episode is funny about Dan Brown, Pierre Plantard, and the Priory of Sion, but it also gives useful context on the Merovingians, Rennes-le-Château, the Languedoc, and the historiography of the Cathars.

[推测] Its limitation is that this installment is mostly a setup episode. Listeners wanting a full explanation of medieval Cathar beliefs or the Albigensian Crusade will need the following episodes, because this one ends just as the deeper medieval argument begins.

[推测] It is especially suitable for listeners interested in how conspiracy theories are assembled, how popular history spreads, and how medieval subjects can be reshaped by novels, tourism, forged evidence, and academic debate.
