# Chile, Allende, and the Road to the 1973 Coup

## 概览

This episode frames the 11 September 1973 coup in Chile as one of the defining Cold War crises in Latin American history. It begins with Salvador Allende’s final radio address, then asks why his overthrow by General Pinochet became such a powerful symbol of American intervention, anti-communism, and the collapse of democratic hopes.

The discussion argues that US involvement was real, extensive, and documented: American administrations funded anti-Allende forces, supported opposition media, pressured Chile economically, and explored ways to prevent or reverse his presidency. At the same time, the hosts stress that Chilean politics had its own internal dynamics, and that Chileans were not simply puppets of Washington.

The episode follows Allende from his biography and earlier presidential defeats through his 1970 victory, his ambitious socialist reform program, the economic crisis that followed, and the rising tensions between his government, the opposition, the courts, Congress, business groups, and the military.

## 分段落总结

[00:31] **Allende’s Last Address and the Coup’s Historical Weight**

[事实] The episode opens with Salvador Allende’s final radio address on 11 September 1973, delivered hours before his death and before General Pinochet’s coup prevailed.
[事实] The hosts describe Allende’s overthrow as a seismic event in Latin American history and a central example often cited in debates about American imperialism during the Cold War.
[推测] The use of Allende’s speech at the beginning presents him as both a political actor and a symbolic martyr whose memory shapes how the coup is understood.

[02:26] **The Coup as a Cold War Myth and Moral Argument**

[事实] The hosts say the Chilean coup is often treated abroad as a textbook case of American ruthlessness, especially associated with Richard Nixon and Henry Kissinger.
[事实] They note that Kissinger’s reputation has remained linked to accusations about Chile and wider claims that he was a war criminal.
[推测] The episode sets up a tension between the simplified myth of US omnipotence and the more complicated historical reality the hosts want to examine.

[03:30] **September 11 and Chile’s Place in Global Memory**

[事实] The hosts point out the grim irony that 11 September is remembered in the United States for the 2001 attacks, while Chileans associate the same date with the 1973 coup.
[事实] They argue that the coup’s resonance is easy to underestimate in the English-speaking world, even though Pinochet’s regime lasted until 1990.
[事实] They also connect the coup’s legacy to later debates about torture, death tolls, economic policy, and British politics in the 1990s.

[05:25] **Chile’s Background: Geography, Wealth, and Democratic Stability**

[事实] Chile is described as a very long, narrow country that became independent from Spain in 1821.
[事实] The hosts say Chile became wealthy through mineral exports, especially copper, and was seen by the late nineteenth century as one of the richer developing countries.
[事实] From the 1930s onward, Chile is presented as an oasis of democratic stability in Latin America, with a military tradition of non-intervention in politics.
[推测] This background makes the later coup appear especially shocking because it broke with Chile’s self-image as a constitutional and democratic outlier.

[07:09] **Allende’s Formation and Rise on the Left**

[事实] Salvador Allende was born in 1908 in Santiago into an upper-middle-class, reformist, liberal family.
[事实] He studied medicine at the University of Chile, became involved in student activism, and later entered socialist politics in the 1930s.
[事实] He helped establish a branch of the Socialist Party of Chile in Valparaíso, became Minister of Health, served as a senator, and helped establish Chile’s National Health Service.
[推测] The hosts present Allende as a figure combining elite education, reformist credentials, and long-term left-wing organization.

[11:01] **Allende as Parliamentary Marxist and Repeated Candidate**

[事实] The hosts describe Allende as a Marxist, but not as a revolutionary paramilitary figure or someone seeking power outside parliamentary politics.
[事实] He made unsuccessful presidential bids in 1952, 1958, and 1964, improving from 5% in 1952 to 28.5% in 1958.
[事实] He joked that his tombstone would say, “here lies the next president of Chile,” because he kept losing elections.
[推测] His repeated candidacies made him a familiar and credible figure for the Chilean left, rather than simply a failed candidate.

[12:24] **US Intervention Before the 1964 Election**

[事实] The Kennedy administration feared another Latin American country moving toward Marxism after the Cuban Revolution and the Bay of Pigs failure.
[事实] The United States backed the Christian Democrat Eduardo Frei as a reformist center-right alternative to Allende.
[事实] The CIA spent money on direct support for Frei and on anti-Allende propaganda, including films, radio, pamphlets, leaflets, and murals.
[推测] The hosts argue that US influence mattered, but also warn against treating Chilean politics as merely a playground for American puppet masters.

[16:20] **Business Interests and the 1970 Election**

[事实] By 1970, American business interests were especially concerned about Allende, including Anaconda, Kennecott Copper, and ITT.
[事实] ITT had major holdings in Chile, including interests in the Chilean telephone company, hotels, and the right-wing newspaper El Mercurio.
[事实] These interests supported Allende’s conservative opponent Alessandri and lobbied the Nixon administration against Allende.
[推测] Economic self-interest and Cold War anti-communism reinforced each other in Washington’s hostility to Allende.

[18:47] **Allende’s Victory and Nixon’s Response**

[事实] Allende won the 1970 election by 36% to 35% over Alessandri in a split vote.
[事实] Because no candidate had an absolute majority, Congress had to choose the winner, and Chilean tradition favored the candidate with the most votes.
[事实] Declassified documents show Nixon, Kissinger, John Mitchell, and CIA director Richard Helms discussing ways to stop Allende, with Helms recording the phrase “make economy scream.”
[推测] The hosts present the US response as an attempt to prevent a democratic result from taking effect because Washington considered the stakes too high.

[21:16] **Track One, Track Two, and General Schneider**

[事实] The Americans considered “track one,” persuading or bribing Congress not to ratify Allende, and “track two,” encouraging a military coup.
[事实] Chilean army commander René Schneider opposed military interference in politics and insisted the armed forces should defend the constitution.
[事实] Attempts were made to kidnap Schneider before the congressional vote; during the third attempt in October 1970, he was shot and killed.
[事实] After Schneider’s death, Allende pledged to respect the constitution, Congress ratified him, and he became president on 3 November 1970.

[24:35] **Chile’s Shadow Over the Western Left**

[事实] After the break, the hosts discuss how Chile influenced left-wing fears in Britain and Europe about US-backed coups, intelligence interference, and hostile media campaigns.
[事实] They mention Chris Mullin’s A Very British Coup as an example of British political imagination shaped by the Chilean precedent.
[事实] The hosts say American officials would have justified such tactics as necessary in a global struggle against communism.
[推测] The episode treats Chile as both a real historical case and a template through which later left-wing movements interpreted their own vulnerability.

[27:10] **Allende’s First Reforms as President**

[事实] Allende began with a rapid program of change after taking office in November 1970.
[事实] His government pursued nationalization of major industries, especially banking and copper, and expanded spending on health and education.
[事实] The program included free milk for children, land redistribution, scholarships for Mapuche children, social security, amnesties for political prisoners, tax relief for the poorest taxpayers, literacy campaigns, a new minimum wage, and free school meals.
[推测] The hosts frame the early Allende government as a dramatic left-wing reform project that created enthusiasm among supporters while alarming opponents.

[29:09] **Culture, Dignity, and Allende’s International Appeal**

[事实] The hosts say Allende’s government also invested in cultural projects such as music festivals and poetry publishing.
[事实] They connect this cultural dimension to Allende’s later appeal among European intellectuals and exiles, including figures such as Isabel Allende and Ariel Dorfman.
[事实] Ariel Dorfman is quoted as emphasizing not only redistribution but also the dignity felt by disadvantaged citizens.
[推测] The cultural and moral language around Allende helped turn his government into a cause that resonated beyond Chile.

[30:16] **Economic Crisis and Social Division**

[事实] The hosts say Allende’s timing was poor because the world economy was entering the crisis of the early 1970s and the copper price was falling sharply.
[事实] They argue that Allende’s government also pumped money into the economy to pay for its program, contributing to hyperinflation.
[事实] Inflation was said to be 163% at the beginning of 1973 and 500% by the end of that year, while real GDP contracted by around five to six percent year on year.
[事实] The hosts say real wages were probably worth about half their 1970 value by around 1973.
[推测] The economic crisis widened the gap between enthusiastic beneficiaries of reform and middle-class opponents whose savings and security were being eroded.

[32:38] **Cuba, Castro, and Fears of Radicalization**

[事实] Allende restored relations with Cuba, accepted aid from the USSR, and invited Fidel Castro to Chile in 1971.
[事实] Castro was meant to stay for seven days but remained for 23 days, traveling around the country and praising Allende’s reforms.
[事实] Castro warned Allende that opponents might move against him and gave him a gun, identified by the hosts as an AK-47.
[推测] Castro’s visit intensified fears among Allende’s opponents that Chile might become another Cuba, even though Allende remained committed to constitutional politics.

[34:29] **US Economic Pressure and Opposition Funding**

[事实] Two days after Allende’s inauguration, Nixon convened the National Security Council to discuss ways to bring about Allende’s downfall.
[事实] The United States cut off financing and loans to Chile, pressured private investors and international banks, and disrupted supplies of US parts needed for Chilean trucks, cars, factories, and machines.
[事实] The CIA funded opposition newspapers, business groups, truck owners, and the Christian Democrat Party.
[事实] El Mercurio is described as crucial in creating a pre-coup atmosphere and openly arguing that someone had to step in against Allende.
[推测] US pressure did not create all of Chile’s problems, but it deliberately worsened the economic and political crisis.

[37:51] **Institutional Strain and Allende’s Constraints**

[事实] Allende did not have an absolute majority in Congress, and the Chilean Supreme Court was dominated by more conservative figures.
[事实] The hosts describe growing tension between Allende on one side and Congress and the Supreme Court on the other by 1973.
[事实] They also say Allende did not appear to be violent, repressive, or eager to use force against opponents.
[推测] Chile’s democratic institutions were still functioning, but the intensity of Allende’s program and the opposition’s resistance placed them under unprecedented pressure.

[39:18] **The 1973 Congressional Election and the Road to Coup**

[事实] Opposition forces hoped the March 1973 congressional election would give them a two-thirds majority to block or impeach Allende.
[事实] Although opposition parties won a majority of votes overall, Allende’s side gained seats in both the Senate and Congress.
[事实] The hosts say this frustrated the CIA, the US embassy, the opposition press, truckers, and middle-class opponents because the parliamentary route had failed.
[推测] The hosts present this election result as a key turning point after which Allende’s enemies increasingly saw a full military coup as the remaining option.

[40:52] **Chile’s Democratic Identity Before the Break**

[事实] The hosts close by emphasizing how deeply rooted Chilean democratic identity appeared to be in early 1973.
[事实] Chileans are described as seeing themselves as unlike Brazil or Argentina: democratic, civilized, and constitutionally stable.
[事实] The episode ends by setting up the next installment, which will cover the coup that toppled Allende and brought Pinochet to power.
[推测] The ending underscores the tragedy that Chile’s strong democratic self-image did not prevent the coming collapse.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it avoids a one-dimensional account. It accepts the documented reality of US subversion while also emphasizing Chilean agency, Allende’s own policies, global economic pressures, and institutional conflict inside Chile.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the clear chronological build-up from Allende’s biography to the 1970 election, the failed attempts to block him, his reform program, and the crisis of 1973. The discussion makes the coup feel less like a sudden event and more like the result of escalating pressures.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode stops before the coup itself, so Pinochet, repression, and the long-term consequences are mostly deferred to the next episode. It is best suited for listeners who want the political and diplomatic background to 1973 rather than a full account of the dictatorship.
