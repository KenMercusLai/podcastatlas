# Who Are We Now? England, Englishness, and Modern Identity

## 概览

This episode discusses England as a nation with a complicated identity: politically old, culturally diverse, often submerged within Britishness, and newly visible through debates about devolution, immigration, Brexit, football, and the future of the United Kingdom.

The conversation begins with George Orwell’s wartime writing on England, then uses Jason Cowley’s book *Who Are We Now? Stories of Modern England* to explore whether Englishness is inherently nostalgic, whether it can be progressive, and why it so often appears through paradox.

A central conclusion is that English identity is contested but not static. The speakers argue that Englishness has been associated with loss, empire, countryside, class, football, and democratic turbulence, yet it is also being reshaped by diversity, black English identity, and a renewed search for national meaning.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Orwell’s England and the Question of Continuity**

[事实] The episode opens with a passage from George Orwell describing England through everyday scenes, manners, crowds, landscapes, and fragments of national life.

[事实] The hosts ask whether Orwell’s famous evocation of England is now outdated, while also noting that England has become more diverse since Orwell wrote.

[推测] The opening frames the episode around whether Englishness is a real continuity through time or a pattern imposed on a changing and muddled society.

[02:20] **England as a Nation Without a Nation-State**

[事实] England is described as a famous example of a nation that is not a nation-state and does not have its own seat at the United Nations.

[事实] The speakers note that many people in England describe themselves as British rather than English, while England and Britain are often confused abroad and sometimes domestically.

[事实] They also say the English sense of themselves within the United Kingdom has grown over the past decade.

[推测] The lack of separate English institutions makes English identity more ambiguous than Scottish, Welsh, or British identity.

[03:06] **Jason Cowley, Cricket, and English Embarrassment**

[事实] Tom Holland recalls playing cricket with Jason Cowley on the day Princess Diana died, with the match continuing in an embarrassed atmosphere after a brief gesture around a half-mast flag.

[事实] Jason Cowley is introduced as editor of *The New Statesman* and author of *Who Are We Now? Stories of Modern England*.

[推测] The anecdote uses cricket, embarrassment, class, and ritual as a miniature example of the Englishness the episode goes on to examine.

[06:24] **Orwell, War, and the Awakening of England**

[事实] Cowley says Orwell’s *The Lion and the Unicorn* was begun in spring 1940 and completed during the Blitz.

[事实] He explains that Orwell used England and Britain almost interchangeably, and that the danger of invasion after Dunkirk gave Orwell a clarifying sense of national unity.

[事实] Orwell is described as ambivalent: an old Etonian who hated public schools, a former policeman in Burma who loathed empire, and a critic of “book trained socialism.”

[推测] The speakers treat war as a force that can make national identity more coherent, comparing Orwell’s England with Ukraine’s wartime national identity.

[09:12] **Devolution and the Return of Englishness**

[事实] Cowley argues that English national identity has intensified since Tony Blair’s 1997 election, devolution, and the creation of the Scottish Parliament.

[事实] Scottish nationalism, the 2014 independence referendum, Brexit, Corbynism, Trump, Nigel Farage, and UKIP are all cited as part of a period of upheaval.

[事实] Cowley says English identity is often unfairly associated with reaction, loss, and nostalgia, but he argues for a progressive and positive modern English identity within Britishness.

[推测] Englishness appears in the discussion partly as a response to the perceived fragility of the United Kingdom.

[11:12] **Englishness, Nostalgia, and Loss**

[事实] The speakers discuss Cowley’s idea that Englishness and loss are often treated as inseparable.

[事实] England is described as an extremely old political construction, with Denmark mentioned as a possible rival among early nation-states.

[事实] Examples of English nostalgia include Shakespeare’s John of Gaunt, Civil War invocations of Anglo-Saxon liberty, Housman, and memories of what was lost after the Norman Conquest.

[推测] The episode suggests that anxiety about decline may itself be one of the most enduring features of English identity.

[14:12] **Englishness Lost Within Britishness**

[事实] Cowley says Englishness was lost within Britishness after the 1707 Act of Union, as the English were encouraged to see themselves primarily as British within the imperial project.

[事实] England is said not to have its own discrete political institutions, unlike Scotland after devolution.

[事实] George Robertson’s claim that devolution would kill Scottish nationalism “stone dead” is mentioned as having proved wrong.

[推测] The lack of a modern institutional arena for England is presented as one reason English identity has become unsettled.

[15:05] **Empire, Violence, and the Left’s Suspicion of Englishness**

[事实] The hosts challenge the idea of the English as gentle by pointing to empire, slavery, colonialism, Ireland, India, the Caribbean, and Orwell’s own experience in Burma.

[事实] Cowley says Englishness is contested and is often viewed by the left with suspicion, reaction, and even racism.

[事实] He contrasts idealized images such as *Antiques Roadshow* with violence in Newcastle nightlife and on football terraces in the late 1970s and early 1980s.

[推测] The episode presents Englishness as morally complicated by the gap between self-image and imperial or social violence.

[17:49] **Anti-Patriotism and British Inclusiveness**

[事实] The speakers discuss a strain of English anti-patriotism, connecting it to Orwell, Tom Paine, Whiggish dissent, the French Revolution, and reactions against Tory England.

[事实] Cowley says Britishness has been a non-racial, inclusive, plural identity and argues that postwar Britain has welcomed migrants better than any other European country, while acknowledging racism and hostility.

[事实] He says Britain has not seen the emergence of a neo-fascist right comparable to France.

[推测] Britishness is treated as easier than Englishness to imagine as civic and inclusive.

[20:34] **Immigration, 2004, and Nigel Farage**

[事实] The speakers discuss rising immigration after 1997 and ask why it amplified English or Scottish identity rather than Britishness.

[事实] Cowley links 2004 to both the Morecambe Bay cockle-picking disaster involving undocumented Chinese migrants and EU enlargement.

[事实] Cowley says Nigel Farage told him immigration and the EU became an issue after 2004, and that Farage framed the issue as numbers and loss of control rather than ethnicity or race.

[事实] The UK, Sweden, and Ireland did not impose seven-year transition controls on migrants from new eastern EU member states, while other major economies did.

[推测] The discussion implies that migration, devolution, and political control became entangled in the rise of English identity.

[23:26] **Blair’s “Young Country” and Deep England**

[事实] Cowley says Tony Blair spoke of wanting Britain to be a “young country” and did not speak about England in that formulation.

[事实] The speakers argue that Blair did not fully understand the cultural and political power of nationalism.

[事实] Cowley says many people in “deep England,” including shires, smaller county towns, neglected coastal towns, and Harlow New Town, felt ignored, mocked, and anxious about globalization.

[推测] Blair’s modernizing Britishness is presented as disconnected from older local and national attachments.

[25:40] **London, Youth Culture, and the Paradox of Britishness**

[事实] Tom Holland argues that since the Second World War England has successfully reinvented itself as young through youth culture, including Swinging London, punk, and raves.

[事实] The speakers describe London as both the English and British capital, but also as a global city that feels more British than English.

[事实] Cowley says many people attracted to Farage and UKIP did not feel represented by modern cosmopolitan London Britishness.

[推测] London functions in the episode as both a symbol of successful plural Britishness and a source of alienation for people outside it.

[28:17] **London’s Depth and the Book’s Central Question**

[事实] Cowley recalls Alex Salmond describing London as a dark star drawing energy from the rest of the United Kingdom.

[事实] Cowley replies that London also contains some of the country’s poorest boroughs and deep intergenerational inequality.

[事实] Cowley quotes Orwell’s idea that it is important to determine what England is before guessing what role England can play in large events.

[推测] Cowley’s book appears to use paradox rather than certainty as its method for understanding modern England.

[32:12] **Landscape, Population, and Environmental Anxiety**

[事实] After the break, the episode turns to Philip Larkin’s fear of England being replaced by “concrete and tires.”

[事实] The speakers say the English are famous animal lovers and countryside lovers, while England is environmentally depleted and its biodiversity is in crisis.

[事实] Cowley notes England’s small landmass and links Scottish independence to potential loss of territory, waters, and landmass within Britain.

[推测] Anxiety about population, housing, and landscape is presented as another modern form of the older English fear of loss.

[34:28] **Countryside, Suburbia, and the Rural Ideal**

[事实] The conversation links English identity to landscape, the countryside, “green and pleasant land,” Larkin, Housman, Wordsworth, and the Romantic tradition.

[事实] Cowley says rural towns face pressure from new housing while young people also need places to live.

[事实] Stanley Baldwin and John Major are discussed as politicians who invoked rural English imagery even as England became suburban and urbanized.

[推测] The countryside in this discussion is as much an imagined national inheritance as a physical place.

[37:00] **Football as a Vehicle of Englishness**

[事实] The speakers describe football as one of England’s most famous exports and possibly the single most important vehicle of Englishness.

[事实] Cowley says the England football team is a mirror of the changing nation.

[事实] He recalls Euro 96, when he was struck by the visibility of the St George’s flag, contrasting it with 1966 World Cup footage dominated by the Union flag.

[推测] Football is presented as a bottom-up expression of Englishness rather than a state-directed project.

[39:05] **Race, Southgate, and Progressive Englishness**

[事实] Cowley says the England team was long associated with hooliganism, far-right factions, racism, and overseas violence.

[事实] He contrasts that with Euro 2020, delayed by the pandemic, where many leading players were black English players comfortable with the St George’s flag.

[事实] Marcus Rashford is described as identifying with benevolent Englishness, and Gareth Southgate is praised for thinking deeply about English identity.

[事实] Southgate’s “Dear England” essay is described as combining love of the Queen, monarchy, military, and Second World War family memory with progressive causes.

[推测] Southgate’s England is treated as one of the clearest examples of tradition and diversity coexisting in modern Englishness.

[41:45] **Club Football, Globalization, and Local Continuity**

[事实] Tom Holland questions whether club football matters more to most fans than the national team.

[事实] The speakers describe elite clubs as tied to oligarchs, autocrats, kleptocrats, and global capitalism, while still serving as vessels of local tradition.

[事实] The European Super League controversy is described as a moment when something profoundly English seemed hollowed out and threatened by a plutocratic elite.

[事实] Cowley quotes Eric Hobsbawm’s idea that an imagined community of millions can feel more real as a team of eleven named people.

[推测] Football embodies both the continuity people seek and the globalization many feel has taken local control away.

[45:03] **Black English Identity**

[事实] Cowley says the England team matters because it expresses the changing English nation and helps people grapple with contested identity.

[事实] He says some black friends in the 1980s and 1990s were uncomfortable combining blackness and Englishness and described themselves as black British.

[事实] Cowley says a black English identity has developed in recent times and that he welcomes it.

[推测] This is one of the episode’s strongest examples of Englishness becoming broader rather than simply nostalgic.

[45:58] **Labour, England, and Political Realignment**

[事实] The speakers turn to the left’s difficulty with Englishness and Cowley’s idea of the death of a kind of Labour England.

[事实] Cowley says Labour was once hegemonic in Scotland, but in the 2015 general election it went from 41 Scottish Westminster seats to one while the SNP won 56.

[事实] He argues Labour must win in England to regain a UK majority, but has been rejected in traditional heartlands in the North, Midlands, and Essex.

[事实] Cowley says Labour is strong in cities and among educated urban middle-class voters, but weak elsewhere.

[推测] The discussion suggests Labour’s problem is not only electoral but cultural: it has lost contact with older forms of working-class and place-based identity.

[49:01] **Democratic Turbulence and Brexit**

[事实] The speakers describe the past decade as convulsive, turbulent, exhausting, and upsetting, but also as an affirmation of democracy.

[事实] They cite the Scottish independence referendum and Brexit referendum as two existential votes whose results were accepted.

[事实] Cowley says Brexit shocked the “cognitive elite,” swept David Cameron from power, and ultimately brought Boris Johnson to Downing Street.

[事实] The speakers note that some remainers wanted a second referendum, while Cowley describes Cameron’s binary plebiscite on EU membership as a major risk.

[推测] The episode treats Brexit as both disruptive and democratic: a “tug from below” in which ordinary voters forced elites to listen.

[52:26] **Eighteenth-Century Echoes and the Future of the Union**

[事实] The speakers compare recent politics to the eighteenth century: boisterous, vulgar, argumentative, and full of clashes over Britain’s exceptionalism and Europe.

[事实] They connect modern debates to older divisions between town and country, dissenting Whiggish middle classes, Toryism, Dr Johnson, and John Bull.

[事实] Cowley says the period remains turbulent and asks whether the Scottish national question will return to break the British state or be delayed.

[推测] The comparison with the eighteenth century reinforces the episode’s argument that modern English crises have deep historical echoes.

[54:16] **Changing Changelessness**

[事实] Cowley returns to Orwell’s comparison between England in 1840 and 1940, saying they differ as much as an adult differs from a childhood photograph, yet remain the same person.

[事实] Cowley says his book explores “changing changelessness,” meaning continuities that connect the English present to the deeper English past.

[事实] The episode ends by presenting England as a country full of paradox.

[推测] “Changing changelessness” serves as the episode’s final formulation of English identity: altered by history, but still recognizably continuous.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in making Englishness feel historically layered rather than reducible to nationalism, nostalgia, Brexit, empire, or football alone. It works best when the speakers hold contradictions together instead of trying to resolve them too quickly.

[事实] The discussion covers Orwell, devolution, Scottish nationalism, empire, immigration, Farage, Blair, London, landscape, football, Southgate, Labour, Brexit, and the future of the Union.

[推测] Its limitation is that it moves quickly across large subjects, so some claims about immigration, British integration, Labour’s future, and national identity remain suggestive rather than fully evidenced within the transcript.

[推测] This episode is especially suited to listeners interested in British political history, national identity, Orwell, Brexit-era politics, and the cultural meaning of football in modern England.
