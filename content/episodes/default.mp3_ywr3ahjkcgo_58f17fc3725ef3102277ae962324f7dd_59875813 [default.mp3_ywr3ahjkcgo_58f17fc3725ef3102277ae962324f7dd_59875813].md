# The Fertility Gap: Why People Are Having Fewer Children Than They Want

## 概览

Global fertility has fallen by more than half since 1970, while the U.S. fertility rate has declined steadily since 2007. The episode argues that this is not merely a matter of delayed childbirth: many adults are likely to end their reproductive years with fewer children than they wanted.

The discussion distinguishes low fertility from the “fertility gap.” The latter reflects unfulfilled personal goals and may arise from delayed marriage, intensive parenting norms, reduced face-to-face socializing, inflexible workplaces, and limited access to suitable housing.

Traditional pro-natalist measures such as baby bonuses, tax credits, paid leave, and childcare subsidies receive mixed assessments. By contrast, remote work and easier access to homeownership emerge as potentially significant—though still contested—ways to make family formation more achievable.

## 分段落总结

[01:23] **From Overpopulation Fears to Falling Fertility**

[事实] Global fertility has fallen by more than half since 1970, reversing earlier fears that rapid population growth would produce widespread famine.

[事实] The episode focuses on the fertility gap: the difference between the number of children people say they want and the number they ultimately have.

[事实] The discussion connects fertility with workplace flexibility, housing access, relationships, infertility, and social expectations.

[04:46] **Why the U.S. Decline Is More Than Delayed Childbearing**

[事实] Economist Melissa Kearney explains that U.S. fertility fell sharply in the early twentieth century, rose during the baby boom, declined in the 1960s and 1970s, and remained relatively stable until another sustained decline began in 2007.

[事实] Looking across birth cohorts, researchers see fewer women beginning childbearing at each observable age, rather than a simple short-term response to the Great Recession.

[事实] About half of 30-year-old U.S. women are currently childless, compared with eventual childlessness rates of roughly 10–15% among recent older cohorts.

[推测] Biological limits make it unlikely that later births will fully compensate for the decline among women in their twenties and early thirties.

[08:34] **Pronatalism, Choice, and the Fertility Gap**

[事实] Demographer Lyman Stone describes his goal as helping people achieve the family size they personally want, not persuading unwilling people to have children or forcing fertility to reach replacement level.

[事实] Surveys cited by Stone place the average desired family size among American women of reproductive age between two and 2.7 children, a figure he says has remained broadly stable.

[事实] Desired family size differs internationally: Stone cites approximately 1.8 children in Malta and a disputed range of one to 1.7 in China.

[推测] Stone interprets very low desired fertility as a possible sign of pessimism about a society’s future, although high childrearing costs could also influence responses.

[15:00] **A Long-Term Problem, Not a Sudden Crisis**

[事实] Stone rejects the word “crisis” because fertility decline develops gradually rather than arriving as a sudden turning point.

[事实] Low fertility can strain pension and healthcare systems because fewer working-age people support a larger retired population; South Korea’s fertility rate of roughly 0.7 illustrates the demographic arithmetic.

[事实] Stone places greater emphasis on the emotional cost of failing to have wanted children than on government budget calculations.

[事实] Longitudinal surveys and IVF outcomes cited in the interview associate unfulfilled fertility goals with poorer mental health and greater sadness later in life.

[20:18] **Why Standard Economic Explanations Fall Short**

[事实] Kearney says changes in household income, childcare prices, women’s wages, employment opportunities, college costs, and student debt do not adequately explain why U.S. fertility began falling after 2007.

[事实] She argues that young adults’ priorities have shifted amid changing norms around work, leisure, gender roles, marriage, and individual autonomy.

[事实] Survey respondents still tend to describe an ideal family of about two children, even as their behavior increasingly involves delaying or foregoing marriage and parenthood.

[推测] The gap between stated preferences and actual behavior suggests that family formation has lost priority relative to other attainable lifestyles, rather than that the desire for children has disappeared.

[23:40] **Religion, Pessimism, and Intensive Parenting**

[事实] Kearney says religion may influence fertility, but its independent causal effect is difficult to isolate because religious participation, marriage, and family formation often change together.

[事实] Some young adults say they hesitate to bring children into a world threatened by climate change, instability, or hardship, despite improvements in income, longevity, and employment opportunities.

[事实] Kearney identifies intensive parenting as a likely contributor to lower fertility because contemporary parents are expected to manage children’s activities and safety continuously for many years.

[事实] She suggests that flexible jobs and less demanding parenting norms may matter more than short-term benefits such as expanded parental leave.

[推测] Modern parenting may appear prohibitively expensive and exhausting partly because socially acceptable childrearing has become a premium, highly managed lifestyle.

[30:04] **Marriage Decline, Smartphones, and Social Isolation**

[事实] Stone argues that declining exposure to marriage is the largest recent factor behind falling U.S. fertility; after accounting for years spent married, he says the fertility decline largely disappears.

[事实] He attributes delayed marriage partly to an economy in which young men reach stable earnings later than before.

[事实] Stone also links smartphones to reduced in-person socializing, noting that weekly time teenagers spend with friends outside home, work, and school reportedly fell from about ten hours to less than two.

[事实] A working paper titled “Is the iPhone Birth Control?” attributes a significant fertility decline—especially among women in their twenties—to the staggered rollout of the iPhone.

[推测] Dating apps may not fully replace physical social settings because chemistry, compatibility, and unexpected matches are difficult to evaluate through profiles.

[34:29] **Can Governments Pay People to Have Children?**

[事实] Stone says his meta-analysis finds that child tax credits, allowances, and baby bonuses reliably produce additional births, although paying enough to restore replacement-level fertility could cost about 4% of GDP.

[事实] He supports a proposed Finnish model in which the state invests money at a person’s birth and pays the matured account when that person later has a child; he acknowledges being one of the proposal’s authors.

[事实] Kearney counters that incremental policies—including bonuses, tax incentives, parental leave, and childcare subsidies—have shown null-to-small effects on sustained fertility.

[事实] Stanford economist Nick Bloom likewise describes direct financial incentives as expensive and largely ineffective, while acknowledging that such support can still improve families’ lives.

[推测] The disagreement reflects different interpretations of effect size, cost-effectiveness, and whether modest increases should count as policy success.

[41:13] **Remote Work as a Fertility Policy**

[事实] Bloom says the share of U.S. workdays performed from home rose from about 5% before the pandemic to approximately 25%, with preferences varying by age and family situation.

[事实] His international research finds that opposite-sex couples in which both partners work from home at least one day per week have about one-third of a child more globally and nearly half a child more in the U.S.

[事实] The association appears stronger for additional children than for first births, potentially because remote work makes childcare and family logistics easier.

[事实] Bloom estimates that post-pandemic remote work accounts for roughly 300,000 additional U.S. births annually, or about 8% of all births.

[事实] Another working paper found a slight fertility decrease among married, college-educated women working from home, and the episode notes that this research literature remains unsettled.

[推测] Selection may explain part of the association: remote work could facilitate having children, but people who already want children may also deliberately choose remote-compatible jobs.

[49:57] **What the Baby Boom Reveals About Housing**

[事实] Kearney and Lisa Dettling studied government-backed mortgages created through the National Housing Act of 1934 and the GI Bill of 1944 to understand housing’s contribution to the baby boom.

[事实] Before these programs, typical mortgages required down payments of 50–60%, leaving fewer than 20% of adults aged 18–44 as homeowners.

[事实] Government guarantees enabled mortgages with down payments of 20% or less, fixed interest rates, longer terms, and full amortization, allowing young families to buy homes without first accumulating substantial wealth.

[事实] Their study estimates that every 1,000 government-backed mortgages issued in a state were followed by 305 additional births the next year.

[事实] Because discriminatory lending and redlining largely restricted these mortgages to white households and areas, the observed fertility effect was also overwhelmingly concentrated among white women.

[53:35] **Homeownership and Earlier Family Formation**

[事实] Kearney says the baby boom was achieved partly through earlier marriage and earlier initiation of childbearing, alongside younger access to homeownership.

[事实] Today’s pattern runs in the opposite direction: young adults buy homes, marry, and begin having children later.

[事实] The U.S. homeownership rate among people under 45 fell after the Great Recession from about 40% to about 30%.

[推测] Housing access may influence fertility less through lower monthly costs than by allowing young adults to establish stable households sooner.

[57:03] **Closing the Fertility Gap Through Housing and Flexibility**

[事实] The episode concludes that falling fertility has multiple possible drivers and no single proven remedy.

[事实] Stone views family-friendly housing as especially promising—not necessarily because it is the largest cause of declining fertility, but because evidence suggests policy can make it more affordable and abundant.

[事实] The series continues in a second episode examining why housing reform is difficult and how regulatory barriers restrict supply.

[推测] Remote-work access and housing reform share a common mechanism: both reduce the practical conflict between employment, family formation, and caring for children.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest contribution is its distinction between population-level fertility targets and the personal fertility gap. This reframes the issue around whether people can realize their own family plans, avoiding the assumption that everyone should have children.

Its multidisciplinary approach is another strength. Economists and a pronatalist demographer disagree openly about financial incentives, while the program acknowledges conflicting findings on remote work. The historical mortgage analysis also shows how policies can raise fertility while distributing benefits unequally.

The principal limitation is that several proposed causes—smartphones, shifting priorities, intensive parenting, and pessimism—remain difficult to separate empirically. Remote work’s apparent effect may also partly reflect self-selection, and the episode explicitly notes that the literature is still developing.

[推测] This episode is especially useful for listeners interested in economics, demography, family policy, labor practices, and housing. It offers a strong map of the debate, but not a definitive formula for reversing fertility decline.
