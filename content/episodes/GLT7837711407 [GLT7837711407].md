# The Crimean War: The Road to Sevastopol

## Overview

This episode traces the British and French entry into the Crimean War, from their determination to protect the Ottoman Empire against Russia to the decision to invade Crimea. It explains why Constantinople mattered strategically and how Russia’s advance through the Danubian principalities brought the powers to war in 1854.

Much of the discussion focuses on the commanders chosen for the campaign. Lord Raglan was brave, experienced and diplomatic but had never commanded an army in the field, while class prejudice kept the highly capable Sir Colin Campbell from the top post. The cavalry command was divided among Lord Lucan, Lord Cardigan and Sir James Scarlett, despite widespread concern about Lucan and Cardigan’s bitter personal feud.

The episode then follows the Allied deployment from Constantinople to Varna. Disease, poor logistics, command disputes and Cardigan’s damaging reconnaissance weakened the force before it had fought a major battle. Although the Russians withdrew and the original objective had effectively been achieved, political pressure and public demand for victory encouraged Britain and France to adopt a new target: Sevastopol and the Russian Black Sea Fleet.

## 分段落总结

[02:33] **The Charge of the Light Brigade in Context**

[事实] The episode opens with Tennyson’s “The Charge of the Light Brigade” and identifies the charge of 25 October 1854 as the disastrous climax toward which the series is building.

[事实] Britain and France declared war on Russia on 28 March 1854 to prevent the Ottoman Empire’s dismemberment and the Russian capture of Constantinople.

[事实] The British and French preferred a weakened Ottoman government at Constantinople to the far more powerful Russia of Tsar Nicholas I.

[04:37] **Britain Prepares for War**

[事实] Britain had dispatched a Royal Navy flotilla under Vice Admiral James Dundas to the Bosphorus as early as September 1853.

[事实] Dundas commanded 16 warships and 645 guns, and by January his squadron was openly operating in the Black Sea despite Russian demands that it withdraw.

[事实] Viscount Hardinge, who succeeded Wellington as commander-in-chief of the army, had extensive experience in the Napoleonic Wars and India.

[事实] Hardinge revived large-scale field manoeuvres and promoted adoption of the longer-ranged, more accurate Minié rifle before the French Army did so.

[09:47] **Lord Raglan Takes Command**

[事实] Hardinge selected Lord Raglan to command British forces east of Malta, and Raglan was formally appointed on 21 February.

[事实] Raglan had fought alongside Wellington, lost an arm at Waterloo and displayed exceptional courage and stoicism.

[事实] His career had largely been spent as Wellington’s deputy, giving him considerable diplomatic skill but no experience as an independent field commander.

[事实] Raglan’s charm, fluent French and ability to manage allies were important qualifications because Britain needed to coordinate with both the Ottomans and the French.

[13:50] **Class Prejudice Shapes the High Command**

[事实] Sir Colin Campbell possessed extensive combat and command experience, particularly from India and the conquest of the Punjab.

[事实] Campbell was not considered for supreme command because he was the son of a Glasgow carpenter, lacked wealth and patrons, and remained only a colonel.

[事实] He was instead given command of the three Highland units forming the Highland Brigade.

[推测] The contrast between Campbell and Raglan suggests that social rank remained a decisive constraint on British military appointments, even when more experienced candidates were available.

[18:44] **Raglan and Saint-Arnaud**

[事实] Raglan met Napoleon III and his French counterpart, Marshal Saint-Arnaud, in Paris in April.

[事实] Saint-Arnaud had risen from an unconventional and disreputable early career to gain military experience in Algeria and political influence by supporting Napoleon III’s coup.

[事实] British officers regarded Saint-Arnaud as slippery and potentially untrustworthy, while he sought supreme command over both armies.

[事实] Raglan avoided conceding supreme command, while Britain agreed to provide most of the fleet and the cavalry and France most of the land forces.

[22:16] **A Troubled Cavalry Command**

[事实] The British cavalry was divided into a five-regiment Heavy Brigade and a five-regiment Light Brigade.

[事实] Sir James Scarlett, who commanded the Heavy Brigade, had no combat experience but compensated by surrounding himself with officers who had served in India.

[事实] Lord Lucan commanded the cavalry overall, while his brother-in-law and bitter enemy Lord Cardigan commanded the Light Brigade.

[事实] Officers across the army feared the pairing; one described Lucan and Cardigan as the two biggest fools who could have been selected.

[26:05] **The Case for Cardigan**

[事实] Cardigan had a history of quarrelling with officers, pursuing courts-martial and fighting duels, but Hardinge valued his exacting standards of discipline and appearance.

[事实] Cardigan’s 11th Hussars were exceptionally well dressed, equipped and trained.

[事实] Although Cardigan was disliked by many officers, ordinary troopers appear to have admired his spending on the regiment, his concern for their welfare and his personal courage.

[事实] Cardigan departed London on 8 May, confident that the campaign would bring him lasting glory.

[34:17] **The Cavalry’s Difficult Journey East**

[事实] The Light Brigade embarked in a mood of intense confidence, but storms in the Bay of Biscay and extreme Mediterranean heat caused severe distress among its horses.

[事实] Some horses went mad from heatstroke and had to be shot; most survivors arrived weak and out of condition after a voyage lasting more than a month.

[事实] Cardigan travelled separately through Paris, Marseille and Athens, reaching the Bosphorus in just over two weeks.

[事实] Cardigan’s prized horse Ronald received special treatment during the journey.

[37:15] **Lucan and Cardigan Clash at Scutari**

[事实] The British established themselves at Scutari, a dilapidated barracks area plagued by sewage, fleas and rats.

[事实] Lucan rose at four each morning and issued detailed regulations covering dress, whiskers and even the conduct of washerwomen.

[事实] Cardigan disliked both the conditions and Lucan’s micromanagement and wanted to move closer to possible action against Russia.

[事实] With Raglan’s direct permission, Cardigan departed for Varna without applying through Lucan, violating the normal chain of command and deepening their feud.

[39:17] **Russia Advances on the Danube**

[事实] Russia had occupied Moldavia and Wallachia in 1853 as a potential launch point for an advance on Constantinople.

[事实] In spring 1854 the Russian army advanced across disease-ridden terrain toward Silistria, an Ottoman fortress on the Danube.

[事实] Hunger, mosquitoes and dysentery depleted the Russian force even before the siege began on 11 May.

[事实] Despite Russian confidence, Silistria’s Turkish and Egyptian defenders mounted a ferocious resistance under Musa Pasha.

[事实] Lieutenant Leo Tolstoy served with the Russian forces, and his wartime experiences later informed his writing.

[44:38] **The Allies Move North to Varna**

[事实] Ottoman commander Omar Pasha urged Raglan and Saint-Arnaud to move their armies north from Constantinople and Gallipoli.

[事实] Raglan responded more readily than Saint-Arnaud, and Varna became the next Allied base because it lay roughly one hundred miles south of Silistria.

[事实] Cardigan moved the Light Brigade away from Varna to Devna, a healthier and more attractive valley.

[事实] When Lucan arrived in Varna on 15 June, he found much of the cavalry absent and complained to Raglan about Cardigan’s conduct.

[事实] Lucan’s insistence on obsolete drill commands caused confusion because the cavalry had already adopted newer procedures.

[49:32] **Disease and Cardigan’s Reconnaissance**

[事实] The influx of Allied troops overwhelmed Varna’s sanitation, and dysentery and cholera killed thousands of British and French soldiers.

[事实] The Russians abandoned the siege of Silistria on 25 June and withdrew across the Danube, effectively removing the immediate threat to Constantinople.

[事实] Raglan ordered Cardigan to determine whether Russian forces remained south of the Danube, and Cardigan departed with 121 troopers.

[事实] Cardigan exceeded his instructions, travelled without adequate maps or local knowledge and led the detachment through areas lacking food and water.

[事实] The 17-day expedition put about one hundred horses out of action, yet Raglan promoted Cardigan to major general on 20 July.

[54:27] **Logistical Failure Under Public Scrutiny**

[事实] A fire in early August destroyed an Allied supply depot, including 16,000 boots and large quantities of rations.

[事实] Newspaper correspondents exposed Allied blunders to the British public with an immediacy that had not existed during earlier wars.

[事实] The Times correspondent William Howard Russell was identified as a particularly important source, although the hosts defer detailed discussion of him to the next episode.

[推测] Modern press coverage magnified the political consequences of military mistakes by allowing the public to follow failures in near real time.

[56:29] **Victory Creates a Political Dilemma**

[事实] Once Russia withdrew from the Balkans, the original war aim had been achieved and Constantinople was safe.

[事实] British and French leaders nevertheless faced public pressure for action, glorious victories and a visible return on the expedition.

[事实] Neither government wanted to accept the loss of prestige that might accompany ending the campaign without a major battle.

[推测] Public expectations and national pride helped transform a limited defensive success into pressure for a much more ambitious offensive war.

[57:27] **The Decision to Attack Sevastopol**

[事实] British leaders identified Sevastopol, headquarters of Russia’s Black Sea Fleet, as the target whose destruction could protect Constantinople over the longer term.

[事实] French commanders were initially less enthusiastic, but Napoleon III wanted military glory and wished to preserve the alliance with Britain.

[事实] Conditions at Varna became so deadly that Saint-Arnaud concluded an invasion of Crimea might cost fewer lives than remaining in camp.

[事实] Embarkation orders were issued on 24 August, and approximately 400 Allied vessels sailed from Varna on 7 September, led by the screw-propelled steamship HMS Agamemnon.

[事实] Morale remained high despite disease and logistical failures, but the decision to invade Crimea was principally political and rested on limited understanding of conditions there.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s chief strength is its integration of strategy, personalities and institutions. It shows that the road to the Charge of the Light Brigade was shaped not simply by battlefield decisions, but by social hierarchy, fragile coalition management, personal feuds and political pressure at home.

The portraits of Raglan, Campbell, Lucan, Cardigan and Saint-Arnaud are nuanced rather than purely condemnatory. Cardigan, for example, is presented as arrogant and often reckless but also disciplined, brave and capable of inspiring ordinary troopers; Raglan is shown as an effective diplomat whose suitability for independent command remained uncertain.

The humorous delivery makes a complicated sequence of appointments and deployments accessible, although the frequent jokes and biographical digressions sometimes interrupt the strategic narrative. The episode also ends before testing many of its judgments against battle, leaving the consequences of the commanders’ weaknesses for subsequent instalments.

This episode is especially valuable for listeners interested in the Crimean War, Victorian military culture, coalition warfare and the role of the press in modern conflict. It provides a clear account of how an apparently successful defence of Constantinople evolved into the much riskier invasion of Crimea.
