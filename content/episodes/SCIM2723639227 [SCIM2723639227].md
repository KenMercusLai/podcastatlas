# Deliberate Cold and Heat Exposure with Dr. Susanna Soberg

## 概览

This episode centers on how deliberate cold exposure and deliberate heat exposure affect metabolism, brown fat, cardiovascular markers, neurotransmitters, and subjective resilience. Andrew Huberman speaks with Dr. Susanna Soberg about cold shock, sauna use, brown fat thermogenesis, and practical protocols that can be used outside the lab.

A key conclusion is that benefits appear to come from controlled, repeated temperature stress rather than extreme endurance. Soberg emphasizes short cold exposures that feel uncomfortably cold, sauna bouts that stay within a hormetic range, and ending on cold when the goal is to force the body to reheat itself.

The discussion moves from basic physiology to Soberg’s human study of winter swimmers, then into practical questions: cold showers versus immersion, head dunking, children, women, Raynaud’s syndrome, sleep, fasting, and whether one needs ice-cold water. The recurring theme is that temperature variation trains the body’s thermoregulatory and metabolic systems.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Guest and Core Topic**

[事实] Dr. Susanna Soberg studies deliberate cold and heat exposure and their effects on human metabolism.
[事实] Huberman introduces brown fat thermogenesis as a way the body increases heat production and metabolism.
[事实] The episode covers cold showers, cold immersion, sauna, neurotransmitters, hormones, metabolism, safety, and practical protocols.

[06:35] **Cold Shock and Sympathetic Activation**

[事实] Soberg says cold water immersion is a potent stressor that activates the sympathetic nervous system.
[事实] Cold exposure increases catecholamines such as norepinephrine, epinephrine, and dopamine-related signaling.
[事实] Being warm before entering cold water makes the experience feel less stressful because core temperature is higher.
[推测] The subjective difficulty of cold exposure depends partly on the temperature difference between skin and environment, not just the absolute water temperature.

[08:13] **How Cold Is Cold Enough**

[事实] Soberg says the water is cold enough if it makes the person uncomfortably cold and triggers a cold shock response.
[事实] Cold shock includes faster breathing, hyperventilation, and a gasping reflex, especially in beginners.
[事实] With repeated exposure, the hyperventilation response can subside as adaptation develops.
[推测] The practical target is not maximum cold, but a repeatable stress level that remains challenging without becoming unsafe.

[13:44] **Cold Shower, Immersion, and Cold Air**

[事实] Full cold-water immersion activates more skin cold receptors than standing outside in cold air.
[事实] Cold air, cold showers, and cold water can all activate the sympathetic nervous system and brown fat.
[事实] Soberg says cold shower research is limited and harder to control methodologically than immersion studies.
[事实] She says brown fat acts as a first responder for temperature regulation, while muscles contribute later through shivering.

[18:23] **Cold Receptors, Hypothalamus, and Brown Fat**

[事实] Cold receptors in the skin send signals to the hypothalamus, the brain’s temperature-regulating center.
[事实] Norepinephrine is described as a potent activator of brown fat.
[事实] Soberg describes multiple pathways involving skin receptors, the brain, brown fat, and muscles.
[推测] The existence of several pathways suggests thermoregulation is biologically important enough to have redundant control systems.

[21:35] **Shivering and Afterdrop**

[事实] Soberg says shivering increases metabolism and burns calories as long as exposure does not become dangerously hypothermic.
[事实] Afterdrop occurs when core temperature continues to fall after leaving cold water because blood vessels reopen and cooler peripheral blood returns to the core.
[事实] Shivering after cold exposure can activate brown fat and help rewarm the body.
[事实] Soberg frames shivering as a form of cellular training that can improve metabolism and insulin sensitivity.

[29:00] **Long-Term Adaptations**

[事实] Repeated cold exposure can make people more comfortable in cold environments over time.
[事实] Soberg says adaptation includes more efficient brown fat mitochondria, better capillary constriction, and a reduced stress response.
[事实] She cites winter swimming research showing lower blood pressure, lower heart rate, and improved insulin sensitivity after a season.
[推测] These adaptations resemble exercise adaptations: acute stress triggers later improvements in baseline function.

[33:29] **Brown Fat Biology**

[事实] Brown fat is metabolically active, rich in mitochondria, and distributed in more body regions than previously thought.
[事实] Soberg says brown fat is plastic: it can grow or decrease depending on stimulation.
[事实] A cancer-related condition with chronically high norepinephrine showed that brown fat can expand, but chronic activation is not desirable.
[事实] Studies associate less brown fat with aging and obesity, though Soberg says causality is not yet clear.

[40:11] **Mild Cold Still Matters**

[事实] Soberg says brown fat can be activated by small skin temperature changes, including putting a hand in cold water.
[事实] Cold wind, cooling vests, cold rooms, and sleeping at 19 degrees Celsius have been shown to activate or increase brown fat.
[事实] In one sleep-temperature study, brown fat and insulin sensitivity improved after sleeping at 19 degrees Celsius and decreased at warmer temperatures.
[推测] People do not necessarily need extreme ice exposure to train thermoregulation.

[54:11] **Soberg’s Winter Swimmer Study**

[事实] Soberg’s study began as a proof-of-concept human study comparing experienced winter swimmers with matched controls.
[事实] The winter swimmers had already been practicing for two to three seasons.
[事实] The study focused on men to reduce confounding because women generally have more brown fat than men.
[事实] Participants typically did two to three weekly sessions involving short cold dips and sauna.

[69:29] **Diving Response and Alternating Cold and Heat**

[事实] Soberg says cold-water submersion can activate a diving response that slows oxygen consumption and heart rate.
[事实] She describes cold immersion as activating both sympathetic and parasympathetic branches after roughly one to two minutes.
[事实] The study protocol involved three cold dips and two sauna sessions, starting and ending with cold.
[事实] The sauna temperature was about 80 degrees Celsius, with sessions lasting around 10 to 15 minutes.

[74:39] **Water Temperature and Timing**

[事实] The Danish winter swimming water ranged from about 12 degrees Celsius in October down to around 2 degrees Celsius in January, then rose again.
[事实] Soberg says water did not need to be colder than roughly 2 to 4 degrees Celsius at the coldest point to be effective.
[事实] Participants were not required to practice at a fixed time of day.
[事实] Soberg says people should test whether evening cold exposure affects their sleep because responses vary.

[77:24] **Measured Outcomes**

[事实] Soberg used PET-MRI scanning as a gold-standard measure of brown fat and also used infrared thermography.
[事实] The study included cooling days and a thermocomfortable day.
[事实] Winter swimmers produced less insulin during experimental days.
[事实] After a glucose drink, winter swimmers cleared glucose from the bloodstream faster than controls.

[85:14] **Minimum Effective Dose**

[事实] Soberg calculates the study exposure as about 11 minutes of cold exposure per week.
[事实] She calculates sauna exposure as about 57 minutes per week.
[事实] She recommends dividing cold exposure into short bouts rather than doing one long cold session.
[事实] She cites Finnish sauna research suggesting benefits up to about 30 minutes per sauna session, with no added benefit beyond that threshold.

[91:27] **Keep the Stress Hormetic**

[事实] Soberg warns that excessively long cold exposure can move beyond healthy hormetic stress and become harmful cellular stress.
[事实] Huberman emphasizes that the goal is to keep cold exposure a stimulus rather than prove tolerance through long sessions.
[事实] Soberg says short repeated exposures are more aligned with adaptation than extreme cold endurance.
[推测] For health-oriented users, consistency and appropriate discomfort matter more than performing maximal-duration cold plunges.

[101:40] **Outlier Without Detectable Brown Fat**

[事实] One winter swimmer in Soberg’s study had no detectable brown fat on scanning.
[事实] This participant shivered earlier, had more difficulty regulating temperature, and resembled controls in insulin and glucose responses.
[事实] Soberg treated him as an outlier in the brown fat analysis.
[推测] This case suggests that cold tolerance and metabolic response may vary substantially between individuals.

[109:38] **The Soberg Principle: End on Cold**

[事实] Soberg says ending on cold forces the body to reheat itself.
[事实] Reheating naturally keeps brown fat and muscles active after the session.
[事实] Soberg says this can extend metabolic and neurotransmitter activation for hours.
[事实] Winter swimmers in her study were warmer on the skin at rest and appeared to lose heat faster because of vascular adaptations.

[115:26] **Raynaud’s, Hands, Feet, and Practical Barriers**

[事实] Soberg says she has not seen direct studies on deliberate cold exposure for Raynaud’s syndrome.
[事实] She has heard mixed anecdotes, with some people reporting benefit and others not.
[事实] She says keeping hands out of the water or wearing neoprene boots is acceptable if hand or foot pain prevents exposure.
[推测] Cold and heat contrast might help vascular responsiveness in some people, but the transcript does not establish this as a proven treatment.

[120:00] **Head Dunking and Heat Loss**

[事实] Soberg says submerging up to the neck in zero-degree Celsius water has been shown to reduce brain blood flow by about 30 to 40 percent.
[事实] She says dunking the head can increase heat loss compared with neck-only immersion.
[事实] She warns that head dunking can increase afterdrop and move a person closer to hypothermia.
[事实] Wearing a beanie is common in Scandinavian winter swimming and can help with wind, sensitive ears, dizziness, and brain-freeze sensations.

[127:16] **Children and Sex Differences**

[事实] Soberg says adult cold-exposure protocols cannot be directly transferred to children because children are smaller and lose heat differently.
[事实] A study comparing 12-year-old boys with adult men found boys could defend core temperature briefly but had to use muscles faster.
[事实] Women generally have more brown fat than men and are thermocomfortable at about 24 degrees Celsius, while men are around 22 degrees Celsius.
[推测] Soberg suggests women might need a different minimum dose, but she says the evidence is not yet available.

[132:15] **Variation Over Rigidity**

[事实] Soberg says changing temperature is what challenges and strengthens cells.
[事实] She says people can use cold-to-warm contrast, repeated short dips, seasonal changes, or varied plunge temperatures.
[事实] She says water does not always need added ice, and temperatures around 12 degrees Celsius can still activate brown fat.
[推测] The practical lesson is to train temperature regulation as a process, not to obsess over one perfect water temperature.

[139:00] **Unpublished Follow-Up and Closing**

[事实] Soberg mentions a newer study including both men and women but says the data were still preliminary and not ready to share.
[事实] Huberman closes by emphasizing the need for more modern, high-resolution studies of cold and heat exposure.
[事实] The episode ends with links to Soberg’s research, institute, book, and Huberman Lab resources.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is that it translates a niche physiology topic into practical protocols while repeatedly distinguishing evidence, theory, and open questions. Soberg is careful about dose, safety, and uncertainty rather than presenting cold exposure as a cure-all.

[推测] A major highlight is the discussion of brown fat as a trainable thermoregulatory tissue, especially the link between cold shock, shivering, insulin sensitivity, glucose clearance, and the “end on cold” principle.

[推测] The main limitation is that several practical questions still lack direct studies, including cold showers, Raynaud’s syndrome, fasted versus fed exposure, women-specific dosing, and mental health outcomes.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in evidence-informed cold plunges, sauna, metabolism, and practical health routines, especially those willing to approach cold and heat as controlled stressors rather than extreme challenges.
