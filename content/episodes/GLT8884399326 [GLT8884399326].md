# The Rest Is History World Cup of Gods: First-Round Losers

## 概览

This episode reviews the first half of The Rest Is History’s “World Cup of Gods,” a Twitter knockout tournament in which listeners voted ancient deities through successive rounds. The hosts explain that they chose gods whose worship had at some point ceased, partly to avoid turning currently worshipped gods into poll losers.

The discussion moves through the gods eliminated in the opening round, using each defeat as a route into religious history, myth, archaeology, and later cultural afterlives. The main thread is that ancient gods rarely fit neat modern categories: some may not have been gods at all, some became saints or emperors, and many combined fertility, violence, sex, death, and political power.

A recurring conclusion is that Christianity and Islam helped erase or absorb many older cults, while some divine motifs survived in altered form. The episode ends before revealing the tournament champion, promising a follow-up on the surviving eight gods.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **The World Cup Of Gods Begins**

[事实] The hosts introduce the “Rest is History World Cup of Gods” as a Twitter knockout contest, recorded after the final but before they reveal the result.

[事实] Dominic Sandbrook says the idea came from his nine-year-old son after a previous World Cup of British Prime Ministers.

[事实] The episode is framed as a review of the first sixteen contenders, beginning with the gods knocked out in the group stage.

[02:08] **Eligibility And Dead Gods**

[事实] Tom Holland explains that the tournament avoided gods still continuously worshipped, so as not to cause needless offence.

[事实] Odin and the Olympians were allowed because, although some people worship them today, their worship had lapsed before modern revivals.

[事实] The hosts say this rule excluded many gods from India, China, Japan, and other parts of Asia where religious continuity is stronger.

[事实] Holland argues that Christianity and Islam acted like “neutron bombs” for older divine systems in Europe and the Near East.

[推测] The tournament’s Eurocentric field is presented less as pure cultural bias than as a by-product of which religious traditions became historically “defunct.”

[05:12] **Controversial Entrants**

[事实] Moloch and Bridget are introduced as controversial choices because both may not have been gods in a straightforward sense.

[事实] Dominic says he was more accepting of Moloch because of the child-sacrifice association, but was initially sceptical of Bridget.

[事实] Bridget’s inclusion is described as partly appealing to Irish listeners.

[06:09] **Moloch And Child Sacrifice**

[事实] Moloch is described as Canaanite or Phoenician, mentioned in Leviticus, and associated with child sacrifice.

[事实] Holland says it is contested whether Moloch was a god or whether the word referred to a ritual of child sacrifice.

[事实] The hosts discuss Gehenna, tophets, Baal, and the possibility that some condemned practices in the Hebrew Bible may have been linked to Yahweh worship.

[事实] They connect the theme of child sacrifice to Abraham and Isaac and, cautiously, to Christian ideas of God sacrificing his son.

[事实] Classical sources, Carthaginian material, archaeological evidence, and Milton’s Paradise Lost are cited as part of Moloch’s later sinister reputation.

[12:51] **Xipe Totec, The Flayed One**

[事实] Xipe Totec is described as an Aztec god associated with flaying, fertility, agriculture, and spring renewal.

[事实] Holland says the god skins himself to give food to humanity, while Aztec priests wore the skins of flayed prisoners for twenty days.

[事实] Xipe Totec is also said to be associated with pimples, rashes, and eye infections.

[事实] The hosts use him to illustrate how many gods could both bless and harm their worshippers.

[事实] Xipe Totec lost heavily to Odin despite the hosts seeing him as a vivid and distinctive contender.

[18:10] **Ishtar, Love, War, And The Underworld**

[事实] Ishtar, also known as Inanna, is identified as a very ancient Mesopotamian goddess linked to Uruk.

[事实] Holland says she becomes associated with Sargon of Akkad and later with Assyrian and Babylonian kings.

[事实] She is described as both a goddess of love and a war goddess, showing how divine roles accumulate and change over time.

[事实] The hosts discuss debated traditions around sacred prostitution, cross-dressing priests, same-sex relations, and ritual suspension of normal rules.

[事实] Her descent to the underworld is summarized as a myth involving loss of power, death, libido disappearing on earth, and her husband Dumuzid taking her place for part of the year.

[24:00] **Ishtar’s Afterlife And Defeat**

[事实] Holland says Ishtar’s worship fades in the Christian and Muslim periods, but her brother Shamash may have a possible living echo in Yazidi tradition.

[事实] Simone de Beauvoir is mentioned as presenting Ishtar as a figure written out of history by patriarchal men.

[事实] Ishtar loses to Mithras, which Dominic jokes proves de Beauvoir’s point.

[25:25] **Bridget Between Goddess And Saint**

[事实] Bridget is introduced as a debated Irish figure often cited as a pagan goddess who became Saint Bridget of Kildare.

[事实] Holland notes evidence connected to Brigantia in Britain and Cormac’s Glossary in Ireland, which refers to Bridget as a goddess.

[事实] An alternative argument from scholar Lisa Bitel is presented: Saint Bridget may have been historical, and goddess-like traits may have attached to her later.

[事实] Bridget is associated with poetry, healing, smithing, miracles, humble origins, and stories such as hanging a cloak on a sunbeam and turning water into beer.

[推测] The discussion suggests Bridget’s supernatural traits may have offered Christian writers a way to express female spiritual authority in a society where women had limited formal power.

[29:45] **Bridget’s Poor Showing Against Apollo**

[事实] Bridget loses to Apollo and is said to have performed poorly in the vote.

[事实] Dominic acknowledges that Holland’s case for Bridget produced an interesting story even if voters were unconvinced.

[30:12] **Augustus As A God**

[事实] Augustus is introduced as an unusual contender because he was historically a man before being deified.

[事实] The hosts describe him as Octavian, the adopted heir of the deified Julius Caesar, victor over Mark Antony and Cleopatra, and the first Roman emperor.

[事实] Holland says Augustus was worshipped outside Rome during his life and officially deified after his death in AD 14.

[事实] His funeral involved spectacle, and a senator’s claim to have seen his spirit rise to heaven helped support his divine status.

[事实] Holland says Augustus’s cult spread extremely quickly across the Roman Empire.

[33:09] **Mortal, Immortal, And Imperial Power**

[事实] Dominic argues that Augustus’s deification complicates modern assumptions that mortal and immortal were always separate categories.

[事实] Holland links Augustus’s divinity to gratitude for bringing peace after Roman civil war.

[事实] The hosts discuss language of “good news” around Augustus and compare it with early Christian claims about Jesus as son of God and prince of peace.

[事实] They note that ruler worship also drew on Hellenistic precedents such as Alexander, the Ptolemies, and pharaonic traditions.

[事实] Augustus loses to Athena, and the hosts suggest voters may have struggled to see past his human origin.

[36:56] **Loki’s Surprise Defeat By Anubis**

[事实] Loki’s loss to Anubis is described as a major shock.

[事实] Dominic says he voted for Anubis partly because of a childhood association with making an Anubis mask.

[事实] The hosts suggest Loki may have suffered from an anti-Marvel or anti-Tom Hiddleston backlash.

[事实] Holland notes that Norse mythology has always been mediated and rewritten, with Marvel as another modern iteration.

[38:23] **Loki As Trickster And Threat**

[事实] Loki is described as a trickster and father of Hel, Fenrir, Jormungandr, and Odin’s eight-legged horse Sleipnir.

[事实] The hosts recount Loki’s role in Balder’s death and his punishment with serpent venom.

[事实] Loki is seen as malignant but entertaining, often making Norse stories more interesting.

[推测] Anubis may have beaten Loki partly because voters liked the dog-headed imagery more than the trickster archetype.

[40:59] **Cybele, Ancient Mother Goddess**

[事实] Cybele is described as a Phrygian goddess from Anatolia, possibly linked to very ancient mother-goddess figures from Catalhoyuk.

[事实] Holland says that if this continuity is correct, Cybele may have been worshipped longer than any other deity discussed.

[事实] The Romans adopted her cult during the Punic War after being told to bring it to Rome.

[事实] The hosts describe older traditions associating Cybele with stones, totems, and mother-goddess imagery.

[43:28] **Cybele, Attis, And Ritual Castration**

[事实] The myth discussed has Cybele beginning as hermaphroditic, cutting off her testicles, and those testicles producing a fruit tree.

[事实] A river nymph becomes pregnant from the fruit and gives birth to Attis, with whom Cybele later falls in love.

[事实] At Attis’s wedding, Cybele appears, Attis goes mad, castrates himself, and flees.

[事实] Cybele’s priests, the Galli, are described as self-castrating, cross-dressing devotees associated with the Day of Blood.

[事实] Roman authors such as Seneca are said to have disapproved, and Christianity eventually displaced Cybele’s cult.

[47:48] **Prince Philip As A Modern God**

[事实] Prince Philip is included because he was worshipped on Tanna in Vanuatu by a local group.

[事实] Dominic connects this to Melanesian cargo cults, especially those that developed after the Second World War around mysterious goods and outside visitors.

[事实] The hosts mention John Frum as a famous cargo-cult figure.

[事实] Prince Philip’s connection may relate to the royal visit to Vanuatu in 1974, though the exact origin of the cult is unclear.

[推测] Dominic suggests sceptically that repeated media visits may have helped sustain outside fascination with the cult.

[50:34] **Prince Philip’s Divine Persona And Exit**

[事实] Prince Philip lost comfortably to Dionysus.

[事实] The hosts discuss a listener’s argument that Philip had a godlike backstory, including exile, marriage into monarchy, athleticism, and a dangerous streak.

[事实] Holland says a god should have some sense of danger rather than being merely safe or agreeable.

[事实] The episode ends by saying the next episode will cover the last eight gods and reveal the champion.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its ability to turn a comic Twitter tournament into a compact tour of comparative religion. The hosts use defeats in a mock sporting contest to explain real issues: cult continuity, syncretism, sacrifice, deification, gendered power, and the afterlife of myths.

[推测] Its strongest moments come when familiar categories break down: Moloch may be ritual rather than god, Bridget may be saint before goddess, Augustus shows a human becoming divine, and Prince Philip shows modern media and anthropology colliding with worship.

[推测] The limitation is that the tone is deliberately jokey and fast-moving, so complex subjects such as child sacrifice, gender-fluid ritual, and indigenous religion are treated as entertaining podcast material rather than deeply developed scholarship.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who enjoy ancient history, mythology, and irreverent historical conversation, especially those comfortable with humour around strange or violent religious practices.
