# Harald Hardrada, Byzantium, and the Road to 1066

## 概览

This episode follows Harald Hardrada after his enlistment in the Varangian Guard, tracing his campaigns across the Byzantine world and the blend of history, saga, and literary invention that surrounds them.

The hosts repeatedly stress that much of Harald’s story comes from Icelandic sagas written centuries later, so they compare saga material with Byzantine sources where possible. The result is a deliberately tentative reconstruction: some campaigns are well-attested, while episodes involving serpents, disguises, miraculous rescues, and spectacular escapes remain uncertain or legendary.

The episode’s larger arc shows Harald turning battlefield service into wealth, status, and political capital. After returning north, he marries Elisif in Kiev, maneuvers against Magnus the Good, becomes king of Norway, and ends up as a hard, centralizing ruler whose ambitions set the stage for 1066.

[事实] The episode opens with a dramatic dungeon scene involving Harald, corpses, filthy water, and a giant serpent.
[事实] The hosts reveal the passage is from Dominic Sandbrook’s *Adventures in Time: Fury of the Vikings*, not from J. R. R. Tolkien.
[推测] The opening frames Harald’s life as both historical biography and heroic adventure story.

[02:00] **Harald Joins the Varangian Campaigns**

[事实] By 1035, Harald has joined the Varangian Guard in Constantinople.
[事实] News arrives that Arab corsairs are raiding Greek islands and enslaving people.
[事实] The hosts say Harald likely spends the next six or seven years on campaign.
[推测] His Byzantine service is presented as the key phase in which he gains experience, fame, and wealth.

[03:07] **Uncertain Sources**

[事实] The hosts emphasize that much of Harald’s story comes from Icelandic sagas written at least 200 years later.
[事实] They note that Byzantine sources help establish the kinds of campaigns the empire was fighting.
[推测] The episode treats the sagas as valuable but unreliable, especially when they use familiar heroic formulas.

[03:52] **Aegean and Armenian Warfare**

[事实] The hosts say the Varangians likely fought pirates in the Aegean in spring 1035.
[事实] They connect saga material with John Skylitzes’ account of North African ships attacking the Cyclades.
[事实] Harald may later have been sent to Armenia, where the imperial army besieged Berkri near Lake Van.
[事实] George Maniakis, a powerful Byzantine general, appears as Harald’s commander and rival.

[06:12] **Jerusalem and the Holy Sepulchre**

[事实] The emperor had made a deal with the Fatimids to rebuild the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem.
[事实] The sagas say Harald served as part of a Varangian escort for the expedition.
[事实] Snorri says Harald bathed in the Jordan like other pilgrims.
[推测] The hosts regard the pilgrimage detail as plausible, though not firmly proven.

[08:57] **Sicily and Saga Set Pieces**

[事实] Harald’s longest posting was probably in Sicily from about 1038 to 1041.
[事实] The sagas credit him with dramatic tactics involving burning birds, tunnels, and a fake funeral.
[事实] The hosts point out that similar bird stories appear elsewhere, including in stories about Olga of Kiev.
[事实] They state that Byzantium did not actually conquer Sicily; it retained only a limited foothold.

[15:48] **Promotion and the Bulgar Campaign**

[事实] After Sicily, Harald is said to have been promoted to an elite imperial bodyguard rank.
[事实] In 1040, Emperor Michael IV took Harald on campaign against the Bulgars.
[事实] Cecaumenus later wrote that “Araltes” performed great deeds of valor.
[事实] Harald received the court rank of Spatharokandidatos, a prestigious honor for a foreigner.

[17:51] **Byzantine Court Politics**

[事实] Michael IV’s health deteriorated from epilepsy and dropsy, and by late 1041 he was dying.
[事实] His relatives arranged for another Michael to succeed him as Michael V.
[事实] Zoe remained central because she carried the blood of the Macedonian dynasty.
[推测] The hosts present Byzantine court politics as a shift from heroic military adventure into palace intrigue.

[22:04] **Michael V Deposes Zoe**

[事实] At Easter 1042, Michael V accused Zoe of trying to poison him and sent her to a nunnery on the Prince’s Islands.
[事实] Zoe was stripped of her purple robes and her head was shaved.
[事实] The people of Constantinople reacted violently when they heard what had happened.
[推测] Michael V’s removal of Zoe is described as a major political miscalculation.

[25:13] **Riot or Counter-Coup**

[事实] Rioters entered Hagia Sophia, involved the patriarch, and chaos spread through Constantinople.
[事实] The hosts suggest the Varangians may have sided against Michael V.
[事实] Michael V fled to the Studios Monastery but was dragged out and blinded.
[事实] The sagas claim Harald himself blinded the emperor, though the hosts treat this cautiously.

[31:41] **Checking the Legend**

[事实] After the break, the hosts pause to question how much of Harald’s story can be trusted.
[事实] They identify three unresolved saga elements: imprisonment, a possible love affair, and an escape by ship.
[事实] They say biographers must stitch together a plausible narrative from contradictory sources.
[推测] The episode openly admits that parts of Harald’s Byzantine story may be heroic reconstruction.

[33:33] **Gold, Zoe, and Maria**

[事实] The sagas say Harald had sent a huge treasure hoard ahead to Kiev.
[事实] A possible love story involves Maria, identified in the discussion with Maria Skleraina at the Byzantine court.
[事实] The hosts say Maria existed, but doubt that Harald had an affair with her.
[推测] The story may preserve a memory of court tension while reshaping it into romance.

[37:10] **Dungeon, Serpent, and Rescue**

[事实] Some sagas say Harald, Haldor, and Ulf were imprisoned in a dark tower or dungeon.
[事实] The prison story includes a giant serpent or dragon that fed on corpses.
[事实] Harald kills the creature, then receives help after a vision involving his dead brother Olaf.
[推测] The serpent episode is treated as highly legendary, though the hosts do not rule out a smaller real incident behind it.

[39:12] **Escape from Constantinople**

[事实] The saga escape involves Harald and his men seizing two galleys in the Golden Horn.
[事实] They escape by rocking a ship over the great chain that blocked the harbor.
[事实] One boat breaks up, but Harald’s group survives and heads toward the Black Sea.
[推测] The hosts suggest a real court feud, pressure over Harald’s departure, or a coming Kievan Rus attack may lie behind the tale.

[42:07] **Return to Kiev and Marriage**

[事实] Harald was probably back in Kiev by about 1043.
[事实] He was around 28 years old and very wealthy.
[事实] He married Elisif, daughter of Yaroslav.
[事实] The hosts say his fame and Varangian command would have made him a valuable political match.

[44:07] **A Changed North**

[事实] Canute was dead, his North Sea empire had fallen apart, and Edward the Confessor ruled England.
[事实] Norway was ruled by Harald’s nephew Magnus the Good.
[事实] Denmark was contested by Svein, Canute’s nephew.
[推测] Harald’s return placed him into a wider web of Scandinavian and English dynastic politics.

[46:43] **Harald Challenges Magnus**

[事实] Around 1045, Harald arrived in Sweden and learned Magnus had become king of both Norway and Denmark.
[事实] Harald met Magnus in Skane and asked about dividing the kingdom.
[事实] Magnus gave a diplomatic answer but did not immediately concede power.
[推测] Harald’s wealth made him dangerous, even though Magnus had stronger existing political support.

[48:41] **Gold for Kingship**

[事实] Harald contacted Svein and threatened to restart war against Magnus.
[事实] Magnus eventually agreed to share Norway if Harald shared his gold.
[事实] The sagas describe Harald publicly displaying his treasure before dividing it.
[推测] The bargain shows that Harald’s Byzantine wealth became the practical foundation of his claim.

[51:07] **Magnus Dies and Harald Wins**

[事实] In 1047, Magnus fell ill after a dream in which Olaf offered him a choice between long life with damnation or early death with salvation.
[事实] Magnus died on 25 October 1047.
[事实] Harald then became king of Norway.
[推测] The hosts present Harald’s rise as the fulfillment of his long ambition, but not as a happy ending.

[52:49] **A Difficult Norwegian Reign**

[事实] Harald spent much of his reign in prolonged war against Denmark.
[事实] He had a large warship called the Great Serpent.
[事实] He tried to centralize Norway, raise taxes, develop coinage, and support Christianity.
[事实] The hosts connect his model of kingship to stronger states such as England and Byzantium.

[55:32] **Hardrada the Severe Ruler**

[事实] In 1064, farmers in Norway’s uplands resisted taxation.
[事实] In 1065, Harald carried out a harsh campaign against them, involving seizure, maiming, killing, and confiscation.
[事实] The hosts identify this severity as the origin of the name Hardrada.
[推测] Harald’s rule is portrayed as modernizing but brutal.

[58:03] **Toward 1066**

[事实] By the end of 1065, Harald was about 50 years old.
[事实] Elisif had borne him two daughters, and a second wife, Tora, had borne him two sons.
[事实] The episode ends by previewing 1066: Edward the Confessor’s death, Harold Godwinson’s accession, Harald Hardrada’s invasion, Stamford Bridge, William of Normandy, and Hastings.
[推测] The closing frames Harald as restless and unfinished, ready for one last attempt at glory.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s main value is its handling of uncertainty. It gives listeners the excitement of saga narrative while repeatedly marking where the evidence becomes thin, contradictory, or obviously literary.

Its strongest feature is the way it connects Harald’s personal story to wider Byzantine, Kievan, Scandinavian, and English politics. The episode makes clear that his later role in 1066 cannot be understood without his years in Constantinople and Kiev.

[推测] The main limitation is that listeners wanting a strictly source-critical reconstruction may find the saga episodes too heavily dramatized. The hosts do flag those problems, but they still spend substantial time enjoying the legendary material.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in medieval power politics, Viking saga traditions, Byzantine court intrigue, and the long build-up to 1066.
