# Chainsaw sputtering: Milei’s experiment falters

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* opens with Argentina’s Javier Milei and the state of his radical free-market experiment. The discussion presents him as still confident and combative, but facing a more fragile political and economic moment than his rhetoric suggests.

The central tension is that Milei has delivered visible wins on inflation, poverty reduction, debt payments and market confidence, while voters are increasingly focused on jobs, wages and growth. The programme argues that those areas remain weak, making the next election a test of whether his “chainsaw” agenda can still command public support.

The episode then turns to Saudi Arabia’s sports-investment push, especially the troubles around LIV Golf, using it to argue that money alone cannot buy fans, institutional disruption or sporting legitimacy. It closes with India’s elephants, whose cultural and ecological importance contrasts with shrinking habitats and rising human conflict.

[事实] Saudi Arabia won the right to host the 2034 World Cup, requiring major internal investment in stadiums and related infrastructure.

[事实] The second lesson is that creative destruction is difficult in long-established sports.

[事实] It also cites Grand Slam Track, started by Michael Johnson, which filed for bankruptcy less than a year after its first fixture.
[推测] The segment suggests that sports institutions are more resistant to outside disruption than many conventional industries.

[事实] The episode says there is still strong investment interest in sports from financial institutions.

[20:47] **India’s elephants: revered but declining**

[事实] The segment opens by noting that elephants were prized in the ancient Indian statecraft manual *Arthashastra*.
[事实] Elephants are also linked to Ganesha, the elephant-headed Hindu god.
[事实] A Wildlife Institute of India survey counted 20% fewer elephants last year than in 2017.
[推测] The episode contrasts cultural reverence for elephants with inadequate practical protection.

[21:35] **Habitat loss and human conflict**

[事实] Villages are scattered through forests in densely populated India.
[事实] Elephants live in narrow, confined habitat corridors, and those corridors are shrinking.
[事实] Roads, railways, power lines and mining sites cut through forests and push elephants toward farms and villages.
[事实] Humans sometimes drive elephants away with loud noises, fireballs and crackers despite a Supreme Court ban.
[事实] Elephants have learned to break electric fences with their tusks, which are poor conductors of electricity.

[22:31] **Protection efforts and ecological importance**

[事实] Poaching has fallen since the 1980s.
[事实] India now has a Wildlife Crime Control Bureau, and states have anti-poaching plans.
[事实] India’s Supreme Court upheld the removal of commercial activity from an elephant corridor in Tamil Nadu.
[事实] Elephants disperse seeds, shape forests, fertilise soil, open canopies, sustain insects through dung and help keep wetlands alive.
[推测] The closing argument is that protecting elephant corridors is not only conservation work but ecosystem maintenance.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strongest section is the Milei interview analysis, because it ties personality, policy, macroeconomic indicators and electoral incentives together. It avoids presenting inflation reduction as either a total success or a total failure, instead showing why voters may change priorities once prices stabilise.

The Saudi sports segment is useful because it treats sports investment as a business and political strategy, not just spectacle. Its clearest insight is that established sports depend on fans, institutions and time horizons that cannot be quickly bought.

The elephant segment is shorter and more compressed, but it provides a clear conservation takeaway: reverence is not the same as habitat protection. [推测] Listeners interested in global politics, economics, sports business and environmental policy would get the most value from this episode, though each topic is handled briefly rather than in documentary depth.
