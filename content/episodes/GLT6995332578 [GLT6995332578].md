# Caesar’s Assassins, Octavian’s Rise, and the End of the Roman Republic

## 概览

This episode follows the aftermath of Julius Caesar’s assassination, beginning with the image of Brutus seeing a dreadful apparition that foretells Philippi. The hosts use that scene to frame the wider tragedy: the conspirators killed Caesar to save the Republic, but failed to control what came next.

The discussion traces how Antony seized the initiative in Rome, how Caesar’s funeral turned public emotion against the assassins, and how Cicero tried to rally constitutional forces against Antony while lacking military power. Into that vacuum stepped Octavian, Caesar’s 18-year-old heir, who used Caesar’s name, money, and political ruthlessness to become a decisive actor.

The second half follows the proscriptions, Cicero’s death, the legal reversal of the assassins’ amnesty, and the gathering of vast armies by both sides. The story culminates at Philippi, where Cassius and then Brutus kill themselves, leaving the Republic effectively dead and Octavian on the path to becoming Augustus.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Brutus’s Apparition and the Episode’s Frame**

[事实] The episode opens with Brutus seeing a strange and terrifying apparition that tells him he will see it again at Philippi.

[事实] The hosts note that Shakespeare later reworked this into the ghost of Caesar himself.

[推测] The apparition frames the episode as a story of guilt, doom, and historical tragedy rather than a simple military narrative.

[02:27] **The Liberators Fail to Seize Rome**

[事实] After Caesar’s murder, Brutus tries to speak but panic has emptied the public spaces and gripped the city.

[事实] Antony initially fears he may be next, disguises himself as a slave, and retreats to his house.

[事实] The conspirators withdraw to the Capitol instead of reconvening the Senate, declaring Caesar a tyrant, and taking control of the Republic’s machinery.

[推测] Their failure to act immediately gives Antony room to define the political aftermath.

[04:40] **Amnesty, Provinces, and a Fatal Funeral Decision**

[事实] On March 17, Antony convenes the Senate near his house, with Dolabella beside him.

[事实] Cicero proposes an amnesty for the assassins, and the dictatorship is abolished forever.

[事实] Provinces are allocated: Decimus Brutus receives Cisalpine Gaul, Trebonius receives Asia, and Dolabella receives Syria.

[事实] The hosts identify the decision to allow Caesar a public funeral as a disastrous mistake by the assassins.

[06:58] **Caesar’s Funeral Turns the Crowd**

[事实] At Caesar’s funeral on March 20, his body, blood-stained toga, and a wax effigy showing his wounds are displayed in the Forum.

[事实] Antony reads Caesar’s will, including gifts of gardens and cash to the Roman people.

[事实] Public anger rises against the assassins, and a poet named Cinna is mistaken for a political target and killed by a mob.

[事实] Cleopatra leaves Rome for Egypt as the city becomes increasingly unstable.

[推测] The funeral transforms Caesar from a murdered politician into a popular martyr.

[11:09] **Cicero Becomes the Voice of the Republic**

[事实] Cicero’s letters show disappointment that freedom seemed restored but the Republic itself had not been secured.

[事实] He considers leaving for Athens but returns to Rome after friends challenge him for running away.

[事实] As many conspirators leave Rome, Cicero remains and becomes the leading spokesman for the traditional constitution.

[事实] Cicero identifies Antony as the main remaining threat of tyranny.

[13:41] **The Military Problem Behind Cicero’s Politics**

[事实] Lepidus controls a legion on the island in the Tiber, making him a threat to the conspirators.

[事实] Decimus Brutus has legions assigned to him in Cisalpine Gaul, but those are Caesar’s legions and their loyalty is uncertain.

[事实] Cicero has no army and relies on oratory to target Antony.

[事实] Cicero works to turn the consuls-designate Hirtius and Pansa against Antony.

[推测] Cicero’s strategy depends on using other people’s military power to defend a constitutional order that no longer has force of its own.

[15:58] **Octavian Enters the Story**

[事实] Octavius is 18 when he learns Caesar has made him his principal heir.

[事实] Despite his mother’s warning not to go to Rome, he crosses to Italy and takes the name Gaius Julius Caesar.

[事实] He uses Caesar’s name and fortune to build popularity, pay Caesar’s promised bequests, and recruit a private bodyguard of 3,000 men.

[事实] He briefly occupies the Forum and later tries to win Cicero over in Naples.

[推测] Octavian’s youth makes him look vulnerable, but his control of Caesar’s name and money makes him politically dangerous.

[19:54] **Mutina and the Return of Civil War**

[事实] Antony goes north to raise troops as his consulship nears its end, while Decimus Brutus tries to hold Cisalpine Gaul.

[事实] Antony besieges Decimus Brutus at Mutina, reigniting civil war.

[事实] Cicero persuades the Senate to legitimize Octavian’s private army and attach him to Hirtius and Pansa.

[事实] Cicero privately jokes that Octavian should be praised, honored, and then “raised to the skies,” a remark that reaches Octavian.

[22:13] **Antony’s Defeat and Decimus Brutus’s Death**

[事实] In spring 43, Hirtius and Pansa advance to relieve Mutina.

[事实] Antony attacks Panza at Forum Gallorum; Panza is badly wounded, but Antony is then ambushed by Hirtius and forced to retreat.

[事实] The Senate declares Antony a public enemy after news of his defeat.

[事实] Hirtius dies in battle and Panza dies of his wounds, leaving Octavian as the remaining major commander near Rome.

[事实] Antony joins Lepidus in Gaul, Decimus Brutus flees, is captured by a Gallic chieftain, and is put to death.

[23:50] **Octavian Forces the Consulship and Forms the Triumvirate**

[事实] In late July, a centurion demands the consulship for Octavian and threatens force if the Senate refuses.

[事实] On August 19, Octavian is formally elected consul at age 19.

[事实] Octavian then meets Antony and Lepidus instead of fighting them, and they form the Second Triumvirate.

[事实] The triumvirs claim authority for five years, control lawmaking, bypass Senate and people, and extend martial law to Rome.

[推测] The assassination meant to stop autocracy directly accelerates military dictatorship.

[27:01] **Proscriptions and the Blood Price of Power**

[事实] The hosts quote Suetonius’s claim that few of Caesar’s murderers outlived him by more than three years or died naturally.

[事实] Antony, Octavian, and Lepidus present themselves as avengers of Caesar.

[事实] They need money to fight Brutus and Cassius, so they use proscriptions against wealthy senators, following Sulla’s earlier model.

[事实] Rewards are offered for killing proscribed men and bringing their heads to the triumvirs.

[事实] The proscriptions produce both stories of betrayal and stories of loyalty, including wives who try to save their husbands.

[30:15] **Cicero’s Death and the Silencing of Speech**

[事实] Antony wants Cicero dead because Cicero has written increasingly abusive speeches against him.

[事实] Octavian agrees that Cicero should be killed, just as Antony and Lepidus sacrifice relatives they might otherwise have protected.

[事实] Cicero is caught by bounty hunters after hesitating over where to flee.

[事实] His throat is cut, and his head and hands are displayed in the Forum.

[事实] Fulvia, Antony’s wife, pulls out Cicero’s tongue and stabs it with hairpins.

[推测] The treatment of Cicero’s tongue and hands symbolizes the destruction of Rome’s tradition of free political speech.

[32:04] **The Assassins Are Condemned**

[事实] Trebonius, who had kept Antony from entering the Senate House during Caesar’s murder, becomes one of the first assassins killed.

[事实] Dolabella captures Trebonius in Asia, has him tortured, and places his severed head beneath a statue of Caesar.

[事实] Quintus Pedius passes the Lex Pedia, reversing the amnesty and sentencing the assassins to death.

[事实] The hosts argue that the assassins’ initial failure to secure Rome makes everything that follows possible.

[33:55] **Brutus and Cassius Build Their Own War Machine**

[事实] Brutus and Cassius raise money and troops effectively in the East.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that brutality is not confined to the triumvirs; Brutus and Cassius also commit atrocities.

[事实] Brutus destroys an entire city, and its inhabitants are driven to mass suicide.

[事实] Brutus also kills Antony’s brother after taking him prisoner.

[事实] Brutus and Cassius gather 20 legions, just under 100,000 men, along with cavalry and mounted archers.

[35:31] **The Road to Philippi**

[事实] Antony and Octavian raise a matching force of 20 legions and cross the Adriatic, leaving Lepidus behind.

[事实] Brutus and Cassius move through Macedonia and position themselves near Philippi.

[事实] They block the Via Egnatia and hold a strong supply position, with access to the nearest port.

[事实] Antony faces Cassius, while Octavian faces Brutus.

[事实] Octavian is ill on the eve of battle, while Antony takes the more active battlefield role.

[37:24] **Cassius and Brutus Fall**

[事实] Antony tries to outflank Cassius through marshland, triggering battle.

[事实] Brutus’s forces attack and capture Octavian’s camp.

[事实] Antony defeats Cassius’s forces, and Cassius kills himself before learning that Brutus has won on his side.

[事实] Brutus holds his position for three weeks, then comes out to fight, loses, and kills himself.

[推测] Cassius’s premature suicide may have decisively damaged the assassins’ chances.

[38:36] **Philippi and the Memory of the Republic**

[事实] The hosts discuss another ghost story in which Caesar’s ghost is said to predict Octavian’s victory.

[事实] They consider whether Brutus and Cassius might have restored liberty at sword point if Philippi had gone differently.

[事实] One tradition says Antony treats Brutus’s body honorably; another says Octavian orders it beheaded and sent to Rome.

[事实] Captured noblemen salute Antony as imperator but curse Octavian.

[事实] Later writers remember Brutus and Cassius as “the last of the Romans.”

[42:40] **From Republic to Augustus**

[事实] Octavian later defeats Antony and Cleopatra, receives the name Augustus, and rules as Rome’s first emperor.

[事实] The hosts state that the Republic as it had been understood for centuries is gone.

[事实] The last assassin, Cassius Parmensis, is murdered in 30 BC, meaning the hunt for Caesar’s killers lasts about 14 years.

[事实] Augustus’s success depends partly on people being willing to forget the civil wars.

[事实] Augustus learns from Caesar’s example and presents himself with more modesty as a servant of the Senate.

[45:12] **Was the Republic Already Doomed?**

[事实] The hosts debate whether the Republic was doomed by Rome’s expansion, wealth, empire, and outdated governing machinery.

[事实] One view presented is that Caesar’s personal ambition, genius, and lack of sentimentality toward Republican traditions helped destroy it.

[事实] Another view is that the system had become too unstable and that some figure would eventually do what Caesar did.

[事实] Octavian is described as terrifying and ruthless, but also as an exceptionally gifted statesman who stabilizes the situation.

[推测] The bloodshed after Caesar’s murder may have made Augustus’s later settlement politically acceptable.

[49:44] **Caesar, Brutus, and Historical Sympathy**

[事实] The hosts discuss why modern audiences often sympathize more with Caesar than with his assassins.

[事实] They argue that political values shape whether Caesar’s murder is seen as tyrannicide or betrayal.

[事实] Shakespeare’s play is discussed as a way to explore the murder of rulers indirectly.

[事实] The French Revolution and a 2017 Central Park production portraying Caesar with Trump-like features are used as examples of changing political readings.

[事实] The hosts end by noting that Caesar’s clemency toward former enemies made his assassination feel especially loaded, even though Roman ideas of pardon differed from modern Christianized assumptions.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its clear explanation of how a political assassination can fail when its planners do not control the institutions, the streets, or the armies afterward. It is especially strong on the chain from Caesar’s funeral to the Second Triumvirate and Philippi.

[推测] A major strength is the way the hosts connect political calculation, military force, public emotion, and later literary memory. The episode avoids treating Brutus and Cassius as purely noble idealists by emphasizing their own brutality in the East.

[推测] Its limitation is that some battlefield details at Philippi are summarized quickly, with more attention given to political meaning than tactical reconstruction. Listeners wanting a military history of the battle may need a separate account.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in Roman political crisis, the collapse of republics, and the relationship between historical events and later cultural memory.
