# The Stranger Who Made an Eight-Year-Old Feel Safe

## 概览

Wendy McDowell recalls being abandoned at Chicago O’Hare Airport when she was eight years old. After their connecting flight was canceled, the 19-year-old family friend accompanying her booked another flight and left Wendy alone without a plan or responsible adult.

An unknown woman noticed Wendy crying, approached her gently, and repeatedly asked whether she felt safe. She contacted Wendy’s mother, booked a new ticket, and accompanied Wendy on the two-and-a-half-hour flight home—even though she had originally planned to travel elsewhere.

The stranger’s compassion reshaped Wendy’s understanding of how people can choose to help others. Wendy later became a school psychologist and has shared the story with her son as an example of the security and kindness people can offer in moments of need.

## 播客点评/总结

This brief episode presents a sharp contrast between abandonment and compassion. Its strongest moment is not simply the stranger’s willingness to help, but her care in asking Wendy for permission and checking whether the child felt safe.

The story shows how a seemingly temporary act of kindness can influence another person’s worldview, parenting, and professional life for decades. Wendy’s recollection also offers a memorable example of treating a frightened child as someone whose feelings and consent matter.

[推测] Because the episode relies on Wendy’s childhood memories and provides little information about the unidentified woman, it cannot explore the helper’s motivations or what happened to her afterward. That absence, however, reinforces the episode’s central theme: ordinary people can profoundly change a life without receiving recognition.
