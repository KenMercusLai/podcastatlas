# Advice Line with Ben Goodwin of Olipop

## 概览

Guy Raz is joined by Ben Goodwin, co-founder of Olipop, to advise three food and retail founders facing different growth problems: how to explain an unfamiliar ingredient, how to scale a fresh allergy-friendly bakery, and how to revive sales at a community grocery co-op.

The episode’s recurring message is that customers rarely start with deep education. Ben and Guy repeatedly steer callers toward clearer buying triggers: taste, comparative value, trust, convenience, curation, and the in-person experience.

Ben also frames entrepreneurship as a mix of craft, mission, stress tolerance, and instinct. His advice emphasizes staying close to the real customer behavior while preserving the deeper story for people who want to go further.

## 分段落总结

[00:23] **Show Setup and Ben Goodwin’s Context**

[事实] Guy Raz introduces the Advice Line format and says the show helps callers work through business challenges.

[事实] Ben Goodwin is introduced as co-founder of Olipop, a prebiotic soda brand Guy says has been valued at more than $2 billion.

[事实] Guy notes Ben previously appeared on the show to tell the Olipop story, including walking away from an earlier soda brand before later building Olipop.

[01:46] **Olipop’s Brand Refresh, Remote Culture, and Growth Options**

[事实] Ben says Olipop’s original brand and packaging created a strong foundation, and the product’s taste kept people coming back.

[事实] The new refresh includes visual identity, sonic identity, and a fiber education campaign intended to better communicate the brand mission.

[事实] Ben says early advertising traction is reaching men and older demographics at higher rates and is the company’s most effective advertising to date.

[事实] Olipop remains fully remote, has about 280 employees, and may approach 300 by the end of the year or the first quarter of the next year.

[04:15] **Mission Before Exit Strategy**

[事实] Guy asks about Olipop’s future after PepsiCo acquired Poppi, one of Olipop’s competitors.

[事实] Ben says Olipop’s mission impact is the company’s north star, and he does not want the product degraded or delisted in the future.

[事实] Ben says Olipop published human research showing its Vintage Cola did not spike blood sugar and kept blood sugar stable.

[事实] Ben says his current target is $1 billion in gross revenue, and Olipop is already well over half a billion.

[06:19] **Rejecting “Market” as an Excuse**

[事实] Ben says he rejects advice that tells founders something is “market” when it means compromising standards, integrity, or quality.

[推测] His broader point is that entrepreneurs should not automatically accept industry norms when those norms weaken the product or mission.

[07:23] **Gheelish’s Ghee-Based Snack Challenge**

[事实] Darren Alpert, founder and CEO of Gheelish in Raleigh, says his snack brand is made with organic ghee and reached about 4,000 stores in two years.

[事实] Gheelish started with organic popcorn, later launched a pop chip or PopCorners-style product, and has a sweet snack planned for the fall.

[事实] Darren says he started with homemade samples, cold-emailed Sprouts, got on Sprouts shelves nationwide, and later raised money from Gary Vaynerchuk and friends and family.

[事实] Gheelish did about $1.8 million in sales in 2025, expects about $3.5 million this year, and sells wholesale without DTC or Amazon.

[09:54] **Whether to Educate Consumers About Ghee**

[事实] Darren asks how to educate shoppers about ghee because many people only know it as clarified butter in a jar.

[事实] Ben says he personally uses ghee heavily in home cooking and is interested in its Ayurvedic history and use as a nutrient carrier.

[事实] Darren says Gheelish is organic and has 30% fewer calories and 60% less fat than SkinnyPop.

[推测] The core problem is not only ingredient awareness, but how quickly shoppers can understand why they should switch snacks.

[12:11] **Lead With Taste and Simple Comparison**

[事实] Ben says consumer motivation starts with price as a value-to-dollar question, but taste is the most important product driver.

[事实] Ben says if Gheelish cannot win blind taste tests against major competitors at its price point, the structural product argument becomes almost irrelevant.

[事实] Ben advises leading with simple mainstream claims such as less fat, fewer calories, better taste, and real ingredients.

[事实] Guy adds that shoppers may not need ghee education first if the product tastes good and has far lower calories and fat than leading brands.

[14:00] **Use the Deeper Ghee Story in the Right Places**

[事实] Ben agrees the deeper ghee story still matters, including the founder story, ghee’s cultural and medical history, and fat as a transporter of flavor.

[事实] Ben suggests putting comparative nutrition clearly on the front of the pack while using the back of the bag, QR codes, and social media for deeper education.

[事实] Guy says Gheelish has strong velocity and notes that snack foods are a difficult category.

[推测] The advice separates mass-market conversion from evangelist education: the front pack should sell quickly, while deeper channels can build loyalty.

[18:04] **Sarah’s Gluten-Free Goods and Fresh Allergy-Friendly Baking**

[事实] Sarah Godfrey, owner of Sarah’s Gluten-Free Goods in Westfield, Massachusetts, makes gluten-free desserts that also cater to allergies such as dairy, nut, corn, and oat allergies.

[事实] Sarah operates with a cottage food permit, cooks from home, sells at three farmers markets, and has products in two or three small local grocery stores.

[事实] She says her products are fresh rather than frozen and are made in a facility safe for multiple allergies.

[20:17] **Freshness, Perishability, and Early Profitability**

[事实] Sarah says she has been open about a year, had negative $2,000 in net profit last year, and expects about $20,000 in net profit this year.

[事实] Her question is how to provide fresh, never-frozen gluten-free goods to the local community despite shelf-life and perishability issues.

[推测] Sarah’s differentiation is tied to freshness and trust, but that same positioning creates operational pressure as demand grows.

[22:05] **Capital, Grants, and the Timing of Scale**

[事实] Ben asks Sarah about her capital needs and risk tolerance, including equity versus debt.

[事实] Sarah says she is debt-averse and previously received a $3,500 local small-business grant that helped her website.

[事实] Guy argues Sarah is still far from raising outside money and should first show more traction and velocity.

[事实] Guy suggests she could become the bakery behind local coffee shops, co-ops, or small stores with a limited set of best-selling items.

[25:00] **Capacity, Liability, and Moving Beyond One Person**

[事实] Sarah says she currently drops off small batches to three local shops every Tuesday, with the shops taking a 10% commission.

[事实] She says some coffee shops resist carrying her products because of allergy liability concerns.

[事实] Sarah says local TV, radio, newspaper, and gluten-free magazine coverage have increased demand, but she is stretched as the only baker, marketer, and operator.

[事实] Ben confirms that Sarah has standardized recipes, which means she could theoretically hand existing formulas to staff.

[27:22] **Commercial Kitchen Options and Freezing Tradeoffs**

[事实] Ben says the logical next step may be a commercial kitchen, but cross-contamination is a challenge for Sarah’s model.

[事实] Sarah says several people have suggested she create a commercial kitchen for allergy-focused producers, but she is unsure how to fund it.

[事实] Guy says Sarah herself is central to the trust story because she personally has to eat this way.

[事实] Guy suggests testing frozen products where quality holds up, and Sarah says blondies and muffins freeze well but breads do not.

[29:26] **Bootstrap Further Before Taking on Bigger Risk**

[事实] Ben suggests Sarah and her husband honestly assess how much more output they can create with current resources before adding major costs.

[事实] Ben says grants are worth pursuing, but a dedicated facility would likely require tens of thousands or even around $100,000, not just $3,500.

[事实] Ben suggests looking at small-business loans, possibly through a local credit union, while recognizing that financial stress may replace overwork stress.

[推测] The advice is to grow deliberately until the pain of constrained capacity clearly justifies taking on larger operational and financial risk.

[33:00] **Bexley Natural Market’s Turnaround Problem**

[事实] John Groner calls about Bexley Natural Market, Central Ohio’s only cooperative grocery store, which has existed for 50 years.

[事实] John retired as a pediatric trauma surgeon in 2024 and joined the board when the store was facing financial trouble.

[事实] The co-op sells natural and organic products, buys from local vendors and farmers, and has Olipop near the cash register.

[事实] John says pre-pandemic sales were about $1.2 million to $1.4 million per year, current sales are about halfway back, and the store needs about 20% more sales to feel safe.

[35:38] **Why the Website Has Not Solved Sales**

[事实] John built a GraceCart website that automatically lists inventory, allows ordering and payment, and was intended to compete with Kroger’s grocery delivery.

[事实] John says the website has had little traction.

[事实] Guy cautions that marketing e-commerce is hard and expensive because customer acquisition costs are high.

[事实] Guy says the co-op’s advantage is not online scale, but exclusive products, curated items, and real local stories large chains cannot tell as well.

[38:25] **Bring the Community Story Into the Store**

[事实] Guy suggests hosting in-store events such as meet-the-farmer Saturdays and product tastings.

[事实] When John mentions an existing farmers market, Guy says the activation needs to happen inside the store.

[事实] Ben identifies three co-op levers: unique high-quality ingredients, community events, and a stronger in-store shopping experience.

[事实] Ben suggests using available outdoor space for recurring community moments and arranging the store so it feels pleasurable when shoppers enter.

[42:00] **Curation Over Assortment**

[事实] John asks how to balance local brands with national brands in a cooperative store.

[事实] Guy says the key is curation, comparing the idea to Trader Joe’s, where customers trust that products have been selected and tested.

[事实] Guy says the store should carry practical products people want to use, cook with, and eat, whether those products are local or national.

[推测] The recommendation is to make Bexley Natural Market feel like a trusted filter, not just another place with groceries.

[44:11] **Ben’s Closing Advice to Founders**

[事实] Guy asks Ben what advice he would give his earlier self as an entrepreneur.

[事实] Ben says founders often have instincts that others may not recognize clearly, and they need to learn how to listen to, calibrate, and trust those instincts.

[事实] Ben says a craftsman mentality can act as a grounding force for entrepreneurs.

[事实] The episode ends with a clip from Ben’s earlier Olipop interview about years of fermentation experiments and early product development.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode is most useful for early food, beverage, and retail founders because the advice stays close to packaging, sales channels, production capacity, and in-store behavior rather than abstract startup theory.

[推测] Its strongest thread is the repeated reminder that customers need a fast reason to care: better taste, fewer calories, safer food, trusted curation, or a better shopping experience. Deeper mission and ingredient education matter, but they work best after the basic purchase reason is clear.

[推测] The main limitation is that each caller gets only a short diagnostic window, so legal, financing, food safety, and operational details are not fully explored. The episode works better as strategic guidance than as a complete execution plan.
