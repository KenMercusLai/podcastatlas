# The Rest Is History: January 1 in Rome and Byzantium

## 概览

This episode uses New Year’s Day as a doorway into two Roman stories: the first from AD 69, when the Rhine legions helped trigger the Year of the Four Emperors, and the second from 1068, when Romanus IV became emperor of the Eastern Roman Empire.

The first half follows the collapse of Nero’s regime, Galba’s fragile legitimacy, and the mutiny among the Rhine armies that pushed Vitellius toward an imperial bid he did not seem eager to make.

The second half shifts to Byzantine history, tracing Romanus IV’s rise from accused rebel to emperor, then his defeat at Manzikert in 1071 and brutal downfall after a civil conflict inside the empire.

## 分段落总结

[00:29] **New Year’s Day and Roman Ritual**

[事实] The hosts open the episode as a New Year special for January 1, 2022.
[事实] Tom explains that January 1 mattered in Rome because it was the start of the civil year, when the consuls took office.
[事实] Under the Caesars, January 1 also involved rituals, sacrifices, auguries, and public affirmation of loyalty to the emperor.
[推测] The episode frames New Year’s Day not as a cheerful calendar marker, but as a date when Roman political legitimacy was publicly tested.

[02:01] **Nero’s Last New Year**

[事实] Tom describes January 1, AD 68, the final year of Nero’s life.
[事实] On that day, the keys to the temple could not be found, and Sporus gave Nero a ring showing Persephone being taken by Hades.
[事实] Later tradition remembered these details as ominous signs before Nero’s downfall.
[事实] Julius Vindex then raised rebellion, Galba declared himself emperor in Spain, Nero committed suicide, and the Senate accepted Galba.

[04:16] **Galba and the Rhine Legions**

[事实] On January 1, AD 69, Galba was emperor and performed the rituals that Nero had performed the year before.
[事实] The same loyalty rituals also took place in legionary bases across the empire, especially along the Rhine frontier.
[事实] The Rhine legions resented Galba because he had stopped them from plundering Gaul after they moved against Julius Vindex.
[事实] They also disliked Galba’s stern, uncharismatic style as a commander.
[推测] Galba’s problem was not only military discontent, but a broader failure to make soldiers feel that his authority was worth obeying.

[07:04] **Mutiny in Upper Germany**

[事实] In Lower Germany, the legions reluctantly swore loyalty to Galba.
[事实] In Upper Germany, Aulus Caecina stirred up mutiny at Mogontiacum, modern Mainz.
[事实] Caecina had previously supported Galba but faced trial after corrupt practices were uncovered.
[事实] The mutinous soldiers smashed Galba’s statues and swore loyalty to the Senate and people of Rome rather than to an individual emperor.
[推测] The oath to the Senate and people functioned less as a settled constitutional position than as a temporary gesture while the soldiers searched for a replacement emperor.

[10:51] **Vitellius as Reluctant Candidate**

[事实] The mutineers looked beyond Caecina because he was too young and junior to be a credible Caesar.
[事实] The governor of Upper Germany, Hordeonius Flaccus, is presented as too ineffective to be a serious choice.
[事实] The governor of Lower Germany, Vitellius, had distinguished pedigree and became the preferred candidate.
[事实] Vitellius hesitated when urged to become Caesar and was said to fear a prophecy that military command would end disastrously for him.
[事实] Once news arrived that Galba had been assassinated and replaced by Otho, the Rhine commanders were already marching south toward Italy.

[13:44] **The Year of the Four Emperors**

[事实] Tom identifies the unfolding conflict as the Year of the Four Emperors.
[事实] Galba was murdered by Otho, Otho was defeated by Vitellius, and Vitellius was defeated and killed by supporters of Vespasian.
[事实] Vespasian became emperor by the end of the year and founded the Flavian dynasty.
[事实] Tom links this period to his book project, which begins with Nero’s death and continues through Vespasian, Titus, Domitian, Nerva, Trajan, and Hadrian.
[事实] He says the period is framed by two Jewish revolts, including the destruction of the Temple and later changes in Judea.

[15:39] **A Turn to Byzantium**

[事实] After the break, Dominic introduces his long-standing interest in Byzantine history.
[事实] He notes that the people now called Byzantines understood themselves as Romans.
[事实] The second story is set on January 1, 1068, when Romanus IV became emperor.
[事实] The hosts briefly discuss that the Byzantine Empire had declined and recovered over earlier centuries.
[推测] The episode deliberately pairs the western imperial crisis of AD 69 with a later Eastern Roman crisis to show continuity in Roman political culture.

[17:45] **The Eastern Roman Empire in the Eleventh Century**

[事实] Dominic says the empire was largely reduced to the southern Balkans and Asia Minor, but remained rich, powerful, and sophisticated.
[事实] Romanus Diogenes came from a wealthy, well-connected military aristocratic family in Anatolia.
[事实] He had served on the Danubian frontier and was a popular and successful general.
[事实] After Emperor Constantine X died in 1067, his widow Eudokia acted as regent for his young sons.

[20:06] **Romanus Marries Eudokia and Becomes Emperor**

[事实] Romanus appears to have considered or attempted a coup, but the plan failed.
[事实] He was taken to Constantinople before Eudokia and expected punishment such as execution, blinding, or forced monastic life.
[事实] Instead, Eudokia decided to marry him, and he became emperor.
[推测] Dominic presents this as an extraordinary reversal of fortune, though later events make the promotion look disastrous in hindsight.

[21:35] **Manzikert and Capture by the Seljuks**

[事实] Romanus ruled for about three years before leading the Byzantine army to Manzikert in 1071.
[事实] His opponent was the Seljuk Turkish ruler Alp Arslan.
[事实] The battle went badly for Romanus after military confusion and the splitting of his forces.
[事实] Romanus was captured and brought before Alp Arslan.
[事实] Alp Arslan first symbolically humiliated him, then treated him well during eight days in the Turkish camp.

[23:18] **Romanus’s Return and Blinding**

[事实] Alp Arslan released Romanus after arranging a treaty and ransom.
[事实] When Romanus returned, members of the imperial family had moved against him.
[事实] Romanus eventually surrendered after being besieged in Adana and was promised safety.
[事实] That promise was broken, and he was blinded by an inexperienced executioner who needed three attempts.
[事实] Romanus was exiled near the Sea of Marmara, suffered infected eyes, and died after receiving a hostile letter from Michael Psellos.

[25:07] **Manzikert’s Long Consequences**

[事实] The hosts describe Manzikert as a landmark in the long decline of the Byzantine Empire.
[事实] They stress that the battle itself was not the only issue; the civil war that followed allowed Turkish infiltration into Anatolia.
[事实] The Byzantines never fully recovered their Anatolian heartland, though the empire survived for nearly another 400 years.
[事实] The hosts also mention the 1204 capture of Constantinople by the Crusaders as another major catastrophe.
[推测] The discussion treats Manzikert as a symbolic turning point more than a single instant of collapse.

[26:31] **Counterfactual Empires and Closing Banter**

[事实] The hosts speculate humorously about whether the world would be better if the Byzantine Empire had survived.
[事实] They joke about imperial courts, bishops, generals, stylites, and possible careers in a surviving Eastern Roman world.
[事实] The episode ends with New Year greetings and a promise to return the next day.
[推测] The closing banter reinforces the show’s style: serious historical narrative mixed with playful counterfactual imagination.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its ability to turn one calendar date into two compact studies of imperial legitimacy, military power, and political collapse across very different Roman worlds.

[推测] Its strongest moments are the connections between ritual and power: January 1 becomes a public test of loyalty in AD 69 and an ironic starting point for Romanus IV’s disastrous imperial career in 1068.

[推测] The limitation is that both stories move quickly, especially the Byzantine section, so listeners unfamiliar with Galba, Vitellius, Manzikert, or Byzantine court politics may need outside context.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who enjoy Roman history, Byzantine history, political drama, and historically informed conversation with a comic tone.
