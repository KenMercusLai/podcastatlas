# Advice Line with Daymond John of FUBU

## 概览

This episode features Guy Raz speaking with Daymond John, founder of FUBU and longtime Shark Tank judge, about what makes founders compelling: a clear story, deep customer understanding, command of the numbers, and the resilience to endure rejection.

The advice calls focus on three businesses facing growth and positioning questions: a Chicago salsa brand wondering whether to move beyond farmers markets, an Australian protein bar brand debating whether to keep its jiu-jitsu origin story, and a social enterprise kitchen-linen company trying to grow B2B sales.

Across the episode, Daymond and Guy repeatedly emphasize focused storytelling, gradual expansion, customer feedback, mission alignment, and the need to prove value before chasing large-scale retail or enterprise accounts.

## 分段落总结

[00:19] **Episode Setup And Guest Introduction**

[事实] Guy Raz introduces the Advice Line format as a place where founders call in with business challenges.
[事实] Daymond John is introduced as the founder of FUBU, a Shark Tank judge, speaker, and author.
[事实] Guy references Daymond’s earlier How I Built This episode about building FUBU after working at Red Lobster.

[01:52] **What Daymond Looks For In A Pitch**

[事实] Daymond says he first asks whether he can add value by reducing costs or increasing sales.
[事实] He looks for founders who created something from personal pain or from a desire to bring customers joy.
[事实] He wants founders to know their market, audience, and numbers.
[事实] Daymond says founders should be able to answer “why me, why now, and why this.”

[04:04] **Traits Of Successful Entrepreneurs**

[事实] Daymond says successful entrepreneurs need to be strong storytellers.
[事实] He says they need confidence, vulnerability, and a focus on delivering value to customers.
[事实] He identifies a high tolerance for pain as one of the most important entrepreneurial traits.
[推测] Daymond’s framework suggests that resilience matters as much as product insight because founders must survive repeated doubt and rejection.

[05:04] **Why Founder Stories Matter**

[事实] Daymond asks Guy why he began exploring the minds and stories of founders years before CEOs became widely treated as public figures.
[事实] Guy says business-building stories contain drama, failure, loss, joy, and high stakes.
[事实] Guy says the show tries to share collective wisdom so other founders can learn how people navigated crises and mistakes.
[推测] This exchange frames entrepreneurship as both a practical discipline and a narrative craft.

[09:38] **Sabor A Mexico’s Salsa Business**

[事实] Christopher Navarro calls from Chicago and says he co-founded Sabor a Mexico Salsas with his parents, Marlene and Sergio.
[事实] The company sells authentic family-recipe salsas and has about eight salsas plus one pickled product.
[事实] The business officially started in 2024, with recipes rooted in his grandmother’s taqueria in Mexico.
[事实] Its main sales channel is farmers markets, and it expects about $340,000 in sales this year, roughly double the previous year.

[11:54] **When To Move Beyond Farmers Markets**

[事实] Christopher asks how to know when the business has outgrown the farmers market model and whether to move into retail.
[事实] Daymond warns that retail can mean much more work for less reward because of buyers, vendor requirements, shipping, pricing pressure, and large-company criteria.
[事实] Daymond advises testing small mom-and-pop stores and direct-to-consumer selling instead of jumping straight into big retail.
[事实] Guy agrees and suggests testing five or six small Chicago food stores for six months to a year before pitching larger regional grocers.
[推测] The advice favors staged channel expansion while preserving the high-margin, feedback-rich customer base that farmers markets provide.

[15:52] **Why Small Retailers Can Build The Brand**

[事实] Daymond says mom-and-pop stores can explain a local founder’s story directly to customers in a way large stores often cannot.
[事实] He says small stores helped build FUBU by telling customers about the founders and the quality of the goods.
[事实] Guy says Sabor a Mexico has a strong local family story tied to taquerias in Mexico and recipes brought to Chicago.
[事实] Guy recommends sampling in stores and speaking directly with customers to understand how the product works in retail.

[20:31] **Raised Nutrition’s Protein Bar Positioning**

[事实] Darcy calls from Ballina, New South Wales, Australia, and says he and his wife Jess founded Raised Nutrition.
[事实] Raised makes whole-food protein bars without artificial flavors, sweeteners, preservatives, fillers, or natural flavors.
[事实] Darcy says the brand was inspired partly by RXBar and by his and Jess’s experience training Brazilian jiu-jitsu.
[事实] The business has been live for about seven months, with about $18,000 in sales in the last 30 days, mostly direct-to-consumer.

[22:37] **Whether To Keep The Jiu-Jitsu Origin Story**

[事实] Darcy says most customer uptake is coming from outside jiu-jitsu, especially from health and wellness consumers who read ingredient labels.
[事实] He asks whether the brand should keep the jiu-jitsu story or move away from it to avoid alienating broader customers.
[事实] Daymond advises staying with the jiu-jitsu origin story because it explains why the product was created.
[事实] Guy compares this to Yeti starting with fishermen, Lululemon starting with yoga, and RXBar starting with CrossFit.
[推测] The core advice is to use a niche tribe as a launch engine, while allowing the product to expand naturally into adjacent audiences.

[26:03] **Ingredients, Collagen, And The Australian Angle**

[事实] Guy asks whether there is something specifically Australian about the product or ingredients.
[事实] Darcy says Raised uses Australian grass-fed collagen, Australian-grown fruit where possible, pumpkin seed protein, and organic sprouted brown rice protein.
[事实] Darcy says collagen is the “hero protein,” with about five grams per bar.
[事实] Daymond and Guy suggest highlighting collagen more in marketing and packaging.
[推测] The ingredient story may help Raised appeal beyond jiu-jitsu because it connects the brand to recovery, clean-label nutrition, and Australian sourcing.

[28:33] **Cooks Who Feed’s Social Enterprise Model**

[事实] Seema Sanghavi calls from the Niagara region outside Toronto and says her company is Cooks Who Feed.
[事实] The company ethically produces kitchen linens with a meal give-back model and has provided around two million meals through sales.
[事实] It is a for-profit social enterprise with its own production facility north of Delhi.
[事实] The company hires and trains women from surrounding villages as seamstresses and provides a safe, fair workplace.

[30:04] **B2B Focus And Custom Linens**

[事实] Cooks Who Feed sells products including chef aprons, kids’ aprons, tea towels, grocery totes, wine totes, linen napkins, server aprons, bar aprons, chef coats, and tablecloths.
[事实] Seema says about 75% of sales are currently B2B.
[事实] Customers include restaurant groups, Celebrity Cruises, and culinary schools.
[事实] The company offers extensive customization, including colors, logos, pocket styles, straps, and in-house embroidery.

[30:54] **Convincing Businesses To Switch Vendors**

[事实] Seema asks how to convince B2B customers to switch vendors.
[事实] Daymond says customers often switch when their current vendor starts to feel risky, not simply because a new vendor is better.
[事实] He advises creating an “undeniable package” that lets prospects test Cooks Who Feed on a small scale.
[事实] Daymond says the company should clearly communicate both product value and the meal give-back impact.

[33:05] **Quality, Value, And Mission Alignment**

[事实] Guy says consumers often choose products based on quality and value, even when a social mission is present.
[事实] He asks whether Seema has identified businesses whose own values align with sustainability and social responsibility.
[事实] Seema says she has focused on higher-end businesses where sustainability is already part of the conversation.
[事实] She says Cooks Who Feed can often meet or beat existing prices while adding impact.

[35:18] **Making The Client The Hero**

[事实] Seema says Celebrity Cruises became a major client after Cooks Who Feed helped them create aprons made from recycled plastic bottles with a meal give-back story.
[事实] She says her pitch is about helping the client look better and fit their own goals.
[事实] Guy suggests organizations such as José Andrés and World Central Kitchen could align with the company’s meal-focused mission.
[推测] The strongest B2B pitch is not just “buy from us,” but “this purchase helps you tell a better version of your own brand story.”

[36:27] **Referrals, Direct-To-Consumer, And Live Selling**

[事实] Guy says Cooks Who Feed may benefit from hard referrals from existing clients to larger organizations.
[事实] Seema says she has started working with one Relais & Châteaux location in Canada and hopes to use that relationship to reach other locations.
[事实] Guy cautions against discounting direct-to-consumer sales.
[事实] Daymond recommends live selling on platforms such as Whatnot and TikTok Shops because it provides direct feedback from consumers.
[事实] Guy advises showing images and videos of the aprons being made, dyed, embroidered, and produced.

[39:40] **Daymond’s Advice To His Younger Self**

[事实] Guy asks Daymond what advice he would give himself when he was working at Red Lobster.
[事实] Daymond says he would emphasize financial intelligence and learning how money works.
[事实] He says people should not wait for sudden luck or a big breakthrough and should instead study money and build wealth slowly.
[事实] A clip from Daymond’s earlier episode notes that he once returned to New York with $300,000 in orders and was turned down by 27 banks.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is useful because the advice stays grounded in specific founder decisions: when to expand channels, how long to hold onto an origin story, and how to make a social mission commercially persuasive. Daymond’s strongest recurring point is that story only works when paired with customer value, numbers, and operational discipline.

The best moments come when the hosts push founders away from premature scale. Sabor a Mexico is advised to test small retailers before big chains, Raised Nutrition is encouraged to keep its niche community before broadening, and Cooks Who Feed is guided toward low-risk B2B trials and stronger customer referrals.

[推测] The episode is especially suited for early-stage consumer-product founders who already have traction but are unsure how to grow without losing margins, focus, or authenticity. Its limitation is that the advice is directional rather than deeply tactical; listeners looking for detailed financial models, retail terms, or sales scripts will need to build those next steps themselves.
