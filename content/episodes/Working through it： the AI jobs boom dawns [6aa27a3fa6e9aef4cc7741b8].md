# The Intelligence: Working through it, the AI jobs boom dawns

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* examines whether artificial intelligence is destroying jobs or creating them. The discussion argues that, at least in the American labour market data cited, the feared broad AI jobs apocalypse has not appeared; instead, AI-related investment has coincided with strong job creation in infrastructure, data centres and professional technical roles.

The episode then turns to China’s persistent food-safety problems, using a recent cabbage-formaldehyde scandal to explain why regulation remains difficult in a vast, fragmented food system. It highlights the tension between safety enforcement, low food costs, local economic incentives and recurring scapegoating.

The final segment explores a strange smuggling trend: queen ants trafficked from Kenya to collectors abroad. The discussion links the trade to pandemic-era ant keeping, social media demand, black-market prices and ecological risks for African savannas.

## 分段落总结

[01:55] **Technology, anxiety and the AI jobs question**

[事实] The episode opens the AI segment by comparing current fears about AI to past anxieties caused by mechanical looms, cars, steam engines, telephone exchanges and typewriters.
[事实] The framing is that new technologies often trigger job-loss fears before industries transform and new jobs requiring new skills emerge.
[推测] The hosts use this historical setup to suggest that AI may follow a familiar pattern of disruption plus job creation, rather than only mass unemployment.

[02:34] **US labour data challenges the “AI jobs apocalypse” narrative**

[事实] Alex Domash says the American Bureau of Labor Statistics reported 162,000 jobs created in August, exceeding expectations.
[事实] He says the unemployment rate remained very low, and youth unemployment also stayed quite low despite fears about AI’s effect on young workers.
[事实] Domash says the overall US jobs numbers cut across the idea that AI is already having a significantly negative effect on employment.
[推测] The segment treats current labour-market data as an early check on AI panic, while avoiding the claim that all job growth is caused by AI.

[03:20] **AI-related job losses are outweighed by job creation in the estimate discussed**

[事实] Domash says companies have attributed about 200,000 layoffs over the past few years to artificial intelligence.
[事实] He says that figure is small relative to overall labour-market churn.
[事实] The Economist’s back-of-the-envelope estimate is that around 1 million jobs have been created because of the AI boom.
[事实] A large share of new demand comes from data-centre infrastructure, including chips, construction, cooling systems, racks and power.

[04:29] **Data centres are creating demand for workers without bachelor’s degrees**

[事实] Domash says the data-centre build-out has increased demand for electricians, HVAC installers, utilities workers and technicians who operate and maintain servers.
[事实] He estimates that this infrastructure-related boom has created around 350,000 new jobs in America.
[事实] The calculation focused on five occupations closely tied to the infrastructure boom and compared their growth with the rest of construction and manufacturing.
[事实] Employment in those five categories rose by roughly 360,000 jobs since 2023, and wage gains in related occupations point to labour shortages.

[06:26] **AI is also creating white-collar professional jobs**

[事实] Domash says AI affects work by making some jobs more productive and creating new classes of work.
[事实] He identifies software developers, engineers, data scientists, and mathematical and scientific occupations as roles close to the AI boom.
[事实] The Economist estimates these occupations have increased by almost 750,000 jobs since 2023 relative to a no-AI-boom counterfactual.
[事实] Gad Levanon at the Burning Glass Institute estimates that about 1% of US professional jobs can now be classified as AI jobs, corresponding to roughly 1 million jobs.

[07:41] **Evidence is mixed in jobs once seen as highly vulnerable to AI**

[事实] Domash says employment among paralegals and financial analysts has shown quite strong growth, despite predictions that they would be early AI casualties.
[事实] He says translator job growth is roughly flat.
[事实] He argues that AI can increase productivity, lower the cost of services and raise demand, making it an augmenting technology rather than only a displacing one.
[事实] Customer service representatives and office and administrative assistants have seen job growth decline, with an acceleration over the past couple of years.

[09:22] **Historical precedent suggests AI will hurt some roles but create others**

[事实] Domash says economic historians have long argued that general-purpose technologies such as the internet, computers and electrification hurt some occupations while creating many new jobs.
[事实] He says the history of the US labour market shows that every few decades many people work in jobs that did not exist decades earlier.
[推测] The implied conclusion is that policymakers should focus less on a single apocalypse scenario and more on managing transition, retraining and uneven effects across occupations.

[11:43] **China’s cabbage-formaldehyde scandal**

[事实] The China segment begins with videos posted by a blogger showing truckers in Kangbao dipping freshly harvested cabbages into a container of formaldehyde.
[事实] Gabriel Crossley says formaldehyde keeps cabbages looking fresh but can cause cancer.
[事实] The videos caused panic in China and abroad, and three people had been detained on criminal charges.
[事实] Authorities in Kangbao set up emergency checkpoints to test cabbages leaving the county.

[12:42] **Food safety remains a persistent concern in China**

[事实] Crossley says food safety is still a big concern in China.
[事实] He refers to the 2008 tainted milk powder scandal, which sickened 300,000 children and killed several.
[事实] He says banned pesticides appeared in several cases during the summer.
[事实] Xi Jinping has tied food-safety problems to the Communist Party’s legitimacy, saying trust in the party is linked to its ability to guarantee food safety.

[13:29] **China’s food system is difficult to monitor**

[事实] Crossley says keeping food safe is difficult anywhere, but China’s system is especially vast.
[事实] He says China has a few big farms, 160 million small farmers, and many truckers, traders and logistics companies moving food around.
[事实] Consumers often buy from small wet markets and eat at small restaurants.
[推测] The scale and fragmentation described make enforcement harder because regulators must monitor many small actors across production, transport and retail.

[14:08] **Technology and cold-chain expansion are part of the response**

[事实] Crossley says some restaurants in China have livestreams of their kitchens so customers can monitor hygiene practices.
[事实] Larger companies are using more sophisticated tracking systems to trace food from farm to table.
[事实] The government is expanding refrigerated transport and storage systems.
[事实] These measures raise costs, and Crossley says many people in China either do not want to pay or cannot pay much more for groceries.

[14:49] **Regulatory and incentive problems limit enforcement**

[事实] Crossley says part of the issue is regulatory, and part is a competitive corporate culture where speed can lead to rushed checks.
[事实] The government is trying to encourage workers to report problems directly to regulators rather than bosses.
[事实] He says the government also has conflicting incentives: it wants to protect local jobs and grow local industries.
[事实] In agricultural areas, officials may not want everything shut down because of a food-safety issue.

[15:26] **The latest scandal may not produce structural change**

[事实] Crossley says he is afraid the cabbage scandal probably will not change much.
[事实] He says formaldehyde scandals have happened before in China, with the earliest reported one in 2012 and several since.
[事实] He says the usual pattern is that scapegoats are found and then people move on because structural issues are hard to tackle.
[事实] When Crossley visited Kangbao, local officials avoided answering questions about cabbages.

[16:44] **Queen-ant smuggling from Kenya**

[事实] The final segment begins with a smuggling case at Nairobi airport, where police found more than 2,000 queen ants hidden in a man’s luggage.
[事实] Carla Superana says the ants were packed in individual plastic tubes plugged with damp cotton to keep them alive for weeks.
[事实] Their final destination was China.
[事实] She says cases like this are becoming more common in Kenya.

[17:27] **Why people smuggle ants**

[事实] Superana says people increasingly keep ants as pets and are willing to pay significant amounts for them.
[事实] Some species can sell for up to $200 each on the black market.
[事实] Kenya is a hotspot because it has exotic ants that are hard to find elsewhere and already has established wildlife-smuggling routes.
[推测] Existing smuggling infrastructure appears to lower the barrier for traffickers to move from other wildlife into ants.

[18:08] **Ant keeping grew from a niche hobby into a global phenomenon**

[事实] Superana says ant keeping became popular during the pandemic as people looked for indoor hobbies.
[事实] Enthusiasts enjoy creating colonies and watching ants forage and interact.
[事实] Social media helped expand the hobby, with YouTube channels such as Ants Canada having millions of subscribers.
[事实] She says streamers narrate ant-colony videos almost like nature documentaries.

[18:53] **Legal supply cannot satisfy demand for rare species**

[事实] Superana says much ant keeping is legal, and licensed shops sell many species.
[事实] The problem is that demand for rare ants, including species found in Kenya, is starting to exceed legal supply.
[事实] African harvester ants from the trafficking case are sought after because they are large, strikingly coloured and display complex social behaviours.
[事实] A scientist she spoke to described them as “the tigers of the ant world.”

[19:32] **Ant behaviour makes them attractive to collectors**

[事实] Superana says ants have strict hierarchies, and battles can emerge within those hierarchies.
[事实] She says ants are smart and may try to escape colonies using elaborate plans.
[推测] The appeal for collectors comes not only from rarity and appearance but from observing complex colony drama over time.

[19:57] **Removing queen ants can damage ecosystems**

[事实] Superana says the ants are not going extinct, but traffickers target such large numbers of queens that ecosystems could be damaged.
[事实] Queens are the only colony members that reproduce, so removing one can wipe out a whole colony and the thousands of workers she would have produced.
[事实] These ants matter for African savannas because their nests help hydrate soil.
[事实] They are also an important food source for animals such as pangolins.

[20:43] **Education may matter more than airport inspections**

[事实] Superana says ants are so small and hard to catch that increased airport inspections would struggle to solve the problem.
[事实] She argues collectors need to understand where their ants come from and what the trade does to ecosystems.
[事实] She says smugglers will keep finding ways to move ants as long as people are willing to pay hundreds of dollars for a queen ant.
[推测] The segment frames demand reduction and buyer awareness as more realistic tools than enforcement alone.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strongest segment is the AI jobs discussion because it grounds a high-anxiety public debate in concrete labour-market figures, occupational categories and counterfactual estimates. It is careful to acknowledge AI-related layoffs while showing that, in the data discussed, job creation in infrastructure and technical roles is larger.

The China food-safety segment is valuable because it moves beyond one scandal and explains the structural reasons such problems persist: a huge fragmented supply chain, cost sensitivity, weak incentives and local political pressures. Its limitation is that it does not offer much evidence on how effective specific reforms have been.

The ant-smuggling segment works as a memorable closing story: unusual on the surface, but tied to real issues of social-media-driven demand, black markets and ecological damage. [推测] The episode is best suited to listeners who want concise global business, policy and science-adjacent stories rather than a deep single-topic investigation.
