# The Nine Adaptations: How to Assess Your Own Fitness — Huberman Lab Essentials with Dr. Andy Galpin

## 概览

This is a Huberman Lab Essentials conversation between Andrew Huberman and Dr. Andy Galpin on how to evaluate your own fitness rather than guess at it. Galpin frames exercise goals as falling into two buckets, appearance and functionality, and argues that the real question is knowing which area to prioritize, why a goal isn't being reached, and how to reach it more effectively while keeping it for a long time.

His organizing device is nine physiological adaptations that exercise can develop: skill/technique, speed, power, strength, muscle hypertrophy, muscular endurance, anaerobic capacity, maximal aerobic capacity, and long-duration endurance. The bulk of the episode walks through each one and supplies a practical way to test it, alternating between gold-standard lab or specialist methods and cheap do-it-yourself protocols.

The closing portion covers logistics: run the full battery roughly once a year as a "testing week," spread across about three days, with non-fatiguing tests first and maximal, fatiguing tests last. Galpin emphasizes that you don't need to be optimal in all nine areas, only free of severe "performance anchors," so you can keep optimizing the one or two capacities you actually care about.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Framing: Two Goals and One Question**

- [事实] Galpin says people generally have two major goals with exercise: appearance (wanting to be big, lean, or not too big) and functionality (strength, mobility, energy throughout the day), and that the definition of functionality differs per person.
- [事实] He says the aim is to understand where your exercise training needs to go so you can be as fit and healthy as you want now, and maintain it for a long period of time.
- [推测] The framing sets up assessment as a prioritization tool for people who are not reaching their goals and don't know which capacity is holding them back.

[02:32] **The Nine Physiological Adaptations**

- [事实] Galpin buckets exercise adaptations into nine areas: skill or technique, speed, power (speed multiplied by force), force or strength, muscle hypertrophy, muscular endurance, anaerobic capacity, maximal aerobic capacity, and long duration.
- [事实] Strength is defined as the maximum force produced one time; muscular endurance is how many reps you can do, typically in a five-to-fifty repetition range, and is almost always local to one or a few muscle groups rather than a global limitation.
- [事实] Anaerobic capacity is a total physiological limitation reached with roughly 30 to 45 seconds, possibly up to 120 seconds, of all-out work; maximal aerobic capacity is reached over roughly 8 to 15 minutes and involves true VO2 max; long duration is sustaining submaximal work with no breaks.
- [事实] Hypertrophy is the first of the nine described as an appearance rather than a functional outcome.
- [推测] The list is presented as a diagnostic checklist, so a weakness in one capacity can be identified instead of trained blindly.

[07:12] **Assessing Movement Skill**

- [事实] The gold standard is a highly qualified physical therapist or movement specialist; the do-it-yourself version goes joint by joint through shoulder, elbow, low back, hip, knee, and ankle, using representative movements such as an upper body press, upper body pull, lower body press, and lower body pull (push-up, pull-up or bent row, squat, deadlift).
- [事实] Record a frontal and a side view, do three to 10 slow controlled reps per angle, and look for four things: symmetry, stability, awareness, and full range of motion.
- [事实] Galpin's scoring is 0, 1, or 3: 0 means don't do the exercise because of high acute risk, 1 means a minor flaw that requires caution with load and volume, and 3 means the movement is close enough to good to proceed on a reasonable protocol.
- [事实] He notes that many apparent technique problems are simply awareness issues, citing students who corrected squat technique immediately once told their heels should stay on the ground.
- [推测] Movement screening functions as a gate that should be cleared before heavy or maximal strength testing is attempted.

[11:12] **Speed and Power**

- [事实] Galpin says pure speed generally is not necessary to test for most people; it matters mainly for high-performance athletes, where a 40-yard dash or a velocity transducer on a barbell can be used.
- [事实] Power is assessed with a simple broad jump from a standing position with no running approach, measuring from the start line to the furthest point back where the back heel lands.
- [事实] A basic benchmark is jumping your own height; that target ratchets down about 15% for females, and elite athletic reference points are much higher, such as six-foot NFL players jumping nine to 11 feet.
- [事实] Galpin says the point is to spot red flags, not to optimize this particular test.
- [推测] Because the broad jump is free, quick, and non-fatiguing, it is well suited to being placed early on a testing day.

[12:56] **Assessing Strength**

- [事实] Grip strength can be tested with a hand grip dynamometer costing roughly $20 to $100, using both hands: a minimum of about 40 kilograms for men and 35 kilograms for women, ideally above 60 kilograms, with less than 10% variation between hands; Galpin notes the non-dominant hand is often stronger.
- [事实] A dead hang on a bar should be possible for a minimum of 30 seconds, with 30 to 50 seconds described as good and above 60 seconds as generally satisfying, though the standard scales imperfectly for very heavy or very light people.
- [事实] A leg extension test is preferred over a back squat for average people because it is standardized and not technically demanding; the target is body weight, dropping about 10% per decade after age 40, and repetition conversion equations can estimate a one-rep max if fewer than about five reps are used.
- [事实] An alternative is a front squat or goblet squat hold: hold about half your body weight at the bottom position for roughly 45 seconds, with beginners starting at one-third of body weight for 30 seconds; it also reflects position, core strength, and low back stability.
- [事实] Two caveats are given: do not test to maximum if you are not comfortable with your technique, and standardize your warm-up if you plan to retest and compare results.

[19:47] **Muscle Size and Body Composition**

- [事实] The aesthetic portion of hypertrophy is entirely a personal choice; the part that matters for health is whether you have a sufficient amount of muscle, assessed through body composition testing such as a DEXA scan, described as the gold standard.
- [事实] The number to watch is FFMI, or fat-free mass index. Targets are roughly 20 or higher for men and 18 for women; above 24 to 25 for a man is a lot of muscle mass, assuming reasonable leanness (roughly sub-30% body fat for men and sub-35% for women).
- [事实] Men below 17 and women below 15 are described as being in an area of pretty severe physiological detriment from insufficient muscle.
- [事实] FFMI can be calculated from total body weight, body fat percentage, and height using standard online calculators that also provide grading rubrics.
- [推测] This section separates optional aesthetic preference from a health floor for muscle mass, with the floor treated as non-negotiable.

[21:51] **Muscular Endurance**

- [事实] Standards offered include a 60-second front plank, a 45-second side plank, and 25-plus consecutive push-ups for men and 15-plus for women, with five to 15 described as a marginal range and below five as generally a problem.
- [事实] An alternative is to take the strength test you already performed, load it to 75%, and do as many reps as possible; more than eight reps is the target, and below eight indicates a muscular endurance problem.
- [事实] Push-up form should stay consistent between tests, with full elbow lockout at the top and a full or near-full chest touch at the bottom, so progress can be tracked.
- [推测] Reusing the strength test at a lighter load is presented as an efficient way to get two measurements from one setup.

[23:21] **Anaerobic Capacity**

- [事实] Laboratory options include a Wingate test of 30 seconds maximal work and a Bosco protocol of 60 seconds of maximal vertical jumps on a force plate; field versions can be sprinting, an air bike, or a rower, generally 30 to 45 seconds and up to a minute.
- [事实] Galpin avoids modalities such as kettlebell swings for this test because too many other variables are involved; the effort needs to be maximal without technical constraints.
- [事实] No universal standards exist because the modality changes the result, so what matters is whether you can complete the test and how awful you feel afterwards.
- [事实] The preferred practical metric is heart rate recovery: reach a maximum heart rate (rough guide of 220 minus your age) and track recovery at about half a beat per second, roughly 30 beats within the first minute, 60 by two minutes, with the three-minute value halving again.
- [推测] A recovery curve worse than those markers is treated as evidence of a problem in anaerobic or cardiovascular capacity, and Galpin notes the 220-minus-age figure is extremely generic and not an indicator of fitness by itself.

[26:10] **Maximal Aerobic Capacity (VO2 Max)**

- [事实] The gold standard is a laboratory VO2 max test with a mask and gas collection following a specific protocol for a true maximum test.
- [事实] Without lab access, the 12-minute Cooper test (run as far as possible and enter the distance into an online calculator) or a one-mile walk test (submaximal, requiring a stopwatch or ideally a heart rate monitor, with time and ending heart rate entered into a calculator) give estimated VO2 max values.
- [事实] Reference values given are a minimum of about 35 milliliters per kilogram per minute for men and 30 for women; Galpin wants to see men above 50 and is not really satisfied below 55, and wants women above 50.
- [事实] The one-mile walk test is recommended for people who can't or don't want to run hard, including those with knee or back pain.
- [推测] VO2 max is positioned as a central health marker alongside strength and muscle mass rather than as a performance-only metric.

[29:11] **Long-Duration Steady-State Endurance**

- [事实] The target is maintaining consistent work output for over 20 minutes with no breaks, no intervals, and no downtime, with 20 to 60 minutes described as typical and 30 straight minutes as a good practical marker.
- [事实] Ideally the work is done at a non-walking pace and with nasal breathing only.
- [事实] Walking doesn't really count unless you are very unfit, in which case walking 30 minutes without a break being a challenge is itself the finding.
- [事实] Galpin suggests picking something that fits your lifestyle, such as a loop around your house, and simply testing whether you can keep working without stopping.
- [推测] This is the least standardized of the nine tests, deliberately, so that the assessment stays accessible rather than prescriptive.

[30:16] **How Often and In What Order to Test**

- [事实] Galpin recommends running the full battery once a year, framing it as a testing week and comparing it to going to a physician for annual blood work; he likes placing it at the beginning of the year or whenever training changes.
- [事实] A three-day split is probably best, though it could technically be compressed into two days. Non-fatiguing tests such as body composition and FFMI go first, after 48 hours of rest and no hard exercise the day before, because acute exercise affects inflammation and water storage.
- [事实] Movement and skill tests should be done when fresh; skill, maximum strength, and power go at the beginning of a day, and fatiguing tests go at the end. Power, strength, and muscular endurance can be combined in one day, anaerobic and long-duration tests can be paired or separated, and VO2 max generally needs its own day.
- [事实] You don't need to be optimal in all nine areas to be, in his words, optimally healthy; the goal is to eliminate severe performance anchors that limit you, then pursue optimization in the areas you have a specific passion for.
- [推测] The testing week is framed as motivational rather than disruptive, since Galpin predicts it will feel like a substantial amount of work rather than a week off training.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode's main value is that it converts a vague question, "am I fit?", into a concrete checklist with numbers attached. Most listeners can act on the do-it-yourself versions immediately: a broad jump against their height, a dead hang, a goblet squat hold, a plank, push-ups, a heart rate recovery curve, a 12-minute Cooper test or one-mile walk. Pairing each adaptation with both a gold-standard method and a low-cost substitute makes the material unusually usable.

Its strongest feature is the honesty about test quality. Galpin repeatedly flags which numbers are crude (220 minus age), which tests depend on body size or modality, and which require technical proficiency before a maximal effort is safe. The recommendation to standardize warm-ups and retest conditions, and the refusal to declare universal standards for anaerobic work, are the kind of details that keep self-testing from producing false confidence.

The obvious limitation is scope: this is an assessment episode, and no training protocols are delivered, with Galpin explicitly deferring speed, strength, and hypertrophy programming to a later conversation. Several capacities, especially anaerobic capacity and true VO2 max, can only be estimated outside a lab, and the episode includes sponsor segments that are unrelated to the science. The transcript also ends with lines that do not belong to the discussion, so the very end of the recording should not be read as content.

This suits general exercisers, self-quantifiers, coaches working with non-elite clients, and anyone who has plateaued and wants to know which capacity to work on next. Listeners looking for a training program should treat this as the prerequisite step — the measurement pass that tells them where to aim — and expect the protocols to come later. People wanting a single definitive fitness score will be disappointed, since the whole message is that you are checking nine separate systems for severe anchors, not chasing one number.
