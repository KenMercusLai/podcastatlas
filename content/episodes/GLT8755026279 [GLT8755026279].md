# Coronations from Hanoverian Disorder to Modern Sacral Spectacle

## 概览

This episode traces British coronations from the Hanoverian succession through the impending coronation of Charles III, arguing that the ceremony repeatedly swings between sacred ritual, political theatre, public disorder, and comic administrative failure.

The hosts focus especially on the Hanoverian and early modern royal ceremonies: George I’s contested Protestant succession, George II’s comparatively successful musical coronation, George III’s farcical mismanagement, and George IV’s spectacular but vulgar extravagance.

In the second half, the discussion moves from William IV’s pared-down coronation to Victoria’s mass-tourism event, Edward VII’s imperial turn, George V’s Delhi Durbar, George VI’s radio-age coronation, and Elizabeth II’s television coronation. The closing debate asks whether the sacral meaning of coronation can still resonate in a less Christian and more sceptical Britain.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **George IV as Spectacle**

[事实] The episode opens with Benjamin Robert Haydon’s description of George IV entering Westminster Abbey in satin, feathers, diamonds, and theatrical splendour.
[事实] The hosts describe George IV’s coronation as splendid, vulgar, shambolic, and extraordinarily expensive.
[推测] The opening frames the whole episode’s theme: coronations are both solemn rites and opportunities for absurd public theatre.

[01:55] **George I and the Hanoverian Succession**

[事实] George I became king after Queen Anne died on 1 August 1714, because Parliament preferred a remote Protestant relative to a Catholic Stuart claimant.
[事实] George I did not really speak English, so parts of the coronation reverted to Latin for the first time since Elizabeth I.
[事实] The elderly Archbishop of Canterbury, Thomas Tenison, is described as ineffective, and the ceremony was surrounded by heavy security.
[推测] George I’s coronation shows how the ritual had become entangled with Protestant succession politics rather than simply sacred kingship.

[04:28] **Coronation Riots and Jacobite Feeling**

[事实] George I’s coronation day saw riots in at least 20 English towns, especially in the South and West.
[事实] The rioters are described as Tory, high-church, and sympathetic to Jacobite or anti-Hanoverian sentiment.
[事实] Slogans included support for Henry Sacheverell, denunciations of foreigners, and hostility to “round heads.”
[推测] The riots suggest that coronation could reveal national division as much as national unity.

[07:15] **Maypoles, Berwick, and the Riot Act**

[事实] Maypoles were treated by some as symbols of merry England and Jacobite feeling, while Whigs were imagined as hostile to them.
[事实] In Bedford, a maypole was dressed in mourning.
[事实] Further unrest in 1715 contributed to the passing of the Riot Act, giving rise to the phrase “read the riot act.”
[推测] The discussion uses comic details about maypoles and slogans to show how old Civil War memories lingered in Hanoverian politics.

[09:01] **George II and Handel’s Coronation**

[事实] George II’s coronation is presented as a high point that largely went well.
[事实] Queen Caroline wore a heavily jeweled dress, and attendants were prepared to wipe away oil from the anointing.
[事实] Handel, recently naturalized as British, wrote the coronation music, including “Zadok the Priest.”
[事实] The choir sang “Zadok the Priest” at the wrong point, but the music was immediately admired.
[推测] The hosts treat George II’s coronation as one of the few Hanoverian ceremonies where grandeur and competence briefly aligned.

[12:27] **George III’s Disastrous Organisation**

[事实] George III’s 1761 coronation suffered from huge carriage congestion, delays, and poor preparation.
[事实] The organisers forgot essential items including thrones, the sword of state, and the canopy.
[事实] Crowds pressed heavily enough that soldiers used swords and musket butts to hold them back.
[事实] Spectators described the event as confusion, irregularity, and disorder.
[推测] The scale of the mismanagement makes George III’s coronation one of the episode’s central examples of royal ritual turning into farce.

[14:31] **Picnics, Toilets, and Ceremony Breakdown**

[事实] Many spectators could not hear the service and used the anointing as a chance to eat, creating loud clattering and laughter.
[事实] The ceremony had not been properly rehearsed, and participants repeatedly seemed uncertain what should happen next.
[事实] A retiring chamber had been arranged for the Queen, but she found the Duke of Newcastle using it.
[推测] These details undermine the sacred atmosphere and show the ceremony functioning partly as a crowded public entertainment.

[16:20] **George III’s Banquet Chaos**

[事实] The coronation banquet was lit with linen tapers that showered guests with ash.
[事实] Some groups, including the Barons of the Cinque Ports, were left without proper seating.
[事实] William Talbot, the Lord Steward, had trained his horse to walk backwards away from the king, but the horse repeatedly backed towards the king instead.
[事实] After the banquet, the public traditionally rushed in to seize leftovers, tablecloths, chairs, and plates.
[推测] The banquet reveals how “tradition” could include disorderly public looting as well as formal ceremony.

[19:27] **George IV’s Marital Crisis and Medieval Fancy Dress**

[事实] George IV’s coronation was postponed from August 1820 because of his conflict with his wife, Caroline of Brunswick.
[事实] He tried to exclude Caroline and pursued legislation to remove or divorce her, while the Whigs took her side against him.
[事实] George IV revived medieval and Elizabethan-looking costume, requiring others to pay for their own ceremonial dress.
[事实] The coronation cost about £238,000, which the hosts estimate as roughly £1.3 billion in modern GDP terms.
[推测] George IV’s antiquarianism is presented less as spiritual renewal than as extravagant historical theatre.

[21:59] **Caroline Turned Away**

[事实] Caroline arrived at Westminster Hall around six in the morning with Lord Hood.
[事实] Guards refused her entry because she had no ticket, despite her saying she was the queen.
[事实] She tried multiple entrances and was eventually turned away from Westminster Abbey amid soldiers and crowds shouting “shame.”
[事实] The hosts note that Caroline died two weeks later.
[推测] The scene is treated as both comic and tragic, because public spectacle collided with private marital cruelty.

[23:05] **George IV’s Hot, Expensive Ordeal**

[事实] George IV arrived in a huge Napoleonic-style costume on a warm day and was soaked in sweat.
[事实] He later said he would not endure the day’s sufferings again for another kingdom.
[事实] The service itself was relatively fixed by this point and not especially controversial.
[事实] The choir left early, leaving benches covered with litter as the king processed out.
[推测] The hosts imply that by George IV’s time the ritual content had stabilized, but spectacle and embarrassment dominated the memory of the day.

[24:21] **Canopies, Wax, Horses, and Looting**

[事实] George IV walked quickly to escape the canopy held by the Barons of the Cinque Ports, producing an undignified jog toward the banquet.
[事实] Wax candles melted in the heat and dripped on guests.
[事实] The Marquis of Anglesey, who had lost a leg at Waterloo, struggled to dismount during the banquet ritual and was laughed at.
[事实] After the banquet, crowds and soldiers fought over food and smashed plates until around three in the morning.
[推测] The hosts identify George IV’s coronation as the one they would most like to have witnessed because of its spectacular absurdity.

[27:03] **William IV Cuts the Flummery**

[事实] William IV had little interest in coronation ritual and did not really want a coronation at all.
[事实] His coronation took place amid political controversy before the Great Reform Act.
[事实] He abolished major elements such as the coronation banquet and the king’s champion on horseback.
[事实] The total cost was about one eighth of George IV’s coronation.
[事实] Despite his scepticism, William IV later described feeling awe when the crown touched his temples.
[推测] William IV’s reaction suggests the ceremony could still produce religious or moral seriousness even in a reluctant monarch.

[30:02] **Victoria’s Under-Rehearsed Mass Event**

[事实] Queen Victoria’s 1838 coronation was cheaper than George IV’s and more expensive than William IV’s.
[事实] It became a mass tourism event because railways brought perhaps up to half a million people to London.
[事实] The ceremony included fireworks, park entertainments, hot air balloons, and beer for people in workhouses.
[事实] Witnesses said the service was under-rehearsed, and Disraeli observed that participants often seemed unsure what came next.
[事实] Lord Rolle, aged 82, fell and rolled down the steps.
[推测] Victoria’s coronation is presented as still Hanoverian in its disorder rather than “Victorian” in later assumptions of polished ceremony.

[33:47] **Edward VII and the Imperial Turn**

[事实] Edward VII’s coronation was postponed shortly before the event because he had an abdominal abscess.
[事实] Planned public meals still went ahead, including a coronation dinner for the poor of London.
[事实] Rowntree gave different chocolate tins to poor diners and to stewards, with better chocolate for the socially influential stewards.
[事实] Because many European guests could not wait for the postponed ceremony, the coronation became more British and imperial in character.
[事实] Boer commanders were invited to a naval review soon after the end of the Boer War.
[推测] The postponement helped sharpen the coronation’s imperial emphasis.

[36:17] **George V and the Delhi Durbar**

[事实] George V wrote that his coronation was grand, simple, dignified, and went without a hitch.
[事实] He was moved when his son David, the future Edward VIII, performed homage.
[事实] After the coronation, George V answered telegrams rather than indulging in George IV-style revelry.
[事实] He later went to India for the Delhi Durbar, wearing a specially made Imperial Crown of India because the crown jewels could not leave Britain.
[事实] At Delhi, he and Queen Mary received homage and appeared before huge crowds.
[推测] The contrast between George V’s personal dullness and imperial ceremony is used for comic effect.

[39:20] **George VI and Broadcast Monarchy**

[事实] George VI’s coronation had originally been arranged for Edward VIII, who abdicated to marry Wallis Simpson.
[事实] The coronation emphasized empire and dominions, with Dominion prime ministers and colonial troops present.
[事实] It was the first radio coronation, and George VI gave a broadcast address.
[事实] The BBC televised the procession, though very few people owned televisions at the time.
[推测] The hosts find George VI’s coronation less entertaining than earlier chaotic ceremonies, despite its historical importance.

[41:43] **Elizabeth II, Television, and Everest**

[事实] Elizabeth II’s 1953 coronation took place in rainy, postwar Britain after years of austerity.
[事实] The Queen of Tonga became a popular figure because she did not put up her carriage hood in the rain.
[事实] News that Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay had reached Everest arrived in Britain on the morning of the coronation.
[事实] Around 25 million people watched the coronation on television, despite only about two to three million televisions being in Britain.
[事实] The event is described as a major moment in the mass adoption of television in 1950s Britain.
[推测] The coronation’s power came partly from combining monarchy, media technology, and imperial-era patriotic feeling.

[44:11] **Sacred Ritual and Modern Reception**

[事实] The hosts argue that Elizabeth II devoutly regarded her coronation as a sacramental moment.
[事实] They contrast 1953 Britain, described as largely Christian and deferential to authority, with the more sceptical setting of Charles III’s coronation.
[事实] Tom argues that without the sacral religious service, there is little point in having a coronation.
[事实] Dominic argues that many people mainly enjoy the spectacle rather than share Tom’s conviction about sacrality.
[推测] The central modern tension is whether a ritual built on Christian monarchy can still speak clearly to a secular or indifferent public.

[47:20] **Charles III and the Return of Sacral Emphasis**

[事实] The hosts cite Francis Young’s comments about Charles III’s coronation emphasizing sacral monarchy through oil consecrated in Jerusalem, the Cosmati pavement, relics of the True Cross, the St Augustine Gospels, and greater privacy for the anointing.
[事实] Tom is excited by the sacred and antiquarian elements, while Dominic jokes that he also misses older comic disorder such as horses, toilets, and high-church riots.
[事实] The hosts conclude that coronations turned out to be more interesting than expected.
[推测] The ending suggests that the appeal of coronations lies in the coexistence of deep ritual seriousness and unpredictable human absurdity.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s strength is its balance of historical detail and comic storytelling. It makes coronations feel less like static pageantry and more like moments where religion, politics, class, empire, technology, and crowd behaviour collide.

The most memorable material comes from the Hanoverian and George IV sections, where forgotten thrones, chaotic banquets, rioting crowds, awkward horses, and Caroline’s exclusion turn formal monarchy into vivid drama.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in British monarchy, public ritual, and political culture, especially those who enjoy irreverent historical narration. Its limitation is that some later coronations, especially George VI’s, are treated more briefly and mainly as contrasts to the earlier comic disasters.

[推测] Overall, the episode argues that coronations survive because they are never only one thing: they are sacred services, national spectacles, political statements, and occasions for very human disorder.
