# The French Revolution: Varennes, the Champ de Mars Massacre, and the Road to War

## 概览

This episode resumes The Rest Is History’s French Revolution series in July 1791, after Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette’s failed flight to Varennes. The hosts frame the moment as a decisive shift: the king is no longer widely seen as the misled “good father,” but as a liar, deserter, and possible conspirator against the Revolution.

The central story is the Champ de Mars massacre on 17 July 1791, when a republican petition rally was broken up by the National Guard under Lafayette after panic, rumors, and violence escalated. The massacre exposed a new and dangerous divide: revolutionaries were now using force against other revolutionaries.

The episode then traces the political consequences: Barnave and the Feuillants try to stabilize France through a constitutional monarchy, while Robespierre and the Jacobins gain strength by arguing that conspiracies, betrayal, and enemies within still threaten the Revolution. The closing turn is bleak: Louis and Marie Antoinette publicly accept the constitution while secretly appealing to Austria, setting up the coming war.

## 分段落总结

[00:25] **A Different Bastille Day**

[事实] The episode opens with the “Song of the 14th of July,” written for the second Bastille Day celebration in 1791.
[事实] The hosts contrast the 1791 celebration with 1790: Louis XVI had been central to the earlier event, but in 1791 royal symbols were absent and the word “King” had been removed from the altar inscription.
[推测] The erased royal language signals that the monarchy’s symbolic authority had already been badly damaged before formal abolition became possible.

[03:40] **Recap of Season One**

[事实] The hosts summarize three forces behind the Revolution: France’s financial and political crisis, social distress caused by hunger and weather shocks, and a cultural obsession with virtue, Roman republicanism, and purity.
[事实] They recap the Estates General becoming the National Assembly, the storming of the Bastille, the women’s march on Versailles, and reforms targeting the clergy.
[事实] By summer 1791, prices remained high, hunger continued, and political life in Paris had become more polarized.

[06:32] **The Flight to Varennes Changes Everything**

[事实] Louis and Marie Antoinette attempted to escape Paris, hoping to reach the eastern frontier, gather troops, and possibly march on the capital.
[事实] They were intercepted at Varennes and returned to Paris through hostile crowds and silence rather than gestures of respect.
[事实] Louis had left behind a letter effectively rejecting the Revolution, which made his previous acceptance of reform look dishonest.
[推测] The failed escape made conspiracy politics more credible because the king really had been plotting against the revolutionary order.

[07:20] **The King Becomes a Traitor in Public Opinion**

[事实] Newspapers and radical writers began calling Louis a traitor, a coward, and “Louis the false.”
[事实] The hosts cite radical rhetoric claiming the king had deserted the throne and intended to return with foreign executioners.
[事实] Marie Antoinette was also attacked in violent and sexualized language by radical publications.
[推测] The episode presents the press as both reflecting and intensifying the collapse of trust in the monarchy.

[09:37] **The Sans-Culottes and Political Street Culture**

[事实] Working-class militants known as sans-culottes appeared more forcefully in Paris, often armed with pikes or improvised weapons.
[事实] They pledged loyalty to the Revolution, but also smashed royal statues, busts, signs, and symbols.
[事实] The hosts describe revolutionary street politics as frightening to elites but also carnivalesque and exciting for many participants.
[推测] Political violence is shown not only as ideological, but also as a social experience that gave ordinary people a sense of participation and power.

[11:45] **The Republican Question Emerges**

[事实] Before Varennes, republicanism had been a small minority position in France.
[事实] After the king’s flight, more people began questioning not just Louis personally, but monarchy as an institution.
[事实] The Cordeliers Club, associated with Camille Desmoulins, Jean-Paul Marat, and Georges Danton, pushed petitions calling for the monarchy to be scrapped.
[推测] Varennes transformed republicanism from a marginal idea into a plausible political demand.

[13:12] **The National Assembly Under Pressure**

[事实] By 1791, many original deputies had gone home, leaving around 400 exhausted and overworked members trying to redesign France’s state, law, taxes, and constitution.
[事实] Deputies felt physically unsafe in Paris amid bread riots, political clubs, radical newspapers, and street mobilization.
[事实] The hosts also introduce Saint-Domingue, where slave unrest and rebellion raised difficult questions about liberty, property, and colonial economics.
[推测] The Assembly’s decisions were shaped by fear of disorder as much as by constitutional theory.

[17:39] **Barnave and the Moderate Settlement**

[事实] Antoine Barnave, a young Protestant lawyer from Grenoble and a leading figure in the Assembly, wanted to end revolutionary disorder and stabilize France.
[事实] Barnave and his allies favored a liberal constitutional order, economic liberalization, limits on strikes, and a monarchy with reduced but real powers.
[事实] Barnave had escorted the royal family back from Varennes and began exchanging coded letters with Marie Antoinette.
[推测] Barnave believed he could use the monarchy to complete and contain the Revolution, but this made him vulnerable to accusations of corruption.

[22:19] **The Assembly Lets Louis Return**

[事实] Louis and Marie Antoinette were placed under close supervision in the Tuileries but were treated with relative tolerance by the National Assembly.
[事实] They claimed they had only fled out of fear and denied intending to raise an army or coordinate with foreign powers.
[事实] The hosts state that these denials were lies, because the royal couple had been in contact with Austria and had considered military action.
[事实] On 15 July, the Assembly voted to reinstate Louis if he accepted the constitution.

[24:37] **Radical Anger and the Jacobin Split**

[事实] The Cordeliers and other radicals reacted furiously to the Assembly’s decision to keep Louis.
[事实] On 16 July, Barnave and his allies left the Jacobin Club and formed the Feuillant Club.
[事实] Only a small number of deputies stayed behind at the Jacobins, including Maximilien Robespierre.
[事实] The Cordeliers planned a mass rally and petition at the Champ de Mars demanding that France become a republic.

[27:57] **Robespierre Warns Against the Rally**

[事实] Robespierre warned that a huge rally could give the Feuillants a pretext for repression.
[事实] Other radicals dismissed the warning and expected the crowd’s size to force the deputies to concede.
[推测] The hosts present Robespierre as tactically shrewd even before he becomes the dominant revolutionary figure.

[30:07] **17 July 1791: The Crowd Gathers**

[事实] On Sunday, 17 July 1791, radicals and supporters moved toward the planned meeting point at the Bastille, but National Guard troops blocked the entrances.
[事实] The Paris authorities were led by Mayor Jean Sylvain Bailly and Lafayette, commander of the National Guard.
[事实] Demonstrators moved instead toward the Champ de Mars, with some anticipating trouble and carrying stones or weapons.
[推测] The authorities and protesters both approached the day expecting confrontation, making escalation more likely.

[33:48] **Rumor, Panic, and Two Dead Men**

[事实] At the Champ de Mars, people discovered two men hiding under the altar platform with food and carpenter’s tools.
[事实] The hosts say historians think the men may have intended to drill holes to look up women’s skirts, but rumors spread that they were terrorists with a bomb.
[事实] The men were lynched, hanged from a lamppost, beheaded, and their heads were paraded.
[推测] The bizarre incident became politically explosive because rumor and fear were already shaping revolutionary behavior.

[35:15] **The Champ de Mars Massacre**

[事实] Bailly declared martial law and came to the Champ de Mars with troops, while Lafayette was already there with hundreds of National Guardsmen.
[事实] The stadium contained perhaps 50,000 to 60,000 people, including radical petitioners and ordinary families enjoying a Sunday outing.
[事实] After stones were thrown and a shot rang out, National Guardsmen opened fire; cavalry then charged into the crowd.
[事实] Death estimates vary widely, from around 50 to the radicals’ claim of hundreds killed.
[推测] The massacre marked a major break because the forces of revolutionary order had turned violence on a revolutionary crowd.

[38:04] **Moderates Claim Victory, Radicals See Betrayal**

[事实] Barnave, the Feuillants, Lafayette, and Bailly interpreted the massacre as proof that disorder had to be stopped.
[事实] Radicals interpreted it as evidence that Bailly and Lafayette had joined a corrupt conspiracy linked to Marie Antoinette.
[事实] The Assembly passed a strict anti-riot decree and made it retroactive; more than 200 people were imprisoned, some for stone-throwing or slogans.
[事实] Some detainees were held for weeks in solitary confinement without trial or clear charges.

[41:05] **The Paranoid Style Becomes Normal**

[事实] The hosts draw on historian Timothy Tackett to argue that after Varennes, conspiracy thinking became normalized across the political spectrum.
[事实] Marat’s phrase “the mask of patriotism” captured the idea that apparent patriots might secretly be traitors.
[事实] Jacobin clubs began encouraging denunciations and reports on suspected counter-revolutionaries.
[推测] The episode suggests that revolutionary suspicion was dangerous partly because it was grounded in real plots, not merely fantasy.

[45:39] **Enemies Within and the Logic of Preemption**

[事实] The hosts note that there really were enemies of the Revolution, including nobles, refractory priests, and émigrés across the border in the Rhineland.
[事实] Local authorities searched houses, read letters, detained strangers, and arrested nobles and priests who had not sworn loyalty to the constitution.
[事实] In Brittany, officials justified preemptive arrests by saying enemies had to be seized before they committed crimes.
[推测] The logic of preventive repression foreshadows the harsher politics that will follow later in the Revolution.

[47:43] **Barnave’s Constitutional Monarchy**

[事实] Barnave believed the radicals had overplayed their hand at the Champ de Mars and that he now had space to pass a more conservative constitution.
[事实] The constitutional plan included tax and property thresholds for voting and officeholding, a king with veto power, and royal control over ministers and ambassadors.
[事实] Barnave told Marie Antoinette that these powers should be enough for Louis to remain king.
[推测] Barnave’s strategy depended on the royal family acting in good faith, which the episode later shows they were not doing.

[49:01] **Robespierre as the Incorruptible**

[事实] Robespierre is presented as Barnave’s opposite: uninterested in office, publicly devoted to the common people, and increasingly known as “the incorruptible.”
[事实] He came from Arras, was a lawyer’s son, admired the Roman Republic and Rousseau, and became known for earnest, emotional, morally charged speeches.
[事实] Some contemporaries admired his sobriety and honesty, while others found him proud, prickly, cold, and obsessed with his own virtue.
[推测] The hosts portray Robespierre’s moral certainty as both politically powerful and personally unsettling.

[51:36] **Robespierre’s Conspiratorial Worldview**

[事实] The hosts say Robespierre was unusually prone to conspiracy thinking even before 1789.
[事实] His politics divided the world sharply between virtue and corruption, good and evil.
[事实] In autumn 1791, he repeatedly attacked Barnave and the Feuillants as sell-outs to the Austrian queen and international tyranny.
[推测] Robespierre’s rise depended on a climate in which suspicion had become a persuasive political language.

[54:52] **The 1791 Constitution Is Accepted**

[事实] The constitution was completed and delivered to Louis at the Tuileries in a torchlit procession.
[事实] It made Louis head of the executive, gave him a veto, allowed him to choose ministers, and created a single-chamber assembly.
[事实] Voting was restricted to property-owning taxpayers; the very poor, women, and enslaved people could not vote.
[事实] Louis accepted the constitution and swore to maintain and defend it, prompting public celebrations.

[56:31] **The Revolution Is Declared Finished**

[事实] The National Assembly met for the last time on 30 September, with a new Legislative Assembly already elected.
[事实] The deputies had agreed that none of them would sit in the new assembly.
[事实] Some deputies declared the Revolution complete and believed their constitutional work had finished the great task.
[推测] This confidence looks fragile because the episode has already shown unresolved conflict over monarchy, property, religion, class, and colonial slavery.

[57:46] **Robespierre Says It Is Not Over**

[事实] Near the end of the Assembly, Le Chapelier tried to limit political clubs, arguing they were no longer needed.
[事实] Robespierre objected, saying the constitution still had enemies within and without, and that conspiracy and treachery remained active.
[事实] After his speech, crowds carried Robespierre through the streets.
[推测] This moment shows that Robespierre’s legitimacy increasingly came from popular suspicion of elite compromise.

[58:41] **The Royal Double Cross and the Wind of War**

[事实] Louis and Marie Antoinette had no intention of sincerely working with the new constitution.
[事实] Marie Antoinette secretly wrote to Vienna that they were cooperating with Barnave in order to double-cross him later.
[事实] Louis wrote to Emperor Leopold II saying he was a prisoner under duress and asking for aid for the king and kingdom of France.
[事实] The episode closes by pointing toward war in the next installment.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is that it explains the Revolution as a process of escalating mistrust rather than a simple march from monarchy to republic. Varennes, the Champ de Mars massacre, and the 1791 constitution are linked into one argument about why compromise became harder to sustain.

[推测] Its strongest feature is the attention to political psychology: conspiracy thinking is not treated as irrational noise, but as something made plausible by real royal deception, real émigré plotting, and real fear of disorder.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode is centered on high politics in Paris, even though it briefly gestures toward Saint-Domingue, women, workers, and rural France. Listeners interested in those perspectives may need later episodes for fuller treatment.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who already know the basic outline of the French Revolution and want to understand why 1791 became a turning point between constitutional optimism and the coming politics of war, suspicion, and repression.
