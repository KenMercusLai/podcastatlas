# In Triplicate

## 概览

This episode examines why the United States failed to act on evidence that a simple prescription-monitoring system could curb opioid abuse. Malcolm Gladwell traces “triplicate prescriptions”—three-part paper records of controlled-drug prescriptions—from California official Paul Madden’s 1930s reforms to consumer advocate Sidney Wolfe’s campaign for a national program decades later.

When Purdue Pharma prepared to launch OxyContin, its own research found that triplicate rules made doctors anxious about scrutiny and sharply reduced their willingness to prescribe opioids. Purdue consequently focused its marketing on states without those rules, creating a natural experiment: non-triplicate states later experienced substantially greater OxyContin use and faster growth in overdose deaths.

The episode concludes that society ignored a successful experiment partly because Madden and Wolfe were perceived as overbearing radicals. Gladwell acknowledges his own role as a young Washington Post reporter who dismissed Wolfe’s warnings, arguing that the central lesson is to judge evidence independently of the messenger.

## 分段落总结

[00:48] **The Power of the Washington Post**

[事实] Gladwell recalls working with Michael Spector on the Washington Post’s health and science desk during an era when the newspaper commanded extraordinary influence in Washington.

[事实] Reporters occupied the center of a network of lawmakers, lobbyists, bureaucrats, policy specialists, and confidential sources eager to shape coverage.

[事实] Gladwell introduces Sidney Wolfe, a forceful medical consumer advocate whom he viewed skeptically at the time.

[04:00] **Sidney Wolfe’s Institutional Activism**

[事实] Wolfe found his calling in 1971 after discovering that contaminated saline solution remained on the market despite the existence of alternative supplies; his public campaign helped get it withdrawn.

[事实] He founded Public Citizen’s Health Research Group and repeatedly challenged drug companies, physicians, and the FDA.

[事实] Wolfe believed institutions behaved responsibly only when constrained by effective laws, regulations, and persistent outside pressure.

[事实] Spector describes Wolfe as intelligent, colorful, highly effective with journalists, and firmly committed to his own position.

[06:50] **Paul Madden’s Progressive Approach to Drug Control**

[事实] Paul Madden, appointed to lead California’s Bureau of Narcotic Enforcement in 1939, believed government systems and regulations could solve social problems.

[事实] Officials feared that unscrupulous doctors might supply addictive drugs that patients could divert and sell, while conventional sting operations offered an inefficient response.

[事实] Madden pursued a bureaucratic solution centered on comprehensive records rather than relying solely on investigations of individual doctors.

[09:55] **The Triplicate Prescription System**

[事实] Madden’s regulation required prescriptions for specified addictive drugs to be issued on serialized, state-supplied forms with two carbon copies.

[事实] One copy remained with the physician, another with the pharmacist, and a third went to the state narcotics bureau; the records were retained for inspection.

[事实] The system created a durable record of physician and pharmacist conduct in three locations.

[推测] The paperwork’s visibility was central to its design: it made oversight tangible each time a doctor prescribed a controlled drug.

[13:21] **OxyContin and the Uneven Opioid Crisis**

[事实] OxyContin, launched by Purdue Pharma in 1996, was highly addictive and promoted to doctors more heavily than any previous painkiller.

[事实] By 2002, Purdue had thousands of salespeople promoting the drug, and annual OxyContin prescriptions eventually reached approximately $3 billion.

[事实] The opioid crisis produced hundreds of thousands of overdose deaths, but its severity varied substantially among otherwise similar states.

[事实] Massachusetts suffered far more than New York, while New Jersey suffered more than neighboring New York, raising questions about what caused the disparity.

[16:04] **Purdue’s Buried Research on Triplicates**

[事实] Economists Abbie Alpert, William Evans, Ethan Lieber, and David Powell examined internal Purdue documents disclosed through litigation.

[事实] A 1995 focus-group report repeatedly discussed triplicate prescriptions and their effect on physicians shortly before OxyContin’s launch.

[事实] By the early 1990s, only California, Texas, Illinois, Idaho, and New York retained triplicate programs.

[事实] Doctors and pharmaceutical companies generally disliked the programs because they imposed costs, paperwork, and extensive administrative requirements.

[18:48] **The Psychological “Chilling Effect”**

[事实] Researcher Linda Wastilla explains that triplicate systems reminded doctors that every relevant prescription created a permanent, inspectable record.

[事实] Purdue’s Houston focus group found that triplicate laws had a dramatic effect on prescribing behavior and that potential government scrutiny caused doctors considerable anxiety.

[事实] Purdue questioned whether marketing OxyContin in triplicate states was worthwhile.

[推测] The system worked not merely by collecting data but by making accountability immediate and psychologically salient to prescribers.

[21:24] **A Natural Experiment and Its Results**

[事实] Roughly one-third of Americans lived in triplicate states, which Purdue largely passed over, while other states received the company’s full marketing effort.

[事实] The economists found that non-triplicate states generally had almost twice as much per-capita OxyContin use as triplicate states.

[事实] Overdose deaths increased rapidly in non-triplicate states and more slowly in triplicate states, with the divergence persisting for two decades.

[事实] Had New York experienced Massachusetts’s opioid overdose rate from 2000 through 2019, an estimated 25,000 additional New Yorkers would have died.

[事实] The study also associated triplicate states with stronger economic growth, better birth outcomes, and violent-crime rates as much as 25 percent lower.

[23:54] **The Warning Before OxyContin**

[事实] In 1991, the National Institute on Drug Abuse convened specialists to assess prescription-monitoring programs, five years before OxyContin’s launch.

[事实] Participants disagreed sharply: pain specialist Russell Portenoy emphasized opioid under-prescribing, while the pharmaceutical industry opposed a federal triplicate mandate.

[事实] Brooklyn physician Gerald Deas argued that triplicate regulations were already saving lives in communities affected by drug abuse.

[事实] Wolfe advocated a national triplicate program, maintaining that existing state experiments had already demonstrated its effectiveness.

[27:31] **Wolfe’s Case for National Oversight**

[事实] Wolfe had become concerned about opioid-related problems by the late 1970s or early 1980s through his observation of FDA advisory meetings.

[事实] He believed monitoring doctors through retained prescription records was an important part of the solution.

[事实] At the 1991 NIDA meeting, Wolfe argued that the evidence from California, New York, Illinois, and other states justified nationwide adoption.

[事实] Gladwell and Wolfe agree that a national program would have slowed OxyContin’s spread and reduced the damage caused by the subsequent epidemic.

[29:23] **Gladwell’s Personal Reckoning**

[事实] Wolfe published a 1993 monograph renewing his call for stricter opioid-prescription monitoring, but it received little attention.

[事实] Gladwell was then covering the FDA and medical science for the Washington Post but was more sympathetic to the pharmaceutical industry and distrustful of Wolfe’s regulatory approach.

[事实] Gladwell says he probably received and ignored Wolfe’s monograph, and acknowledges that serious reporting on it might have made some difference.

[事实] He directly apologizes for dismissing Wolfe as a radical whose warnings did not merit attention.

[32:38] **Judge the Message, Not the Messenger**

[事实] Gladwell draws a parallel between the rejection of Madden’s warnings in the 1930s and the dismissal of Wolfe’s arguments in the 1990s.

[事实] Both men were perceived as overbearing or hysterical, leading others to discount evidence supporting their proposals.

[事实] Gladwell identifies the fundamental lesson as judging the message rather than the messenger.

[事实] The episode closes by observing that Wolfe warned of impending disaster in 1991 and that the disaster subsequently occurred.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s principal strength is its clear causal narrative. It connects an obscure paper-based regulation, Purdue’s own marketing decisions, and long-term differences in opioid harm, turning administrative history into a compelling account of how policy can alter public-health outcomes.

Its most effective element is Gladwell’s self-criticism. By admitting that his assumptions about government, industry, and Wolfe’s personality shaped what he considered newsworthy, he makes the episode about more than opioids: it becomes a case study in how credible warnings are ignored.

[推测] The argument gives less attention to the possible costs of triplicate systems, including administrative burden and the risk of discouraging medically appropriate pain treatment. The evidence presented is nevertheless valuable for listeners interested in public health, pharmaceutical regulation, journalism, institutional accountability, and the interpretation of natural experiments.
