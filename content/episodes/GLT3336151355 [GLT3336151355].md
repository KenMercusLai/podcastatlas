# The Flight to Varennes: Louis XVI's Failed Escape

## 概览

This episode follows the attempted escape of Louis XVI, Marie Antoinette, their children, and a small royal party from Paris in June 1791. The hosts frame the flight as a dramatic turning point in the French Revolution, because its failure exposed the king's hostility to the revolutionary settlement and pushed France further toward republicanism.

The discussion first sets the wider context: by late 1790 and early 1791, Paris was politically energized, radical clubs and newspapers were shaping public opinion, bread prices and inflation were feeding unrest, and the National Assembly was exhausted by the scale of remaking France.

The core story traces the escape plan, the delays, the conspicuous carriage, the mistaken assumptions about rural loyalty, the missed military escort, and the recognition of the royal family by Jean-Baptiste Drouet. The hosts conclude that the flight failed not only because of bad luck, but because the revolution had already transformed provincial France.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **A Failed Royal Gamble**

[事实] The episode opens with a letter by the Marquis de Ferrier describing the humiliation and danger caused by the royal family's attempted escape.
[事实] The hosts identify the episode's subject as Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette's disguised flight from Paris with their children in June 1791.
[事实] They describe the failed escape as a turning point in France's movement from monarchy toward republic.

[02:31] **Paris After Versailles**

[事实] The hosts recap the Women's March on Versailles in October 1789, after which the royal family was brought to Paris and installed in the Tuileries Palace.
[事实] By late 1790, political life in France had shifted from Versailles to Paris, where tricolor cockades, newspapers, patriotic songs, and revolutionary enthusiasm were highly visible.
[推测] The hosts present Paris as a city where politics had become part of daily life rather than a distant elite activity.

[04:19] **Radical Political Culture**

[事实] Radical journalists such as Camille Desmoulins and Jean-Paul Marat are described as shaping an increasingly populist tone.
[事实] Their rhetoric divided the virtuous people and nation from alleged conspirators such as foreigners, priests, and aristocrats.
[事实] The National Assembly and political clubs increasingly imagined that France, and perhaps the world, could be remade from scratch.

[06:30] **Exhaustion And Clubs**

[事实] The National Assembly had about 1,200 members, but only around 400 were still attending regularly.
[事实] Many nobles and priests were staying away because they were worried, hostile, or alienated by the revolution's direction.
[事实] The Jacobin Club had busts of Brutus, Cato, and Benjamin Franklin, and there were about 400 affiliated clubs across France by early 1791.

[09:27] **Citizenship, Democracy, And Unrest**

[事实] The emerging constitution distinguished between active citizens, mainly taxpaying men over 25, and passive citizens without voting rights, including women.
[事实] Bread prices remained high, paper money was being printed, inflation rose, and Paris saw a wave of strikes in the winter of 1790-1791.
[推测] Economic frustration made the revolution harder to stabilize, because people judged it by whether life had materially improved.

[11:33] **Mirabeau And The Moderate Hope**

[事实] Mirabeau warned that people had been promised more than the revolution could deliver quickly, especially on work, wages, and living standards.
[事实] Some expected Mirabeau to guide France toward a British-style constitutional monarchy, but he died in April 1791.
[事实] After him, moderate Jacobins such as Antoine Barnave wanted to calm politics, restrain unrest, and reach a settlement with the king.

[14:20] **Louis's Public Oath And Private Rejection**

[事实] Louis appeared publicly willing to work with the revolution, including swearing to uphold the future constitution at the 1790 Bastille anniversary.
[事实] The hosts say historians later found letters showing that Louis privately hated the revolution and regarded concessions as having been forced from him.
[事实] Marie Antoinette is described as strongly opposed to the revolution after being traumatized by her treatment.

[16:37] **Religion And Paralysis**

[事实] Louis was deeply religious, took mass daily, and viewed himself as an anointed king chosen by God.
[事实] The Civil Constitution of the Clergy, and the pope's warning against taking the oath, placed Louis between loyalty to France and loyalty to his faith.
[事实] The hosts describe Louis as well-meaning but indecisive, which delayed his decision to flee.

[18:11] **Fersen's Escape Plan**

[事实] Axel von Fersen, the Swedish count and friend of Marie Antoinette, drew up the escape plan and visited the Tuileries in disguise.
[事实] Fersen initially wanted the royal party to travel separately, but Louis and Marie Antoinette insisted on taking their children, the king's sister Elizabeth, nannies, and bodyguards.
[事实] The enlarged party required a large luxury coach rather than a discreet escape arrangement.

[20:21] **The Conspicuous Carriage**

[事实] The main coach, a berline, took three months to build, cost 6,000 French pounds, and had a leather interior, padded seats, bottle racks, a picnic set, and a leather-covered chamber pot.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that the carriage was not inconspicuous: it was black with a yellow frame.
[推测] The carriage symbolized the royal family's inability to fully understand the practical demands of going undercover.

[22:25] **The Saint-Cloud Shock**

[事实] Louis wanted to go to Saint-Cloud for Easter because priests there had not sworn the constitutional oath.
[事实] A crowd blocked the royal carriage for nearly two hours, abusing the king and queen and convincing them that they were prisoners.
[事实] The National Assembly then tightened restrictions on the royal family and sent much of their court away.

[24:03] **The Montmedy Objective**

[事实] The plan was to reach Montmedy, near the Austrian Netherlands, where the Marquis de Bouille waited with Swiss and German troops.
[事实] Louis rejected a more direct road through Reims because he feared being recognized there, preferring a route through smaller towns and villages.
[事实] An Austrian ambassador warned Marie Antoinette that the plan might provoke civil war and could be disastrous if they were caught.

[28:18] **The Day Of Departure**

[事实] The escape was set for Monday, June 20, and the royal household maintained appearances during the day.
[事实] Marie Antoinette went to mass, had her hair done, drove around the city, and dined with family as usual.
[事实] Louis finished a letter explaining that he was fleeing voluntarily and rejecting what he called the destruction of monarchy and the rise of anarchy.

[31:19] **Leaving The Tuileries**

[事实] Marie Antoinette, Madame de Torzel, the children, and others slipped out through back routes after disguises had been prepared.
[事实] Axel von Fersen drove one of the waiting cabs in disguise and behaved convincingly as a Parisian coachman.
[事实] Louis was delayed by a late visit from Lafayette and Bailly, putting the plan behind schedule from an early stage.

[34:18] **Early Delays Outside Paris**

[事实] Louis left in disguise around 11:30, and Marie Antoinette nearly encountered Lafayette while leaving.
[事实] The party transferred to the larger berline near the Saint-Martin gate but lost more time finding it in the dark.
[事实] By the time they left Paris, they were already about two hours behind schedule.

[36:26] **Disguises And False Confidence**

[事实] The party planned to travel under assumed identities: Madame de Torzel as the Baroness de Corfe, the children as her daughters, Marie Antoinette and Elizabeth as servants, and Louis as Monsieur Durand.
[事实] Louis grew more relaxed as they moved farther from Paris, chatted during horse changes, and followed the route with maps and an itinerary.
[推测] His confidence rested on the belief that rural France remained loyal to him, an assumption the episode later undermines.

[39:20] **Recognition In The Countryside**

[事实] The bodyguards wore yellow uniforms that drew attention because yellow was associated with the Prince de Conde's counterrevolutionary forces.
[事实] Louis was recognized before Varennes, including by people at Montmirail, Chantrix, and Chalon.
[事实] Although crowds formed and people whispered, no one stopped the carriage at those earlier points.

[42:28] **The Missing Escort**

[事实] The royal party expected to meet a military escort under the Duc de Choiseul near the Argonne Forest.
[事实] Choiseul's cavalry had attracted attention and alarm from local peasants, and he withdrew after the king fell far behind schedule.
[事实] Monsieur Leonard carried messages telling other cavalry detachments that the "treasure" was not coming that day.

[44:35] **Drouet Identifies The King**

[事实] At Saint-Menu, postmaster Jean-Baptiste Drouet recognized Marie Antoinette and then identified Louis from the face on paper money.
[事实] Drouet told local officials that the king and queen were fleeing, likely to gather a foreign army against the revolution.
[事实] He and Jean Guillaume rode ahead toward Varennes to warn the next town.

[47:35] **Stopped At Varennes**

[事实] The royal party reached Varennes near midnight and found no replacement horses because the expected arrangements had failed.
[事实] Drouet arrived first and alerted local officials, councilors, National Guard members, and the magistrate Jean-Baptiste Sauss.
[事实] The travelers' papers seemed in order, but Drouet insisted they were the king and queen, so the local officials delayed them overnight.

[49:07] **Identity Confirmed**

[事实] The royal family stayed in Sauss's house above a grocer's shop while officials sought someone who had seen the king and queen before.
[事实] A judge named Destez recognized Louis, knelt before him, and confirmed his identity.
[事实] Louis embraced municipal council members and argued that he had come to the countryside seeking freedom and tranquility.

[52:30] **The Town Chooses The Revolution**

[事实] Varennes had become politicized since the revolution, with delegates, meetings, and a local Jacobin club.
[事实] Local fears of brigands, foreign armies, and invasion shaped how residents interpreted the king's flight.
[事实] By dawn, armed crowds had gathered, and local leaders told Louis they could not let him continue east.

[53:50] **Ordered Back To Paris**

[事实] Couriers from Paris arrived with decrees from Lafayette and the National Assembly ordering the royal family to be stopped.
[事实] Marie Antoinette reacted angrily to the message, while Louis said there was no longer a king in France.
[事实] At 7:30, the family was put back into the carriages to return to Paris.

[54:31] **The Humiliating Return**

[事实] The return journey to Paris took four days and was hot, dusty, crowded, and hostile.
[事实] Crowds shouted abuse, repeated old attacks on Marie Antoinette and Louis, and threw objects at the royal bodyguards.
[事实] A local nobleman who tried to shout support for the king was pursued and killed.

[56:38] **Barnave And The Shifting Center**

[事实] Three National Assembly representatives joined the royal family on the return journey, including Antoine Barnave.
[事实] Barnave sat beside Marie Antoinette, spoke with her, and began to think that he could do business with her.
[推测] The hosts treat this as a political miscalculation, because the revolutionary center was moving faster than Barnave understood.

[57:32] **Back In Paris**

[事实] Paris turned out in silence to watch the royal carriage return, with people keeping their hats on instead of honoring the king.
[事实] The bodyguards were attacked when the carriage reached the Tuileries, and Lafayette's men had to pull them to safety.
[事实] Louis's written declaration made it clear that he had never truly accepted the revolution.

[59:20] **Why The Flight Failed**

[事实] The hosts argue that the escape could theoretically have worked, but delays, recognition, and poor execution undermined it.
[事实] Louis's indecision mattered because he had waited until 1791, when the revolution had already penetrated the countryside.
[事实] The hosts conclude that the revolution itself stopped him: even towns like Varennes now sided with elected deputies and the National Assembly over the king.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode's main value is its combination of narrative tension and political analysis. It treats the flight almost as a thriller, while still connecting each mishap to larger revolutionary forces.

[事实] The hosts repeatedly stress that the failure was not just accidental. The conspicuous carriage, delayed timetable, missing escort, public recognition, and Louis's misunderstanding of rural France all contributed to the outcome.

[推测] A limitation is that the episode focuses heavily on royal decision-making and the drama of the escape, while less time is spent on the perspectives of ordinary Parisians or villagers beyond their role in stopping the king.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in the French Revolution, political legitimacy, monarchy under pressure, and historical turning points told through vivid narrative detail.
