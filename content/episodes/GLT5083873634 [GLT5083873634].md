# Iran: Carter, the Shah, and Khomeini

## 概览

This episode frames the Iranian Revolution through the irony of Jimmy Carter’s New Year’s Eve 1977 toast to the Shah, in which he praised Iran as an “island of stability” just days before revolutionary unrest began accelerating.

The discussion contrasts three forces: the Shah’s Western-backed modernization and authoritarian rule, Carter’s moralistic but strategically conflicted foreign policy, and Ayatollah Khomeini’s religious, anti-imperialist challenge to the Pahlavi state.

A central conclusion is that Iran’s oil boom and modernization did not simply strengthen the regime. They also produced inflation, inequality, urban dislocation, cultural alienation, and a large educated youth population whose frustrations helped fuel revolution.

The hosts argue that both the Shah and the United States badly misread events, partly because they understood unrest through Cold War and state-security categories rather than through Shia religious politics, clerical networks, and revolutionary symbolism.

[00:31] **Carter’s Toast and the Coming Irony**

[事实] The episode opens with President Jimmy Carter’s toast to Mohammad Reza Pahlavi and Empress Farah in Tehran on New Year’s Eve 1977.

[事实] Carter praised Iran as stable and close to the United States in military and diplomatic terms.

[事实] The hosts stress that this came only days before protests began that would help bring down the Shah.

[推测] The opening is designed to show how badly American confidence in the Shah misread the fragility of his regime.

[03:48] **The Series Stakes**

[事实] The hosts introduce the wider series as covering the fall of the Shah, the rise of the Ayatollahs, the seizure of the US embassy, the 444-day hostage crisis, and Operation Eagle Claw.

[事实] They describe the Iranian Revolution as a turning point that transformed Iran from a close US ally into a major opponent.

[事实] They compare the revolution’s global significance to the French and Russian revolutions in cultural and political consequences.

[推测] The episode treats 1979 as a starting point for later waves of Islamic militancy.

[05:46] **Tehran’s Oil-Fueled Transformation**

[事实] Tehran had grown from about half a million people in the 1940s to nearly five million by Carter’s 1977 visit.

[事实] The hosts link this rapid growth to oil money, new housing, factories, migration, and severe pressure on infrastructure.

[事实] Tom describes modern Tehran’s traffic and infrastructure strain as a legacy of the 1960s and 1970s boom.

[推测] Tehran’s growth is presented as a symptom of broader social instability rather than simple progress.

[07:04] **The Shah and Iran’s Ancient Identity**

[事实] Mohammad Reza Pahlavi was born in 1919 to Reza Pahlavi, an army officer who later became Shah.

[事实] The hosts emphasize that Iran is not Arab, has an ancient Persian civilization, and has Shia Islam as a defining state religion.

[事实] They explain that monarchy and priesthood both have deep historical roots in Iran.

[推测] The Shah saw himself as heir to thousands of years of Persian kingship.

[09:18] **Foreign Intervention and Tainted Legitimacy**

[事实] Britain and Russia had repeatedly intervened in Persia, and British oil interests became central after oil was found in 1908.

[事实] Reza Pahlavi took power after a 1921 coup with British support, making Mohammad Reza crown prince at age two.

[事实] In 1941, Britain and the Soviet Union occupied Iran, and Britain helped force Reza Shah to abdicate in favor of his son.

[事实] In 1953, a CIA-backed coup helped remove Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh after his move to nationalize the Anglo-Persian Oil Company.

[推测] These events left the Shah vulnerable to charges that he was a Western puppet.

[13:44] **Modernizer, Celebrity, and Personality Cult**

[事实] From the late 1950s onward, the Shah asserted himself more strongly and pursued modernization and Westernization.

[事实] By the 1960s he was developing a personality cult and presenting himself as heir to figures such as Cyrus the Great.

[事实] Western visitors saw him as polished and impressive, while the hosts describe him as shy, reserved, and personally insecure.

[推测] His public image of regal confidence masked private weakness.

[15:17] **Royal Glamour and Court Corruption**

[事实] In 1967 the Shah elevated himself to the ancient title “King of Kings.”

[事实] In 1971 he staged an extravagant celebration at Persepolis for the 2,500th anniversary of Persian monarchy.

[事实] The hosts describe imported French food, global dignitaries, multiple palaces, mistresses, and kickbacks around the court.

[事实] They cite a British Foreign Office report after the Shah’s fall that judged the court to have been highly corrupt.

[推测] The display of luxury fed resentment by making inequality and elite detachment visible.

[18:40] **Oil, Arms, SAVAK, and US Support**

[事实] The 1973 OPEC oil shock nearly quadrupled oil prices, giving Iran, then the world’s second largest oil exporter, a huge windfall.

[事实] The United States backed the Shah as a key anti-Soviet ally in the Persian Gulf.

[事实] The CIA helped fund and train SAVAK, the Shah’s secret police, which had a reputation for torturing dissidents.

[事实] After Nixon signaled support in 1972, the Shah spent billions on weapons, including major purchases from Britain and the United States.

[推测] American strategic priorities encouraged Washington to overlook repression and instability.

[21:59] **Jimmy Carter’s Background**

[事实] Jimmy Carter was born in 1924 in Plains, Georgia, to a peanut-farming family.

[事实] He attended the US Naval Academy and worked as an engineer in the nuclear submarine program.

[事实] The hosts describe him as conscientious, hardworking, moralistic, stubborn, competitive, and personally reserved.

[事实] After losing a congressional race in 1966, Carter experienced a crisis and became born again.

[24:27] **Carter’s Populist Rise**

[事实] Carter ran for governor of Georgia in 1970 as a populist outsider against establishment Democrat Carl Sanders.

[事实] He presented himself as an ordinary person, a Southern outsider, an evangelical Christian, and a champion of the “little guy.”

[事实] Watergate and Ted Kennedy’s Chappaquiddick scandal helped open space for Carter’s presidential run.

[事实] Carter won the Democratic nomination and then defeated Gerald Ford.

[推测] His appeal depended less on detailed policy than on moral promise and anti-Washington symbolism.

[27:48] **Carter in Office**

[事实] The hosts judge Carter to have struggled as president because he lacked congressional relationships, was not naturally charming, and disliked compromise.

[事实] They describe him as a micromanager who overworked and overprepared.

[事实] Carter’s cardigan television appearance and thermostat appeal are used as examples of his ordinary-person image clashing with expectations of presidential authority.

[事实] His foreign policy promised to reflect American decency and avoid another Vietnam.

[30:30] **Carter’s Foreign Policy Pressures**

[事实] Carter achieved a major foreign policy success with the Camp David Accords between Egypt and Israel in 1978.

[事实] The hosts say his administration operated under the shadow of Vietnam and fears that the United States was losing ground in the Cold War.

[事实] By around 1978 Carter had become harder on the Soviet Union.

[推测] Carter’s moral language overlapped with later Cold War rhetoric but did not give him a clear framework for Iran.

[32:34] **Carter Bets on the Shah**

[事实] Carter knew the Shah faced criticism over human rights and applied some pressure for reform.

[事实] In 1977 the Shah allowed the Red Cross into prisons and loosened restrictions on writers and political discussion.

[事实] Carter told Ambassador William Sullivan to treat the Shah as a close friend and emphasized Iran’s importance to US security.

[事实] US listening posts on Iran’s Soviet border were described as a major strategic reason for supporting the Shah.

[推测] Carter’s human rights agenda was compromised by Cold War priorities.

[35:19] **The New Year’s Eve Banquet**

[事实] Carter visited Tehran on New Year’s Eve 1977 after the Shah had visited Washington in November.

[事实] The banquet is described as lavish, with caviar, Dom Perignon, roast partridge, and a large ice-cream flambé.

[事实] Carter praised the Shah’s wisdom, judgment, sensitivity, insight, and supposed commitment to human rights.

[事实] He also described seeing thousands of Iranians and Americans welcoming him in Tehran.

[推测] Carter’s remarks show that he did not realize he had tied his presidency to a doomed regime.

[37:42] **Khomeini Enters the Episode**

[事实] After the break, the hosts play words attributed to Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini from exile in Najaf in February 1978.

[事实] Khomeini denounced imperialists, the Iranian government, America, the Shah, and the Shah’s circle.

[事实] He called on “sons of Islam” to rouse people in streets, bazaars, universities, workplaces, and villages.

[推测] The contrast with Carter’s banquet speech highlights two incompatible visions of Iran’s future.

[39:17] **Khomeini’s Formation**

[事实] Khomeini was born in Khomein in central Iran, probably in 1900, into a clerical family.

[事实] His father was killed when Khomeini was a toddler, and he was raised by relatives.

[事实] He studied in Qom, became a respected scholar, and focused on law, philosophy, poetry, mysticism, and Shia thought.

[事实] By about age 50, people were calling him Ayatollah.

[推测] The seminary gave him order and refuge in a violent period of Iranian life.

[42:35] **Shia Islam and Revolutionary Memory**

[事实] The hosts explain Shia Islam through the succession dispute after Muhammad and the claim that leadership should have stayed within the Prophet’s family.

[事实] They identify Ali and Hussein as central figures, with Hussein’s death at Karbala in 680 treated as a defining catastrophe.

[事实] Ashura is described as the great Shia day of mourning.

[事实] They explain the belief in the hidden Imam, the Mahdi, who will return and avenge Hussein.

[推测] The hosts see Shia memory of martyrdom, oppression, and apocalypse as essential to understanding the revolution.

[47:03] **America’s Religious Blind Spot**

[事实] The hosts argue that the American foreign policy establishment did not understand the Shia apocalyptic framework.

[事实] They say this lack of comprehension shaped Washington’s inability to deal with Khomeini as he emerged.

[推测] US officials were using the wrong map of Iranian politics.

[47:51] **Clerics, Bazaars, and Local Authority**

[事实] Shia clerics had major local influence as judges, teachers, storytellers, mediators, and literate authority figures.

[事实] People often looked to clerics rather than the state, and clerics could function as a counterweight to state power.

[事实] In towns and cities, clerics were closely connected with merchants and artisans in the bazaars.

[事实] The hosts identify this cleric-bazaar alliance as a key social force behind the revolution.

[49:11] **The Clerical Problem of Power**

[事实] The hosts explain that traditional Shia thought regarded legitimate governance as having perished with Ali and Hussein.

[事实] They say Shia clerics historically did not usually aspire to control the state.

[事实] This created a problem for anti-Shah clerics: if the monarchy fell, it was not obvious who should govern.

[推测] Khomeini’s later project mattered because it moved beyond older clerical reluctance to rule.

[50:22] **Pahlavi Modernization Against Clerical Power**

[事实] Reza Pahlavi pursued modernization policies including electrification, identity cards, railways, Western clothing, and restrictions on veils and turbans.

[事实] The hosts say clerics hated these measures and saw the Pahlavis as illegitimate Westernizers.

[事实] The younger Shah’s White Revolution in the 1960s included public works, land reform, literacy, women’s voting rights, women’s rights, and expanded state education.

[事实] These reforms threatened clerical land, legal authority, educational control, and social standing.

[推测] Modernization weakened the traditional institutions that many Iranians still relied on.

[52:48] **Khomeini’s Political Breakthrough**

[事实] Khomeini entered politics in the 1960s by denouncing the Shah as a tyrant, traitor, and puppet of the United States and Israel.

[事实] He was arrested, placed under house arrest, released, and then attacked a deal granting legal immunity to American military personnel in Iran.

[事实] Khomeini argued that the deal reduced Iran to colonial status and used it to portray the Shah as an American agent.

[事实] The Shah arrested him again and expelled him from Iran.

[推测] Khomeini’s appeal came from combining religious traditionalism with anti-colonial nationalism.

[55:04] **Exile and Cassette Sermons**

[事实] Khomeini settled in Najaf, Iraq, where Ali is buried.

[事实] He lived with his wife, followers, and acolytes, teaching, praying, reading, and giving lectures.

[事实] His supporters recorded his sermons and lectures and smuggled the tapes into Iran.

[推测] Exile helped turn Khomeini into a symbol of resistance rather than removing him from politics.

[56:00] **Modernization’s Mixed Blessing**

[事实] The hosts say the Shah expanded railways, health care, education, and industry.

[事实] Women and minorities were described as better off under the Shah than before in Iranian history.

[事实] The Shah was contemptuous of Islamic dress codes, and women who were not traditionally devout could dress as they liked.

[事实] Rural migrants were shocked by Western billboards, women in miniskirts, alcohol, and mixed social life in Tehran.

[推测] Social liberalization became a visible symbol of what traditional Muslims feared.

[57:43] **Inflation, Youth, and Urban Alienation**

[事实] Oil wealth helped create inequality between rural peasants and the urban poor on one side and metropolitan winners on the other.

[事实] Inflation in the 1970s was around 15 percent a year.

[事实] Half of Iran’s population in the 1970s was under 16.

[事实] Tehran absorbed large numbers of rural migrants into grim concrete tower blocks, while traffic, electricity, and rent costs became severe problems.

[推测] The Shah’s development model produced a young, frustrated urban population vulnerable to radical politics.

[59:18] **Western Presence and the Search for Meaning**

[事实] Around 50,000 Americans lived in Iran.

[事实] Masoome Ebtekar later wrote that many Americans in Iran behaved with self-importance and that American culture flooded the country.

[事实] Tehran and other big cities had about 200,000 students as a result of the Shah’s modernization project.

[事实] Some students turned to socialism or communism, but many found Islam a more authentically Iranian source of meaning and order.

[推测] The revolution’s appeal grew because it answered cultural alienation as well as economic grievance.

[61:20] **American Blindness**

[事实] The hosts say US attention in the early 1970s focused more on Vietnam, the Soviet Union, Eastern Europe, Cuba, and other Cold War theaters than on Iran.

[事实] Ambassador Sullivan later wrote that few US embassy staff spoke Farsi or had been outside Tehran.

[事实] The embassy had many links to the Shah’s court but few to opposition groups or clerics.

[推测] Washington’s information about Iran was filtered through elite and pro-regime channels.

[62:00] **The January 1978 Article and Qom Protests**

[事实] After Carter left Iran, Khomeini’s taped sermons were circulating widely and becoming more ferocious.

[事实] On January 7, 1978, a major Iranian newspaper published an article attacking Khomeini as a tool of “red and black imperialism.”

[事实] The article insulted Khomeini’s ancestry and clerics more broadly.

[事实] Protests quickly erupted in Qom, religious students poured into the streets, and police fired into crowds.

[推测] The article was a major regime miscalculation that helped ignite revolutionary momentum.

[64:30] **The Forty-Day Cycle**

[事实] Forty days after the Qom deaths, memorial services led to more demonstrations across Iran.

[事实] In Tabriz, protesters attacked cinemas, banks, and symbols of Western influence, and the army was sent in.

[事实] A cycle developed in which deaths led to memorials 40 days later, which then produced more protests and deaths.

[事实] This cycle continued into mid-May 1978.

[推测] Shia mourning practices helped structure the rhythm of revolutionary escalation.

[65:28] **The Shah’s Illness and Misread Response**

[事实] The Shah made concessions including firing the head of the secret police and promising free parliamentary elections.

[事实] He believed the unrest was probably being masterminded by communists and the British.

[事实] The Shah had leukemia, but doctors initially concealed the seriousness of his illness from him.

[事实] US officials continued reporting that the Shah was fine and that his regime could handle unrest.

[推测] His illness, isolation, and misdiagnosis of the opposition weakened his response.

[68:03] **Cinema Rex Fire**

[事实] On August 19, 1978, four men barred the doors of Cinema Rex in Abadan, poured petrol on the doors, and set them alight.

[事实] About 500 people were burned to death.

[事实] The hosts say most historians outside Iran now agree the attack was the work of Islamic militants.

[事实] In Iran, many people blamed the Shah’s secret police, and the Islamic Republic still presents that as the official line.

[推测] The political effect of the fire mattered more than the factual uncertainty in public opinion.

[70:48] **Black Friday and the Shah’s Collapse**

[事实] By September 1978, Tehran saw constant demonstrations calling for Khomeini and the end of the Pahlavi monarchy.

[事实] On Friday, September 8, the Shah declared martial law, and troops fired on demonstrators at Jaleh Square.

[事实] About 100 people were killed, and exaggerated rumors spread about helicopters and Israeli troops firing on crowds.

[事实] By autumn, strikes had shut down oil fields, crowds filled the streets, and revolutionary strike committees had taken control in some towns.

[事实] On November 9, Ambassador Sullivan sent a secret cable titled “Thinking the Unthinkable,” saying the Shah was finished and the US might need to deal with Khomeini.

[推测] By this stage, the regime’s loss of legitimacy was becoming irreversible.

[73:52] **Episode Close and Next Steps**

[事实] The hosts end by previewing the next episode: the revolution accelerating, the Shah fleeing Iran, and Khomeini returning to Tehran in triumph.

[事实] They also say the next episode will include another well-known Carter anecdote involving a fishing trip on a swamp.

[推测] The closing sets up the next installment as the point where diplomatic uncertainty becomes regime collapse.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s value lies in connecting high politics, religious history, social change, and personality. It does not treat the Iranian Revolution as a sudden eruption, but as the result of long pressures: foreign intervention, royal authoritarianism, clerical networks, oil wealth, cultural resentment, and American misperception.

A major strength is the contrast between Carter, the Shah, and Khomeini. Carter appears moralistic but strategically trapped; the Shah appears powerful but isolated and ill; Khomeini appears austere, learned, and politically effective in turning religious grievance into mass mobilization.

[推测] The main limitation is that this is the first episode of a series, so it stops before resolving the central diplomatic question: whether the United States would abandon the Shah or try to accommodate Khomeini. It also focuses heavily on elite and ideological framing rather than detailed voices from ordinary Iranians.

[推测] This episode is especially useful for listeners who want historical context for US-Iranian hostility, the 1979 revolution, and the role of Shia political culture in modern Iran.
