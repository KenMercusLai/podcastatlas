# Christmas in Connecticut: Arnold Schwarzenegger’s Wild Directing Detour

## 概览

This episode reconstructs the improbable making of the 1992 television remake of *Christmas in Connecticut*. Producer Stan Brooks initially envisioned a modest update of the 1945 screwball comedy, only for Arnold Schwarzenegger—fresh from *Terminator 2*—to volunteer as its director.

Interviews with the writers, producers, executives, and crew describe how Schwarzenegger transformed the project. He requested more action, danger, and comedy; recruited advice from prominent filmmakers; and presided over an eccentric cast led by Dyan Cannon, Kris Kristofferson, and Tony Curtis.

The resulting film was critically mixed and did not launch Schwarzenegger’s directing career, but its production became a treasured memory for many involved. The episode ultimately argues that the joy of making something, and the friendships created in the process, can matter even when the finished work is deeply flawed.

## 分段落总结

[00:39] **A Christmas rerun and an unlikely cinematic mystery**

[事实] Malcolm Gladwell introduces the episode as a rerun of the previous year’s Christmas story about a made-for-television movie directed by Arnold Schwarzenegger.

[事实] Gladwell contrasts his minimalist attitude toward Christmas with producer Ben Naddaff-Hafrey’s enthusiasm for Christmas traditions and films.

[事实] The central question is why Schwarzenegger would direct an obscure remake of a classic Christmas romantic comedy.

[04:06] **The original Christmas in Connecticut**

[事实] Naddaff-Hafrey identifies the 1945 *Christmas in Connecticut* as his favorite Christmas movie and says he has watched it nearly every year since childhood.

[事实] The original stars Barbara Stanwyck as a celebrated domestic columnist whose supposed Connecticut farm, cooking ability, and family life are inventions.

[事实] Her deception becomes difficult to maintain when her publisher arranges for her to host a returning war hero at Christmas.

[事实] Naddaff-Hafrey discovered that the film had received a very different, action-oriented television remake directed by Schwarzenegger.

[06:16] **A modest remake conceived for cable television**

[事实] Independent producer Stan Brooks developed the remake for TNT during the early cable-TV boom, when networks needed original programming to attract subscribers and fill their schedules.

[事实] Brooks updated the heroine into a Martha Stewart-like television personality whose domestic empire is fraudulent.

[事实] Screenwriter Janet Brownell wrote an initial draft that remained close to the charm and structure of the original film.

[事实] The project was conceived as a small, low-budget Christmas movie, with Dyan Cannon cast in the leading role.

[08:32] **Schwarzenegger makes an unexpected offer**

[事实] Agent Lou Pitt contacted Brooks and proposed Arnold Schwarzenegger as director, prompting Brooks to initially assume the suggestion was a joke.

[事实] Schwarzenegger had recently completed *Terminator 2* but wanted to explore directing through a low-risk, family-friendly production with a small budget.

[事实] TNT approved the arrangement and offered Schwarzenegger $100,000 to direct.

[事实] Schwarzenegger accepted but required the production to shoot in Los Angeles within specific dates.

[12:05] **The script becomes “Schwarzeneggerized”**

[事实] During their first meeting, Schwarzenegger asked for more humor and jeopardy, including a large opening rescue sequence.

[事实] Brownell felt the project took a dramatic turn away from her original conception, while Brooks was determined not to lose Schwarzenegger.

[事实] *Commando* writer Jeph Loeb and his writing partner were hired to perform a comedy rewrite, though they said the underlying structure remained Brownell’s.

[事实] Schwarzenegger solicited feedback from filmmakers including Ivan Reitman and Steven Spielberg as the rewriting continued.

[事实] TNT executives recognized that the script was changing far more freely than would normally be permitted.

[17:47] **An eccentric cast and a high-profile gamble**

[事实] Dyan Cannon was joined by Tony Curtis and Kris Kristofferson, a combination that TNT executive Nick Lombardo considered unusually mismatched.

[事实] The production had a budget of approximately $3 million and a script derived from a 1940s comedy but revised by writers associated with an action blockbuster.

[事实] Participants were nervous because the budget was substantial by television-movie standards and Schwarzenegger had not previously directed a feature-length film.

[事实] Brooks believed Schwarzenegger wanted to prove that he could direct.

[19:34] **Production begins with action-movie ambitions**

[事实] Filming began in Los Angeles roughly two months before Christmas, with scenes shot largely in story order to simplify continuity for the inexperienced director.

[事实] The remake opens with park ranger Jefferson Jones undertaking a rescue during a blizzard, an action sequence added at Schwarzenegger’s request.

[事实] Crew members recalled that the limited soundstage set and blocking made the supposedly epic rescue look unconvincing.

[事实] Most of the production then moved to a house in South Pasadena, where the fictional Christmas television special was staged.

[22:35] **Star egos and uncontrolled screwball chaos**

[事实] Schwarzenegger’s exceptionally large trailer caused friction when lead actor Dyan Cannon questioned why the director’s trailer was bigger than hers.

[事实] The episode describes screwball comedy as controlled chaos, while this production combined a short schedule, limited budget, hastily revised script, and strong personalities.

[事实] The remake gives Jefferson Jones an incongruous background as a former comparative-literature professor and department-head candidate.

[事实] Naddaff-Hafrey characterizes Kristofferson’s performance as alternating between stiffness and unexpectedly sexual behavior.

[25:10] **The disastrous baby-bath scene**

[事实] Both versions include a scene in which the supposedly capable Elizabeth cannot bathe a baby and Jefferson assists her, helping establish their romantic connection.

[事实] In the remake, the actors cover the baby’s hair with shampoo and fail to rinse it properly, despite the scene requiring Jefferson to appear highly competent.

[事实] Interviewees describe the sequence as clumsy and identify the selected take as a directing failure.

[事实] Unscripted reactions acknowledging the remaining soap briefly conflict with the scripted premise that the bath is going well.

[28:08] **A chaotic set filled with genuine enthusiasm**

[事实] Crew members recalled visible sleigh wheels, repeated attempts to film elaborate comic mishaps, and greater chaos behind the camera than appeared onscreen.

[事实] Despite those problems, most interviewees remembered the production happily and appreciated its sheer improbability.

[事实] While *Terminator 2* was surpassing $500 million at the box office, Schwarzenegger remained intensely focused on the details of this small television movie.

[事实] His favored insult, “forehead,” entered the film’s dialogue and appeared on commemorative crew sweatshirts.

[事实] Production concluded after 20 shooting days with a party at Schwarzenegger’s restaurant.

[33:09] **The hurried creation of “Cooking for Two”**

[事实] At a later live event, author Mitch Albom explained that Brooks, his former college roommate, asked him to compose an upbeat food-themed song for the film.

[事实] The production had considered using Harry Connick Jr.’s “Recipe for Love” but could not afford it.

[事实] Albom wrote “Cooking for Two” on a two-day deadline, and his wife recorded the vocal because there was no time to find another singer.

[事实] Schwarzenegger selected that version, and the song was intended to play over the closing credits.

[38:03] **An outsized premiere and mixed reception**

[事实] Unlike an ordinary television movie, the remake received a large Directors Guild of America premiere with packed theaters, a red carpet, and a press line.

[事实] Hollywood figures including Ivan Reitman and James Cameron reportedly attended, while Brownell cried because she considered the film poor and her name remained attached to it.

[事实] TNT promoted the movie heavily and premiered it on April 13, 1992, shortly before Easter rather than during the Christmas season.

[事实] Schwarzenegger arranged another elaborate screening in Washington attended by politicians and other prominent figures.

[事实] Reviews were mixed, and the film did not establish Schwarzenegger as a director.

[41:30] **A flawed film remembered with affection**

[事实] Naddaff-Hafrey says he does not consider the film good, but loves its strangeness, total commitment, and joyful quality.

[事实] He frames the production as a story of friendship between Schwarzenegger and Brooks, who stayed close after filming even though they never made another movie together.

[事实] After becoming governor of California, Schwarzenegger appointed Brooks to the California Film Commission.

[事实] Brooks helped advocate for tax credits intended to combat film production leaving California; the first credits passed in 2009.

[43:50] **The gift of getting to make things**

[事实] Brooks told Schwarzenegger that he preferred regularly making television movies to waiting years between prestigious feature films, because his goal was simply to make movies.

[事实] Naddaff-Hafrey identifies with the pleasure of creating something even when it is improbable, overly elaborate, or imperfect.

[事实] He concludes that the visible enjoyment behind the production gives the film much of its enduring appeal.

[事实] The episode closes with a passage from *A Christmas Carol* about accepting other people’s laughter and finding satisfaction in one’s own joyful heart.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its oral-history approach. Accounts from writers, executives, producers, and crew members turn an obscure television movie into a vivid study of Hollywood ambition, celebrity influence, and the practical difficulties of directing comedy.

Its comic analysis is unusually specific: awkward blocking, mismatched performances, trailer politics, a badly handled baby bath, and an improvised insult all demonstrate how production choices shaped the finished film. The affectionate tone also prevents the discussion from becoming a simple catalogue of failures.

[推测] The episode occasionally indulges its subject at greater length than listeners seeking a concise film history may prefer, and the absence of a new interview with Schwarzenegger leaves his motives filtered through collaborators’ memories.

[推测] It will be especially rewarding for listeners interested in filmmaking, Hollywood production lore, Christmas cinema, creative failure, or the idea that an imperfect work can retain value because of the enthusiasm and relationships behind it.
