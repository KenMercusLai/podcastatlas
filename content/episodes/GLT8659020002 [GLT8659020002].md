# Roman London: A Walking Tour Through Londinium

## 概览

This episode is a walking tour of Roman London, moving from London Bridge through Bank, the Mithraeum, Cannon Street, Leadenhall Market, the Barbican, an underground car park, and finally the Gherkin.

The central argument is that Londinium existed because of geography: it was the lowest practical crossing point on the Thames, a port, a road hub, and therefore an obvious place for commerce, military control, and provincial administration.

The episode repeatedly stresses how fragmentary Roman London is. Most remains are underground, reused, destroyed, or hidden in modern settings, so the history has to be reconstructed from archaeology, scattered texts, and later myths.

It ends on a melancholy note with the burial of an unknown Roman London girl near the Gherkin, using her grave to reflect on the decline and disappearance of Roman London.

## 分段落总结

[00:54] **Opening With Conrad And The Lost City**

[事实] The hosts open with a passage from Joseph Conrad’s *Heart of Darkness* imagining a Roman encountering Britain before London existed.
[事实] They frame Roman London as fascinating because the modern imperial capital once did not exist at all.
[推测] The Conrad passage is used to make London feel strange, peripheral, and almost post-apocalyptic before and after Rome.

[02:55] **London Bridge And The Geography Of Londinium**

[事实] The hosts begin on London Bridge, emphasizing that the Thames area was once marshy, undeveloped, and very different from modern London.
[事实] London developed because this was the lowest bridging point on the Thames and also a viable port.
[事实] After the Roman invasion in AD 43, a bridge and roads helped make the site strategically important.
[推测] The episode presents geography, more than deliberate urban planning, as the reason London became the natural capital of southern Britain.

[06:18] **Why Rome Invaded Britain**

[事实] The pre-Roman population is described as Celtic but already influenced by Rome through trade, wine, coinage, and political contact.
[事实] Britain is described as agriculturally rich, mineral-rich, and attractive to an expanding empire.
[事实] Claudius is said to have needed military prestige and political legitimacy from the invasion.
[推测] The invasion is presented as both economically useful and personally valuable to Claudius.

[08:38] **The Earliest Roman London**

[事实] Londinium grew organically and became a military base controlling access to the continent.
[事实] A wooden bridge was probably built by AD 47.
[事实] An IOU dated 8 January AD 57 is cited as evidence of early written and commercial life in London.
[事实] By the end of the first century, London had likely replaced Colchester as the provincial capital.

[10:49] **Number One Poultry And Boudicca’s Destruction**

[事实] At Number One Poultry, excavations found 48 skulls of young men and charred buildings.
[事实] The burnt remains are dated to around AD 60 and are linked to Boudicca’s revolt.
[事实] The hosts describe this as the first of three major burnings of London, followed later by the Great Fire of 1666 and the Blitz.
[事实] The Walbrook, a now-hidden river, helped preserve organic Roman material in its wet deposits.

[13:09] **Boudicca’s Revolt**

[事实] Boudicca was queen of the Iceni, whose territory is associated with East Anglia and Norfolk.
[事实] After her husband Prasutagus died, the Romans abused Boudicca and her daughters, provoking revolt.
[事实] Her forces burned Colchester, London, and Verulamium before being defeated by Suetonius Paulinus.
[推测] The skulls found at Number One Poultry may have been civilian victims or head-hunting trophies, but the hosts say this cannot be known securely.

[15:27] **Who Lived In Roman London**

[事实] The hosts say London’s population probably included Britons, settlers, traders, and people from Gaul.
[事实] A tombstone of a Gaulish administrator is mentioned as evidence of people coming to Britain after Boudicca’s revolt.
[事实] London is described as more closely connected to Gaul than to Rome itself.
[推测] Roman London is portrayed as a mixed colonial and commercial city rather than a purely Italian settlement.

[16:26] **The London Mithraeum**

[事实] The Mithraeum was found in the 1950s near the Walbrook, after wartime destruction opened opportunities for archaeology.
[事实] The temple was inconvenient for developers and was moved to an unsympathetic roadside setting.
[事实] Bloomberg later restored it in a new underground display as part of a planning condition.
[事实] The hosts describe the current Mithraeum experience as free, atmospheric, and worth visiting.

[18:02] **Mithras, Documents, And Preserved Objects**

[事实] Mithras worship is described as an all-male cult with ranks, initiation, and useful social connections.
[事实] The London Mithraeum is said to have been built around AD 240.
[事实] The museum includes the earliest written document found in Britain and another tablet from around AD 65-70 containing the name Londinium.
[事实] Preserved finds include coins, amphorae, keys, combs, an oar, a door fragment, and sandals.

[20:56] **Fragments, Gaps, And Myth**

[事实] The hosts stress that there is limited written evidence for Roman London and that many buildings have vanished.
[事实] Archaeology is therefore essential for reconstructing the city’s history.
[事实] Later generations turned Roman fragments into myths, legends, and fantasies.
[推测] The episode treats uncertainty as part of Roman London’s appeal, not just a problem for historians.

[22:57] **The London Stone**

[事实] The London Stone, near Cannon Street, is first mentioned in the 12th century.
[事实] It was associated with the idea that someone who struck or touched it could claim authority over London.
[事实] Jack Cade is said to have struck it with his sword in 1450 and claimed to be Lord of London.
[事实] Theories about the stone connect it with Brutus, druids, the Palladium, and London folklore.
[推测] One theory is that it may preserve a memory of the entrance to the Roman governor’s headquarters, but the hosts stress that this is uncertain.

[27:04] **Why So Little Roman London Survives**

[事实] The modern City of London does not follow the Roman street plan; its layout is essentially Anglo-Saxon.
[事实] Roman stone was reused in later buildings.
[事实] After the Romans left, the interior of London appears to have been abandoned, while Anglo-Saxon settlement developed further west at Aldwych.
[事实] Remaining Roman traces include the Mithraeum and the amphitheatre beneath Guildhall.

[30:14] **The Forum And Basilica**

[事实] The hosts walk toward Leadenhall Market, where the Roman basilica and forum once stood.
[事实] The basilica served as a market, assembly space, town hall, and law court.
[事实] It was three stories high and became one of the largest Roman buildings north of the Alps.
[推测] The hosts use the basilica to show that Londinium projected Roman civic grandeur despite Britain’s peripheral status.

[31:29] **A Roman Wall In A Barber Shop**

[事实] In the basement of Nicholson and Griffin in Leadenhall Market, a surviving pier from the basilica is visible.
[事实] The hosts identify the masonry by its recognizable Roman brickwork.
[事实] A display states that the basilica was built around AD 70 and enlarged from around AD 90 to 120.
[事实] The Roman forum is contrasted with the later irregular street plan of the City of London.

[35:07] **The Roman City Wall**

[事实] The London wall was built toward the end of the second century.
[事实] The hosts say it may have been associated with Clodius Albinus, governor of Britain and later usurper.
[事实] The wall is interpreted less as a response to immediate military threat and more as a symbol of capital status.
[事实] Later gate names such as Ludgate, Newgate, Bishopsgate, and Aldgate preserve traces of the wall’s line.

[37:21] **Barbican, Cripplegate, And The Roman Fort**

[事实] The Barbican takes its name from a late Latin word meaning watchtower.
[事实] The visible wall near the Barbican is medieval rather than Roman, though it follows the Roman line.
[事实] Near Cripplegate, the hosts identify the area as the site of the Roman fort, built around AD 120.
[事实] The fort was joined into the later wall system and protected the city and governor.

[40:10] **The London Wall Car Park**

[事实] The hosts enter the London Wall underground car park, built during post-Blitz redevelopment.
[事实] The street named London Wall follows the line of the old city wall.
[事实] A chunk of the original Roman wall was discovered and preserved inside the car park.
[事实] The hosts note that Britain has unusually strong archaeological knowledge of its Roman province because of conservation laws and a long antiquarian tradition.

[43:19] **Hadrian, Fires, And Redevelopment**

[事实] The hosts discuss fires in London around the time of Hadrian’s visit to Britain.
[事实] Hadrian may have sponsored redevelopment after those fires.
[推测] It is unclear whether the fires were accidents or evidence of unrest.

[44:26] **An Authentic Roman Wall In Parking Bays**

[事实] At bay 52 of the car park, the hosts find an authentic stretch of Roman wall.
[事实] The wall occupies space where cars would otherwise park and is fenced off with a no-parking sign.
[事实] The hosts emphasize the contrast between ancient masonry and the unromantic modern car park setting.
[推测] The comic disappointment of the setting is used to make the survival of the wall feel stranger and more memorable.

[47:01] **The Unknown Girl Near The Gherkin**

[事实] The final stop is near the Gherkin and Holland House, where an unknown Roman London girl is buried.
[事实] The inscription identifies her as an unknown girl from Roman London.
[事实] Her bones were dated to around AD 350-400.
[事实] Burial inside the city limits was unusual for Romans.
[推测] Because the burial was inside the city, the hosts suggest she may have been Christian.

[48:35] **The End Of Roman London**

[事实] By the late fourth century, London was in decline, with falling population and buildings giving way to market gardens.
[事实] Activity may have concentrated closer to the port and docks.
[事实] The girl’s remains were stored by the Museum of London before being reburied in 2007 after the Gherkin was completed.
[事实] A memorial service and procession accompanied the reburial.
[推测] Her grave becomes a symbol of how Roman London fades from history while still resurfacing through archaeology.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s main strength is its combination of place, archaeology, and historical imagination. It shows Roman London not through grand surviving monuments, but through fragments hidden under offices, markets, churches, and car parks.

[推测] The walking-tour format makes the subject accessible because the hosts keep connecting ancient Londinium to familiar modern landmarks. Their humor also helps carry sections where the material evidence is physically small or visually underwhelming.

The limitation is that several important claims remain uncertain by necessity: the London Stone, the identity of the unknown girl, the cause of Hadrianic fires, and the meaning of some archaeological finds cannot be settled from the transcript alone.

[推测] This episode is especially suitable for listeners interested in London, Roman Britain, urban archaeology, and the way modern cities preserve buried layers of earlier history.
