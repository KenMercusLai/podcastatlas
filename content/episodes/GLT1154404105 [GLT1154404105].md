# The Fall of Robespierre: The Coup of Thermidor

## 概览

This episode reconstructs the fall of Maximilien Robespierre over 26–27 July 1794. Drawing heavily on Colin Jones’s research, the hosts argue that the outcome was neither inevitable nor simply a principled reaction against the Reign of Terror.

Robespierre’s position deteriorated amid personal feuds, institutional rivalries, public discontent and fear of another purge. His vague, accusatory final speech united potential targets against him, while his allies repeatedly failed to act decisively.

The crisis culminated in Robespierre’s arrest, a briefly successful rescue by the Paris Commune and the collapse of an attempted insurrection. His execution enabled former participants in the Terror to blame him for the regime, but it also unleashed a broader dismantling of revolutionary government.

## 分段落总结

[03:08] **Robespierre’s Final Political Mistake**

[事实] The episode opens with extracts from Robespierre’s speech to the National Convention on 26 July 1794, in which he rejects accusations of tyranny and presents himself as a persecuted defender of liberty.

[事实] The hosts describe the speech as rambling, self-pitying and politically disastrous, although one of them jokingly praises its rhetoric.

[事实] This was effectively Robespierre’s final audible public speech and the beginning of the climax of the podcast’s French Revolution series.

[05:10] **Why the Fall Was Not Inevitable**

[事实] Detailed reports commissioned after the event allow historians to reconstruct 26–27 July almost minute by minute, although private conversations and motives remain uncertain.

[事实] The hosts stress that Robespierre’s fall was contingent and could easily have ended differently.

[事实] They argue that the struggle of 9 Thermidor was driven less by disagreement over the Terror than by personal survival and competition for power.

[07:40] **The Great Terror Accelerates**

[事实] The Law of Prairial expanded the definition of “enemies of the people,” removed legal representation and witnesses, and left death as the only sentence following conviction.

[事实] In the seven weeks between 10 June and 27 July 1794, approximately 1,400 people were guillotined—more than during the previous fourteen months.

[事实] Victims included prominent figures such as Malesherbes, Antoine Lavoisier and André Chénier, alongside ordinary people punished for complaints or allegedly counter-revolutionary remarks.

[事实] Contemporary observers reported that fear had become so pervasive that citizens could not feel confident they would still be alive two days later.

[11:14] **Divisions Inside the Committee of Public Safety**

[事实] The Committee contained Robespierre and his allies Saint-Just and Couthon, indispensable technocrats such as Carnot, Prieur and Lindet, and radicals including Collot d’Herbois and Billaud-Varenne.

[事实] Surviving records indicate that the technocrats drafted most Committee orders, while Robespierre and Couthon contributed relatively little to its routine administrative work.

[事实] Committee members worked extreme hours and were exhausted, increasing resentment toward Robespierre’s absences and lectures about virtue.

[事实] Political, religious and personal divisions grew as atheistic radicals opposed Robespierre’s Cult of the Supreme Being and technocrats tired of factional conflict.

[19:17] **The Catherine Théot Affair**

[事实] The Committee of General Security resented the encroachment of the Committee of Public Safety and Robespierre’s creation of an independent police bureau.

[事实] Marc-Guillaume Vadier publicized the case of Catherine Théot, an elderly religious visionary who allegedly described Robespierre as a prophet.

[推测] The hosts consider it likely that Vadier intended to use coerced testimony or forged evidence to associate Robespierre with claims of divinity.

[事实] Robespierre blocked an open tribunal case against Théot, but the dispute left relations between the two governing committees close to collapse.

[22:48] **Military Victory and Robespierre’s Withdrawal**

[事实] The French victory at Fleurus on 26 June opened the way into the Low Countries and demonstrated that France was no longer in immediate danger of invasion.

[事实] Saint-Just returned to find Robespierre engaged in a furious dispute with fellow Committee members, who accused him of dictatorship.

[事实] Robespierre then stayed away from the Committee and the Convention for approximately six weeks.

[推测] Historians cited in the episode suggest that Robespierre came to regard his colleagues as corrupted or treacherous and may have contemplated another purge.

[27:10] **A Coalition of Frightened Enemies**

[事实] Deputies feared that Robespierre was preparing another purge, particularly those implicated in corruption, dechristianization or excessive violence in the provinces.

[事实] Joseph Fouché spread rumours that Robespierre possessed a list of intended victims and encouraged threatened deputies to cooperate.

[事实] Paul Barras and Jean-Lambert Tallien also had reasons to fear Robespierre; Tallien’s imprisoned lover, Thérésa Cabarrus, urged him to act.

[事实] These figures did not yet possess a detailed common plan, but their desire for self-preservation created a growing anti-Robespierre alliance.

[32:23] **Public Discontent in Paris**

[事实] The government banned spontaneous celebrations of the victory at Fleurus because they had not been officially authorized and brought different social classes together.

[事实] Inflation, food shortages and rising bread prices produced strikes and protests among Parisian workers.

[事实] The Commune suppressed meetings and industrial action, while new wage controls threatened severe reductions in workers’ incomes.

[推测] These policies weakened the willingness of the sans-culottes to defend the Commune and Robespierre when the political crisis arrived.

[35:40] **A Failed Attempt at Reconciliation**

[事实] Robespierre returned to the Committee of Public Safety on 23 July after six weeks away, and members made a temporary effort to display unity.

[事实] They agreed that Saint-Just would present a report to the Convention reassuring deputies that the governing committees remained united.

[事实] The following evening, Couthon attacked the Committee of General Security and vaguely denounced corrupt, bloodstained enemies without naming them.

[事实] Robespierre was simultaneously preparing his own major speech for 26 July.

[40:19] **The Speech That United Robespierre’s Enemies**

[事实] Robespierre’s two-hour speech accused the finance committee, the Committee of General Security and members of the Committee of Public Safety of corruption or failure.

[事实] He repeatedly referred to conspirators and monsters but named few specific targets.

[推测] The hosts interpret the speech as preparation for a future purge involving the Commune and the sans-culottes.

[事实] Because the accusations were vague, many deputies feared they might be targeted, producing resistance rather than intimidation.

[48:00] **The Convention Answers Back**

[事实] Pierre-Joseph Cambon, head of the finance committee, unexpectedly challenged Robespierre and accused him of paralyzing the Convention.

[事实] Billaud-Varenne, Vadier and Amar joined the attack, criticizing Robespierre’s absence and his unsupported allegations.

[事实] The Convention refused to circulate Robespierre’s speech throughout France, an unusual and humiliating rejection.

[事实] At the Jacobin Club that evening, however, Robespierre received enthusiastic support, while Collot and Billaud were threatened with the guillotine and forced to leave.

[52:02] **The Committees Break Apart**

[事实] Collot and Billaud returned to the Committee offices and furiously accused Saint-Just, Robespierre and Couthon of plotting against the Republic.

[事实] Saint-Just initially promised to submit his planned report for Committee approval before presenting it to the Convention.

[事实] He later changed his mind, possibly after meeting Robespierre, and decided to address the Convention without prior approval.

[事实] Meanwhile, Tallien spent the night encouraging moderate deputies to resist Robespierre.

[57:55] **The Arrest on 9 Thermidor**

[事实] Saint-Just began speaking at noon on 27 July but was interrupted almost immediately by Tallien, who denounced Robespierre as a tyrant and displayed a dagger.

[事实] Collot, presiding over the Convention, repeatedly denied Robespierre the opportunity to speak while calling his opponents to the rostrum.

[事实] Vadier ridiculed Robespierre’s supposed connection to Catherine Théot, and the chamber’s laughter undermined Robespierre’s moral authority.

[事实] As Robespierre struggled to make himself heard, a deputy declared that “the blood of Danton” was choking him.

[事实] The Convention voted to arrest Robespierre, Saint-Just, Couthon, Augustin Robespierre, Philippe Le Bas and National Guard commander François Hanriot.

[65:02] **The Commune Launches an Insurrection**

[事实] The Paris Commune declared an insurrection, mobilized National Guard units and ordered city prisons not to accept the arrested deputies.

[事实] Robespierre and his allies were consequently released or recovered and brought toward the Hôtel de Ville.

[事实] Many sans-culottes hesitated to answer the Commune’s call because of wage controls, suppressed local politics and resentment toward the authorities.

[事实] The conflict also appeared to many Parisians as an obscure struggle between rival Montagnard factions rather than a clear contest between revolution and counter-revolution.

[71:28] **Hanriot Throws Away the Decisive Opportunity**

[事实] Hanriot was freed by approximately 3,000 guardsmen with thirty cannons, who positioned themselves near the undefended Convention.

[事实] Instead of attacking, Hanriot ordered his forces to regroup at the Hôtel de Ville, allowing the Convention time to organize its own troops under Barras.

[推测] The hosts identify this hesitation as the decisive turning point: an immediate assault might have saved Robespierre and transformed the Revolution’s subsequent course.

[73:08] **Robespierre Fails to Lead**

[事实] Robespierre reached the Hôtel de Ville at about 10:30 p.m. and was welcomed as the expected leader of the resistance.

[事实] Exhausted and hoarse, he provided no clear direction while the assembled guards gradually drifted away.

[事实] The Convention, by contrast, declared its intention to destroy the alleged conspirators and sent troops toward the Hôtel de Ville.

[推测] The hosts argue that Robespierre’s attachment to legality and self-image as a virtuous republican left him unable to act like the dictator his enemies claimed he was.

[78:13] **The Collapse at the Hôtel de Ville**

[事实] By approximately 2:00 a.m., the square outside the Hôtel de Ville was nearly deserted, and Convention forces entered with little resistance.

[事实] Augustin Robespierre and Hanriot fell from windows, Couthon was thrown down stairs, and Le Bas killed himself.

[事实] Saint-Just remained calm, while Maximilien Robespierre was found with his jaw shattered by a pistol shot.

[推测] Although opponents later credited a Convention soldier, the hosts accept the prevailing interpretation that Robespierre probably wounded himself in a failed suicide attempt.

[81:14] **Humiliation and Execution**

[事实] Robespierre was taken back to the Committee of Public Safety, where a surgeon removed loose teeth and bandaged his shattered jaw.

[事实] Despite intense pain and mockery, he attempted to restore his appearance and preserve his dignity.

[事实] Robespierre, Couthon, Saint-Just and their associates were guillotined publicly on 28 July before a hostile crowd.

[事实] Robespierre was thirty-six years old when he died.

[86:00] **Why Robespierre Fell**

[事实] The episode rejects the conventional explanation that Robespierre was overthrown by principled opponents of the Terror.

[事实] Many leading figures in his downfall—including Collot, Billaud, Barrère, Tallien, Barras and Fouché—had themselves been enthusiastic practitioners of revolutionary violence.

[事实] The hosts attribute his defeat to making too many enemies, failing to build alliances, issuing vague threats and treating disagreement as evidence of corruption or treason.

[推测] Robespierre’s rigid division of politics into virtue and treachery made his behaviour predictable and ultimately allowed more pragmatic opponents to outmanoeuvre him.

[88:13] **The Construction of the Robespierre Myth**

[事实] After his death, former allies rapidly denied supporting Robespierre and blamed him for the Revolution’s violence.

[事实] The expression “Reign of Terror” came into use after the Thermidorian coup as his former associates portrayed him as a dictator.

[事实] The hosts acknowledge that Robespierre was the Terror’s most articulate defender but emphasize that he was neither its sole architect nor its only enthusiastic supporter.

[推测] Making Robespierre the singular villain allowed other participants to obscure their own responsibility.

[89:51] **Thermidor Dismantles the Terror**

[事实] Robespierre’s opponents initially expected to continue governing through the existing revolutionary system, but they unleashed political forces they could not control.

[事实] The Convention stripped powers from the committees, repealed the Law of Prairial and eventually closed the Jacobin Club.

[事实] Wage and price controls were abolished, contributing to severe inflation and economic hardship for much of the population.

[事实] Surviving Girondins returned, moderates gained control of the Convention, and former terrorists themselves faced arrest, deportation or execution.

[93:04] **The Revolution Turns on Its Prosecutors**

[事实] In March 1795, Collot, Billaud, Barrère and Vadier were prosecuted as terrorists and sentenced to deportation to French Guiana.

[事实] Revolutionary Tribunal prosecutor Fouquier-Tinville faced a forty-one-day trial involving more than 400 witnesses.

[事实] Fouquier-Tinville defended himself by arguing that he had merely performed his duties and followed the law, but he was sentenced to death.

[事实] His execution symbolized the revolutionary machinery turning against those who had previously operated it.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s greatest strength is its detailed, suspenseful reconstruction of Thermidor as an open political crisis rather than an inevitable historical outcome. It connects speeches, personal rivalries, administrative exhaustion, economic grievances and split-second decisions without reducing the fall to a single cause.

Its central insight is that Robespierre was not simply defeated by opponents of political violence. He was destroyed by former collaborators who feared becoming its next victims, after his rhetoric made nearly everyone feel threatened.

The conversational humour makes a complex sequence accessible, although frequent jokes and modern analogies occasionally interrupt the narrative and can blur the distinction between documented evidence and interpretation.

[推测] The episode is especially valuable for listeners interested in revolutionary politics, coalition-building and the role of individual decisions during institutional collapse; newcomers may benefit from familiarity with the earlier episodes on Danton, the Terror and the Cult of the Supreme Being.
