# Revenge of the Forgotten Borough

## 概览

The series finale revisits Staten Island’s 1993 secession referendum, which passed locally by nearly two to one but was later blocked by the New York State Assembly. It argues that the referendum’s real political legacy was not independence, but the unusually high Staten Island turnout that helped elect Rudy Giuliani mayor.

The episode identifies resentment as the shared engine behind secession and Giuliani’s rise: residents of peripheral places felt deprived of money, political power, and respect by an urban center that neither understood nor valued them. Giuliani appealed to that grievance and delivered tangible benefits to Staten Island, including a free ferry, waterfront investment, and the closure of the Fresh Kills landfill.

The narrative then connects Giuliani’s politics to Donald Trump and January 6, tracing a recurring strongman appeal rooted in perceived abandonment, racial reaction, and hostility toward governing elites. It closes at the transformed Fresh Kills site, arguing that a democracy organized around equal individuals must still recognize the importance of place—and remember communities that see themselves as forgotten.

## 分段落总结

[00:59] **The Secession Landslide That Went Nowhere**

[事实] On election night in 1993, approximately 66% of Staten Island voters supported secession, producing celebrations across the borough.

[事实] The referendum expressed Staten Island’s desire to leave New York City, but final authority rested with the state government.

[事实] When the proposal reached the New York State Assembly the following year, the speaker stopped it, leaving Staten Island within the city.

[推测] The vote’s importance ultimately lay less in its formal result than in the political force and turnout it revealed.

[04:28] **Molinari Chooses Giuliani’s Victory**

[事实] Staten Island borough president Guy Molinari was absent from the island’s secession celebration because he was in Manhattan with Rudy Giuliani as Giuliani won the mayoralty.

[事实] The host presents the 1993 election as closely connected to the 2016 presidential election and argues that the Staten Island movement anticipated contemporary American politics.

[事实] Giuliani and the secession campaign are described as products of the same political engine.

[06:40] **Giuliani’s Outer-Borough Alliance**

[事实] Giuliani first became nationally prominent as a federal prosecutor pursuing organized crime, Wall Street misconduct, and government corruption.

[事实] Molinari encouraged Giuliani’s political ambitions, supported his first mayoral campaign, and shared with him an Italian American, working-class, outer-borough outlook.

[事实] Giuliani’s appeal on Staten Island combined resentment toward Manhattan, identification with police, and a promise to restore order through zero-tolerance government.

[推测] Molinari’s project was not merely to remove Staten Island from New York, but also to remake city government around the values and grievances of its outer boroughs.

[12:47] **The 1992 Police Riot at City Hall**

[事实] About 10,000 off-duty police officers protested Mayor David Dinkins and proposed civilian oversight of the police; many participants were drunk, displayed racist imagery, used racial abuse, and charged the steps of City Hall.

[事实] Giuliani was asked to help calm the crowd but instead blamed Dinkins for low police morale, attacked his policies, and intensified the protesters’ anger.

[事实] The disturbance spread to the Brooklyn Bridge, where protesters blocked traffic, threatened drivers, assaulted a reporter, and rocked a car occupied by older Black passengers.

[事实] Former deputy mayor Norman Steisel said he regarded Giuliani’s speech as incitement.

[19:04] **The Geography of Resentment**

[事实] Political scientist Katherine Cramer studied political conversations in rural Wisconsin and repeatedly encountered resentment toward urban centers.

[事实] She grouped that resentment around three perceived inequalities: money and jobs, political power, and social respect.

[事实] Cramer found that these feelings intensified beginning in the 1970s and extended beyond a simple rural-versus-urban divide to places on the periphery of expanding cities.

[推测] This framework explains Staten Island’s secession movement as one local expression of a broader politics of remoteness and perceived disrespect.

[21:43] **How Secession Elected Giuliani**

[事实] The episode says secession and support for Giuliani were two expressions of the same resentment.

[事实] The referendum drove Staten Island turnout far above its 1989 level, and 82% of the borough’s voters backed Giuliani.

[事实] Dinkins campaign officials identified Staten Island’s unexpectedly large numbers as the decisive difference in the mayoral result.

[事实] Staten Island’s votes helped make Giuliani New York City’s first Republican mayor in decades.

[23:03] **What Giuliani Delivered—and at What Cost**

[事实] Giuliani made the Staten Island Ferry free, invested nearly half a billion dollars in the waterfront, supported a baseball park, and worked with Molinari to close the Fresh Kills landfill.

[事实] The last regular barge of city trash left Fresh Kills on March 22, 2001, ending what Giuliani called Staten Island’s inequitable burden.

[事实] Elsewhere in the city, his administration denied or conditioned shelter for homeless people, often refused meetings with Black leaders, and ordered police to clear the streets.

[事实] Frank Cipolla recalled Giuliani insulting reporters and refusing to engage with questions he considered stupid.

[推测] Giuliani demonstrated how a leader could materially reward one neglected constituency while directing coercion and contempt toward others.

[25:16] **Giuliani as Trump’s Model**

[事实] In a 1999 television appearance, Donald Trump praised Giuliani as the greatest mayor in New York City history.

[事实] The episode describes both men as outer-borough figures seeking recognition and status in Manhattan.

[事实] Giuliani celebrated his 1993 victory at the New York Hilton, the same venue where Trump celebrated his presidential victory 23 years later.

[推测] Giuliani offered Trump an early model of leadership built around confrontation, personal dominance, and the promise to champion people who felt disrespected.

[27:12] **The Politics of the “Forgotten”**

[事实] The episode links Staten Island’s identity as the “forgotten borough” with Trump’s promise that America’s “forgotten men and women” would no longer be forgotten.

[事实] It says that, as the civil rights movement advanced, many white middle- and working-class Americans increasingly viewed themselves as neglected in the name of progress.

[事实] Research cited in the episode argues that populism often arose not simply among the poorest individuals, but in places that perceived themselves as politically and culturally irrelevant.

[事实] The host distinguishes a 1993 Giuliani vote from a 2016 Trump vote while arguing that both drew energy from the same underlying resentment.

[29:04] **From City Hall to January 6**

[事实] Twenty-nine years after the City Hall protest, Giuliani told Trump supporters on January 6, 2021, to have “trial by combat.”

[事实] Former members of the Dinkins administration saw strong parallels between the 1992 police protest and the attack on the US Capitol.

[事实] Marcia Smith connected the reaction to New York’s first Black mayor with the later reaction to the country’s first Black president.

[推测] The episode presents the 1990s New York conflict as an early version of later national struggles over race, legitimacy, and who is entitled to wield power.

[30:46] **Fresh Kills: From Landfill to Park**

[事实] Fresh Kills, once the world’s largest landfill, is being transformed into one of New York City’s largest parks.

[事实] Methane pipes, paths, benches, grass, and open spaces now cover buried waste, although deep-rooted trees cannot safely be planted there.

[事实] Anthropologist Robin Nagle describes the landfill as a material record of ordinary New Yorkers who lived in or passed through the city between 1948 and 2001.

[事实] Nagle observes that New Yorkers routinely occupy land built from discarded material without remembering its origins.

[推测] Fresh Kills becomes a physical metaphor for the episode’s argument: suppressed histories and neglected people remain beneath the civic landscape even when their presence is forgotten.

[34:25] **A Democracy of People Must Still Remember Place**

[事实] The host identifies a national tension between equal voting rights for individuals and equal representation for states regardless of population.

[事实] After the Board of Estimate was abolished in 1989, New York City shifted toward representation based on people rather than boroughs as places.

[事实] The episode argues that this reform overlooked the continuing political importance of geography and remoteness.

[事实] Its closing prescription is to create a shared civic story that recognizes every place and every person, rather than allowing neglected communities to turn repeatedly toward strongmen.

[36:00] **Putting Staten Island Back in the Song**

[事实] Woody Guthrie’s daughter Nora discovered that an early draft of “This Land Is Your Land” originally said “from California to Staten Island” before Guthrie replaced it with “the New York Island.”

[事实] During the secession era, Staten Islanders petitioned her to restore the original wording, but the familiar version had already been published and widely adopted.

[事实] The episode closes with musician Sam Lewis performing a version that reinstates Staten Island.

[推测] Restoring the discarded lyric symbolically fulfills the episode’s central demand: to include the forgotten borough within the nation’s story.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s principal strength is its ability to connect municipal history, political psychology, race, geography, and national populism without losing sight of Staten Island’s specific experience. Archival recordings and testimony give its argument a strong narrative foundation, while the Fresh Kills visit turns an abstract discussion of neglect into a memorable physical image.

Its treatment of Giuliani is especially effective because it holds two realities together: his administration delivered significant, concrete gains to Staten Island, yet its methods and priorities imposed serious costs on other communities. This complicates any simple account of either grievance or political leadership.

[推测] The broad parallel between 1993 and 2016 is persuasive as an interpretive framework, though some listeners may find its language of “cosmic” connection more evocative than analytically precise. The episode is best suited to listeners interested in New York history, urban politics, populism, racial backlash, and the enduring political consequences of geographic inequality.
