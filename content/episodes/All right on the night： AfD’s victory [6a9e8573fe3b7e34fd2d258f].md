# All Right on the Night: AfD’s Victory

## 概览

This episode of *The Intelligence* covers three stories: the AfD’s unexpectedly large victory in Saxony-Anhalt, the climate case for reducing aircraft contrails, and young South Koreans’ renewed interest in Buddhism.

The first segment argues that the AfD’s 44% showing is a major political shock for Germany. The result weakens the Christian Democrats, raises the possibility of a far-right state premier, and challenges Germany’s post-war political norm of excluding extremists from power.

The second segment explains that persistent aircraft contrails may account for a large share of aviation’s warming effect. A North Atlantic trial, Operation Blue Skies, is testing whether small flight-altitude changes can reduce contrails safely and cheaply.

The final segment looks at Buddhist expos, raves, retreats, and modernized religious products in South Korea. It presents Buddhism as appealing to some young Koreans because it offers calm and perspective without demanding belief in a deity, while also noting concerns about commercialization.

[01:30] **AfD’s Election Breakthrough**

[事实] The Alternative for Germany party won a major victory in the East German state of Saxony-Anhalt.
[事实] The party is described as so extreme that some other European populist-right organisations refuse to work with it.
[事实] The episode says the AfD has moved closer to the prospect of a right-wing nationalist leader running a German state for the first time since the Second World War.
[推测] The segment frames the result as both an electoral shock and a test of Germany’s democratic guardrails.

[02:23] **Election Night Shock**

[事实] Tom Nuttall attended the AfD’s election-night party in Magdeburg.
[事实] Exit polls projected the AfD at 44% of the vote, higher than opinion polls had suggested.
[事实] AfD figures and supporters reacted ecstatically, singing German songs and the national anthem.
[事实] Tom says the party expected to come first, but not by such a high margin.

[03:12] **CDU Collapse**

[事实] Saxony-Anhalt had been governed by the centre-right Christian Democrats, the party of Chancellor Friedrich Merz.
[事实] The CDU’s vote roughly halved to 17%.
[事实] Tom says some tactical voting may have moved voters to parties such as the Greens to help them pass the 5% threshold.
[事实] Some people called for the resignation of Sven Schulze, the CDU premier of Saxony-Anhalt.
[推测] The result creates pressure not only at state level but also for Friedrich Merz nationally.

[05:22] **BSW as Potential Kingmaker**

[事实] The Bündnis Sahra Wagenknecht, described as populist-left, pro-Russian, and anti-elite, entered parliament with five of 83 seats.
[事实] Unlike other parties, the BSW had not ruled out working with the AfD.
[事实] Its lead candidate said she could imagine cooperation on specific projects.
[事实] Possible areas mentioned include Russia sanctions, energy policy, and school curricula.
[推测] The BSW’s entry changes the arithmetic and may be crucial to whether the AfD can gain power.

[06:41] **Stalemate Risk**

[事实] Ulrich Siegmund of the AfD has said he would not want to lead a minority government.
[事实] Tom says there does not appear to be an alternative to a minority arrangement at that point.
[事实] Saxony-Anhalt law has no time limit for a minister-president vote.
[事实] Sven Schulze could therefore continue as acting premier more or less indefinitely.
[推测] Fresh elections sometime the following year are presented as one possible outcome if parliament reaches stalemate.

[08:01] **Germany’s Political Reckoning**

[事实] Tom describes the AfD’s 44% as a body blow to a country whose post-war identity was built around excluding extremists from power.
[事实] The state’s intelligence services describe the AfD as right-wing extremist.
[事实] The AfD is described as supporting “re-migration,” containing ethno-nationalist elements, sceptical of Germany’s role in the EU and NATO, and in large parts pro-Russian and anti-Ukrainian.
[事实] Some people in the German mainstream think the party should be banned.
[推测] The result intensifies Germany’s debate over whether the AfD should be treated as a normal opposition party or an anti-system threat.

[08:58] **European Implications**

[事实] Tom says there is no direct European foreign-policy impact from the Saxony-Anhalt result because German states do not control foreign policy.
[事实] Many right-wing nationalist parties in Europe celebrated the result.
[事实] Marine Le Pen’s National Rally was presented as a notable exception because of poor relations with the AfD.
[推测] The AfD victory may boost morale among European nationalist parties by suggesting that if this can happen in Germany, it can happen elsewhere.

[11:01] **Contrails and Operation Blue Skies**

[事实] The episode says people have known for about 30 years that aircraft contrails can warm the Earth.
[事实] Oliver Morton says a large trial over the North Atlantic is beginning.
[事实] The trial is called Operation Blue Skies.
[事实] The North Atlantic is described as a sensitive area for contrails and close to an ideal test location.

[11:45] **Why Contrails Warm the Planet**

[事实] Contrails can reflect sunlight into space, which cools, but they also trap outgoing infrared heat and re-emit it downward.
[事实] On average, contrails are warming, especially at night when there is no sunlight to reflect.
[事实] Oliver says contrails’ warming effect may be somewhat less than, or similar to, the warming effect of aircraft carbon dioxide emissions.
[事实] He says one-third of aviation’s climate impact from contrails is a more secure estimate, while up to half is possible.
[推测] The segment presents contrail reduction as one of the more significant near-term aviation climate interventions.

[13:17] **How Persistent Contrails Form**

[事实] The most important contrails are the ones that last, spread out, and become broader pale clouds.
[事实] They form when aircraft pass through cold, damp areas called ice supersaturated regions.
[事实] These regions are usually high in the troposphere, around 8 to 13 kilometres above the ground.
[事实] Oliver describes them as broad but thin, like pancakes, sometimes hundreds of kilometres across but only a few hundred metres deep.
[事实] If pilots know where these regions are, they can often avoid them by flying slightly lower.

[14:47] **Testing Flight Adjustments**

[事实] Operation Blue Skies is testing whether air-traffic control can ask planes to make small altitude changes safely and routinely.
[事实] Oliver says dropping a few hundred metres can often avoid a contrail-forming region.
[事实] He compares this to altitude changes planes already make to avoid turbulence.
[事实] The trial also tests whether systematic changes actually create skies with significantly fewer contrails.

[15:44] **Measuring Contrails Over the North Atlantic**

[事实] The North Atlantic is watched continuously by two weather satellites.
[事实] The satellites observe in visible and infrared wavelengths, which helps detect contrails.
[事实] Google is using machine learning to identify which contrails come from which flights and where they go.
[事实] Oliver says the North Atlantic may represent about 5% of the global contrail problem while accounting for only a fraction of 1% of airspace used by aircraft.
[推测] The region’s disproportionate contrail impact makes it a practical place to test targeted interventions.

[16:29] **Costs for Airlines**

[事实] Flying lower is slightly less efficient and slightly slower.
[事实] Oliver says it may add a few minutes to a journey and 1% to 2% to fuel costs for affected flights.
[事实] Researchers at contrails.org estimate that changing at most 5% of flights, and perhaps 2%, could deliver about 80% of potential benefits.
[事实] Contrails are more likely over places such as Canada and the North Atlantic than in temperate and subtropical Asia.
[推测] Because costs and responsibilities would fall unevenly across airlines and routes, implementation may be politically difficult even if technically cheap.

[18:15] **Air-Traffic Control and Externalities**

[事实] Oliver says using air-traffic control may be the simplest and most direct solution.
[事实] Airlines may lobby against the changes because they guard their fuel bills closely.
[事实] He argues that contrails are an externality imposed on the rest of the world by airlines serving passengers.
[事实] He says it would be reasonable for airlines, and potentially passengers, to bear that cost.
[推测] The segment treats regulation or coordinated air-traffic management as more reliable than voluntary airline action.

[19:33] **Young Koreans and Buddhism**

[事实] Buddhist events are appearing across South Korea, including raves with sutra-inspired tracks, meditation festivals, and matchmaking events.
[事实] Buddhist groups hope these events can revive interest in the religion.
[事实] South Koreans, especially young ones, are becoming less religious overall, and the number of practicing Buddhists is shrinking.
[事实] Buddhist leaders have discussed how to create lasting engagement without being coercive.
[推测] The revival efforts are presented as an attempt to adapt religious practice to a secular, stressed younger audience.

[事实] Soobin Kim attends a Buddhist expo organised by the Jogye Order of Korean Buddhism in Seoul.
[事实] She describes large crowds and many stalls.
[事实] Stalls sell Buddhist-themed art, prayer beads, singing bowls, and other items.
[事实] One stall showcases a funeral provider that still conducts Buddhist-style ceremonies.

[21:06] **Traditional Funeral Experience**

[事实] A Buddhist funeral prepares six offerings: rice, candles, incense, flowers, tea, and pure water.
[事实] These offerings represent the six paramitas, or virtues practiced in Buddhism.
[事实] Soobin tries a simulated funeral experience by putting on a robe and lying in a coffin.
[事实] The robe includes a passage from the Diamond Sutra about the impermanence of life.
[推测] The scene shows how the expo turns solemn religious ideas into participatory experiences for visitors.

[22:15] **Modern Buddhist Reinventions**

[事实] Some expo stalls try to give Buddhism a modern twist.

[23:09] **Buddhism as Refuge**

[事实] For a growing number of young Koreans, Buddhism offers refuge from a stressful, competitive, and uncertain pace of life.
[事实] Buddhism begins from the idea that suffering is an inevitable part of life and offers ways to confront it with perspective.
[事实] A 25-year-old attendee, Kim Soo-jin, says Buddhism has an advantage over Christianity for some secular Koreans.
[事实] She says Buddhism encourages people to have faith in themselves rather than in a deity.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it links three very different stories through practical consequences: electoral arithmetic in Germany, operational fixes for aviation emissions, and cultural adaptation in South Korean religion. The strongest segment is the AfD discussion, which clearly explains why the result matters beyond a single state election.

The contrails segment is also useful because it translates a technical climate issue into concrete mechanisms: altitude changes, satellite monitoring, machine learning, fuel costs, and air-traffic control. It avoids presenting the idea as effortless, while still showing why it may be unusually cost-effective.

The Buddhism segment works best as a reported cultural snapshot. It gives vivid examples from expos, funerals, AI chatbots, temple stays, and young attendees, but it also admits uncertainty about whether the trend will last.

[推测] The episode is well suited to listeners who want concise international analysis with enough detail to understand stakes and tradeoffs, rather than a deep specialist treatment of any one subject.
