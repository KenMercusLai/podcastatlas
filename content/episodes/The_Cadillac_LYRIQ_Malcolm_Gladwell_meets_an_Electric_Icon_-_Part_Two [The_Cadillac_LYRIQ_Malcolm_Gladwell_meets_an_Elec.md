# Cadillac LYRIQ: Malcolm Gladwell Meets an Electric Icon, Part Two

## Overview

In this Cadillac-sponsored episode, Malcolm Gladwell and his producers take the all-electric Cadillac LYRIQ on a road trip through New York’s Hudson Valley. They test whether the SUV can accommodate a heavily equipped family outing, provide a quiet ride for a toddler, and appeal to an enthusiast devoted to classic Cadillacs.

The episode presents practicality, comfort, and heritage as complementary parts of the LYRIQ’s identity. Its cargo space passes Gladwell’s playful “helicopter parent test,” while the quiet cabin helps his daughter fall asleep.

The central comparison places the LYRIQ beside a 1963 Cadillac Coupe de Ville. After driving both cars over the same route, classic-car enthusiast Dan finds that the electric model preserves the smooth, unhurried, premium character he associates with Cadillac, despite the absence of a gasoline engine.

[Fact] Gladwell and his producers load the LYRIQ with large suitcases, car-seat components, a stroller, backpacks, and recording equipment without folding the rear seats.

[Fact] The LYRIQ is introduced as an all-electric SUV and the lead model in GM’s move toward electrification.

[Fact] Gladwell says the LYRIQ comfortably holds children, parents, potentially grandparents, and the gear needed for a family outing.

## Podcast Review/Summary

The episode’s strongest feature is its accessible, experience-based structure. Cargo loading, a toddler’s nap, and back-to-back drives turn vehicle attributes such as space, quietness, comfort, and design continuity into concrete scenes rather than a list of specifications.

The comparison with the 1963 Coupe de Ville gives the discussion a clear thematic focus: electrification does not necessarily require abandoning a brand’s established identity. Dan’s response provides the episode’s most meaningful evidence that the LYRIQ can evoke the smoothness and composure associated with classic Cadillacs.

The principal limitation is that this is explicitly sponsored content. The tests are informal, the overall assessment is overwhelmingly positive, and the episode does not examine range, charging time, price, efficiency, or shortcomings in depth. It is therefore most useful for listeners interested in Cadillac’s design heritage and the intended experience of its electric transition, rather than those seeking an independent or comprehensive vehicle review.
