# Can Meta Finally Make Smart Glasses Cool?

## 概览

This episode of Marketplace Tech examines Meta’s effort to make its Ray-Ban smart glasses more culturally acceptable, especially among women and consumers outside the tech bubble. Megan McCarty Carino interviews New York Magazine contributing writer Carrie Batten about Meta’s shift from a gadget-focused product toward fashion, creator marketing, AI features, and pop-culture endorsements.

The core tension is that the glasses are designed to make recording seamless, but that same seamlessness creates social and privacy discomfort. Batten describes how use cases like travel, cooking, and gardening can make sense, while real-world experiences also reveal how difficult it is to avoid capturing bystanders.

The episode concludes skeptically: Batten says the glasses could become mainstream, but likely through Meta’s marketing power and creator-gifting machinery rather than a clear, organic embrace based on utility.

## 分段落总结

[00:01] **The Question: Can Meta Make Smart Glasses Cool?**

[事实] The episode opens by asking whether Meta can finally make smart glasses cool, after earlier products like Google Glass and the first Meta Ray-Bans were seen as uncool.

[事实] Carrie Batten recently wrote about Meta’s effort to make the glasses more appealing to women and people outside the tech bubble.

[00:49] **The First Meta Ray-Bans Were a Sales Flop**

[事实] Batten says the original Meta Ray-Bans were conceived around 2019 and launched in 2021.

[事实] She describes them as unpopular and widely considered a sales flop.

[事实] The people who did buy them seemed mostly to be gadget-focused consumers trying them out experimentally.

[01:14] **Meta’s New Strategy: Features, Fashion, and Influencers**

[事实] Meta added more technical capabilities to the glasses, including a major AI integration and live-streaming capability.

[事实] The company expanded into outdoors and point-of-view adventure culture through an Oakley collaboration in 2025.

[事实] Meta also moved into fashion through a Caperni runway collaboration and promotions involving figures such as Teyana Taylor and Kylie Jenner.

[02:14] **Kylie Jenner Campaign Raised Visibility and Privacy Concerns**

[事实] Kylie Jenner promoted her own model of the glasses, called Starfire, through a first-person-style video showing her day.

[事实] Batten says the campaign increased visibility for the glasses.

[事实] She also calls it a misstep because it made people more aware of possible misuse and privacy concerns.

[推测] The campaign appears to have made the product more culturally visible while also making its risks harder to ignore.

[03:06] **Appealing Use Cases: Travel, Cooking, and Gardening**

[事实] Batten spoke with a woman who had been gifted the glasses by Meta and took them on a family vacation in Europe.

[事实] The woman saw travel activities such as gondola rides, mountain tours, and cooking classes as good use cases.

[事实] Cooking creators and gardening enthusiasts were interested in the glasses because they allow hands-free recording of two-handed activities.

[03:58] **Real-World Use Created Social Friction**

[事实] The vacation user later reviewed footage from a cooking class and realized she had captured strangers despite warning them she was recording.

[事实] Batten says the glasses’ perspective made it almost impossible not to film other people in that setting.

[事实] The footage showed people reacting negatively, including rolling their eyes, giving side-eye, and whispering.

[推测] The problem was not just the recording itself, but the social atmosphere created by wearing a device that can record at eye level.

[04:45] **“Creep Glasses” and the Privacy Problem**

[事实] The host notes that some people call the devices “pervert glasses” or “creep glasses.”

[事实] Batten says misuse is probably not rampant, but she believes the association hurts Meta’s ability to appeal to women.

[事实] She argues that having to reassure people that you are not invading their privacy can be more disruptive than simply taking out a phone to record.

[推测] The glasses’ central promise of effortless recording clashes with ordinary social expectations around consent and visibility.

[07:23] **AI Marketing and the Gender Gap**

[事实] The host says survey and AI company data show women using AI tools at lower rates.

[事实] The conversation mentions public figures such as Reese Witherspoon and Sandra Bullock talking about the need to get on board with AI.

[事实] Batten says it is hard to tell whether this is covert marketing or a talking point spreading quickly.

[事实] Batten notices what she calls the “girl bossing of AI” and mentions the phrase “Claude girly.”

[推测] The episode connects Meta’s smart-glasses push with a broader effort to frame emerging tech as more accessible or appealing to women.

[08:51] **Will the Glasses Go Mainstream?**

[事实] Batten says that if Meta’s glasses go mainstream, it will be through “sheer brute force,” not necessarily organic consumer embrace.

[事实] She says Meta, Facebook, and Instagram have an intense creator partnership and marketing mechanism.

[事实] Batten says many glasses appear to be gifted for free across fashion communities, mommy Instagrammers, and adventure content creators.

[事实] She had difficulty finding people who had bought the glasses themselves rather than receiving them for free.

[推测] If the product becomes a cultural staple, the episode suggests that marketing investment may matter more than demonstrated everyday utility.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s value lies in treating smart glasses not only as a hardware product, but as a social object. It focuses on who is expected to wear them, who is being marketed to, and how bystanders experience a device that can record from the wearer’s face.

A strong point is the use of concrete examples: travel, cooking, gardening, creator gifting, influencer campaigns, and the cooking-class privacy incident all help explain why the product can seem useful and uncomfortable at the same time.

[推测] The limitation is that the conversation is based mainly on Batten’s reporting and commentary rather than a broad technical review or detailed sales data. It is best suited for listeners interested in consumer tech culture, privacy norms, influencer marketing, and how companies try to push new devices into mainstream life.
