# Babylon: Glamour, Empire, Exile, and Afterlife

## 概览

This episode traces Babylon from its mythic self-image as the center of creation to its historical height under Nebuchadnezzar in the sixth century BC. The hosts frame the city as both dazzling and sinister: a place of immense wealth, monumental architecture, cosmopolitan life, and lasting associations with tyranny, corruption, and doomed arrogance.

The discussion moves from physical Babylon, including the Ishtar Gate, city walls, ziggurat, and possibly mythical Hanging Gardens, into Babylon’s role in biblical memory. The exile of the Judean elite becomes central: Babylon destroys Jerusalem and Solomon’s Temple, but that catastrophe sharpens Judean identity and helps shape later Jewish and Christian images of imperial power.

The final part follows Babylon’s fall to Persia, its survival under Alexander and the Seleucids, and its long cultural afterlife. Babylon becomes a metaphor for Rome, the Catholic Church in Protestant polemic, colonial and police power in Rastafarianism, and modern imperial capitals more generally.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Babylon as Myth and Obsession**

[事实] The episode opens with a dramatic passage about Babylonian creation mythology, in which Marduk forms the first building and humanity from earth, water, blood, and mud.
[事实] Tom Holland says Babylon had fascinated him since childhood because it appeared both in Greek history books as glamorous and in biblical imagery as sinister.
[事实] The hosts describe Babylon as ancient, romantic, exotic, confusing, and associated across later traditions with corruption and evil.
[推测] The opening establishes Babylon less as a single historical city than as a layered symbol of civilization, power, beauty, and danger.

[04:12] **Choosing Nebuchadnezzar’s Babylon**

[事实] The hosts decide to focus on the best-known and most colorful period of Babylonian history, from Nebuchadnezzar onward.
[事实] Babylon was probably founded around 1900 BC, but the city associated with its famous monuments had been rebuilt after the Assyrian king Sennacherib flattened it.
[事实] Babylon stood in modern-day Iraq, south of Baghdad, on the Euphrates, within Mesopotamia.
[事实] Babylonians saw their city as the center of the world, the center of time, and the great home of culture.

[07:39] **Babylon in the Sixth Century BC**

[事实] Nebuchadnezzar comes to power around 605 BC after Babylon defeats Egypt in Syria.
[事实] At this time Athens and Sparta are emerging, Rome is in its early phase, and Persia is beginning to rise under the shadow of the Medes.
[事实] Babylon is described as the greatest city yet seen, with about a quarter of a million inhabitants.
[事实] The Babylonian victory over Egypt opens the way into Syria and the kingdom of Judah.

[09:21] **Herodotus and the Problem of Evidence**

[事实] Herodotus probably visited Babylon, but his account mixes useful detail with Greek assumptions and questionable stories.
[事实] He describes customs such as marriage auctions and compulsory temple sex, which the hosts treat as doubtful or distorted.
[事实] Tom explains that older evidence points instead to women dedicated to the sun god Shamash who may have lived more like celibate religious figures than temple prostitutes.
[推测] Herodotus is valuable in the episode not because he is always reliable, but because his account shows how Babylon was already becoming a place of fantasy and projection.

[12:03] **Walls, Gates, and the Tower of Babel**

[事实] Babylon’s walls are presented as one of its great wonders, extending across a vast area and surrounded by moats.
[事实] Nebuchadnezzar also built the Median Wall, said in the discussion to run for about 50 miles.
[事实] The Ishtar Gate had blue and gold glazed brickwork, later excavated by German archaeologists and reconstructed in Berlin.
[事实] The great ziggurat, Etemenanki, had around 17 million bricks, rose roughly 100 meters, and became associated with the biblical Tower of Babel.
[事实] In Genesis, the Tower of Babel story is about human arrogance rather than slave labor.

[16:26] **Babylon as City of Pleasure and Power**

[事实] A contemporary inscription describes Babylon as wealthy, celebratory, and full of rejoicing and dance.
[事实] The city combined ordered grid planning with confusing inner labyrinths.
[事实] Babylon is described as the largest and most cosmopolitan city in the world, with banks and major commercial families such as the Egibis.
[事实] The hosts argue that Babylon became an archetype of the wealthy, imperial, multicultural city associated with pleasure, sex, trade, and danger.

[18:47] **The Hanging Gardens Question**

[事实] The Hanging Gardens are discussed as the most enigmatic of Babylon’s wonders.
[事实] Herodotus does not mention the gardens, and no archaeological evidence for them has been found in Babylon.
[事实] Later tradition, including Josephus, says Nebuchadnezzar built them for a Median wife homesick for mountains.
[事实] Stephanie Dalley’s argument is presented: the Hanging Gardens may actually have been in Nineveh and connected to Assyrian engineering.
[推测] The uncertainty around the gardens reinforces the episode’s wider theme that Babylon’s fame is built from both evidence and powerful later imagination.

[22:52] **Why Judeans Were in Babylon**

[事实] Tom says the people should be called Judeans at this point rather than Jews.
[事实] After the united monarchy split, the northern kingdom of Israel was destroyed by the Assyrians, while Judah survived around Jerusalem.
[事实] Nebuchadnezzar conquered Jerusalem in 597 BC, took the king and elite Judeans to Babylon, and installed Zedekiah as a puppet ruler.
[事实] After Zedekiah rebelled, Babylon destroyed Jerusalem and Solomon’s Temple, killed Zedekiah’s sons before him, blinded him, and carried him to Babylon.
[事实] The exiles included nobles, priests, and scribes rather than the whole peasant population.

[25:42] **Exile and the Formation of Jewish Identity**

[事实] Deporting conquered elites was a standard Mesopotamian imperial practice.
[事实] The Babylonian exile raises major questions about the composition and shaping of biblical books.
[事实] Jeremiah foretells Jerusalem’s destruction but also imagines Babylon’s eventual ruin.
[事实] Tom argues that the Judeans’ faith became sharper and more monotheistic through the experience of exile.
[推测] The episode presents the exile as a crisis that paradoxically strengthens Judean identity rather than erasing it.

[27:02] **Babylon as the Enemy of God**

[事实] Tom favors the view that hostility to Babylonian idols and temple worship helped intensify Judean opposition to idolatry.
[事实] Jeremiah’s language about Babylon later influences the Book of Revelation.
[事实] Babylon is described in biblical prophecy as destined to become heaps and a desolate dwelling place.
[事实] The hosts stress that Judeans interpret Babylon’s actions as part of their God’s plan, even though Judah is politically tiny.
[推测] This makes Babylon central to a biblical way of thinking about empire: powerful rulers may appear dominant, but they remain instruments within a larger divine story.

[30:40] **Nebuchadnezzar, Nabonidus, and Cyrus**

[事实] In biblical interpretation, Nebuchadnezzar is both a tyrant and an instrument of God’s punishment.
[事实] After Nebuchadnezzar, Nabonidus becomes king; he is described as an elderly general, an antiquarian, and a devotee of the moon god Sin.
[事实] Nabonidus spends about ten years in Tayma in northern Arabia, leaving Belshazzar as regent in Babylon.
[事实] His absence disrupts the Babylonian New Year ritual centered on Marduk and makes him unpopular.
[事实] Cyrus the Great defeats the Medes and Lydians, presents himself as respectful of Babylonian gods, and benefits from Babylonian unwillingness to fight for Nabonidus.

[34:51] **Belshazzar’s Feast and the Book of Daniel**

[事实] After the break, the hosts discuss the Book of Daniel and the famous scene of Belshazzar’s feast.
[事实] A phantom hand writes on the wall, and Daniel interprets the words as announcing that the kingdom has been numbered, weighed, divided, and given to the Medes and Persians.
[事实] Tom says the Book of Daniel was probably written centuries after Babylon’s fall, perhaps in the third century BC.
[事实] Daniel’s version of Babylonian history is described as distorted, with elements of Nabonidus’s story transferred to Nebuchadnezzar.
[事实] The episode links this scene to later artistic depictions by Rembrandt and John Martin.

[38:03] **Daniel’s Imperial Imagination**

[事实] The Book of Daniel fixes Babylon in Judeo-Christian imagination as a place of power, corruption, luxury, hubris, feasting, and looming disaster.
[事实] Daniel also contains visions of successive empires, later read as pointing from Babylon to Persia, Greece, and Rome.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that Daniel is written with knowledge that Babylon had already fallen and was declining.
[推测] Daniel matters historically in the episode less as accurate chronicle than as a source of enduring political symbolism.

[39:35] **Babylon After the Persian Conquest**

[事实] Babylon remains the largest city in the world after its absorption into the Persian Empire.
[事实] A rebellion under Xerxes badly damages the great ziggurat.
[事实] Alexander enters Babylon, spends time there near the end of his reign, and dies there.
[事实] Babylon is not renamed by the Greeks because it is too famous.
[事实] Alexander clears rubble from the ziggurat and intends to rebuild it, but the project is not completed.

[41:00] **Decline Under the Seleucids and Rome**

[事实] After Alexander, Mesopotamia becomes part of the Seleucid Empire.
[事实] The Seleucids build Seleucia on the Tigris, and one source says Babylon’s population was ordered to move there.
[事实] The Esagila still seems to function into the Roman period, and Antiochus III performs rituals there in Nebuchadnezzar’s robe.
[事实] Trajan visits Babylon and sacrifices to Alexander at the place where Alexander was said to have died.
[事实] By the first or second century AD, Babylon is becoming deserted and ruinous.

[42:53] **Ruin, Memory, and Jewish Scholarship**

[事实] Babylon was built largely of clay and mud, so its remains gradually melted into mounds and sludge.
[事实] The site itself became difficult to identify, though a settlement called Babel preserved some continuity near a former palace area.
[事实] The Quran mentions Babylon in connection with Harut and Marut, two angels associated with teaching sorcery.
[事实] Medieval Jewish visitors came looking for Babylonian sites, partly because Babylonia had remained a major center of Jewish learning.
[事实] The Babylonian Talmud was produced in Babylonia.

[44:47] **The Whore of Babylon and Rome**

[事实] In Christian and post-Christian imagination, Babylon lives on most vividly through the image of the Whore of Babylon.
[事实] From Luther onward, Protestant polemic often identified the Whore of Babylon with the Catholic Church.
[事实] In Rastafarianism, Babylon becomes the antithesis of Zion and represents white power, colonialism, slavery’s legacy, police, authority, and “the man.”
[事实] Tom explains that in Revelation, Babylon means Rome and draws directly on Jeremiah’s language about Babylon.
[事实] Revelation is described as understanding that imperial cities depend on trade routes, tribute, merchants, wealth, and desire.

[48:04] **Anti-Imperial Babylon**

[事实] Tom argues that Western anti-imperial ideas about corrupt capitals that must fall have roots in Jeremiah and Revelation.
[事实] The hosts connect this imagery to later ways of imagining imperial cities such as New York as centers of luxury, vice, and power.
[事实] They also link the familiar cinematic image of elites feasting while disaster approaches to Belshazzar’s Feast.
[事实] D. W. Griffith’s Intolerance is mentioned as literally rebuilding Babylon for film.
[推测] The episode treats Babylon as a template for later narratives in which spectacle and decadence foreshadow judgment.

[50:16] **Saddam Hussein, Iraq, and Modern Babylon**

[事实] Saddam Hussein promoted Babylon in a secular nationalist phase because it could serve as a shared Iraqi identity across religious communities.
[事实] He rebuilt parts of Babylon and stamped mud bricks with his name, imitating Nebuchadnezzar’s practice.
[事实] Tom says Islamic State attacked Assyrian monuments because pre-Islamic heritage could support national identities that competed with religious identity.
[事实] After the Iraq War, American forces built a large tank park on the archaeological site of Babylon, damaging remains including the processional way.
[推测] Modern Babylon remains politically charged because different powers use, erase, or damage the past to define identity and authority.

[52:55] **Rivers of Babylon and the Dignity of Exile**

[事实] The hosts close by discussing Boney M’s “Rivers of Babylon,” which draws on a Psalm about Judean exiles weeping by Babylon’s rivers.
[事实] Tom says the song preserves the idea of dignity in exile and defeat.
[事实] He notes that the song omits the Psalm’s final violent line about revenge against Babylon’s children.
[事实] Tom concludes that Babylon is now dust while the Bible is still read.
[推测] The ending frames Babylon’s story as a struggle between overwhelming imperial force and the survival of the defeated people’s memory.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is its range: it moves from archaeology and ancient Near Eastern politics to biblical literature, Christian symbolism, Rastafarian language, cinema, Saddam Hussein, and the Iraq War. Babylon is treated not only as a city but as one of the most durable images of empire in Western and religious imagination.

A major strength is the way the hosts distinguish historical Babylon from later Babylon. They repeatedly flag uncertainty around Herodotus, the Hanging Gardens, Daniel, and Nabonidus, while showing how inaccurate or distorted stories can still become historically powerful.

The limitation is that much of the account depends on Tom’s synthesis and on literary afterlife rather than sustained archaeological detail. [推测] Listeners wanting a narrowly technical history of Babylonian administration, language, or material culture may find the symbolic and biblical emphasis stronger than the institutional history.

[推测] This episode is especially suited to listeners interested in how ancient history becomes myth, how defeated peoples preserve identity, and how the language of “Babylon” still shapes modern ideas of empire, corruption, exile, and resistance.
