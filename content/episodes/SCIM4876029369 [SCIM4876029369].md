# Huberman Lab Essentials: Jet Lag, Shift Work & Circadian Tools

## 概览

This episode explains jet lag and shift work through the mechanics of circadian rhythms: the body’s 24-hour timing system, its relationship to light and darkness, and the way body temperature helps synchronize organs, metabolism, mood, immunity, sleepiness, and alertness.

The core practical idea is the “temperature minimum,” usually about 90 minutes to two hours before a person’s average wake time. Light, exercise, meals, and temperature changes can shift the circadian clock differently depending on whether they occur before or after that point.

The episode argues that behavioral tools such as timed light exposure, local meal timing, exercise, hot or cold exposure, and non-sleep deep rest can often be more flexible and preferable than relying on melatonin, especially because melatonin has broader endocrine effects and mainly helps induce sleepiness rather than maintain sleep.
