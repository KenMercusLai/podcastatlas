# Building Hospitality into a Brand: Advice from Danny Meyer

## 概览

Danny Meyer, founder of Union Square Hospitality Group, joins Guy Raz to advise three emerging food-and-beverage companies on brand-building, channel expansion, and sustainable growth. Meyer begins by defining company culture as “how we do things around here,” arguing that clear expected behaviors are more useful than describing a business as a family.

Across the calls, a shared principle emerges: growth should begin with clarity about the experience a brand wants to create. For a canned cocktail company, that means turning packaging into a bartender and community ambassador. For a smoked-salmon producer, it means testing new distribution channels without outgrowing supply, capacity, or quality controls.

The final case examines how a fast-growing, protein-rich couscous brand might enter food service. Meyer recommends proving the product with smaller restaurants and influential chefs before approaching national chains. He closes with a broader lesson from four decades in hospitality: downturns eventually reverse, and strong businesses distinguish themselves by solving problems well with good people.

## 分段落总结

[00:26] **Danny Meyer’s Evolving Role**

[事实] Guy Raz introduces Danny Meyer as the founder of Union Square Hospitality Group and the restaurateur behind businesses including Union Square Cafe, Gramercy Tavern, The Modern, and Shake Shack.

[事实] Meyer says becoming executive chairman was a step up rather than retirement because it allowed him to concentrate on company culture, culinary products, and creative new-business opportunities.

[事实] He no longer spends most of his time managing day-to-day operational execution.

[02:36] **Culture as Expected Behavior**

[事实] Meyer defines culture as the way an organization does things and says every company, family, team, or group has a culture whether it develops one intentionally or not.

[事实] He moved away from calling company principles “family values” because a business is not a family, and the metaphor makes necessary accountability or dismissal feel like personal rejection.

[事实] He instead emphasizes specific expected behaviors that everyone understands and is responsible for demonstrating.

[事实] Meyer summarizes culture as the wanted behaviors an organization actively celebrates minus the unwanted behaviors it tolerates.

[04:48] **Recognizing Genuine Hospitality**

[事实] Meyer says customers can assess a hospitality culture by observing whether employees perform their jobs well while also enjoying working with one another.

[事实] Technical perfection without warmth or human energy is not enough to make him want to return to a restaurant.

[事实] He argues that management bears primary responsibility for whether a healthy, engaging service culture exists.

[事实] Union Square Hospitality Group uses a “day one mentality,” asking long-established teams to preserve the pride, care, and attention to detail associated with opening day.

[07:46] **Ponyboy Slings and the Challenge of Packaged Hospitality**

[事实] Janelle Bass and her husband created Ponyboy Slings, a 7% alcohol canned bourbon cocktail offered in strawberry, cream soda, and tart black cherry flavors.

[事实] The company launched at the end of 2023, sells in Kentucky and Tennessee, and uses real juice, Kentucky bourbon, and bitters without preservatives.

[事实] Bass reports that in-store tastings convert roughly 60% to 70% of participants into buyers, but the product is harder to distinguish within a crowded ready-to-drink aisle when the founders are absent.

[事实] Her central question is how to embed hospitality into a packaged product and preserve that experience as the business scales.

[10:45] **Selling Community, Not Just a Drink**

[事实] Meyer asks who personifies the brand and suggests that the founders’ homemade story could provide the human connection sometimes supplied by celebrity partners.

[事实] Ponyboy already organizes pop-ups and tastings using a 1977 Chevrolet van, music performed by Bass’s husband, and a retro 1970s aesthetic inspired by the inclusive party atmosphere associated with Studio 54.

[事实] Meyer says these events make consumers feel part of a community and reframes the offering as an opportunity to belong rather than merely a product to purchase.

[事实] He says the desired feeling when customers encounter Ponyboy without its founders present should become the basis of the company’s brand strategy.

[13:53] **Making the Can the Bartender**

[事实] Raz recommends treating the can itself as Ponyboy’s bartender, using playful and unexpected language to invite customers into the brand’s party atmosphere.

[事实] He observes that the existing design communicates the product name, ingredients, and retro identity but could do more to create an interactive experience.

[事实] Meyer points to canned wine brand Ramona as a case study in packaging that conveys the founder’s personality and intended audience.

[事实] The advisers conclude that packaging can serve as both the customer’s introduction to the company and an ambassador for its hospitality.

[17:34] **Angel Oak Smokehouse Weighs Direct-to-Consumer Sales**

[事实] Jade Taylor co-founded Angel Oak Smokehouse in Charleston, South Carolina, drawing on her father’s four-decade history of smoking fish in northern England.

[事实] The company sells cold-smoked and hot-smoked salmon and, 15 months after launch, had entered 42 Whole Foods stores, secured an order from Harris Teeter, and planned to launch in 170 Fresh Market locations.

[事实] Taylor asks whether the company should add direct-to-consumer shipping despite the risk that delivery delays or improper handling could spoil the salmon and damage its premium customer experience.

[21:43] **Using Logistics Partners and Controlled Experiments**

[事实] Meyer suggests exploring Goldbelly, a company in which his organization invested, because it handles logistics for highly perishable foods and can provide access to lower shipping rates.

[事实] Raz advises prioritizing the promising retail business and, if the company tests direct-to-consumer sales, limiting the experiment geographically and tracking repeat orders.

[事实] Raz argues that repeat purchases are essential evidence of a viable direct-to-consumer business rather than an expensive alternative to buying the same product in a store.

[事实] Meyer agrees that Angel Oak should first clarify its growth goals, salmon supply, smoking capacity, and readiness in case a new channel performs exceptionally well.

[24:45] **Testing Demand Without Overextending**

[事实] Meyer proposes a limited Goldbelly presence, potentially operating only one day per week, as a way to reach national customers and gather geographic demand data without adding physical locations.

[事实] When Taylor raises the possibility of shipping to gourmet retailers, Meyer recommends starting with one or two prestigious stores and using controlled scarcity to increase the brand’s appeal.

[事实] Raz adds that wholesale shipments should meet meaningful minimum-order thresholds so the company does not absorb shipping complexity for very small purchases.

[事实] Both advisers support small, measurable experiments while protecting the company’s existing retail momentum.

[28:12] **Boost Coos Reinvents Couscous**

[事实] Barb Ruhis introduces Boost Coos, a gluten-free couscous alternative made from chickpea, lentil, and pea flours.

[事实] Each serving contains 18 grams of protein and 11 grams of fiber, and the product uses three ingredients without xanthan gum.

[事实] The founders spent approximately a year and a half developing the product before launching it in December.

[事实] Ruhis says the bootstrapped company has shipped more than 150,000 boxes, reached a $2 million run rate, and remained profitable on customers’ first orders.

[31:42] **Entering Food Service from the Bottom Up**

[事实] Ruhis asks how Boost Coos can enter food service, particularly fast-casual chains such as Cava and Sweetgreen that serve customizable bowls.

[事实] Meyer recommends beginning with one- or two-location restaurants that already serve couscous, reducing the need to educate buyers about the category while also proving operational performance.

[事实] He notes that large chains require reliable supply, extensive quality assurance, and numerous purchasing requirements that may be difficult for a young company to satisfy immediately.

[事实] His proposed sequence is to learn through smaller customers, accumulate successful case studies, and only then approach buyers who require both education and proof.

[34:05] **Chefs as Product Testers and Advocates**

[事实] Raz cites Impossible Foods’ early work with prominent chefs as an example of using culinary experimentation to build credibility and attention before broad retail expansion.

[事实] He says restaurant trials can reveal whether Boost Coos cooks quickly, remains stable in service containers, and performs well in a high-volume serving line.

[事实] Ruhis reports that chefs and online creators have already used the product in dishes such as dolmas and sushi, with some of that content going viral.

[事实] Meyer suggests identifying six to twelve recognized chefs who already use couscous and recruiting them as proof points and advocates.

[35:50] **Preparing the Company for Scale**

[事实] Meyer stresses that a product’s suitability for a large restaurant chain is only half the question; Boost Coos must also determine whether large-scale food service works for its own organization.

[事实] Scaling may require more employees, equipment, sourcing capacity, and deliberate cultural development.

[事实] The advisers identify several potential customer groups, including people seeking more protein, parents, fitness-oriented consumers, and users of GLP-1 medications who value protein and fiber.

[事实] Ruhis says the company is preparing to hire its first employee, making culture an immediate operational concern.

[38:34] **Resilience Through Business Cycles**

[事实] Asked what he would tell his younger self, Meyer says not to become overly distressed when the business graph declines because it can rise again, potentially to a new high.

[事实] He recalls fearing that the 1987 market crash might end his career and later enduring 9/11, Superstorm Sandy, the Great Recession, and the pandemic.

[事实] Meyer presents hospitality as an antidote to pessimism and fear and associates the business with hope.

[事实] In a clip from an earlier interview, he recalls his grandfather’s advice that problems define business and that the best operators solve them better, enjoy the process more, and work with better people.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s greatest value is its consistent translation of hospitality from an abstract ideal into practical business choices: employee behavior, packaging language, distribution experiments, customer communities, and growth sequencing.

[推测] The three cases are especially useful for early-stage consumer food and beverage founders. Together, they show that channel expansion is not automatically desirable; it should be tested against repeat demand, operational capacity, quality control, and the founders’ own ambitions.

[推测] The advice remains strategic rather than financially detailed. The discussion does not deeply examine margins, customer-acquisition costs, cold-chain failure rates, retailer terms, or production economics, so listeners would still need quantitative analysis before implementing the recommendations.

[推测] Founders, hospitality leaders, and consumer-brand marketers will find the episode most relevant, particularly those deciding how to preserve a distinctive customer experience while moving beyond direct founder involvement.
