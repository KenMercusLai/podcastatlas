# The Rest Is History: China, Continuity, and Modernity

## 概览

This episode uses Michael Wood’s expertise to address a broad question the hosts openly admit they know too little about: how to understand China’s 4,000-year history as both an ancient civilization and a modern power.

The discussion repeatedly returns to continuity. Wood argues that China is best understood as the rhythm of one civilization, not simply a series of unrelated states, while also stressing that today’s borders were shaped by imperial expansion, especially under the Qing.

The episode moves from Tang cultural memory, Qin unification, Confucian and legalist traditions, and Song sophistication to the Opium Wars, Taiping Rebellion, Communist victory, Mao’s contested legacy, Xi Jinping’s use of history, and the unfinished question of Deng Xiaoping’s opening up.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Introducing China as a Historical Blind Spot**

[事实] The hosts introduce China as a 4,000-year-old civilization and a major power that many Western listeners know little about.
[事实] Dominic says he knows Mao and the 20th century vaguely, but cannot confidently name premodern Chinese rulers or politicians.
[事实] Michael Wood joins as guest after Tom cites Wood’s book *The Story of China*.
[推测] The episode frames Western ignorance of Chinese history as the reason for taking a wide, thematic survey rather than a narrow chronological one.

[02:03] **What Chinese People See as Key Historical Moments**

[事实] Wood says informal interviews with Chinese tourists at the Shanghai Expo produced no answers naming the 1949 revolution as the most important event.
[事实] Many people chose the Tang dynasty as a favorite period because of its culture, poetry, and openness to the world.
[事实] When asked about events, people often named the Opium Wars, the “century of humiliation,” and Deng Xiaoping’s opening up in 1978-79.
[事实] Wood says one Chinese friend eventually named Qin Shi Huangdi and the unification of China in the third century BC.

[06:32] **The First Emperor and the Idea of Unity**

[事实] The hosts discuss the film *Hero* as a story about assassins who decide the Qin emperor must live so China can be unified.
[事实] Wood says China is a continuum, unlike Western history’s usual pattern of multiple successive civilizations.
[事实] He cites the idea from *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* that empires that fall apart come together again, and united empires eventually fall apart.
[推测] The discussion links ancient unification to modern political concerns about unity, separatism, and national identity.

[08:55] **Xi, National Studies, and Legalist Traditions**

[事实] Wood says President Xi emphasizes the greatness of China’s past and that “national studies” are important to the current regime.
[事实] Qin Shi Huangdi’s empire lasted only about ten years but left an emblematic legacy.
[事实] Wood describes Qin legalism through Lord Shang as a harsh system of population control, strict punishment, and authoritarian order without Confucian humanism.
[推测] The segment suggests that contemporary Chinese politics selectively draws on older imperial and legalist models.

[10:22] **Why China Has Been So Large**

[事实] Wood says China has broken apart at times, including the 10th century, but repeatedly returned to the concept of unity.
[事实] He links this drive to Confucian ideas of shared culture, speech, writing, and customs.
[事实] He says China’s size has expanded and contracted, and that today’s map largely reflects Qing conquest of Mongolia, Xinjiang, and Tibet.
[事实] Wood distinguishes the Chinese heartland ruled by dynasties such as the Song and Ming from later imperial expansion over non-Chinese peoples.

[14:03] **Continuities in Chinese Civilization**

[事实] Tom quotes Wood’s comparison of 18th-century Qing China to pharaonic Egypt surviving into the modern age with ancient forms still intact.
[事实] Wood says some Western scholars in the 1950s saw Mao-era China as replicating centralized imperial bureaucracies from the Ming and Qing.
[事实] He identifies centralized bureaucracy, the figure of the sage emperor, Mao’s ruler cult, and Confucian ethics as major continuities.
[推测] The episode treats Chinese continuity as institutional, moral, and social, not only dynastic.

[17:43] **Confucian Humanism and Collective Life**

[事实] Wood describes Confucian thought as concerned with justice, virtue, civility, civil order, and the ruler’s moral conduct.
[事实] He says Confucius was not focused on gods or the afterlife, but on balanced civil life on earth.
[事实] Wood argues that Chinese sociability, dense population, eating together, and collective habits remain important.
[事实] He cites neighborhood organization and mutual help during COVID as examples of collective behavior beyond Communist Party command.

[20:10] **Zheng He and the Question of Expansion**

[事实] Listener questions ask why China gave up its exploratory advantage after Zheng He and how capable it was of imperial expansion.
[事实] Wood says a 15th-century Confucian bureaucrat would not see conquering other peoples as the goal of Chinese civilization.
[事实] He cites Matteo Ricci’s comparison between restless European expansion and China’s different civilizational priorities.
[事实] Wood notes that Han and Tang China expanded along Silk Road routes, but says this was not the same as conquering other nations.

[22:50] **Song Achievement and the Great Divergence**

[事实] Wood says the Zheng He voyages aimed to protect trade routes and establish bases, not merely to show the flag.
[事实] He describes Song China as highly advanced in science, technology, gunpowder, street lights, naval technology, and printing.
[事实] He cites Kenneth Pomeranz’s *The Great Divergence*, especially arguments about coal and material conditions.
[推测] The episode explains Europe’s later rise as partly contingent on resources, technology, maritime aggression, and New World extraction.

[27:42] **Western Admiration Before the Opium Wars**

[事实] After the break, the hosts again acknowledge how little they know about much of Chinese history.
[事实] Tom says Jesuits found Chinese civilization extraordinarily sophisticated and believed China had to be treated carefully.
[事实] He mentions Dr. Johnson’s wish to see the Great Wall and Voltaire’s fascination with China as a model of enlightened government.
[推测] The conversation presents 19th-century European condescension as a later shift enabled by military and industrial power.

[29:52] **The Opium Wars and China’s Victim Narrative**

[事实] Dominic summarizes the Opium Wars as British gunboats forcing China open to merchant ships selling opium.
[事实] Wood says this is how the wars are taught in China, though some Chinese scholars and friends are frustrated by the official victim narrative.
[事实] Wood argues the British became a catalyst in a dynastic cycle of collapse and rebirth, with new China beginning to form in Shanghai in the 1860s.
[推测] The segment separates the brutality of coercive trade from the later political use of humiliation as a national story.

[31:10] **Macartney, India, and the Trade Triangle**

[事实] Wood says Macartney’s 1793 mission sought official trade, ambassadorial status in Beijing, and an offshore island base.
[事实] Britain had lost its American colonies while gaining wealth in India, and trade with China became important through a triangular system involving India.
[事实] Qianlong rejected Western manufactures as unnecessary, though Wood says the Chinese showed interest in military technology.
[事实] Macartney later saw China as an old but fragile vessel kept afloat by gifted captains and in need of modernization.

[37:22] **The Taiping Rebellion and Internal Breakdown**

[事实] Tom frames China as anxious about ideological infection, including the possible effect of Christian missionaries.
[事实] The Taiping Rebellion is described as lasting 16 years through the 1840s, 1850s, and mid-1860s.
[事实] Wood describes its leader as a failed ex-student who believed he was chosen by the Christian God and was God’s Chinese son.
[事实] Wood says he does not think a Taiping victory would have helped China’s transition to modernity.

[40:03] **Modernization Debates Before 1911**

[事实] Wood says there were major peasant uprisings every year between the Opium War and 1911.
[事实] Chinese thinkers argued for Western technology, naval dockyards, railways, constitutional monarchy, and regional assemblies.
[事实] By the 1880s, feminist voices and manifestos on women’s liberation were emerging.
[事实] Wood says the rural problem became central because 80-90% of the population still worked in the fields.

[43:00] **Why the Communists Won**

[事实] Dominic asks whether Communist victory came from land reform, Russian aid, contingency, or the failures of rival warlords.
[事实] Wood says contingency mattered and recounts Mao saying Japanese invasion helped make his victory possible.
[事实] The Communists survived nationalist attacks, undertook the Long March, established a northern base, and gained credit through land reform and anti-Japanese guerrilla activity.
[事实] Wood says Chinese resistance to Japan was a major factor in victory in the Pacific War.

[46:47] **Mao’s Contested Legacy Under Xi**

[事实] Wood says the current Chinese government is restrictive about criticism of Mao.
[事实] He says Xi rejects dividing Chinese Communist history into pre-1976 and post-1976 periods.
[事实] Wood says the Communist Party’s 1981 verdict described Mao as a great leader who made massive historical mistakes.
[事实] He says the government now plays down the assault on landlords, the Great Leap Forward, and the Cultural Revolution.

[48:42] **Xi, Confucius, and History as Political Language**

[事实] Wood says Xi makes the greatness of Chinese civilization part of the regime’s stance in the world.
[事实] Wood says Xi has endorsed reading Confucius and is a serious reader of history.
[事实] Wood’s *Story of China* films quickly circulated online in China, though material on Tiananmen Square was cut out.
[事实] Wood says the Central Commission for Discipline used his Du Fu film to discuss anti-corruption, Confucian ethics, and loyalty to the state.

[53:07] **Deng Xiaoping and the Unfinished Modern Story**

[事实] The hosts say they have not had enough time to cover the Communist period, the Cultural Revolution, Deng Xiaoping, and modern China.
[事实] Wood says some eminent American sinologists considered Deng’s opening up one of the greatest events in modern history.
[事实] Wood mentions interviewing Deng’s translator, peasants who broke with the Communist system, and people who took exams in 1978-79 after manual labor.
[事实] He says Deng’s visit to America, including the cowboy hat and rodeo, became a powerful televised moment in both America and China.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in turning a vast subject into a set of recurring questions: unity, continuity, expansion, humiliation, modernization, and the political power of history.

[事实] Its scope is very broad, moving from Qin, Tang, Song, Qing, and the Opium Wars to Taiping, Mao, Xi, and Deng, while the hosts explicitly acknowledge that they cannot cover the Communist period adequately in one episode.

[推测] The main limitation is compression: listeners get a thematic map rather than a detailed chronology. It is best suited to listeners who want an accessible entry point into Chinese history and its relevance to modern Chinese politics.
