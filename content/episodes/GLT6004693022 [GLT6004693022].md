# The Hundred Years War: Chivalry, the Black Prince, and Poitiers

## 概览

This episode continues the Hundred Years War story after Crecy, Calais, and the Black Death. The hosts argue that plague paused the war but did not end Edward III’s ambitions or the rivalry over chivalric prestige between England and France.

A major theme is the contradiction inside medieval chivalry: it could celebrate honor, tournaments, sacred orders, and Arthurian pageantry while also permitting deception, looting, civilian devastation, and conduct that modern listeners would see very differently.

The episode builds toward the Black Prince’s campaigns in Gascony and the Battle of Poitiers in 1356, where a trapped and exhausted English-led army defeats a much larger French force and captures King John II.

## 分段落总结

[02:34] **Sir Nigel and the Chivalric Frame**

[事实] The episode opens with a reading from Arthur Conan Doyle’s *Sir Nigel*, which the hosts connect to the popular image of the Hundred Years War as a gallant chivalric adventure.

[事实] The hosts say Conan Doyle valued the Sir Nigel stories highly, even though Sherlock Holmes received far more attention.

[推测] The reading is used to set up the episode’s central tension: medieval chivalry could be glamorous in literature while brutal and destabilizing in practice.

[03:07] **Crecy, the Longbow, and Changing Warfare**

[事实] The hosts recap that the previous episode ended with the Battle of Crecy, the longbow, and the argument that Crecy may be seen as an early modern battle because projectiles were decisive.

[事实] They argue that the English way of fighting began to diminish the traditional rank and status of knightly classes.

[推测] The episode frames English military success as both a triumph of chivalric kingship and a force undermining old chivalric ideals.

[04:33] **The Black Death Pauses the War**

[事实] The Black Death arrives in Europe shortly after the capture of Calais, reaches France that winter, England the following summer, and then Scotland.

[事实] The hosts say it wipes out up to a third of Europe’s population and causes a pause in the fighting.

[事实] They also stress that the plague does not end Edward III’s war aims.

[05:56] **Edward III’s Strategic Position**

[事实] Edward remains in a strong position after Crecy and Calais because he has both military advantage and prestige.

[事实] The hosts repeat that Edward did not initially go to war simply to become King of France; his French claim functioned as leverage.

[事实] His practical aim is described as gaining possessions from the French king and having them recognized as sovereign, without requiring homage to France.

[推测] Edward’s public claim to the French crown is presented as political theatre needed to make his bargaining position credible.

[07:43] **Edward as a New King Arthur**

[事实] The hosts describe Edward as presenting himself as the greatest king in Christendom and as a new King Arthur ruling over a new Camelot.

[事实] They call this a paradox because the military methods associated with his reign would help erode the knightly ideals he celebrated.

[推测] Edward’s chivalric display is treated as propaganda as well as personal ambition.

[09:07] **Geoffrey de Charny and French Chivalry**

[事实] Geoffrey de Charny is introduced as the emblematic figure of French chivalry, a younger son from Burgundy who rose through tournaments, crusading, and writing on chivalric ideals.

[事实] He is described as the first known owner of the Turin Shroud.

[事实] The hosts say Charny understood that France needed practical military success, not just chivalric reputation.

[10:17] **The Failed French Plot to Retake Calais**

[事实] In late 1349, Charny tries to retake Calais by bribing an Italian mercenary, Almerich of Pavia.

[事实] Almerich takes the money but informs Edward III, allowing Edward, the Black Prince, and others to cross to Calais and ambush the French plan.

[事实] Charny is captured and taken to England.

[推测] The episode uses this incident to show that chivalric honor did not exclude deception or counter-deception.

[12:04] **John II Becomes King of France**

[事实] Philip VI dies in August 1350 and is succeeded by John II.

[事实] The hosts describe John II as kingly in appearance but insecure, obstinate, and ineffective.

[事实] Barbara Tuchman is cited as saying John could have served Machiavelli as the model for the anti-prince.

[推测] John’s personal weaknesses are presented as a major reason France’s political crisis deepens.

[13:26] **The Order of the Star**

[事实] After Charny is ransomed in July 1351, he helps John II create the Order of the Star.

[事实] The order is dedicated to God, Our Lady, the heightening of chivalry, and the augmenting of honor.

[事实] Unlike older chivalric orders centered on tournaments, membership emphasizes deeds in battle, and members swear never to retreat.

[推测] The Order of the Star is portrayed as a French attempt to adapt chivalry to the military lessons of English victories.

[14:43] **The Order’s Embarrassing Start**

[事实] The first meeting of the Order of the Star takes place on 6 January 1352, but few people attend.

[事实] One knight leaves a key castle near Calais to attend, and while he is away, the English seize it.

[推测] The episode presents the order’s early failures as symbolic of France’s difficulty converting chivalric ideals into effective strategy.

[15:41] **The Order of the Garter**

[事实] Edward III founds the Order of the Garter on St George’s Day 1349, during the height of the Black Death.

[事实] Despite advice from physicians to socially distance, Edward holds a large tournament.

[事实] The hosts say the Garter fuses Arthurian spectacle with celebration of military victory more effectively than the Order of the Star.

[17:18] **Windsor, Camelot, and the Garter Motto**

[事实] Windsor is described as significant because it was thought to have been founded by Arthur, and Edward III built the heart of Windsor Castle to look like Camelot.

[事实] The hosts say the famous garter story involving a lady’s dropped garter is late.

[事实] They argue the motto was more likely associated with Edward’s Crecy campaign and the claim that his cause was legitimate and blessed by God.

[19:01] **Lancaster, the Garter, and English Commanders**

[事实] The Earl of Lancaster, later Duke of Lancaster, is enrolled as the second knight of the Garter and is described as Edward’s leading English commander.

[事实] The Black Prince and 26 knights in total are enrolled, most of whom had participated in the Crecy campaign.

[事实] Sir Thomas Holland and Sir John Chandos are among the figures mentioned.

[19:50] **Sir John Chandos and the Black Prince**

[事实] Sir John Chandos is described as a younger son who rose to become one of Europe’s great chivalric and military figures.

[事实] Chandos had reconnoitered the French fleet at Sluys and stood beside the Black Prince at Crecy.

[事实] He becomes the Black Prince’s right-hand man, friend, and military adviser.

[21:10] **The Black Prince’s Reputation**

[事实] The hosts discuss competing explanations for the name “Black Prince,” including armor and his dark reputation in France.

[事实] He is described as already the most famous knight in Christendom by the time of the Order of the Garter.

[事实] The hosts stress that 14th-century chivalry did not prohibit actions that would now be described as war crimes.

[推测] The Black Prince is presented as both a chivalric celebrity and a terrifying military operator.

[22:25] **John II Alienates the French Nobility**

[事实] Edward III is described as keeping his nobility close through friendship and shared prestige, while John II alienates his nobles.

[事实] John executes the Constable of France after he returns from ransom and appoints his own favorite, Charles of Spain.

[事实] This favoritism causes resentment among French nobles.

[推测] The contrast between Edward and John is used to explain why England appears politically coherent while France grows unstable.

[23:21] **Charles the Bad Enters the Story**

[事实] Charles of Navarre, known as Charles the Bad, is introduced as another rival connected to the French succession through Joan and Philip of Evreux.

[事实] He is described as a schemer, backstabber, and constantly shifting political actor.

[事实] He commissions his younger brother to murder Charles of Spain.

[推测] Charles the Bad functions in the episode as a destabilizing force whose alliances make France’s crisis worse.

[25:17] **Navarre, John II, and Failed Diplomacy**

[事实] After Charles of Spain is murdered, John II confiscates Charles the Bad’s lands in Normandy.

[事实] Charles negotiates with the English, John backs down, and Charles repeatedly changes sides.

[事实] John pushes the papacy in Avignon to work toward a peace treaty between England and France.

[事实] Edward considers peace but turns it down because he distrusts the pope and because Charles the Bad again promises support to the English.

[26:33] **Edward’s Planned Triple Invasion**

[事实] In 1355, Edward plans a triple invasion: Edward to Calais, the Duke of Lancaster to Normandy, and the Black Prince to Gascony.

[事实] Charles the Bad then switches sides again and reconciles with John II.

[事实] Edward and Lancaster change plans, but the Black Prince sails from Plymouth for Gascony on 9 September 1355.

[推测] The failed coordination shows how much English strategy depended on exploiting French internal divisions.

[28:51] **The Black Prince Arrives in Gascony**

[事实] The Black Prince arrives in Bordeaux on 20 September 1355 with Sir John Chandos.

[事实] He quickly begins a chevauchee, looting and pillaging across southern France.

[事实] Because French leaders are worried about northern landings, the south is relatively undefended.

[29:54] **The Southern Chevauchee**

[事实] The Black Prince recruits several thousand Gascon troops and moves very quickly through rich southern French lands.

[事实] The army advances almost to the Mediterranean and returns to Aquitaine by the end of November.

[事实] The campaign brings back major loot, many prisoners for ransom, and is said to have destroyed 500 villages and burned a dozen towns.

[31:13] **Civilian Suffering and Chivalric Brutality**

[事实] The hosts explicitly acknowledge that for ordinary people, the Hundred Years War is terrible.

[事实] They say most chroniclers do not dwell on civilian suffering, though some, including Froissart later, do recognize the innocence of victims.

[事实] The hosts state that peasant lives do not really count within the brutality of the chivalric code.

[推测] This is one of the episode’s clearest attempts to puncture the glamour of knightly warfare.

[32:51] **French Confidence Collapses**

[事实] The Black Prince’s deep raid into French territory is described as a shattering blow to French self-confidence.

[事实] John II’s reputation suffers further, and some French lords openly denounce him as a bad ruler.

[事实] Charles the Bad exploits discontent among nobles, especially in Normandy.

[33:51] **Conspiracy Against John II**

[事实] Charles the Bad wins over the debt-ridden Dauphin Charles and plots with Norman nobles against John II.

[事实] In April 1356, John arrives in Rouen, arrests Charles the Bad, and executes several conspirators.

[事实] The executed men’s bodies are displayed brutally, worsening John’s relationship with the aristocracy.

[35:37] **Normandy Opens to the English**

[事实] The House of Navarre repudiates loyalty to the French crown after Charles the Bad’s arrest.

[事实] Normandy falls into chaos and aligns with England.

[事实] In July 1356, the Duke of Lancaster lands at Cherbourg, and relatives of executed Norman nobles pledge loyalty to Edward III.

[36:24] **Lancaster and the Black Prince Aim to Join Forces**

[事实] Lancaster raids across Normandy and Brittany before heading south toward Gascony.

[事实] Meanwhile, the Black Prince moves north from Gascony toward the Loire.

[事实] The English plan is for Lancaster and the Black Prince to meet at the Loire and combine their armies.

[37:23] **John II Raises a Great Army**

[事实] John II recognizes the danger and summons a large French army, described as probably the largest seen in 14th-century France.

[事实] He personally leads it and brings his crown jewels and all four sons.

[事实] Geoffrey de Charny carries the Oriflamme beside the king.

[推测] Bringing the crown jewels is treated by the hosts as ominous overconfidence.

[39:19] **The Loire Blocks the English Plan**

[事实] Sir John Chandos leads an advance party to find a crossing of the Loire.

[事实] Although he beats off French troops at a possible ford, the river is too swollen to cross.

[事实] The Black Prince cannot reach Lancaster, and Lancaster cannot reach him.

[40:29] **The Black Prince Is Trapped Near Poitiers**

[事实] The French cross the Loire at Blois and close in on the Black Prince.

[事实] The Black Prince retreats toward Poitiers, but the French army cuts off the road back to Gascony.

[事实] He decides he has little choice but to offer battle and moves through a forest to avoid alerting the French.

[41:29] **The English Take Position**

[事实] On 17 September, Gascons emerge from the forest and rout part of the French rearguard.

[事实] On 18 September, the English occupy a hill about five miles south of Poitiers overlooking a village called Ney.

[事实] Their situation is poor because they lack food and water and are exhausted from marching and moving wagons through woods.

[42:22] **Failed Papal Mediation**

[事实] Papal ambassadors led by the Cardinal of Perigord try to mediate.

[事实] The Black Prince is willing to negotiate because his position is weak, but John II refuses because he thinks he has the English trapped.

[事实] Mediation fails, and members of the cardinal’s entourage join the French, reinforcing English suspicion of papal bias.

[43:13] **French Battle Lines**

[事实] The French form three lines: the Dauphin commands the first, the Duke of Orleans the second, and John II the third.

[事实] Scots under Lord Douglas advise the French to dismount rather than repeat the cavalry disaster of Crecy.

[事实] John accepts this advice, so much of the French army fights on foot.

[44:26] **The Battle Begins**

[事实] The French briefly consider starving the English out instead of fighting.

[事实] The Black Prince attempts a difficult retreat through the woods, but mounted French troops notice and charge.

[事实] English archers destroy the charging horsemen, and the first French line advances to support them, beginning the battle.

[45:19] **French Confusion**

[事实] The fighting lasts around two hours without the French breaking through the English lines.

[事实] The Dauphin is taken away to avoid capture, and the Duke of Orleans misreads the situation as defeat and withdraws with two younger royal brothers.

[事实] John II, humiliated and bound by the ideals of the Order of the Star, advances rather than retreats.

[46:16] **The English Counterstroke**

[事实] By the time John’s line advances, English archers are tired and have run out of arrows.

[事实] An English captain spots a chance for a flanking maneuver and attacks the French rear.

[事实] The Black Prince and Chandos then order mounted English knights to launch another flanking attack.

[事实] The French are surrounded; those who do not flee are destroyed or captured.

[47:12] **Poitiers Becomes a Disaster for France**

[事实] Froissart describes heavy slaughter near the walls and gates of Poitiers as routed French forces try to escape.

[事实] Geoffrey de Charny is killed holding the Oriflamme.

[事实] John II is captured, along with his son Philip, around 2,000 noblemen, and the crown jewels.

[推测] The battle is presented as a catastrophic repetition of French failure after Crecy, despite attempts to adapt tactics.

[48:23] **Victory, Captivity, and London Triumph**

[事实] Edward III receives news of the victory and says he takes no pleasure in slaughter but rejoices in God’s bounty and hopes for peace.

[事实] John II spends the winter with the Black Prince in Bordeaux and is then taken to London.

[事实] On 24 May, the Black Prince and John enter London, with the Black Prince riding a shorter horse to preserve the French king’s dignity.

[事实] Crowds are so large that it takes them three hours to cross the city.

[49:36] **The Savoy and Arthurian Celebration**

[事实] John II is housed at the Savoy, built by the Duke of Lancaster with loot from French prisoners.

[事实] Ransom negotiations begin there.

[事实] The episode ends with a description of dancing, hunting, hawking, jousts, and banquets, all compared to the court of King Arthur.

[推测] The ending deliberately returns to the Arthurian fantasy introduced at the start, while leaving open the darker consequences for the next episode.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s strongest value is its ability to connect battlefield narrative with political culture. It does not treat Poitiers as only a military event; it shows how chivalric prestige, dynastic claims, noble resentment, and personal rivalry all shaped the campaign.

[事实] The hosts use a lively, comic style, especially in their treatment of John II, Charles the Bad, and English-French rivalry. They also pause to acknowledge the suffering of ordinary civilians, which complicates the otherwise entertaining account of raids, tournaments, and heroic reputations.

[推测] A limitation is that the tone often leans into English triumphal banter, so listeners looking for a sober French-centered account may find parts of it uneven. Still, the transcript makes clear that the hosts are aware of the brutality beneath the chivalric glamour.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners interested in narrative medieval history, the Hundred Years War, chivalric culture, and the rise of the Black Prince. It is less suitable as a standalone introduction unless the listener is comfortable with many named nobles, shifting alliances, and references to earlier episodes.
