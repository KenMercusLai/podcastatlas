# Peter the Great: The Boy Tsar and the Streltsy Revolt

## 概览

This episode introduces Peter the Great through the violence, factional politics, and cultural isolation of late 17th-century Muscovy. It frames Peter not simply as a future reformer, but as a child shaped by trauma: at age 10, he witnessed the Streltsy revolt and the slaughter of relatives and ministers inside the Kremlin.

The discussion places Russia in a wider geopolitical world. By Peter’s birth in 1672, Russia was geographically vast but politically and culturally isolated, surrounded by the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Ottoman Empire, and Sweden. The hosts emphasize that Russia lacked ports, a navy, universities, newspapers, and many institutions associated with Western Europe.

The episode then follows the succession crisis after Tsar Fedor’s death, Sophia’s seizure of the regency, Peter’s semi-exile from power, and his growing obsessions with soldiers, ships, Western technology, and practical skills. By the end, Sophia’s position collapses, foreign officers and the Streltsy shift to Peter’s side, and Peter enters Moscow as the 17-year-old master of Russia.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Peter, Poltava, and the Great Northern War**

[事实] The episode opens with Peter the Great’s proclamation to his troops before the Battle of Poltava on June 26, 1709.

[事实] The hosts describe Poltava as a decisive battle in the Great Northern War and as a turning point in Russia’s emergence as a major power.

[事实] They note that Peter’s rhetoric still has political resonance in modern Russia, including in comparisons made by Vladimir Putin.

[推测] The opening frames Peter’s life as a story about Russia’s transformation from vulnerable outsider to European great power.

[03:39] **Peter’s Extraordinary Life as Historical Drama**

[事实] The hosts preview Peter’s life: he became Tsar at 10, experienced violent palace politics, traveled incognito in Western Europe, worked in shipyards, cut off beards, fought Sweden and the Ottomans, founded St. Petersburg, and tortured his own son to death.

[事实] They describe Peter as an enduring symbol of Russia’s struggle between East and West, Europe and Asia.

[推测] The hosts present Peter as both a modernizing hero and a disturbing autocrat, making the story morally complicated rather than celebratory.

[06:02] **Peter’s Birth and Muscovy’s Background**

[事实] Peter was born in Moscow at 1 a.m. on May 30, 1672, the son of Tsar Alexis and Natalia Naryshkina.

[事实] Moscow is described as a wooden city of about 200,000 people, prone to fire, plague, violence, and disorder.

[事实] Muscovy saw itself as the heir to Orthodox Christianity after the fall of Constantinople in 1453, calling itself the Third Rome.

[事实] Ivan IV had crowned himself Tsar of “all the Russias,” with “Tsar” derived from Caesar.

[09:19] **Russia’s Enemies and Isolation**

[事实] By Peter’s birth, Russia was the largest country on earth, stretching across Siberia to the Pacific and down toward the Caspian.

[事实] Despite its size, the hosts describe Russia as a weak giant surrounded by stronger rivals: Poland-Lithuania, the Ottoman Empire, and Sweden.

[事实] Russia lacked secure warm-water access: Archangel was often frozen, the Black Sea was under Ottoman power, and the Baltic was dominated by Sweden.

[推测] Russia’s lack of ports helps explain why ships and naval power became central to Peter’s later ambitions.

[11:40] **Orthodoxy, Backwardness, and the Culture of Deference**

[事实] Russian society is described as overwhelmingly rural, illiterate, and dominated by the Orthodox Church.

[事实] The hosts say Russia had no universities, schools, playwrights, scientists, parliament, newspapers, or navy.

[事实] The Tsar was treated with extreme reverence, with nobles prostrating themselves and using deferential language.

[推测] The political culture Peter inherited was not merely autocratic but sacralized, making his later attacks on tradition especially disruptive.

[13:34] **The Kremlin as Stage for Violence**

[事实] The Kremlin is described as a citadel within Moscow, separated by walls and a moat.

[事实] Cathedral Square, the Palace of Facets, and the Red Staircase become central locations in the episode’s political drama.

[推测] The repeated return to the Red Staircase turns it into a symbol of both dynastic legitimacy and traumatic violence.

[14:28] **Peter’s Family and Rival Dynastic Factions**

[事实] Peter’s father Alexis had children by two wives: Maria Miloslavskaya and Natalia Naryshkina.

[事实] Maria’s sons included Fedor, who was bright but sickly, and Ivan, who had serious physical and speech difficulties.

[事实] Alexis’s second wife, Natalia, was promoted through the influence of Artamon Matveyev, a modernizing chief minister.

[推测] The rivalry between the Miloslavsky and Naryshkin families created the political fault line that shaped Peter’s childhood.

[16:22] **Matveyev and Western Influence in Moscow**

[事实] Artamon Matveyev is described as a meritocrat and modernizer who collected books, had a laboratory, and maintained a private theatre.

[事实] His wife, Mary Hamilton, was the daughter of a Scottish royalist exile.

[事实] Foreign merchants and diplomats increasingly lived in a segregated “German suburb” outside Moscow, where they could smoke, stage plays, and follow foreign customs.

[事实] Russian authorities and the Orthodox Church strongly disapproved of foreign habits such as tobacco, foreign hats, and shaving.

[19:03] **Alexis Dies and Peter Loses Protection**

[事实] Tsar Alexis died in 1676 after catching a chill, when Peter was about three and a half.

[事实] Alexis was succeeded by Peter’s half-brother Fedor, who was 14 and so sickly that he had to be carried to his coronation.

[事实] Matveyev was arrested, stripped of property, and exiled to an obscure Arctic town.

[事实] Fedor treated Peter decently and ensured he was educated, but Fedor died after six years, creating a succession crisis.

[21:01] **The 1682 Succession Crisis**

[事实] In 1682, Peter was 10 and Ivan was 16; the Naryshkins backed Peter, while the Miloslavskys backed Ivan.

[事实] The patriarch asked the crowd whether they wanted Ivan or Peter, and the crowd shouted for Peter.

[事实] Peter was initially accepted as Tsar with his mother Natalia as regent.

[推测] The public acclamation looked decisive, but it left Sophia and the Miloslavsky faction with strong incentives to resist.

[22:31] **Sophia Enters the Struggle**

[事实] Sophia, Peter’s half-sister, was nearly 25, unusually educated, politically experienced, and had attended council meetings under Fedor.

[事实] In a deeply sexist political culture, royal women were normally confined to the Terem and excluded from power.

[事实] Sophia demanded a joint monarchy between Peter and Ivan and claimed that Fedor had been poisoned.

[推测] Sophia’s political survival depended on preventing Peter’s faction from fully controlling the throne.

[24:00] **The Streltsy as Armed Power Brokers**

[事实] The Streltsy were Moscow’s professional standing troops, founded by Ivan the Terrible.

[事实] They had become a hereditary military caste and were resentful of officers who abused them and used them for private labor.

[事实] Sophia and her allies exploited Streltsy grievances by spreading rumors that Fedor had been poisoned by foreign doctors and that the Naryshkins threatened Orthodoxy and the army.

[推测] The Streltsy revolt combined factional manipulation with real military resentment.

[26:09] **The Streltsy Storm the Kremlin**

[事实] On May 15, Sophia’s allies told the Streltsy that the Naryshkins had murdered Ivan and were taking over the Kremlin.

[事实] The Streltsy marched into the Kremlin, demanding the Naryshkins and Matveyev.

[事实] Natalia brought Ivan and Peter onto the Red Staircase to prove Ivan was alive.

[事实] Matveyev initially calmed the Streltsy, but Prince Michael Dolgoruky provoked them by threatening punishment.

[29:02] **Massacre on the Red Staircase**

[事实] The Streltsy threw Dolgoruky over the balustrade onto pikes and then killed Matveyev in the same way.

[事实] Natalia, Peter, and Ivan were left terrified while the Streltsy searched the palace for Naryshkin relatives.

[事实] The Streltsy used threats to force court servants and others to reveal hiding places.

[事实] The violence continued through the afternoon, with bodies dragged into Red Square.

[32:40] **Natalia Forced to Surrender Her Brother**

[事实] After the first day of killing, the Streltsy returned and demanded Natalia’s brother Ivan Naryshkin.

[事实] Sophia told Natalia that surrendering her brother was the only way to save their lives.

[事实] Natalia’s brother was given communion, handed an icon, and then taken out, tortured, mutilated, and killed.

[事实] The Streltsy were rewarded with money, made palace guards, and commemorated with a triumphal column listing their victims as criminals.

[36:06] **Joint Coronation and Peter’s Trauma**

[事实] Peter was not deposed, but Ivan was made joint Tsar and listed first, while Sophia became regent.

[事实] Peter and Ivan were crowned with the Golden Cap of Monomakh, associated with Moscow’s claim to Byzantine inheritance.

[事实] The hosts state that Peter saw around 40 relatives slaughtered in horrific circumstances.

[推测] This childhood trauma fed Peter’s later hatred of the Streltsy and of what he saw as Russia’s medieval superstition and barbarism.

[38:30] **Sophia’s Regency and Golitsyn’s Reforms**

[事实] Sophia ruled with the help of the Streltsy commander Shaklovity and Prince Vasily Golitsyn.

[事实] Golitsyn was a Westernizer, fluent in Latin and Greek, with a palace full of Western art, maps, and mirrors.

[事实] He admired Louis XIV and supported ideas including religious tolerance, serf emancipation, and a reformed army.

[事实] The hosts say Sophia and Golitsyn governed Russia well for about a decade.

[41:01] **Foreign Policy and the Crimean Problem**

[事实] Golitsyn negotiated a treaty with Poland-Lithuania that gave Russia territory including Smolensk, Kiev, and eastern Ukraine.

[事实] The treaty required Russia to join Poland-Lithuania, the Habsburgs, and Venice against the Ottomans.

[事实] Golitsyn chose to attack the Crimean Khanate, an Ottoman vassal.

[推测] This foreign-policy obligation became the weakness that helped bring down Sophia and Golitsyn.

[42:04] **Peter’s Youth Away from Power**

[事实] During Sophia’s regency, Peter was mostly a figurehead, appearing at banquets and ceremonies.

[事实] Foreign observers described him as attractive, lively, frank, and promising.

[事实] Peter spent much time with his mother at Preobrazhenskoye, his father’s former hunting lodge.

[推测] Being away from the Kremlin gave Peter space to develop interests outside traditional court culture.

[43:00] **Peter’s War Games and Mock Army**

[事实] Peter developed an obsession with military exercises and created a play army from servants, nobles, serfs, and young men who came to join him.

[事实] The troops wore Western-style uniforms and eventually evolved into the Preobrazhensky and Semyonovsky Guards regiments.

[事实] Peter insisted on starting as a common soldier rather than commander, later promoting himself gradually.

[推测] The hosts interpret this as politically meaningful in a rigidly hierarchical society, suggesting Peter’s interest in merit and practical competence.

[45:54] **Role-Playing, Authority, and Practical Curiosity**

[事实] Peter created mock institutions and enjoyed role-playing, practical jokes, and military performance.

[事实] The hosts argue that his role-playing subverted traditional ideas of Tsarist dignity without rejecting absolute rule.

[事实] Peter had enormous curiosity about practical skills, including clocks, carpentry, lathes, masonry tools, ivory carving, and woodturning.

[推测] Peter wanted to redefine what a Tsar should be: not less powerful, but more active, technical, and personally engaged.

[48:02] **The English Boat and Peter’s Naval Obsession**

[事实] In 1688, Peter received a sextant and learned from a Dutchman, Franz Timmerman, who taught him geometry and geography.

[事实] Peter and Timmerman found an English boat in a shed; another Dutchman, Karsten Brandt, repaired it.

[事实] Peter sailed the boat daily, and the hosts say it remains in the Naval Museum in St. Petersburg as the “Grandfather of the Russian Navy.”

[事实] Peter later built a boatyard and ordered more vessels, including frigates and yachts.

[推测] The hosts interpret Peter’s love of sailing as a desire to escape the claustrophobic world of Moscow and look westward.

[50:17] **Marriage to Eudokia**

[事实] Peter’s mother became worried about his time with Dutchmen and boats and arranged a Russian wife for him.

[事实] Peter married Eudokia Lopukhina in January 1689.

[事实] Eudokia came from a conservative Moscow family, and Peter showed little interest in her.

[事实] They had a son, Alexis, while a second son died as a baby; Peter did not attend the funeral.

[52:02] **Golitsyn’s Crimean Failure**

[事实] Natalia urged Peter to return to Moscow in 1689 because Golitsyn’s failed Crimean campaigns had weakened Sophia’s regime.

[事实] The first campaign in 1687 failed before reaching Crimea.

[事实] The second campaign in 1689 ended badly, with the army retreating after about 20,000 deaths and 15,000 prisoners taken.

[事实] Golitsyn returned to Moscow and declared victory, but Peter refused to attend the celebration.

[54:02] **Peter and Sophia Move Toward Confrontation**

[事实] Sophia received an anonymous note claiming Peter planned to attack her with his young soldiers.

[事实] Peter’s allies warned him that Sophia had mobilized the Streltsy and might strike first.

[事实] Peter fled to the Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius, a holy monastery with strong defensive walls about 40 miles from Moscow.

[推测] The crisis appears to have been intensified by people behind the scenes who encouraged mutual suspicion.

[55:00] **The Standoff at the Monastery**

[事实] Through August 1689, Sophia remained in the Kremlin while Peter stayed at the monastery, with each side accusing the other of treason.

[事实] Sophia tried to go to the monastery but was blocked by musketeers and forced to return to Moscow.

[事实] The hosts compare the shifting momentum to the failed 1991 coup against Mikhail Gorbachev.

[推测] Peter’s masculinity, royal status, and military associations made it easier for elites to rally to him over Sophia.

[56:34] **Foreign Officers Decide the Crisis**

[事实] The deadlock was broken when foreign officers from the German suburb chose Peter.

[事实] General Gordon, a Scotsman in Russian service, decided Peter had the stronger ruthless instinct and led foreign officers to the monastery.

[事实] The officers arrived at dawn and kissed Peter’s hand, signaling allegiance.

[事实] News of this caused the Streltsy to switch sides and arrest their commander Shaklovity, who was beheaded.

[58:24] **Sophia Falls and Peter Takes Power**

[事实] Golitsyn was stripped of property and exiled to the Arctic, where he spent the next 25 years.

[事实] Sophia was sent to a convent and was not seen in public again.

[事实] Peter entered Moscow in triumph only after Sophia was safely removed.

[事实] At age 17, Peter became the effective master of Russia, with his mother serving as regent.

[推测] The episode ends with Peter’s power secured but his central conflicts unresolved: revenge against the Streltsy, Westernization, and war with Sweden.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main strength is its vivid narrative pacing. It turns Peter’s childhood into a political thriller, while still explaining the broader structures that mattered: Orthodoxy, dynastic faction, military resentment, foreign influence, and Russia’s strategic isolation.

The most valuable historical thread is the connection between Peter’s personal trauma and his later reforms. The episode does not present modernization as a neat intellectual project; it shows how Peter’s hatred of old Muscovy was bound up with memories of bloodshed, fear, and humiliation.

A limitation is that Sophia and Golitsyn are mainly discussed through their relationship to Peter’s rise, though the hosts do note that they ruled effectively and were themselves open to Westernizing reforms. [推测] Listeners interested in Sophia as a ruler in her own right may want more detail than this episode provides.

[推测] This episode is especially suitable for listeners interested in Russian history, early modern state-building, court politics, and the origins of Russia’s later imperial identity.
