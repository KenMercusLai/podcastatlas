# Cortés Sets Out: Faith, Gold, La Malinche, and the Road Inland

## 概览

This episode follows Hernán Cortés after Diego Velázquez sends him from Cuba toward the Mexican coast. The hosts frame Cortés as a man whose ambitions quickly outrun his official instructions: he is told to explore, assist Grijalva, spread Christianity, and gather information, but not to conquer.

A central theme is motive. The discussion stresses that gold, glory, adventure, and Christian conviction all mattered to the Spaniards, and that their conquest cannot be understood only as cynical greed or only as religious mission.

The episode then turns to the practical reasons Cortés becomes dangerous: steel swords, horses, ships, legal maneuvering, and above all interpreters. Gerónimo de Aguilar and especially La Malinche give Cortés access to local languages, politics, rivalries, and alliances.

By the end, Cortés has founded Vera Cruz, neutralized Velázquez’s authority, beached his ships, and committed his men to march inland toward the Mexica capital.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **Cortés Frames the Expedition**

[事实] The episode opens with a dramatic reading of Cortés addressing his men before setting out, promising wealth, hardship, loyalty, victory, and service to God.
[事实] The hosts identify the scene as part of Dominic’s children’s book about Cortés, Moctezuma, the Spaniards, and the Aztecs.
[事实] The Latin motto on Cortés’s standard is explained as a call to follow the cross and trust that victory will come through it.
[推测] The opening establishes the episode’s central tension: conquest is presented simultaneously as religious mission, personal ambition, and search for wealth.

[02:13] **Velázquez’s Orders and Cortés’s Motives**

[事实] Velázquez sends Cortés west because he cannot leave Cuba, which is being ravaged by smallpox among the Taíno population.
[事实] Cortés is instructed to find Grijalva, explore the Yucatán coast, look for a route to China and India, investigate reports of unusual peoples, spread Christianity, and seek information about gold.
[事实] Velázquez does not instruct Cortés to conquer the lands he encounters.
[推测] The instructions are contradictory enough to give Cortés room to reinterpret his mission once opportunity appears.

[04:11] **Christianity, Empire, and Moral Ambivalence**

[事实] The hosts argue that Cortés and other conquistadors should be understood as genuinely Christian, not merely as men using religion as a cover.
[事实] They cite Fernando Cervantes’s argument that the conquest cannot be understood without taking the conquistadors’ Christianity seriously.
[事实] Las Casas is discussed as a figure who argued that Indigenous peoples had souls and rights, and who condemned Spanish abuses.
[推测] The episode presents Spanish imperial ideology as internally conflicted: violent conquest coexisted with legal and theological anxieties about its legitimacy.

[08:20] **Cortés Breaks Away from Velázquez**

[事实] Velázquez becomes alarmed by Cortés’s behavior and tries to cancel the expedition, but Cortés ignores him and sails away.
[事实] Cortés leaves on February 10, 1519, with 11 ships and about 500 men.
[事实] The expedition includes Spaniards, Taíno servants and slaves, likely African slaves, and at least one known African freedman, Juan Garrido.
[推测] Cortés’s decision to leave despite Velázquez’s objections marks him as more ambitious and opportunistic than Velázquez had assumed.

[09:56] **The Expedition as Armed Entrepreneurship**

[事实] Cortés recruits Pedro de Alvarado, described as charismatic, ruthless, bloodthirsty, and hot-tempered.
[事实] The Spaniards are not a disciplined army; they are rival factions, often bound by family or regional connections, temporarily cooperating for gain.
[事实] Matthew Restall’s phrase “armed entrepreneurs” is used to describe the conquistadors.
[推测] Cortés’s leadership problem is political as much as military: he must manage factions that could turn against him.

[14:40] **Spanish Military Advantages**

[事实] The hosts argue that gunpowder is often overstated as the key Spanish advantage, since cannons are unreliable though frightening and sometimes deadly against massed attackers.
[事实] Steel Toledo swords are presented as a major technological advantage over local clubs and obsidian weapons.
[事实] The Spaniards have 16 horses, and horses terrify people in the Americas because they are unknown there.
[事实] Ships are described as crucial because they allow more people and resources to arrive from across the sea.
[事实] Smallpox is identified as an unwitting biological weapon that will later devastate Indigenous populations.

[17:21] **Cozumel and the First Conversions**

[事实] Cortés lands on Cozumel and reacts to temples, idols, and signs of sacrifice.
[事实] He has idols rolled down temple steps and an image of the Virgin Mary put up.
[事实] He tells locals to stop human sacrifice and accept Christ; the locals appear to comply or pretend to comply.
[推测] The local response is likely shaped by fear of the Spaniards’ unfamiliar weapons and animals.

[18:28] **Gerónimo de Aguilar Appears**

[事实] Maya locals tell the Spaniards that a white Christian man is living on the mainland.
[事实] A bearded Spaniard, Gerónimo de Aguilar, arrives by canoe and speaks to the Spaniards.
[事实] Aguilar says he had been shipwrecked, captured, and enslaved after reaching the Yucatán coast.
[事实] Another survivor, Gonzalo Guerrero, had integrated into Maya society, married a Maya woman, had children, tattooed his face, and later fought against the Spaniards.
[推测] Aguilar’s account is treated cautiously because the hosts note that it comes from him and cannot be fully verified.

[22:01] **Aguilar Becomes an Interpreter**

[事实] Aguilar can speak a Mesoamerican language, making him an important asset to Cortés.
[事实] The hosts stress that this is the first time the Spaniards have a Spaniard with them who can speak one of the local languages.
[事实] The episode then introduces La Malinche as an even more important figure.
[推测] The arrival of interpreters begins to change the expedition from blind coastal raiding into political intervention.

[23:07] **La Malinche’s Origins**

[事实] La Malinche grew up in Coatzacoalcos and was ethnically Nahua, speaking the same language as the Aztecs though not being Mexica herself.
[事实] After her father died and her mother remarried, she was taken to the coast and sold into Maya slavery.
[事实] She eventually ended up in Potonchan, a Maya trading town and slave market.
[推测] The hosts present parts of her early life as uncertain, including the exact role of her mother and her original name.

[24:59] **Potonchan and the Gift of Enslaved Women**

[事实] Cortés reaches the region of modern Tabasco and goes inland after hearing of a town.
[事实] The townsfolk first welcome the Spaniards, then conflict with them; the Spaniards use cannon and horses to defeat and scare them.
[事实] The Maya agree to embrace Christ and acknowledge the King of Spain, though the hosts doubt the sincerity or meaning of these promises.
[事实] The people of Potonchan give the Spaniards food and 20 enslaved women, one of whom is La Malinche.

[27:07] **La Malinche Reveals Her Importance**

[事实] Near the Isle of Sacrifices, Totonacs come from the mainland to meet Cortés.
[事实] Aguilar cannot understand the Totonacs because he speaks Maya, but La Malinche understands them because they speak Nahuatl.
[事实] A translation chain forms: La Malinche translates Nahuatl into Maya, and Aguilar translates Maya into Spanish.
[事实] La Malinche later learns Spanish, reducing Aguilar’s importance.
[推测] Her linguistic skill makes her indispensable because she can explain not only words but social rules, politics, rituals, and rivalries.

[29:24] **La Malinche’s Agency and Hatred of the Mexica**

[事实] The hosts argue that La Malinche hated the Mexica because she blamed them for the suffering and slavery imposed on her.
[事实] They note that Mexican tradition has often treated her as a traitor, while more recent interpretations emphasize her victimhood.
[事实] The hosts also stress that she had agency and may have shaped Cortés’s understanding of events.
[推测] La Malinche may have seen the Spaniards as a vehicle for vengeance against the Mexica and as a power worth backing because she understood that more Spaniards could arrive by sea.

[31:49] **Cortés Depends on La Malinche but Minimizes Her**

[事实] Cortés’s letters to Charles V depend on information filtered through La Malinche because he does not speak the local languages.
[事实] Cortés mentions translators only occasionally and does not dwell on La Malinche’s role.
[事实] Indigenous accounts and painted books often give La Malinche a prominent place.
[事实] Some Indigenous accounts call Cortés “Malinche,” blurring the two figures.
[推测] Cortés may have resented how visibly his power depended on a young Indigenous woman.

[34:35] **Totonacs, Aztec Envoys, and First Diplomacy**

[事实] The Totonacs are friendly to the Spaniards and provide food including beans, turkey, and tortillas.
[事实] On Easter Sunday, representatives of the Mexica arrive, led by a regional official called Tendile.
[事实] Tendile says the emperor is pleased by the Spaniards’ arrival and gives them gifts including gold and feathers.
[事实] Cortés gives Tendile items including a coat, beads, and an armchair, which Tendile reportedly regards with contempt.

[36:32] **The Requerimiento and Spanish Legalism**

[事实] Spanish captains are legally required to read the Requerimiento when seeking to subjugate new peoples.
[事实] The Requerimiento explains Christian history, the authority of the Spanish king, and the demand that Indigenous peoples submit and become Christian.
[事实] Las Casas condemned the Requerimiento as unjust, impious, scandalous, irrational, and absurd.
[事实] The hosts emphasize that Spaniards took legal forms very seriously, even amid violence and conquest.
[推测] The Requerimiento is aimed as much at European legal justification as at Indigenous understanding.

[39:29] **Rights, Land, and Legal Consequences**

[事实] The hosts argue that the Requerimiento implicitly recognizes that Indigenous peoples have some kind of claim to their lands, even while demanding submission.
[事实] Later in the 16th century, Indigenous delegations go to the Spanish royal court to argue that agreements made with conquistadors should be honored.
[事实] The hosts say some of these delegations win their cases.
[推测] Spanish legalism, despite its violence and hypocrisy, later provides tools for Indigenous claims within the imperial system.

[40:53] **Firepower, Gold, and the Question of Divinity**

[事实] Cortés demonstrates cannon fire and horsemanship to Tendile, frightening the Aztec representatives.
[事实] Cortés asks to meet the emperor and visit the capital, but Tendile refuses, citing drought.
[事实] Cortés is said to have claimed that Spaniards suffer from a sickness of the heart cured only by gold, though the hosts treat this story as possibly mythologized.
[事实] Modern historians are described as highly skeptical of the claim that the Aztecs thought the Spaniards were gods.
[推测] The “Spaniards as gods” story may have arisen from later misunderstandings, translation shifts, or Spanish reinterpretation of high-status Nahuatl language.

[44:51] **Vera Cruz and the Legal Coup**

[事实] Cortés faces a choice between returning to Cuba as ordered or going inland toward the Mexica capital.
[事实] He proposes founding a town on the coast called Villa Rica de la Vera Cruz.
[事实] The Spaniards hold town council elections, and Cortés formally resigns as expedition leader before being appointed chief justice and captain general of the town.
[事实] This maneuver gives Cortés a legal basis to act independently of Velázquez.
[推测] The founding of Vera Cruz is a legal fiction that turns rebellion against Velázquez into service to king and God.

[46:33] **Treasure, Ships, and No Turning Back**

[事实] Cortés sends treasure to Spain rather than Cuba, using Francisco de Montejo, a Velázquez ally, as the messenger.
[事实] This removes Montejo from the expedition and helps Cortés appeal directly to the king.
[事实] Contrary to legend, Cortés does not burn his ships; he beaches them and uses some timber for the new town.
[事实] Beaching the ships prevents an easy return and signals that the expedition will continue inland.

[48:00] **The March Inland Begins**

[事实] On August 16, 1519, Cortés assembles his men on the beach, attends mass with them, and prepares to announce his plan.
[事实] The episode ends by setting up the march toward Tenochtitlan and the coming confrontation between Cortés and Moctezuma.
[事实] The hosts raise the possibility that Cortés may have been encouraged, misled, or manipulated by La Malinche or by Mexica strategy.
[推测] The cliffhanger frames the inland march as a gamble whose success depends on diplomacy, misunderstanding, ambition, and local rivalries as much as on Spanish force.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it avoids a single-cause explanation of conquest. It treats Christianity, legalism, violence, greed, ambition, and Indigenous politics as overlapping forces rather than competing simplifications.

Its strongest section is the treatment of La Malinche. The hosts make clear that she was enslaved and vulnerable, but also politically decisive, linguistically gifted, and possibly able to shape the Spanish understanding of the world they entered.

The main limitation is that much of the story depends on Spanish accounts and later traditions, which the hosts acknowledge at several points. Claims about Aguilar’s captivity, Cortés’s famous gold remark, and Aztec interpretations of the Spaniards are all treated with caution.

This episode suits listeners interested in the conquest of Mexico, early modern empire, Indigenous diplomacy, and the way historical myths form around dramatic events.
