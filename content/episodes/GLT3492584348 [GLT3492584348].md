# The Irish War of Independence: From Dáil Éireann to Bloody Sunday

## 概览

This episode traces the Irish revolutionary struggle from Sinn Féin’s 1918 electoral victory into the first phase of the Irish War of Independence. It opens with “The Galtie Mountain Boy” and then complicates the romantic image of Irish balladry by focusing on the harsher realities behind figures such as Dan Breen and Seán Hogan.

The central turning point is 21 January 1919: Sinn Féin MPs founded Dáil Éireann in Dublin, while the Soloheadbeg ambush in Tipperary killed two Irish Catholic policemen and is presented as the first engagement of the war. The episode then follows how a shadow Irish state, IRA violence, boycotts, labour action, propaganda, and international diplomacy interacted.

By 1920, the conflict had intensified. The British state relied increasingly on the Black and Tans and Auxiliaries, while the IRA developed flying columns, intelligence networks, and localised guerrilla tactics. The discussion closes with sectarianism, the Government of Ireland Act, the creation of Northern Ireland, and the approach to Bloody Sunday at Croke Park.

## 分段落总结

[00:10] **The Ballad and the Mythic Frame**

[事实] The episode begins with “The Galtie Mountain Boy,” described as a song about a teenage volunteer who joins the Irish Republican cause in 1916, fights in the War of Independence, and is later sentenced to death by Free Staters in the Civil War.

[事实] The hosts use the ballad to introduce Seán Hogan and Dan Breen, who will feature in the story of the Soloheadbeg ambush.

[推测] The opening sets up a contrast between romantic nationalist memory and the more morally difficult history discussed in the episode.

[02:22] **Setting the Political Context**

[事实] The hosts are recording in Dublin at the Royal Irish Academy and continuing the story from December 1918.

[事实] Sinn Féin had won a landslide in Ireland on a pledge to build an independent Irish Republic and not take seats in the House of Commons.

[事实] Professor Paul Rouse joins the episode as the Irish historian guiding the discussion.

[03:54] **21 January 1919 and the First Dáil**

[事实] On 21 January 1919, 27 of the 73 elected Sinn Féin MPs met at the Mansion House in Dublin and established Dáil Éireann.

[事实] Countess Markievicz was absent because she was in Holloway jail, but she is described as the first woman elected to the House of Commons and as a major organiser in the Irish Revolution.

[事实] The Dáil produced four foundational texts: a constitution, a declaration of independence, a message to the free nations of the world, and a democratic programme.

[推测] The Dáil’s aim was not merely symbolic; it sought to make the Irish Republic appear practically real by creating institutions that could rival British authority.

[08:17] **Soloheadbeg and the First Shots**

[事实] On the same day, James McDonnell and Patrick O’Connell were escorting gelignite to a quarry near Soloheadbeg when a group including Seán Treacy, Dan Breen, Seamus Robinson, and Seán Hogan attacked.

[事实] The attackers wanted arms and explosives, and Paul Rouse says they also wanted to kill someone in order to start a war.

[事实] The two dead policemen were Irish Catholic members of the Royal Irish Constabulary, not British soldiers.

[事实] The killings caused local revulsion, outrage in newspapers, and criticism from pulpits and from some people in the movement.

[12:43] **From Set-Piece Rebellion to Guerrilla War**

[事实] The hosts contrast the Easter Rising’s set-piece occupation of buildings with the later War of Independence as a “hedge war” and urban street war.

[事实] After Soloheadbeg, Breen and Treacy went on the run, while Hogan was later captured and freed in a violent train rescue.

[事实] Paul Rouse describes Dan Breen as a hard, violent man who was unrepentant about killing.

[推测] The episode presents the early IRA not as polished heroes but as young, often ruthless militants operating in a messy and improvised conflict.

[14:45] **Low-Level Violence and Boycotts in 1919**

[事实] Much of 1919 involved small-scale violence, raids for arms, police station attacks, boycotts, assaults on police and loyalists, and resistance to arrest.

[事实] Boycotts against RIC members made everyday life difficult for policemen and helped destabilise the police’s place in Irish society.

[事实] Ten policemen were shot during the rest of 1919, and soldiers were also attacked, including an incident involving Shropshire Light Infantry soldiers going to a Methodist service.

[推测] The campaign’s strength lay partly in social pressure as much as in direct armed action.

[17:58] **The Dáil as an Alternative State**

[事实] Dáil Éireann tried to operate as a state despite harassment and suppression by British authorities.

[事实] De Valera escaped from Lincoln jail with help including Michael Collins, returned as president, and key figures such as Collins, Griffith, Markievicz, and others led ministries.

[事实] Collins raised a loan from around 150,000 people and was also involved in intelligence and violence, including a unit that killed Alan Bell, who had pursued Dáil funds.

[事实] The Dáil established courts, including parish courts, district courts, and a Supreme Court, which helped withdraw allegiance from British institutions.

[23:14] **International Recognition and Paris**

[事实] The Dáil sought international recognition at the Paris Peace Conference and appealed to Woodrow Wilson’s language of self-determination.

[事实] Seán T. O’Kelly went to Paris and sought a seat at the table, but Ireland did not secure recognition.

[事实] Paul Rouse stresses that Ireland differed from postwar states emerging from defeated empires because it sought independence from Britain, a victorious empire at the height of its power.

[推测] British leaders feared that conceding Ireland could encourage anti-imperial movements in places such as Egypt and India.

[25:51] **De Valera in America**

[事实] De Valera went to America in June 1919 to seek recognition for the Irish Republic and raise money.

[事实] He drew large crowds, including 50,000 people at Fenway Park, and a bond drive raised 5.5 million dollars.

[事实] The American mission raised money but did not secure decisive political support from Democrats or Republicans.

[推测] De Valera’s absence allowed Collins and Griffith, especially Collins, to grow in stature inside Ireland.

[27:36] **Labour, Strikes, and the Wider Movement**

[事实] Labour action formed part of the revolutionary pressure, including the Limerick general strike, a two-day strike for political prisoners, a railway munitions strike, and Dublin dockers refusing to handle war material.

[事实] These actions affected Britain’s ability to combat the IRA.

[事实] The Dáil received recognition from the international socialist conference in Bern, but did not secure recognition from Moscow.

[推测] The episode suggests that the struggle was broader than armed nationalism and included labour, propaganda, finance, and diplomacy.

[29:05] **Who Joined the IRA**

[事实] By 1920, “Irish Republican Army” had become the common term for the Irish Volunteers.

[事实] Paul Rouse says IRA members were often in their late teens and twenties, born in the 1890s, and included literate, educated, and skilled people as well as farm workers and labourers.

[事实] Many young people had been unable to emigrate during the First World War and remained in Ireland.

[推测] The hosts compare the IRA’s young militants to postwar paramilitary groups elsewhere in Europe, while noting the Irish context had its own features.

[32:17] **History, Ballads, Sport, and Radicalisation**

[事实] Paul Rouse discusses the contested teaching of Irish history and claims that schools and cultural memory helped shape nationalist radicalisation.

[事实] Ballads such as “A Nation Once Again” are presented as part of the emotional and cultural background of nationalism.

[事实] The Gaelic Athletic Association is described as an Irish response to the English sporting revolution and as a training ground for organisers such as Michael Collins, Harry Boland, and J. J. Walsh.

[推测] Sport and historical memory helped create networks, confidence, and identity that could later be used in politics and revolution.

[33:22] **Women in the War of Independence**

[事实] Women were victims of violence, including degrading treatment, head-shaving, and rape.

[事实] Women also supported the IRA through logistics, espionage, gun trafficking, intelligence, and propaganda.

[事实] Mary and Muriel MacSwiney travelled to the United States after Terence MacSwiney’s death, and Mary spoke at 300 meetings in 58 cities.

[推测] The episode deliberately expands the story beyond armed young men by emphasising women’s organisational and propagandistic roles.

[37:49] **Escalation in 1920**

[事实] By early 1920, the war became fiercer as the IRA attacked police barracks, courthouses, and taxation offices.

[事实] By Easter 1920, about 350 police barracks had been destroyed, and the RIC withdrew toward larger towns.

[事实] Some British officials continued to frame the conflict as criminality and a law-and-order problem rather than a war.

[推测] The destruction of local state infrastructure made British authority visibly retreat in many areas.

[39:21] **Black and Tans and Auxiliaries**

[事实] As RIC members left the force, Britain recruited unemployed former soldiers from Britain into policing roles at about 100 per week until June 1920.

[事实] These recruits became known as the Black and Tans because of their distinctive uniforms.

[事实] The Auxiliaries, mainly former British Army officers, also arrived in 1920, and the combined forces may have numbered around 10,000.

[事实] Paul Rouse says these forces became known for brutality, drunkenness, and unrestrained violence in some places.

[41:53] **British Confusion and Weak Command**

[事实] The IRA’s guerrilla tactics frustrated British forces because attackers could emerge from and disappear into the civilian population.

[事实] Paul Rouse says British leadership in Dublin Castle and the Viceroy’s office failed badly, and Lord French did not take the Republicans seriously until too late.

[事实] Sir Warren Fisher’s report criticised Dublin Castle’s administration as almost “woodenly stupid.”

[推测] British failure was caused not only by guerrilla tactics but also by divided command, poor administration, and political uncertainty.

[43:43] **Reprisals and the British Dilemma**

[事实] The British response included reprisals by Black and Tans and Auxiliaries, including destruction in places such as Cork, Balbriggan, Boyle, Lahinch, Tralee, and Listowel.

[事实] British public opinion, Labour Party delegations, and newspapers such as the Manchester Guardian included criticism of British actions in Ireland.

[事实] Britain was financially strained and tired of war after the First World War.

[推测] The British state was caught between insufficient conciliation and politically impossible total repression.

[46:18] **Flying Columns and Intelligence**

[事实] Flying columns emerged from the summer of 1920, not in 1916 as the ballad suggests.

[事实] The IRA gained more arms through routes involving America, Liverpool, England, and Scotland.

[事实] Michael Collins’s intelligence network included informants in Dublin Castle, the GPO, railways, and among typists and junior detectives.

[事实] Violence was highly localised, with some counties seeing intense action and others very little.

[48:39] **Sectarianism and the Peter Hart Debate**

[事实] The hosts discuss whether IRA violence had a sectarian edge, especially in relation to Peter Hart’s book on the IRA in Cork.

[事实] Paul Rouse says the War of Independence was not a sectarian war, but he rejects the idea that sectarian motives were entirely absent.

[事实] Hart’s controversial topics included the Kilmichael ambush and the murders of 13 Protestants in the Bandon Valley after the truce.

[事实] Paul Rouse says Hart made serious mistakes, especially in footnoting interviews and in using loose, provocative language such as “ethnic cleansing.”

[54:05] **Catholicism, Republicanism, and Irish Identity**

[事实] The hosts ask whether the older ideal of Protestants and Catholics sharing a single island was fading by 1920.

[事实] Paul Rouse says Irish nationalism became increasingly identified with Catholicism during the nineteenth century, especially through Daniel O’Connell and the growing power of the Catholic Church.

[事实] He also notes that a secular republican strand rejected the Catholic Church.

[推测] The episode frames Irish nationalism as internally diverse but increasingly shaped by Catholic identity before and during the revolutionary period.

[55:21] **Government of Ireland Act and Northern Ireland**

[事实] In 1920, Britain passed the Government of Ireland Act, creating two home rule parliaments, one in Dublin and one in Belfast.

[事实] The Act effectively established Northern Ireland as six counties rather than the historic nine-county Ulster.

[事实] Paul Rouse says six counties gave Northern Ireland a 34% Catholic population, compared with about 43% across nine counties in the 1911 census.

[事实] The hosts argue that the Act was irrelevant to Sinn Féin because 26-county home rule was far short of its aims.

[57:52] **Toward Bloody Sunday**

[事实] By autumn 1920, the conflict had lasted almost two years and involved reprisals, burned houses, and families driven from homes.

[事实] The episode points ahead to 21 November 1920, the day of the Dublin versus Tipperary Gaelic football match at Croke Park.

[事实] The hosts identify that day as Bloody Sunday and describe it as the most controversial day of the conflict.

[推测] The ending positions the next episode as the culmination of the escalation described here.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its refusal to treat the Irish War of Independence as either simple patriotic romance or simple criminal violence. It shows how elections, courts, finance, labour, propaganda, gender, guerrilla war, and imperial politics all formed one connected struggle.

[推测] Its strongest feature is Paul Rouse’s ability to hold together detail and moral complexity: the episode recognises the legitimacy crisis of British rule while also dwelling on murdered policemen, sectarian motives, reprisals, and the psychology of young armed men.

[推测] The limitation is that the episode covers a wide arc quickly and leaves some major events, especially Bloody Sunday and Kilmichael, for later discussion. It is best suited to listeners who already have some interest in Irish history and want a structured but conversational account of how the conflict escalated between 1919 and 1920.
