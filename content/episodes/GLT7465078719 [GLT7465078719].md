# The World Cup of Gods Kicks Off

## 概览

This episode previews The Rest Is History’s “World Cup of Gods,” a mock tournament in which historical deities compete by public vote. The hosts explain that eligible gods must no longer be actively worshipped in a major living religious tradition, though revived or marginal worship does not necessarily disqualify them.

The discussion introduces the 16 contenders, mixing historical background with sports-style punditry. The hosts move from unseeded entrants such as Anubis, Augustus Caesar, Brigid, Cybele, Ishtar, Moloch, Prince Philip, and Xipe Totec to seeded figures including Mithras, Zeus, Odin, Isis, Dionysus, Apollo, Loki, and Athena.

The episode’s main conclusion is not a scholarly ranking but an invitation to listeners to take part in the tournament on Twitter. The hosts frame the event as a playful combination of history, mythology, fandom, and sporting spectacle, ending with the promise of a later full-length analysis after the final.

## 分段落总结

[01:01] **Launching the World Cup of Gods**

[事实] The hosts announce the start of the “World Cup of Gods,” comparing the atmosphere to a major sporting event with crowds, banners, vuvuzelas, and ticket touts.
[事实] They explain that this follows an earlier “World Cup of British Prime Ministers.”
[事实] The winner will be treated as the “world champion of Gods,” under the slogan “there can be only one.”
[推测] The tournament format is designed as comic public history: historical knowledge is presented through the language of sports coverage.

[01:56] **Eligibility Rules**

[事实] The hosts say that gods currently worshipped by more than 10 people are excluded.
[事实] A god can qualify if worship of that deity historically ceased, even if there are later revivals.
[事实] Odin is given as an example: modern Odin worshippers exist, but the hosts treat that as a revival rather than continuous worship.
[推测] The “dead gods” rule lets the show avoid living major religions while still allowing famous mythological figures into the competition.

[03:26] **Anubis and Augustus Caesar**

[事实] Anubis is introduced as an Egyptian god associated with the underworld and mummification.
[事实] The hosts say Anubis was worshipped across the long span of Egyptian civilization, possibly for more than 3,500 years.
[事实] Augustus Caesar is included because he was worshipped as a god after his death, despite being “clearly a man.”
[事实] Augustus lived from 63 BC to AD 14, and his cult is described as probably the fastest-growing cult in the first century AD.
[推测] Anubis is treated as a strong historical contender, while Augustus is framed as a more controversial entrant.

[04:57] **Brigid and Cybele**

[事实] Brigid is described as a controversial qualifier and as the Irish goddess of poetry, wisdom, and spring.
[事实] The hosts compare Brigid to Denmark at Euro 92: unexpected, perhaps not supposed to be there, but potentially dangerous.
[事实] Cybele is described as an Anatolian mother goddess whose worship may go back to the sixth millennium BC and continue into around 500 AD.
[推测] Brigid is positioned as a possible dark horse because Irish supporters may rally behind her.
[推测] Cybele’s main strength in the tournament is her extraordinary historical longevity.

[06:34] **Ishtar and Moloch**

[事实] Ishtar, also known as Inanna, is introduced as the Mesopotamian “queen of heaven” and goddess of sex, love, and beauty.
[事实] The hosts call her the Babylonian equivalent of Aphrodite or Venus and say she represents love goddesses in the tournament.
[事实] Moloch is described as a Canaanite child-sacrifice god associated with the Old Testament, Lebanon, and Carthage.
[事实] The hosts note that some scholars or commentators dispute whether Moloch was an actual god or a product of Israelite imagination.
[推测] Moloch’s performance in the tournament is jokingly presented as a test of whether he was “real” in cultural memory.

[07:55] **Prince Philip as a Late Entrant**

[事实] Prince Philip is included because he had been worshipped as a god by the Yaohnanen tribe on Tanna in Vanuatu.
[事实] The hosts say that after Prince Philip’s death, veneration was transferred to Prince Charles.
[事实] Prince Philip is described as a late entrant who performed strongly in the qualifiers.
[事实] The hosts mention his sporting associations, including cricket and carriage driving.
[推测] His inclusion creates comic contrast between modern royalty and ancient deities.

[09:06] **Xipe Totec and the Appeal of Style Clashes**

[事实] Xipe Totec is introduced as an Aztec god discussed previously in an episode with Camilla Townsend.
[事实] He is described as the Aztec god of vegetation and harvest.
[事实] His distinctive association is flaying, including the demand that sacrificed people be flayed.
[事实] The hosts joke that he may “flay the opposition.”
[推测] The tournament’s appeal comes partly from placing gods from different regions and eras into imagined head-to-head contests.

[10:24] **Seeded Contenders: Mithras, Zeus, and Odin**

[事实] Mithras is introduced as a seeded contender and as a rising force within the Roman Empire before being overtaken by Christianity.
[事实] The hosts say Mithras is ultimately associated with Persia and was taken up by the Romans.
[事实] Zeus is seeded seventh and described as the Greek king of the gods and lord of Olympus.
[事实] Odin is seeded sixth and compared with Zeus as another father-of-the-gods figure.
[推测] A Zeus versus Odin match is framed as one of the dream fixtures of the tournament.

[12:25] **Isis, Dionysus, and Apollo**

[事实] Isis is the fifth seed and is described as a major Egyptian goddess with later popularity in the Roman Empire.
[事实] The hosts identify Isis as mother of Horus and connected with Osiris, the murdered lord of the underworld.
[事实] Dionysus is the fourth seed and is described as the Greek god of wine, partying, and “shenanigans.”
[事实] Apollo is presented as a stylish Greek god associated with golden beauty and archery.
[推测] Isis is portrayed as almost a “team” because Horus and Osiris are bundled into her mythological story.

[14:26] **Loki and Athena**

[事实] Loki is the second seed and is described as Scandinavian, crafty, and skilled at bending the rules.
[事实] The hosts mention Loki’s profile in superhero films and note that Thor did not qualify after losing to Brigid.
[事实] Athena is the top seed and is identified as Mary Beard’s favorite.
[事实] Athena is associated with a helmet, owl, and Athens.
[推测] Loki is framed as a dangerous tournament competitor because trickery may matter as much as prestige.

[15:24] **Predictions and Vulnerable Seeds**

[事实] The hosts discuss possible early exits among the seeded gods.
[事实] Isis and Mithras are mentioned as potentially vulnerable.
[事实] Athena receives support partly because Mary Beard backs her, while Zeus is associated with Gary Lineker’s support.
[事实] One host suggests Loki will be hard to beat, while Prince Philip is floated as a possible semi-finalist.
[推测] The hosts’ predictions are intentionally playful rather than based on a formal ranking system.

[16:51] **Voting Schedule and Tournament Mechanics**

[事实] The episode says the draw will be conducted by football journalist Jonathan Wilson.
[事实] Four matches are scheduled for Saturday the 22nd and four for Sunday, with matches starting at 10 o’clock.
[事实] Listeners are told to vote on Twitter through The Rest Is History account and the hosts’ own accounts.
[事实] Voting runs until midnight, and the final is scheduled for Thursday the 27th.
[事实] The hosts say they will later record a full-length feature reviewing the tournament and offering deeper analysis of the contenders.
[推测] The voting process makes the episode a call to audience participation rather than a standalone historical lecture.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in making ancient and historical religion approachable through a deliberately unserious tournament format. It gives listeners quick reference points for a wide range of deities while keeping the tone light and accessible.

[推测] Its main strength is the hosts’ ability to turn brief mythological facts into memorable comparisons, especially through football, rugby, and punditry language. The format encourages curiosity about figures such as Cybele, Mithras, Ishtar, and Xipe Totec without becoming a dense survey.

[推测] The limitation is that the discussion is introductory and comedic, so it does not provide deep historical explanation of each cult, region, or source problem. It is best suited to regular listeners, mythology enthusiasts, and audiences who enjoy history presented through playful competition.
