# Cockroaches 1, Modi 0: India’s remarkable protests

## 概览

This episode of The Intelligence covers three stories: a youth-led protest movement in India that forced the resignation of education minister Dharmendra Pradhan, an experiment showing why macro trading remains hard even with advance news, and the global appeal of Invader’s street mosaics.

The India segment argues that the “cockroach” movement began as online satire but became a serious challenge to Narendra Modi’s image of political invulnerability. The protests focused on exam leaks, youth unemployment, education failures and a shrinking space for dissent.

The markets segment shows that knowing tomorrow’s headlines is not enough to trade well: ordinary participants and AI models struggled mainly because they misread expectations and sized bets badly. The final segment presents Invader’s mosaics and app as a rare mobile game that pushes people to look more closely at the physical city.

## 分段落总结

[01:18] **A Seismic Resignation In India**

[事实] The episode says education minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned after growing youth protests, an event described as seismic in India.
[事实] Kira Huyu says that in 12 years of Narendra Modi’s rule, no cabinet minister had previously resigned because of public pressure.
[事实] She says the resignation was unexpected even to many protesters.
[推测] The resignation is framed as a rare breach in Modi’s reputation for resisting public pressure.

[02:17] **How The “Cockroach” Movement Began**

[事实] The movement began after India’s chief justice seemed to compare jobless youth to cockroaches.
[事实] A tweet by Abhijit Tipke asking what would happen if all cockroaches came together became a satirical online meme.
[事实] The movement grew into a large youth campaign with 27 million Instagram followers and AI images of fist-raising cockroaches set to Hindi rap.
[事实] Beneath the humor, the movement focused on serious grievances around the NEET medical-school entrance exam leak, which affected more than two million exam takers.

[03:14] **Exam Scams, Pressure And Youth Anger**

[事实] The NEET leak is described as having been linked to more than 20 suicides among students trying to take the test.
[事实] The episode says India has an intensely competitive exam culture, few high-quality colleges and few jobs at the end of that path.
[事实] Kira says these pressures helped give the movement force.
[推测] The cockroach label resonated because it condensed broader feelings of humiliation, precarity and anger among young Indians.

[03:56] **Hunger Strike And Police Removal**

[事实] On July 18, plainclothes police arrived at the Delhi protest site and removed veteran activist Sonamji to a government hospital against his will.
[事实] Sonamji was on day 21 of a hunger strike supporting the demand that the education minister resign.
[事实] Protesters described the removal as a kidnapping.
[事实] Tens of thousands, maybe hundreds of thousands, then staged Delhi’s biggest protest in more than half a decade.

[04:51] **Patriotic Protest Meets Crackdown**

[事实] Kira describes the protests as initially peaceful and patriotic, with students carrying copies of the Constitution.
[事实] Protesters tried to show they were defending the country’s future, partly because the BJP often brands critics as anti-national.
[事实] Riot police without name tags later beat protesters with sticks and fired tear gas.
[事实] Police were later found to have used pellet guns, and authorities shut down the internet in central Delhi and closed 17 metro stations.

[05:59] **A Protest Bigger Than One Minister**

[事实] Protesters told Kira they would not leave until the education minister resigned.
[事实] Hunger striker Yash Mishra said education was like water and accused Modi’s government of failing to get things back on track.
[事实] The episode says about 65% of India’s population is under 35, and 25% are not in college, jobs or training.
[事实] Kira says there have been about 150 known exam leaks in the past decade, and public education spending is closer to 4% of GDP despite a stated goal of 6%.

[07:27] **Fear, Jokes And A Shrinking Space For Dissent**

[事实] Yash Mishra said he felt energetic because of “the fear” protesters had induced in the government.
[事实] Kira says conversations with protesters often moved beyond paper leaks to a broken system.
[事实] She says India has seen more than a decade under the BJP of shrinking space for dissent.
[事实] She argues that young people are not only less scared of criticizing Modi but are also making jokes about him.

[08:07] **Modi’s Response And The Movement’s Next Step**

[事实] Modi first ignored the protests, then announced policy changes that were not on the movement’s demand list.
[事实] He later released an unusual late-night video apologizing and acknowledging students’ pain.
[事实] Gen Z protesters mocked the video with online tutorials about lighting, camera angle and script.
[事实] Kira says protesters now see the resignation as a historic win and say they will return to Parliament Street if the government takes them for granted again.
[推测] The movement’s future is uncertain, especially because the BJP remains politically strong and the opposition is described as weak and divided.

[11:31] **Trading With Tomorrow’s News**

[事实] The second segment discusses an experiment in which 120 people were shown historical “tomorrow’s newspapers” for 15 trading days and asked to trade stocks and bonds.
[事实] The experiment used Wall Street Journal front pages from 2008 to 2022.
[事实] Participants could use leverage up to 50 times, similar to bets hedge funds can place.
[事实] Only about half finished with more money than they started, one in six went bust, and the average participant ended with about $51 from a $50 starting point.

[14:56] **Why Knowing The News Was Not Enough**

[事实] Josh Roberts explains that markets move not only on news itself but also on what investors had already expected.
[事实] Participants guessed market direction correctly only about 51% of the time.
[事实] The bigger problem was bet sizing: participants did not adjust their trades well according to their confidence.
[事实] Some took too much risk when they were not very sure, which caused them to go bust.

[16:38] **AI Models And Expert Macro Traders**

[事实] The researchers repeated the experiment with AI models, which guessed market direction about 60% of the time.
[事实] The AI models were still poor at sizing bets and some lost money.
[事实] Five expert macro traders took the same test, and all five made profits.
[事实] The experts’ key advantage was adjusting position size sharply according to confidence, including choosing not to bet on some days.

[18:22] **The Investment Lesson: How Much Matters**

[事实] Josh says markets can be both rational and completely wrong.
[事实] He argues that investors often spend too much time asking what to buy and too little time asking how much to buy.
[事实] He says quantifying confidence and sizing a bet accordingly may be one of the most important questions in investment.
[推测] The segment’s practical lesson is that information alone is not an edge unless it is paired with disciplined risk sizing.

[20:11] **Searching London For Invader Mosaics**

[事实] Caitlin Talbot walks through Covent Garden looking for mosaics by the French street artist Invader.
[事实] She finds a small yellow pixelated creature on James Street and another pixelated figure on Maiden Lane.
[事实] Invader has placed more than 1,500 mosaics in Paris and thousands of artworks in more than 80 cities.
[事实] His works have appeared on the Eiffel Tower, the Hollywood sign and the International Space Station.

[21:36] **Flash Invaders And Looking Up**

[事实] Invader often works at night and keeps his identity secret.
[事实] Fans use the Flash Invaders app to score points by finding and scanning the mosaics.
[事实] Players have logged more than 47 million flashes.
[事实] The mosaics use tiles as pixels, drawing on retro video games and making the images scannable by phone.
[事实] Invader says the app is meant to interact with fans and spark curiosity.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strongest segment is the India report, because it combines on-the-ground observation, protester voices and political context. It shows how a meme can become a vehicle for grievances about exams, jobs, education and democratic accountability.

The markets segment is valuable because it turns an abstract investing lesson into a clear experiment: even advance knowledge of headlines does not solve the harder problem of judging expectations and sizing risk.

The Invader segment is lighter but effective, ending the episode with a cultural story about how digital play can make people more attentive to real streets and public art. [推测] The episode suits listeners who want a mix of politics, finance and culture rather than a single-topic deep dive.
