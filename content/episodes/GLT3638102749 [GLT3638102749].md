# Ashanti: War, Gold, and the Golden Stool

## 概览

This episode follows the rise of the Ashanti Kingdom in what is now Ghana, placing it within a wider world of Atlantic trade, inland African power politics, Islamic literacy from the north, and European coastal commerce. The hosts frame Ashanti history as a subject often unfamiliar to non-African audiences because pre-colonial sub-Saharan African history is less widely known outside Africa.

The discussion moves from origin traditions and state formation under Denkyira dominance to Osei Tutu, Okomfo Anokye, the Golden Stool, Kumasi, and the militarized expansion of Ashanti power. A recurring theme is that Ashanti political authority was expressed through sacred symbols, material culture, status display, military organization, and oral tradition rather than written bureaucracy.

The second half focuses on the nineteenth-century collision between the Ashanti and the British. The hosts emphasize both British admiration for Ashanti sophistication and British alarm over slavery, conquest, and alleged human sacrifice, while also noting the hypocrisy and violence of British imperial expansion.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Bowditch and the Ashanti court**

[事实] The episode opens with T. E. Bowditch’s description of the Ashanti king’s court after his visit in May 1817.
[事实] Bowditch describes a spectacular scene with large umbrellas, costly cloths, gold ornaments, silver pipes, canes, and ceremonial weapons.
[事实] The hosts introduce the episode as part of a World Cup-themed series on countries that qualified for Qatar, with this episode focused on Ghana and the Ashanti Kingdom.

[02:16] **Ghana as a historical crossroads**

[事实] The hosts say Ghana’s coast was known as the Gold Coast and had been connected to Europeans since the arrival of the Portuguese.
[事实] They describe the region as linked both to European powers on the coast and Islamic civilizations to the north.
[事实] Among the peoples moving into what became Ghana, the Ashanti are presented as especially famous and impressive for empire-building and city-building.

[03:47] **Ashanti origin traditions**

[事实] The hosts recount several Ashanti origin traditions: that they fell from the sky, emerged from holes in the ground, or came from Lake Bosomtwe.
[事实] One tradition described in the episode says not only the Ashanti but the whole human race emerged from that lake.
[推测] These stories are presented as ways of asserting a deep, original connection between the Ashanti and the land.

[05:09] **Migration, trade, and Denkyira dominance**

[事实] The hosts say the more likely historical tradition is that the ancestors of the Ashanti came from the north during the Middle Ages.
[事实] They stress that written records are scarce and archaeology is difficult because of the rainforest environment.
[事实] With the Portuguese buying gold and slaves, trade structures and state-building began to develop along the coast and inland.
[事实] By the seventeenth century, the Denkyira were the dominant power, with the people who would become the Ashanti subordinate to them.

[06:37] **Consolidation before independence**

[事实] By the seventeenth century, an Ashanti state was beginning to develop inside the wider Denkyira empire.
[事实] The hosts connect this consolidation to iron, forest clearance, farming, and the introduction of crops from the Americas, especially maize.
[事实] Gold wealth also began to filter back to the Ashanti despite their subordination to the Denkyira.
[事实] In 1680, an Ashanti king, Obiri Yeboah, was killed in battle and his head was taken by the Denkyira.

[09:35] **Osei Tutu’s rise**

[事实] Osei Tutu, the nephew of Obiri Yeboah, is described as the founder of Ashanti greatness.
[事实] The hosts recount a tradition that his mother became pregnant after praying at a powerful river shrine.
[事实] Osei Tutu was sent as a hostage to the Denkyira, treated as a prince, and became shield-bearer to the Denkyira king.
[推测] His time among the Denkyira is presented as politically formative because it allowed him to study the methods of the dominant power.

[11:52] **Okomfo Anokye and sacred kingship**

[事实] After fleeing a scandal at the Denkyira court, Osei Tutu meets Okomfo Anokye, who is described as a powerful magician-like figure.
[事实] The hosts compare Osei Tutu and Okomfo Anokye to pairs such as King Arthur and Merlin.
[事实] Osei Tutu recognizes that overthrowing the Denkyira requires forging many scattered groups into a single people.
[推测] The episode treats sacred narrative and political unification as inseparable in Ashanti state formation.

[13:23] **The Golden Stool**

[事实] The hosts explain that local leaders’ authority was symbolized by stools, often elaborately crafted.
[事实] In the story told, Okomfo Anokye burns the existing stools to show that there can be only one supreme symbol.
[事实] The Golden Stool descends from a dark cloud and lands in Osei Tutu’s lap.
[事实] The stool is called Sika Dwa Kofi, the Golden Stool created on a Friday, and is described as containing the soul of the new people.

[16:22] **Kumasi and the war machine**

[事实] Osei Tutu chooses the capital by planting trees in three places and selecting the place where the tree grows fastest: Kumasi.
[事实] The hosts say Osei Tutu then builds a disciplined, professional military force.
[事实] The Ashanti army is compared to the Spartans and Zulus in its discipline and ability to maneuver, hold the center, and envelop enemies.
[事实] The Ashanti compare their army to a porcupine, bristling with weapons, including spears and guns.

[18:13] **War with the Denkyira**

[事实] The initial Ashanti struggle against the Denkyira goes badly, but Okomfo Anokye is said to secure defections and intervene through magic in the traditional story.
[事实] At Feasi, the Ashanti win a major victory and defeat the Denkyira.
[事实] The Denkyira king is captured after staying away from the battle, and he says he lost his crown and empire because of war.
[事实] The hosts explain that “Ashanti” is said to mean “because of war” in Twi.

[21:44] **Eighteenth-century expansion**

[事实] After defeating the Denkyira, the Ashanti expand across much of what is now Ghana.
[事实] Their power is underpinned by gold and slave trading with European coastal powers.
[事实] The hosts say British influence on the Gold Coast grows during the eighteenth century as other European powers fade.
[事实] Ashanti kings employ literate Muslims from the north to help run the bureaucracy.

[23:40] **Status, symbols, and political order**

[事实] The hosts describe Ashanti society as sophisticated and status-conscious.
[事实] Because the political system is not based on widespread writing, visual markers such as staffs, swords, gold ornaments, and stools carry major significance.
[事实] The hosts compare these symbols to courtly signs of rank in places like Byzantium.
[推测] The episode suggests that Ashanti political culture should be understood through ceremony, display, and material hierarchy rather than only through written institutions.

[24:47] **British impressions of Kumasi**

[事实] British observers are described as impressed by Kumasi’s planning, spacious streets, cleanliness, toilets, daily waste collection, and architecture.
[事实] A stone palace built in 1822 is described as having gold-covered doors and windows.
[事实] The hosts say British stereotypes of Africans as uncivilized did not fit the sophistication they encountered in Ashanti society.
[事实] The episode also notes strict laws, including a claimed death penalty for sneezing.

[28:16] **Slavery, sacrifice, and moral tension**

[事实] The hosts say slavery was widespread in Ashanti society and that British hostility to the slave trade became a source of tension by the 1820s.
[事实] They discuss human sacrifice but repeatedly qualify that they are not specialists and do not know the latest academic thinking.
[事实] The episode says accounts they have read portray sacrifice as important to Ashanti state ritual, especially around war and royal death.
[推测] The discussion uses this topic to show how nineteenth-century imperial narratives can be morally charged, contested, and difficult to interpret cleanly.

[30:41] **Charles MacCarthy and the first major clash**

[事实] Sir Charles MacCarthy, governor of British territories along the Gold Coast, is described as a committed abolitionist who corresponded with Wilberforce.
[事实] In 1823, MacCarthy launches an expedition against the Ashanti but takes too few men.
[事实] He is ambushed at Nsamankow, killed, and his skull is taken to Kumasi, lined with gold, and used as a drinking cup.
[事实] The Ashanti then try to attack the Gold Coast, but the British resist with Denkyira support.

[31:59] **Border and uneasy coexistence**

[事实] By 1826, the British force the Ashanti to a stalemate with help from Congreve rockets.
[事实] A river becomes the marker between the two powers, and the border is confirmed in 1831.
[事实] Through the nineteenth century, the relationship becomes a mix of trade and war.
[事实] British industrial goods enter Ashanti elite culture and take on status significance.

[33:25] **Industrial Britain and shifting power**

[事实] The hosts say there are four more wars during the nineteenth century, with the last three particularly damaging to the Ashanti.
[事实] By then, Britain is described as being at its imperial and industrial peak.
[事实] The Ashanti underestimate Britain, while the British unfairly see the Ashanti as frightening savages.
[事实] The hosts explicitly say neither side’s characterization of the other is fair.

[34:11] **Wolseley and the destruction of Kumasi**

[事实] In 1872, the Ashanti attack the Gold Coast, are beaten off, and take missionaries.
[事实] Sir Garnet Wolseley leads British reprisals and sends an ultimatum to the Ashanti by traction engine.
[事实] The Ashanti reject the ultimatum, and Wolseley’s campaign becomes a rout.
[事实] Wolseley enters Kumasi, admires its sophistication, but is appalled by what British accounts describe as evidence of human sacrifice.
[事实] He then blows up parts of the city.

[37:11] **How to judge empire and violence**

[事实] The hosts discuss how modern arguments over Ashanti and British violence often reflect people’s political assumptions about imperialism.
[事实] Tom Holland says both the British and Ashanti were empires, and empires are founded on violence.
[事实] Wolseley is described as admiring Ashanti discipline, courage, and character while still feeling morally justified in destroying a sacred capital.
[推测] The episode’s position is that neither anti-British nor anti-Ashanti moral simplification is adequate for understanding the conflict.

[39:42] **Deposition and the War of the Golden Stool**

[事实] In 1896, the British depose King Prempeh and send him to the Seychelles.
[事实] The expedition is associated with Robert Baden-Powell, later founder of the Scouts.
[事实] In 1900, the British governor demands the Golden Stool and asks why he is not sitting on it.
[事实] Because nobody sits on the Golden Stool, the demand is presented as a profound insult that triggers the War of the Golden Stool.

[41:31] **Survival of the Golden Stool and the kingdom**

[事实] The war causes thousands of deaths, and many Ashanti elites are exiled to the Seychelles.
[事实] The Golden Stool is hidden so the British cannot seize it.
[事实] In the 1920s, Ashanti workmen find it and strip it of gold, causing outrage; the British refuse demands for their execution but send them into exile.
[事实] The British formally promise to respect the stool.
[事实] In 1933, Prempeh II returns from the Seychelles and becomes king in a traditional ritual centered on the Golden Stool.

[43:02] **Modern Ashanti continuity**

[事实] The hosts say there is still an Ashanti king today as a constitutional monarch.
[事实] They identify the current king as Osei Tutu II and note that he is connected to Freemasonry.
[事实] They describe the later Ashanti story as involving an “Anglo-Ashanti” fusion of Boy Scouts, Freemasonry, and the Golden Stool.
[推测] This ending presents Ashanti history not as a vanished pre-colonial world but as a living tradition shaped by conflict, adaptation, and symbolic continuity.

[44:07] **Historical blind spots**

[事实] The hosts say they are not Ashanti experts.
[事实] They describe pre-colonial sub-Saharan African history as a major blind spot outside Africa.
[事实] They say the lack of written evidence means Europeans only begin to get a clearer sense of such states in the early modern period.
[推测] The episode uses the Ashanti as an example of how global history can challenge an overly Anglo-centered historical imagination.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is as an accessible narrative introduction to the Ashanti Kingdom for listeners who may know little about pre-colonial West African history. It is strongest when linking myth, military organization, trade, sacred kingship, and imperial conflict into one coherent story.

[推测] Its most interesting feature is the refusal to make the story morally simple: the Ashanti are shown as sophisticated empire-builders, not passive victims, while the British are shown as admiring, violent, hypocritical, and sometimes genuinely motivated by abolitionist ideals.

[事实] The hosts repeatedly acknowledge that they are not specialists, especially on contested questions such as human sacrifice and the reliability of British accounts. [推测] For that reason, the episode works best as a lively gateway rather than a final scholarly account.

[推测] It is especially suitable for listeners interested in Ghana, African empires, imperial encounters, sacred political symbols, and the broader problem of how history is preserved when written sources are scarce.
