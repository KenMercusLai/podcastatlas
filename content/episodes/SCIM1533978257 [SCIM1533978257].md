# Science, God, Religion, and the Practices That Shape Human Flourishing

## 概览

This episode features Andrew Huberman speaking with Dr. David DeSteno about whether science and belief in God are necessarily opposed. DeSteno argues that science cannot prove or disprove God because God cannot be experimentally manipulated, but science can study the effects of religious practices on health, behavior, grief, loneliness, morality, and meaning.

A major conclusion is that the measurable benefits come less from merely saying “I believe in God” and more from engaging in practices: prayer, meditation, ritual, gratitude, community worship, mourning customs, and surrender to a higher power. DeSteno presents religion as a set of “spiritual technologies” that can shape mind and body, usually toward prosocial behavior, though they can also be misused.

The conversation moves from philosophical questions about God, evolution, intelligent design, and miracles into practical mechanisms: slowed breathing during prayer, motor synchrony in ritual, gratitude reducing cheating, death contemplation changing values, and religious community reducing loneliness. The episode ends with DeSteno’s personal agnostic stance and his advice to explore traditions through practice rather than requiring full intellectual certainty first.

## 分段落总结

[00:00] **Episode premise and guest introduction**

[事实] Huberman introduces Dr. David DeSteno as a professor of psychology at Northeastern University who studies morality, religion, and the health benefits of belief and religious practice.
[事实] The episode frames science and religion as commonly seen as mutually exclusive, then sets out to challenge that view.
[事实] Huberman says the discussion will cover prayer, rituals, superstition, cults, lying, empathy, miracles, the origin of life, and the afterlife.

[02:13] **Why science cannot settle the existence of God**

[事实] DeSteno says the question “Does God exist?” is important but not scientifically useful because it cannot be tested through experimental manipulation.
[事实] He says there is no empirical evidence that God exists, but science also cannot prove that God does not exist.
[事实] He argues that insisting on definitive scientific proof or disproof tends to polarize people rather than clarify the issue.
[推测] His working boundary is that science should study the consequences of belief and practice, not claim authority over metaphysical certainty.

[07:22] **Evolution, creation, and Pascal’s wager**

[事实] Huberman asks about creation, evolution, the Big Bang, and the endless regress of “what caused that?”
[事实] DeSteno says evolution can be accepted scientifically while a believer may still interpret evolution as the way God works.
[事实] DeSteno explains Pascal’s wager as a decision-theory argument for belief, then adds that religion also appears to provide benefits in this life.
[事实] He cites epidemiological data associating religious engagement over 15 to 20 years with a 30% reduction in all-cause mortality and a 25% reduction in death from cancer and cardiovascular disease.

[12:24] **Religion’s measurable health effects**

[事实] DeSteno says religious engagement is linked to lower anxiety and depression and higher meaning and flourishing.
[事实] He distinguishes these findings from simple cross-sectional claims by pointing to Tyler VanderWeele’s longitudinal work following people over time.
[事实] He says the benefits are not only from community, because religious communities show larger effects than ordinary social clubs.
[事实] He says private practices such as prayer and meditation appear to buffer anxiety and depression in young adults.

[18:23] **Russell’s teapot, William James, and rational faith**

[事实] Huberman presents Russell’s teapot as an argument that the burden of proof lies with the person making an unfalsifiable claim.
[事实] DeSteno responds with William James’s idea of an “over-belief”: a belief lacking direct evidence but not disproven, which feels right and leads to positive outcomes.
[事实] DeSteno says rational arguments such as fine-tuning can bring people to a point, but belief or disbelief ultimately still requires faith.
[推测] The episode treats belief not as irrational by default, but as a personal stance that may be evaluated partly by its effects on life.

[23:12] **Religio-prospecting: studying ancient practices scientifically**

[事实] Huberman compares religious practices to behavioral tools such as meditation, breathwork, exercise, sauna, prescriptions, and supplements.
[事实] DeSteno compares the scientific study of religious practices to pharmaceutical “bioprospecting,” where researchers learned from traditional cultures to find useful compounds.
[事实] He calls for “religio-prospecting”: examining old religious practices with rigorous scientific methods to see what they actually do.
[推测] This framing separates respect for inherited practices from uncritical acceptance of every religious claim.

[28:01] **Meditation, compassion, and aggression**

[事实] DeSteno describes an eight-week meditation study in which participants were later placed in a waiting room with a person on crutches who appeared to need a seat.
[事实] About 15% of control participants offered help, while close to 50% of meditation participants did.
[事实] In another study involving provocation, people who had meditated were unwilling to inflict pain on the person who insulted them, though they still judged the behavior as wrong.
[推测] These studies support the idea that contemplative practice can change behavior, not only subjective mood.

[32:38] **Prayer as breathwork and stress regulation**

[事实] DeSteno says formal repeated prayers, such as the rosary or Hindu sutras, tend to slow respiration and lengthen exhalations.
[事实] He says these breathing changes increase vagal tone, reduce heart rate, reduce cortisol responses, and signal safety to the mind.
[事实] Huberman emphasizes that signals travel upward through the vagus nerve as well as downward from brain to body.
[推测] Repetitive prayer may help partly because it pairs troubling thoughts with a physiological state of safety.

[37:05] **Rituals, mourning, and synchrony**

[事实] DeSteno describes rituals as sophisticated mind-body practices and uses mourning rituals as a key example.
[事实] He says eulogizing helps consolidate positive memories of the deceased, which bereavement researcher George Bonanno identifies as important for moving through grief.
[事实] In Jewish shiva, covering mirrors may reduce emotional intensification, reducing self-focus may reduce grief, and communal prayer creates motor synchrony.
[事实] DeSteno says motor synchrony increases felt connection, compassion, and helping behavior, and similar synchrony appears in singing, praying, sitting, and kneeling together.

[44:50] **Cross-cultural grief practices and continuing bonds**

[事实] Huberman asks about grieving practices in other traditions, including Irish wakes.
[事实] DeSteno says eulogizing appears across faiths, and some Irish and Hindu mourning practices also involve covering mirrors.
[事实] He describes Chinese ancestor rituals involving paper “ghost money” and symbolic objects burned for deceased relatives.
[推测] Such rituals may ease grief by maintaining a felt relationship with the dead rather than treating the loss as total severance.

[50:12] **Belief in God versus religious practice**

[事实] DeSteno says God-belief and religion are separable, and that outside Christianity religion is often more about what one does than what one believes.
[事实] He says many Jews and Hindus may be atheists while still engaging deeply in religious practices.
[事实] He says people who believe in God but do not participate in practices do not show the same health benefits.
[事实] He says prayer or chanting has stronger effects when meaningful religious content is combined with bodily synchrony.

[54:17] **Psychedelics, ritual containers, and spiritual experience**

[事实] DeSteno says psychedelics in traditional contexts are often embedded in ceremony, guidance, chanting, drumming, and a sense of safety.
[事实] He reports that about 25% of psychedelic trips are bad and about 8% are severe enough to require mental health intervention.
[事实] Huberman says he had frightening psychedelic experiences when young and later revisited psychedelics with therapeutic support.
[推测] The discussion treats psychedelics as spiritually adjacent but riskier when separated from structured support and integration.

[61:28] **Good, evil, moral variability, and cheating**

[事实] Huberman raises Milgram-style evidence that ordinary people can do harmful things under certain conditions.
[事实] DeSteno says moral behavior is more variable than people predict, and moral processing often happens below conscious awareness.
[事实] In DeSteno’s cheating studies, people often endorse fairness but then cheat when they can benefit privately and avoid reputational cost.
[事实] He says participants then rationalize their cheating with stories that preserve their self-image as good people.

[68:17] **Religious context and gratitude reduce dishonesty**

[事实] DeSteno says work by Dimitris Xygalatas found that cheating drops dramatically when people perform a money-related task in a temple rather than a restaurant kitchen.
[事实] He says gratitude practices, such as counting blessings for five minutes, sharply reduce cheating in his lab.
[事实] In one study, cheating dropped from roughly 25-30% to about 2% after a gratitude induction.
[推测] Religion may improve moral behavior both top-down through beliefs about accountability and bottom-up through cultivated emotions such as gratitude.

[71:19] **Loneliness, prayer, and God as a relationship**

[事实] Huberman says prayer and belief in God can make him feel less alone because the relationship with God feels permanent.
[事实] DeSteno says people engaged with religion report less loneliness, though data do not fully separate community effects from the felt relationship with God.
[事实] He describes some evangelical traditions as emphasizing training people to listen for God and develop a two-way relationship.
[推测] For believers, God can function psychologically like a reliable “3 a.m. friend,” especially in periods of isolation.

[78:12] **Intelligent design, evolution, and awe**

[事实] Huberman discusses the eye as a common intelligent-design example while also explaining that eye development can be understood through genes, transcription factors, proteins, and evolution.
[事实] DeSteno says he does not believe in intelligent design and regards the scientific method as one of humanity’s greatest discoveries.
[事实] He says awe at complexity can make people more open to supernatural explanations, citing work by Carlo Valdesolo.
[推测] The conversation suggests that scientific explanation and spiritual awe can coexist when explanation is not treated as reducing meaning.

[91:06] **Death anxiety and afterlife belief**

[事实] DeSteno says death anxiety follows an inverted-U pattern: strong afterlife believers show the least anxiety, firm nonbelievers show somewhat more, and uncertain people show the most.
[事实] He says religions often use fear of death to shape moral behavior, but chronic fear can be unhealthy.
[事实] He describes Buddhist death meditations, Ash Wednesday, and Rosh Hashanah prayers as examples of ritualized death contemplation.
[事实] He says contemplating death briefly and non-morbidly can reorient values toward loved ones, service, and legacy.

[97:37] **Time, continuity, and tradition**

[事实] Huberman asks how religion helps people connect themselves across time rather than only to the present moment.
[事实] DeSteno recounts a rabbi explaining that praying in Hebrew links present worshippers to Jews who used the same words for thousands of years.
[事实] He says some people are returning to more traditional forms such as Latin Mass or Orthodox Judaism because these practices feel continuous through time.
[推测] Repeated ritual language and holidays may reduce existential isolation by placing individual life inside a longer human sequence.

[103:38] **Addiction, surrender, and higher power**

[事实] Huberman notes that 12-step programs include surrender to a higher power and says the serenity prayer acknowledges limits of control.
[事实] DeSteno says religious practice is associated with some protection against addiction.
[事实] He says surrender does not mean becoming passive; it means trying one’s best while accepting that one cannot control everything.
[推测] Surrender may reduce stress by making recovery feel like a partnership rather than a purely individual act of willpower.

[109:04] **New religions and Burning Man**

[事实] DeSteno says a scholar told him that roughly 100 to 200 new religions form each year, though most disappear quickly.
[事实] He says religions tend to last when they are backed by power or when they meet a real need through practices and ideology.
[事实] He discusses Molly Crockett’s work on Burning Man, where some people report profound self-transcendent experiences that persist after returning home.
[事实] He describes Burning Man’s liminal setting, gifting culture, harsh environment, and dependence on others as conditions that can produce spiritual experiences.

[115:55] **Cults, spiritual technologies, and misuse of religion**

[事实] Huberman distinguishes cults by charismatic leaders, claims of special status, life restructuring, exploitation, crime, or mass harm.
[事实] DeSteno says cults often center on worship of a charismatic leader, but established religions can also cause harm.
[事实] He calls religious practices “spiritual technologies” that can move hearts and minds for good or ill.
[事实] He says threat can make people imagine God as more punitive and aggressive, and can lead them to recall vengeful passages rather than merciful ones.

[122:36] **AI, social media, intelligence, and religious branding**

[事实] DeSteno says current technological and social change may create conditions for new religious or spiritual forms, including churches built around AI.
[事实] Huberman describes seeing highly intelligent people publicly align with traditional religion through social media.
[事实] DeSteno says religiosity does not correlate simply with intelligence; brilliant people and less intelligent people can be believers or atheists.
[事实] The discussion turns to religious “branding,” where visual stereotypes, leaders, clothing, tone, and media presence influence whether people are drawn to or repelled by a tradition.

[132:38] **Visibility, institutional flaws, and direct experience**

[事实] Huberman says modern visibility makes it harder for famous people, politicians, religious leaders, and institutions to control their reputations.
[事实] The Catholic Church’s abuse scandals are discussed as examples of severe institutional failure involving a subset of people in the Church.
[事实] DeSteno says the impact of visible flaws may differ across religions depending on whether an intermediary such as a priest is central to religious practice.
[推测] Traditions emphasizing direct experience of God may be less dependent on institutional credibility than traditions where formal mediation is essential.

[135:15] **DeSteno’s personal stance and advice**

[事实] DeSteno says he prays at stressful times but does not have prayer as a regular practice.
[事实] He says he was raised Catholic, served as an altar boy, left the Church, and now describes himself as agnostic rather than atheist.
[事实] He practices gratitude and tries to meditate because he believes the data support their benefits.
[事实] His advice is to try different traditions, see what resonates, and remember the saying “we will do and then we will understand.”

[139:38] **Closing reflections**

[事实] Huberman thanks DeSteno for giving people a roadmap for asking difficult questions about God, meaning, death, and religion without telling them what to believe.
[事实] Huberman emphasizes that the data discussed repeatedly point back to the value of practices.
[推测] The closing message is that religious exploration can increase agency rather than reduce self-control when approached through disciplined practice and honest questioning.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode is valuable because it avoids a simple pro-religion or anti-religion frame. Its strongest contribution is the distinction between metaphysical claims that science cannot settle and behavioral practices that science can study.

The most practical material concerns prayer, gratitude, meditation, synchrony, mourning rituals, and death contemplation. These are presented not as abstract beliefs but as repeatable practices that may change physiology, moral behavior, stress, loneliness, and grief.

A limitation is that many points rely on correlational or indirect evidence, and DeSteno repeatedly notes where the data cannot isolate causes, such as whether reduced loneliness comes from God-belief, community, or both. Some claims about current public figures and emerging AI-based religious forms are framed more speculatively.

[推测] This episode is best suited for listeners interested in psychology, religion, contemplative practice, moral behavior, and the science of flourishing, especially people who want to think seriously about faith without abandoning scientific rigor.
