# Top Ten Tenuous Christmas Churches

## 概览

This festive episode of *The Rest Is History* brings back Rachel Morley of Friends of Friendless Churches to make a playful “top ten” list of British churches with Christmas connections.

The discussion ranges widely: church music, charitable giving, M. R. James ghost stories, Dickens, baking, Saint Nicholas, Scottish Christmas bans, medieval calendars, Arthurian literature, nativity wall paintings, and several seasonal customs.

The core conclusion is that churches preserve far more Christmas history than worship alone: they hold traces of music, folklore, literature, charity, art, calendars, food, film, and local memory.

## 分段落总结

[03:26] **Setting up the festive church list**

[事实] Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook introduce the episode as the second of two festive specials.
[事实] Rachel Morley returns after an earlier popular “top ten British churches” episode.
[事实] The new challenge is to cover ten British churches with festive or Christmas associations in about an hour.
[推测] The list is deliberately playful and elastic rather than a strict architectural ranking.

[06:51] **Christmas music and Chichester Cathedral**

[事实] Rachel begins with Christmas churchgoing, candles, singing, and the emotional mix of darkness, light, solemnity, and joy.
[事实] She notes that composers associated with sacred Christmas music, including Haydn, Handel, Peter Warlock, and Thomas Wilkes, were not personally saintly figures.
[事实] Thomas Wilkes, director of music at Chichester Cathedral in the 17th century, is said to have been fined for urinating on the dean from the organ loft and for loud blasphemy during services.
[推测] The segment uses comic contrast between beautiful sacred music and unruly human behavior.

[10:17] **Giving, benefaction boards, poor boxes, and Watton Church**

[事实] Churches preserved benefaction boards recording gifts for schools, almshouses, churches, and the poor.
[事实] Examples include Holy Trinity Blatherwick, where Thomas Coles left money for plum pudding for poor elderly men at Christmas, and St James’s Southleigh, where John Hart left money for coats for the parish poor.
[事实] Rachel links alms boxes to Boxing Day, when money collected through the year could be distributed to the poor.
[事实] Her chosen church for this theme is Watton Church in Norfolk, with a 17th-century carved donation box shaped as a small figure receiving coins.

[16:41] **M. R. James and Christmas ghost stories**

[事实] Rachel discusses M. R. James, scholar, Cambridge figure, and writer of ghost stories often read in Christmas settings.
[事实] James wrote stories about antiquarians, lost manuscripts, artifacts, churches, and supernatural forces disrupting rational assumptions.
[事实] His own work with manuscripts, stained glass, and church history fed directly into the atmosphere of his fiction.
[事实] Rachel says his grave at St John’s Cemetery in Eton is simple, overgrown, and hard to find.

[25:50] **Christmas films, Dickens, and St Chad’s in Shrewsbury**

[事实] Rachel links Christmas films to churches, mentioning *Die Hard*, *Chitty Chitty Bang Bang*, and *A Christmas Carol*.
[事实] She says Dickens wrote *A Christmas Carol* in six weeks, initially earned less than hoped, and later saw the story become massively popular.
[事实] St Michael’s Cornhill is presented as the likely London church tower associated with Scrooge.
[事实] The 1984 screen version of *A Christmas Carol* was filmed in Shrewsbury, and a prop gravestone inscribed “Ebenezer Scrooge” remains in the churchyard of St Chad’s.

[31:59] **Christmas baking, Henry Jones, and Llanfihangel Rogiet**

[事实] Rachel connects Christmas baking to Henry Jones, born in Monmouthshire in 1812, who invented self-raising flour in 1845.
[事实] Jones became purveyor of patent flour and biscuits to Queen Victoria.
[事实] Florence Nightingale is said to have helped get self-raising flour used by the army for biscuits during the Crimean War.
[事实] Jones married at St Michael’s and All Angels, Llanfihangel Rogiet, a Friends of Friendless Churches church.

[35:38] **Saint Nicholas and Winchester Cathedral**

[事实] Rachel describes Saint Nicholas as a fourth-century Turkish bishop and patron saint of children.
[事实] She recounts miracles associated with him, including gifts of gold, reviving murdered boys, and the miracle of the cup.
[事实] Her chosen example is the 12th-century Tournai marble font at Winchester Cathedral, carved with Saint Nicholas miracle scenes.
[事实] The hosts discuss how Dutch “Sint Niklaas” and “Sinterklaas” developed into “Santa Claus.”

[39:52] **St Mungo’s Cathedral and cancelled Christmas**

[事实] Rachel says that in 1583 a decree at St Mungo’s Cathedral, Glasgow Cathedral, rejected Christmas and Yule celebrations.
[事实] In Scotland, Christmas celebration became illegal in 1640, and even baking Christmas bread could be criminal.
[事实] The ban was repealed in 1712, but Christmas did not become a public holiday in Scotland until 1958, while Boxing Day became one in 1974.
[事实] Rachel also notes that England’s Long Parliament stopped recognizing Christmas in 1647, leading to unrest.

[42:58] **A medieval calendar at St Mary’s East Street**

[事实] Rachel chooses St Mary’s East Street in Kent for a medieval dominical or Sunday-letter dial carved into a pillar in the 1320s.
[事实] The dial has concentric circles, 28 parts, and letters used to calculate Easter and other movable feast days.
[事实] Rachel says the system is difficult to explain and that its calculations should remain valid until 2099.
[推测] The example shows how parish churches could hold practical mathematical and liturgical tools, not only decoration.

[46:56] **Sir Gawain, the Green Chapel, and Ludd’s Church**

[事实] Rachel retells the Christmas-season opening of *Sir Gawain and the Green Knight*, where Gawain accepts the Green Knight’s axe challenge.
[事实] Scholars have tried to identify the poem’s Green Chapel, described as an earthen mound with a cavern.
[事实] Her ninth choice is Ludd’s Church in Staffordshire, a deep green chasm also associated with Lollard worship.
[事实] She also mentions Nan Tor Cave near Wetton Mill as another possible Green Chapel candidate.

[51:49] **Nativity wall paintings and St Botolph’s, Hardham**

[事实] Rachel says nativity scenes, angels, shepherds, Magi, Joseph’s dream, the flight into Egypt, and the massacre of the innocents appear in wall paintings across England and Wales.
[事实] She praises Saint Elian’s, Llanelian, for painted panel ceilings, and South Newington for rich 14th-century oil-painted scenes.
[事实] Her final top-ten choice is St Botolph’s, Hardham, West Sussex, which has some of the earliest wall paintings in the UK, dating from around the 1100s.
[事实] She highlights Hardham’s holy infancy scenes, dramatic massacre imagery, and green pigment made from copper, honey, salt, and urine.

[57:28] **Honorable mentions and extra Christmas customs**

[事实] Rachel says St Andrew’s Boynton would have made the list because of William Strickland and turkey imagery, but it had already appeared in a previous top ten.
[事实] She mentions the Welsh Mari Lwyd, Kent hoodening horses, Cotswold bull-head customs, and East Midlands goat-head customs.
[事实] She discusses three horse skulls found in a sealed cavity in the spire of St Cuthbert’s, Elsdon.
[事实] She ends with tinsel, saying it was invented in Nuremberg in 1610 and noting a major tinsel factory in Cwmbran.

[63:46] **Closing and future Christmas episodes**

[事实] The hosts encourage listeners to support Friends of Friendless Churches.
[事实] They thank Rachel and wish listeners a happy Christmas and New Year.
[事实] Tom and Dominic announce a “12 days of Christmas” run of daily episodes about events or moments connected to each day.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its range: it turns “Christmas churches” into a broad cultural map covering music, charity, medieval art, literature, folklore, film, food, and calendar science.

[推测] Its strongest feature is Rachel Morley’s eye for vivid material detail, especially donation boxes, wall paintings, fonts, pigments, inscriptions, and overlooked graveyards.

[推测] Its limitation is also part of the joke: several entries are “tenuous,” so listeners wanting a systematic guide to Christmas worship or church architecture may find it digressive.

[推测] It is best suited to listeners who enjoy British history, church heritage, Christmas customs, literary history, and humorous conversation built around specialist knowledge.
