# British Jubilees and the State of the Nation

## 概览

This episode traces the history of British royal jubilees from George III through Elizabeth II, using them as moments when Britain paused to define itself. The hosts argue that the most revealing parts of jubilees are not the monarch on the balcony, but the surrounding pageantry, press commentary, political anxieties, and national self-image.

The discussion moves from the biblical and Catholic roots of the word “jubilee” into Britain’s first royal jubilee in 1809, then spends most attention on Queen Victoria’s jubilees, especially the 1897 Diamond Jubilee as the high point of imperial confidence. Later jubilees show a different Britain: humbler in 1935, economically troubled in 1977, media-savvy and pop-cultural in 2002 and 2012.

A recurring conclusion is that jubilees often mix celebration with unease. Even at the apparent height of imperial power, Kipling’s “Recessional” warned of decline; in later jubilees, monarchy becomes less about empire and more about resilience, public affection, and adapting to modern mass culture.

## 分段落总结

[00:30] **Opening With Peter Hitchens And The Jubilee Theme**

[事实] The episode opens with a Peter Hitchens passage attacking the 2012 Buckingham Palace concert and what he saw as the monarchy’s capitulation to celebrity culture.

[事实] The hosts identify the quote as Hitchens writing about Elizabeth II’s Diamond Jubilee in 2012, then connect it to the then-current Platinum Jubilee.

[事实] They frame jubilees as “punctuation points” when Britain stops to take stock of itself.

[推测] The opening contrast between royal service and pop spectacle sets up one of the episode’s central tensions: monarchy as solemn institution versus monarchy as mass entertainment.

[03:27] **The Origins Of Jubilees**

[事实] The hosts explain that “jubilee” is biblical, from Leviticus: after seven sabbatical cycles, the 50th year became a jubilee.

[事实] They mention Catholic jubilees introduced by Pope Boniface VIII and note that the Catholic Church now has jubilees every 25 years.

[事实] They also compare royal jubilees with ancient Egyptian Sed festivals and the Shah of Iran’s 1970s celebration of 2,500 years of Persian monarchy.

[推测] The discussion presents British royal jubilees as part of a much older tradition of rulers using anniversaries to renew legitimacy and public symbolism.

[05:54] **George III’s 1809 Jubilee**

[事实] George III’s jubilee is described as Britain’s first royal jubilee, held in October 1809 as he entered his 50th year on the throne.

[事实] The hosts say the timing mattered because Britain was in the Napoleonic Wars, morale was low, food prices were high, and people needed cheering up.

[事实] Celebrations included ox roasts, beer, sermons, singing “God Save the King,” fireworks at Windsor, and commemorative mugs and plates.

[事实] Military deserters and prisoners of war were pardoned, but not French prisoners, and the date coincided with the anniversary of Agincourt.

[推测] This jubilee is presented less as national introspection than as wartime morale-building around a popular but frail king.

[11:02] **Victoria’s Golden Jubilee In 1887**

[事实] The hosts move to Queen Victoria’s Golden Jubilee in 1887 and describe Britain as being in a very different position from 1809.

[事实] They say this jubilee had a stronger imperial element and included foreign kings and princes, partly because European royalty were closely related to Victoria.

[事实] Victoria paraded through London on 21 June, with Indian troops in the procession and the Queen of Hawaii among those present.

[事实] Victoria wore a United Kingdom dress with shamrocks and roses, reflecting the Irish question then in the air.

[推测] The Golden Jubilee helped reinforce Victoria’s recovery from earlier unpopularity after Prince Albert’s death.

[14:05] **The Fenian Plot And Lord Salisbury**

[事实] The hosts describe an alleged false-flag terrorist plot around the 1887 Jubilee, connected to Lord Salisbury’s desire to discredit Irish nationalist leader Charles Stewart Parnell.

[事实] They say General Francis Millen, linked to Irish-American Fenian circles, was actually working for British intelligence.

[事实] Two men, Thomas Callan and Michael Harkins, arrived too late for the planned procession attack, then travelled around Britain and exposed parts of the Fenian network.

[事实] The head of the Metropolitan Police’s secret department reportedly told his children not to attend the event.

[推测] The story is used to show that jubilee pageantry could coexist with political manipulation, imperial policing, and Irish nationalist conflict.

[18:18] **Victoria, India, And Abdul Karim**

[事实] Victoria’s role as Empress of India is described as central to the 1887 Jubilee’s meaning for her.

[事实] Victoria wanted Indian servants for the Jubilee, and Abdul Karim arrived in June 1887.

[事实] Karim served Victoria at breakfast two days after the Jubilee, became close to her, taught her Urdu, and was resented by others around the court.

[事实] The hosts say some correspondence was destroyed on Edward VII’s orders after Victoria’s death.

[推测] The Abdul Karim story illustrates both Victoria’s personal fascination with India and the racial and courtly tensions surrounding that relationship.

[21:55] **Victoria’s 1897 Diamond Jubilee As Imperial Peak**

[事实] The hosts call Victoria’s 1897 Diamond Jubilee the “top jubilee” because it coincided with the height of British imperial self-confidence.

[事实] They quote Jan Morris’s description of colonial troops and police from across the empire in the procession.

[事实] They note that Britain’s empire contained about 450 million people and had expanded to ten times its size when Victoria came to the throne.

[事实] Joseph Chamberlain wanted the jubilee to be a “festival of empire,” and Victoria sent a telegraphic message of thanks across the empire.

[事实] Newspapers and overseas observers, including the New York Times, treated the event as evidence of Britain’s global dominance.

[28:53] **Warnings Beneath The Imperial Spectacle**

[事实] After the 1897 Jubilee, Chamberlain hosted a colonial conference and pushed for a permanent imperial council or federation, but colonial leaders resisted because they wanted autonomy.

[事实] The hosts identify Kipling’s “Recessional” as the most famous literary product of the Jubilee.

[事实] They say Kipling’s poem warned that imperial pomp would vanish, even though Kipling also believed deeply in empire.

[事实] The hosts note that Britain was already being overtaken economically by the United States and Germany.

[推测] The episode treats 1897 as both the summit of British imperial confidence and the moment when signs of future decline were already visible.

[33:27] **George V’s 1935 Silver Jubilee**

[事实] George V’s 1935 Silver Jubilee is described as a “poor relation” compared with Victoria’s jubilees.

[事实] The hosts say George V had steered Britain through the First World War and the Depression, while other monarchies such as Germany’s and Russia’s had collapsed.

[事实] The jubilee had less imperial spectacle and took place under the shadow of unemployment and economic hardship.

[事实] George V’s speech acknowledged people still without work, and he was reportedly surprised by the affection shown to him.

[推测] The hosts present George V’s ordinariness and dullness as politically valuable, especially when contrasted with fascist spectacle in Europe.

[38:47] **Elizabeth II’s 1977 Silver Jubilee**

[事实] The hosts remember the 1977 Silver Jubilee as taking place during a bleak period for Britain, after the IMF bailout and very high inflation.

[事实] They mention Jim Callaghan’s government, Tony Benn’s proposed coal-carved vase, and the eventual cabinet gift of a silver coffee pot.

[事实] The Jubilee is linked to the Sex Pistols, Union Jack merchandise, tea towels, and special jubilee editions of adult magazines.

[事实] A republican rally on Blackheath organized by libertarian communists reportedly drew only five people.

[事实] The Times and Time magazine are quoted as seeing the Jubilee against a backdrop of imperial loss, economic weakness, and national decline.

[推测] The hosts portray 1977 as a party that briefly banished grim realities rather than resolving them.

[43:22] **Derek Jarman’s Jubilee And Late-1970s Bleakness**

[事实] The hosts discuss Derek Jarman’s film “Jubilee,” released after the 1977 celebrations.

[事实] They describe its plot as Elizabeth I being transported by John Dee into a bleak modern Britain where Elizabeth II has apparently been murdered.

[事实] The film is described as full of punks, drizzle, fires, and late-1970s misery.

[推测] One host suggests Jarman’s “Jubilee” is the 1977 equivalent of Kipling’s “Recessional,” a cultural work that exposes the darker underside of a national celebration.

[43:38] **The 2002 Golden Jubilee And Pop Culture**

[事实] The hosts say there was a familiar pre-jubilee pattern in 2002 of predictions that nobody would attend and the event would fail.

[事实] They cite Guardian skepticism about the limited list of planned local events.

[事实] In practice, they recall huge crowds and major palace concerts, including Brian May performing on Buckingham Palace.

[事实] The “Party at the Palace” included Paul McCartney and Eric Clapton, and more people watched it than watched Live Aid.

[事实] The Queen attended but wore earplugs.

[推测] The hosts argue that embracing pop culture helped the monarchy recover its image after the difficulties following Diana’s death.

[48:13] **2012 Diamond Jubilee And The Coming Platinum Jubilee**

[事实] The 2012 Diamond Jubilee is described as a sequel to the 2002 Golden Jubilee, again using concerts and modern media spectacle.

[事实] The hosts recall the Thames flotilla, bad weather, and Prince Philip standing for hours before later suffering health consequences.

[事实] BBC coverage of the river pageant was widely mocked for mistakes and tone, including incorrect royal style.

[事实] At the time of recording, the hosts discuss the coming Platinum Jubilee and speculate about public events, Ed Sheeran, Tom Cruise, Prince Andrew, Harry, and Meghan.

[事实] They close by comparing favorite jubilees, with 1897 and George III’s 1809 celebrations both appealing to the hosts for different reasons.

[推测] The final discussion shows how modern jubilees are now inseparable from media coverage, celebrity participation, and scrutiny of royal family controversies.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s value lies in treating jubilees not as isolated royal ceremonies but as snapshots of British political culture. Its strongest material is the movement from imperial confidence in 1897 to economic anxiety in 1977 and media-managed monarchy in 2002 and 2012.

[推测] The tone is conversational and humorous, with digressions into Peter Hitchens, Kipling, punk, newspapers, and personal memories. This makes the history lively, though listeners wanting a tightly chronological institutional history may find the asides distracting.

[推测] The episode is best suited to listeners interested in British monarchy, empire, national identity, and how public rituals reveal political mood. Its limitation is that some claims, especially around intelligence plots and motives, are presented conversationally rather than with detailed source discussion in the transcript.
