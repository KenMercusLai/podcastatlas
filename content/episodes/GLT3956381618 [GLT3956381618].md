# The Rest Is History: Alfred the Great and the Fifth Monarchists

## 概览

This episode closes the show’s 12 days of Christmas marathon by marking two January anniversaries: Alfred the Great’s crisis at Chippenham in 878 and the Fifth Monarchist uprising in London in 1661.

The first half follows the Viking pressure on Anglo-Saxon England, showing Wessex as the last surviving independent kingdom after Northumbria, East Anglia, and Mercia had fallen or been compromised. Alfred’s escape, recovery, victory at Eddington, and settlement with Guthrum are presented as decisive for the later emergence of England.

The second half shifts to Restoration London through Samuel Pepys’s diary, using the Fifth Monarchists to explore religious radicalism after the Civil Wars. The hosts discuss how apocalyptic political energy survived into 1661, how violently it briefly erupted, and how later English public life changed in tone while dissenting energies continued in other forms.

[01:40] **Final Christmas Special And January 6 Theme**

[事实] The hosts introduce this as the final instalment of their 12 or 13 days of Christmas marathon.
[事实] They frame January 6 as a date with two notable historical anniversaries to discuss.
[事实] The first anniversary is the Viking ambush of Alfred the Great at Chippenham in 878, described as probably occurring around midwinter after Twelfth Night.
[推测] The episode is structured around moments of crisis whose later consequences were larger than the immediate events.

[03:24] **The Great Heathen Army And The Fall Of Anglo-Saxon Kingdoms**

[事实] The discussion sets Alfred’s crisis against the Great Heathen Army, a Viking invasion force that landed in 865 and attacked Anglo-Saxon kingdoms.
[事实] Northumbria is described as toppled, while Edmund of East Anglia is captured, shot with arrows, beheaded, and later associated with a legend about his head being guarded.
[事实] Mercia and Wessex remain after the devastation of Northumbria and East Anglia.
[推测] The hosts present Wessex’s survival as increasingly exceptional rather than inevitable.

[04:32] **Alfred Becomes King In A Dangerous Moment**

[事实] Alfred is described as the youngest of five brothers and as someone who had not expected to become king.
[事实] He becomes king in 871, already experienced as a fighter against Vikings.
[事实] His reign begins badly when he is defeated at Wilton and has to buy off the Vikings.
[推测] Alfred’s later reputation is framed as emerging from recovery after early weakness, not from uninterrupted success.

[05:26] **Mercia’s Collapse And Wessex As The Last Kingdom**

[事实] In 873 the Vikings move against Mercia, whose king Burgred is closely allied with Wessex through marriage to Alfred’s elder sister Athelswith.
[事实] The Vikings occupy Repton, described as a major burial place for Mercian kings, symbolising Mercia’s fall.
[事实] Burgred and Athelswith flee abroad, and a figure called Ceolwulf is placed on the Mercian throne.
[事实] Alfred’s Wessex is described as the “last kingdom” after East Anglia, Northumbria, and Mercia are effectively lost or controlled.
[推测] The hosts treat the political collapse around Wessex as essential context for why Alfred’s survival mattered.

[07:08] **The Chippenham Ambush**

[事实] The Vikings attack Wareham and Exeter in the mid-870s, and Wessex is partly saved when a Viking fleet is destroyed in a storm.
[事实] In 877 Alfred reaches terms with the Vikings, including a hostage exchange, and the Vikings withdraw from Wessex.
[事实] Alfred trusts the treaty enough to celebrate the Christmas season at Chippenham.
[事实] The Vikings exploit the festive period and launch their ambush.
[推测] The hosts imply that the timing of the attack made Alfred especially vulnerable.

[08:06] **Why Alfred’s Survival Matters**

[事实] The hosts argue that if Alfred had been killed, the Vikings would probably have taken over Wessex.
[事实] They say Anglo-Saxon civilisation would not necessarily have disappeared, but the English-speaking kingdom of England likely would not have emerged in the same way.
[事实] They connect Alfred’s survival to the later influence of England on British, European, and world history.
[推测] The counterfactual discussion is deliberately playful, but it rests on the claim that Alfred’s life was a hinge point for English history.

[10:33] **Athelney, Eddington, And Guthrum’s Conversion**

[事实] Alfred escapes to the Isle of Athelney in the Somerset Levels, described as a secure island surrounded by marshes and swamps.
[事实] He sends messages across Wessex calling people to meet at Egbert’s Stone, then leads them against the Viking army at Eddington.
[事实] Alfred wins a major victory, besieges the Vikings, and after two weeks they surrender.
[事实] Guthrum converts to Christianity, takes the name Athelstan, and Alfred stands as his godfather.
[推测] The hosts present Eddington as the dramatic reversal that turns near-disaster into the foundation for political recovery.

[11:48] **London, Mercia, And The Road To England**

[事实] Under the treaty terms discussed, Alfred gains London and recognises it as key to controlling the south.
[事实] Alfred fortifies London, and the hosts mention surviving traces of the Anglo-Saxon city.
[事实] A later settlement divides Mercia, with Alfred controlling the western side while avoiding branding it as a West Saxon takeover.
[事实] Alfred’s daughter Aethelflaed marries the Mercian leader Aethelred, tying Wessex and Mercia together politically.
[推测] Alfred’s strategy is presented as both military and diplomatic: securing territory while managing local legitimacy.

[13:05] **Alfred’s Legacy And The Last Kingdom**

[事实] Alfred’s defence of Wessex is described as the launch pad for later conquests by his son Edward and daughter Aethelflaed in 917.
[事实] The hosts praise the television adaptation of Bernard Cornwell’s The Last Kingdom for depicting Alfred’s setback, comeback, and victory.

[14:40] **Pepys And The 1661 London Rising**

[事实] After the break, the episode moves to 1661 and Samuel Pepys’s diary entry for Monday, January 7.
[事实] Pepys records news of a major disturbance in London caused by “fanatics” who had killed several men.
[事实] Pepys also notes fear that the fanatics might rise again, and that people were being strictly examined on the streets.
[事实] The uprising itself is identified as having begun the previous day, January 6.
[推测] Pepys’s diary functions as the entry point into the second anniversary, giving the event immediacy and urban atmosphere.

[16:30] **A Small Group Causes A Large Panic**

[事实] Pepys’s account says the fanatics routed trained bands, put the King’s lifeguards to flight, killed around 20 men, and broke through city gates twice.
[事实] Mr Davis tells Pepys that people believed there were at least 500 attackers, though there were only around 31.
[事实] Their motto is described as “King Jesus and the heads upon the gates.”
[事实] Some expected Jesus to come and reign in the world imminently.
[推测] The hosts emphasise the disproportion between the group’s small size and the fear it generated.

[17:21] **Who The Fifth Monarchists Were**

[事实] The attackers are identified as Fifth Monarchists, or Fifth Monarchy Men.
[事实] They are described as an extreme Puritan sect that developed in the 1650s after the execution of Charles I.
[事实] Their beliefs are linked to the Book of Daniel and the idea that four earthly monarchies would be followed by a fifth monarchy, the kingdom of Christ.
[事实] The belief became popular among some people in the New Model Army.
[推测] The hosts portray the movement as an apocalyptic political religion shaped by the Civil War era.

[18:55] **From Harrison To Thomas Venner**

[事实] General Thomas Harrison is named as an important Fifth Monarchist figure and is said to have been executed after Charles II’s restoration.
[事实] Leadership later passes to Thomas Venner, a cooper who had spent 20 years in New England.
[事实] Venner and his followers are said to be convinced that Jesus may return within hours.
[事实] Many of them are described as New Model Army veterans who believe the “good old cause” is being wasted.
[推测] Venner’s background helps the hosts connect radical religion, artisan politics, and transatlantic Puritan experience.

[20:07] **The Uprising In The City**

[事实] The Fifth Monarchists meet in a tavern in Swan’s Alley off Coleman Street and decide to launch an uprising.
[事实] They go to St Paul’s, ask a bookseller called Mr Johnson for the keys, and break in after he refuses.
[事实] They shoot a man through the heart after he says he supports King Charles.
[事实] Authorities send musketeers and trained bands against them, but the Fifth Monarchists repeatedly beat them back.
[推测] The uprising appears chaotic and improvised, but the attackers’ religious certainty makes them unusually determined.

[21:17] **Defeat, Capture, And Execution**

[事实] The group withdraws to woods outside the city and returns to cause more damage.
[事实] On Wednesday they are eventually rounded up by the King’s lifeguards after fierce fighting.
[事实] Venner kills three men with a halberd in Threadneedle Street.
[事实] The final stands occur at the Helmet Tavern on Threadneedle Street and the Blue Anchor on Coleman Street.
[事实] Venner is captured and later hanged, drawn, and quartered.
[推测] The hosts use the brutal punishment to underline how threatening the restored monarchy considered this kind of radical challenge.

[22:49] **Explaining Failed Prophecy And Religious Violence**

[事实] Tobias Smollett is cited as saying Venner’s followers maintained that if they had been deceived, the Lord himself was their deceiver.
[事实] The hosts call this a poor excuse and joke that it blames God or Jesus.
[事实] They describe the uprising as disrupting simple periodisation: the 1650s are often seen as radical and religious, while the Restoration is imagined as parties and dancing.
[推测] The event shows that Restoration culture did not immediately erase the political and religious energies of the Civil War period.

[23:42] **From Civil War Enthusiasm To Later Public Life**

[事实] The hosts describe the uprising as a last gasp of the idea that religious enthusiasm should be acted on violently.
[事实] They compare the shock and confusion caused by the attacks to later urban terror events, while acknowledging the difference in context.
[事实] Dominic asks where the religious and political enthusiasm of the Civil War era went, given that later public life became associated with coffee houses and newspapers.
[事实] Tom argues that dissenting energy remained profound, even if it was no longer expressed through armed violence.
[推测] The discussion suggests that radicalism did not vanish but was redirected into religious dissent, abolitionism, the American War of Independence, and broader cultural and economic change.

[26:38] **Class, Politics, And Upcoming Episodes**

[事实] Tom says the 1650s involved people such as barrel makers feeling they had a right to contribute to the state of the nation.
[事实] The hosts contrast this with a later return to assumptions that great aristocrats should dominate politics.
[事实] They mention future episodes on Charles I, 1922, crossing the Rubicon, the Falklands War, America’s holy wars abroad, and the Roman Empire.
[推测] The ending connects the specific stories to the show’s broader interest in how political authority, popular participation, and historical memory evolve.

## 播客点评/总结

The episode’s strength is its ability to treat two apparently separate January 6 stories as examples of fragile political order: Alfred’s Wessex nearly collapses under Viking pressure, while Restoration London briefly faces apocalyptic insurgency from a tiny sect.

The Alfred section is especially effective in showing how a single survival story can be tied to larger questions about England’s emergence, language, and global historical consequences. Some of the counterfactual claims are explicitly speculative and are best read as interpretive framing rather than settled fact.

The Fifth Monarchist section works well because it uses Pepys’s diary to make the event vivid, then broadens into a discussion of Civil War radicalism, Restoration settlement, and the transformation of dissent. Its limitation is that the discussion is brisk and conversational, so some historical claims are sketched rather than fully argued.

[推测] This episode is best suited to listeners who enjoy British political and religious history, counterfactual historical thinking, and lively conversation that mixes narrative, jokes, and historiographical reflection.
