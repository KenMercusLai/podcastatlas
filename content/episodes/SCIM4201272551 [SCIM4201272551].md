# Learning, Neuroplasticity, Exercise, and Brain Health with Dr. Tommy Wood

## 概览

This episode centers on adult neuroplasticity: how the brain changes through strengthened and weakened connections, why pruning is as important as growth, and how learning can continue across the lifespan without requiring large-scale neurogenesis.

A major theme is that maintaining brain health requires both continuing meaningful activities and taking on novel, difficult challenges. Dr. Tommy Wood emphasizes broad, human skills such as dancing, sports, language learning, music, martial arts, and creative arts because they combine cognition, movement, social interaction, attention, and error correction.

The discussion also contrasts flow with “clutch states.” Flow is framed as one form of high performance, while clutch states involve focused effort, friction, and hard work. The episode argues that learning often happens outside flow, especially when people make mistakes, tolerate discomfort, and push slightly beyond current ability.

Later sections focus on exercise, nutrition, supplementation, dementia prevention, concussion recovery, and strongman training. Aerobic exercise is tied more strongly to gray matter and hippocampal function, while resistance training is linked to white matter and executive function. Nutrition and supplements are discussed through the lens of avoiding deficiencies, testing biomarkers, and being cautious about unproven interventions.

[00:00] **Episode Setup and Core Question**

[事实] The episode opens with a study in older adults comparing low-intensity activity, zone-two treadmill work, and high-intensity interval training using the Norwegian four-by-four protocol.

[事实] Andrew Huberman introduces Dr. Tommy Wood as a medical doctor, neuroscience researcher, chief science officer of Better Brain, and expert in neuroplasticity and human performance.

[事实] The stated focus is how brain states, exercise, mental training, nutrition, and supplementation can support learning, focus, skill expression, and brain health.

[推测] The cold open signals that the episode will treat exercise not merely as general health advice but as a specific lever for brain plasticity.

[04:00] **What Neuroplasticity Means**

[事实] Dr. Wood defines neuroplasticity as the brain responding to inputs so people can better navigate and survive their environment.

[事实] He emphasizes that learning involves strengthening some synapses, weakening others, and pruning unnecessary connections.

[事实] He distinguishes neuroplasticity from neurogenesis, noting that adult brains do not make many new neurons across most regions but still change through altered connections.

[推测] The discussion reframes adult learning as circuit refinement rather than simple brain “growth.”

[10:00] **Pruning, Aging, and Maintaining Function**

[事实] Wood says motor learning likely requires both strengthening useful connections and downregulating connections that interfere with accurate movement.

[事实] He links later-life functional loss partly to the brain no longer receiving inputs needed to maintain certain abilities.

[事实] Huberman asks whether people should exercise, keep doing known activities, or seek new challenges to keep the brain young.

[推测] The episode treats “use it or lose it” as true but incomplete without novelty and challenge.

[12:00] **Novel Activities and Cognitive Aging**

[事实] Wood says observational and intervention studies suggest cognitively engaging hobbies are associated with slower decline, better maintenance of function, or lower dementia rates.

[事实] Novel activities such as language learning, coordinated movement, exercise, musical training, and music theory have shown improvements, especially in executive function.

[事实] He mentions large interventions such as Maintain Your Brain in Australia and the POINTER trial in the United States.

[推测] Novelty appears most valuable when it forces active adaptation rather than merely repeating familiar routines.

[16:00] **Brain Training and Processing Speed**

[事实] The Maintain Your Brain study included diet, physical activity, behavioral therapy, and online brain training based on individual risk factors.

[事实] The POINTER trial used diet, exercise, cardiovascular risk monitoring and treatment, and brain training.

[事实] BrainHQ is described as a platform focused partly on processing speed, influenced by Michael Merzenich’s work.

[事实] A secondary analysis of the ACTIVE trial found that processing-speed training with booster sessions at one and three years was associated with lower dementia risk 20 years later.

[20:00] **Processing Speed Versus Reaction Time**

[事实] Wood distinguishes simple reaction time from processing speed: processing speed requires interpreting and remembering information, not merely responding to a stimulus.

[事实] He cites a large study using implicit association test data showing reaction time slowly declines from adulthood, while processing speed is more stable until around age 60 and then tends to drop.

[事实] Processing-speed training requires people to remember what they saw and where they saw it after brief visual or auditory exposure.

[推测] Activities that demand fast interpretation may protect capacities that ordinary later-life routines often stop challenging.

[22:00] **Choosing Better Plasticity Stimuli**

[事实] Wood says crosswords and Sudoku may be focused but are not always true cognitive challenges.

[事实] He argues that building capacity requires being both focused and challenged.

[事实] Broad skills, such as learning a ball sport, may transfer better than narrow computer-based brain training because they recruit motor, social, visual, auditory, and decision-making systems.

[推测] The preferred learning target is not simply “harder” but richer across multiple brain networks.

[26:00] **Dance, Sports, Arts, and Language**

[事实] Wood identifies dancing as one of the strongest examples of a plasticity-promoting activity, especially ballroom and line dancing in older-adult meta-analyses.

[事实] Dancing combines movement, skill learning, social interaction, and musical timing.

[事实] Other suggested activities include ball sports, board sports, team sports, skateboarding with a helmet, martial arts such as Brazilian jiu-jitsu, creative or visual arts, language learning, and video games.

[推测] The strongest activities seem to blend novelty, feedback, social stakes, movement, and rapid adjustment.

[30:00] **Mistakes, Shame, and Adult Learning**

[事实] Wood says adults often avoid new skills because they dislike being bad at things.

[事实] He argues that mistakes and discomfort are critical for neuroplasticity.

[事实] He encourages acknowledging the shame component of beginner learning and leaning into it.

[推测] Social embarrassment may be part of why effective plasticity triggers are avoided in adulthood.

[34:00] **Challenge as a Psychological Need**

[事实] Wood says people like to be good at things and to overcome challenges.

[事实] He connects physical, cognitive, and psychological challenges with growth when the challenge is meaningful and possible to improve at.

[事实] He warns that challenges that are too hard can have the opposite effect if they produce repeated failure without progress.

[推测] A useful challenge sits near the learner’s edge: hard enough to require effort but not so hard that it destroys engagement.

[36:00] **Flow and Its Limits**

[事实] Huberman describes flow as a frequently misused term and distinguishes the original idea from the popular notion of effortless power.

[事实] Wood defines flow as maximal expression of a complex learned skill near the edge of ability.

[事实] He says optimal performance does not always require flow.

[事实] He argues that optimal learning does not require flow because mistakes, errors, and friction often move people out of flow.

[推测] The episode positions flow as a performance state, not the central engine of learning.

[38:00] **Clutch States**

[事实] Wood introduces “clutch states” from sports psychology as states of high performance that still feel effortful and demanding.

[事实] In a clutch state, the person is at an optimal arousal level but experiences the task as hard cognitive and physical work.

[事实] Huberman connects clutch states with “limbic friction” and the anterior mid-cingulate cortex, a brain region he associates with tenacity and taking on hard tasks.

[推测] The episode suggests that clutch states may be more useful to seek deliberately than flow when the goal is learning and durable performance.

[42:00] **Mastery, Virtuosity, and the Unknown**

[事实] Huberman describes a framework of unskilled, skilled, mastery, and virtuosity, where virtuosity involves a master pushing into uncertainty.

[事实] Wood compares this to classic descriptions of elite performance, including Ayrton Senna driving beyond what felt like his conscious capability.

[事实] The discussion links virtuosity to moments where high skill meets uncertainty and extreme demand.

[推测] The conversation treats virtuosity as a temporary performance state built on long preparation rather than a permanent trait.

[46:00] **Fundamentals and the Rule of Thirds**

[事实] Huberman notes that elite dancers maintain basic drills daily before working on new performance pieces.

[事实] Wood says training sessions often follow a “thirds” pattern: one third feel great, one third feel average, and one third are difficult.

[事实] He says difficult sessions are not evidence that training is going wrong.

[事实] A piano study found that practice amount, not age, was the strongest predictor of performance on piano-specific tasks among younger and older amateur and expert pianists.

[推测] Maintaining fundamentals may be essential, but the way they are maintained depends on the skill.

[52:00] **The 10,000-Hour Rule and Practice Duration**

[事实] Wood says the original 10,000-hour finding described the average accumulated practice of expert violinists by age 20, not a universal threshold for expertise.

[事实] The violinists in that study tended to practice two sessions of 60 to 90 minutes per day.

[事实] Wood says hard cognitive work is usually sustainable in chunks of about 20 to 30 minutes, with breaks.

[事实] Huberman relates this to writing and deep work, noting that very long work sessions often cause a later drop in output.

[推测] Effective practice is constrained by attention and recovery more than by heroic daily duration.

[56:00] **Distraction, Phones, and Mental Space**

[事实] Wood says eliminating distractions is one of the best ways to do deep focused work.

[事实] He notes that people can become trained into patterns of distraction and may reach for the phone even without a notification.

[事实] Huberman mentions research suggesting that even a phone in a bag can impose cognitive costs.

[事实] Wood suggests that mind-wandering time, such as in the shower or during meditation-like quiet, may help integration and idea formation.

[推测] Focus is treated as a trainable capacity, not only a matter of willpower.

[60:00] **Diet Patterns and Brain Health**

[事实] Wood says many cognitive and psychological diet interventions use versions of the Mediterranean diet, including the MIND diet.

[事实] The MIND diet combines ideas from Mediterranean and DASH diets and emphasizes foods such as seafood, vegetables, berries, and whole grains.

[事实] A New England Journal of Medicine trial comparing the MIND diet with caloric restriction found similar benefits in both groups.

[事实] Wood says Mediterranean-style dietary benefits may reflect overall whole-food quality and nutrient density rather than one specific food such as olive oil.

[推测] The practical message is to prioritize adequate energy, nutrient density, and whole-food quality before arguing over named diets.

[64:00] **Energy, Nutrients, and Supplements**

[事实] Wood describes three nutrition dimensions: energy, nutrients, and overall dietary pattern.

[事实] He says both chronic caloric restriction and chronic caloric excess are associated with smaller brain volume in population comparisons.

[事实] He identifies vitamin D, iron, omega-3 fatty acids, B12, folate, and possibly B6 and riboflavin as nutrients with strong evidence related to cognitive function or dementia risk.

[事实] He also discusses magnesium, zinc, choline, ethanolamine, polyphenols, carotenoids, dietary fiber, coffee, tea, chocolate, berries, nuts, seeds, eggs, and seafood.

[推测] Nutrient sufficiency, rather than high-dose supplementation for everyone, is the main nutritional target presented.

[68:00] **Omega-3 Timing and Body Stores**

[事实] Wood says the body has more nutrient buffering than people may assume.

[事实] He explains that adipose tissue can store long-chain omega-3 fats and release them during sleep or lipolysis.

[事实] He says phospholipid omega-3 forms may enter the brain more after a single dose, but over several days triglyceride forms can still become available through fat stores.

[事实] He says someone who eats fish early in the week does not necessarily need omega-3 supplements later in the same week if overall intake is sufficient.

[推测] The discussion pushes against overly rigid daily supplement timing when the body can store and redistribute certain nutrients.

[72:00] **Testing, Homocysteine, and Nutrient Interactions**

[事实] Wood recommends testing vitamin D, hemoglobin, iron, omega-3 status, and homocysteine when possible.

[事实] He says normal lab ranges are not always the same as risk-optimized ranges, using homocysteine as an example.

[事实] He describes trials showing that omega-3 status and B-vitamin status interact: benefits may not appear if one side of the pathway is insufficient.

[事实] He mentions the VITACOG, B-PROOF, and Omega AD trials in this context.

[推测] Single-nutrient trials may miss effects when they fail to account for interacting deficiencies.

[76:00] **Multivitamins and Population-Level Supplementation**

[事实] Wood says the COSMOS study found cognitive benefits from a basic Centrum Silver multivitamin in older adults.

[事实] He says the multivitamin supplied roughly recommended daily allowance levels rather than large doses.

[事实] He says a cocoa flavanol supplement seemed beneficial mainly in people with poorer baseline diet quality.

[推测] A basic multivitamin may help some people by covering gaps, while targeted antioxidants may matter most when diet quality is low.

[80:00] **Raising the Floor Versus Pushing Optimization**

[事实] Wood distinguishes population-level brain health from individual optimization.

[事实] He says improving food quality and access can raise the floor for many people.

[事实] He says evidence is strongest for avoiding deficiency or insufficiency, while benefits of pushing already-good nutrient levels higher are unclear.

[事实] He supports personal experiments when variables are controlled and outcomes are meaningful.

[推测] The episode is open to optimization but cautious about claiming benefits beyond known sufficiency thresholds.

[86:00] **Peptides, Safety, and Creatine**

[事实] Huberman describes public interest in GLP drugs, peptides, BPC-157, and GHK copper.

[事实] Wood says he would want evidence that a substance works in humans for the intended purpose and evidence that it is safe.

[事实] He notes that most peptides lack high-quality human evidence outside approved GLP medications and some growth hormone secretagogues for specific indications.

[事实] Wood identifies creatine as a strong example of a supplement with good safety evidence and potential benefits for physical performance or cognition in some people.

[推测] The discussion warns that popularity and anecdotal enthusiasm are not substitutes for safety and efficacy data.

[92:00] **Arousal, Stimulants, and Exercise**

[事实] Huberman says there is no plasticity pill, but arousal, alertness, and focus appear to set the stage for plasticity.

[事实] Wood says how an athlete feels often predicts performance better than HRV or blood tests.

[事实] He warns that stimulants such as caffeine can make people feel they are performing better while worsening some complex cognitive tasks.

[事实] He contrasts this with exercise-induced arousal, which he describes as a more reliable way to improve performance without the same dissociation.

[推测] Exercise is framed as a safer arousal tool than simply adding stimulants.

[100:00] **Acute Exercise Before Cognitive Work**

[事实] Wood says a 20- to 30-minute jog has strong meta-analytic support for improving cognitive function immediately afterward.

[事实] Short resistance training or brief intense sprints with long rests may produce similar arousal-related benefits.

[事实] He cautions that exhaustive exercise can impair cognitive function afterward.

[事实] Huberman notes that excessive training volume can make him drag later in the day, while leaving some volume “in the tank” helps.

[推测] The best pre-workout for thinking may be moderate, capacity-appropriate exertion rather than maximal depletion.

[104:00] **Long-Term Exercise, HIIT, and the Hippocampus**

[事实] Wood describes a study where older adults brisk-walked three times per week for 40 minutes for a year and showed increased hippocampal volume, fitness, circulating BDNF, and memory.

[事实] He then describes a newer trial comparing low-intensity exercise, zone-two treadmill work, and high-intensity interval training using the Norwegian four-by-four protocol.

[事实] The HIIT group improved fitness similarly to the zone-two group but had better hippocampal function and structural maintenance on MRI.

[事实] The HIIT group maintained benefits for five years after the six-month intervention.

[推测] High-intensity exercise may provide a strong long-term brain stimulus even if it is not ideal immediately before demanding cognitive work.

[108:00] **Cortisol and Acute Stress**

[事实] Huberman and Wood argue that cortisol is not inherently bad and that acute cortisol increases from exercise can be adaptive.

[事实] Wood distinguishes acute stress, which drives adaptation, from chronic stress, which increases allostatic load and disease risk.

[事实] He says regular exercise can improve resilience to psychological stress.

[事实] The discussion criticizes conflating all stress with harmful chronic stress.

[推测] The useful target is intermittent stress followed by recovery, not stress avoidance.

[114:00] **Resistance Training and White Matter**

[事实] Wood says short resistance workouts can acutely improve cognition through arousal.

[事实] Longer-term resistance training studies in older adults often use two or three weekly sessions, five or six exercises, and three sets of 8 to 12 reps.

[事实] Resistance training is linked to white matter structure and executive function, while aerobic exercise is linked more to gray matter, hippocampal function, and memory.

[事实] Wood suggests IGF-1 may be one mediator of resistance training’s effect on white matter.

[推测] A complete brain-health exercise plan likely needs both aerobic and resistance training because they affect different brain systems.

[122:00] **Dementia and Alzheimer’s Risk**

[事实] Wood defines dementia as the end stage of cognitive decline where daily functioning is impaired.

[事实] He says Alzheimer’s disease accounts for about 60 to 80% of dementia cases, with vascular dementia also common and often overlapping with Alzheimer’s pathology.

[事实] He distinguishes rare monogenic early-onset Alzheimer’s from the more common late-onset form, where ApoE, polygenic risk, lifestyle, and environment matter.

[事实] He says it is increasingly accepted that potentially about half of dementia cases may be preventable.

[推测] The episode treats dementia risk as modifiable long before diagnosis, similar to earlier intervention before diabetes develops.

[126:00] **Modifiable Dementia Risk Factors**

[事实] Wood cites the Lancet Commission on Dementia Prevention, which identified 14 modifiable risk factors accounting for about 45% of dementia risk or cases.

[事实] Listed factors include early-life education, high blood pressure, diabetes, hearing loss, vision loss, brain trauma, high LDL cholesterol, and low physical activity.

[事实] Sleep loss and nutrient status were discussed but not included in that Lancet estimate.

[事实] Wood says dementia risk appears to rise especially when people consistently sleep fewer than about six hours.

[推测] The estimate of preventable dementia may be conservative if sleep, nutrient status, and socioeconomic factors are included.

[130:00] **Shingles Vaccine and Dementia Signals**

[事实] Wood describes natural experiments in Wales, Australia, and Canada where eligibility for shingles vaccination changed sharply by birth date.

[事实] Those eligible for or receiving the shingles vaccine had lower dementia risk in those observational natural experiments.

[事实] He says the older Zostavax vaccine and newer Shingrix vaccine have both been associated with lower dementia risk, with Shingrix showing a lower risk than Zostavax in a U.S. comparison.

[事实] He notes that randomized controlled trial evidence is still needed.

[推测] The possible mechanism may involve reduced shingles illness, immunomodulation, or effects on herpes viruses, but causality is not established.

[136:00] **Serious Illness and Stepwise Cognitive Decline**

[事实] Wood says one of the best ways to maintain long-term function is to reduce the chance of getting seriously sick.

[事实] Studies such as Adult Changes in Thought and the Rush Memory and Aging Project found that cognition often declines stepwise after major illness rather than only steadily with age.

[事实] Hospitalization was associated with larger step changes downward in cognitive function.

[推测] Vaccinations and illness prevention may protect cognition partly by reducing periods of inflammation, inactivity, poor intake, and reduced stimulation.

[138:00] **Concussion: Immediate Priorities**

[事实] Wood says anyone with a significant head injury should receive appropriate medical evaluation and scans when needed.

[事实] He says preventing hyperthermia after concussion is important and that the brain does not tolerate getting too hot after injury.

[事实] He recommends avoiding very sugary refined carbohydrates after acute brain injury because blood sugar elevations are associated with worse outcomes.

[事实] He recommends avoiding alcohol, partly because it impairs sleep, and raises caution about caffeine acutely after injury.

[推测] The practical early goal is to reduce additional metabolic strain on an already energy-impaired brain.

[142:00] **Concussion Supplements**

[事实] Wood says creatine has evidence after concussion, including a pediatric trial using 0.4 grams per kilogram per day.

[事实] He discusses magnesium, suggesting 400 milligrams once or twice daily and preferring a bioavailable form such as magnesium glycinate.

[事实] He says omega-3 supplementation may be useful long term for people at concussion risk, with studies in collegiate football players showing less accumulation of injury biomarkers across a season.

[事实] He also mentions melatonin, high-dose branched-chain amino acids for sleep in TBI studies, choline, Boswellia, and Enzogenol as possible symptom- or evidence-dependent options.

[推测] The supplement discussion is oriented around low-risk, plausible supports rather than definitive concussion cures.

[149:00] **Return to Activity After Concussion**

[事实] Wood says concussion care has shifted away from simply resting in a dark room for prolonged periods.

[事实] He recommends return to physical activity as soon as tolerated, without risking another head injury.

[事实] A typical progression starts with low-level aerobic activity below symptom-worsening threshold, then skill-specific work, full training, and finally return to play.

[事实] He mentions physical therapy, vestibular therapy, ocular motor training, eye tracking, and virtual or augmented reality approaches for persistent symptoms.

[推测] Recovery is presented as active and staged, with symptom-guided progression.

[152:00] **Strongman Competition and Adult Athletic Challenge**

[事实] Wood says he is 41 and competes in drug-tested natural strongman.

[事实] He describes strongman events including farmer’s walks, yoke walks, circus dumbbell, loading medleys, max squat, log press, sled push, and sled drag.

[事实] He competes in the lightweight masters category and discusses cutting below 200 pounds before rehydrating after weigh-in.

[事实] Huberman connects Wood’s continued competition to the episode’s broader theme of taking on adult athletic challenges.

[推测] The strongman discussion serves as a concrete example of pursuing difficult, skillful physical challenges in midlife.

[158:00] **Closing**

[事实] Huberman says he learned a great deal from Wood and wants to bring him back to discuss brain injury, stroke, and recovery.

[事实] He directs listeners to Wood’s work and book through the show notes.

[事实] The episode closes with standard requests to subscribe, follow, review, comment, and sign up for the newsletter.

## 播客点评/总结

This episode’s main value is that it makes neuroplasticity practical without reducing it to a single trick. The strongest throughline is that the brain changes through input, effort, error, recovery, and repeated exposure, and that the most useful inputs are often broad, difficult, and embodied.

A highlight is the distinction between flow and clutch states. The conversation pushes back against the idea that learning should feel effortless, and it makes a clear case that friction, mistakes, and focused strain are often where real adaptation happens.

The episode is also useful because it separates acute and long-term effects: a moderate jog or resistance session may help immediate focus, while harder HIIT or sustained resistance training may drive structural brain changes over months. The nutrition and supplement discussion is similarly careful, emphasizing sufficiency, testing, and interactions rather than universal high-dose recommendations.

[推测] The episode is best suited for listeners interested in learning, aging, cognitive performance, exercise programming, and brain health. Its limitation is breadth: it covers many domains, so some topics, especially concussion supplements, peptides, dementia mechanisms, and sex differences, are introduced without enough time for full clinical nuance.
