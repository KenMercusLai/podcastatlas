# England and America: Six Episodes in an Unequal Relationship

## 概览

This episode uses England’s World Cup match against the United States as a prompt to trace the changing relationship between Britain/England and America. The hosts frame the relationship through power imbalance: in the 19th century America was more preoccupied with Britain, while in the 20th century Britain became increasingly preoccupied with America.

The discussion moves through six historical vignettes: the War of 1812 and the burning of Washington, Charles Dickens’s disillusioning American tour, Andrew Carnegie and the shift of economic power, Black American GIs in wartime Britain, the Beatles and the British Invasion, and American views of Britain in the 1970s and after.

A recurring conclusion is that the “special relationship” is less a simple alliance than a mixture of shared language, cultural inheritance, rivalry, resentment, admiration, dependence and mockery. By the end, the hosts suggest that Britain may now be more Americanized than ever, especially through media, social media and the language of the internet.

## 分段落总结

[00:33] **Macmillan’s Greeks-and-Romans Frame**

[事实] The episode opens with Harold Macmillan’s wartime comparison of Britons to Greeks serving in an American Roman Empire.
[事实] The hosts connect this to the World Cup series, explaining that England is playing the United States and that they are examining England/Britain’s relationship with America.
[事实] They argue that less powerful countries often become more obsessed with more powerful ones than vice versa.
[推测] This frame sets up the episode as a story about cultural closeness shaped by unequal power.

[02:33] **From American Anglophilia to British Americanization**

[事实] The hosts state that in the 19th century the United States was more obsessed with Britain, while in the 20th century the roles reversed.
[事实] They say politicians dislike the phrase “special relationship,” but that culturally there is a special relationship because early American culture came predominantly from Britain.
[事实] They point to the continuing prominence of British actors in American television and film as evidence of cultural integration.
[推测] The discussion treats Anglo-American culture as deeply shared, even when political power is unequal.

[04:13] **America as a Branch of Early Modern England**

[事实] The War of Independence is described as, in many ways, a civil war over issues rooted in English constitutional history.
[事实] The hosts mention Louis Hartz’s argument that the United States preserved aspects of 17th-century England, including constitutional language, political obsessions and religiosity.
[事实] They note that debates over the Founding Fathers can seem strange from a secular 21st-century British perspective.
[推测] The hosts use this idea to explain why America can feel both foreign and historically familiar to Britain.

[06:01] **The War of 1812 and Britain’s Secondary Theatre**

[事实] The hosts say they have divided Anglo-American history into six key episodes, beginning with the British burning of the White House in 1814.
[事实] Britain’s main focus in 1814 was the Napoleonic Wars in Europe, making the American war a secondary concern.
[事实] American grievances included British impressment of sailors and the vulnerability of British Canada.
[事实] Congress voted for war in 1812, the first declaration of war in United States history and, according to the hosts, the closest such vote.

[10:22] **The Burning of Washington**

[事实] The American attempt to take Canada failed, including a humiliating surrender at Detroit.
[事实] As Napoleon’s position collapsed, Britain had more ships and men available for action against America.
[事实] In August 1814, George Cockburn and Robert Ross led 4,500 troops up the Potomac, brushed aside American defenses and entered Washington on 24 August.
[事实] British forces ate the banquet Madison had left behind, burned public buildings including the White House, but did not burn private residences.
[事实] The hosts say the action helped bring Madison to negotiate terms and that the United States abandoned its original war aims.

[14:23] **American Memory of 1812**

[事实] The hosts say the War of 1812 is not generally remembered in the United States as a defeat.
[事实] The bombardment of Fort McHenry inspired the poem later set to music as “The Star-Spangled Banner,” which became the U.S. national anthem in 1931.
[事实] The British defeat at New Orleans on 8 January 1815 occurred after the peace treaty had been signed but before news reached America.
[事实] Andrew Jackson became a major American hero after the battle, in which around 2,000 British soldiers died and around 70 Americans were lost.
[推测] The hosts present American memory of the war as an example of turning partial defeat into national defiance.

[17:08] **Charles Dickens’s American Tour**

[事实] British and American cultural links remained strong, and British writers were widely read in the United States.
[事实] Charles Dickens travelled to America in 1842 because he was popular there, wanted copyright reform, wanted material for a book and wanted a break.
[事实] He was celebrated in Boston and New York, including at a Boz Ball attended by 3,000 people, and he met writers including Edgar Allan Poe, Washington Irving and Longfellow.
[事实] Over time Dickens became disenchanted with America, frustrated by constant attention and angered by American unwillingness to change copyright law.
[事实] He was especially revolted by slavery and also criticized the Mississippi, the Midwest, Ohio and the treatment of Native Americans.

[24:26] **Dickens’s Backlash Against America**

[事实] The copyright issue between American publishers and British writers remained unresolved until 1891.
[事实] Dickens’s American Notes attacked slavery but also criticized Americans as vulgar, crass and overbearing.
[事实] Poe described American Notes as a suicide note for Dickens’s popularity in America.
[事实] Martin Chuzzlewit included caricatured American chapters, and Washington Irving broke off his friendship with Dickens.
[事实] Dickens portrayed American conversation as dominated by “dollars.”
[推测] This segment shows how British stereotypes of American vulgarity and money-mindedness hardened in the 19th century.

[27:11] **Andrew Carnegie and the Shift of Economic Power**

[事实] Andrew Carnegie was born in Dunfermline in 1835 and emigrated to Pittsburgh with his family in 1848.
[事实] He rose from work in a cotton mill and as a telegraph operator to become a major capitalist and monopolist.
[事实] In The Gospel of Wealth, Carnegie argued that the rich should get rich and then use their wealth to help the poor improve themselves.
[事实] After selling his companies to J. P. Morgan in 1901, Carnegie used his fortune to fund parks, libraries, schools and institutions in America and Britain.
[推测] The hosts treat Carnegie as a symbol of economic leadership passing from Britain to America.

[30:57] **The Diplodocus as Anglo-American Symbol**

[事实] On 12 May 1905, Carnegie visited London’s Natural History Museum for the unveiling of a cast of a Diplodocus fossil.
[事实] Carnegie said his accumulation of capital enabled the gift and presented the dinosaur as a symbol of the links between Britain and America.
[事实] The hosts say Anglophobia persisted in the United States, including through Irish-American hostility to Britain.
[事实] Carnegie nevertheless embodied the idea that Britain and America had much in common and could form an alliance for peace.
[事实] The hosts connect this to the later American entry into the First World War on Britain’s side.

[35:27] **The Second World War and American Arrival**

[事实] The second half turns to the 20th century, when America overtakes Britain as the dominant power.
[事实] After Pearl Harbor, American GIs began arriving in Britain in January 1942, eventually numbering around three million.
[事实] The hosts say the GIs were admired and resented by locals in equal measure.
[事实] George Orwell wrote that it was hard to go around London without feeling Britain was occupied territory.
[事实] Around 150,000 of the American troops in Britain were Black soldiers, generally serving in labor, transport or engineering units rather than frontline roles.

[38:31] **Black GIs and British Reactions to Segregation**

[事实] The United States Army was segregated, and the Roosevelt administration formally asked Britain to uphold segregation.
[事实] Britain refused to segregate pubs, restaurants and public spaces outside American bases.
[事实] The hosts say many Britons were shocked by white American soldiers’ attitudes toward Black soldiers.
[事实] Black journalist Roy Ottley wrote that Britain offered Black Americans a degree of social equality not found at home.
[事实] Pubs often became places where Black GIs mixed more easily with locals.

[43:43] **Violence Over Race and Military Discipline**

[事实] In Bamber Bridge near Preston, local pubs reportedly responded to American demands for a color bar by putting up “black troops only” signs.
[事实] In June 1943, fighting between Black troops and white officers in Bamber Bridge escalated into gunfire; Private William Crossland was killed and 27 Black GIs were convicted.
[事实] The hosts describe similar incidents in Cornwall in September 1943 and in Bristol in July 1944.
[事实] These conflicts involved racism, white officers’ anger at Black soldiers socializing with white British women, armed military police and secret or severe sentences.
[推测] The incidents reveal a clash between American military segregation and British local resistance to enforcing it.

[48:47] **Hull, Cottingham and Local Solidarity**

[事实] The hosts describe Black GIs stationed near Hull and Cottingham as having been warmly received by locals.
[事实] African American soldiers reportedly visited the Wilberforce monument and kissed the ground connected to Britain’s anti-slavery history.
[事实] Some Black soldiers adopted three orphaned Black children from the Simmons family as unit mascots and raised £160 for their clothes and education.
[事实] Ellen Cole refused a colonel’s request to stop being friendly with Black serviceman Wiley Young, saying he was not in America now.
[推测] These stories serve as a counterpoint to the violence, showing everyday solidarity across racial lines.

[51:24] **Sex, Status and Postwar American Cool**

[事实] The hosts discuss British resentment of American GIs as “overpaid, oversexed and over here.”
[事实] They say British women and girls were attracted by American money, uniforms and glamour.
[事实] Postwar Britain is described as gray and austere, while America appears bright, wealthy, fast and sexy.
[事实] From about 1942 for roughly 22 years, America is said to have had the upper hand in both high culture and popular culture.
[事实] British teenagers copied American fashions and music, and the word “teenager” is identified as an American invention.

[54:16] **Black American Music and the Beatles**

[事实] British bands in Liverpool and elsewhere were heavily inspired by Black American music, including blues.
[事实] Lonnie Donegan and Acker Bilk had limited American success before the Beatles.
[事实] Capitol Records initially doubted the Beatles would succeed in the American market because Britain seemed gray, old-fashioned, dowdy and uncool.
[事实] Once Capitol committed, it spent heavily on promotion, sent materials to disc jockeys and helped create hype before the Beatles arrived in New York on 7 February 1964.
[事实] The hosts say the airport crowd was given a dollar and a Beatles T-shirt.

[57:40] **The British Invasion**

[事实] The Beatles’ Ed Sullivan Show appearance is described as a milestone in popular culture, watched by 73 million people.
[事实] In spring 1964, the Beatles held multiple positions in the Billboard chart, including the top five.
[事实] From February 1964 for the next two years, British acts held the top Billboard spot in one out of every two weeks.
[事实] The hosts argue that the Beatles succeeded partly by repackaging American culture through English language, wit and self-mockery.
[事实] James Bond is presented as another example of American money and markets selling an idea of British cool.

[61:36] **Post-Imperial British Cool and Its Decline**

[事实] The hosts say the 1960s British cultural breakthrough could happen only once Britain was no longer seen primarily as an imperial bully.
[事实] They emphasize that the Beatles and Sean Connery were not upper-class figures, making them less like old imperial stereotypes.
[事实] By the end of 1966, the San Francisco sound and more folky, hippie and political American music began displacing many British acts.
[事实] The Beatles and the Rolling Stones continued, but Britishness lost some of its 1960s cool.
[推测] Britain’s appeal in this period depended on being stylish, self-mocking and non-threatening.

[64:23] **Fawlty Towers and American Views of Declining Britain**

[事实] The final vignette begins with the 5 March 1979 Fawlty Towers episode “Waldorf Salad.”
[事实] The American visitor Mr. Hamilton complains about British roads, weather, licensing hours, hotel service and food.
[事实] The hosts say the episode captures Anglo-American cultural differences at the end of the 1970s.
[事实] American guidebooks from the period described Britain through decline, shabbiness, strikes, bad weather, poor hotels and economic problems.
[事实] Paul Theroux’s Kingdom by the Sea is cited as another example of harsh American criticism of British hotels.

[68:14] **American Contempt and Modern Americanization**

[事实] Henry Kissinger is quoted describing Britain in 1975 as a tragedy and a scrounger.
[事实] Gerald Ford warned in January 1977 that it would be tragic if America went down Britain’s path.
[事实] Samuel Beer’s 1982 book Britain Against Itself is cited as reflecting a widespread American view of Britain as a country on its knees.
[事实] The hosts connect this attitude to New York Times coverage of Britain in the 1970s and after Brexit.
[事实] They argue that Britain is now more Americanized than ever because of social media, American media power and the American language of the internet.
[推测] The closing Fawlty Towers reference reinforces the episode’s final image of Britain both mocking itself and losing the argument to America.

## 播客点评/总结

[推测] The episode’s main value is its use of vivid episodes rather than a straight diplomatic chronology. The War of 1812, Dickens, Carnegie, Black GIs, the Beatles and Fawlty Towers give the Anglo-American relationship a social and cultural texture beyond leaders and treaties.

[推测] Its strongest thread is the repeated reversal of power: America begins as the anxious younger nation in Britain’s shadow, then becomes the richer, sexier and more culturally dominant force. The hosts are especially effective when showing how admiration and resentment operate on both sides.

[推测] A limitation is that the structure is deliberately selective and humorous, so it does not provide a comprehensive history of diplomacy, empire, economics or race. It is best suited to listeners who want a lively interpretive tour of Anglo-American cultural relations rather than a formal survey.
